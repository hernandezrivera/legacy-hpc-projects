Project Summary

Caritas Bangladesh (CB) has played a pivotal role as a primary responder to the Rohingya crisis since the onset of the refugee influx in 2017, assisting to both the host community and Rohingya populations. CB has been operating an education program for children, adolescents, and youth since 2020
The project aims to continue into the upcoming year, 2024. It focuses on providing education for children aged 3 to 14, following the Myanmar Curriculum. The project will be implemented through Learning Centres (LCs), where the enrolled children will carry on with their education in the year 2024. The education program will strictly adhere to the workbooks, learning materials, and guidelines provided and endorsed by the Education Sector. This project is focused on gender mainstreaming education project, aiming for a balanced male-female ratio with 68% girls and 32% boys.
In collaboration with Library Without Boarder (Bibliothèques Sans Frontières- BSF) CB has been implementing education activities for Rohingya Adolescent and Youths of aged 15-18, focusing on Access to information, education, and learning through setting-up a library space with an Ideas Box, a multi-media hub that provides educational and cultural resources to vulnerable groups to meet and engage in activities together in a safe space. Through its modularity and the variety of contents and tools it offers, Ideas box increases the scope of existing activities and fosters local initiatives. Another innovation of the project is Ideas Cubes to conduct outreach sessions to improve access to information and learning for extremely vulnerable or isolated people who are restricted in their movement due to social or other barriers. There will be specific sessions especially geared towards adolescents and women of 15-18 years of age. The library will also have dedicated times for free use and provide open access to its content for the targeted group for IT equipment is using as a tool to facilitate sessions with the beneficiaries, as well as to overcome limitations.
The project encompasses the following key components:
Pre-primary Education (KG): Children aged 3-5 years will receive pre-primary education, focusing on ECCD through interactive and joyful activities, play, and audio-visual sessions, preparing them for Learning Centre in the and primary school in host community.
Primary and Secondary Education: Children aged 6 to 10 will engage in primary education, spanning Grade-I, Grade-II, and Grade-III, while those aged 11 and above will receive secondary education based on competency.
Initiatives will be undertaken to address existing access barriers, including the utilization of remote and blended teaching-learning methods to reach more learners. A community-based approach will be promoted, and radio, tablet, and IT-based learning will serve as alternatives for hard-to-reach children, even after regular school hours. The educational facilities will be inclusive and sensitive to the needs of children with disabilities and girls.
Access to information, education, and learning for adolescents and youth: Providing meaningful and functional numeracy, literacy, and life skills education as part of pre-vocational education to individuals aged 15-18. Basic literacy and numeracy courses, available in both Myanmar and English languages, will equip adolescents and youths with the competencies to function effectively in their daily lives and pave the way for pursuing studies in academic and vocational areas as they desire.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The Rohingya population in Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh has suffered from genocide, with their children denied education since 1982 in Myanmar. Currently, there are 960,539 Rohingya refugees in Bangladesh, including 52% children (GoB-UNHCR Joint Registration Exercise, March 31, 2023). The universal right to education is upheld by international agreements that host countries must honor. UN Sustainable Development Goals 4 (Quality Education) and 5 (Gender Equality) stress the importance of education for Rohingya children, whether in Bangladesh or elsewhere.Adolescents and young people aged 15 to 18 are highly vulnerable due to their developmental stage. Shockingly, only 3% of this group are currently engaged in any educational program. Gender disparities in access to learning opportunities persist in the camps, particularly for those with limited access to schooling and recreational activities. Deep-seated social norms result in women and girls being assigned fewer responsibilities within families (Source: UNHCR).Significant improvements have been made in delivering education to children in the camps, but addressing the educational needs of adolescents and youth remains challenging. This is due to limitations on formal education and the absence of equivalent programs like the Myanmar Curriculum. Youth in emergency situations face safety and resource access risks. Direct education delivery to them is insufficient; a locally developed multicultural education approach with specialized content and delivery methods is essential. Transitioning to the Myanmar Curriculum is expected to enhance education quality and acceptance. The pandemic's impact, including Learning Center closures and remote learning challenges, underscores the critical need for educational continuity.In 2024, the Education Sector will persist in its efforts to ensure that Rohingya refugee children and youth have access to equitable and high-quality education opportunities, utilizing the Myanmar curriculum and progressive learning in a safe and protective environment.According to the JRP 2024 document, the education sector's objective is to guarantee that 100% of children aged 3-14 have access to quality education through community engagement. Also the necessity for basic literacy, numeracy, life skills, and pre-vocational training for youth and adolescents, especially those who have lacked prior educational opportunities. Additionally, there is a focus on enhancing access to education for women and girls. In the 15-18 age group, 97% of females and 87% of males have not attended educational facilities. Similarly, in the 19-24 age group, 99% of females and 97% of males have not attended educational facilities (Education Needs Assessment – Rohingya Refugee Response, 2019).An assessment by Library Without Borders and Caritas Bangladesh underscores the need for innovative community engagement techniques and the challenges in implementing such approaches due to methodological and cultural disparities in traditional methods, as well as the lack of training for humanitarian workers. The Ideas Box and Ideas Cube are key innovations in this project. Social-emotional learning can mitigate risks and enhance the effectiveness of educational programs, leading to positive impacts on academic outcomes, school retention, and attendance rates. It also reduces stress and anxiety, mitigates negative student behavior like bullying and violence, addresses SEA, and juvenile crime, while improving overall health outcomes, including reduced drug use.Furthermore, according to the Education Sector's progress data as of August 2023, the coverage for the host community stands at only 16%, leaving a significant 84% gap as indicated in the Joint Response Plan (JRP) for 2023. Acknowledging the urgency of the situation, Caritas Bangladesh is dedicated to extending its education program for Rohingya children, adolescents, and youth into 2024, with potential expansion to the host community.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Caritas Bangladesh (CB) is concerned about the safety and dignity of vulnerable Rohingya individuals, including women, girls, boys, persons with disabilities, and the elderly. To address these concerns, CB has strategically placed learning facilities in safe areas, away from poorly lit pathways, hazardous slopes, and isolated zones. These facilities feature separate, clearly labeled latrines for girls and boys, emphasizing the organization's commitment to ensuring Protection from PSEA and maintaining a strong focus on Safeguarding within its all response.In pursuit of inclusivity, CB conducts consultations with stakeholders to ensure access to services for everyone. An approach centered on the community fosters participation, empowerment, and community strengthening, reducing risks and enhancing resilience. CB maintains an Accountability to Affected People (AAP) framework, remaining responsive to community needs through its Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) structure and Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM). Real-time monitoring informs project adaptations, acknowledging the dynamic nature of the situation. Protection mainstreaming is a significant component of CB's programs, aligning with its Protection Policy, Safeguarding Policy, and Code of Conduct.CB follows Gender Policy in operation and mainstreams gender considerations. In this project, CB will continue its services to ensure female participation in all committees and decision-making processes. Female teachers and volunteers will be deployed for girls-only sessions. CB will also focus on girls-friendly and gender-equal environment within the learning facilities. All project staff will receive training and resources to engage in gender-sensitive programming.

3.3 - Age & Disability

Caritas Bangladesh (CB) adheres to a comprehensive Child and Vulnerable Adult Safeguarding Policy, customized for the humanitarian context. CB has established a Safeguarding Committee and designated a Safeguarding Focal Point in all offices, including the Cox's Bazar office. Training in Safeguarding, the Code of Conduct, and Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) orientation is conducted annually for all staff and volunteers. To ensure compliance, staff and volunteers are required to sign a Safeguarding Policy, Code of Conduct, and self-declaration form upon joining, and their references are checked before employment. Beneficiaries are introduced to the safeguarding policy through orientation sessions prior to service delivery and distribution activities. Safeguarding is integrated into the agendas of all meetings, trainings, and workshops. A Bengali version of the Safeguarding policy and a one-pager are distributed among staff and host volunteers. Furthermore, all visitors, advisors, and vendors are required to read and sign the safeguarding form before visiting the camps.The same obligations and protocols apply to the staff, teachers, and volunteers working under the proposed project.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

A comprehensive MEAL (Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning) mechanism is in place to uphold Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) principles, incorporate the Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM), and engage in regular stakeholder consultations. These measures are designed to enhance the quality of education within the project. CB is committed to building the capacity of its staff and volunteers, empowering them to facilitate culturally sensitive programming that promotes sustainability and can potentially be replicated within the community. All policies and guidelines of CB will be strictly maintained to ensure compliance with emergency response standards as well as Education Sector standards. The project actively involves parents, Learning Centre based Community Education Support Group (CESG), and Parent-Teacher Associations (PTAs) in the education process. Community members participate in the needs assessment through Participatory Action Research (PAR) methods. Impact findings are shared with beneficiaries, and monthly meetings with CESGs and parents are held to collaboratively decide on ways to ensure quality education for their children. Feedback from diverse learner populations, including children, adolescents, and youths, is given significant importance, and the project encourages community participation in all stages of implementation. Community suggestions at the field level are highly valued.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Caritas Bangladesh (CB) has a robust Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policy in place and is an active member of the PSEA network. In line with CB's established practices, the following measures will be undertaken within this project to ensure the mainstreaming of PSEA: Strong Adherence to PSEA Policy: The existing PSEA policy will be rigorously maintained and enforced throughout the project. Code of Conduct: To further strengthen the commitment to PSEA, a Code of Conduct will be implemented within the project. This code will be applicable to all project stakeholders, including staff, volunteers, vendors, and suppliers, emphasizing the importance of appropriate behavior and ethics. Establishment of Reporting Mechanism: A confidential and accessible reporting mechanism will be established to facilitate the tracking and mainstreaming of PSEA. Investigations and Response Mechanism: A clear and functional mechanism for investigating and responding to reported incidents of sexual exploitation and abuse will be in place. Incidents will be handled promptly, impartially, and in compliance with legal requirements. Accountability: All project staff and partners will be held accountable for adhering to the PSEA policy and the Code of Conduct. PSEA Focal Point: To oversee and ensure the effective mainstreaming of Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, a dedicated PSEA Focal Point will be appointed. This individual will be responsible for coordinating PSEA activities and upholding the organization's commitment to PSEA within the project. Caritas Bangladesh is committed to maintaining the highest standards of protection and accountability in all its humanitarian efforts. Through these PSEA measures, CB aims to create a protective environment, prevent sexual exploitation and abuse, and ensure the safety and dignity of all beneficiaries involved in the project.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Emergency preparedness and response activities are integral to the proposed project. Caritas Bangladesh (CB) is committed to providing services that ensure each learning facility has a Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) contingency plan in place and that these plans are followed in close collaboration with the community. A key aspect of the proposed project is the capacity building of various stakeholders, including teachers, Community Volunteers, Learning Center Management Committee (LCMC) members, and other community stakeholders, in the context of Preparedness for Emergency Response and Urgency (PERU) and DRR program management. Their active engagement in these initiatives is essential to prevent and mitigate potential crises. By enhancing their knowledge and skills and involving them in decision-making processes and program implementation, the community is empowered to build sustainability and resilience. To further enhance preparedness, hazard mapping orientations will be provided to project staff and volunteers. The results of the hazard mapping exercise will inform the development of comprehensive development plans and contingency plans for all Learning Facilities. These plans will be continuously maintained and updated throughout the year in 2024, ensuring that the project remains ready to respond effectively to any emergencies or crises that may arise.
