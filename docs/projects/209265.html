Project Summary

Due to persistence outbreak of war in April 2023 in Khartoum, Sudan between RSF and SAF, there is continuous violence against marginalized women and girls, particularly those experiencing intersecting forms of discrimination as pressing issue in Sudan. Gender-based violence (GBV) is a pervasive issue affecting individuals, families and communities worldwide. Gadarif State, Sudan has witnessed a concerning rise in GBV cases, necessitating urgent intervention. This project proposal aims to address GBV in 12 localities of Gadarif State, Sudan through a comprehensive approach involving prevention, response and support mechanisms.
This proposal aims to outline a comprehensive project that addresses this problem by implementing targeted interventions, raising awareness and empowering the affected communities. By addressing the root causes and providing support systems, we can create a safer and more inclusive environment for marginalized women and girls in Gadarif State. To address access challenges of GBV intervention focus on updating referral pathways in collaboration with local actors, especially women-led organizations. Given the heightened sensitivity surrounding GBV, awareness campaigns will be essential to build trust with communities and survivors. The plans to create or support Community-based Protection Networks and local women’s networks to enhance awareness efforts. The GBV intervention to prioritize assist humanitarian actors to integrate GBV risk mitigation

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

Feedback is collected through Surveys and questionnaires ,Focus groups and interviews, suggestion boxes, Regular meetings and workshops and Monitoring and evaluation. The project team will analyse and synthesize the information to identify common themes, patterns, and areas of improvement, then use this feedback to make necessary adjustments to activities, modify strategies, introduce new initiatives that better align with the community’s needs and expectations. Continuous feedback loops ensure that the project remains responsive and adaptable to the evolving needs of the community.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

The SUDO will developed accountability to affected population as part of its good and great standards a mechanism of Client Responsiveness to ensure that the target population is involved in the whole project cycle. through various approaches including country leadership, emphasis on transparency, feedback and complaints mechanisms, participation in project implementation, monitoring and evaluation. The SUDO will adopt and coordinate a client-responsiveness approach integrated into the SUDO programs. This will be guided by the SUDO’s guidelines for client responsiveness and feedback mechanisms. To provide and manage context-appropriate communication and feedback channels that are safe, accessible, transparent and confidential for clients to provide feedback and complaints on services as well as seeking information and receiving timely, appropriate responses about SUDO’s actions and decisions. SUDO will use both proactive and reactive feedback channels. Proactive feedback channels will deliberately and systematically solicit feedback from clients on SUDO services through feedback and information sessions, community leaders’ forums, FGDs, short surveys and individual patient exit interviews. Reactive feedback channels are mechanisms that the SUDO will provide to its clients and other stakeholders to communicate with the SUDO at the time and on the subject they choose. The combination of consultation and participation will be incorporated throughout the life of the project
