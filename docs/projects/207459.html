Project Summary

QC will continue the shelter interventions to provide the safe living conditions to Rohingya Refugees / FDMNs, with careful consideration of Age, Gender, Diversity Mainstreaming (AGDM) and Extremely Vulnerable Individuals (EVI) families. As part of Sector Objective 3: Provide safe living conditions to Rohingya refugees/FDMNs and targeted host community assistance, including shelter and LPG assistance, to reduce exposure to physical and protection-related risks, QC is planning to construct 1,000 MTS in Camp 21. The design and location of these MTS in Camp 21 will be based on an assessment conducted by the SCCCM Sector and approved by the Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commissioner (RRRC). In the event that space is unavailable or an emergency arises in another camp, QC will collaborate closely with the SCCCM Sector, RRRC, CiC and camp focal points to achieve the intended objectives.
The proposed shelter program will follow an inclusive and community-driven approach, emphasizing active involvement from the community members. Special consideration will be given to female-headed households, the elderly, households with special needs, and persons with disabilities. QC will meticulously document data related to these vulnerable groups, using the SADDD (Sex, Age and Disability Disaggregated Data) framework. It is anticipated that 12% of persons with disabilities (PWD) will receive shelter assistance based on the criteria established by the Washington Group on Disability.
In addressing Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) concerns, QC will adhere to the principles outlined by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) pertaining to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, in addition to following the established guidelines of the Cox's Bazar PSEA network as required. QC is an active member of the PSEA network in Cox's Bazar and is committed to upholding high standards in this regard. As part of our commitment, all staff members will undergo two refresher training sessions throughout the project's duration to ensure that they are well-informed and vigilant in preventing PSEA.
Furthermore, QC will ensure effective coordination and open communication with various stakeholders, including the Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commissioner (RRRC), Inter Sector Coordination Group (ISCG), SCCCM Sector, WASH teams, Protection agencies, Camp-in-Charge (CiC), Site Management teams, Deputy Commissioner (DC), Upazila Nirbahi Officer (UNO), and any other relevant stakeholders.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The total number of Rohingya refugees in Cox’s Bazar and and Bhasan char is around 961,729 individuals. A high majority of the Rohingya refugee population is concentrated in 33 extremely congested camps within Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazilas of Cox’s Bazar district, Bangladesh. The refugees are dependent on the assistance provided by the humanitarian community and the government of Bangladesh. In the camps, shelters are exposed to cyclic monsoons and face risk of floods, landslides, fire and cyclones. Temporary materials such as bamboo and tarpaulin have a limited capacity to resist weather impacts, and thus require regular repairs and replacement. Use of adequate material (treated bamboo, good quality tarpaulin) along with the design, site plan, proper technical details for the materials connections, can improve lifespan of materials if properly followed (Shelter Performance Standard Assessment Report 2023). The findings of the 2023 Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (J-MSNA) indicate that access to food and shelter materials is the foremost priority among the needs in the Rohingya camps and the host community. Specifically, the assessment reveals that 41% of refugee households have not been able to enhance their shelter conditions. Among this group, the majority reported the lack of improvement was primarily due to a shortage of humanitarian assistance (60%) or the assistance received being insufficient (34%). 4W data (as of 31st July 2023) from the Shelter and NFI (SNFI) sector showed that around 21% of Rohingya households reached with shelter assistance. (TSA, SUM, R&M, MTS, HH level site improvement). This highlights the pressing demand for heightened support and additional resources to meet the shelter needs of the Rohingya refugees, totaling 931,574 individuals, residing in the Cox's Bazar camp areas. The challenging combination of adverse weather conditions and the effects of climate change has made the shelters susceptible to heavy monsoons, flash floods, landslides, fires, and cyclones. Following government guidelines, these shelters are constructed using bamboo and tarpaulins, which need to be replaced annually, causing significant environmental impact. In an effort to reduce the strain on forests and extend the lifespan of bamboo, many shelter partners are utilizing treated bamboo. Nonetheless, sourcing high-quality treated bamboo remains a challenging task. However, it is imperative to explore alternative solutions that are weather-resistant and fire-resistant. Shelter partners must collaborate with the RRRC, ISCG, SCCCM sector to advocate for these alternative solutions in the camps, ensuring the provision of more durable and sustainable shelter options that can withstand the adverse environmental conditions and reduce the ecological impact. The Shelter Performance Standard Assessment (2023) provides valuable data to assess the overall conditions of shelters and housing in the surveyed areas. While progress has been made in certain areas, there are still challenges to address to ensure that shelters meet the necessary standards and provide adequate protection and privacy for the residents. Efforts should be directed toward improving ventilation, drainage, and fire protection, as well as addressing concerns related to eviction and privacy. By addressing these issues, the living conditions and safety of residents can be enhanced, leading to more resilient and sustainable shelter solutions for the future.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Rationalization aims to ensure that all Rohingya refugees in Bangladesh have equitable access to all basic services in a predictable, efficient, and timely manner, and that the humanitarian community is more transparent and accountable. Qatar Charity (QC) has established a robust presence in Camp 21, with a health post, water purification plant, shelter initiatives, Solar Street lamps, and Non-Food Items (NFI) interventions. For the proposed project, QC is committed to working in close collaboration with other operational partners operating in the same areas. A key priority is to prevent duplication and overlap in efforts. This collaboration extends to various stakeholders, including the Camp-in-Charge (CiC) office, site management teams, UNHCR shelter unit, shelter focal points, refugees and other relevant organizations. To ensure effective coordination and transparent communication, QC will regularly update and share 4W reports with the SCCCM sector focal points. This coordinated approach will help streamline efforts and enhance the impact of the project while avoiding unnecessary duplication. Our approach to program response is typically grounded in our technical expertise and our well-established geographical presence. We prioritize program implementation in various camps based on the need and after obtaining the required approval from the Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commissioner (RRRC) and relevant sectors. Additionally, we actively engage in capacity building sessions organized by NGO platforms and regularly participate in coordination meetings with different sectors. To enhance collaboration and transparency, we share our 4W (Who is doing What, Where, When) reports with the relevant sectors, fostering a well-coordinated and informed response to the challenges at hand. This ensures that our initiatives are well-informed, compliant with regulations, and effectively address the specific needs of the refugee and host communities we serve.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

QC is committed to mainstreaming protection in all Shelter activities, adhering to guiding principles such as non-discrimination, participation, and inclusiveness. In the implementation of Shelter projects, QC will conduct assessments to ensure that gender-related considerations are integrated, aligning with the updated minimum shelter performance standards. During beneficiary identification and data collection using the SADDD framework, specific barriers for Extremely Vulnerable Individuals (EVIs) will be assessed. Shelters will be made accessible through consultations with refugees and relevant stakeholders, with adjustments implemented wherever feasible. A special emphasis will be placed on supporting all Extremely Vulnerable Individuals (EVIs) with porter assistance, including shelter construction. To enhance awareness and vigilance in preventing Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), all staff members, including supplier personnel, will undergo two refresher training sessions throughout the project's duration. This comprehensive approach aims to ensure that Shelter activities are inclusive, protective, and aligned with established standards. Qatar Charity will follow the internal and established complaint feedback referral pathway within the camp setting, specifically at Camp 21. We will maintain strong communication channels with protection actors to facilitate protection referrals, and a dedicated emphasis will be placed on collaborating with Shelter-CCCM actors to address shelter-related complaints and feedback efficiently.

3.3 - Age & Disability

The proposed shelter interventions aim to establish safe and secure living spaces suitable for various age groups, taking into consideration child safeguarding issues. Qatar Charity (QC) will assess the vulnerability of children within the family and community, paying special attention to child-headed households, separated children, or those at risk. Special consideration will be given to female-headed households, the elderly, households with special needs, and persons with disabilities. QC will meticulously document data related to these vulnerable groups, using the SADDD (Sex, Age and Disability Disaggregated Data) framework. It is anticipated that 12% of persons with disabilities (PWD) will receive shelter assistance based on the criteria established by the Washington Group on Disability.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Qatar Charity is committed to upholding and promoting compliance with the commitments outlined in the Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) framework. Throughout the project lifecycle, including planning, implementation, and post-project phases, community consultations will be actively conducted. This engagement will take place through various channels such as house visits, Focus Group Discussions (FGD), and Key Informant Interviews (KII). To further enhance open communication channels, complaint boxes and festoons will be strategically placed in targeted communities, sites, and public spaces, with information presented in the local language. In addition, Qatar Charity will establish complaints and feedback desks at all distribution points, providing Rohingya families with a dedicated platform to share feedback and voice any concerns about the assistance they receive. This comprehensive, multi-level feedback mechanism will extend beyond shelter activities, encompassing various aspects of assistance, thereby enriching the overall effectiveness of the program.

Yes

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

In addressing Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) concerns, QC will adhere to the principles outlined by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) pertaining to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, in addition to following the established guidelines of the Cox's Bazar PSEA network as required. QC is an active member of the PSEA network in Cox's Bazar and is committed to upholding high standards in this regard. As per guiding principles of “PSEA Network Standard Operating Procedure for SEA Complaint Referral 2023”, all SEA-related information will be kept confidential, identities will be protected, and the personal information on survivors shall be collected and shared only with the informed consent of the concerned individuals or caregiver. Where the survivor gives such consent, only purpose-specific and on a need-to-know basis information shall be shared for the purpose of helping the survivor, such as referral for services or for investigation. As part of our commitment, all staff members will undergo two refresher training sessions throughout the project's duration to ensure that they are well-informed and vigilant in preventing PSEA.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, the project incorporates several Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) elements, considering the historical threat of climate change and disasters in the area. In response to the needs of the population, the shelter project will utilize treated bamboo and metal footing. This approach aims to prevent the poles from being affected by insects, thereby extending the lifespan of the poles, reducing reliance on natural forest resources, and minimizing solid waste at the camp level. Qatar Charity (QC) will collaborate with site management to implement household-level site improvements, including guide walls and drainage around the shelter, to enhance the safety of beneficiaries. Additionally, the incorporation of tie-down kits, metal footing, and proper bracing serves as protective measures to enhance the resilience of shelters against cyclones, contributing to a more robust Disaster Risk Management (DRM) strategy within the project, ensuring a more robust and resilient response to potential disasters and adverse weather conditions.
