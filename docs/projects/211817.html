Project Summary

Syrian context continues to be marked by ongoing conflict and multiple interconnected humanitarian crises. In 2024, 16.7 million Syrians require humanitarian aid, up from 15.3 million in 2023. Of these, 5.5 million are displaced, with 2 million in last resort sites. Earthquakes in 2023 worsened the situation, uprooting families and causing casualties. Basic services eroded, exacerbating challenges, including food insecurity and malnutrition. Economic downturn and conflict persist, fueling poverty and reliance on aid. Currency depreciation and high inflation deepen economic woes. Ongoing conflict hampers humanitarian access, hindering assistance delivery and endangering civilians. The HNOs estimates that 15.3 M Syrians are in need of ERL assistance, with 43 sub-districts scoring severity 4 or 5.
Against this background, by aligning with the strategic objective 3 (SO3) to enhance community resilience and improve access to livelihood opportunities and basic services, COOPI remains dedicated to providing life-saving assistance, but also catalyzing positive change and sustainable development in Syria. COOPI will support vulnerable communities across the Governorates of Damascus, R. Damascus, Dar’a, Aleppo, Hama, Homs, Idleb, Lattakia, and Raqqa. Leveraging its past and current expertise, COOPI is dedicated to a community-driven approach, bolstering initiatives in Protection, FSA, ERL, WASH and Education to ensure access to essential services for underserved areas and the most vulnerable beneficiaries. In 2024-2025 COOPI’s strategy will extend beyond meeting immediate needs, striving for sustainable recovery and resilience-building among targeted HHs.
The selection of intervention locations and beneficiaries is informed by a comprehensive multi-sectorial needs assessment conducted by COOPI in September 2023, alongside ongoing assessments of areas where COOPI is active. Our programs integrate ERL, Protection, and FSL interventions to provide comprehensive support to communities facing severe challenges, scoring between 3 and 4 on the 2024 severity scale.
Empowering women and youth remains a priority, as their engagement not only enhances family and community well-being but also supports IGAs. COOPI prioritizes vocational training to equip beneficiaries with the necessary skills and knowledge to initiate their own businesses, subsidizing training costs to ensure participation.
Vocational trainings will encompass diverse areas, from vegetable transformation and conservation to dairy product production, emphasizing economic principles and practical techniques. COOPI will build on a strong expertise gathered in the past year, and will implement a UNDP-funded project in Aleppo. COOPI will also invest in essential equipment to sustain food production and storage, facilitating long-term food security and income generation within communities.
Furthermore, COOPI will engage in infrastructure rehabilitation projects, offering short-term employment opportunities in sectors such as sewage line repair, debris removal, and road and classroom rehabilitation. Targeting vulnerable families in both urban and rural areas, COOPI provides tailored support to reduce social vulnerability and foster sustainable coping strategies.
Expanding its scope, COOPI's ERL strategy prioritizes providing electricity and water access to rural communities, laying the groundwork for social and economic recovery. This aligns with our overarching goal to increase resilience among affected communities, particularly focusing on HHs headed by women, vulnerable youth, families with PwDs, and IDPs and returnees, fostering cohesion and integration with host communities.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

COOPI's ERL intervention in Syria for 2024-2025, as part of the Syria ERL HRP, will employ a community-centered approach to address the multifaceted needs of affected populations while prioritizing inclusivity and gender balance. A focus will be given to ensure sustainable access to electricity, installing transformers for the provision of electricity in underserved areas, while solar systems will be procured and installed in public facilities and households, leveraging local expertise and partnerships for implementation (CA1, CA2). Rehabilitation efforts for electrical networks and stations will involve technical assessments, repair works, and capacity-building initiatives to enhance infrastructure resilience (CA3, CA4). In education, COOPI will collaborate with local authorities and communities to rehabilitate schools, including the installation of WASH facilities and procurement of furniture, promoting safe and conducive learning environments (CA8). Similarly, road repair, sewage line rehabilitation, and debris removal will employ community-based approaches, engaging local labor and resources to maximize impact and ownership (CA10, CA11, CA13). Community participation will be central to neighborhood cleaning initiatives, with COOPI providing training, tools, and logistical support to mobilize volunteers and foster a sense of ownership and pride (CA17, CA18). Entrepreneurship support will involve capacity-building workshops, mentorship programs, and access to micro-grants, enabling aspiring business owners to develop viable enterprises and contribute to local economic growth (CA19, CA20, CA21). Vocational and technical training will be tailored to local market demands, with curricula designed in consultation with industry stakeholders and delivered through partnerships with vocational institutions and private sector actors (CA22, CA23). Workshops will facilitate dialogue between beneficiaries and potential employers, fostering connections and job placement opportunities (CA25). During infrastructure rehabilitation projects, COOPI will prioritize the engagement of local labor and contractors, providing employment opportunities and skills development through on-the-job training and capacity-building initiatives (CA26, CA27, CA28). Targeted assistance for vulnerable groups will be delivered through cash transfer programs, case management support, and market-based interventions, ensuring equitable access to livelihood opportunities and essential services (CA33, CA34). By implementing these activities with a participatory, community-driven approach, COOPI aims to empower affected populations, strengthen resilience, and contribute to sustainable recovery and development in Syria.
