Project Summary

The humanitarian intervention aims to ensure that girls and boys, especially the most vulnerable and those affected by humanitarian situations, are protected from all forms of violence, exploitation, abuse and harmful practices. The persistent drought and economic shocks have increased the vulnerability of children, adolescents and women to protection risks and may be exacerbated by the probable El Niño. This proposal is targeting 100,000 people, primarily children and their caregivers, adolescents especially girls including adolescent mothers, community, child protection structures as well as other humanitarian sectors responding to the effects of drought in 15 districts targeted by the child protection sub-sector. In cases of drought girls are the most affected as they suffer from family separation, child labour and child marriage. Children adopt negative coping mechanisms such as migration and this heighten increase exposure to sexual violence, exploitation and other forms of abuse. Furthermore, family tension escalate children and particularly women suffer from domestic violence.
The implementation scale and scope of humanitarian intervention is to strengthen CPiE coordination, capacity building of Child Protection sub-sector community based mechanisms, integrate CPiE prevention and response in other intervention sectors, strengthen the resilience of children and adolescent and provide quality comprehensive child protection case management services.
The objective is ensure that girls and boys rights to protection from violence, abuse and exploitation are sustained and promoted in humanitarian situations. Vulnerable children and those engaged in risky coping mechanisms are reached with child and social protection support to keep them safe.
The priority intervention is support the availability and accessibility of quality comprehensive and vulnerability sensitive child protection services for all children with special needs including separated children, children with disabilities, children in contact with the law children with HIV and child survivors of violence. This is building on the National Case Management Systems which is operational in all districts. There is an existing community workforce of 10,500 Community Childcare Workers (CCWs) who have limited understanding of CPiE whose capacity requires to be strengthened.
The expected output from this support is improved coordination of the child protection sub-sector response, strengthened capacity of child protection actors who are responsive to the needs of children and adolescents. The following target group will benefit children without appropriate care including separated and unaccompanied children, children at risk of violence, children at risk of exposure to child labour, pregnant adolescents and young mothers in need of psycho social support.
