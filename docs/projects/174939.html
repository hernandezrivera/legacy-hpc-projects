Project Summary

This Project derives from the Cooperation Programme between UNICEF and the Ministry of Basic Education. It aims to improve the learning environment in 22 schools (70 classrooms) from 03 subdivisions by acting on two levels. Firstly, the project intends to alleviate the overcrowdness of classrooms and provide additional infrastructures for the enrollment of out-of-school children. Secondly, the project will improve access and utilization of quality wash in school infrastructures by constructions of 86 latrines and 22 water points.

1 - Besoins / Needs

About 440,000 school-age children (47% of girls) need assistance in the education sector in the Far North region, particularly in Logone-et-Chari, Mayo-Sava, and Mayo-Tsanaga. Due to the displacement of families, the host schools integrate additional displaced students, which constitute a burden for already week education services. According to the 2018 report of ministry of basic education, the student/teacher ratio in the Far-north region is 127 children per teacher and the ratio classrooms/children is 1/118. Considering Cameroon's standard of 60 learners per class, we note in this situation that 49.1% of learners do not have a classroom that conforms to the national standard. Besides, almost all schools have latrines built but only 57% of them are usable. Without latrines, learners practice open defecation with all the attendant safety and hygiene risks. In addition, the lack of safe drinking water in schools disrupts learning when learners must leave school to fetch water outside the school as well as when teachers send them to the village to fetch water.Furthermore, as a result of the conflict, 67 schools remained closed and 50 schools were destroyed, depriving an estimated 35,307 children of their right to education. In order to allow these children currently deprived of their education to have access to learning activities, to contribute to the disgorgement of the currently overcrowded classrooms, to facilitate access to water and latrines, the construction of school structures is essential.
