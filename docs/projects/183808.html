Project Summary

It is worth mentioning that Sudan country in a political transitioning period that is applying different economical political reforms. While the country in is also facing multiple crisis such as political, food insecurity, economic crisis, conflicts, COVID-19 pandemic and floods. Such situation is hard for a transitioning country to overcome easily without suffering of people particularly children, women, elderly and people with special needs as main groups affected by such situation. To contribute to improving this situation, this project is designed to address the needs of the vulnerable people focusing life saving and economic recovery assistance through Cash and Vouchers while considering durable and sustainable livelihoods and food security actions applying nexus programming.
This project directly targets 115,000 vulnerable people (32257 adult females,30992 adult males, 23460 girls, 22540 boys, 2933 elderly females and 2817 elderly males) including children, people with disabilities, women headed households, IDPs, Returnees, adolescents and youth girls in South Kordofan, North Kordofan, White Nile, North Darfur, Kassala and Khartoum states including peace building strengthening where necessary. the target localities selected based on the IPC findings and sector prioritization. The organization endeavours will continue to mobilize resources and increase the target where possible and reach as many people in need as possible.
As Plan International has functioning offices in the target localities and states as well as ongoing projects that maintain the integration of our response and maintain the quality of the implementation of this project.

1 - Needs

Globally, food insecurity is on the rise and becoming a life-threatening concern as stated in the state of food security and nutrition report (2021) more than 928 million people worldwide face hunger. This makes it more challenging to meet the commitment towards achieving the Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) for Zero hunger and ending all forms of malnutrition. The report also stated that currently about 40 million people in 43 countries, including Sudan, are on the brink of starvation, which further undermines any gains made in eradicating hunger. Additionally, the COVID-19 pandemic combined with key food insecurity drivers such as conflict, extreme weather, and economic collapse causes very high food insecurity rates in Sudan. A contributing factor is the economic crisis in the country where the inflation rate has reached 412.75% as of June 2021. This reality adds more pressure to people, particularly the poor communities, who are struggling to meet their basic needs. They are increasingly becoming unable to afford basic food and services, as prices have increased by 400%. Another concerning factor is climate change, especially increasing seasonal floods, which annually damages the economic welfare of vulnerable people as well as infrastructure and services.Based on the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification IPC (2021), 7.3 million people in Sudan (15.7% of the total population) are experiencing crisis or worse levels of food insecurity ( IPC level 3 or above) during the period from (April – May 2021) and about 9.8 million people for projection period (June - September 2021).In Khartoum, 1,670,174 people (19% of the total population in Khartoum) are in phase 3 food crisis and at risk of facing food emergency level (IPC phase 4). In South Kordofan, 505,362 (26%) of population are in (phase 3 food crisis ) and are at risk of facing food emergency level (IPC phase 4). Conflict, particularly inter-communal violence between the Kenana, Kawahla, and Hawazma (Dar Ali) tribes that erupted on 5th June 2021 and across villages in Kalogi, South Kordofan over the issue of land ownership , have also increased food insecurity and driven need. In North Darfur, 712, 069 (29%) of population are in (phase 3 food crisis ) and facing food emergency level (IPC phase 4, food emergency). North Kordofan , Population in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse are observed with (20%). Tensions over this border are likely to continue with increased military presence. Economic crisis in the country where Inflation rate reached 412.75% as per June month which is adding more pressure to the people particularly the poor communities in ND, WN, NK, SK, KHRT, Gadaref and other states who are struggling to meet their basic necessities of life to of survival are prices of the based food and services increased to 4 times. This in addition to the climate changes and seasonal floods that which is increasing the economic fragility of the people as well as infrastructure of the services. In White Nile state has 489,520 representing (18%) in crisis phase 3 and +) while based on OCHA Floods Flash Update (23 September 2021) over 64,000 people from the host community in Guli and Aj Jabalain localities and other 40,000 people including host communities in 53 villages affected by flash floods. All these are contributing factors and drivers for sever food insecurity in the target states and localities.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Plan International Sudan is an active member of National FSL sector, INGOs forum and other forums coordinated by OCHA. the organization is leading the coordination of states emergency forums/committees in White Nile and North Kordofan states that are responding to people affected by crisis. In addition, Plan has a long experience in applying and establishing Community Feedback and Response Mechanisms which considers one of the accountability strengthening measure. Community Feedback & response Mechanism (CFRM) Plan International Sudan will ensure that target communities are fully oriented and consulted on establishing the CFRM; how they want to raise feedback and complaints, to interact with the organization, and to be acquainted with the purpose of this system. CFRM will ensure multiple layers or channels are available for affected populations to report, share their feedback, suggestions for improvement and report for any type of violations or abuse of resources. This will include Focus Group Discussions, high visibility of Plan staff e.g. community meetings, sharing of appropriate contact details with community members (hotlines and national hotline for PSEA), billboards and suggestion boxes. During activities banners/signposts with PIS details will be visible, especially during distributions. This will help the organization to avoid risks of serious harm of the organizations’ image and profile as well sustaining good relationship with the people it is trying to serve and impacting their lives. Plan International’s Global Policy on Safeguarding Children and Young People puts procedures in place to prevent and deal with the actions/behavior of our staff, associates, visitors or us as an organization that result in violence against a child or young person and/or places them at risk of the same. Plan staff are obligated to ensure children and young people we work with are aware of our responsibilities to prevent and respond to any harm against them arising from actions and behaviors of our staff, associates and visitors, and the routes for reporting such incidents. In addition to Plan’s global safeguarding policies, Plan International Sudan has committed to the Joint Framework for Action on PSEA under leadership OCHA and in partnership with other humanitarian actors.
