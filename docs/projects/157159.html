Project Summary

This project aims to contribute to the promotion of the protection and empowerment of women and girls in the North East of Syria (NES), through working with different local partners in the region of NES. The project aims, on the one side, to enhancing partners’ capacity to deliver quality GBV case management and mitigate the consequences of GBV; ensuring GBV survivors and others at risk, particularly women and girls, have access to holistic and specialized services. Furthermore, increase public awareness on GBV and GBV life-saving services as well as link up GBV survivors and others at risk with reliable and confidential service provision.
Target group
The partners will provide access to survivors of GBV, those at risk of being subjected to GBV and women and girls at large. Women and girls are particularly at risk due to unequal gender power dynamics in Syria and in the North East of Syria. Their vulnerability is increased by factors such as being single heads of households, subjected to increased rates of forced and early marriage, traveling alone and internal displacement, or living in isolation with limited access to services. With the understanding that effective programming relies on addressing the immediate and underlying causes of GBV, the project activities will also include activities that target men and boys to the extent possible.
Goal
The project strives to contribute to the overall goal of responding to and preventing of GBV in North East Syria, focusing currently on the areas of Ras Al Ayn, Qamishli, Mabadah and Al Hassakah cities. While also enhancing women and girl protection and empowerment, public awareness and integration of services cross-sector.
Building on the achievements acquired and trust built with our partners, the UPP will work over the next year to continue providing women and girls with essential, lifesaving services. While service provision for survivors of GBV and other individuals at risk will continue in UPP’s partner women house centers; currently supporting four women’s centers, in 2019 the focus of the project will shift toward expanding its reach to the communities through use of mass media channels and opening live on-air radio discussions around GBV with the community; reaching household members and listeners who are at potential risk of GBV and ensuring that those who have limited or no access to the partners centers, receive support and assistance. This will be handled very sensitively and concerns with the community will be transmitted through private phone call, i.e, off air. Every intervention will follow the ‘do no harm’ humanitarian principal, and will be survivor-centered to ensure women’s and girls’ safety, security, privacy and dignity. Efforts will further ensure that GBV survivors and other women and girls at risk are successfully integrated into society, are active members of their community, and take part in services and activities. To promote project sustainability, efforts, as in previous months, will also be devoted to building the capacity of local partners in becoming active, positive agents in protecting and empowering women and girls in their communities.
The continuation of a targeted, evolving mass information campaign will also be crucial to ensure that internally displaced persons and others, particularly women and girls at risk, know where to go and how to access response services that meet their needs. The UPP is uniquely placed in these four areas as a leading voice in advocacy and capacity building of partners in GBV programming, and plans to continue collaboration with these partners and other social protection partners to consolidate work on key issues for the NES context, most notably ensuring that findings and recommendations and GBV concerns in general will be mainstreamed across partners, as all sectoral partners must ensure the safety and well-being of women and girls.

Needs

Syria is facing a multi-faceted humanitarian crisis, resulting from seven years of conflict on multiple fronts that have taken a huge toll on the population. The consequent waves of displaced populations affect the entire country. The draft of the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2019 reports 13.2 million people in need in Syria. Gender-based violence (GBV), including verbal and sexual harassment and early and forced marriage, remains a huge challenge Syrian society has to face. According to HNO 2019, 11% of adolescent girls consider the existence of early marriage as a “common issue”, and 33% of the same age/gender group as an issue that happens “sometimes”. Now approaching its eighth year, the civil war has resulted in a protracted severe humanitarian crisis where GBV continues to be widespread. The length of the crisis is normalizing this kind of violence resulting in a deterioration of women’s rights. The recent review of the assessment findings of the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2019, for the Whole of Syria, claims that the forms of GBV continue to be pervasive in terms of GBV type and impact. Violations include early and forced marriage that can lead to unwanted pregnancy and sexual and reproductive health implications. Other consequences include girls forced to drop out of school and face additional restrictions of freedom and movement. This leads to isolation from social inclusion and mental illness. Due to stigma and shame, GBV is underreported. Through focus group discussions UPP conducted with different women of the community, some of the negative coping mechanisms were reported to include: girls running away from their homes, girls and women join armed groups, restriction of movement by members of the household, domestic violence, and survival sex. The risk women and girls face related to exploitation and violence are heightened even more by the diminishment of protection options, further increasing their need to support. Although women’s rights law in NES strictly outlaws polygamy, underage marriage and violence against women, threats faced by women and girls are not alleviated. The HNO assessment report highlights the psychosocial impact of the prolonged crisis in Syria and its relation to increase of GBV concerns faced by women and children. This takes into consideration also the socio-economic situation and increase of poverty, that causes higher risks for women in terms of all forms of violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse.

Implementation of activities

In alignment with its capacity building activities and information outreach dissemination strategies, UPP has been able to form a gradual relationship with the vulnerable community through the Houses of Women (in Kurdish Mala Jin; in Arabic Beit Marraat) it supports. With over seven years’ experience in supporting women’s issues, the House of Women supports who suffer from gender based-violence and marginalization in the society, creating a solid reputation among women and girls. Men alongside women, have freedom of access to this establishment which also provides legal and psychosocial support to issues concerning both women and men. Against this background, most cases of GBV are self-referred to the House of Women. To increase their efficacy in GBV case management and ensuring a survivor centered approach is maintained, UPP continues to align the work of the House of Women with international guiding standards on GBV case management, a main area of service delivered at the house of women. Since April 2018, UPP has enabled three safe spaces and trained nine staff from the Houses of Women and KRC to provide comprehensive, confidential, and survivor-centered GBV case management. Since then, 100% of women and girls seeking assistance received shelter and treatment (75 cases between June and August). An increasing number of protection cases has been identified, including, only in June 2018, two rape cases and three cases at risk of honor killing who received protection services through local protection partners working with the Houses of Women. In this respect, GBV case data inform there is a concern that cases of rape are as prevalent as are honor killings. Against this background, UPP is now engaging in the creation of a solid linkage between its Health and GBV projects to provide specialized and immediate life-saving assistance to survivors of rape. In doing so, staff capacity will be built through specialized training such as Clinical Management of Rape (CMR). Moreover, since January 2018 UPP has held daily RH awareness sessions and support groups in 3 KRC clinics, one hospital, and in municipalities (Komins), empowering more than 500 women and girls with knowledge and information on various relevant topics as: use of contraceptives, family planning, maternal and child health. As part of its community awareness program, UPP engaged in mass information dissemination activities to ensure that women and girls and GBV survivors are aware of protection services and are encouraged to access them. In 2019, UPP will continue to support these services and to enhance the capacity of service providers with trainings and mentoring. Moreover, another House of Women will be supported in Hassake City. The structure has strong links with the houses of women in Al Hol camp and Shaddadah and, thanks to constant information campaigns and word of mouth, it receives also cases from Areesheh camp. This will gradually give UPP a reliable channel to strengthen women institutions across the region and expand services and awareness in very complex areas where no sound GBV services are established. To promote project sustainability, additional efforts will also be devoted to building the capacity of 28 staff coming from 3 different partner organizations. Social workers, enumerators, PSS councillors and outreach volunteers will become active and positive agents in protecting and empowering women and girls in the community. Activities will expand from the provision of GBV case management, psychological support and RH counselling to the creation of women group/circles, the promotion of recreational activities, activities specifically aimed at targeting youth and adolescent girls, and community based dialogue sessions with women, men, boys, and girls.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The assumption behind the Action is that a stable situation allows the Women Houses to be operative 7 days a week. The system has been built based on this primary assumption and proved to be effective in ensuring GBV Services in Hassake Governorate. However, operational capacity of centers and outreach teams depend not only on financial support, but also on the general geopolitical situation. The main risks are the following:- Escalation of violence between SDF and GoT and new displacement waves (high risk);- Start of violence between GoS and SDF (medium risk);- Withdrawal of US/Coalition Forces may lead to destabilization in NES (high risk);- IS is not defeated and takes back some areas or infiltrates carrying out attacks (medium risk);- Use of chemical weapons (medium risk);- Lack of access and difficulties to provide services and supplies; difficulty to monitor the activities in case of remote management (medium risk);- Cross-border INGOs relocate staff further west and therefore are more limited in their capacity to respond while active hostilities are ongoing (medium risk);- Project staff become a target in the conflict (medium risk); Mitigation strategy consists in building the capacity of the staff with intensive training and mentoring so that in case of evacuation and interruption of funds knowledge and best practices inform the response of local organizations. In case of a serious deterioration of the situation in NES and of complete lack of accessibility for international staff, depending on the context, UPP will choose between a mixed strategy of direct and remote management, maintaining its field office with national/local staff under the supervision of senior management responsible for the Action from Dohuk office in KRI. In case UPP is obliged to close its Syria mission, the Action will be remotely managed by UPP from Dohuk office in KRI, and the activities will be carried out by the implementing partner.
