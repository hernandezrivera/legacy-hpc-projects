Project Summary

BRAC will design projects for the JRP 2023 aiming to enhance the quality and ensure equitable access to WASH services with establishment of susceptible, cost-efficient and sustainable WASH services targeting the gaps of ten camps (1E, 1W, 2E, 2W, 3, 10, 14, 20Ext, 21, 25). BRAC project design includes integration with relevant sectors and special focus on protection, skill development in both the refugee and host communities and entrepreneurship development in the host community. BRAC will also focus community engagement and building ownership for ensuring regular repair and maintenance of WASH facilities by strengthening capacity of the WASH management committee and user groups where women leadership will be ensured. The sector will consider comprehensive and inclusive approaches to tailor the needs for ensuring WASH services through sex-disaggregated data, person with disability data, LGBTQ+ and with key steps in the implementing processes. BRAC will continue to prioritize water quality at household level and at water points. This will include periodical water quality testing, along with operation and maintenance of water sources, treatment, and equitable distribution of water to the target population. BRAC has piloted RWHS in camp and gets very positive learning from the pilot. To reduce the tension of groundwater depletion, BRAC will also install rainwater harvesting systems at both communities based on previous learning, use of surface water treatment and take initiative for ground water recharge. In addition to regular repair and maintenance of WASH facilities BRAC will also upgrade and construct new WASH facilities as per needs focusing the reallocation of Rohingya and in the gap areas in coordination with CIC, RRRC and DPHE offices and address the needs of the special vulnerable groups such as women, children & adolescents, elderly people, person with disability and LGBTQ+. BRAC will allocate 74% and 26% of the proposed funds for refugees and host communities respectively. BRAC will work for the enterprise development and linkages on plastic waste management and environment friendly reusable sanitary napkin production to make a sustainable supply chain of WASH products for both the refugee and host communities. BRAC will be strengthening collaboration and coordination with donor agencies and WASH sector to fill the WASH gaps where needed. BRAC will also be working with the local organization on WASH and will work for the capacity building of the local WASH organization under the pool funding. BRAC has project implementation quality (PIQ) indicators based on which project monitoring plan will be developed in alignment with the JRP monitoring framework and will be monitored periodically by the BRAC MEAL team for the improvement and necessary adjustment of the WASH programme. BRAC will continue with emphasis on preventing high-risk behaviour and thus enabling safe water use, healthy hygiene practice and solid waste containment through the integration with health, nutrition, and education and protection sectors. BRAC will have a contingency plan to address AWD outbreak, Covid-19 aftermath, Scabies, dengue prevention, special incident & monsoon rain, cyclone & and any other natural disaster in host and Rohingya community. BRAC will also develop different knowledge products and conduct research on FSM in collaboration with international University, engage the private sector in FSM & advocacy on WASH. WASH sector strategies and the guidelines will be followed in all stages of the implementation without any overlapping considering CHS standards and AAP principle. BRAC has a very rich and historical background in managing WASH programs in different approaches such as enterprise development, micro-financing and private sector engagement and those knowledge and learnings will be capitalized for the improvement of the BRAC WASH response as and where needed.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Due to limited funding coverage, the needs for 2022 identified through the J-MSNA haven't been fully met. Therefore, integrated service model will be the key approach of BRAC in next year to effectively use and equitable distribution of resources among the targeted camps and in host communities. Although most of the camps are covered with piped water networks, still people need access to the DTWs for bathing, maintaining personal hygiene, washing clothes and cleaning WASH facilities which require provision of regular repair and maintenance. The indicator “having enough water to meet all HHs need” significantly increased to 90 % in most of the camps. However; the needs are still required in some camps where more than 25% (wash sector monitoring report October, 2022) HHs doesn’t meet enough water for all purposes. Resources for the provision of new water supply will be required as a significant number of DTWs has been decommissioned and will become inactive due to groundwater depletion. J-MSNA report says, one third of the HHs in host communities don't have enough water for drinking, cooking or personal hygiene which haven’t changed adequately in 2022. Therefore provision of water supply in host communities is still a critical need for 2023. According to the Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (J-MSNA) 2021 report, there was 19% gap of latrine facilities in camps which most of them are addressed to fill the gaps. However, there is a possibility to further up gradation and modification of these latrines to make accessible for vulnerable groups especially for women, children and PWDs. Commonly reported problems are non-functionality, lack of cleanliness, lack of lighting inside latrine, long queues and overcrowding etc. which were not changed significantly in 2022 and need regular repair and maintenance with up-gradation as per the feedback received from BRAC CFRM. On the other hand, 27% of the host community households (J-MSNA, 2021) need more latrines that need continuous support to reach the gap. According to J-MSNA report, almost one in five households reported using an unimproved sanitation facility in the host community. However the proportions of households reportedly having used an unimproved sanitation facility appeared to be lower in camp vicinity. The most commonly reported problems related to latrines are lack of light inside latrines, latrines being unclean and latrines not functioning where the support is still critically needed. Moreover, regular repair and maintenance of FSTP with regular desludging of latrine cubicles are the key priority needs in camps for 2023. The finding in this LQAS has shown that some gaps identified in the 2018 LQAS have not changed, where solid waste management indicators have shown the highest gap and are a priority for intervention. According to J-MSNA report 73% of HHs are reportedly covered by SWM systems in the camp areas which were not fully covered in 2022 and hence solid waste management including plastic waste management services has become an emerging need for 2023. Hygiene practices among the Rohingya communities are still a major concern. To strengthen personal hygiene practices among the Rohingya community, regular HP sessions with different groups should be continued. To maintain proper hygiene practices, regular supply of hygiene items in camps including distribution of reusable sanitary napkins will still be a key priority for 2023. Due to geographical situation, camps are likely to be exposed to many natural and human made disasters. Therefore, WASH infrastructure should be resilient enough and BRAC requires a contingency plan to deal with any kind of natural disasters and incidental event.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Anticipating inadequate funding opportunities in 2023, BRAC will design the projects with the provision of integration among different sectors such as health, protection and livelihood where integrated service delivery models have been adopted in order to provide better WASH services among the Rohingya and host community. The key area of focus of BRAC project will be the skill development, entrepreneurship development, WASH innovation, delivery quality services by partnering with local NGOs and private sector with emphasis on inclusiveness and cost effectiveness during the design of the project. BRAC will also continue with the role of WASH sector co-lead and will strengthen WASH sector coordination and building strong relation with RRRC office, CIC offices, DPHE offices and other relevant government offices

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

To mainstream protection, BRAC will address four protection principles including safety, dignity and do not harm; meaningful access; accountability; & participation and empowerment. Furthermore, to integrate protection this project will also address protection in three critical phases at prevention, response and risk mitigation. To ensure project participant’s safety and dignity through Do No Harm approach, BRAC will install separate WASH facilities by consulting diverse community groups. Additionally to ensure participation and empowerment of disadvantaged community people, BRAC will run skill development training for the production of reusable sanitary napkins and create WASH friendly WFS to reduce the impact of physical and psychological harm that is raised from violence.

3.2 - Gender

BRAC will design its activities to address the distinct needs of women and girls such as distribution of reusable sanitary napkins and construction of women hygiene centers etc. However, one half of women representation and women leadership role in both camp WASH management committee and user group committee will be ensured so that they can raise their voice for their needs.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

BRAC will ensure inclusive design of WASH facilities so that elderly people will have access to all WASH services. WASH staff members and volunteers will be sensitized regarding BRAC safeguarding and PSEA policy. No child labors will be allowed during the implementation of WASH activities. BRAC designed the project and included the activities for assessing the CFS to reflect child protection concerns in terms of upgrading WASH facilities at CFS such as latrines, hand washing facilities and drainage systems. To archive children nutritional outcomes integrated WASH, health and nutrition messages will be developed and delivered.

3.4 - Diversity

BRAC will design the projects to meet the needs for both host and Rohingya community participants for all group of peoples regardless of gender, sex, race/ethnicity, religious and color, who are vulnerable. Additionally, BRAC will specially focus on the WASH needs of women, girls, PWDs and LGBTQ+. BRAC is a multicultural organization where more than two hundred staff members are working regardless of gender, sex, race/ethnicity, religious and color in WASH sector.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

BRAC will involve the community to identify the needs. Community both guest and host will be involved during implantation of the project. BRAC will effectively manage the CFRM approach to get community feedback on WASH through which community feedback will be accumulated and response. BRAC included PDM activity in the project to know community feedback regarding the WASH NFI distribution. PDM report will help us re-design WASH NFI kits and effectiveness of NFI distribution through which we will be able to make ourselves accountable to our participants.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

BRAC will include activities to contribute to DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures are: i. Installation of rainwater harvesting system ii. Ground water recharge iii. Water trucking iv. Procurement of portable water treatment units v. plastic recycling plant. BRAC has an Environmental and Social Safeguard Framework (ESSF), which applies a science-based precautionary approach to project design and implementation. BRAC’s all interventions anticipate and address any potential harm before it happens. Where there are threats of serious or irreversible damage, circumstances of not having scientific certainty will not be used as a reason for avoiding cost-effective measures to prevent environmental degradation in BRAC’s project implementation mechanism. BRAC is conducting an environmental assessment to evaluate the environmental condition around the primary healthcare centres and ensure that the Environmental Action Plan is followed properly.
