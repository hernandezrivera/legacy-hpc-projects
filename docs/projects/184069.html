Project Summary

This project aims to continue the delivery of CMAM services for SAM and MAM cases within the 19 existing health system in KRT (2 center in Umbada, 1 center in Shurg Elneel and 2 center in Jabal Awlia localities),SK (2 center in Dilling,3 centers in Liri localities) /WKordofan (2centers in Lagawa), ED (3 centers in Abu Jabra , 2 centers in B.Elarab, 1 center Eldeein and 1 centar in Alfardous localities) States through the provision of full package of treatment and prevention of acute malnutrition among (boys&girls) U5 and PLW.The project targets acutely malnourished children (SAM with or without complication and MAM) either through treatment or rehabilitating services. Improve the capacity of MOH staff to provide quality services and community volunteers ,CBOs to mitigate the risk towards food insecurity and reduction of malnutrition, the project enhancing the need analysis of thenutrition situation through conducting rapid nutrition surveys involving the target communities in identifying the needs,planning and in implementation as well as monitoring of project on regular basis. Moderate Acute malnutrition and the underlying causes of malnutrition will be managed through participatory nutrition education activities using the NIPPapproaches through targeting predominantly mothers and fathers of moderately malnourished children. The NIPP circles emphasize behavior change, communication & counseling (for improved awareness), livelihood (for improved food/nutritionsecurity)andcookingdemonstrationusinglocallyavailable foods (for improved feeding and care practices).Community screening of cases will equally target boys and girls & depending on their nutritional status they will be referred to the OTP or NIPP circles based on the admission criteria. Mother to mother groups where a group of mothers with malnourished children, grandparents group) to help empowering Mothers to solve issues related to the nutrition with focus on IYCF practices and knowledge and coordination with relevant sector (for multi-pectoral nutrition activities). Mainstreaming child protection through reflecting in the assessment,design, monitoring and evaluation of the project. Girls and boys of all ages and their caregivers, especially pregnant and breastfeeding women and girls, have access to awareness about women and child rights for the prevention of malnutrition Gender, COVID19, HIV/AIDS and the environment are mainstreamed throughout the project activities.The ultimate goal of the project “save the lives” of acute malnourished children U5 in Refugees, IDP and host communities. In addition the project is seeking to provide the medical treatment and referral for the vulnerable populations(according to HNO 2022 and AMVO previous experience) within the 19 existing health system in KRT (2 center in Umbada, 1 center in Shurg Elneel and 2 center in Jabal Awlia localities), SK (2 center in Dilling,3 centers in Liri localities) /WKordofan (2centers in Lagawa), ED (3 centers in Abu Jabra , 2 centers in B.Elarab, 1 center Eldeein and 1 centar in Alfardous localities) States,through targeting the health system in the stated localities in coordination with the Health sector and MoH&other service providers to fill-in the existing gab through ensuring the implementation of SO1(Increase equitable access to humanitarian lifesaving and life-sustaining basic health services for those residing in high risk areas and those most vulnerable and in need)&SO2 (Strengthen health sector capacity to prepare and deliver timely response to affected population by conflict, natural emergencies, and disease outbreaks).This will be achieved through construction/rehabilitation of existing health facilities(19 OTPs and PHC”),well trained staff, purchase of medical equipment and referral of cases to the health facilities in the areas, as well as taking necessity precautions health procedures to ensure safety of staff, volunteers and community from the most communicable diseases such as COVID19.

1 - Needs

Acute and chronic malnutrition are problems of public health significance in Sudan for the past several decades. The drivers of malnutrition are multi-sectoral that include high income poverty, poor WASH conditions leading to epidemics, limited access to health services, illiteracy and high food prices contributing to inadequate food intake and dietary diversity at household level. Inappropriate feeding practices and cultural norms affect children’s growth and development from an early age. Displacement either due to floods or inter-communal clashes continue to be one of the main drivers of malnutrition as it disrupts affected communities’, access to health and wash services and therefore increasing risks to disease outbreaks and food insecurity in limited access to farming land, availability, and utilizations of food. COVID-19 continues to contribute to loss of livelihoods, disrupting access to food and the health care system. The ongoing currency inflation has reduced purchasing power implying increased families with food insecurity and therefore malnutrition risks. Moreover, Humanitarian crisis tend to increase the nutrition related protections risks especially among young children that might engage in child labor, early child marriage that increase risks of being malnourished. About 59%, acutely malnourish under-five children and pregnant and lactating women live in 106 areas classified as extreme and catastrophic by the health sector, thereby increasing the risk of morbidity and mortality associated with lack or poor health services. The overall nutrition sector people in need (PIN) has slightly increase by 7 percent from 3.6 million in 2021 to 3.9 million in 2022 with under-five children accounting for 77% and 23% pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) in need of life-saving treatment and preventive responses. To mitigate and prevent morbidity and mortality associated with malnutrition, AMVO according to nutrition sector HRP will focus on the immediate life-saving nutrition needs that include: Out-patient treatment and in-patient treatment of severe acute malnutrition, treatment of moderate acute malnourished under-fives and global acute malnutrition among PLWs) in prioritized localities. Preventive nutrition needs are Infant and Young Child Feeding practices, capacity building of staff strengthening. Multi-sectoral nutrition sensitive interventions (Health, protection). These needs have significant impact on physical, mental and wellbeing of children, pregnant and lactating women, and community in general. With respect to refugees, approximately 137,191 under-fives, pregnant women are in urgent need of nutrition interventions all over Sudan of which 92% are children under-five. For refugees living in camps in East Sudan, the latest SENS survey (2019) indicated a GAM rate of 13 per cent in the Shagarab camps, and over 11 per cent for all other camps. Stunting prevalence is critical in Shagarab camps at 53 per cent, as well as for Abuda, Um Gargour and Fau 5 at 45 per cent. The SENS survey also indicates high rates of anaemia (>40 per cent) among children under five years and women aged 15-49 years in refugees. (Source HNO 2022)

2 - Activities/Outputs

This project aims to continue the delivery of CMAM services for SAM and MAM cases in the 19 proposed areas in Khartoum (2 center in Umbada, 1 center in Shurg Elneel and 2 center in Jabal Awlia localities), SKordofan (2 center in Dilling,3 centers in Liri localities) /WKordofan (2centers in Lagawa), EDarfur (3 centers in Abu Jabra , 2 centers in B.Elarab, 1 center Eldeein and 1 centar in Alfardous localities) States through: Nutrition:- Screening for the malnourished U5 children.- Admission and treatment of SAM/MAM U5 children to the OTPs- Provision of IYCF component.- Provision of NIPP services to the targeted U5 MAM children in the OTPs.- Capacity building for OTP technical staff and community volunteers in the sites.Health: - Construction/rehabilitation for the facilities.- Medical treatment for the vulnerable through fixed clinics and Mobile.- Ensure referral system to the hospitals is strengthened.- Provision of drugs/labs consumable materials.- Capacity building training for the technical health staff.Also AMVO will improve site level services providers’ knowledge and awareness on AAP, GBV and disability through capacity building and referral pathways that will be organized in collaboration with the respective sectors

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Environmental mainstreaming will be integrated through the entire project management cycle. Environmental mainstreaming will be integrated in the activities to ensure that environmental harm is reduced or eliminated and environmental benefits are maximized. Key environmental concerns will be built into the project in order to reduce deforestation in Sudan. Almanar will attempt to switch away from fired bricks to alternative construction methods such as stabilized soil blocks (SSB) or concrete blocks (CSB) depending on the prerequisites of the region. Environmental mainstreaming will be included during monitoring and evaluation of the project outcomes of the response to ensure that the effects sought was indeed delivered, and to help identify possible negative environmental effects in time in order to adjust activities accordingly. It is a necessary part of ensuring that the principle of do no harm is fulfilled. Furthermore, to help identify best practices and lessons learnt that offer valuable insight for making improvements in future aid efforts. Almanar will work with local NGOs, CSOs and communities including working with the national and state governmental authorities, since Community consultation and participation is a key part of environmental management.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Almanar through coordination with the local government authorities and humanitarian partners will incorporate protection principles with DO NO HARM and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity in the implementation of this project by avoiding causing harm preventing and minimizing as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the project activities which can increase people’s vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks.Protection mainstreaming is one of the most significant factors, which is increasingly recognized as an integral component of UN programming, is the gender equality and protection of girls and women. Inequalities between the sexes typically reflect, and can increase, vulnerabilities of pregnant and lactating women and girls, may face different protection risks, have different needs and choices, and possess different skills, knowledge and coping strategies. The project activities, all throughout the execution, will aim to ensure community participation through the project cycle, capacity and vulnerability analysis, reduce the level of exploitation to women and promote protection of girls in communities and families with the support of legal and traditional protection systems. The project will use and advocate for Minimum Standards of Protection package amongst the targeted communities all through the project implementation. This project through the referral pathway between the two targeted sectors will ensure that women, girls and boys victims of S/GBV, elderly, disables and their families and guardians are fully aware of the project interventions in 4 targeted States.Almanar will ensure the served locations are safe and accessible to all without discrimination, and ensure that the beneficiaries receiving services in dignity and confidentiality.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

In the all stages of the project the communities are kept engaged and involved through community dialogue meetings, project dissemination. Accountability will be ensured across the activities in this project through establishment of complaints and response mechanisms at project level aimed at ensuring that the voices of the affected populations will be heard. The established nutrition and health community committees and community leaders networks including volunteers will facilitate community engagement at all levels of the project including planning, management, monitoring and evaluation.These committees are expected to be gender and ability sensitive with the inclusion of both males, females, elderly and people with disabilities to ensure that all voices, including those of the marginalized are heard, equality and equity are realized with regards to access to services that will be provided by the project. In addition, AMVO will ensure that all staff/volunteers are trained on PSEA policies and procedures which are in line with UN's protocols. The Complaints handling mechanisms will be established in the targeted area and fully inform the community about the complaint mechanisms, their purposes and use, and what constitutes a complaint to allow people confidentially report their concerns and with developing monthly tracker to ensure that communities complaints/feedback are comprehensively analyzed and responded to with the beneficiaries getting feedback on a monthly basis. Project staff will ensure adequate consultation with the communities to ensure that existing complaints handling mechanisms are incorporated into the complaints and response mechanisms to be established by the project. I.e.(complain box, a complaint committee or direct complaints to project staff members trained to act as “focal points,” receiving complaints sensitively and without judgment, and ensuring the complainant’s safety.
