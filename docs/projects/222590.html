1 - Besoins, Activités et Resultats Attendus

Logistics services are essential to facilitate the humanitarian response in Chad through transport of actors and humanitarian assistance. United Nations Humanitarian Air Service (UNHAS) provides this service to organizations involved in the humanitarian response in Chad. In Chad, road infrastructures are inadequate and often in poor conditions, and long distances between the capital and the areas of intervention combined with insecurity makes access to many humanitarian intervention sites difficult. Although Royal Airways, the national Airline is in place since November 2023 and still not yet go through safety and aviation security certification, UNHAS remains the only air service available for humanitarian community. Hence, the continuation of the humanitarian air service remains high as regular and safe access to implementation sites is a challenge for most humanitarian organizations operating remotely. In addition, many of these areas, particularly in the east of the country, become completely inaccessible by road during the rainy season (from July to October) due to floods and the inaccessibility of many fields roads. In these conditions, air transport is the safest and most reliable way for humanitarian workers to travel safely from N'Djamena, Abéché, Goz Beida to more than fifteen other localities towards the country to deliver humanitarian assistance. The United Nations Humanitarian Air Service (UNHAS), operated by WFP, is designed to support the immense humanitarian effort in Chad, through the provision of safe, reliable, efficient and cost-effective air transport to the humanitarian community, donors and government counterparts in areas where surface transport is limited or unavailable. In addition to providing scheduled passenger and light cargo transportation services, UNHAS facilitates critical lifesaving medical and security evacuations, upon user requests along with undertaking rehabilitation of airstrips to provide cost efficient and direct access to deep-field implementation sites. UNHAS also facilitates high-level visits that are critical to supporting the humanitarian community in its advocacy efforts. The weekly flight schedule contains 19 destinations on a regular basis. In 2024, the use of the service split by organizations stood at 54 percent for NGOs, 42 percent for UN Agencies and 4 percent for Government counterparts, Diplomatic Corps and Donor community. In 2025, UNHAS plans to operate with a fleet of one medium size inter-regional aircraft Emb145 (Jet) used for long distance destinations, two medium size De Haviland Canada Dash-8 (37 seats) short take of and landing aircrafts and one small size aircraft Caravan C208B (12 seats) that serve the deep field locations with rough airstrips. A 'hubs and spokes' network structure was established whereby the medium size Jet performs longer range connections between hubs and provide loads to the smaller aircraft shuttling to smaller, challenging airfields. Two aircraft are strategically based in the East of the country bordering Sudan - in Abeche and Goz-Beida, two other aircraft are based in N'Djamena, providing connections between the West and the East of the country, the Southern region bordering Central African Republic and the Western region, i.e. the Lake Chad area, at the border with Nigeria, Niger and Cameroon. UNHAS expects to transport a total of 30,000 passengers in 2025, subject to user demands. In 2024, the service transported a monthly average of 2700 passengers and 8.7 MT of essential light cargo accessing 19 regular destinations. It also carried out 140 life-saving medical evacuations and security evacuation of humanitarian actors. UNHAS will continue to provide critical access and increasing frequencies in Farchana and Adré due to the scale-up of humanitarian demands in the east following the eruption of the Sudanese crisis in April 2023. For 2025, UNHAS will continue to rely on security networks to obtain information to mitigate threats to the security of the operation. UNHAS Chad will continue to provide air services to NGOs, UN agencies, donor representatives, the diplomatic community and implementing humanitarian partners, with the necessary access to rapidly reach remote or inaccessible beneficiaries. UNHAS considered all the main risks associated with the project and presented mitigation measures for each possible risk. In addition, building the capacity of international and national staff, NGOs and key authorities is a major component of UNHAS's footprint at the local level. UNHAS will continue to implement activities aimed at advancing the transfer of knowledge from aviation-related core disciplines and transferring skills towards strengthening national authority. UNHAS will continue to seek funds towards improving key airfields whereby rehabilitation works are planned.
