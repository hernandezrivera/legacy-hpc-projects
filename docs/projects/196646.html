Project Summary

Eleven years of conﬂicts were enough to weak the Syrian citizens and create execrable humanitarian situation. The degree of resilience and positive coping mechanism of the affected population have reduced due to the protracted nature of the conﬂict. The economic and social situation in Syria continues to decline and severely affect everybody especially those who have been displaced from or returned to their homes. Statistics reveal that two-thirds of Syrians are living in extreme poverty, unable to meet basic food and non-food needs. The main reasons for poverty are loss of property and jobs, loss of access to public services, including health and clean water, and rising food prices.
According to HNO 2023, Nearly 85 per cent of the overall population reported a deterioration in their ability to meet basic needs, demonstrated through a significant increase in the inaccessibility of NFI items, such as clothing, mattresses, and blankets.
Coupled with the deterioration of shelter integrity, increasing shelte deterioration of shelter integrity, increasing shelter adequacy issues (i.e. limited electricity/lighting and poor insulation from heat/cold) households are exposed to harsh weather conditions with a high risk of loss of life, particularly during winter. Adequate shelter conditions and access to basic NFIs is vital for maintaining good physical health, mental well-being, and to sustain learning and educational opportunities.
It was reported that 5,7 M people are in need for NFIs assistances in Syria, this project will contribute to provide sustainable resilience to those must in need for NFIs.
The purpose of this project is to provide winterization items to affected people living in target areas.
If the commodities in local markets available, the distributions modality in this intervention will be through vouchers for winter items, Otherwise, it will be in-kind aid according to sector standards.
This project will target the following areas through 2023 project:
1- Rural Damascus: Al-Tall, Yabroud, and Sahnaya. these areas are with severity scale 3 and Pin 91377 persons. through this project 2000 H.H will be targeted with winterization items.
2- Dara'a, the severity scale is 3, PiN 54,732, and 2000 H.H will be targeted with winterization items.
3- Rural Idleb: Khan Shakhon, severity scale is 4, PiN 2,500. and 500 H.H will be supported with winterization items.
These activities will carry on from October 2023 till end of January 2023.
This project will target the most venerable families thriugh applying the following selection criteria's:
- Female headed household
- Families with pregnant women.
- Families with disabilities and elder people
- Families did not received any winterization items before.
- Families with large number of household members (more than five)
Also, IDP households who have not received any supports before are prioritized.
It is expected that at least men, women, girls and boys will be supported (assuming 5 family members per household). The amount of each H.H is USD 429.
During the life of this project, GOPA-DERD will coordinate with the community leaders, other humanitarian actors providing assistance in this area, NFIs sector through the sector meetings.
GOPA-DERD will coordinate with other actors working in the field in terms of different projects being implemented , to avoid duplication and to coordinate with other activities being implemented to target the most needed households in addition to implement joint activities in the same targeted location.

3 - Needs

The scale and scope of life-saving needs continue to increase. Lack of access to many affected people in need remains one of the main impediments to addressing needs, which requires increased innovation and flexibility.GOPA has its own Beneficiary Registration Management System (BRMS) that reflects the needs assessment carried out by GOPA-DERD mobile teams in all 13 out of 14 governorates in Syria. With some situational displacement expected to continue, and a potential increase in the rate of return (IDP and refugee), the scale and severity of need for shelter support is projected to remain through 2023, with IDPs and returnees being the worst affected by poor shelter conditions. As such, the sector projects the number of ‘people in need’ to increase in line with the population growth and continued deterioration in economic conditions. Geographically, the number of people requiring shelter support is widespread with some concentration in Aleppo, Idleb, Dara'a, and Rural Damascus.In 2023, the estimated number of people requiring NFI support has increased by 15 per cent to 5.7 million. With funding shortages currently at a historic low, the limited response of the NFI sector in 2022 is further expected to exacerbate needs as 2023 unfolds.This project will provide the Key NFI items such as, winter clothing, thermal blankets, and heaters are essential and accessible but are unaffordable for most individuals.

4 - Implementation of activities

Distribution of winterization assistance to target communities following a set vulnerability criteria GOPA-DERD will be responsible for needs assessments, beneficiary verification data based on selection criteria's mentioned in project summary.GOPA-DERD's team will conduct a market study in each target area, and based on the result the distribution modality will be decided either vouchers, Cash, or in kind assistance.Since this project will target some rural areas where there is no functioning market yet, wherefore using vouchers as a modality is not suitable in this case. but when the market has the capacity to provide the winterization commodities the distribution modality will be as a vouchers.GOPA-DERD follows a participatory approach throughout the project phases (planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation). The opinions and views of the various stakeholders are elicited and taken into consideration regarding the various project components and through various channels: focus group discussions, interviews, meetings, and field visits.For all components of the program, GOPA-DERD will apply its M&E Policy Framework reflecting standardized monitoring processes and criteria that will facilitate ongoing information gathering and reporting throughout the project implementation period.
