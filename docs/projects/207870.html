Project Summary

"In 2024, HEKS/EPER will implement WASH response through gender-responsive, rights-based, adolescent, and inclusive services in Ukhiya and Ramu Upazila's Healthcare facilities. Health is one of the most critical areas of operation that should reflect the exact needs of the people. Uninterrupted access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH), and proper medical waste management and cleaning in healthcare facilities is a key to ensuring quality care and achieving Universal Health Coverage (UHC). HEKS/EPER will also address adequate and equitable access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene in healthcare facilities, and continuously advocates those issues. All activities will ensure the do-no-harm principle by prioritizing the safety and dignity of women and girls. HEKS/EPER will continue its sensitization regarding safety measures before and during natural disasters, AWD/dengue outbreaks, IPC/COVID issues, etc.
To improve the WASH in the targeted healthcare facilities, operations and maintenance and key stakeholders' engagement, community feedback, and response mechanism will be addressed or aligned with other activists, during the project implementation period. HEKS/EPER will continue to operate and maintain the WASH facilities in the HCF (Healthcare facility), and targeted schools of the host community to increase the knowledge and practices on IPC, MHM, and handwashing. To improve the overall situation and increase the quality of healthcare services and ensure a safe healthcare environment, this project aims to enhance infection prevention and control as well as the hygiene situation in health facilities. Through this project HEKS/EPER directly covered 39969 individuals in Ukiya Upazila.
For this purpose, HEKS/EPER:
- Will visit health care facilities (HCF) in host communities and collect information based on the WASH-FIT indicators through observation and interview methods by using the Kobo Platform.
- Will conduct a series of WASH-FIT training programs, as required, in order to build/enhance the capacity of HCF Managers/Doctors/Nurses/WASH focal point. Including installation of handwashing facilities, upgradation of waste management systems, and distribution of hygiene kits.
- Will strengthen the existing monitoring mechanism by incorporating the HCFs and conduct follow-up monitoring visits/ supportive supervisions using at least one monitoring visit of each HCFs.
- Will organize training at the Upazila level on different components, procedures, and the importance of healthcare waste management."

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"Concern over the management medical waste, water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) has become one of the major issues in Cox's Bazar district due to the ongoing spread of infectious diseases among health workers, sanitation workers, waste handlers, and patients. Healthcare facilities still lack the capacity in healthcare facilities to overcome the poor state of WASH and medical waste management, which is considered essential to encourage health and WASH users to re-evaluate and follow the standard rules and practices of quality and safe healthcare. The problem of medical waste disposal and other toxic hazardous waste is growing rapidly throughout the world because of rapid urbanization, population growth, and climate change. Medical waste or clinical waste that poses serious threats to environmental health requires specialized treatment and management before its final disposal. Simply disposing of it in dustbins, drains, and canals or finally dumping it on the outskirts of the community poses a serious public health hazard. It was observed that the capacity of healthcare facilities during the spread of novel corona virus.Cox’s Bazar is one of the vulnerable districts of Bangladesh regarding the WASH in Health and IPC, because it is a tourist area and a huge number of Rohingya FDMN are living in this area. After the secondary level of discussions with the health care authority and ground-level health care providers, both parties agreed and conducted a joint assessment at Ukhiya & Ramu, to see the field-level challenges, gaps/limitations, and way forward strategy. Which represents the other Upazila of Cox's Bazar. The information collected through KoBo tools, observation, KII, and JMP SAS v12.6 was used to make all the quantitative analyses and other associated supports in this assessment. Some significant sources indicate that this could increase the risk of COVID-19 and other transmittable diseases in the host community due to the lack of waste management, inappropriate medical waste segregation, lack of safe water, sanitation facilities, hand washing facilities, and lack of adequate IPC materials. Only 3% of healthcare facilities have access to the waste management and treatment process. Only 21% of facilities have a reliable source of drinking water for staff, patients, and visitors. 68% of the facilities do not have adequate numbers of useable latrines for the staff, patients, and visitors. 97% of infectious waste is not stored in protected areas before treatment. 55% of the facilities have hand washing stations within their 5m periphery areas."

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project contributes to reaching the government WASH in Healthcare facilities standards and the SDG-Sustainable Development Goals (Goal 3: Good health and well-being, Goal 4: Quality education, Goal 5: Gender equality, SDG 6: ensuring availability and sustainable management of water and sanitation; and Goal 10: Reduced inequalities). The main objective of the project is to increase access to quality of WASH in healthcare facilities, including medical waste management, and strengthen the inclusiveness of facilities and community participation, and empowerment. The project activities are cohesive with Child Protection, Health, and WASH. In our working areas targeted beneficiaries will get all mentioned component's services from the project, and as well as outreach team will enhance the community people’s knowledge on all components then beneficiaries/stakeholders’ meaningful participation, and empowerment will increase. As a result, the health and livelihood of the host community increase. During the project planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation, HEKS/EPER follows the humanitarian core standards and PSEA policy and procedures. The project follows the “do no harm” and “reach as many people as possible” approaches.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

"This project is an integrated project, the main components are Health, WASH, and Protection, all targeted host communities as well as Rohingya beneficiaries will be getting better services from the mentioned intervention. The healthcare facilities will be improved by WASH, Health, and Protection services, by constructing/repairing/maintenance of gender/(Male/female/LGBTQ)/age/person with disability-friendly structures.Access to water, sanitation and hygiene facilities is a precondition of good health for all persons. Article 28 of the Convention ensures ‘equal access by persons with disabilities to clean water services, and to ensure access to appropriate and affordable services, devices and other assistance for disability-related needs. The person with disability (PWD) often have limited access to water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) services in normal and emergency situation at healthcare facilities. To ensure WASH facilities and services for persons with disabilities in healthcare centers by providing universal design, development of hygiene-related materials, and activation of the WASH FIT committee. The project aims to build hygiene promotion, menstrual hygiene knowledge and practices by delivering health education session at girl schools, awareness-raising activities and NFI materials distribution. To ensure transparency, accountability, and quality by maintaining complaints, feedback, and complain response mechanism at all stages."

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Under the AAP, IASC and HEKS/EPER framework, all activities will be following three main phases. (1) Taking account: the project team will involve beneficiaries at the beginning of the project in order to update the baseline data and to share all relevant information related to the project and to involve them in the decision-making process. (2) Giving account: HEKS/EPER will organize a series of consultations focused on sharing information with communities related to the progress of the project activities, especially during multisectoral community outreach sessions/counseling. (3) Being held to account: HEKS/EPER will organize formal and informal meetings at key moments of the project (start, annually, and end-line evaluation) to allow the communities to assess the project and to give positive and negative feedback related to the project performances.

This project will consider the following strategy- Facilitated the Healthcare facility management committee, to develop a comprehensive improvement plan for the targeted HCF, and advocate with the government, WHO, and donors as well.- Design and implement disaster-resilient infrastructures and use environment-friendly materials in WASH facilities in health care. - Support government and healthcare centers pre, during, and post-disaster by providing, training, Hygiene, water, sanitation, and cleaning materials distribution/repair and maintenance."
