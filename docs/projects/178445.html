Project Summary

Due to the dire impact of losing WASH services and supplies on human life, BO has put providing water and sanitation facilities among the top priorities of its interventions in crisis. BO designed its 2021 WASH intervention based on the analysis of humanitarian needs (MSNA) in NW Syrian, need severity for WASH assistance, as well as, in line with SOs of HRP and WASH Cluster. The project aims to restore functionality or keep water infrastructure at the minimum level of operation; and to carry out life-saving WASH interventions including, provision of water and sanitation facilities and services, and distribution of NFI kits, as well as improvement of hygienic behavior and practices of most vulnerable people. The project is consisted of three main components targeting ~60,000 INDV (29000M/31000F)
*Repairing and rehabilitation of water systems to enable greater quality control, improve access to safe water, and minimize financial burden on already impoverished families targeting 32000 INDV (15200M/16800F)
Communities in NW Syria are overburden due to high IDP numbers resulting from continues displacement in addition to limited functionality and efficiency of water infrastructure and services. Therefore, BO will rehabilitate/expand 75Km of water network and 75Km of sanitation network and 2 elevated tanks. Also, BO will provide operation support for 90 days to supply water to the most vulnerable people, in meanwhile, will develop a tariff-based system management in coordination with relevant stakeholders (mainly the Local Council in the project area). 200 latrines along with water tanks will be established in 25 schools.
*Distribution of essential WASH NFIs and conducting Hygiene promotion activities to improve hygienic behavior and practices targeting 5000 HHs (30000 person 14000M/16000F) of total, 65% are children under 18 years old and 25% are girls and women in reproductive age. In crisis, people tend to follow survival instinct which means prioritize avoiding tangible risks with less care to intangible risk such as dangerous impact of incorrect hygiene practices. BO will conduct 5 Hygiene promotion campaigns along with distribution of the 5,000 WASH-NFIs to improve and encourage beneficiaries to strengthen already well-established hygienic behavior, knowledge, attitude, and practices among the most vulnerable people. The hygiene kits will be customized to address needed items for all gender and age groups (including tooth paste and toothbrush for children, and sanitary pads, feminine disinfectant ..)
*Improving access to emergency WASH facilities and services to reach SPHERE standards, both in terms of the quality of services and gender/protection considerations with no credible solution blooms in the horizon for the conflict in Syria, BO have set up an emergency plan for lifesaving intervention to provide water and sanitation facilities to potentially new displaced people through provision
-1 elevated cement water tank with capacity of 150 cubic meters to improve access to clean and safe water for 20,000 INDV (9700M/10300F) near overcrowded and HTR IDPs settlements
-40 WASH unit that include 160 water tanks (2 cubic meters) and 320 latrine blocks (toilets and baths), as well as, water trafficking. Desludging and solid waste management for 8,000 INDV (2560B, 2640G, 1500W, 1300M) for 90 days
BO will ensure that the support will be provided to the most vulnerable people through transparent process based on clear vulnerability criteria. Principles of Do No Harm and Accountability to Affected People are incorporated in the project design and will be ensured during the project timeline. Also, MEAL team of BO will conduct ongoing monitoring of project activities to verify achievement of indicators and to correct any deviation

4 - Needs

According to HNO2021 WASH assessments, 59% of the Syria population requires WASH assistance, nevertheless, to adequately understand scale and diversity of WASH needs, the overall number of people in need of WASH assistance - 12.2 million, needs to be completed with figures presenting specific WASH needs related to: water quality – 3.3 million and quantity – 1.4 million people, sanitation – 3.2 million people, solid waste management – 4.1 million people, and heavy financial burden linked with purchasing water – 7.5 million or hygiene supplies – 6.4 million people. Further 5.1 million people face barriers to effective handwashing. Over 7 million people is highly dependent on humanitarian WASH assistance, including 1.9 million people living in IDPs last resort sites that require comprehensive and continued WASH assistance to survive. WASH infrastructure requires significant support and investments With 36% of general population relying on alternative to piped water and often unsafe water supply modalities to meet or complement their water needs, at least 70% of sewage being discharged untreated and at least half of the sewerage systems not functional, and about 29% of garbage inappropriately disposed, the WASH infrastructure and service provision require significant repair and operational support. Such needs are particularly high in the areas underserved by public services, host communities with high ratio of IDPs and returnees, schools, healthcare facilities, this is true of both in urban and rural communities. WASH needs in institutions, like schools or healthcare facilities, remain very high. With ongoing COVID-19 pandemic appropriate WASH conditions in healthcare facilities are critical.

Establishing 1 cement elevated water tanks (150 cubic meter) as an emergency response • Preparing BoQs, SoWs, and engineering and construction layouts by technical experts• Implementing and supervising the building processes of the water tank CLWASH/CA5. targeting newly IDPs settled in informal sites with considering SPHERE standards• Distributing 160 water tanks (2 cubic meter) in accessible places for women and girls• Installing 40 prefabricated double latrines (gender separated), each latrine will be prepared with 4 toilets, 2 bathrooms (25 person / toilet) (50 person / bathroom)• Water trafficking of 120 cubic meters of water on daily bases (~15 L/P/D), with desludging, and solid waste management for 90 days

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: - Project’s area is safe, secure and accessible for both genders of staff and beneficiaries while implementing activities. - Procurement of items/ materials won’t affect the availability of commodities or prices. - Availability of clean and safe water sources for trafficking water. - Local councils are cooperative with BO team. [*] Risk of theft of WASH rehabilitation/construction materials such as generators and construction materials (during delivery/storage/ after installation) by armed groups and used in conflict activities.likelihood: Low Impact: HighMitigation: MoUs to be agreed with local authorities/councils to ensure local ownership of materials and rehabilitated/new facilities. promote community ownership. [*] Cash exacerbates existing tension in HHs and leads to domestic violencelikelihood: High Impact: HighMitigation: Involve community in programme design (e.g. in determining frequency/timing/type of delivery), recognizing they may already have mitigation measures toward protecting their own asset. Also ensure coordination among cash implementing organisations, such that they apply best practice and share knowledge and experience.
