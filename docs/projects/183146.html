Project Summary

In the context of Covid-19 border closures have meant families are often stuck and in need of humanitarian assistance and children often face stigmatization, social exclusion, violence and extreme poverty, especially returnees. In Guatemala, hurricanes ETA & IOTA left unprecedented damages, with nearly 500 schools affected and a growing number of students without access to formal or non-formal education, delaying school reopening in 2021. Through this action UNICEF will create opportunities for children exposed to high levels of violence in communities that include returnees or displaced families, through the provision of multiple pathways to education, access to quality learning materials and
psychosocial support that facilitate staying in school. UNICEF will include the provision of WASH in schools to ensure a safe “back to school”. UNICEF will work to reintegrate children that have been out of school in 2020 and prevent their school desertion once schools open. For children, C-19 has essentially meant the loss of one year of learning. Urgent action is paramount to ensure access to education for the most vulnerable children currently at risk of losing a second year of education. This would have a devastating impact on their right to learn and thrive, and children will face increased exposure to violence both if their families stay or embark on a dangerous migration route. The departments of Alta Verapaz, Izabal and Quiché are among the most impacted by Eta and Iota. With schools reopening school´s repairs are urgent, especially with regards to safe WASH facilities which are mandatory for reopening. Through the proposed action UNICEF´s support to the Ministry of Education will increase the number of students that benefit from a restored school infrastructure and WASH facilities an ensure opportunities for education continuity.

1- Project Activities

Provide opportunities for safe reinsertion and quality continuity of Education for out-of-school, returned migrant and other at-risk children and adolescents, through the access to safe and protective emergency services by:1) Ensure the access to Education in Emergency services to children at risk and on the move with flexible modalities of formal and non-formal education for continuous distance learning and support for social integration through the development of alternative educational opportunities.2) Provide essential teaching and learning materials to ensure quality and continuity strategies for inclusive education in emergency services. Minimum packages of learning as well as teaching aids/materials will be provided to students including textbooks, teaching guides and stationery according to the different education and grades modalities.3) Ensure essential WaSH in Schools (WiS) that will contribute to the elimination of physical and administrative barriers to the reopening of schools in Central America through low-cost critical WASH facilities rehabilitation such as for hand washing points, toilets, water systems storage and/or purification, among others.4) Support non-specialized Child Protection services delivery to students and education personnel, through the provision of trainings, guidelines and tools for the prevention, opportune detection, delivery of PSS and referral to local and specialized services to address the suffering due to traumas, stigma and discrimination.5) implement of multi-level communication for development (C4D) model, which would combineadvocacy and community actions in support of what are considered the most suitable flexible and alternative learning and teaching modalities for the targeted population groups.
