Project Summary

Improve accessibility to quality lifesaving nutrition services through integrated rehabilitation, prevention and treatment services for acutely malnourished children (boys and girls 0 59 months of age) and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) among the most affected communities and improve the capacity of national staff. The project target locations are: Woqooyi Galbeed, Awdal, Togdheer, Sool & Sanaag, Bari, Nugaal, Mudug, Galgaduud, Hiraan, Bay, Bakool, Banadir & Lower Juba regions.

Needs

In 2018, the overall food security situation in Somalia has improved when compared to a year ago, the latest food security and nutrition assessment by FAO’s FSNAU, has revealed. This is due to the above-average performance of the April to June Gu rains. The results indicate that most pastoralist areas have seen an improvement in pasture, water availability and increasing herd sizes. However, despite the positive outlook and gains made, humanitarian assistance needs to be sustained to support the people in need. Most importantly these gains has not translated into improvement in the nutrition situation and categorically the nutrition situation remains of serious concern. Recent nutrition surveys across the country indicates slight improvement in few areas and stagnated nutrition situation in most parts of the country. The trends in GAM prevalence(WHZ) across remained at serious levels when compared to last six months back. Furthermore, this is evidenced by the high admissions in nutrition sites/centers over the months as well as compared to similar months of previous years. Several nutrition surveys between May to July, indicate that the overall nutrition situation is at sustained levels despite improved food security, reduced disease outbreaks and sustained humanitarian interventions. As a result, nearly 954,007 children are expected to be acutely malnourished, including 173,594 who are expected to be severely malnourished during the next one year period (Sep 2018-Sep 2019) throughout the country. The SCI areas of interventions recorded the following prevalence of malnutrition:Bari: GAM 17.1% SAM 3.3%Nugaal: 16.7% and 2.5% Mudug: 16.6% and 3.4% Woqooyi Galbeed : 15.6% and 4.7% Burao: 6.3% and 0.5%Sool & Sanaag: 9.7% and 1.6%Galgaduud: 14.0% and 1.6% Hiraan: 15.7% and 3.3% Banadir: 16.7% and 4.1% for IDPsBanadir Urban 13.6% and 2.6% Bay IDPs: 17.7% and 3.6% Bakool: 10.1% and 0.6%Kismayo 14.4% and 2.6%Dhobley 12.1% and 2.6%

Activities

1.1 Screening of under five children and Pregnant and Lactating mothers for acute malnutrition and referring to the treatment nutrition centers 1.2 Treatment of MAM and SAM cases directly in MOH health facilities and/or through mobile teamssession in all supported static and outreach sites 1.3 Provide micro nutrient supplementation to include Vitamin A, MMN and de-worming for children 6 59 months1.4 Coordinate activities with the government and Nutrition Cluster 1.5 Integrating nutrition with WASH and provision of Hygiene kits for mothers with children 1.6 Referring mothers with cases of acute malnutrition, children less 24 months to be included under unconditional cash transfer 1.7 Provision of Blanket Supplementary Feeding Programme for children and PLW1.8 Training MOH nutrition staff on CMAM and BSFP 1.9 Conduct nutrition assessments: SMART surveys, coverage surveys, KAP survey1.10 Treatment of SAM cases without complications in difficult to reach villages through the ICCM Plus approach1.11 Training of Family Health workers on ICCM Plus as part of community resilience

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

- The Nutrition interventions will be linked with the food security and livelihood resilient interventions to include the KFW project in Mogadishu and the BRICS II project in Hiran region for sustainability purposes.- The Nutrition interventions to be provided to the beneficiaries are in line with the Somalia IMAM guidelines and protocols and also meet the international Nutrition standards, and it’s therefore legally recognized by the Ministry of Health, the Somalia authorities- The listed locations are relatively stable in terms of security and therefore access to the beneficiaries is viable.- All these Nutrition interventions are linked within the existing health systems in Somalia, and for areas within Somaliland and Puntland, MOH staff are in the forefront of the implementation of these interventions with Support from SCI- capacity building, technical, logistical. In the South Central Somalia area, the nutrition interventions are linked where possible with Ministry of Health, and has a lot of focus on the training and capacity building of the MOH/National staff.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Increased protection risks and violations are common in situations of drought, mostly as a consequence of the disaster and ensuing displacement, but also due to the increased stress disaster creates in a community and fragmentation of informal protection structures. Save the Children implements do-no-harm principle in its entire humanitarian and development work. This is ensured through the quality benchmarks that have been developed for all the specific activities carried out by the organization. Save the Children promotes impartiality at the heart of its work, and this is reflected in our Code of Conduct signed by Save the Children staff, as well as MOH health facility personnel, and it is included in MOUs with the MOH and community groups.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Nutrition services at both the facility based sites and community level are provided on based need and vulnerability criteria, regardless of religion, socio-economic status, sex or clan. For activities requiring beneficiary selection/targeting, beneficiary selection will be conducted through project committees with adequate knowledge on the target population, their needs, conflict dynamics and vulnerability of different groups especially on the referral to the cash transfer programs available in the same locations where nutrition interventions are implemented. Use of community structures will ensure the project does not cause conflict or escalate existing tensions through unfair targeting processes. Effort will also be made to ensure that sites selected for health service delivery are located in a safe and accessible place for beneficiaries, and background check on conducted on service providers behaviors. Nutrition services will be provided without biased or discrimination, and SC will work closely with community leaders’/clan leaders to ensure inclusion/representation of minority population. Children who come into contact with Save the Children as a result of our activities must be safeguarded to the maximum possible extent from deliberate or inadvertent actions and failings that place them at risk of child abuse, sexual exploitation, injury and any other harm. This responsibility falls upon all of our staff and representatives and is reflected across many policies. This duty of care is enshrined in our Child Safeguarding Policy. All staff are required to undertake child safeguarding training as part of their induction process and are required to sign and adhere to the child safeguarding policy. The Policy requires that everyone associated with the organization is aware of their obligations and responds appropriately to issues of child abuse and the sexual exploitation of children. In this way we make Save the Children safe for children.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

• Beneficiaries and their communities face particular risks relating to armed conflicts and violence and as such this contributes significantly to internal displacements. We will uphold the humanitarian principle of neutrality and impartiality and will take the service as close to the affected communities as possible but the project will not be expected to aggravate the exposure of targeted communities to insecurity and other impacts of armed conflicts.
