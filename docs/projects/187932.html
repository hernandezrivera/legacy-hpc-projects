Start Date

01/01/2022

End Date

31/12/2023

Project Summary

11% of the planned budget is secured for (2022)
The project will target 6000 families with regular (monthly) food assistance through two appropriate modalities:
1- Cash for food: GAN will target 3000 (1500 HH in 2022, 1500 HH in 2023) most vulnerable families in village and camps with cash for food, the cash intervention is feasible in these camps as they all have access to different main markets in the targeted communities, eight rounds of cash for food will be distributed, each round consists of 20 days for distribution and 10 days for validation and PDM, 60$ will be allocated for each family as recommended by FSL cluster and CWG, the data gained form the FSL cluster indicate this areas as high priority ranking and no partner plan to target the same vulnerable and underserved camps, according to the needs assessment, the first priority is food, the proposed modality is a cash for food, as it is considered one of the most effective modality since the markets are available near camps. (21% of the value secured for 2022)
2- E-voucher for Food: GAN will target 3000 (1500 HH in 2022, 1500 HH in 2023) most vulnerable families in the village with food voucher with the aim of saving lives and ensuring continued and regular access to food for 8 months for most vulnerable groups, beneficiaries could replace the monthly top-up assistance (60$ per family) with the main food items from the markets contracted with GAN and will be able to buy among 40 specified items with restricted amounts that allowed us to fulfill the calories min standard set by the WFP. (Unsecured)
The project will target the IDPs with emergency food assistance through four appropriate modalities:
1- Providing cooked meals to 1500 families (750 HH in 2022, 750 HH in 2023) of newly IDPs in the targeted locations 30 times respectively within 30 days, this intervention is considered as a rapid response for affected IDPs suffering from lack of resources, instability, and don’t have cooking facilities, GAN designed the cooked meals based on its experience along past 10 years, and each beneficiary will receive 500g of high-quality cooked food daily for 30 days (this period of this type of assistance is recommended by FSL cluster). (50% of the value secured for 2022)
2- Providing RTERs to 1500 families (750 HH in 2022, 750 HH in 2023) of newly IDPs in the targeted locations enough for 5 persons for 7 days, this intervention is considered as a rapid response for affected IDPs suffering from lack of resources, instability and don’t have cooking facilities (50% of the value secured for 2022)
3- Providing food baskets to the beneficiaries (1500 HHs) who have received Cooked meals/RTERs as a next step toward securing their food security which will last for 3 months (10% of the value secured for 2022)
4- Bread bundles: provide bread to 1000 HHs in the targeted camps for 30 days frequencies. (50% of the value secured for 2022)
GAN will use beneficiary selection criteria that prioritize vulnerable HHs, including female or children-headed households, those with elderly or disabled family members, chronic ill family members, pregnant, and households with more than six persons.
Monthly market assessment in terms of availability of the items, prices, and accessibility will be conducted to ensure the local markets are functioning and all beneficiaries have good and equal accessibility to the different goods.

4 - Needs

Throughout 2021, the macro-economic context continued to deteriorate sharply and is accelerating into 2022. The combined effects of currency depreciation, soaring prices, reduced fiscal revenue increasing domestic debt, widespread job loss. The long-standing needs of an estimated 6.9M IDPs remain staggering, particularly for over two million people in 1760 informal settlements and planned camps. With a disproportionate impact on female-headed households, older persons without family support, persons with disabilities, and children. An increasing number of women and adolescent girls are forced to work in order to provide for their families, either as sole breadwinners or contributing to the family income.The mounting food insecurity almost 60% of the population. Stunting increased by 4%, SAM increased by 29% and MAM increased by 45%.With average household expenditure now exceeding income by 50%, compared to 20% in August 2020, only 10% of households have an income above the cost of SMEB.The national average FBP has increased by 27% in the past 6months (May 2021) and by 115% in the last year (November 2020).Households are now reverting to negative coping mechanisms more frequently than before. This includes child labor, child marriage, and the sale of productive assets.IDP households also report negative coping strategies more frequently than other population groups, such as selecting less expensive or preferable food and purchasing food on credit.

5 - Implementation of activities

Regular assistance: 1- Food voucher: provide life-saving humanitarian food assistance to 3000 HHs. The project will provide food assistance by restricted voucher modality (e-voucher) with the aim of saving lives and ensuring continued and regular access to food by a monthly 60$ which will last for 8 months with a total of 480$ for each HH. 2- Cash for food: provide life-saving humanitarian food assistance to 3000 HHs, through cash for food modality using (e-voucher) regular access to food by providing a monthly 60$ per family which will last for 8 months. Emergency response: 1- Cooked meals: provide emergency life-saving assistance through cooked meals to 1500 HHs new arrivals IDPs for 30 days frequencies. 2- RTERs: Provide emergency life-saving assistance through RTERs to 1500 HHs new arrivals IDPs.3- Food basket: provide emergency life-saving assistance through food basket to the beneficiaries (1500 HHs) who have received Cooked meals/RTERs as a next step toward securing their food security which will last for 3 months.4- Bread bundles: provide bread to 1000 HHs in the targeted camps for 30 days frequencies.The project targets camps and out-off camps, so blanket distribution will be applied in camps and the criteria will be applied out-off camps, whereas there will be prioritization criteria as follows: vulnerable HHs, including female or female/youth-headed households, those with elderly or disabled family members, and households with more than six persons.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

GAN is a member of PSEA network, attending a monthly meeting and following the guidelines, so to mainstream the protection from sexual exploitation and abuse in the project, GAN have developed a policy stating standards of conduct, including acts of SEA, and a work plan to implement the policy in place, GAN will ensure all staff members are signed and adhered to a code of conduct policy, PSEA and will support the members to access relevant pieces of training (PSEA, GBV), ensuring that non-humanitarian stakeholders who will be in touch with beneficiaries such as (suppliers) will be informed and adhere to the humanitarian principles and code of conduct, PSEA posters will be available at distribution points. GAN recognizes the rights of all stakeholders, particularly the vulnerable, to access humanitarian programs without the risk of sexual exploitation or abuse. GAN intends to prevent abuse by implementing procedures to protect beneficiaries through best recruitment practices, staff induction, creating an open and aware culture and assigning clear management responsibilities, ensuring that victims are supported, and perpetrators held to account. Information on the referral pathway will be circulated through posters and banners at the distribution points. The distribution points will be inside the camps itself, during daylight hours, and safeguard that there is appropriate segregation for women to create a culturally sensitive and safe operational environment


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: - The MTA using the e-voucher modality are available and functioning.- Markets are functioning properly. - Adequate presence of vendors to support the voucher program - Access to Syria remains possible and safe through the neighboring countries - COVID context is continued Risk: 1: the risk of that one money transfer service is responsible for transferring the cash amount: Likelihood: low, Impact: high. GAN has a prior fundamental condition for a money transfer agent: the payment is conducted after distribution. 2: COVID-19 spread in target communities: Mitigation: GAN team takes all necessary measures to avoid the transmission and spread of the disease infection, such as wearing a face mask, gloves, providing hand sanitizers, maintaining social space spacing, and measuring the temperature of the beneficiaries before entering the distribution point, GAN will use the latest version of recommendations in regards of distributing NFI under the cotext of COVID-19 3: Due to the unpredictable situation in NW Syria, there is a risk that markets are not functioning properly due to lack of access, or other unexpected issues. Likelihood: low, Impact: high. Mitigation strategy: GAN confirmed the markets are functioning and the security situation allows the implementation of the project. The situation of the market will be monitored on a regular basis by the program team
