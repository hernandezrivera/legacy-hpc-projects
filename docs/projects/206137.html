Project Summary

According to HNO 2022, about 3.6 million people, 97 percent of whom are children, are estimated to need education services. Protracted impact of COVID 19 school closure, local conflict, returnees displaced by Sudan conflict and broader socio-economic hardship exacerbated existing education challenges and inequities. In addition, about 139,000 refugee children also need education support. Prior to the COVID-19 school closures, about 2.2 million children were already out of school. The school closures further impacted the education of over 2.7 million additional children. In May 2021, after 14 months of closure due to COVID-19, schools in South Sudan re-opened. The current estimated number of out-of-school children is 2.8 million. Additionally, gross enrolment decreased by roughly 7 percent between 2020 (2,727,209) and 2021 (2,523,204). Insecurity is a significant challenge to school functionality. In Jonglei, the Greater Pibor Administrative Area, Lakes, Upper Nile, Central Equatoria, Warrap, Unity, and Western Equatoria, active conflict and recurring communal violence throughout 2021 led to school closures. Safety and security incidents in and around education facilities, as well as destruction or occupation, were also mentioned in the qualitative component of the FSNMS+ 2021 as barriers to accessing education facilities. Learners in the 2021 Education Need Assessment (ENA) cited insecurity in and around schools as a major reason for learner absenteeism and drop-out in 2021. School functionality has also been severely impacted by flooding in 2021 and was mentioned as an impediment to learners' access to school in the qualitative component of the FSNMS+ 2021. Jonglei, Unity, and Upper Nile have been the worst impacted by floods.
In August 2022, heavy fighting between armed factions in Tonga resulted in the displacement of the population. An estimated 18,000–27,000 people were reported displaced from Tonga, Panykang County, and its surroundings (August 2022, OCHA flash report). According to the latest Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) cluster update, about 18,345 internally displaced persons (IDPs) from Panyikang and surrounding areas have arrived at Malakal Protection of Civilian (PoC) and Malakal Town as of September 19, 2022. Prior consultation on education needs and gaps in Malakal Town and PoC highlighted that the schools and learning spaces in both locations have been overstretched and are facing funding constraints to expand education access for needy children. With the increasing arrival of IDPs and returnees from Sudan to Malakal Town and PoC, the current learning spaces and schools do not have the capacity to absorb additional children. As a result of the compounded impact of violence, conflict, and flooding and Sudan returnees in Malakal, Fashoda, Melut, Panyikang, Pigi Canal, and Bor South, the functionality of several schools has been affected, including the displacement of children and damage to school infrastructure. More than 260 returnees and refugees arrived to South Sudan since April 2023 (IOM-UNHCR Report) of which 75% crossed through Upper Nile Border areas specially Renk. This project envisages providing integrated education and child protection in emergency services to 8,000 children affected by conflict and flood-induced displacement and returnees from Sudan in Malakal, Fashoda, Panyikang, Melut, Bor South, and Pigi Canal Counties. The project will strengthen the capacity of teachers on teaching methodology and focus on enhancing the capacity of parent-teacher associations (PTAs) and school management committees (SMCs) in education in emergencies and school management in line with government education sector priorities and the humanitarian response plan (HRP).

2. Project activities

In addition to creating access to education, the project also aims to promote life-saving protection, psychosocial support, and healing messaging to protect children from physical dangers, abuse, exploitation, recruitment into armed groups, and psychological distress. Accordingly, War Child Holland will implement the below activities to achieve learning outcomes for the conflict- and flood-affected children in Malakal, Fashoda, Melut, Renk, Panyikang, Bor South, and Pigi Canal Counties. - 1) There were 8,905 (4,830 female and 4,075 male) learners enrolled in the Early Childhood Development (ECD) Primary and Accelerated Learning Program (ALP). 2) 100 kits of teaching and learning materials of different types were distributed to 8,905 learners and 143 teachers. 3) 18 Temporary Learning Spaces (TLS) with segregated 18 WASH facilities in Malakal, Fashoda, Melut, Renk, Panyikang, Bor South, and Pigi/Canal were re-established and supported to create education access for 8,905 children. 4) 143 volunteer teachers (114 male and 29 female) trained in Education in Emergency (EiE), basic pedagogy, and learner-centered methodologies inclusive of psychosocial support (PSS), gender inclusion, and safeguarding. 5) 143 (114 male and 28 female) volunteer teachers were provided with teaching incentives over a 12-month period at the cluster rate. 6) 34 school governing bodies, 40% female (Parent Teachers Association (PTA) and School Management Committees (SMC), trained their roles and responsibilities in education in emergency and school management. Ensuring accountability to affected people: War Child Holland approaches education needs through a child protection lens, with specific attention to affected people's experiences as a result of their special needs, age, gender, inclusion, and status. War Child will ensure that communication with and feedback, including PSEA, from the affected community informs the design and implementation of the response through the establishment of feedback centers in schools and during activities such as distribution and awareness.Activity reports and 5Ws reports will be shared with the donor and protection cluster on a monthly basis for their actions.
