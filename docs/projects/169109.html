Project Summary

The NRC Education response includes two key modules:
1) School Repair and Rehabilitation: Targeted support to public and semi-private schools that were damaged by the Beirut Port explosions with rehabilitation and repairs, as well as teacher professional development on classroom-based PSS for teachers of the rehabilitated schools.
2) Better Learning Programme: A package of social emotional learning skills to process traumatic experiences and feel safe to learn. This is combined with focused Psychosocial Support (PSS) for children based on a nightmare screening tool with the Better Learning Programme (BLP), NRC’s evidence-based programme that is approved and used by MEHE.

1 - Needs

The Government of Lebanon (GoL) estimate that approximately 300,000 people have been made homeless as a result of the explosion, with thousands more suffering structural damage to their housings. Multiple medical facilities have also sustained damage and hospitals, which were already under pressure due to COVID-19, have been completely overwhelmed, with casualties reportedly being treated in parking lots or even being turned away. The explosion comes at a time when Lebanon was already in an extremely fragile state. The economic crisis has resulted in the devaluation of the Lebanese Pound (LBP), losing 80% of its value since October 2019.The unofficial exchange rate hovers around 8,000 LBP to the USD, although the official exchange rate remains 1,500LBP/USD. More than 50% of Lebanese are under the poverty line, with unemployment reaching 30% in May 2020. COVID-19 has exacerbated the situation. two-thirds of households had experienced a drop in income due to COVID-19, while 40% had incurred debt to buy food or pay rent. Food prices have continued to rise, with annual food inflation reaching 190%. This economic insecurity means that many will not be able to afford to repair their homes themselves, and with imports being impeded, a restricted supply of building materials may result in price increases, further hampering these families’ attempts to carry out their own repairs. In addition to the above needs, there is a risk that the explosion will lead to a spike in COVID-19 cases, placing further pressure on the health system. In terms of children’s needs, rapid needs assessment conducted by UNICEF shortly after the Beirut Port explosions indicated that 50% children were showing changes in behaviour or signs of trauma or extreme stress. University of Tromso and NRC identify two broad categories of children have been affected by the Beirut explosion:Directly affected children: those living in close proximity to the explosion, those who have lost family members, seen people being injured and/or experienced loss or damage to property. Indirectly affected children: those whose family members, friends and relatives have been directly affected; children living in Lebanon who have been watching high-intensity media coverage with grotesque scenes. Vulnerable children: How severely affected the children in the two categories are may depend on their individual vulnerabilities. Children are in general vulnerable; those with previous trauma exposure such as war experiences are in particular vulnerable. Children are more likely than adults to be affected by disasters, and more severely so. On average, children and young people seem to be less well equipped than adults to cope with disasters. The cognitive abilities and lack of life experience of children may reduce their ability to handle acute helplessness, or comprehend and make sense of the world; and may cause a loss in perceived safety and social support. Children become even more vulnerable when their parents are affected.Traumatized parents may have less capacity to support their children.Children may feel worried/scared concerning their parents’ changed behavior. Previous experiences of trauma often represent a vulnerability in the face of new crises or catastrophes because they can awaken same types of feelings as the earlier trauma:fears of death and destruction.In terms of school’s needs, it is both the hard and soft components for children and to feel that they have a safe place to learn. MEHE rapid assessment indicates 159 schools were affected with severe, moderate, or light damage, which has knock-on effects on 85,313 students. To add to this, teachers are likely not well equipped to handle the psychosocial challenges of children once they enter the classroom.

2 - Activities

MODULE 1: SAFE LEARNING ENVIRONMENTSBased on years of experience and existing technical capacity, NRC will support schools in need rehabilitation and teacher professional development to support students with a safe learning environment in which to learn including the following activities: School rehabilitation: NRC’s Shelter/WASH programme will conduct a technical assessment based on experience with MEHE to develop the appropriate BOQs for rehabilitation and repairs. Once completed, NRC will provide guidance to school administration and personnel, including janitors, for upkeep and operational maintenance where appropriate. Teacher Professional Development on PSS: In order for returning children to feel safe, NRC will also support the professional development needs of teachers of the repaired schools with the MEHE- and CERD-approved BLP package. Where appropriate, NRC will use CERD Master Trainers who have previously delivered the training for public school teachers and PSS counsellors. MODULE 2: BETTER LEARNING PROGRAMME NRC will implement the evidence-based Better Learning Programme directly through its Emergency Response Unit (ERU) as well as partners. This includes two interlinked programmes: BLP 1 and BLP 2: NRC will tailor its BLP to the needs of children directly and indirectly affected by the Beirut Port explosions in the catchment areas of Achrafieh fonciere, Marfa' fonciere, Mdaouar fonciere in two short-term learning centres. The content of the materials include BLP1-coping skills, BLP 2-study skills, combined with the arts for children to have a sense of normalcy and develop the necessary skills to thrive in life and education. The total duration of the programme is 8 two-hour sessions. BLP 1 which supports coping skills is based on the five evidence-based key principals: 1) being able to calm down, 2) sense of stability, 3) the power to change the situation, 4) connecting with others, 5) reestablishing hope. BLP 2 which supports study skills is based on executive functioning skills that are relevant and contribute to improved capacity to learn. BLP 3: NRC will utilize its focussed PSS intervention for children who suffer from nightmares and bedwetting after traumatic events based on academically-vetted nightmare screening questionnaires and processes. The total duration of the programme is 4 one-hour individual sessions, after attending BLP 1+2. It is essential that NRC and its partners link the BLP 1+2 and BLP 3 programmes as children will be in a safe space with trusted adults that they know, rather than executing referrals to other organisations.NRC Module 1 School Rehabilitation 12 schools | 7200 students (3600 M, 3600 F) NRC Module 1 Teacher Professional Development 120 teachers (42 M, 78 F) NRC Module 2 BLP 1+2 288 children (144 M, 144 F) NRC Module 2 BLP 3 20 children (10 M, 10 F) Partners Module 2 BLP 1+2 288 children (144 M, 144 F) Partners Module 2 BLP 3 20 children (10 M, 10 F)
