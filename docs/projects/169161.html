Project Summary

For the health response UNOPS proposes to contribute to restore Beirut health facilities' operational capacity by undertaking the preliminary phase of the reconstruction of the destroyed health facilities (4 hospitals - INSTEAD OF 5 INITIALLY PROPOSED - and 4 PHCs), the provision of renewable energy solutions for health facilities, and procurement of PPEs and medical equipment, in an integrated manner. The project entails four components which can all be implemented independently. See details in "Activities".
All procurement to be carried out under UNOPS emergency procurement procedure (EPP) to ensure speedy delivery under 3 months. IN TERMS OF CAPACITY, UNDER THE EPP, UNOPS HAS SUCCESSFULLY AND SIMULTANEOUSLY PROVIDED COVID-19 RESPONSE MEDICAL EQUIPMENT AND PPEs FOR DIFFERENT MINISTRIES OF HEALTH IN THE REGION IN A 6-8 WEEKS TIMEFRAME, FOR A TOTAL AMOUNT OF 16 MILLION USD.
ON HEALTH FACILITIES LOCATIONS, UNOPS DID NOT TAKE PART IN THE INITIAL ASSESSMENTS AND THEREFORE REMAINS FLEXIBLE TO IMPLEMENT REHABILITATION FOR ANY GAP OR NON-COVERED HEALTH FACILITIES WHICH WOULD BE IDENTIFIED BY THE HEALTH CLUSTER, AND ASSIGNED TO US ACCORDINGLY. UNOPS COMMITS TO CLOSE COORDINATION WITH HEALTH SECTOR AND KEY HEALTH ACTORS REGARDING REHABILITATION AND RECONSTRUCTION PLANS FOR HEALTH FACILTIIES TO AVOID ANY DUPPLICATION OF EFFORTS AND ENSURE EFFICIENCY OF THE RESPONSE.
Estimated costs and target values are based on the available needs assessment results, and costs of similar projects implemented by UNOPs in Lebanon and the region.
UNOPS has extensive experience procuring medical equipment and COVID-19 related supplies for different ministries in the region, including the MoPH in Lebanon. UNOPS is currently equipping 220 health facilities with renewable energy solutions in Yemen. Finally, UNOPS has extensive constructing and/or rehabilitating health facilities in Jordan, Yemen, Iraq and Syria.
UNOPS is submitting a multi-sectoral response, with proposals in HEALTH, SHELTER and EDUCATION, therefore support costs may be slightly decreased when selection of project is communicated.

1 - Needs

On Tuesday, August 4, 2020, a massive explosion caused by 2,750 tonnes of ammonium nitrate stored at the port, hit Beirut and led to the destruction of large swaths of the capital. This explosion came on top of the COVID-19 pandemic, the socio-economic crisis and the growing social tensions. According to OCHA (Sitrep #4) and WHO, six major hospitals sustained heavy damages and were partially to fully destroyed. Moreover, according to the and the National Primary Health Care Network, 4 of the Primary Healhtcare Centers (PHCs) were completely destroyed. A preliminary rapid assessment has revealed that, out of 55 medical facilities, only half are fully operational and around 40 per cent have suffered moderate to serious damage and are in need of rehabilitation. An approximate 500 beds were lost from the total health response capacity. According to the Beirut Blast Health Sector Action Plan, coordinated by the Health Cluster, and consultations with UNICEF, Relief International and IMC, rehabilitations of moderately damaged PHCs will be well covered. Therefore UNOPS proposes to focus on the heavily or fully destroyed health facilities which will require reconstruction. The 4 destroyed PHCCs estimated to cost approximately 168,838 USD each as per the preliminary assessment conducted by the Health Interagency Group in Lebanon. An additional budget will be required to cover the rent expenses of alternative locations during the rehabilitation period. Secondly, in terms of electricity, the electricity supply has been largely limited, due to the financial crisis and the subsequent fuel cost inflation. Health facilities have not been an exception to blackouts, and the provision of services has been drastically compromised in health facilities in order to save on energy bills. Installation of an environment-friendly and cost efficient energy solution will contribute to not only provision of critical health services amid the crises but also contribute to the alleviation of the Government’s financial burden of its power supply costs with a cumulative debt of USD 30 billion accounts for about 40% of Lebanon’s GDP. According to OCHA (Sitrep #4) Nabaa and Qarantina neighborhoods reportedly lack electricity. Other neighborhoods continue to have electricity at preexplosion levels (i.e., less than six hours daily). Reportedly, cables and generators within the power grid were severely damaged cutting off electricity provision. While power outages of three to six hours a day are common in Lebanon, they went up to 20 hours a day in July due to fuel shortages. Finally, in the explosion, medical equipment and Personal Protective Equipments (PPEs) were destroyed. The COVID-19 response remains critical, especially given the recent upward epidemiological trend (increase of 44% over the past two weeks according to WHO, and Lebanon declared in phase 4, as community transmission is now widespread). According to the National PHC Network, the costs of Personal Protective Equipment (PPEs) for the PHCCs’ staff to ensure both beneficiary and healthcare providers’ safety amidst the COVID-19 pandemic is estimated at 800,000 USD, and the need is proportionally much higher for hospitals. Approximately 55 per cent of health facilities also expressed the need for equipment.

2 - Activities

*ORIGINAL PROJECT PLANS: 1) REHABILITATION/Reconstruction of 4 (NUMBER UPDATED) partially or fully DAMAGED hospitals (AS MENTIONED PREVIOUSLY, UNOSP REMAINS FLEXIBLE ON THE HEALTH FACILITIES ACCORDING TO THE GAPS IDENTIFIED BY THE COORDINATION AND WHICH WOULD BE ASSIGNED TO US) - this is only the first phase of the reconstruction process including in-depth technical assessment, engineering design, design review, AND MINOR REHABILITATION WHEN APPLICABLE. The subsequent reconstruction will, however, fall under the recovery and reconstruction phase, after the 3 months. 2) REHABILITATION/Reconstruction of 4 PARTIALLY DAMAGED OR FULLY DESTROYED PHCs ((AS MENTIONED PREVIOUSLY, UNOSP REMAINS FLEXIBLE ON THE HEALTH FACILITIES ACCORDING TO THE GAPS IDENTIFIED BY THE COORDINATION AND WHICH WOULD BE ASSIGNED TO US) - this is only the first phase of the reconstruction process including in-depth technical assessment, engineering design, design review, AND MINOR REHABILITATION WHEN APPLICABLE. The subsequent reconstruction will, however, fall under the recovery and reconstruction phase. 3) Provision of renewable energy solutions for the functional health facilities (20 hospitals and 23 PHCs). This includes technical assessment of the current electric installation, design review, procurement of equipment (solar photovoltaic systems and solar water heaters), installation and training on maintenance for the relevant health facility staff. 4) Provision of medical equipment and/or PPEs for hospitals and PHCs, ACCORDING TO THE NEEDS COMMUNICATED BY THE COORDINATION AND THE HEALTH FACILITIES. *UPDATE NOVEMBER 2020: Funding has not yet been mobilized for the above activities yet they remain critical to support. Funds are urgently needed to begin the assessment and planning process for sustainable electrical solutions for blast-affected health facilities.
