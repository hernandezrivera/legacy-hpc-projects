Project Summary

Under the first objective of Cameroon HRP 2020, the WASH Sector has planned to provide services as per the agreed standards for immediate, early and short-term response, to address context specific WASH needs of NWSW regions of Cameroon. As per UNICEF’s project, under Cameroon HRP 2020’s Outcome, UNICEF plans to provide lifesaving assistance to people newly displaced by conflict using early recovery approaches to improve the overall access to WASH services, connecting the need for short-term emergency interventions with long term solutions and applying development principles by working directly with local communities to facilitate ownership and accountability. UNICEF seeks to improve and sustain access to safe water in target communities in South West and North West regions, and on improving and sustaining access to safe water and sanitation facilities and hygiene messages addressing malnutrition related risks and increase community resilience to disease outbreaks.
Data collected about the situation through the 2019 MSNA and MIRA were used to design the project. According to specific information gathered during assessment visits, IDPs are in urgent need of:
- Water supply systems as a majority have been neglected and in need of repair/rehabilitation
- Knowledge of good water, sanitation and hygiene practices as basis for survival
- WASH kits (family kits, menstrual kits and water treatment kits)
- Household water treatment technics
- Open Defecation Free environment
- Adequate personal hygiene for health improvement
Based on the actual situation, UNICEF plans to conduct the following WASH intervention:
- Support hygiene, sanitation and environmental health promotion, as well as awareness about water quality in affected areas and host communities;
- Increase access to safe drinking water through community training of water management committees, rehabilitation of water sources and distribution of household water treatment kits;
- Provide WASH kits to ensure a clean environment, maintain contingency stock for cholera and ensure adoption of good water, hygiene and sanitation practices;
- Ensure promotion of menstrual hygiene.
The response targets 150,000 IDPs and host communities. Proposed interventions will be implemented with other key sectors with priority focus on nutritional and health related issues. Other partners will be involved in the WASH response; this includes local and International
UNICEF will make two staff members available for the WASH response coordination and provide technical support to actors involved in carrying out the emergency WASH response during the intervention. Specific needs will receive special attention, as well as beneficiaries’ protection during the emergency WASH intervention, particularly at sites where internally displaced populations are gathered.

Besoins / Needs

The humanitarian situation in the North-West (NW) and South-West (SW) regions of the country continues to appear to be worsening with frequency infighting and incidents reported among the non-State armed groups (NSAGs) and the military. 1.86 million people are estimated to be in need of assistance with more than quarter of the population and over 530,000 people are internally displaced. The situation has forced over 437,000 people to flee across four regions (HRP 2019) seeking in villages and bushes that are hard to reach subjecting them to different kinds of suffering e.g. lack of access to basic services like water, health and educationIn terms of WASH, primary water sources and quality vary greatly, with populations residing in more rural areas seemingly unable to provide clean water for the households. Water treatment is not widely practiced, nor are some primary hygiene practices such as hand-washingWater supply coverage is still low with an overall of more than 55% of the population in urban and rural areas lacking access to an improved water source. A total of 30% people living in the bush, rural and urban settings have no capacity to secure enough water for domestic use. Over 50 % indicated a problem with access to enough quantities of water most notably in bush areas. Even though only 20 % are accessing non – unimproved water sources, in 93 % of the communities, few to no families treat water (WASH Needs Assessment Report (2019).The overall sanitation situation in the region is dire with 73% of the communities practice open defecation or using unimproved sanitation facilities, of those, more than 40% of the assessed communities practice open defecation . Access to hygiene is very limited (MICS 2015), with 71% of key informants interviewed reporting limited to no use of soap or alternatives for hand-washing. Access to soap was more limited for displaced communities, with 77% of people interviewed reporting that only few or no families had soap in comparison to 59% of key informants for non-displaced communities. Overall, only a minority of people possessed fix or mobile devises designated for hand washing, with 79% of key informants reporting that either only few people or nobody had any. Those findings are particularly concerning, especially if analysed in conjunction with access to soap, as ownership of hand washing materials is one the most reliable proxies for hand washing behaviour. Regarding Hand washing behavior, findings on hand washing practices were broadly in line with the ones on ownership of soap and hand washing facilities. Most key informants (80%) reported that only few people or nobody usually washed hands with soap at five critical times. The lack of adequate WASH facilities and services has exacerbated the situation with an ongoing cholera outbreak in Ndian division where 14 people have died and more than 302 cholera cases reported, highlighting the multifaceted challenges and needs of the people. Based on these needs, the organizations plans to provide WASH services using minimum WASH standards of SPHERE and/or national standards. The project will deliver an integrated package of WASH infrastructure and services improvements to communities and institutions, with due consideration for solutions that are resilient particularly to Climate related disasters, hygiene promotion, and capacity building activities for the main stakeholders, within the framework of National WASH priorities. It will provide tangible improvements in living conditions for an estimated 1500,000 people. The expected ultimate outcome of the project is reduced burden of WASH-related diseases among men, women, boys and girls in SWNW.
