Project Summary

Through this project, Street Child aim to respond to worsening child protection needs by providing an action in the Far North as well as North-west and South-west regions, that focuses on (i) responding to the results obtained from the context analysis and needs assessment, capacity and system strengthening for government and non-government actors for prevention and delivery; and preventive measures serving most vulnerable children and communities. This project is therefore specifically designed to ensure that every child, including adolescents, is protected from violence, exploitation, abuse, neglect and harmful practices. This will be realised through the following objectives.
• Stronger Cluster coordination through evidence based, data analysis and harmonization of practices to identify trends, patterns and gaps to improve Child Protection policies, programmes and services.
• Child protection systems and structures at national and local levels reinforced to enhance functionality and sustainability in humanitarian settings.
• Community engagement approaches and processes strengthened with increased evidence of inclusive civil and child participation, social behavioural/attitudinal change, and accountability to affected populations.
Lack of capacity and effective systems in place continue to hamper the provision of quality child protection services, leading to an increase in the vulnerability of affected children. Furthermore, the social and legal systems in these communities have been weakened leading to increasing risks in the protection of children. Children now experience significantly more physical and sexual violence in these communities, especially adolescent girls. CPAoR partners in the NWSW have been reporting steady increase in SGBV involving adolescent girls within crisis context in the last 3 quarters. Girls being at higher risk of experiencing sexual violence, particularly rape and child marriage, aggravated by gender inequalities that have been influenced by power dynamics. It is crucial to address these gender disparities and promote gender equality in order to effectively protect all children, especially girls from violence and crate a safe environment for them. This will be done through the following activities:
• Strengthen the capacity of 500 government social workers, teachers, health professionals, judiciary and frontline professionals on child protection issues (on CPiE, CPiHA, MHPSS, PFA, strategies-including violations of Child Rights, communicating with children in distress, survivor centered approach etc.).
• Strengthen the capacity of 300 local organization actors on child protection issues (on CPiE, CPiHA, MHPSS, PFA, Managing Children’s Data, strategies-including violations of Child Rights, communicating with children in distress, survivor centered approach etc.)
• 40,000 people have access to safe channels in reporting sexual exploitation and abuse by personnel who provide assistance to affected populations
• Strengthen 60 community-based Child Protection Mechanisms structures that are available, active and dynamic in ensuring children are protected from violence, abuse, neglect, and exploitation. These CBCPMs structures will ensure that 25,000 children have access to community-based MHPSS.
• Capacity building for local organizations facilitating Social Behavioural Change (SBC) and CBCPMs.
• Organise sporting activities for 2,000 boys and girls, with involvement of caregivers and other community members for awareness raising
• Train 15 coaches and 15 other facilitators to deliver MHPSS though sport.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The ongoing crisis in NWSW Regions of Cameroon continue to increase violence, exploitation, and abuse against children. The fragile conflict situation characterized by instability, destabilization of functional family support systems and community structures, distortion/destruction of sources of livelihoods, school closure, disruption of safety nets, and lack of alternative descent work opportunities creates conditions for diverse forms of child neglect, exploitation and abuse including child labour. In the NWSW Regions, over 19,000 children have been separated from their primary caregivers (HRP 2023) and most of them have become victims of different forms of exploitation. This has weakened the protective environment for children in these conflict affected communities thereby further limiting children’s access to education and causing harms to their physical, mental and social wellbeing. Moreover, these issues are exacerbated by the fact that many have been displaced, lack birth documentation and most of them have been out of school for protracted periods (Danish Refugee Council, 2023– Report of Child Labour Assessment in the SW Region). In the Far North Region where the Boko Haram crisis in the Lake Chad Basin has displaced over 657,000 including 134,000 refugees, 385,000 IDPs and 138,000 returnees (HRP 2023), most of them are women and children. Most children and their caregivers are exposed to several protection risks including family separation due to forced population movements, and the abduction of children often related to the forced recruitment by NSAGs with heightened risk of sexual exploitation and trafficking for girls. Most of these children in conflict situations, especially girls, also face vulnerabilities related to early marriage, teenage pregnancies and harmful gender norms, attitudes and practices. Children living with disability, those belonging to indigenous minority groups (e.g. Bororo children in the NWSW Regions), children of mothers in detention, orphaned children and those infected or affected by HIV often face additional risks of stigmatization and discrimination. Children in conflict with the law in Cameroon, including those who are victims of arbitrary arrests in crisis affected areas, face a number of protection barriers including the absence of juvenile courts and juvenile judges; the lack of alternative punishments to the deprivation of freedom (imprisonment of minors in conflict with the law at the expense of their placement in rehabilitation institution); the inadequate legal representation of children during the judicial process; and the low speed in the execution of court decisions concerning children in conflict with the law (Cameroon National Child Protection Policy Paper, 2017). The impact on education is still very glaring, with just 46% of schools in the NWSW operational in 2022, and about 10% of IDP children having access to education (HRP, 2023), hence, and an increase in child protection concerns. Lack of capacity and effective systems in place continue to hamper the provision of quality child protection services, leading to an increase in the vulnerability of affected children. Furthermore, the social and legal systems in these communities have been weakened leading to increasing risks in the protection of children. Children now experience significantly more physical and sexual violence in these communities, especially adolescent girls. CPAoR partners in the NWSW have been reporting steady increase in SGBV involving adolescent girls within crisis context in the last 3 quarters.
