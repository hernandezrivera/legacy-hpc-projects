Project Summary

“Roots to Resilience” is a one-year project designed to enhance food security and promote sustainable livelihoods for both the host communities and Rohingya refugees in Cox’s Bazar. This initiative focuses on empowering vulnerable populations through integrated activities including DRR that improve access to nutritious food, foster economic resilience, and promote sustainable agricultural practices. Key activities such as homestead gardening, household-level poultry rearing, promoting green agriculture, mushroom cultivation, DRR awareness, nursery development, planting, and cash-for-work will be prioritized this JRP. These activities are designed to improve nutritional intake, generate income, and foster sustainable agricultural practices along with disaster risk reduction. Additionally, the project emphasizes positive coping mechanisms, a vulnerability-based program approach, and comprehensive livelihood support to build economic resilience and self-reliance.
By integrating these diverse activities including DRR awareness and actions, the project aims to create a foundation for long-term resilience and prosperity as indicated in REVA-7 and RIMA-3. Beneficiaries will gain improved access to nutritious food, preparedness to fight and cope with disaster risks, enhanced income opportunities, and strengthened community infrastructure. Overall, “Roots to Resilience” is committed to fostering inclusive and equitable support for both host communities and Rohingya refugees, ensuring a holistic approach to food security and livelihoods. Oxfam along with implementing Partner Mukti Cox’s Bazar and Prottyashi will work together for camps 03, 04, 12, 19, 22. Palongkhali and Razapalong of Ukhiya and Hnilla and Whykong of Teknaf Upazila will be covered as host communities. 18+ to 30 Aged group population (female and youth priority who are vulnerable to disaster risks) will be the prime project participants and at least 3.02% PWDs will be covered as direct project participants.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The “Roots to Resilience” project addresses several critical needs within the Cox’s Bazar host communities and Rohingya refugee populations. According to REVA-7, Over the course of seven assessments, Bangladeshi households reported a gradual decline in all indicators, with highly vulnerable households increasing to 30 percent from 13 percent in 2017. Both Rohingya and Bangladeshi households are struggling to secure sufficient food and are frequently resorting to negative coping strategies to meet their food needs. About two-thirds of households in the camps (67 percent) and half of the households in the Bangladeshi community (50 percent) relied on less preferred or less expensive food as their most frequently used coping strategy. On the other hand, RIMA-3 also highlighted that, In Rohingya camps, food security conditions significantly deteriorated, with an increase in food-insecure households from 50 to 58 percent in 2023. Around 93 percent of households adopted consumption-based coping compared to 68 percent in 2022. Reliance on less preferred food (88 percent) and borrowing food (46 percent) are the two most adopted food-based coping mechanisms. Additionally, 93 percent of households adopted livelihood-based coping, compared to 75 percent in 2022. Stress strategies (46 percent), like purchasing food on credit and borrowing money to meet essential needs, are the widely adopted livelihood-based coping strategies. The project aims to improve food security through homestead gardening, poultry rearing, and mushroom cultivation, providing fresh vegetables and protein sources. There is a significant need for income-generating opportunities to reduce poverty and enhance economic stability. The project supports livelihood activities such as green agriculture, cash-for-work interventions, and market linkages to create sustainable income streams. Tailored interventions are necessary to address the specific needs of the most vulnerable groups, including women, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities which was also highly recommended by REVA-7 and RIMA-3. The project adopts a vulnerability-based approach to ensure equitable support. The adoption of environmentally friendly and climate-resilient agricultural practices is essential to ensure long-term food production and environmental health. Training in green agriculture and sustainable farming techniques addresses this need. Many community members rely on negative coping strategies due to stress and economic hardship. The project promotes positive coping mechanisms and provides psychosocial support to improve mental well-being.Gender considerations are integral to the “Roots to Resilience” project, ensuring that both men and women benefit equitably from the interventions. The project emphasizes the skills and empowerment of women through targeted training and livelihood opportunities. Activities such as poultry rearing, homestead gardening, mushroom production, and aquaculture followed by market linkages are designed to enhance women’s economic independence and decision-making power. This proposed project provides gender-sensitive training and creates safe spaces for women to engage in agricultural and livelihood activities. The project recognizes and addresses the unique needs of women, such as access to childcare, flexible working hours, and safety concerns. These considerations are integrated into the design and implementation of all activities.Oxfam also promotes gender equality by challenging traditional gender roles and encouraging shared responsibilities in household and community activities. By addressing these identified needs and integrating gender considerations, the “Roots to Resilience” project aims to create a more inclusive, equitable, and sustainable impact on food security and livelihoods for both host communities and Rohingya refugees in Cox’s Bazar.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Protection Mainstreaming is one of the key pillars of Oxfam for Humanitarian response. Protection mainstreaming in the “Roots to Resilience” project ensures that all activities prioritize the safety, dignity, and rights of beneficiaries, particularly the most vulnerable groups. This includes creating safe and inclusive environments, promoting gender sensitivity, safeguarding children, and ensuring accountability to affected populations. By integrating Oxfam’s protection principles into every aspect of the project, we aim to foster an environment where all individuals, especially women, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities, can participate safely and with dignity. Regular training, capacity building, and monitoring will be conducted to identify and mitigate protection risks, ensuring that the project remains responsive to the needs and rights of all community members.

2.3 - Age & Disability

The project is committed to inclusivity, ensuring that the needs of all age groups and individuals with disabilities are addressed. Special considerations are made to create accessible and age-appropriate activities, ensuring that elderly participants and those with disabilities can fully engage in and benefit from the project. This includes providing necessary linkages with expert organizations and networks, mobility aids and tailored training sessions, to ensure their active participation. By fostering an inclusive environment, the project aims to empower all community members, recognizing the valuable contributions of every individual regardless of age or ability, and promoting equal opportunities for all. The project team will ensure regular home visits to ensure regular support and services are in place for the people in need which also emphasized in REVA-7.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The “Roots to Resilience” project is dedicated to ensuring accountability to the affected population by establishing transparent and responsive feedback mechanisms. Regular consultations and participatory assessments will be conducted to gather input from community members, ensuring their voices are heard and their needs are addressed. Accessible complaint and feedback systems will be put in place, allowing beneficiaries to express concerns and suggestions. This approach fosters trust and ensures that the project remains adaptive and responsive to the evolving needs of both the host communities and Rohingya refugees, ultimately enhancing the effectiveness and impact of the interventions. Apart from these, frequent HHs visits and consultations will take place by the project team and a summary will be shared with team meetings at a regular basis. Oxfam’s MEAL guidelines specifically on CFRM will be practiced properly to ensure most vulnerable and in-need persons are not left out from the project.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The proposed project integrates climate change adaptation and mitigation strategies to enhance the resilience of both the host communities and Rohingya refugees in Cox’s Bazar. Recognizing the significant impact of climate change on food security and livelihoods, the project promotes sustainable agricultural practices that improve soil health, conserve water, and reduce greenhouse gas emissions. Key activities include training farmers in climate-resilient techniques such as crop rotation, organic farming, and integrated pest management. Homestead gardening and mushroom cultivation are encouraged as they require less water and are more resilient to climate variability. Behavioral change communication (BCC) session, Farmers Field Day (FFD) will be organized at a regular basis during agricultural sessions to strengthen knowledge connectivity and practices related to climate action improved practices and results. By integrating these adaptation and mitigation measures, the “Roots to Resilience” project aims to build a sustainable and climate-resilient future for the communities in Cox’s Bazar, ensuring that they are better equipped to cope with the challenges posed by climate change.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Oxfam and Partners are jointly taking actions at a regular frequency to address emergency needs with appropriate responses. We have capacitated Emergency Response Team (ERT) and they will be in close connection with the project and take the lead to capacitate the project team from time to time. The “Roots to Resilience” project incorporates robust emergency preparedness and response strategies to ensure that both the host communities and Rohingya refugees in Cox’s Bazar are equipped to handle potential crises. This includes developing and disseminating emergency plans, conducting regular disaster preparedness training, and establishing early warning systems. During the project tenure, project participants will be capacitated on DRR initiatives. By integrating these measures, the project aims to enhance community resilience, minimize the impact of emergencies, and ensure a rapid and coordinated response to protect lives and livelihoods.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

The project places a strong emphasis on youth engagement, recognizing the vital role that young people play in building resilient and sustainable communities. The project will actively involve youth in all key activities, including homestead gardening, poultry rearing, and green agriculture, providing them with training and resources to develop valuable skills. By creating opportunities for youth to participate in cash for work programs and market linkage initiatives, the project aims to enhance their economic prospects and foster a sense of ownership and responsibility. Additionally, youth will be encouraged to take on leadership roles within their communities, promoting positive coping mechanisms and contributing to the overall resilience and sustainability of the project. Through these efforts, the “Roots to Resilience” project seeks to empower the next generation, ensuring they are equipped to drive positive change and support their communities in the face of future challenges.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

The “Roots to Resilience” project aims to establish strong development linkages within the Rohingya camps by integrating various livelihood and skills development activities that connect beneficiaries to broader economic opportunities. This includes creating market linkages for products such as handicrafts, mushrooms, and poultry, ensuring that these goods can reach local and regional markets. Food Security Sector related specific guidelines will be followed to strengthen development linkages. The project will also facilitate partnerships with local businesses, NGOs, and government agencies to provide training, resources, and support for sustainable income generation. By fostering these connections, the project not only enhances the economic resilience of the Rohingya refugees but also promotes their integration into the local economy, contributing to the overall development and stability of the region.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

This proposed project aims to strengthen development linkages within the host communities by integrating livelihood and skills development activities that connect beneficiaries to broader economic opportunities. This includes facilitating market access for products such as vegetables, poultry, and handicrafts, ensuring that these goods can reach local and regional markets. The project will also establish alignment and linkages with local businesses, the food security sector (FSS), NGOs, and government agencies to provide training, resources, and support for sustainable income generation. By fostering these connections, the project enhances the economic resilience of the host communities, promotes local economic growth, and ensures that the benefits of development are widely shared, contributing to the overall stability and prosperity of the region.
