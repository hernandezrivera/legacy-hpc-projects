Project Summary

This project aims to ease and improve access to water, sanitation and hygiene facilities in most affected municipalities hosting refugees in East and Adamawa regions of Cameroon. Those municipalities are Garoua Boulaï, Mandjou, Kette, Ndelele, Kentzou, Betare Oya, Ngoura in East and Ngaoui, Djohong in Adamawa.
About drinking water facilities, construction of 10 smalls water supply network powered with solar energy will be prioritized in host community including Borgop and Ngam sites. In refugees sites (Gado, Lolo, Mbile, Timangolo) where there are already a drinking water network, a focus will be made on the reinforcement of water production through construction/transformation of 05 boreholes with a minimum flow of five cubic meters to autonomous water point
On hygiene and sanitation issues, CLTS will be implemented in host communities as well as construction of 24 gender sensitives latrines in school and health centers hosting CAR refugees. But in refugees sites, a support to construction of 100 sustainable latrines will be made.
30,000 people will benefit from the provision of a minimum of drinking water, 12,000 people with access to sanitation facilities in schools, health centers and communities, 40,000 people (50 % will be CAR refugees) reached with critical wash supply and services including hygiene menstruation management.
This project will also include a component relative to capacity building of WASH actor at regional level on key issues such as design, installation and maintenance of solar system for water supply, and gender mainstreaming in WASH sector.

1 - Besoins / Needs

In the CAR refugee sites of the eastern and Adamawa region (Timangolo, Lolo, Mbile, N’Garissingo Gado and Ngam) access to water, hygiene and sanitation services is still below standards. Indeed, following the efforts made by UNHCR, UNICEF and their partners since the creation of the sites in 2014, access to wash facilities is still below humanitarian reference. In site people do not have a minimum of 15 liters of drinking water per day and the number of people per latrine is more than 20. Overall, the number of WASH facilities (water points, latrines, showers, garbage pits) remains insufficient and half of the existing latrines and showers need to be rebuilt with more durable materials. Added to this is poor hygiene practice which can increase the risk of spreading water and sanitation-related diseases. This situation of poor access to WASH services is exacerbated by the many humanitarian crises affecting the country. Beyond the refugee situation, the structural situation of basic equipment and practices, particularly in terms of hygiene and sanitation, in the eastern regions of Adamawa is historically poor. The additional pressure from refugees in the area further aggravates this situation and has also exacerbated the needs of the local populations. Insufficient investments and low allocation of the state budget in the water and sanitation sector, the absence and/or non-implementation of policies, laws and strategies, poverty and Galloping demographics are aggravating factors. Although in the Adamawa region the practice of open defecation is not a major problem with an open defecation rate of 2.5%, it has a significant impact in the eastern region with 6.5% open defecation rate (EDS 2018).Community-Led Total Sanitation activities have been carried out in some localities in the Eastern region. However, some factors did not make it easy to achieve results. These factors include the weight of cultural constraints, the mobility of populations and the low involvement of CTDs. But this approach remain the best to reach sustainable end open defecation status at low cost.
