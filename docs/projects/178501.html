Project Summary

UNHCR as a lead agency coordinating the NFI responses in Syria and from Gaziantep (Turkey), will provide through this project the lifesaving and life sustaining NFI support, through distribution of NFIs and seasonal supplementary items to IDPs, returnees and affected host communities to high return areas and locations in need as identified by the Sector. Resources permit, UNHCR also acts as the provider of last resort for the Sector.
The project is in line with both the priorities of the HRP and Sector Strategy and the project will be completed in line with Sector guidance. UNHCR’s NFI programme recognizes the different needs and vulnerabilities of boys, girls, men, and women of different ages and strives to address the specific needs of each group through a range of NFI interventions. Emergency NFI response will be provided through inter-agency convoy deliveries to hard-to-reach locations and through distribution of seasonal and complementary items.
This project aims to assist some 2.4 million individuals with emergency NFIs kits and seasonal NFI kits (winter/summer items). The response will be implemented from Turkey and from inside Syria.
The overall project targets for two types of assistance package, however a portion of beneficiaries will receive both types of kits, depending on their needs and the season in which assistance is provided. NFI kits will be provided to IDPs, returnee families and host communities, in line with vulnerability criteria. A standard winter kit will be provided as one-time distribution in addition to the NFIs.

4 - Needs

According to the 2020 Multi-Sector Needs Analysis (MSNA), 76 per cent of respondents reported being unable to meet basic needs. Food security surpassed livelihoods and became the first priority with a substantial increase of 15 per cent to 29 per cent in 2020. This indicates a drastic increase in the economic vulnerability in the country. Provision of non-food items remains one of the most effective response for newly displaced population, as well as newly returned IDPs and refugees, especially taking into account the possible lack of access to market and supply in the place of displacement or return. UNHCR’s NFI response will focus on newly displaced populations as identified by the inter-agency assessments, such as rural areas of Aleppo, newly accessible areas of Idlib, underserved areas in Rural Damascus, as well as camps in North East Syria, areas hosting high number of new returns, as well as persons of concern in camps and settlements in North East Syria (NES).

5 - Implementation of activities

Resources permit, UNHCR plans for the provision of regular and seasonal core relief items for over 450,000 households. A core relief items kit includes blankets, mattresses, sleeping mats, plastic sheets, hygiene kits and solar lamps amongst others. Vulnerable groups such as children, women, the disabled and older people will be prioritized. Beneficiaries are selected based on the degree of eligibility and vulnerability, with priority given to newly displaced, IDPs living in hard to reach locations and returnees. Within these population groups most vulnerable members like female headed households, persons with disabilities, and elderly persons at risk require core assistance, supplementary and seasonal assistance. UNHCR continues to maintain an emergency stockpile inside Syria, including tents, emergency shelter kits and core relief items. Procurement will be conducted directly by UNHCR and distribution will be carried out through partners. Identification of the beneficiaries is conducted by UNHCR Field Offices in close coordination with authorities and sector partners to minimize gaps and avoid duplication. UNHCR’s partners and communities play a critical role in the identification and distribution phases. For emergency response, blanket distribution will be conducted to cover all newly displaced households. For return areas, distribution will target newly returned households. For winterization, selection criteria is aligned with the Sector criteria, mainly focusing on protection vulnerabilities. Based on the needs assessments, the annual NFIs procurement and distribution plan for each Governorate is prepared and adjusted in line with developments of situation in the field.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Increased community tension due to the assistance, and/or unclear beneficiary selection criteria leading to perceived discrimination. Mitigating measures: Provision of standardized assistance packages with guidelines followed by all sector partners. Harmonized and transparent selection criteria used by all partners. Awareness raising conducted with communities. Regular monitoring, consultation, post distribution monitoring and adjustments to the program to prevent and manage any tensions which may arise. Risk 2: Interference with beneficiary selection may lead to potential fraud and aid diversion resulting in financial or reputational risks. Mitigating measures: Capacity building of partners to improve vulnerability assessments in accordance with AGD. Advocate with relevant authorities and partners to apply shelter sector criteria implementation in beneficiary selection process. Emphasize importance of impartiality to partners and systematically monitor adherence and feedback from beneficiaries, with corresponding corrective measures. Establish an active and informed complaint mechanism.

UNHCR is co-leading the PSEA Working Group in Syria. 8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Limited ability to conduct monitoring. Mitigating measures: Advocacy with authorities for access to beneficiary data. Strengthen risk based monitoring tools and establishing remote/third party monitoring arrangements where UNHCR does not have direct access. Strengthen communication with communities and feedback mechanisms. Risk 2: Lost and damage of items in warehouse and during distribution Mitigating measures: Ensure timely coordination to minimize the period of items stored in partner warehouse. Training and monitoring on warehouse management of partners Strengthen partner distribution management through SOPs and trainings Risk 3: Interference with beneficiary selection. Mitigating measures: Provision of standardized assistance packages. Harmonized and transparent selection criteria. Awareness raising with communities. Regular monitoring, consultation, post distribution monitoring.Risk 4: Planned activities obstructed by the volatile economic situation, such as price inflation, currency depreciation and shortage of supply. Mitigating measures: Early release of resources to partners to allow for more time for implementation. Flexibilities in planning and implementation in response to market changes, such as allowing for increased price and reduced target. Risk 5: Continued pandemic and related restrictive measures. Mitigating measures: Advance procurement to ensure sufficient stock to ensure emergency preparedness. Awareness raising and provision of PPEs to partners and communities.Risk 6: Security risks which impede access. Mitigating measures: Close coordination with UNDSS to obtain updated information. Senior level advocacy to ensure humanitarian space is maintained.
