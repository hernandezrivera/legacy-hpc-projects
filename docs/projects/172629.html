Project Summary

The Humanitarian WASH response project will address threats to human life such as potential flooding and associated epidemics in addition to threats associated with drought situation. The interventions will centre on enhanced access to safe water as well as improved access to critical WASH related Hygiene infrastructure and practices.

1 - Activities

All the targeted districts fall in the severe and extreme severity ranking categories. The activities to be undertaken by the project cut across all the targeted districts and are as follows: 1. People affected by a humanitarian crisis have access to safe water of appropriate quantity to fulfill basic needs (mainstreaming GBV, disability and PSEA).• Water point assessments • Repair of boreholes and their headworks• Refresher Training for Water Point User Committees (WPCs).• Training and equipping Village Pump Mechanics (VPMs).• Rehabilitation of Solar Powered Piped Water Schemes• Piped Water Scheme Committees training • Procurement of water quality monitoring kits and consumables.• Water quality monitoring2. People affected by a humanitarian crisis have access to critical WASH related Hygiene kits and improved awareness of safe hygiene and sanitation practices.• Procurement and distribution of kits (buckets, jerry cans, soap, water treatment chemicals, IEC material and menstrual hygiene management materials).• Conducting of post distribution monitoring to determine effectiveness of the distributed material. • Establishing of hand washing stations (HWS)at public places to increase positive handwashing practice.• Conducting of trainings on “hygiene education; disease control & prevention; Accountability to Affected Population (AAP); Gender Based Violence (GBV); Prevention of Sexual Exploitation & Abuse (PSEA) targeting the relevant stakeholders who include District Water and Sanitation Sub-Committees (DWSSCs), Environmental Health Technicians (EHTs), Village Health Workers (VHWs), School Health Teachers (SHTs), School Health Clubs (SHCs), Community Health Clubs (CHCs) and volunteers in communities.• Support for the revival of school and community health clubs• Conducting forum theatre performances at public places.

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

Throughout the project AAP will be mainstreamed into the activities in a manner that ensures the affected people are at the center of our work, and this will be achieved by ensuring the following; 1. Communities participate in the project in a safe and appropriate manner that ensures equal opportunities for all including the vulnerable. 2. Communication and information with the affected populations will be shared in a two-way communication channel that ensures the targeted communities are aware of their rights in the project and the obligations of the Partner while working in their community. Community dialogues and inception meetings from District level down to ward level and even village level as the project progresses will be some of the tools to be utilised. 3. Feedback and complaint mechanisms will be established to get feedback on experiences and perspectives of the project implementation. Mechanisms such as help desks, suggestion boxes and feedback sessions will be implemented community level. All feedback received will be recorded and addressed in a manner that also safeguards the communities. 4. The Partner will also work with other existing WASH partners in the Districts to ensure that communities are not overburdened, and efforts are also not duplicated.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

CC ensures that all WASH projects are environmentally and socially sound, and would not lead to any environmental and social negative effect at current time as well as in the future. Thus, CC in the area of operation will screen for social and environmental risks to ensure these are adequately mitigated. Positive impacts of WASH intervention can be obvious through the improved quantity and quality of safe drinking water, reduction in water related diseases such as diarrhea, dysentery, cholera, typhoid and thereby minimizing the cost of healthcare in households, reduction in infant, child and maternal mortality and morbidity due to improved health and sanitation services, reduced distances to water points which will lead to gains in productive time for women and girls. However, where risks are noted including potential risks from natural hazards these will be embedded into the water safety and security planning component. Christian Care will also do the following; • monitor the quality of water on a quarterly basis in the targeted areas;• follow guidelines from Ministry of Health when constructing any sanitation facilities to avoid contaminating underground water; • Ensuring adequate drainage including where handwashing stations are installed.
