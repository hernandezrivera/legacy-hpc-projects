Project Summary

The proposed project is in line with the education cluster HNO strategic objectives 1, 2, and 3, and is aligned with the MoE Strategy and Plan in adopting the blended learning modality in education, as response to the COVID- 19 crises. The project aims to ensure that boys and girls have equitable safe access to inclusive and quality educational services in vulnerable MoE schools in the Gaza Strip (East Khanyounis, Rafah, Gaza, and North Gaza). The project fits under a multi-year NRC education intervention applying, of providing protection of education and psychosocial support and co-leading the EiE Strategic Advisory Group in Gaza. The project- is intended to focus on the following approaches: i) Implementation of the Psycho-Educational “Better Learning Program” (BLP), reinforcing the system through training new cohort BLP Master Trainers at General Directors, subject supervisors; Capacity building of teaching and non-teaching staff in MoE schools; provision of emergency kits to the schools; awareness sessions for parents; and provision of a “Supporting the Supporter” package for teaching and Non-teaching staff in need; ii) Strengthen the best practices in education contingency and response plans at school level; establish emergency team in each target school; linkage target schools with emergency actors in Gaza; capacity building of emergency team on best practices during, within and before the emergency; revise and update the emergency plan in target schools in line with the specificity of each target school. iii) Carry out remedial/catch up classes for children not active in virtual classes and who are stuck in disrupted academic functioning; improving access of children to distance learning platforms through providing tablets to specific group of children; printing self- learning materials; rehab. schools’ ICT infrastructure. iv) Provision of technical support to education sub-cluster in Gaza through the continuation of co-leading the EiE Strategic Advisory Group to steer the EiE priorities. v) conduct situational analysis on OOSC needs, gaps and priorities, as well as contributing in enhancing their access to quality basic education. vi) Conduct a situational analysis on adolescent/youth needs and priorities, in addition to contributing in enhancing vulnerable youths’ empowerment and skills, capacity and resilience development.

The action is also contributing in improving both skills and knowledge of teaching and non-teaching staff on PSEA principles, and reporting mechanisms on any violation against the main principles. Teaching and non-teaching staff will be benefited from a tailored training on PSEA principles, and received a well-prepared leaflets and printed materials linked to the principles.

1 - Needs

Approximately 600,000 Palestinians (56 per cent females) across the oPt, including around 13,400 Children with Disabilities (CWDs), need humanitarian assistance to access education in 2022. Nearly 74 per cent live in the Gaza Strip and over 26 per cent in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem. Among the longstanding issues affecting access to education include insufficient and/or inadequate safe school infrastructure. The Gaza Strip is most affected with around 65 per cent of schools operating on a double-shift system. In 2020- 2021, these challenges were compounded by successive COVID-19 pandemic surges, leading to prolonged closures of schools and kindergartens, as part of the measures adopted to contain the pandemic (HNO 2022). The recent escalation in Gaza in May 2021 degenerated the education situation leading to the early termination of the school year: Accordingly, 1.3 million children in the oPt lost at least three months of schooling and most have not been able to engage in summer or extracurricular activities. While the Ministry of Education (MoE) and UNRWA put in place various distance learning programmes to mitigate the effect of COVID-19 to learning, these were not fully accessible to many children living in homes without internet connectivity, or who could not collect the printed self-learning materials being distributed by schools. Many children with disabilities resource room students, and children in preschools were excluded from online education. In June 2021, NRC education assessed 100 PA schools for the purpose of identifying the most vulnerable ones for emergency response. The assessment confirmed that Covid-19 is one of the main factors impacting students’ psychological well-being. 99% of school reported having people infected with Covid-19 in their schools, and this led to depression, stress, anxiety, violence and low academic achievement. In addition, the assessment confirmed a number of factors leading to distress and fears including the household environment that substantially affected students’ well-being. Teachers and counsellors lacking the skills and response methodology to identify and responding to children requiring support among others. In September 2020, a rapid assessment through phone survey was conducted across the Gaza Strip on 30 teachers from 30 schools out of the 118 targeted in the ongoing BLP program. The findings indicate that: 81%of teachers are still in need capacity building particularly skills for in running the virtual classes and managing the lessons content. 60%of teachers confirmed not to have received the MoE led training in virtual classes. Only 21%of students are active in virtual classes a factor; attributed to the lack of ICT equipment. 90%of teachers confirmed that, not satisfied by students’ participation in virtual classes, with 100%of teachers confirming a lack of appropriate skills and techniques for improving and evaluating students’ participation in virtual classes. From the foregoing assessment as well as the evidence from the education cluster and NRC experience there is a need for a clearer and improved technical capacity and leadership in designing and rolling-out the distance-learning scheme, including catch-up classes for those at high risk of dropout. Support is particularly required in regards to content, pedagogical approach as well as tools and materials for education professionals and students. At household level, material support (ICT) to families with school-aged children who have limited access to distance learning possibilities is a must. According to Multi- Sectorial Needs Assessment- MMSNA conducted by REACH in September 2021, 88% of Gaza strip Households with school-aged children reported a higher need for catch-up learning programs because of the twin loss of learning resulting from COVID-19 and May escalation schools' closures. In addition, 5% of HHs with school-aged children reportedly a child dropout of school during the pandemic year. The latest escalation imposed additional challenges to the education sector. 31 percent of the HHs interviewed reported having trouble meeting essential education needs (such as tuition fees, books) in the last 30 days. NRC's direct work and communications with more than 100 schools in the Gaza Strip, will guide the implementation of this project. NRC has experience in the most excluded areas of the Gaza Strip has identified the challenges and barriers to children access to education system. In addition NRC has identified teachers’ needs and support required to lead the bended education process, including psychosocial support, and leaning loss.

2 - Activities

NRC has a MoU with the MoEHE for collaboration and engagement across the West Bank and Gaza. A Framework Agreement has been drafted between NRC and MoEHE outlining an overall commitment to ensure the adequate implementation of the project. The activities are clustered in five main areas based on the findings of vulnerability/ needs assessment. The needs assessment will target all target schools, community; parents, children, teaching and non-teaching staff and school management. The local community represented by each target schools Parents Council will be also engaged in the assessment. 1) Structured and unstructured Psychosocial Support. 100 MoE schools' benefit from PSS training, delivered by well-trained teachers and counsellors at school level. -Based on the assessment results, the schools will be provided with emergency educational kits, in line with the approved MoEHE SoPs, which may include some of the following items: stationery, educational games and educational PSS aid kits. The kits’ items and materials will be identified to facilitate the implementation of BLP, and to ensure delivering the BLP in a high-quality manner. To assure sharing the experiences among teachers, NRC in parallel will provide a ToT to 10(4F) MoEHE Subject Supervisors and Head of Department to become BLP Master Trainers. These master trainers will support the process of supervision and coaching of teachers during the implementation. 339 (200F) teachers benefit from BLP1 training- Increasing Learning Capacity among pupils in Ongoing Crisis' and BLP2 training - Group Sessions Improving Study Skills and 2,325(1,375F) teachers in target schools' benefit from the BLP cascading delivered to the remaining teachers at school level. 113(70F) counsellors benefit from BLP3 training `Fighting Nightmares and sleeping problems. - 1,130(670F) students benefit from BLP2 sessions led by teachers under the supervision of NRC education team., 904 (535F) students benefit from the group and individual sessions of BLP3 delivered by trained counsellors, and 15,375 (9,031F) students benefit from BLP1 group sessions. Before the implementation of PSS with children, 292 (173F) teaching and non- teaching staff will benefit from a structured PSS (support supporter) through the GCMHP (local partner). 2,260 (1,506F) parents benefit from awareness sessions on child protection and community engagement by teachers in collaboration with NRC staff. 2) Community- based Remedial classes: During the implementation of BLP sessions, and in collaboration with school management, NRC education team and teachers will identify students of low academic achievement. The process of identification will base on students’ grades, and both teachers, and counsellors' consultation. As a result of the identification process, students will be referred to 4 CBOs (service contracted CBOs) according to their living areas for getting benefit from after school remedial classes. NRC will deliver a structured training to the CBOs education staff on remedial classes, and other relevant topic required. The remedial classes will focus on the main basic skills of both Math and Arabic, as well as will be following MoE SoPs and protocols. (Students from grade 4-8 in each target schools will benefit from the remedial classes delivered by the education staff of CBOs inside, and under the supervision of MoE and NRC education staff. In collaboration with local partners, 10 most vulnerable children from remedial/catch up classes in each target school identified according to Education cluster launched criteria get benefit from tablets distribution for improving their access to distance learning classes, and the rest of students will get benefit from the printed MoE’s self-learning materials/cards. Moreover, due to the needs assessment findings, NRC will support the ICT environment of target schools by providing laptops and computer facilities based on needs. NRC will continue engaging with the community; parents and parents council and schools' representatives by sharing with them the progress made by the students, and collaboratively agree on steps forwards. 3) Contingency Planning: part of the needs assessment exercise is prepared to figure out the level of target schools preparedness planning against emergencies and crises. Accordingly, NRC will work on supporting the - establishment of most vulnerable schools Emergency response committees; (3 members: 1 teacher, 1 school mgt., 1 school counsellor), as well as building the capacities of school’s emergency teams for possible contingency plan implementation. Part of the contingency planning is Supporting schools in carrying out simulation and 1 school-based campaign exercises based on each school needs and updated contingency/emergency plan. 4) Continue work in co-leading the Strategic Advisory Group and support education sub-cluster in Gaza in steering the priorities of EIE. NRC will support in the coordination and contribute in developing and adjusting the strategic framework, priorities and work plan of the Cluster. 5) Enhancing vulnerable children and youths' access to quality basic-level education in a safe, inclusive and protective environment. NRC intends to conduct a situational analysis on out of school children, Youth/ adolescents under this action. The situational analysis aims at responding effectively to OOSc and youth needs and priorities lately, as expressed and as supported by the findings of the situational analysis.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

NRC recognises that safeguarding of children and vulnerable adults engaged in their programmes is a fundamental responsibility. All staff must sign and are trained on NRC’s Code of Conduct, and are required to sign and be trained on the organisation’s Child Safeguarding Policy. The training for Education staff and staff with regular contact with children includes content on child rights, understanding child abuse and sexual abuse, reviews behaviour and conduct required from staff and how to receive disclosures and report and refer cases. BLP content is also designed to promote safe and inclusive learning environments for children and should support staff and teachers’ understanding of child protection. NRC Action trusts working on incorporating protection principles and prompting meaningful access, safety, and dignity in the proposed humanitarian aid. At the very beginning of the action in the needs assessment phase, the NRC will identify the people at risk, kind of risks they face and expected to face, and why they face such kind of risks, as well as activating a structured referral system who people who seek more help and support. The NRC action follows the participatory approach, where beneficiaries will be included from the early beginning of the action, participating in the needs assessment, monitoring and evaluation phase, and in identifying the area of improvement. Moreover, allbeneficiaries will have the same chance of receiving the service (e.g. school children will receive the service at a voluntary manner and with a respected informed consent from their parents). Both teachers, and counselors as a main beneficiaries in NRC action will participate in a voluntary manner, and due to their interest. A follow up mechanism will be shared with all NRC suggested beneficiaries, where open channels of communication will be offered by NRC (e.g. Generic emails, field visits etc) with all beneficiaries that guarantee the confidentiality.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The NRC action focuses on three dimensions of intervention: i) a general class room based intervention approach targeting all children in target schools. Its suitable for all children in any context, as it is designed to reach large numbers of children through classroom delivery. This dimension is designed to ensure and guarantee equal chances of access for all children both males and females and their specific needs. ii) The second dimension offers a special kind of support to children who need additional support, to support resilience among them for the early recovery or protracted displacement when conditions for learning have stabilized. iii) The third dimension is a specialised clinical one that is designed to address nightmares, which many children experience as a chronic symptoms of traumatic stress. The equal chances of both males and females, boys and girls, children with or without special needs are affordable based on the real needs, beneficiaries interest and potential. As well as without any kind of discrepancies among the beneficiaries either they are children or adult. The NRC action welcomes all children being part of the service delivered regardless of the differences among them based on inclusion approach. For the inclusive education and contingency plans component and catch up classes, the project will target the most excluded children (boys and girls) at school level. The project team will work at directorate and school level, to define the criteria for those children involved and benefited by the project
