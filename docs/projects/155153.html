Needs

Insecurity in Rakhine, Myanmar has led over half a million Rohingya refugees to cross the border into Bangladesh since violence erupted in Rakhine State in late August 2017. As of 26 April 2018, an estimated 898,300 Rohingya refugees were living in Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh, with 687,000 having arrived since 25 August 2017 (IOM, 2018). RI carried out two rapid surveys in December 2017, and a multi-sector assessment in April 2018. RI's April 2018 assessment was carried out in the proposed areas of Nuralipara, Leda, and Shah Parir Dwip and focused on access to protection and health services. There is a major need for mental and psychosocial health service integration. The population has been traumatized by violence. The lack of physical space is affecting GBV response and case management. Complicating these protection concerns is the lack of information on available services being provided by humanitarian organizations and other actors. Across all sites, even in makeshift settlements where agencies are actively responding to basic needs, many new arrivals have little knowledge of how to access services and are unaware of referral pathways. In RI's April 2018 assessment, respondents described access to mental and psychosocial counseling services and GBV treatment and services as “difficult” or “very difficult” (43% and 52% of respondents, respectively). Respondents, most of whom were women (98%), identified distance to services, the limited number of female providers and fears of providers “misbehaving” and mistreating patients as significant barriers to access.

Activities or outputs

(1) Establish a “one stop shop” to address the immediate health needs of GBV survivors: RI will provide GBV survivors with psychological first aid, psychosocial support, and counselling, and will have a designated medical doctor embedded onto its health team who will be responsible for providing clinical management of rape (CMR). RI's will comply with principles on care practice and client sensitivity and ensure GBV survivors have a safe, confidential area for treatment and counselling. RI will have a Case Management Officer who will manage cases in one privacy room adjacent to the health facilitites. He/she will provide GBV survivors with psychological first aid, psychosocial support, counselling, and referrals when necessary. RI will also establish a private breastfeeding corner/counseling room for women at health facilities. (2) Support community awareness raising campaigns and community-based protection: RI will organize and facilitate groups of women and girls to be able to discuss and share their knowledge and ideas on women related issues, such as GBV concepts, violence against women and services to support GBV survivors. At the same time, RI will link women to support each other by establish and strengthen women support network. Finally, RI will engage men & boys to promote women right and protect women and girl from gender-based violence. RI recruit men & boys group facilitator and GBV community mobilizer focusing on collaborate with men community leader (Majhi, religion leader, teacher, ...etc) and men and boys to mobilize the group discussions on position theme to promote friendly environment to entire communities. Activities will engage men and boys to lead the prevention of communities violence and disseminate positive messaging through men-to-men. RI staffing; 1 protection manager who will oversee all program, 1 GBV coordinator who is main focal person for coordination between GBV case management staffs, GBV prevention and awareness, medical team. We will have 5 case workers, 2 psychologists, one GBV officer and two GBV community mobilizers. This is going to have total 40 protection volunteers in all area. Gender mainstreaming was incorporated into RI management. RI maintained a gender balance in the recruitment of national staffs and camp-based volunteers. RI ensures non-discrimination and meaningful access to our services; gender and vulnerable inclusion. With current funding from UNHCR, RI is currently providing case management and community-based child protection services to vulnerable boys and girls. RI is also actively involved with child protection case management, which includes the identification of Children at Risk or Survivors of Violence; the identification, registration, and documentation unaccompanied and separated children; and the assessment of child needs and appropriated referral based on child’s best of interest, client’s center approach and informed consent. RI is also offering case management services to GBV survivors, providing basic psychosocial support to vulnerable women and girls and referring GBV survivors to RI case management and other appropriated services eg., health center, safe shelter, legal assistance center. RI's GBV team will work with other sectors (Health, Nutrition) to ensure its staff are trained on protection mainstreaming, case identification, and referral pathways to ensure protection risks are taken into account throughout implementation. RI's Protection Team will also be involved with the design of other programs (e.g. they will be consulted during site identification, and health clinic set up to ensure any protection risks can be addressed and mitigated). All new RI staff and camp based volunteers are oriented on protection, gender, GBV, and PSEA awareness. RI currently supports project monitoring and reporting, and includes age and gender disaggregation in data collection.
