Project Summary

Care Plus Foundation (CPF) has proposed a project which endeavors to improve Learning Environment through Provision of Integrated services in Schools to Promote Quality Education. The project aims at promotion of Access to Basic, Quality, Protective and Inclusive Education to vulnerable learners, Out of School Children and Youths will be focused in Northern Bahr El Ghazal State. The project will equally support teachers and PTA members. The programme will seek to improve the access to quality education among the crisis affected children and youths as an effort to promote sustainable basic quality education with incentivized enrollment and attendance to learning, reduce risks of cognitive underdevelopment and malnutrition, and provide access to safe and protective learning environments. The response will focus at increasing enrolment and retaining children in Pre-primary, Primary and secondary schools/learning centers, establish temporary learning spaces and classrooms, rehabilitation of temporary learning spaces and classrooms, provision of young children with ECD kits, establishing gender segregated latrines and water points and hygiene promotion, rehabilitation of gender segregated latrines and water points and hygiene promotion, procuring and supplying Sanitation and Hygiene materials (hand washing soap, sanitizers, masks, etc.) to students, procuring and supplying sanitary and hygiene materials (hand washing soap, sanitizers, masks, etc.) to teachers, provision of emergency-affected children with quality education recreational materials, training of teachers in EiE pedagogical skills and Child-centred methodologies to improve teaching -learning process for emergency-affected children, training and updating teachers and learners on PSS and the impact of COVID-19 and its protection mechanisms, provision of life-skills and basic vocational training to youth and adolescents, training of teachers and education personnel on referral pathways on protection/GBV, COVID-19 and health and nutrition, training of SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management and governance, psychosocial support (PSS) and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), printing and distribution of appropriate Information, Education and Communication (IEC) materials (i.e. posters, leaflets) on COVID 19 prevention and mitigation measures, organizing back to learning campaigns while integrating with already existing projects in intra-sectors (especially Health, Nutrition, FSL, WASH, and Protection). The project equally hopes to enhance psychosocial development of learners besides improving hygiene and sanitation. The project will use its existing M&E system to monitor report, feedback, document, and share evident lessons. The project is also designed to ensure maximum impact is achieved through the proposed interventions besides ensuring accountability to the affected population. The project intends to hold periodic stakeholders/ community representatives’ consultative meetings for enhanced engagements to enhance accountability to the affected population.
The project will ensure that it eliminates the issues of sexual exploitation by enhancing the adoption of code of conduct. The project will develop a feedback/ complain mechanism system which will include suggestion boxes, quarterly briefs forums with the stakeholders and reporting desks in specific locations. Risk analysis will be conducted to identify and mitigate potential risks. Mapping of the targets will be conducted to eliminate potential barriers to access. The project will ensure that there is enhanced protection of learners and reduced inequalities while ensuring equity. Gender aspects will be considered in all through the programme by ensuring that the target has a gender segregated approach and gender sensitive.

1 - Needs

According to the South Sudan HNO 2022, Humanitarian access to essential services, including health care, education, water and sanitation, as well as protection and legal services, remains a challenge in an already complex context. Between January and December 2021, 591 reported humanitarian access incidents were recorded. These ranged from violence against humanitarian personnel and assets to operational interference. In the wake of persistent fragility and economic crisis, an estimated 3.4 million (51 per cent girls) school-aged children have lack of or inadequate access to education services in 2021. This is a slight increase from the 3.1 million girls and boys in need in 2020. In addition, there are 98,500 school-aged refugee children, of whom 18,000 children are out of school. The negative impact of COVID-19 and subsequent school closures in 2020 and part of 2021 have resulted in an increased number of children in need of education support. In 2022, 3.6 million people, 97 per cent of whom are children, are estimated to need education services. Following COVID- 19, the closure of schools and broader socio-economic hardship exacerbated existing education challenges and inequities. In addition, about 139,000 refugee children also need education support. Prior to COVID-19 school closures, about 2.2 million children were already out of school. The school closures further impacted the education of over 2.7 million additional children. In May 2021, after 14 months of closure due to COVID-19, schools in South Sudan re-opened. The current estimated number of out of school children is 2.8 million. Additionally, gross enrolment decreased by roughly 7 per cent between 2020 (2,727,209) and 2021 (2,523,204), (HNO, 2022).There are multiple reasons for the worsening education situation, including displacement and wide spread conflict, poverty, damaged and inadequate numbers of classrooms, shortage of teachers (especially female teachers) and relevant learning and teaching resources, cultural norms which deprioritize education for girls and long distances to schools for many children. (OOSC Report, 2018). According to the 2018 education cluster assessment findings, it was evident that nationally, the biggest support required by primary schools is teaching and learning supplies (46.2%), this is followed by school feeding (44.5%), teacher salaries (43.7%), rehabilitation of infrastructure (35.2%) and teacher training (23.2%) (ECA, 2018). An estimated 3.6 million girls and boys between ages 3 and 17 in conflict- and crisis-affected areas are estimated to have inadequate or no access to education in 2022, including more than 125,000 teachers and members of school management committees requiring humanitarian assistance. In addition, some 139,000 refugee children will need education support in 2022, (HNO, 2022). Girls are more likely to be excluded from receiving education as parents prefer to send boys to school. This increases the risk of girls ending up in an early marriage. In 2019, about 17 per cent of girls were enrolled in pre-primary, 58 per cent in primary and only 25 per cent of girls received a secondary level education, (FSNMS, 2020). The Impact of Covid 19 on Schools unpublished report indicates that besides the economic barriers such as lose of source of livelihood, leaners enrolments and retention has been affected by the following barriers: lack of materials 22%, lack of books 21%, early pregnancy 27%, early marriage 5%, floods 3%, school fees 16% and Uniform 7%. Additionally the pandemic as affected the academic achievements of leaners as stipulated: learning time loss 86.5%, mental problems 42.2%, protection issues 37.9% and nutrition problems at 23%. The risk of Ebola Virus and COVID 19 Pandemic continues to give a serious threat to the school calendar calling for efforts to sustain education in schools in a safe manor.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will develop a feedback/ complain mechanism system which will include suggestion boxes, quarterly briefs forums with the stakeholders and reporting desks in specific locations. This system will provide an opportunity for the community, leaders and the beneficiaries to be able to launch any form of complains besides voicing their opinions, perceptions and ideas to the project steering committee. The project will develop a stakeholders forum in which the community and the project steering committee will be meeting to discuss the progress, this forum will be essential in ensuring that community participation and engagement is attained and that there perspectives on the project is considered, this forum will also be used to select a few stakeholders who will occasionally be involved in joint monitoring of the project and report back to the forum.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Actively seek the views of affected populations to improve policy and practice in programming, ensuring that feedback and complaints mechanisms are streamlined, appropriate and robust enough to deal with (communicate, receive, process, respond to and learn from) complaints about breaches in policy and stakeholder dissatisfaction. For example, a complaints and feedback suggestion boxes will be placed at the school and in strategic public places identified and agreed by the community. Weekly, the project team will open and collect any feedback and complaints deposited in the suggestion boxes. A log of all feedback received will be kept and at the end of every month analysis of the log sheet is done to inform project modifications.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

In order to ensure that the project eliminates the issues of sexual exploitation, the project will be guided by the organization code of conduct that stipulates on the guidelines and measures of how to deter and handle sexual exploitation cases and abuse. The project will also use it's internal mechanism of PSEA reporting to ensure that all the cases are reported and appropriate actions are taken as guided by the policy.The project will conduct an inception meeting with all the stakeholders where the PSEA policy will be read and signed by key stakeholders. A PSEA focal point will be selected among the beneficiaries, trained properly on PSEA concept and assigned the role of receiving complaints related to sexual exploitation. We will also provide our office contacts to the beneficiaries relating to PSEA case reporting

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Enable affected populations in all categories including individuals living with disability to play an active role in the decision-making processes that affect them through the establishment of clear guidelines and practices to engage them appropriately and ensure that the most marginalized and affected are represented and have influence.
