Project Summary

Partners for Sustainable Development seeks to establish a project that will provide catch-up learning and support education for children who face learning loss. This will be done by hiring 100 assistant teachers that will provide support in the children’s classes with their main teachers and for the children with the most need for support they will be taken one hour after school when it is possible or in the nearest community-based organization twice a week to compensate the loss in learning and to build their skills that should have been established during the past three years. This occurred due to the school closures that were imposed due to COVID-19 during 2020 and part of 2021. In addition to the unexpected teachers' strikes that occurred during the 2022 school year, the occupation and its continuous attacks in and around schools contributed greatly to the disruption of students’ academic path. Therefore, in order to address the loss incurred, particularly in school aged children from the most vulnerable areas in the West Bank and in the most vulnerable ages, PSD will focus on the second to the fifth graders, who were the most affected ages by the lockdown and experienced disruption in learning.
The project intends to make in-school assistance a priority, meaning 100 teachers will be assigned to 50 schools, 50 of them math teachers and 50 Arabic teachers. The teachers will be assigned to schools in the most vulnerable areas in the West Bank, specifically Area C, H2 in Hebron, Area B, and a few schools in Area A (those that most need the additional assistance) will also be added. An evaluation will be carried out to choose the schools in most need of catch-up education. Each teacher will work with 40 students, around four hours per day, at an estimated 15 days per month. The project intends to target students in the third and fourth semesters of the school year, this will include a summer program for 3-4 weeks, and will resume in the first and second semesters in the following school year. The hired teachers will work with teachers already present at the schools and in the targeted classes, they will coordinate with them, the school administration and the parents in order to get a better understanding of student needs.
Based on learning, impact and assessment from a running project working with schools (Shababek). PSD has identified how many students at each school will be chosen to receive the extra assistance. The results indicate that out of the 100 schools that PSD is currently working with, 2,213 school-aged male students and 1,632 school-aged female students need catch-up and support education. Based on these results a catch-up program will be designed, this will include the number of children that PSD will be working within each school, the type of assistance they need in the core subjects mentioned and if any additional assistance will be needed. The activities will focus on catch-up education, remedial classes that intend to break tasks down and use different teaching approaches (the use of games and educational computer programs), after-school activities, and an intensive summer school program. Also, online and hardcopy games for Arabic language and for math will be available for students by PSD. These games were designed and printed locally by Palestinian teachers for the learning loss that was incurred during COVID-19. All material given to students can be adapted to the needs of children with disabilities.

1 - Needs

During the last two years students around the world have experienced a lack of stability in the educational system and the overall learning environment. In Palestine specifically the pandemic, in addition to teachers’ strikes, and attacks on education by the occupation has caused years of academic regression amongst students. (Global Coalition to Protect Education from Attack 2022) (HNO 2022) Also, lack of technological resources and knowledge in its use has caused the most vulnerable school children to fall behind in particular subjects and in many cases in the general curriculum. Equally, teachers lack the resources and time needed to compensate students for the learning loss they have experienced. As per the HNO of 2022, MSNA findings reported that 88 per cent of households in Gaza and 72 per cent in the West Bank experienced loss of learning and needed catch up learning programs. According to the MSNA findings of July 2022 for the school year of 2021-2022 19% of school-aged boys and 13.6% of school-aged girls reported dropping out. The two primary reasons for the drop out of students was first the protection risks of a political character while traveling to and from school, this was about 32.8% of those that dropped out, the second was school closures which was at 26.9%. As is seen in the findings, the school aged boys drop-out rate is higher than that of the school aged girls, similarly the reasons for dropping out of school for boys reflect the severe effects that closures had with 54.8% dropping out due to protection risks and 53.6% dropping out due to school closures, and for the girls it was 36.1% and 29.3% respectively. Additionally, according to PSD findings from the Shababeek project the number male students needing support education and catch-up learning was 30% higher than that of female students. Moreover, in West Bank, according to the MSNA findings which were conducted in partnership with the Palestinian Central Bureau for Statistics (PCBS) of 2022, it was made evident that almost all areas reported a high need for catch-up learning due to school closures due to COVID-19 and/or other reasons. The area with the highest need for catch-up learning was H2 (Hebron) with 83.5%, as for Areas A, B and C they also reported a high need for catch-up learning all of which were an estimated 71% percent per area. This indicates that those affected the most by the school closures were primarily boys and girls from the most vulnerable areas, although it also indicates that the need for support education and catch-up learning programs is very much needed in the entirety of the oPt. The loss of learning is further exacerbated by already existing problems such as overcrowded classrooms, lack of teacher assistants, of after school programs, of summer programs and of strengthening courses during the year. This gap in the school system is causing children to be moved up in grade levels without the proper academic skill set to be able to continue properly in their academic path. The lack of funding and resources that the school system receives contributes to the increased learning loss that has occurred. Hence, PSD seeks to propose a project that will make strengthening programs specifically in the core subjects such as math and Arabic available to children in the most vulnerable and disadvantaged areas within the public-school system. This will address the learning loss incurred and will focus on catch up learning curriculum, meaning they will go back to the basics and essentials needed in in the core subjects mentioned in order to keep students on their age and grade appropriate academic level and to assure they receive the educational foundation needed to continue to the next grade levels. The intervention aligns specifically with the Education Cluster’s second objective as well the strategy put forward by the MoE which seeks to increase equal access to quality educational services to vulnerable children. The children most affected are those from age 7-16, PSD intends to target the grade levels that are in the most need of catch-up learning/education and that have been most affected by the learning loss. Specifically, those that are in lower grade levels that require a strong baseline education to continue properly in their academic paths. Additionally, it targets those in the most vulnerable areas that lack connectivity and ICT tools, families with multiple school-aged children and students with disabilities that have been affected more, since most of the educational online materials are not digitally accessible, there was no accommodation during the remote sessions.This information is based on PSD's direct contact with schools, and feedback from educational supervisors and officials.

2 - Activities

1. PSD intends to hire 100 teachers that have already been pre-approved by the MoE, who have been informed and have a memorandum of understanding with, and are waiting to be assigned schools. These teachers will be taking the role of assistant teachers. The gender of the teachers will be chosen based on the age of the students and the area of the school. Hiring female teachers to work with male students when and where it's possible to compensate for their learning loss, helping to balance the lack of gender diversity specifically in the school system. 2. Teachers will be trained on catch-up and support education using games by PSD and learning packages by the MoE. These programs are adapted according to student needs which are based on gender, family situation, disability and parents’ ability to assist with students’ education. 3. Group students according to their needs as per their grade, subject and school. The focus will be on male students more than females because female teachers were more committed to remote learning than male teachers. Due to lockdown male students used to work (child labor), hence they are more prone to drop out. Based on the assessment we conducted on 100 schools the male schools reported more number of male students need catch up education than female schools do. 4. Coordinate with the school principals and teachers to create a comprehensive approach to conduct the catch-up and support education. This will be specific to each school’s approach and availability of space and time, also to what aligns with the teachers’ recommendations. 5. Coordinate between the teachers and teacher assistant on the one hour of extra support after school or during school, this will be based on the situation on the ground and what it allows. 6. Coordinate with community based organizations to host students and teachers in cases of not being able to use school facilities for the after school program.
