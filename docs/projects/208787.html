Project Summary

The project seeks to enhance the agency of girls and vulnerable women through a multiplicity of actions that improve access to comprehensive services for GBV survivors including providing access to legal justice, improve community safety initiatives, facilitate community norm sessions, and amplify the ‘Voices’ of girls and vulnerable women whilst building life skills, personal safety and self-defense skills, livelihood skills and economic empowerment.
This project will ensure synergistic multi-sectoral approaches, that address the root causes of vulnerabilities, providing a comprehensive protective shield for girls and vulnerable women with markers for Persons living with disability through GBV-health related prevention, interventions, education and economic empowerment. 
At the end of the project, we seek to reach 24,000 Girls and Vulnerable Women with GBV Prevention and response services

1 - Needs

With over 13 years of protracted humanitarian crises, the humanitarian context in north-east Nigeria remains formidable, as around 7.8 million people are anticipated to be of Humanitarian need in 2024. Children, especially girls, bear the greatest impact, comprising 83% of those requiring assistance, alongside women. Despite the variance in level of vulnerabilities based on predominant factors such as location, age, and displacement status, there are still key factors that predispose women and girls to risks such as conflict and insecurity, the ramifications of climate change, disease outbreaks, food insecurity and malnutrition, population movements and camp closures, economic considerations, and historical underdevelopment. The crisis has further exacerbated protection issues and human rights violations including sexual and gender-based violence, abductions by non-state armed groups (NSAGs), barriers to accessing both formal and non-formal education services and essential NFIs. The ongoing crises including the relocation of IDPs has placed women and girls at a heightened risk of gender-based violence (GBV), sexual exploitation and abuse, lack access to land and livelihood opportunities. Among those in need, women and children constitute 81 percent (6.7 million), with women and girls comprising the majority at 53 percent. The Gender-Based Violence Information Management System (GBVIMS) reports for (January - June 2023), showed that GBV survivors were mostly women and girls with 83% (over 18 years old), and 17% were children (<18years) with Borno having the highest percentage of reported of GBV incidents at (84%). (GBVIMS mid-year report 2023). Likewise, the closure of camps in Borno and influx of returnee in Askira-Uba and Mobbar LGAs will exacerbate the existing protection issues and overburden the already not enough resources.

2 - Activities

This project seeks to contribute to the reduction of GBV incidents and mitigate their impact by strengthening survivor-centered GBV preventive and response services in Askira Uba and Mobbar LGAs in Borno State . A survivor-centred approach to programming will be utilized so that everyone engaged in SGBV programming prioritizes the survivors’ rights, and needs which will involve GBV safety audit in locations, Needs assessment, capacity building for GBV Frontline workers on MHPSS, GBV Case Management &Referral Pathway System, mass awareness campaigns on GBV/PSEA/SRH and production of IEC Materials; training for men and boys to become allies and agents of change in GBV prevention and response; and supporting community-based protection networks and participation in GBVSS coordination forums Project intervention will include:1. Capacity-building training for service providers and duty-bearersThere will be of 30 GBV Frontline Workers on GBV prevention and response services, CMR, Psychological First Aid (PFA), Psychosocial support, GBV Case Management, SEA complaints mechanisms and referrals for other multisector services. In addition, there will training on disability inclusion to improve skills and knowledge around disability sensitivity and increase the accessibility of services Likewise, there will be training for 40 Men and Boys as Anti-GBV Community Champions to meaningfully engage them to challenge negative notions of masculinity and promote healthy, gender-equitable relationships in the camps. These champions will foster dialogues on GBV eradication, prevention and enhanced access to service. 2. Establishment of Women & Girls Safe Space to enhance safety networks for Girls &Vulnerable Women through Community Based and Schools integrated Activities- Life and Safety Skills, Livelihood Skills and Economic Empowerment, ICT etc Including provision of Dignity Kits 3. Strengthening the provision of GBV specialized Services including GBV Case Management and referral pathways for the vulnerable population, especially Women and Girls.The Frontline workers will support GBV service provision at 4 health Facilities and WGSS in the LGAs. They will also conduct outreach campaigns on GBV prevention, response and mitigation, and engage with communities to discuss GBV and disability risks including identifying ways of addressing them and providing referrals. Also, the vulnerable population will be supported with dignity kits to maintain their self-esteem and confidence 4. Organise SGBV sessions with adolescent girls and young women in safe spaces in the communities to help contribute to GBV prevention and response as well as provide them with the life skills and knowledge to identify types of GBV and how to seek support services including SEA complaints mechanisms 5. Enhance access to livelihood support to mitigate the risk of GBV, and survivors' access to socio-economic support at the WGFS. They will be equipped with the practical skills necessary to begin a small-scale business. The targeted beneficiaries of the skills acquisition programme will be married adolescent girls, survivors of GBV, adolescent heads of households, widowed adolescent girls etc. A total of 500 beneficiaries will be targeted. At the end of the training, adolescent girls and young women will be supported with startup kits and supplies to start small businesses which will help to increase or diversify their sources of income and prevent the adoption of negative coping strategies. 6. Intensive Community Led Awareness Raising/Risk Communication and Community Engagement on GBV Prevention, Mitigation and Referrals. Also, the project will support the integration of GBV survivors into the Community through group activities that focus on building support networks, increasing confidence and skills, and promoting economic empowerment. 7 Organise Community Stakeholders Meetings to share project updates, and get feedback

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

In this regard, the project will actively promote adherence to Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) that describe the roles, responsibilities, guiding principles, and procedures for prevention of and response to any form of gender-based violence affecting the community. All the health workers will be sensitized about PSEA and Do No Harm, and sign a Do No Harm Pledge. AHI will ensure that the GBV frontline workers and volunteers apply culturally sensitive approaches, as well as, understand the structural and cultural factors that contribute to women’s and girls’ vulnerability to GBV and SRH risks. In line with this, the project will ensure the application of the humanitarian guiding principles of “do no harm”, confidentiality, safety, nondiscrimination and impartiality in service provision such that priority is given to those who most need it, regardless of their affiliation. Attention will also be paid to practicing a survivor-centred approach which establishes a relationship with the survivor that promotes their emotional and physical safety, builds trust and helps them to restore some control over their life. In recognition of the fact that the project is targeting the most vulnerable populations and its main beneficiaries are adolescent girls, women of childbearing age especially those with specialized GBV needs and persons living with disabilities, the service providers will include both male and female health workers to facilitate acceptability for women and girls to benefit from services. Furthermore, care will be taken for all intended programme beneficiaries especially GBV survivors and persons living with disabilities to access services in a manner that is acceptable considered appropriate by them. Because of the on-going insecurity, AHI will ensure, to the best of its ability, that the proposed activities are located as closely as possible to the beneficiaries. Finally, we will ensure that the project’s feedback and complaints mechanisms are appropriate and robust enough to communicate, receive, process, respond to and learn from, with a view to improving programming, understanding community perceptions, promoting beneficiary empowerment and assisting in detecting misconduct. The Community based feedback and response mechanism we emplace will be developed in consultation with the persons of concern. It will document and track the incidence and resolution of feedback/complaints from them on the services and goods they are provided by the programme, and ensure that responses and information is provided to them in a timely and appropriate manner protecting and ensuring confidentiality.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The Project outcome indicators are crafted in view to harness feedback from vulnerable population and AHI emphasizing its dedication to being accountable to affected populations through a pivotal approach. This commitment entails not only minimizing potential risks but also implementing measures to mitigate them, along with fostering awareness about the purpose of interventions and their intended beneficiaries. In order to facilitate effective communication and the dissemination of information, AHI will establish committees for community-based project management. These committees will act as a bidirectional communication channel, promoting consultation and the provision of information between AHI and the Community Members
