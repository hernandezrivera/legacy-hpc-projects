Project Summary

This project will ensure 13,607 of the most vulnerable Palestinian children (7,430 boys and 6,177 girls aged 12-17 years old including 204 children with disabilities) exposed to conflict-related violence, and 10,729 caregivers are reached with protection, impact mitigation, rehabilitation and prevention services in the West Bank. It will also foster increased respect for IHL and IHRL and accountability for violations against children through documenting and monitoring violations to inform advocacy and increase accountability.
This project is in line with and contributes to the Protection Cluster's first and second objectives that aim to increase respect for IHL, IHRL, and accountability for violations as well as ensure that protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict-related violence.
The main objective of this child protection project is for children in the West Bank to be safe, protected, and secure from violence, exploitation, and grave violations. The project will:
- Support initiatives to engage vulnerable and at-risk children positively in structured recreational, educational, awareness-raising, life skills, and resilience-building activities to improve their resilience and coping mechanisms.
- Support awareness-raising for vulnerable families and communities regarding children’s right to protection; preventing and responding to Violence Against Children (VAC);
- Support vulnerable children affected by conflict-related violence and their families with integration and rehabilitation services especially structured counseling and mental health and psychosocial support including through helpline services. (Level 3),
- Support monitoring, data analysis, and reporting on grave violations trends related to child protection concerns in Israel/Occupied Palestinian Territory (the WB) to provide evidence and inform advocacy.
- Support legal assistance for children affected by conflict-related violence.
- Ensuring compliance with PSEA principles and procedures, implementing partners and project beneficiaries will be oriented on sexual exploitation and abuse and will receive information on UNICEF email, as well as other interagency channels including SAWA Free toll number, social media platforms, and WhatsApp to report on PSEA.

1 - Needs

During 2022, in the West Bank including East Jerusalem, the rise in demonstrations, Israeli forces’ operations, clashes and arrests since April 2021 continued, along with continuing negative trends of settler violence and evictions and demolitions of Palestinian property, particularly in Area C and East Jerusalem. In August 2022, another escalation of hostilities between Israel and Palestinian armed groups in Gaza occurred, following on the major escalation of May 2021. These events exacerbated already-severe protection needs throughout the oPt, particularly among children. In 2021, 2,934 grave violations were verified in Israel and the State of Palestine (A/76/871-S/2022/493), including 1,216 children (914 boys, 302 girls) killed or maimed; 134 attacks on schools, hospitals and related protected personnel; and 1,583 incidents of denial of humanitarian access affecting children. In addition, 637 children (624 boys, 13 girls) arrested and detained for alleged security-related offences; and 156 other interferences with education and health were verified in the same period. The coercive environment in the occupied West Bank intensifies, driven by demolitions, forced evictions, discriminatory planning, access restrictions, settlement expansion and settler violence, generating a risk of forcible transfer for many Palestinians in Area C, East Jerusalem and the H2 area of Hebron city, H2. Since the beginning of 2022, 27 Palestinian boys were killed by live ammunition and 784 children (771 boys, 13 girls) were reported injured (OCHA Casualties data to 03 Oct 2022) during conflict-related incidents in the West Bank including in East Jerusalem. Between January and September 2022, at least 11,458 children were affected by demolitions and 776 people, including 367 children were displaced, and 683 structures were demolished of which 102 are donor-funded. 79% of demolitions occurred in Area C and 14% in East Jerusalem. Of particular concern are the vulnerable communities living in Area C and East Jerusalem in proximity and around the so-called settlement blocs in light of the threat of annexation in the West Bank and the Israeli settlement expansion plans and settlers’ violence in those areas. In 2021, 1,536 Palestinian children were arrested in the West Bank including East Jerusalem (Source: Israeli Police data, based on NGO freedom of information requests; age and gender disaggregation unavailable). Detained children remain at high risk of violence, ill-treatment and due process violations during their arrest, transfer, interrogation and detention, affecting their psychosocial well-being even after release (NIAF 2020). Owing to prevalent gender and social norms, adolescent boys are the most affected, and when released, they are at risk of school dropout and/or mental health disorders. Direct acts and threats of violence, including killing and injuring of children, as well as arrest and detention, and other impacts of the coercive environment negatively impact on children’s mental wellbeing, particularly where children are exposed on a recurrent basis, and where distress and psychosocial impacts are under-treated. Many children previously affected by violations remain in need of continuing psychosocial support or case management support, in some cases requiring previously closed cases to be re-opened. The impacts and after-effects of the COVID-19 pandemic continue to affect the most vulnerable families, as two years of restrictions, school closures, job losses and socio-economic pressures exacerbated vulnerabilities and led to reported increases in domestic violence and GBV (2020 NIAF), and reported increased resort to negative coping mechanisms such as child labour and child marriages, exposing children to further protection risks. Already across, the State of Palestine, 92% of boys and 88% of girls face violent discipline, with 14% of children in West Bank subject to severe physical violence from caregivers (boys 23%, girls 17%). 10% of children (age 5-17) are engaged in child labour, with boys twice as likely as girls to be working and six times as likely to work in hazardous conditions. There is urgent need for quality prevention, impact mitigation and protection services to vulnerable children. The CP-AOR estimates that 182,000 children and 65,000 caregivers in the West Bank are in need of CP and MHPSS services, including 35,000 children in need of focused non-specialized support or specialized services. There is also a need for monitoring, documentation and verification of grave violations, to ensure timely reporting and analysis to inform the humanitarian response.

2 - Activities

This intervention aims for children (boys and girls aged 12-17 years old) in oPt to be safe and protected from violence, exploitation and grave violations. The project’s main goal is to reduce vulnerabilities of children and families in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, and improve their resilience. It also aims to document Children affected by Armed Conflict (CAAC) incidents within the oPt/Israel as a 'Situation of Concern'. This will be achieved through: 1) Protecting children at-risk of being impacted by conflict-related violence through coordinated community level prevention programmes and protective services in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem; 2) Responding to the needs of directly affected children and their families through provision of impact mitigation services and psychosocial support and 3) Documenting and reporting on CAAC incidents within the oPt/Israel as a "situation of concern". To address the increasing needs resulting from increased conflict-related violence and Violence against Children (VAC), the intervention will prioritize children at risk of domestic violence, children with disabilities, children exposed to conflict related violence and children living under the poverty line. Partners will ensure provision of both face-to-face and remote services upon need. Activities and interventions are designed and implemented based on inter-agency standing committee (IASC) guidelines, child protection minimum standards (CPMS), and with reference to UNICEF global standards and locally established guidances and standards of the CP-AOR and Protection Cluster. UNICEF and partners will consult children to review existing feedback mechanisms including accessibility, and then process their feedback, and complaints in line with UNICEF Operational Guidance on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP). Acknowledging the different needs of boys and girls and thus the need to design interventions accordingly, gender will be mainstreamed throughout the programme cycle; during implementation and monitoring. The project approach is framed through a gender sensitive lens that recognizes that the vulnerabilities of girls and boys frequently manifest themselves differently; girls are more susceptible to distress and mental health disorders, violence and negative coping strategies such as child marriage especially with many confined in their households and with schools intermittently open. Boys are more likely to engage in child labour, and without structured recreational support, they are likely to come into contact with Israeli Forces and Israeli settlers. As a result, there is a strong emphasis on reaching boys at risk with preventative measures. The project will also increase opportunities for girls and women to be involved and effectively participate in the design and implementation of different activities. The project will seek to achieve the following outputs and activities: Output 1: Support vulnerable children and their families to access prevention and protection services to improve their resilience and coping mechanisms in the worst affected areas in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem. (Target: 13,607 children including 204 CwD and 10,729 adults); Activity 1.1 Engage vulnerable and at-risk children and their caregivers positively in structured recreational, educational, life skills and resilience building activities to improve their resilience and coping mechanisms.Activity 1.2 Raise public awareness and commitment of caregivers and community members on children’s right to protection, preventing and responding to VAC. Activity 1.3 Provide vulnerable children affected by conflict-related violence and their families and their families with integration and rehabilitation services especially structured counselling and mental health and psychosocial support including through helpline services.Activity 1.4. Legal assistance for children affected by conflict-related violence Output 2: Support monitoring, data analysis, documentation, monitoring and reporting on grave violations trends related to child protection concerns in Israel/State of Palestine (WB including East Jerusalem) to provide evidence and inform advocacy. (Target: 24 children and 48 adults); Activity 2.1 Ensure partners have capacities to monitor and document of violations against children, verification and legal redress of cases of children arrested, detained, indicted and sentenced.Activity 2.2 Support in-house internal capacity for documenting, reporting and advocacy. The activities are to be delivered primarily through existing implementing partnerships, thereby capitalizing on existing resources and established interventions in the targeted communities.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

This project aims to engage with diverse community members on child protection concerns both in relation to the VAC agenda and the impact of conflict-related violence on children. This engagement will include community groups discussions, awareness raising sessions, social media campaigns etc. In all of these activities, UNICEF and its partners will ensure a diverse participation of community members including girls and boy, women and men, adolescents, the elderly and people with disabilities. Participation of children and their parents/caregivers in these activities will be voluntary and based upon their informed consent/assent. Global guidance such as the Child Protection Minimum Standards (CPMS) and UNICEF’s internal policies on informed consent/assent, confidentiality, data protection and information sharing will be applied in accordance with the best interest of the child principle. UNICEF and partners will determine whether informed consent/assent is legally required or expected of a parent/caregiver. They will also determine from whom informed consent/assent is legally required in cases of child-headed households or children in institutions. They will ensure that consent is given in a form and language that reflects the capacity of the child. Data confidentiality and data protection will be ensured through all phases of collection, transfer, storage and communication. These procedures will guide UNICEF and its implementing partners interaction with children and their parents/caregivers as well as between UNICEF and the implementing partners. UNICEF will also ensure that its own staff adhere to its child safeguarding policy and that the selected implementing partners have such a framework put in place. Children with disabilities (CWDs) will be included in the various phases of the project in line with the Do No Harm principle. Also, children will be engaged in ways that are appropriate to their level of capacities taking into account power imbalances that may increase risk of vulnerability for CWDs. Children will be listened to as a first step to enhance their participation in the decision making processes, the design and development of activities, and the implementation of the evaluation of impact of the intervention. CWDs will have sufficient spaces to contribute to forums/discussions the same way other children are able to take part in. They will also have opportunities to challenge some decisions that are made by adults in the communities where they live and which impact their access to quality child protection services. Gaps in formal services provision will be addressed by partnering with local organizations of people with disabilities or community-based programmes. All implementing partners will be requested that facilities and applications are in formats accessible to CwDs and to include at least 1.5% of Children with Disabilities (WDS) beneficiaries under each partnership agreement with UNICEF. Partners will also be requested to include CWDs in all planned surveys, researches and other analyses to better understand the situation of CWDs and difficulties they and their families face. UNICEF and implementing partners will continue to discuss and agree on recruitment of specialist professionals to deliver services to CWDs. Implementing partners will also receive capacity building to enhance their knowledge and skills in the field of CP service delivery to CWDs. This will be done either directly by UNICEF support or under the umbrella of the Child Protection Area of Responsibility. UNICEF will follow up with implementing partners on coordinating their interventions among other child protection /MHPSS actors to avoid duplication of services and harming the affected people. The partners will also report on their interventions using the available IM tools of the CPAOR and Protection cluster. In addition, the East Jerusalem coordination committee will coordinate their activities with the CPAOR.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project will be informed by the United Nations Response Strategies that highlight the needs of children in State of Palestine: the findings and recommendations of the East Jerusalem Action Plan, the Evaluation of EJ Action Plan and the consultations conducted by UNICEF with key stakeholders. In addition, UNICEF and its implementing partners will involve children, adolescents and caregivers by conducting virtual and in person (when possible) focus group discussions, and community outreach activities. Children will be consulted to review existing feedback mechanisms, to express their views and provide suggestions on how to make those mechanisms more accessible. Upon collection and receipt of complaints, responses and feedbacks, UNICEF and partners will process the feedback, responses and complaints in line with UNICEF Operational Guidance on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) developed in the State of Palestine. UNICEF and their implementing partners will promote the active participation of the most affected children and caregivers throughout the project and will ensure complete and equal access into different opportunities offered by this project. The project will raise awareness among caregivers and the community of the importance of female participation and coordinate closely with caregivers who might express concerns over their children’s participation. To ensure the relevance of the interventions, a systematic, objective and continuous context humanitarian analysis is conducted while designing and planning the project, while keeping an open eye to the changing needs, capacities, and contexts and thus adapting the interventions accordingly whenever required. UNICEF identified some key risks and mitigation measures as per the following: Risk 1: Widespread violence resulting from prevailing frustrations and hopelessness affecting youth, disadvantaged and disenfranchised population strata. This violence could be related both to discontent with the prevailing occupation measures, or discontent within and among Palestinians. Mitigation measures 1: Ensure that there is enough flexibility to allow reprogram of activities depending on the emerging needs. Risk 2: Communities refuse to allow children and families to participate in prevention activities for various reasons. Mitigation measures 2: Ensure continued dialogue and participation of key community members on the importance of prevention initiatives and sensitize them on the impact of rights violations.
