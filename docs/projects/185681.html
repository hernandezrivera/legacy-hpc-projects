Project Summary

IMC UK has a strong capacity to raise funds from other donors to complement UNHCR funds, has gained acceptance from all stakeholders, including other implementing partners, representatives of line ministries, administrative authorities, and has a strong knowledge of the context. IMC UK has extensive experience in implementing activities in Cameroon in general and the Far North in particular. Similarly, IMC UK has gained the trust of the population benefiting from its services provided in all intervention sites. IMC UK has been present in the Eastern region since 2008, and in the Far North since 2010, providing cholera relief services in the Mokolo and Maga health districts, conducting ECHO-funded nutrition interventions in Kaele, Guidiguis and Moulvoudaye, and health and nutrition interventions in Minawao camp since 2014 serving Nigerian refugees. IMC UK has the capacity to respond quickly and in a timely manner to sudden humanitarian emergencies, including IMC UK’s response to the Goura humanitarian crisis following the massive influx of Nigerian asylum seekers fleeing attacks of violence in Rann, Nigeria in February 2019. IMC UK has provided support for the fight against cholera in the Kaele Health district in 2019 through funding from the START Fund. IMC UK also responded to the COVID-19 pandemic in the Far North region in March 2020 with funding from START-MERF, and AFENET. With the support of its donors, such as ECHO, BPRM, START, AFENET, CDC, BHA, UNFPA and UNHCR, IMC UK is currently implementing health, nutrition and protection services in five regions: Far North, East, Adamaoua, North and North West.
The success of International Medical Corps UK depends vitally on its ability to manage and safeguard UNCHR resources, both governmental and private, that are entrusted to it. A sound financial management and control structure and the judicious use of these funds are essential to achieving this goal. IMC UK's financial management and accounting system tracks all revenues and expenditures by project, segregates costs by project and expenditure category, and accounts for eligible costs. IMC UK has established policies to meet the highest standards of financial management in all its operations. IMC UK has implemented UNHCR-funded projects in recent years and has been subject to internal and external UNHCR audits. IMC UK has been able to demonstrate compliance with donor regulations and has implemented their recommendations accordingly. IMC UK’s procurement procedures are designed to ensure transparency and fair competition, with the ultimate goal of providing the quality goods and services to beneficiaries at the best possible price. IMC UK adheres to the following procedures that guide the awarding of procurement contracts: all contracts over $1,000 are awarded through a full competitive bidding process, with at least three vendors contacted to submit written quotes; all vendors awarded a contract above this value must be registered with IMC UK and provide the necessary legal documents and company registration that proves their existence and ability to do business in the area in which they are competing; all purchases of medical products can only be made through pre-qualified suppliers who have been screened and inspected to ensure compliance with good procurement, storage and distribution practices in accordance with WHO regulations; for products purchased on a regular basis, a blanket purchase agreement or long-term contracts are signed after a competitive bidding process to expedite the procurement process and ensure that programs receive products at the right time; and regular training is provided to staff to ensure a clear understanding of the organization's procedures.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Project Goal: To improve the health and nutrition status of refugees and host communities in Minawao camp through the provision and integration of comprehensive health and nutrition services geared towards reducing of morbidity and mortality. As part of the implementation of the multi-year strategic plan for 2022-2026 developed by UNHCR, IMC UK intends to contribute towards the improvement of access to health and nutrition services for refugees through a results-based approach. IMC UK will aim to empower and improve the lives of refugees in Minawao camp through a continuation of existing services which began in 2020 while building upon and strengthening these services to improve local capacity and ensure local ownership. IMC UK will support the Mokolo Health District in providing comprehensive health care to refugees in Minawao camp and host communities in the surrounding villages. In line with the vision of empowerment and health resilience of the Minawao refugee camp initiated in 2020 and expected to be fully realized by 2026, IMC UK will contribute to the progressive ownership of services and activities to integrate refugees into the comprehensive health care system and services provided by the integrated health center in Minawao camp. In order to achieve the expected outcomes of empowering refugees and improving their health and nutrition status, IMC UK has designed an intervention focusing on the following objectives: Objective 1: To improve the autonomy of the Minawao Camp Integrated Health Center to provide quality and sustainable care of beneficiaries;Objective 2: To improve the health status of Nigerian refugees in Minawao camp and the surrounding villages;Objective 3: To ensure the population has optimal access to reproductive health and HIV services.Objective 4: To improve the nutrition status of refugees and host communities in and around the Minawao camp. As part of this intervention, IMC UK will carry out activities which aim to improve and ensure sustainability through three main axes: (1) The implementation of advocacy and lobbying activities: IMC UK will continue the process of transforming the Minawao health center into a district medical center (CMA). It will build upon actions which began in 2021 where health care workers are recruited by the health district and paid incentives by the project, under the guidance and technical supervision from IMC UK. IMC UK will facilitate the assignment of state health personnel to the center so that the project-supported staff can be gradually replaced by state health personnel. (2) Refugee empowerment: Over the period of 2022 to 2026, IMC UK will focus on strengthening the involvement, empowerment, and leadership of refugees and host community members in community-based health activities.(3) Progressive financial participation in health care by refugees: through community participation with the village health committee (COSA), IMC UK will put in place mechanisms to allow refugees to progressively contribute to the cost of their health care. This intervention will target refugees living in Minawao Camp and host populations living in the surrounding villages.
