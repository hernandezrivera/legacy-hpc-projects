Project Summary

Lebanon is facing the most devastating socio-economic crisis in its recent history. The
economic and financial crisis, accelerating since the end of 2019, has been compounded by the COVID-19 pandemic and the Beirut Port explosions. The current economic crisis and food price increase may have a widespread impact on the nutritional and health status of the population. Gains in child survival over the past few decades in Lebanon are likely to be threatened and millennium development goals will be harder to achieve. Among Lebanese nationals and the migrants, the nutrition sector estimates that 5,000 children and 27,285 pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) will suffer from acute malnutrition in the coming year. Additionally, 101,575 children under 5 and 136,425 PLWs need to be supported to access anaemia prevention interventions.
To overcome this challenge, UNICEF, through the Emergency Response fund will lead and support the delivery of critical life-saving nutrition interventions to respond to the needs identified under the nutrition sector. Priority will be given to vulnerable Lebanese and migrants, complementing the on-going nutrition response under the Lebanon Crisis
Response Plan that has a focus on the refugee population. UNICEF, in coordination with MOPH, the nutrition sector partners, and the key UN agencies will work together to deliver high quality life-saving nutrition services at scale in line with nutrition sector priorities Country level normative guidance.
Specifically, UNICEF will support the delivery of:
• Direct interventions for malnutrition treatment: UNICEF will support the effective management of severe and moderate acute malnutrition among 5,000 children and 27,285 Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLWs). Alongside this, UNICEF will strengthen the referral pathways by scaling up active case finding through the family MUAC approach. Continuum of care for treatment will be strengthened through catering for the transportation costs and consultation fees among 3,000 vulnerable families. To strengthen technical and implementation capacities among MoPH and implementing partners, UNICEF will organize formal trainings on the in-patient management of severe acute malnutrition as well as management of acute malnutrition among infants to 50 health workers in 10 selected private and public hospitals. This action will be implemented through establishment of expanded partnerships with nutrition sector partners.
• Direct malnutrition preventive interventions: UNICEF, through expanding and equipping the Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) Specialist networks will scale up the promotion of exclusive breast feeding among children aged below 6 months and improve the consumption of quality and maternal diets through household micro-nutrient supplementation to101,575 children aged 6-59 months and 136,425 PLWs. 161,498 PLWs and caretakers of children aged 6-23 months will receive skilled and specialized maternal, infant and young child feeding counseling and support through the IYCF specialists.
• Support for Non-Breast Fed Infants: Through this action, UNICEF, will support the establishment of small scale, need-based, targeted, controlled and coordinated programme for artificial feeding support for the extremely vulnerable infants aged below the age of 6 months in 10 primary health care centers a last resort. Provision of this support will be done in strict accordance to the law 47/2008. It will be done as a last resort, after the full examination of infants by qualified trained health personnel; and when all other options to re-initiate breastfeeding have been fully explored and completely failed. In such circumstances, provision of artificial feeding support will be provided solely upon the recommendation of the health personnel, through the health system and upon a thorough evaluation of its acceptability, feasibility, affordability, sustainability and safety.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Sector Objective 1.  Support 87,599 vulnerable households with 161,498 vulnerable PLWs and caretakers of children aged below 2 years with IYCF counselling integrated with hygiene promotion. UNICEF will support the establishment of 216 mother to mother support groups to promote peer to peer learning whilst strengthening awareness and compliance on the code for marketing of breastmilk substitutes. For anaemia prevention, UNICEF will procure and provide micro-nutrient supplements to 94,575 children aged 6-59 months at community level and 131,925 PLWs at health facility level integrated in routine primary health care reproductive services. Sector Objective 2:  Support the treatment of severe and moderate acute malnutrition among 5,000 children and 25,740 PLWs. UNICEF will also support the procurement of adequate nutrition supplies and anthropometric equipment for these services. Cash transfer for 1,000 vulnerable caretakers of non-breast-fed infants enrolled in programmes for artificial feeding support as a last resort and 2,000 caretakers of children with SAM/MAM enrolled in malnutrition treatment programmes to cover for transportation costs and consultation fees. UNICEF will train 50 pedestrians on implementation of high qualities services for in-patient management of SAM, management of acute malnutrition among infants and care for non-breast-fed infants in 10 selected private and public hospitals.  UNICEF will continue leading the coordination of the sector

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

UNICEF is committed in advancing PSEA and Child Safeguarding (CS) minimum standards within Implementing Partners (IPs) working under the ERP umbrella through the roll-out of the PSEA/CS capacity assessment followed by trainings, provision of technical support on PSEA and CS. Additionally, the communities coping mechanisms will also be assessed to mitigate adverse impacts on their safety. This is in line with Core Humanitarian Standard that encompasses the ‘Do no harm’ principle.UNICEF, through its mandate as sector lead U for nutrition sector will support partners to include the needs and voices of children, PLWs and persons with disabilities at all stages of the response. UNICEF, from a vantage point of co-leading the child protection sub-sector will ensure protection concerns for children are identified and addressed in line with the strategies outlined in UNICEF’s Core Commitment for Children for humanitarian action.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

UNICEF has made Accountability to Affected People (AAP) a priority in its Strategic Plan, UNICEF is adopting an institutional strategy and action plan for scaling up AAP. UNICEF’s responsibility to ensure programmes generate meaningful and relevant results and outcomes for girls, boys, women and men, in accordance with their specific needs, priorities and preferences. It involves working in ways to protect, facilitate and enable them to exercise their rights, including the right to safe, fair, equitable access to quality services and accurate, reliable and relevant information, the right to share their views and opinions about the quality and effectiveness of programmes, and participate in decisions that affect them.UNICEF will scale up the use of the SMS based 24x7 U-report system to ensure community feedback is collected and analyzed systematically. The U-report will complement other forms of community based beneficiary feedback and complaint handling mechanisms.
