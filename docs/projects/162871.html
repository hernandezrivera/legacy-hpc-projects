Project Summary

Prolonged conflict and instability in Darfur and the surrounding regions has caused mass displacement and necessitated the establishment of Internally Displace People (IDP) camps. Indicated inadequate access to safe water supply, shortfalls in Sphere standards in terms of gender-sensitive sanitation facilities with large number of old and non-functional latrine; poor hygiene practices such as open defecation is widely practiced particular among children, particularly IDP and vulnerable host communities are extremely exposed to vector and water-borne diseases. Plan International will implement activities to improve access to improved WASH services, including potable water, Sanitation facilities and hygiene promotion in targeted locations is thereby reduce associated prevalence of water related and vector-borne disease. Gender equity will be incorporated throughout detailed project design and implementation strategies, including emphasis placed on ensuring the participation of women and children and all other community groups are involved. In addition, women and girls will be engaged in leadership, PTAs, WASH committees, and mothers committee. Therefore, community awareness raising about gender roles and relationships will be conducted to promote (aspirations for) gender equality by providing opportunities for women and men in community committees to discuss gender relations and to set their own a agenda for change. Adding to that to the project will strengthen project team’s understanding of gender analysis to develop practical skills for gender monitoring and collect sex disaggregated information. During the project implementation Plan International will keep all the relevant WASH partners including NGOs, government, Humanitarian Aid Coordination (HAC), and UN agencies informed during the scheduled periodic coordination sector meetings taking place at community, state and federal level. This will ensure avoiding duplications of efforts, create better coordination, and learn from other organizations experiences, particularly in Cash Based Programming (CBP).

1 - Needs

Prolonged conflict and instability in Darfur and the surrounding regions has caused mass displacement and necessitated the establishment of Internally Displace People (IDP) camps. Based on the 2017-2019 multiyear humanitarian response plan (MYHS) and HRP sectors priority for 2018, Dar-Al salaam and Tawilla are considered as deprived areas with a gap in humanitarian services. In Tawilla, Dar Elsalam, Mellit, Tawilla there is a gap in water and sanitation services. Water quantity and quality is an issue: households collect approximately 42 litres of water per day per household, i.e. approximately only 7 litres of water per day per person. Meanwhile water quality is a concern due to the fact that some wells are often unprotected and the leaching of latrines close to the water points contaminate the water. It is observed that households do not treat water for drinking, while water is being used from unprotected sources. There are only few humanitarian actors to support people with less capacity, leaving majority of actual needs are unaddressed. Indicated inadequate access to safe water supply, shortfalls in Sphere standards in terms of gender-sensitive sanitation facilities with large number of old and non-functional latrine; poor hygiene practices such as open defecation is widely practiced particular among children. In the same vein, sanitation facilities where the coverage is below SPHERE standard compounded by poor hygiene practices. All these impacts the lives of poor families in the target areas, both from the IDPs and host communities, making them extremely vulnerable to vectors and water borne diseases. Plan project aims to increase access to WASH services to IDP, Returnees and host community in Darfur reduce dependency on external aid. Activities will be cross-cutting to not only increase access but also utilization and reduce diarrhea and other common diseases, and ultimately affect children the most. North Darfur state has gone through Acute Watery Diarrheal disease in 2017-2018.

2 - Activities / Outputs

1. Rehabilitation of water distribution pipelines and construction of 4 distribution points in Tawilla, Mellit and Dar Elsalam. 2. Rehabilitation of 10 Hand Pumps using solar system in Tawilla, Mellit and Dar Elsalam.3. Conduct water quality and quantity monitoring visits in Tawilla, Mellit and Dar Elsalam.4. Training of community leaders and WASH committees on WASH facilities management using PHAST in Tawilla, Mellit and Dar Elsalam. 5. Monthly hygiene promotion campaigns (12 in each location, including home visits, public sensitization sessions, garbage cleaning preceded by cleaning tools and jerry cans cleaning campaigns, to be conducted by trained community hygiene promotion committees.6. Distribution of menstrual hygiene kits targeting 240 girls in grades seventh and eighth in basic schools in Tawilla, Mellit and Dar Elsalam. 7. Provision of 1500 Jerri cans for water collection to 750 vulnerable households in Tawilla, Mellit and Dar Elsalam 8. Carry out of 4 Media/radio messages and sessions on public health and hygiene promotion, one each 3 months (quarterly) 9. Implement CLTS approach in 9 communities the targeted locations in Tawilla, Mellit and Dar Elsalam localities

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Above can only select one but will have multiple channels including face-to-face with community leaders, billboards, complaints box, and help desk during distributions. For Community Feedback & response Mechanism (CFRM) Plan International Sudan will ensure that target communities are fully oriented and consulted on establishing the CFRM; how they want to raise feedback and complaints, to interact with the organization, and to be acquainted with the purpose of this system. CFRM will ensure multiple layers or channels are available for affected populations to report, share their feedback, suggestions for improvement and report for any type of violations or abuse of resources. This will include Focus Group Discussions, high visibility of Plan staff e.g. community meetings, sharing of appropriate contact details with community members (hotlines and national hotline for PSEA), billboards and suggestion boxes. During activities banners/signposts with PIS details will be visible, especially during distributions. This will help the organization to avoid risks of serious harm of the organizations’ image and profile as well sustaining good relationship with the people it is trying to serve and impacting their lives. Plan International’s Global Policy on Safeguarding Children and Young People puts procedures in place to prevent and deal with the actions/behavior of our staff, associates, visitors or us as an organization that result in violence against a child or young person and/or places them at risk of the same. Plan staff are obligated to ensure children and young people we work with are aware of our responsibilities to prevent and respond to any harm against them arising from actions and behaviors of our staff, associates and visitors, and the routes for reporting such incidents. In addition to Plan’s global safeguarding policies, Plan International Sudan has committed to the Joint Framework for Action on PSEA under leadership OCHA and in partnership with other humanitarian actors.
