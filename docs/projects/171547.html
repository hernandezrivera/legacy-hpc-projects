Project Summary

This project responds to rising WASH needs among drought, flood and conflict-affected IDP and host populations in urban and peri-urban areas in Lower Juba and Gedo regions. In areas where there are sufficient quantities of water, serious concerns remain over cross contamination between water and sanitation systems. This is exacerbated by poor hygiene practices which puts women, girls, boys and men at risk of water-borne infections. Lack of or poor access to latrines and water points affects women, young girls and boys in particular due to serious risk of exposure to violence especially sexual abuse. Persons living with disability on the other hand and those with limited mobility such as elderly feel discriminated as the few WASH facilities available are user-unfriendly.
The project will repair 15 existing shallow wells which are by far the most common sources of water. The most strategic ones will be selected through consultations with the communities. HAPEN will put in some deliberate efforts to consult all groups including men, women, girls, boys, the disabled and the elderly on the most appropriate location, safety, ease of access and operability of the facilities.
To make them fully functional, the wells will be cleaned, deepened and also be protected to meet needs of all the population groups. Protection will make them less vulnerable to contamination and physical damage. Pumps, fuel and spare parts for the 15 strategic shallow wells will be provided to serve at least 12,600 IDPs and host communities (2268 men, 2246 women, 3780 boys, 3906 girls and 1260 people living with disabilities). To put the control and sustain the functionality of the rehabilitated water facilities, they will be handed over to community water committees which will be trained in proper management. These committees will have women and the disabled represented.
HAPEN will construct 200 gender sensitive VIP latrines with hand washing facilities for the 12,600 IDPs and host communities in emergency need. The latrines will be designed to make them friendly to people living with disability (1260) and those with reduced mobility including the elderly (849).
200 hand wash facilities will be provided to promote handwashing and general hygiene. The handwashing is a key pillar of covid-19 prevention requirements and will be promoted during and beyond the pandemic. Hand washing with soap, proper cleaning of water containers, utensils and safe waste disposal will be highlighted. Hygiene promotion will target individuals, households, schools and health facilities to maximize impact of good hygiene practice at community level. Just like the rest of the project, the facilities benefiting will be bound by the requirements of gender, disability and any marginalized groups with a few to ensure their needs are addressed.
This project will mainstream gender and ensure that the needs of women, men, girls and boys are taken into account from the initial consultations on the needs to the design, construction/rehabilitation and use of water and sanitation facilities as well as provision of hygiene services. At the critical decision making level, the needs of women, girls, boys and persons living with disability will be taken into serious consideration. Towards this, teams spearheading consultations and collating the views to make decisions will have a representation of at least 30% women and 10% persons living with disability. Location of WASH facilities to be rehabilitated and constructed will be strategic and will consider proximity and easy access for women, girls, boys and people living with disability and those with limited mobility. This will contribute to improved health and productivity, reduce malnutrition and human rights violations associated with increased exposure as the vulnerable groups cover long distances to access these facilities. The activities will also ensure better school attendance especially for girls.

Hygiene promotion will target individuals, households, schools and health facilities to maximize impact of good hygiene practice in the communities.

Mitigation Measures

Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance-Ensure WASH staff, implementing partners and volunteers working with affected populations understand, sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries. -Ensure all staff, implementing partners and volunteers involved in distributions and in direct contact with beneficiaries have received training in prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA)-Ensure women, boys, girls and persons living with disability are consulted from planning to implementation to ensure their needs are factored.-Locate water sources in visible, central locations and not more than 500 metres from settlement-Distributions of hygiene items will take place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.)-Ensure all staff, implementing partners and volunteers involved in distributions and in direct contact with beneficiaries have received training in prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA)-HAPEN will at the earliest opportunity and regularly provide information and raise awareness about services available.-In cooperation with protection actors, enhance the capacity of community WASH workers to monitor, report and refer protection concerns (such as abuse and exploitation) in accordance with standard operating procedures.-In order to cater for different literacy levels, posters, community boards, camp committees, door-to-door, poster, social media and pictogram will be used to ensure all members in the community are reached. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities-Ensure that both women and men are part of water facility management, hygiene and sanitation committees and are fully engaged in the management of WASH facilities.-Consult women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, older persons and marginalised persons on location and design of water facilities to rehabilitate and toilets to construct to collect accurate information about their specific needs and preferences for the design and location of the facilities.-Ensure project committees are representative of all groups within the community (ie. gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, disability).-Ensure the location of WASH facilities considers the convenience of people living with disabilitiesWork with service providers to ensure that infrastructure where necessary is adapted to accommodate people with reduced mobility including smooth and non-slippery access paths and fit ramps for wheelchair access. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms-Set up mechanisms for submitting feedback that do not require the beneficiary exposing themselvesto project staff.-Respond to complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can/need to be put in place.-Organize awareness raising sessions so that people know how it works.-Consider a joint feedback mechanism with other sectors to minimize confusion.
