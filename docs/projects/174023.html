Project Summary

WASH Interventions will aim at ensuring that populations affected by displacement have reduced risk of exposure to water and sanitation related diseases. NRC will provide access to safe water and sanitation as well as promotion of better hygiene practices that lead to behaviour change.
NRC will construct new water points and, in some cases, rehabilitate existing water points or small networks of community based water systems to increase access to adequate and reliable water supply. NRC will ensure that water is safe for drinking through water quality testing at competent laboratory and provision of water treatment equipment, regular monitoring and continuous engagement of the community in water quality management activities. Water committees will be established and trained in the maintenance of water points and water quality management. The establishment, training and continuous support to WASH committees through refresher trainings will be critical in ensuring project interventions are strengthened by a comprehensive understanding of community dynamics that may impact positively or negatively on the programme.
NRC will support communities to construct their own latrines through contribution and/or materials, training of community member and local artisans in construction methods, maintenance of the latrines and technical supervision of the construction of latrine superstructure. In addition, NRC will promote construction of family/household latrines as opposed to communal latrines that have proven difficult to clean and maintain. While latrine use, cleanliness and maintenance will be the responsibility of the beneficiaries, NRC will support the latter with training, sanitation cleaning/construction tools, and related hygiene promotion activities. NRC will also ensure that there is proper lighting for safety of latrines use at night through provision of solar lighting, lamps or flashlights to families. Latrines will be gender-segregated and distinguishable to promote privacy, dignity and overall wellbeing of users. The location of latrines will be determined taking account of geological factors and following consultations with the community and addressing the safety of women and girls when accessing latrines, especially at night. Family latrines will be preferred over community latrines as the latter are difficult to keep clean and well-maintained. Handwashing stations will be placed at strategic points to promote handwashing. Latrine designs will ensure access by all, including people living with disabilities.
NRC will provide some suggested COVID-19 NFI kits which will include bars of soap, a bucket and disinfectant for household surfaces, in this case bleach which can be diluted with water to include IEC materials with instructions on how to use it. The household NFI kits that will be provided will not be any different than usual, additional soap and household bleach will be the difference.
Through hygiene promotion, NRC will seek to improve community hygiene and encourage behaviour change via household visits, community mobilization and focus group discussions and using information, education and communication posters with messaging on proper hygiene practices, such as handwashing at critical times, safe excreta and waste disposal, safe water chain and safe water management. Messages will specifically address how to reduce the risk of exposure to and transmission of communicable diseases. NRC will provide menstrual hygiene kits and training on their use to women and girls of childbearing age.
Target group
NRC target group will include IDPs and vulnerable members of host communities. Persons living with disabilities will be part of the beneficiary selection criteria.

1 - Besoins / Needs

In the NW/SW regions the majority of the displaced population source their water from unprotected/open water sources which are prone to contamination, especially due to the evident cases of open defecation at the water sources. Lack of protected water sources is a characteristic in the areas occupied by the IDPs. The 2020 Cameroon HRP anticipates 1,9 million people in need of WASH and 792,000 for shelter/NFI assistance in NW/SW regions. The most affected are : Menchum (85.6%), Ngo Ketunjia (68.8%), Donga Mantung (68.3%) and Momo (56.1%); Manyu (68.1%), Lebialem (68.1%) and Ndian (56.9%) are the most affected divisions in the south West region.(MSNA, August 2020). The divisions of Fako, Ndian, Lebialem, Meme and Manyu are the most affected in the SW region of Cameroon, while those of Momo, Mezam, and Ngo-Ketunjia have more displacement affected persons in the NW region of Cameroon. Poor water quality in NW/SW regions is attributed to the living conditions in the bush, reliance on surface water from polluted and unprotected water sources (rivers), lack of water treatment facilities and lack of kitchen items to boil water. The handling of water during collection, transportation and storage using open containers increases the risk of contamination, especially from the dirty jerry cans and poor storage methods at household level and in the bushes. Consequently, continuous water treatment is needed to avoid outbreaks of waterborne diseases. The major water problems and needs revolve around polluted and/or dilapidated water infrastructure, limited and damaged water points and overcrowding at water points. In emergency situations such as the one portrayed by the NW/SW crisis, outbreak of diseases is common and possible. This included epidemics like cholera. The risks associated with pandemics, WASH endemics and other communicable outbreak remain high and it is important to curb the spread of the disease - NRC’s WASH programme contributes to a reduction in the risk of transmission. On 17th September 2020 an outbreak of cholera in Buea health district was confirmed. . It is in this context that the NRC’s Humanitarian Response Plan on WASH response fits. MSNA assessment conducted in August reveal that in 71% of assessed village, population need more than 30 minutes for a round trip to fetch water i.e. takes more than 16minutes to the water point (one way). In more rural areas, people walk great distances of more than 1.5 km to get to the rivers where they collect water for domestic use. The rivers are often contaminated with feces and a plethora of pathogens, making the water unsafe for use. Existing sanitation facilities are full, abandoned or in disrepair, and often there is a high user to facility ratio. Poor water quality at water sources is due to multiple factors including open defection around the water points and limited community management of existing water points, leaving them prone to algae contaminations and pollution from waste debris in unprotected water sources.
