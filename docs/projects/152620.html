Project Summary

Wau collective sites have been served by camp management agencies since June 2016 following the outbreak of violent clashes which have been intermittently occurring since. Between ongoing violence and the recent initiative of moving individuals from the PoC to Wau town, there are currently 11,574 individuals residing in collective centres. In 2019, AFOD will continue to provide extensive camp management support in Wau’s five collective centres to meet the needs of the population in the way of: communicating with communities; site maintenance; partner coordination; information management and advocacy. As one of the few national NGOs in the CCCM sector in South Sudan, AFOD is in a particularly unique position and has a clear added value in ensuring strong community engagement and a sustainable impact in meeting the needs of those most affected by displacement in the country. The population of Wau and surrounding areas have been particularly affected by outbreaks of violence since early in the year resulting in significant influxes into collective sites. In 2019, AFOD expects to grow technical capacities and work toward gradual expansion of work in the collective centres whether they be existing or new.

Needs

Over recent months the continued outbreak of sporadic violence has posed major challenges not just for communities within and outside of Wau town but for humanitarian responders who have failed to access areas of Wau state. Impediments to access due to ongoing conflict have been compounded by local authorities implementing stricter restrictions of movement too (South Sudan Humanitarian Bulletin July 2018). Affected populations have lost the confidence to return home. In-turn, there is static presence as well as influxes into collective centres such as Lokoloko where 735 people have most recently been registered. Consequently, the need to enhance coordination amongst partners, advocate for the needs of the population and ensure strong information management in sites continues to grow. Beyond this, capacity building with authorities on humanitarian response and highlighting population needs is also fundamental to ensure multi-sector holistic programming. Of the 11,574 individuals in the collective centers as highlighted in Wau CC site profile August 2018, there is a breakdown of 52% female and 48% female. While it is semi-unusual to have more men than women in a camp setting, what is particularly challenging and unusual in the collective centres is to see 14% of the population being over 60 years of age. These individuals represent some of the most vulnerable in the region where particular attention must be paid to making sites accessible with a specific focus on ensuring site development or maintenance for this older population. Site planning is an ongoing challenge due to the little land space, currently standing at between 10-15m2 per person, which therefore exacerbates protection and health security for all members of the community and particularly vulnerable people. Another sector that is evidently posing concerns include WASH where there are too few taps and latrines per person. It is of fundamental importance that camp management continue to coordinate advocate for shrinking gaps in programming based on ongoing monitoring and gathering feedback from the community.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

AFOD will ensure through the complaint feedback mechanisms, any relevant information on sexual exploitation is disseminated to the IDPs to ensure exploitation of beneficiaries is reported on PSEA. Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Transparent feedback mechanism system has been established and will be strengthened in all collective sites. AFOD Shall ensure two-way feedback platforms are created so that IDPs can share their feelings and perception about the services being provided. AFOD will continue to strengthen the complaint feedback mechanisms/desk to receive feedback and complaints from the beneficiaries about the services, and the feedback and comments of the beneficiaries shall be referred and managed by the respective clusters and partners. During Community engagement, mobilization and sensitization, any feedback shall be transmitted to the IDPs, the project will promote harmony among the IDPs and services will be provided to every beneficiary regardless of race, tribe and ethnic dimensions. The site committees will be empowered to monitor, report and represent the needs of the community and will be facilitated to meaningfully participate in coordination mechanisms with humanitarian agencies and other stakeholders through support from the Camp Management. Further, the beneficiary satisfaction survey will be conducted which allow for a quantitative and comprehensive impartial approach to accounting for the wide population's voice independent of site governance committees.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

AFOD will ensure community engagement in monitoring and evaluation is part and partial of the project. The site committees and community members will be empowered to monitor, report and represent the needs of the community and will be facilitated to meaningfully participate in coordination mechanisms. The beneficiary satisfaction survey will be conducted which allow for a quantitative and comprehensive impartial approach to accounting for the wider population's voice independent of site governance committees. Community leaders or site committees will be involved in weekly site review meetings and coordination meetings with all partners and other stakeholders. AFOD and site communities will also carry out periodic assessments in the collective sites, periodic field visits to verify source of data for accuracy and reliability to guide decision making. Additionally, the camp management beneficiaries’ satisfaction survey will be conducted to assess the impact of the project and allow AFOD to make project changes where required based on the evidence.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

All the collective sites in Wau remain accessible to the population. The community members continue to move in and out of the collective sites with minimal hindrance. AFOD in collaboration with OCHA and RRC, will continuously identify barriers to access and monitor access to ensure safety and all beneficiaries access timely services. AFOD South Sudan will continue to work together with all the clusters to collaborate and coordinate all responses with other partners to address the barriers identified and ensure integration of services, quality of services and timely response are provided to the IDPs. Additionally, AFOD will ensure partners work together to provide assistance and protection in all the collective sites. AFOD will further strengthened the current coordination mechanisms with partners through appropriate information sharing, updated service mapping and collective decision making via working with existing and new camp self-governance structures for a better response.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

AFOD shall ensure the principle of do no harm is adhered to and no beneficiaries are discriminated against because of race, age and sex.

AFOD will establish and strengthen community complain and feedback mechanism in the collective sites etc. All GBV cases or deprivation or neglect will be report to the site committees, local authority and appropriate cluster for effective response.
