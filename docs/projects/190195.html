Project Summary

Tigrai National Regional state is a federated regional state of Ethiopia with nearly 8 million people living in an area of 54,593 km2. The region is divided into 7 administrative zones which are further subdivided into, 60 rural and 33 urban administrative Woredas (CSA, 2018/19). The current conflict in the Tigray region, and the spillover into Amhara and Afar regions account for more than half of the displacements figures and continue to cause large-scale mass displacements and human suffering. The situation is leading to a long-term, protracted-in-the-making crisis, which requires a strengthened protection and solutions response in all affected areas.
Adigrat, Adwa, Axum and Abyi-Ady are among the urban centers stricken hard the human sufferings inflicted the current conflict in Tigray. Adigrat is found in Eastern zone of Tigray while Adwa, Axun and Abyi-Ady are found in Central Zone of Tigray. They are urban centers that host 138,137 new populations in sites, requiring site management service. Out of the affected population 2.,174 are living in sever, 40,192 in extreme, 4,117 in extreme severity and 77,771 in catastrophic conditions according to CCCM Severity Analysis (2022). Displaced people are facing numerous challenges, including limited access to basic services (including health, water and sanitation facilities, education), and protection, as well as a loss of housing, land, other property, eviction and inadequate shelter in collective centers. Government led volunteer return program has already launched to the safe and secure Weredas of Tigray. The return exercise has already started in Mekelle. So far more than 13,000 households become beneficiary of the return program. Now the program is scale up to other towns of Tigray. Following the resume of elementary schools in Tigray, the IDPs from the unsafe weredas are being relocated to different places. However the IDPs living in the host community aren’t beneficiary of either the relocation or return program. . HOP is planning to capitalize these unique advantages and values added in the CCCM areas of operation gained in the course of implementing the past and current projects that in unison benefit 60, 808 IDPs, 67 percent of whom are females that were settling in 9 settlement sites. With the current HRP plan HOP is proposing to expand the CCCM service and continue to work alongside the federal CCCM Cluster Leads to meet the needs of IDPs in host community in Adigrat, Adwa, Axum and Abyi-Ady.
The overall goal of this plan is to improve living conditions and protection of 138,138 IDPs in sites and ensure equitable access to services and assistance of all persons in need, with a focus on moving towards durable solutions with full participation of the displaced and host communities.
The specific objectives of the proposed response plan:
• To ensure a multi-sectoral response that addresses the assistance and protection needs of IDPs in communal settings (collective sites, spontaneous sites, etc.) while advocating for durable solutions.
• Improve beneficiaries’ wellbeing through camp rehabilitation, improvement and upgrade works such as communal facilities construction, partitioning and drainage, as well as decommissioning/restoration of facilities used in displacement hosting.
• To ensure that the humanitarian response is accountable and predictable engaging other actors (GoE entities, agencies, organizations, CCCM global level) to respond and support displacement situations in a principled and timely manner.
• Support opportunities for displaced people to achieve durable solutions.
• To promote local ownership of site management through relevant and meaningful community participation, ensuring that responses are fit for purpose
• Capacitate Stakeholders (IDP representatives, government structures and humanitarian actors) to improve their coordination and implementation, and make them more prepared for new humanitarian crises, through training and support.

1. - Projects activities

Activity 1.1: Establish and maintain a site resident database; Agree on what data needs to be collected and entered into a storage system, Know, understand and apply applicable data protection policies to data collected. Service monitoring is conducted periodically and advocate partners for any service gap identified. Activity 1.2: Facilitate the engagement of the IDPs living in host community and IDP camps through the support of community-led initiatives and structures, such as, the establishment of site committees, and the coordination of regular community consultations conducted by service providers at site level; as well as, the establishment of a two-way communication system which will include feedback and complaint mechanisms (FCM). ). IDPs living in host community will be considered using the urban based approach, and be provided with equal access to information, complains and feedback mechanism, inclusion of governance structures, strengthening of existing service provision in cases when influx of IDPs have increased pressure on local resources. Activity 1.3: improve the living conditions through the mobilization of IDPs and surrounding communities to ensure the proper maintenance and improvement of the sites and its infrastructures including Cash for Work (CfW); as well as capacity building on how to repair and maintain site facilities, and provision of maintenance kits and tools to trained skilled workers. Activity 1.4: Facilitate access to basic services by CCCM actors to the IDPs living in the host community and at the site level; gaps in service provision will be identified and addressed (and monitored). Also, through referrals, HOP partners will escalate gaps that cannot be resolved at the site level to a higher coordination and HOP will be able to keep track of the gap determination progress. HOP will also support the development of contingency plans for spontaneous arrivals, unplanned returns, relocations and closure and other possible events that will affect the site, such as, floods, fire and other hazards. Activity 1.5: Mobilize a Community Feedback Mechanism (CFM) as an AAP channel for displaced households residing in the host community and at the settlement sites. Through a help desk and suggestion box in each site, HOP staff will receive, refer, follow-up and, where possible, close complaint and feedback cases. HOP will also compile CFM data to identify overall trends which will be used to inform advocacy with appropriate partners and clusters at sub-national level. Sector specific complaints mechanisms will also be promoted as possible/available. Consistent CFM/helpdesk access will also allow IDPs, such as those who are newly arrived to the towns mentioned in the HRP program and therefore did not receive distributions of food or other life-saving interventions, to be better able to access and explain their needs. Activity 1.6: Support community self-governance structures to the IDPs in the host community and in settlement sites (Site/Kebele Management/Coordination Committees, Women’s Committees, Youth Committees, etc.) through training of committee members, community mobilization events, consultations, and liaison between beneficiaries and stakeholders and train community self-governance structures about how to use the Community Feedback Mechanism. Raise awareness with the community self-governance structures about how to use the mechanism for their further awareness rising among IDPs in the host community and displacement site.1.7 HOP staff, associates and partners is aware of their role and responsibilities in keeping communities and stakeholders safe from any form of sexual exploitation or abuse

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

As part of its programming HOP will include complains, feedback and response mechanisms where IDPs will be able to share their concerns. CFRM platforms booths will be distributed in all collective centres that HOP will manage. The collection of the concerns will be regular. All IDPs will be informed of the schedule for collecting the information. The collected complaints will often be forwarded as referrals to the humanitarian actors for response. Further IDPs will be informed of the referral pathways with HOP following up to make sure IDPs are attended to satisfactorily. Where such pathways are not available IDPs will be provided with feedback indicating the same. HOP will be keen to uphold the confidentially of matters associated with GBV survivors as well as other protection cases that require similar confidentiality with clear pathways and feedback. HOP will ensure the CFRM platform provides is able to provide the needed assistance and IDPs are enlightened on the processes. A flier or banner indicating the processes and timelines will be provided in each and every site. This will be written in the local language and with diagrams where possible. Hop will reach out to visually impaired IDPs as well as those who are unable to read and write. Assessment of complaints of host community is useful to understand and `resolve any of the pressures that are perceived to be occurring on the host community as a result of IDPs settlement and create harmony between the IDPs and host community. Community complaints will be assessed through the monthly home to home visit of the community mobilizers to sample host community households. To systematize the process of collection of community complaints, HOP will develop and institutionalize structured Questionnaire on Community complaints and Feedback Mechanism. HOP will pursue a people-centered and rights-based approach to project coordination to promote protection and empowerment of minorities, women, and girls. Additionally, regular oversight will be provided, and monitoring visits conducted to observe that the service delivery by this project is up to the standard and up to the full satisfaction of the beneficiaries. Gender and age segregated data collection, analysis and feedback mechanisms will be institutionalized, to draw useful information on the status of children, women, youth, the elderly and PSN and provide timely and appropriate feedbacks.HOP will collaborate with CCCM cluster and service providers at project level to incorporate a robust referral mechanism based on the data gathered and analyzed from the complaint, feedback and response mechanism (CFRM) component and work towards a timely turnaround from the service providers at camp level. HOP will also mainstream Gender, PSEA, AAP and inclusion and has dedicated cluster focal points to these cross cutting AORs particularly in the design and implementation of communal facilities in the camp to make them gender-sensitive and inclusive. HOP will ensure participation in the cluster tri-coordination meetings with Shelter and WASH. CCCM will focus on a people-cantered approach ensuring inclusive and representative self-governance in the way that IDP camps are governed and that IDP leaders are consulted in decision making about relocation and return. HOP emphasizes the centrality of accountability to affected people in its response and will continue to advocate for zero-tolerance on sexual exploitation and abuse against the affected people.
