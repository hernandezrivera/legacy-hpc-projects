Project Summary

CPPLI is proposing a 11-month project that focused to contribute in positively impacting conflict affected children and adolescents’ well-being, enhancing their resilience and reducing their stress thereby restoring sense of normalcy through quality child protection and psychosocial support intervention. The project basically is proposed to be implemented in 5 most critical locations in Adamawa (Maiha, Lamurde, Guyuk, Ganye and Mayo belwa). In line with the sectors’ geographic priorities, child protection activities will be implemented across all the 5 LGAs. The project theory of change (ToC) aims to strengthen prevention and response to child protection risks through community-led approach and durable solutions.
The project fundamentally will achieve 3 main outcomes. The first outcome focus to provide comprehensive case management services through an individualized direct support, linkages and referrals of children and their families affected by crisis who face child protection concerns including children that are trafficed. The second outcome is to support children, adolescents and caregivers affected by crisis to access quality mental health and psychosocial support services and information on childcare and protection through age and gendered appropriate approaches. The third outcome aim at Strengthening community-based protection mechanism including social workers and child protection actors to support prevention and response interventions on issues of child protection concerns.

1 - Needs

In line with HRP 2022, an estimated 2,045,461 million people (1,791,557 million children and 253,904 caregiver) needs child protection services across the BAY states. Approximately 2 million children need child protection services due to a breakdown/absence of child protection systems and community-based safety networks. Symptoms of child emotional distress were reported by 271 respondents in the MSNA, which was most prevalent in IDPs and returnee households. The DTM Site and Location Assessments estimate that 442,600 children have been displaced due to conflict/insurgence, which has a significant impact upon a child’s emotional wellbeing. The conflict has impacted children and adolescents (boys and girls) and people with disabilities in different ways and their vulnerabilities as well as coping mechanism vary. Children and adolescents especially girls have been targeted with rape, abduction, sexual abuse and violence, recruitment into armed forces/armed groups, exposure to neglects and exploitation as well as to serve as sex slaves and conscripted into a broad spectrum of roles including serving as spies for BH. Adamawa have been specifically impacted by the conflict and other natural disaster exposing children to human rights violations, harmful labor, sexual abuse and violence, abduction, targeted killings, separation from families due to force displacement, excessive exploitation, child trafficking and other child right violation.
