Project Summary

Community Based Protection activities directly benefit residents in Camp. Protection activities will focus on capacity-building of Rohingyas to plan and implement protection and psychosocial support activities, with a focus on Gender-Based Violence (GBV) and support of individuals at heightened risk through case management and counselling. The project will ensure safe spaces for women and children, support households in listening and identifying their psychosocial needs, engage men and youths in addressing the protection needs for women and children, and ensured referral services.
92 Volunteers will be selected in discussion with the community and two Volunteers in a unit (one man and one woman) aging from 25-50, having basic education and commitment for social development, well acceptance of people, maintains confidentiality will lead the preparation of Community Based Protection Action Plan (CBPAP) and later for planning and implementing the community based protection activities.
Following the similar criteria, the Refugee community will form a 12-member Community Based Protection Committee (CBPC) comprised of 6 male and 6 female members will lead the preparation of CBPAP by assessing the protection risks, vulnerabilities and capacities and identify the needs around supports and services. Men, women, persons with disabilities, and elderly persons of the each majhee blocks will have the opportunity to participate in assessing risks through FGD, interview, group discussion from their perspectives and be involved in the implementation and monitoring the progress of their action plan. The Volunteers will visit each target household on a weekly basis to listen to and identify the at-risk individuals, those are in need of protection support. During the household visits, Caritas will provide primary level of psychosocial and case management services to the identified individuals who are in need of it. These visits will also identify the critical needs and the required services and supports.

1 - Identified Needs

Strengthen access and quality of protection for individuals at heightened riskDifferent types of violence are present in the camp due to multiple factors where, Gender-Based Violence (GBV) domestic violence, polygamy and early marriage practices are reportedly increasing, and most families experience abuse and neglect in the home in a way or another. Patterns of trafficking and exploitation, including sexual and labor exploitation, also threaten individuals who are already the most vulnerable both in camps and in host communities. Therefore, there is a need to (1) provide basic PSS to respond to distress, which will in turn contribute to mitigate negative coping mechanisms, and (2) provide immediate, life-saving support and protection to the individuals at heightened risk, including by specialized case management. Engagement of refugees in protectionA main component is capacitating and empowering refugees in protection knowledge and skills, including psychosocial support, so that they can support individuals in need. By understanding better existing positive community dynamics and drawing from them, community-led initiatives should be promoted. Engagement of all community members in protection, including men, boys and religious leaders, is needed to ensure a supportive and protective environment for all wherever the community is in the future, considering the many uncertainties surrounding them. Strengthening peaceful coexistenceThere is need for social system strengthening and peaceful coexistence between both Rohingya communities and host communities, which should be provided in close partnership with the local authorities and according to the gaps identified with them. Along with this protection support for Host communities, should there be a focus on activities gathering the two communities when possible, encouraging peacebuilding approaches and mutual learning. CB has a feedback mechanism with different entry points for feedback collection- electronic devices, Hotline, and complaint boxes. These feedbacks are processed, and regularly analyzed by the Safeguarding and M&E team and shared with higher authority to action & program team to re-design the programming. Caritas Bangladesh has a national policy for PSEA, which is adapted for the Rohingya response context. Complaints can be collected through the several entry points mentioned above and are directly processed by the PSEA focal point. Trainings on PSEA and how to report are included in the regular trainings of the volunteers and the staff. Supporting Rohingya civil society- The project capacitates refugees to help themselves, strengthens community protection mechanisms, and enhances resilience wherever the community will be in the future, in Bangladesh or in Myanmar. The objective for the end of 2020 is that refugees can conduct protection outreach and provide counselling without staff supervision. Supporting Bangladeshi civil society- CB is a renowned civil society organization in Bangladesh, partnering regularly both with grassroot organizations and with the government. Caritas Development Institute provides trainings to other organizations and produces continuous research on development work in Bangladesh. Strengthening its capacity contributes to sustainability as it has the potential to scale-up, partner with NGOs and governmental organizations, and foresee programming in CXB on the long-term. COVID 19 integrationIn all activity the principle of social distance will be ensured. COVID prevention activities will be integrated in all activity planning and implementation. Environment and DRRBetter responding to improve the environment and activities related to DRR including monsoon preparedness CB has been working with sector and the PERU team. Capacity building of volunteers is a regular activity of CB aligned with the Protection Sector.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: Training Rohingya refugees, men and women in protection principles, PFA, GBV, anti-trafficking, PSEA, PSS, COVID 19, DRR & monsoon preparedness to become Barefoot Counsellors (BFCs). Activity 2: Deploying Community Volunteers (CV) to conduct household visits to deliver basic PSS; share information on referral mechanisms, COVID 19, monsoon preparedness, services and support available to community members; special focus on female-headed households, individuals with disabilities and unaccompanied children as well as child-headed households. Activity 3: CV identify specific protection risks in the areas and share with the Protection Focal Point for protection monitoring purposes. The protection monitoring that is done at the field level is meant to inform Caritas Bangladesh (CB) management in Cox’s Bazar of the current needs and concerns of the community, and to ensure programming on the ground addresses these.Activity 4: The GBV case management team consists of two GBV officers (including a senior psychologist). The case management services that are provided through the community center are safe, confidential and survivor centered. Activity 5: CB will roll-out GBV prevention sessions targeting men and boys, structured and gender-sensitive PSS sessions with facilitators using No Strings International (NSI)’s film (written and produced in Rohingya language), theatre and puppetry as well as other creative arts approaches. These sessions would also include cyclone and monsoon seasons preparedness activities, and practical measures to mitigate the effects of landslides and flooding (to be co-facilitated with our Shelter team). Activity 6: CB is the Protection Focal Point and Child Protection Focal Point in Camp 4 Extension. CB’s protection team attends and actively participates in the Protection Working Group (PWG) and its sub-sector meetings. CB’s engagement with the PWG sending monthly 5W reports, SitReps and participation in RPAs. BFC Community Centers in Camp 4 Extension has been used by other partners and civil society organisations in the camp for their activities (including Isolation & quarantine centre). CB also works closely with the CiC in seeking approval prior to piloting new activities in the camps and participates in coordination meetings with Site Management and Block Leaders.Activity 7: CB has a feedback mechanism for the Rohingya response with different entry points for feedback collection: electronic devices, hotline, and complaint boxes. Feedback is received and regularly analyzed by the M&E team and shared with program team to re-design the programming. Activity 8: Caritas Bangladesh has a national policy for PSEA. Complaints can be collected through the several entry points mentioned above and are directly processed by the PSEA Focal Point. BFCs and CB staff are trained on PSEA and reporting mechanisms.
