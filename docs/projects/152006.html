Project Summary

Overall Objective: Strengthen access and improve inclusive, quality and equitable basic education for 1,050,000 conflict-affected displaced, returnees, host communities school-aged girls and boys aged 3 to 17 years in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe states of North-East Nigeria. Specific Objective: 1. Strengthen access and improve inclusive, quality and equitable basic education for 1,050,000 children (50% girls & 50,000 children from 3-5 years old) through the provision of essential teaching, recreational materials, support to enrolment drive campaigns (EDC) and Girls for Girls (G4G). 2. Improve the capacity of 4,000 teachers and volunteer teachers (at least 40% females) in psychosocial support, education in emergencies, special education, quality ECD, classroom management and pedagogy. 3. Increase access to safe and equitable and inclusive education for 300,000 children (50% girls) in safe and protective environments by establishing learning spaces with gender-sensitive WASH facilities and water. 4. Enhance the response and protection capacity of education service providers in school governance in emergencies through establishing and/or activating Monitoring of School-based Management Committees (SBMC) in targeted LGAs and building the capacity of 2,400 SBMC (12,000 members) on SBMC effectiveness and provision of education services to out-of-school children, education-in-crisis, and education for vulnerable children including girls and disabled with focus on hard-to-reach areas.

Needs

The situation in northeast Nigeria continues to be a highly complex humanitarian crisis. The eight-year conflict has affected over 14 million people, with approximately 1.7 million currently displaced, predominantly in the north-eastern states of Borno, Yobe, Adamawa. More than 50 per cent of those affected are children, including many unaccompanied minors or children orphaned by the crisis. The conflict, which has seen the direct targeting of schools and other facilities, has disrupted livelihoods for millions of households, leaving over seven million people in need of humanitarian assistance. More than 2.2 million children need urgent education services. Prior to the crisis, the northeast had the highest percentage (52 per cent) of children who had never attended school in Nigeria. Restoring access to education through the provision of safe learning spaces in a child-friendly environment with gender-sensitive WASH facilities, essential learning and recreational materials in emergency contexts for children who are conflict-affected, displaced, returnees and/or in host communities is essential to rebuild them from the effects of conflict and displacement. This is a key priority for the education sector. Lack of educational opportunity combined with poverty plays a pivotal role in seducing children and youth to join armed groups and violence to which they are in fact victims. Education provides an alternative and an opportunity to break away from this vicious trap both in the short-term and later in life. UNICEF aims to improve quality and inclusiveness of basic education for 1,451,500 conflict-affected children (50% girls) through innovative teaching and learning approaches and provision of learning materials in formal and non-formal educational setting including integrated Quranic education. Attention will also be given to the pre-primary school children by proving sensational ECD kits and building the capacity of ECD teachers. To increase access to education to 1,050,000 children (50% girls), UNICEF will construct 400 TLSs and rehabilitate existing 600 classrooms benefiting and providing additional learning spaces for 80,000 children. As part of the support to inclusiveness, UNICEF will construct and/or rehabilitate gender‑sensitive 600 WASH facilities in schools and learning spaces benefiting a minimum of 300,000 children (with an average of 500 children per school/TLS). To enhance the response and protection capacity of education service providers in school governance in emergencies and effectiveness, at least 12,000 School Management Committee (SMBC) members (2,400 SBMCs) will be trained for improved capacity in education-in-crisis, education for vulnerable children including girls, and the role of community in providing education services to out of school children.

Activities

In 2019, UNICEF will focus on increasing access to quality and inclusive basic education for 1,251,500 school-aged conflict-affected, displaced persons, returnees and host community children (50% girls) in the targeted LGAs in 3 states (Adamawa, Borno, and Yobe). The specific activities include the following: Access: 1. Construct 400 temporary learning spaces. 2. Rehabilitate 600 existing classrooms. 3. Construct and rehabilitate 600 gender-sensitive WASH facilities in schools and learning spaces with connecting to water sources. Quality: 4. Train 4,000 teachers and volunteer teachers (at least 40% females) in psychosocial support, education-in-emergencies, special education, quality ECD and classroom management and pedagogy. 5. Provide essential teaching, learning and recreational materials to school-aged children: (a) 25,000 School in a Box, (b) 5,000 Recreational Kits, (c) 951,000 bags and school materials, (d) 500 ECD kits and adolescent kits. Response Capacity of Education Service Providers: 6. Establish/reactivate at least 2,400 SBMCs in conflict-affected schools. 7. Train 12,000 SMBC members on SBMC effectiveness, protection of schools and provision of education services to out-of-school children, education-in-crisis, and education for vulnerable children including girls and disabled with focus on hard-to-reach areas.
