Project Summary

According to Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), an estimated 5,749,000 South Sudanese women, men and children will not have sufficient access to food and livelihoods in 2019. GBI seeks to deliver a 12 months emergency Food Security and Livelihoods activities to critically vulnerable Households through Provision of Emergency Food Relief and Livelihoods Resilience programmes for the vulnerable people in South Sudan (Bor South, Pibor, Aweil East and Magwi Counties).
In line with the FSL cluster strategy and the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), GBI is proposing to scale up response in these locations through distribution of dry season kits (vegetable and fishing kits), livestock vaccination and training of CAHWs, cash transfers, assets creation and unconditional food) supporting existing partners responding or planning a response.
The overall objective of this project is to improve the food security and livelihood status of vulnerable households (IDPs, returnees and host) in Jonglei, Eastern Equatoria and Northern Bahr El Ghazal states. This project is designed to reduce vulnerabilities and risk associated with the current food insecurity and poor economic conditions as a result of conflict, seasonal variability and displacement. A total of 10,000 beneficiaries comprising (returnees, IDPs and host) will be targeted directly with lifesaving livelihoods and food security interventions. The project will require $500,000 to provide the intended services and activities throughout the intervention period.

Needs

Conflict continues to drive severe food insecurity across South Sudan, with armed clashes and attacks against civilians limiting access to agricultural fields and livelihood opportunities (USAID 2018). As majority of South Sudanese have agriculture-based livelihoods which cannot be maintained once displaced, these communities become extremely vulnerable (WFP/UNICEF/FAO 2018). By January-March 2019, 5.2 million people (49% of the total population) are estimated to continue to face acute food insecurity, with 36,000 people in catastrophic conditions (IPC Phase 5) (Sept IPC 2018). Despite improvements in crop conditions, dry conditions in parts of Central Equatoria, Eastern Equatoria, Jonglei, and Northern Bahr el Ghazal states could undermine crop production during the upcoming harvest, FAO reports, 2018. In addition, infestations of fall armyworm—an invasive crop pest that consumes maize and other crops —have damaged cereal crops in some areas. Reduced cereal production, especially in the bread basket regions of Equatorial due to conflict, and the increase in disease outbreaks and other seasonal and climatic shocks such as floods, drought spells and crop and livestock diseases and pests, including the fall army worm infestation, have also further undermined people’s resilience by depleting household assets (HNO 2018). According to CRS, below-average rainfall in many parts of the counties of Bor and Pibor is expected to negatively impact crop yields in the September/October harvest. In addition, the dry conditions have facilitated the spread of crop diseases and pests such as fall army worm (FAW). Following a government decree, several large cattle camps in Kajo-Keji and Magwi Counties were disbanded between February and March. This situation led an estimated 60,000 people, along with 200,000 heads of cattle, to consolidate and move along Kit-Nesitu corridor, south of Juba, ultimately aiming to reach Bor County, Jonglei State as the final destination. The migration was not seasonal, and such a consolidated movement along non-traditional routes in difficult conditions raised potential concerns for the safety of cattle keepers, as well as for the spread of cattle diseases among the herd (REACH 2018). According to FSL cluster gap analysis of the month of September 61% of the population in Jonglei State was not reached by humanitarian actors while the whole state remains in emergency (IPC 4). Continued insecurity along roads and a lack of adequate access to food across South Sudan's greenbelt region in Central and Eastern Equatoria States have caused humanitarian needs to worsen over the second quarter of 2018. Many areas in these states are largely inaccessible to humanitarian actors due to insecurity (REACH 2018). Immediate action to maintain and save livelihoods will have lasting impact; access to farming, fishing and livestock-safeguarding inputs and services have the potential to significantly improve food security (FAO 2018). By working to strengthen the resilience of households to food and nutrition insecurity through diversifying, strengthening and securing livelihood resource bases for at risk households in order to accumulate assets, GBI will support agriculture, livestock and fisheries and urban livelihoods and provide protection to GBV risks to beneficiaries by creating awareness, inclusion of women in decision making groups.

Activities

1) Livestock vaccination and treatment on common diseases2) Provision of drugs and training of Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) on disease investigation, vaccination and treatment and best management practices to increase output. 3) Distribution of seeds, tools, fishing kits and quick maturing vegetable seeds.4) Capacity building and training on demonstration fields on best management practices such as disease management, post-harvest handling, value addition and marketing. 5) Food assistance for the vulnerable and affected population.6) Forming of women or producer groups to be served and trained with ox plough to expand agricultural land. 7) Cash allocations for the most vulnerable people, the elder, widows etc. 8) Procuring, operation and maintenance of cold chain for safe storage of livestock drugs.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Protection is key in this project as 70% of beneficiaries of target population are women and 30% men, 46% are children (31% girls and 15% boys). Agriculture being largely practiced by women in South Sudan, to obtain greater output more resources, time and focus is put on them. Special support will be given to women groups in form of cash transfers, training, and loan for assets restocking. In South Sudan, cattle keeping is largely men's activity involving a lot of hardships in looking after because animals have to go longer distances in look for pasture and water. CAHWs will contain more men than women. Separation of women and men in using facilities at distribution points is an activity aimed at protecting women/girls from GBV related cases and provide comfort of use of the facilities. Having women occupy half or more of positions in groups gives them the chance to participate in design and implementation - raising risk factors connected with the projects. Up to 80% of women known to be engaged in agriculture globally, this creates a case for protection.
