Project Summary

"BRAC Child Protection sector has been implementing child protection interventions since 2017 in both refugee camp and host communities across Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazilas of Cox’s Bazar, contributing to strengthen community based child protection mechanism in close collaboration with the community people through providing psychosocial support, case management, life skills based session etc. BRAC will operate these activities in 13 camps and 11 host community.
Heading towards 2022, BRAC’s Child Protection Sector aims to strengthen the child protection system and services across the camps and host community ensuring the continuation of existing services e.g. unstructured PSS, structured PSS and adolescent engagement activities etc. with a priority focus on integrated services for children with disabilities. In every component of Child Protection programme, children with disabilities will be supported through the different services. BRAC will continue the strong coordination it maintains with CPSS, CMTWG, ERP through attending the meeting, workshop, providing technical support where it is required. BRAC CP will also coordinate with MHPSS Working Group, Age & disability working group, ECD Working Group and Youth Working Group through active participation and contribution. BRAC will strongly coordinate with government e.g. department of social service in referring the cases for the support.
BRAC committedly applies BRAC’s Safeguarding Policy, Child Protection Code of Conduct ensuring the safeguarding of the staff and ensuring the SEA related cases. CPMS guideline will be followed while implementing the interventions to ensure “Do no Harm” for the children and adolescents. Through recruiting volunteers from the Rohingya and Host community, and engage the adolescents in various activities through skills development, dialogue within the community among a range of activities, BRAC intends to promote empowerment in the communities that it works in.
Child Protection Sector will actively consider the emergency preparedness and DRR issues during the monsoon season and develop contingency plan whereby emergency child and caregivers information centres which are operative during disasters. Caregivers and parents are supported to find and get information for their children. In case of any child or adolescent needing specials care during such emergency or crisis, will be able to avail one-to-one counselling at these centres. Regular assessment of the facilities and taking precaution to address the DRR issues and complaints response mechanism will be in general focus for all the CP interventions BRAC will implement.
The introduction of Pashe Achhi Model (Beside You) in 2020 mainly because of the impact that lockdown had on families during COVID 19, has proven effective in the Child protection services as an alternative modality based on telecommunication and BRAC will maintain this approach when it requires. Given the prevailing nature of COVID-19; BRAC will ensure COVID 19 safety measures during implementation of all its activities.
The upcoming JRP will target 27,275 beneficiaries with composition of children, adolescent, community people, stakeholder and child protection staff in 13 camps and 11 host area under Teknaf and Ukhiya upazilas."

1 - Identified Needs

"Child Protection services comprise a huge number of target population as boys and girls represent 52% of all Rohingya population (UNHCR Population factsheet, 31st October 2021). There are a significant number of children exposed to child labour, child trafficking, child marriage, child separation as well as violence towards children in the camps. A joint UNICEF and Government of Bangladesh assessment (An Assessment on the Coverage of Basic Social Services in Cox's Bazar District-2017) on basic services reported that in Cox’s Bazar’s host community, 35% of respondents believe marriage below the age of 18 is acceptable, higher than the national average (31%). Some Rohingya have adopted negative coping mechanism such as early marriage of their girls, more since the adverse impact of lockdown and the J-MSNA 2021 reports that 1% of girls aged 10-17 were married off, which may seem a small proportion but still indicative of a risky and negative trend. Rohingya household reported that at least one child is engaged in child labor. This is also a great concern in the host community as a significant number of working children are involved in hazardous works. According to JSMNA 2021 report and BRAC’s experience, children from large Rohingya households tend to get unattached from their families and become wayward due lack of proper parental supervision. Children moving away from families was also found in case of 4% households having persons with disabilities, compared to 1% of households without persons with disabilities (JMSNA 2021). Unaccompanied and separated children also need special attention as they are more prone to different forms of protection concerns. Violence against children remains a significant issue according to the Child Protection Assessment Report (May 2020) and many parents and caregivers have voiced concern about the safety of their children. Primary concerns include kidnapping and/or trafficking (77%), physical violence (53%), child marriage (47%), exploitative work and child labour (49%). According to population fact sheet by UNHCR (31st October 2021), 1% of total population is person with disabilities who are the most vulnerable. The situation of the camps is known to generate anxiety, aggression, depression and various sorts of psychosocial stress which need to be addressed and treated properly through a more comprehensive and structured Mental Health and Psycho-social support (MHPSS). Limited scope of pre-vocational skills related activities for the adolescents have also exacerbated this problem. Social cohesion needs to be ensured to decrease different forms of tension between the host community and the Rohingya people. The project will, therefore, adopt a comprehensive approach catering to 0-18 years old children and adolescents and above 18 years (both boys and girls in different camps and host community) will be reached with the services as well. A holistic approach will be applied to provide services e.g. case management, structured psycho-social support, life skills, pre-vocational skills and service provision for children with disabilities. It will also engage community people and other important stakeholders to strengthen the overall child protection mechanism."
