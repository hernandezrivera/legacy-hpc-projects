Project Summary

The project contributes to education sector objectives by providing increased safe and equitable availability and access to in-person and formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children and youth, and enhancing quality of formal and non-formal education for children and youth. By encouraging regular attendance and enrollment, the programme contributes to providing protection to children from reverting to negative coping mechanism such as child labor and child marriage in addition to the education. Based on the 2022-2023 Education Sector Response Framework, the programs numbered 1.1a, 1.2a, 1.3, 1.6c, 1.6e, 16f, 1.6j, 1.6k, 1.7a, 1.7b, 1.7c, 2.1a, 2.2a will be implemented.
Within the scope of the project, it is aimed to cover the basic costs of 25 schools, 19 of which are accredited to the ministry of education in the Idleb region and 6 of which are not accredited in the Aleppo region, are currently operated by the IHH Humanitarian Relief Foundation, located in A'zaz, Salama, Shamarin, Al Bab, Ariha, Harim, Dana, Atma, Babisqa, Kafr Kafaldin, Mashhad Ruhin, Sarmada, Idleb, Maaret Tamsrin, Kelly and all targeted sub-districts are in the areas of Education PIN 2023 severity levels 4-5.
In one of the schools in the Aleppo region, basic literacy, mathematics education and rehabilitation services are provided to children with physical disabilities and autism. In the other two schools, the same education are provided to visually impaired and autistic children. The number of students studying in these schools is 160. These schools aim to reach children with disabilities in safe and specially planned areas. It is foreseen that 4% of the total project beneficiaries will be disabled.
Access to basic education will be provided by the rehabilitation of existing classrooms in schools, and within this scope, windows, doors, walls, floors and school gardens will be rehabilitated. WASH facilities will also be rehabilitated in a clean, healthy, accessible way for both genders (male, female) and suitable for the disabled.
Incentives to teachers and other education personnel will be provided as monetary and non-monetary to support retention and contribute to enhanced quality of education for learners. In addition to that, winterization fuel support for 3 months will be provided to the teachers as an incentive from IHH in-kind funding.
A report card festival will be held twice a year for children. Children will have attendance grades on their report cards and summer self-learning training kits will be given to children who attend at least 80% of the lessons.
The quality of education will be improved through contextualized and targeted teacher training to increase awareness for PSEA-CSG and within each school by delivering trainings in coordination with PSEA Network and Child Protection SC. Conducting MEAL activities: field visits, PDM, MEAL reports, feedback mechanism.
Due to COVID-19, there will be comprehensive precautionary measures in the 25 schools, including periodic sterilization process for classrooms and WASH facilities, awareness sessions for students, training for teachers and educational staff, and sterilization provision materials for all schools. The budget of this item will be covered by IHH Humanitarian Relief Foundation with in-kind funding.

3 - Needs

According to Unicef's Humanitarian Action for Children 2022, children are bearing the brunt of the 11-year-old conflict in the Syrian Arab Republic. Over 13.4 million people (6.1 million children) require assistance and 7 million people are internally displaced (3.1 million children). 6.9 million children and teachers need education services (49 per cent male and 51 per cent female).According to HNO2022, self-reported attendance at national level is is 82 per cent. Self-reported attendance does not capture the impact of school shifts and/or closures. Attendance rates between boys and girls were similar but older children (12-17 years) were less likely to attend than younger children (5-11 years). Economic factors, including children working to support the household, continue to be a key reason why children are not attending school. At national level, the data reveals that 18 per cent are out of school and half of the children who are not in school never enrolled. Rates of non-attendance were highest in Idleb (28 per cent), Aleppo (26 per cent) (as project locations). Over a quarter of households with school aged children indicated that their children are not in school because there is no school to send their child to. In 1,017 assessed camp sites in northwest, there are only 196 schools. There is an average of one operational classroom for every 54 school aged child. The highest ratios are found in Idleb (1:178), Damascus (1:101) and Rural Damascus (1:94).

4 - Implementation of activities

IHH aims to discourage drop-out and increase meaningful quality learning, also, to secure the functioning of schools through: Promote child safeguarding (CSG) & education quality in 25 schools in NW Syria by delivering training for Teachers on will (CSG), PSEA, & COVID-19 by the IHH field team, based on three guides included a guidebook for education personnel working in emergencies, CSG guide, and Code of Conduct (CoC). Additionally, teachers & edu personnel will sign IHH’s CSG Policy & CoC. IHH will go with this method only in order to avoid crowd gathering and achieve the widespread of the intended messages. Determine children's educational levels and enable the teachers to plan and focus on the needed educational activities for each student by undergoing an ASER test. Improve children's well-being and learning capacity through recreational activities that will be carried out by the classroom teachers who will be provided with recreational materials. Ensure safe and quality learning environments by the provision of monthly required operational supplies. Conducting rehabilitation works. Provision of Education and COVID-19 prevention materials to schools, teachers, and children, Conduct periodic sterilization process. PTAs establishment per school. Quarterly meetings for PTAs members will be held to set plans for the upcoming period. Conducting MEAL activities: field visits, PDM, MEAL report, feedback mechanism.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

The prior risk is security, as schools have been one of the main targets in the conflict. Although the situation has slightly improved due to the cessation of hostilities, it is unknown how long it will last. The security situation is not one that IHH can play a major role in mitigating, besides providing additional safety and security training for teachers to use with their students if security concerns arise. IHH has chosen relatively safer locations and has historically been locations where IDPs flee to, not where people are fleeing from. Inside learning facilities risks related to bullying, corporal punishment and other threats should be addressed. Child safeguarding measures including violence prevention, feedback mechanisms and engagement with parents and caregivers, and appropriately supported teachers and education personnel are key to mitigating these threats. In order to avoid any CSG and PSEA violation by any education worker, IHH will deliver training on CSG, PSEA and CoC for all educational staff. In case any education worker violates the CoC/CSG/PSEA; IHH will assign a committee including ‘field education team, CSG focal point, CP advisor, and education supervisor’ to verify the incident of violation and communicate with the ED in order to take the appropriate disciplinary actions based on IHH’s CSG/PSEA policy.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The quality of education will be improved through contextualized and targeted teacher training to increase awareness for PSEA/CSG within each school by delivering trainings in coordination with PSEA Network and Child Protection SC. In any case of violation of the CSG/PSEA; IHH will promptly assign a committee including field education team, CSG focal point, CP advisor, and education supervisor to verify the incident of violation and communicate with the ED in order to take the proper disciplinary actions based on IHH's CSG policy. Furthermore, for PSEA investigations, complaints are received through IHH channels, the complaints may be addressed directly to IHH focal point, who will communicate the case to the Executive Director.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Deterioration of the general security situation in the project areas. L: Low, I: High. M1: Closely monitor and follow up security situations and coordinate with the education cluster and follow the instructions issued to maintain the project's safety and employees. IHH will obtain reliable information from available sources, use a rigorous monitoring mechanism, suspend educational activities or move them to safer areas. In coordination with cluster, IHH will look for SLP or distance education opportunities. Risk2: All staff and beneficiaries may be unable to carry out face-to-face activities if COVID 19 cases increase in targeted locations L: Low, I: High. M2: IHH will take into account all Donor and Clusters and Local Government recommendations in conjunction with WHO and Health sector guidelines. Depending on the situation, the number of beneficiaries per session can be reduced, and the online modality can be used to carry out activities in coordination with the donor and the cluster.Risk3: Child safety incidents in school due to bullying and overburdened teachers M3: Training of partners and local teams on Child safety, development of schools’ specific child safety policies; establishment of complaint mechanisms in school; Security/safety training for organization, students and school staff.
