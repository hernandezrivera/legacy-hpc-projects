Project Summary

The project will contribute to the HRP strategic objectives 2 and 3 and the Shelter cluster strategic objective 2. It will do so by improving the living conditions and increase the protection of acutely vulnerable Bedouin and herding households located in 15 communities living in Area C in the North of the West Bank through upgrading substandard shelters and the provision of protective materials/tools (general objective).
The project is based on several needs assessment conducted by PUI in 2018 and 2019 that identified the most pressing shelter needs of vulnerable households living in substandard houses in the targeted communities by this project. The communities identified are herding and Bedouin communities mostly living in shacks and tents. The types of shelter and their specific setting increase household’s exposure to potential consequences of adverse weather conditions such as: flooding, winter storms, extreme heat in the summer as well as access to basic services and geographical isolation. Furthermore, isolated vulnerable households are more likely to be attacked by settlers and most often in times where only a few people are around, in many cases only lonely women, elderly people and children. Another aspect of the coercive environment present in Area C is the procedures around the Israeli permit and planning regime such as demolitions, which are regularly occurring in the herding and Bedouin communities, which add to the multiple layers of vulnerability. In order to respond to the identified needs and to contribute to the HRP and cluster objectives, PUI and its partner will provide two type of assistance.
First, the project will upgrade 60 substandard shelters in order to reduce the exposure of vulnerable households to man-made and natural disasters, harsh weather conditions, risk of water borne diseases and health complications, by enabling them to live with adequate standards which respect their dignity and wellbeing. The scope of the upgrading will be tailored to the needs of each family, with a maximum value of 5000 USD per household. The work will be carried out by using a mixed approach of cash and material supply, when appropriate. At the beginning of the project, during the assessment phase, we will identify the beneficiary households with skilled workers that are able and willing to implement some or all of their shelter upgrades.
Second, the project will provide protective materials and tools to 60 households who are most at risk of settler violence and/or demolition. The selection of protection material and tools will be tailored to the needs of each family, with a maximum value of 500 USD per household. This may include fencing, first aid kit, fire extinguishers etc.
PUI will partner with MAAN; activities will be divided geographically according to the knowledge of and experience with the communities by each partner. MAAN has previous experience in similar actions including the rehabilitation of inadequate shelters in the Jordan Valley in herding and farming communities. PUI will build on its extensive experience working with communities through a “Community Protection Approach” (CPA) to identify vulnerabilities and capacities, and providing emergency responses and protective measures to vulnerable families affected by settler violence, demolitions and/or military operations. Therefore, this project complements the work done by PUI under the West Bank Protection Consortium (WBPC), which include providing emergency response to victims of settler violence and protective measures at community level. The planned Action will avoid any duplication of activity by providing protective assets at household level only. PUI also used the findings of the CPA done under the Consortium to identify some of the communities targeted by this project.
PUI will coordinate its intervention with key actors working under the Shelter Cluster and within the area of intervention.

1 - Needs

In 2018, PUI contributed to Substandard Shelter Assessment led by the Shelter Cluster in area C by surveying 55 communities in the North of the WB. Out of all the 20,846 residential shelters surveyed, 16% of them were considered substandard and in need of rehabilitation. Particular vulnerable group were herding and Bedouin communities (representing 60% of substandard shelters). As a partner of the WBPC, PUI contributed to the update of the Protection Vulnerability Index and protection response plans for 31 communities in the North of the WB, under the CPA, including a shelter Vulnerability sector during March 2019. The methodology included FGDs and meetings with stakeholders in order to identify the major needs in each community. Shelter needs were identified in some of the targeted communities of this project (Tell Al Khashaba, Khirbet Tana, Khirbet Al Marajem, Yanun and Ein Shbli) with a high vulnerability on their quantitative data based on the protection vulnerability indicators. During the HRP process for 2019, PUI identified 22 communities most at risk of settler violence in the Governorates of Nablus, Salfit, Jenin, Tulkarem, and Tubas based on OCHA Settler Violence Severity Interactive Map. The 15 targeted communities by this project are among the 22 communities identified as most at risk. In September 2019, PUI conducted a rapid needs assessment with 12 communities out of 22 identified to update their profiles and identify unmet shelter needs. According to this assessment, the needs identified earlier are still unmet. Following the approval of this HRP project, PUI will conduct another assessment for the remaining 10 communities to assess their unmet needs. Within these 12 communities, 154 households (for a total population number of 926) resided in 161 residential shelters. Out of these 161 shelters, 120 were considered substandard. PUI received a fund to upgrade 60 shelters through the HPF 2nd allocation, however, there is still a need to rehabilitate the remaining 60 shelters in addition to other protective materials needs that we will identify following the planned assessment later. The type of shelters in need of rehabilitation were as follow: • Block with zinc roof: 30 shelters; Concrete with zinc ceiling: 6 shelters; Stone walls with pre-insulated roof panels: 7 shelters; Tent: 63 shelters; Zinc: 14 shelters.The findings of the variability to shelter minimum standards are summarized below for the 12 communities: 1. 91% have frame materials that are not able to support the weight of the roof and any additional live or dead load expected during different seasons like wind and snow.2. 100% have roof which is not suitable to control heat (thermal insulation). 3. 100% have roof and external walls which are not sealed and there are leaks. 4. 100% have external walls which are not insulated for thermal comfort. 5. 91% don’t have a minimum 5 m2 of covered space per person.6. 58% don’t have smooth, level, anti-slip floor in all spaces (suitable for children, elderly and disabled movement) with minimum one layer protection from natural soil. 7. 66% don’t have solid and lockable external door into the shelter. 8. 75% have window units which are not sealed.9. 75% don’t have functioning mobile or fixed internal or external latrine per shelter. 10. 83% don’t have kitchen with adequate space which may be located inside the house unit or outside the unit based on household preferences, including counter and connected to water sources and drainage system. The assessment was conducted at community level, the next step will be to carry a thorough assessment at household level to identify the needs of women, men, girls and boys with and without disabilities at the beginning of the project, as part of the selection of the most vulnerable households in these 12 communities who suffer from settler violence, demolition, and IF military operations and are unable to rehabilitate and upgrade their shelters.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Community outreach, beneficiary selection and technical assessment PUI and MAAN will reach out to the 15 targeted communities through different channels, such as posters in public infrastructure, mosques and village councils, as well as through CBOs/DPOs with information regarding project’s objectives and criteria for eligibility to ensure transparency and an inclusive approach. Local committees will be established with members representing the diversity of the communities in terms of gender, age and persons with and without disabilities, and will be consulted to identify needs, secure accountability towards the community and engage in the risk analyses to prevent doing harm.The assessment of specific needs and selection of most vulnerable households will be based on a technical assessment at household level, including socio-economic criteria. PUI/MAAN will include households to identify the appropriate responses, special needs for PwDs, and needs specific to gender and age. PUI/MAAN will scrutinize and analyse risks for every part of the project cycle to ensure that interventions will not negatively harm the communities or involved staff with a special attention to the legal context since the Action is targeting Area C. Activity 2: Shelter rehabilitation PUI and MAAN will upgrade 60 substandard shelters located in the 15 targeted communities. The scope of the upgrading will be tailored to the needs of each family, with a maximum value of 5000 USD per household, and will use a mixed approach of cash and material supply, when appropriate. Women, men, girls and boys will be consulted during all the design, implementation and evaluation of this activity. Special care will be granted to specific needs in relation to the different groups and in their use of the shelters both for privacy, socializing and work. This project will place particular attention on families with people with disabilities and female-headed households as these are the beneficiary families with members spending more time at home in the area of intervention. Rehabilitation work will include providing latrines, solar lights, insulation, ventilation, flooring, windows, and doors. PUI/MAAN will ensure a good level of public health/personal hygiene by rehabilitating the kitchen and/or bathroom, and improve privacy - separation between females and males, separation between different families in the same housing unit, lockable door for toilet/shower, at least one room with lockable door, upgrades based on specific needs etc. The project complies with the Shelter Cluster minimum agreed standards, gender, and adequate access to housing by people with disabilities. PUI and MAAN will ensure that the beneficiaries are aware of the intervention and in case there is any implication that might affect them. In the case of households with pending legal procedures, lawyers will be consulted before any intervention. Activity 3: Provision of protective materials and tools PUI and MAAN will provide protective assets to 60 households residing in the 19 targeted communities (not the same beneficiaries of activity 2). These communities are often subjected to recurrent alleged IHL/IHRL violations (e.g. harassment/incursions by settlers or IF, stoning, cutting of waterlines) due to their geographic proximity to settlements, outposts and military bases. PUI will design protective responses together with the local committees and the targeted households to reduce the magnitude or/and likelihood of incidents, through the provision of protective assets (such as fences, tents for water tanks, window shutters, fencing, first aid kits, and fire extinguishers). Emphasis will be given to improve the feeling of safety and dignity of the most vulnerable of the households.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

Beneficiaries are involved in all aspects of the Action through a shared decision-making process. The community-based protection approach involves a continuous process of self-assessment and analysis of their vulnerabilities and needs, used as an entry point for the elaboration of local protection strategies. As soon as the action starts, PUI/MAAN will meet with community representatives and all selected beneficiaries to inform them about the accepted project and explain how beneficiaries are expected to participate and contribute for the success of the action. This informative meeting will allow participants to inform PUI/MAAN about their needs, aspiration and involve them in a participatory approach. All participants to this meeting will be informed about PUI Feedback & Response Mechanism (FRM). During those meetings PUI / MAAN staff members will ensure to hand out information cards on the mechanism and explain the FRM. They will pay attention to highlight that feedback, whether positive or negative, will all be taken into consideration and addressed by PUI representatives independent from project team and be dealt with respect for the principles of confidentiality and privacy, without affecting the possibility to receive assistance in the future.PUI will work with the established committees to ensure full transparency and make sure to hand out cards and posters to staff and partners in sufficient quantity to ensure a continuous information on the mechanism in the field. Meanwhile, feedbacks may be provided verbally in the field, through text message, email or phone (using the dedicated address and phone number provided on information cards), in addition to all other available media in the organization. Feedbacks may originate from beneficiaries, non-beneficiaries impacted by PUI / partners interventions as well. PUI’s FRM related document and SoP might be provided upon request.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

PUI/MAAN is fully committed to principles of do no harm, and promoting safety and dignity. For each phase of the proposed intervention, PUI will conduct risks analysis aiming at identifying and mitigating possible risks associated with the action with a special attention to the legal analysis of communities in Area C. This will be done through assessments, continuous evaluation and consultations with community representatives, involved participants and the established committees as well as through the FRM. The targeted communities and beneficiaries will be actively involved in all phases of the Action and all feedback will be taken into account in best possible manners. The community-based protection approach involving CBOs and DPOs and participants of the Action is designed to ensure privacy, safety, dignity and accessibility for men and women, with or without disability and to contribute to the enhancement of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights. Lastly all involved staff is trained in PSEA policies and the action continuously engage in GBV mainstreaming by as an example having staff trained in identifying and referring GBV survivors as non-specialized actors.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Through the use of interviews, focus groups and surveys, the Action aims to involve vulnerable groups and people with specific needs in all aspects of the project cycle. The Action’s participatory assessment is specifically designed to involve vulnerable groups traditionally excluded from decision making. For each phase of the proposed intervention, PUI/MAAN will analyse possible barriers and enablers in cooperation with the established committees, CBOs, PDOs and beneficiaries with a specific focus on age, gender and diversity. Furthermore the Action will pay particular attention to identifying beneficiaries most at need by attributing special attention to inclusiveness of vulnerable groups and people with specific needs. The planned action will target women as well as men with and without disabilities by adapting the implementation to the specific needs of households. Specific vulnerabilities such as e.g female headed households’ participation will be tackled, through engaging actively with the involved households and committees in order to tackle challenges and possible solutions.
