Project Summary

In the context of the protracted Rohingya crisis in Cox’s Bazar, resulting in long-term displacement in camps, refugees are often facing a lack of regular income and livelihoods and thus creates an important dependency towards humanitarian aid. The creation of the camps also generated a loss of cultivable lands and livelihoods for the host communities, also experienced through decreased economic opportunities. Through this project, Action Contre la Faim aims at supporting the 600 (30% female) Rohingya refugees as well as 600 (30% female) host communities in Rajapalong and Ratnapalong in Ukhiya upazila and 300 female host communities at Subrang in Teknaf towards more resilient and sustainable livelihoods as well as food and nutritional security. It will provide technical skills and vocational training to build the capacity of Rohingya female-headed households and youth in the host communities according to their needs to create long-term and sustainable livelihoods. In fact, while lack of livelihoods is affecting the whole population, women and the youth are lacking even more opportunities to access income-generating activities. ACF will also be facilitating training and input support on healthy homestead vegetable gardening and poultry production to the refugee community, as well as conditional cash grant on homestead vegetable gardening and poultry production to host communities. ACF will adopt Climate smart agricultural technology to cope with multi hazards like tidal surges, floods, salinity, drought and others. In addition, ACF will facilitate Cash for Work (CFW) activities to both the refugee and host communities to ensure wage-earning employment to meet the immediate basic needs and temporary employment during the lean period. Workshops on market linkage and income generating activity opportunities will also be facilitated for the host communities. Finally, awareness will be raised among the project participants for social cohesion between the host and refugees' communities as tensions between these communities appear as an obstacle to stability and maximization of opportunities. These activities will allow the participants to acquire marketable skills that will create opportunities for them to engage in self-sustaining and life-enhancing activities, notably through income generation. The new skills and income-earning opportunities created by the proposed project will enable the beneficiaries to build-up on the support they receive from aid agencies and government, and at the same time improve their well-being and regain of self-esteem. Our approach aims to equip people with skills and opportunities to enable them to become self-sufficient and not rely on external assistance. This approach will fully benefit both host and refugees' communities. During the project period ACF will coordinate with CiC, Site management, FS Sector, Government actors and others on regular basis. ACF will participate in monthly/bi weekly coordination meeting with CiC, Sector and other NGOs at Upazila Level. For different workshop and training ACF will communicate in prior basis with relevant NGOs, actors and sectors. ACF will also invite relevant stakeholders for visit the field activities and interventions for getting the feedback, challenge and way forward for further implementation of the project activities and will must submit/share the sectoral reports like 5Ws, CFW and Volunteers Incentive Information and other progress reports timely. To implement the project activities ACF will coordinate with LSDS, ADWG and EETWG when required.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Refugees in Cox’s Bazar generally have no regular income opportunities and lack the skills that would allow them to adapt quickly to change in their situation. The fifth round of the Rohingya Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA 5) published in March 2022, revealed that 95% of all Rohingya households are moderately to highly vulnerable and remain entirely dependent on humanitarian assistance. Furthermore, the results revealed that half of the Rohingya refugee labour force was not engaged in any type of income-generating activity. Therefore, the promotion of self-reliance and livelihoods opportunities through skill development in improved agriculture and creating income opportunities – including through agricultural ventures using available land – are among UNHCR’s key livelihoods objectives for refugees in Bangladesh. Scaling up vocational and skills training, and socio-economic empowerment and self-reliance activities with special attention to uneducated and unskilled persons, youth, and women in the camps and host communities, is critical to enhancing access to economic opportunities. Furthermore, host communities near the camps and settlements have been affected due to the influx of Rohingyas, as land once used for farming and other activities is now occupied by refugees. The influx has placed a strain on infrastructure, services, and natural resources in these communities. ACF will work to mitigate the negative impacts and help host communities benefit from the economic opportunities provided by the new arrivals.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Action contre la Faim (ACF) is an INGO fighting against malnutrition. ACF has been supporting communities in Bangladesh since 2007 and intervening in Cox’s Bazar District since 2009, where it is supporting both host communities and the Rohingya refugee communities through its high-quality interventions. ACF thus has many years of contextual experience and acceptance built through strong engagement of host and Rohingya communities, other organisations, authorities - including at camp level, and other relevant stakeholders. ACF has a strong technical expertise in Food Security and Livelihoods, including skills development, ensuring food and nutrition security as well as resilient and climate adaptive livelihoods. Throughout its long experience in the country, particularly in Cox’s Bazar, ACF has promoted numerous innovative ideas in the field of Food Security and Livelihoods in order to strengthen the resilience of both host and refugee communities through actions similar as the one proposed. ACF has been hosting the NGO Platform from June 2020 to March 2022 and lead the WaSH Cluster up to mid-2021. ACF is also the Assessment and Information Management Technical Working Group Lead, Nutrition and WaSH Sector SAG member and member of different clusters, sectors and working groups, including the Food Security Sector, basing its humanitarian support on its guidelines. ACF has long-term partnerships with UN agencies, including, Unicef, IOM, WHO, UNOPS and WFP. ACF also ran several projects funded by UNHCR, and is thus familiar with its policies, practices and programmes. ACF has also established strong partnerships with INGOs like Cordaid, United Purpose, Plan International and local NGOs such as Sushilan, Friendship, SHED, and SARPV, notably through the lead of several consortium. ACF has its country office located in Dhaka, coordinating and providing support to the Cox’s Bazar office, thus allowing efficient and timely response in the district.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

GBV risk mitigation also integrated throughout. ACF will ensure to mainstreaming GBV issues across all activities, following a survivor-centred approach to ensure vulnerabilities of women and girls are integrated into all analyses and approaches. ACF works for protection mainstreaming, for people with difficulties/disabilities, children and on gender-related issues. ACF will provide integrated, multi-sectoral trainings to ensure that non-protection actors are equipped with basic knowledge and skills to recognize vulnerable individuals and refer them to protection services. Child protection will be included in vulnerable household selection by identifying SAM/MAM/ health cases at high risk of child abuse, to be referred and prioritized for comprehensive child protection assessment and case management. There will be Protection Hotline for reporting, complaints and feedback. A conflict-sensitive lens is also applied throughout and security assessments are conducted to ensure protection.

3.2 - Gender

ACF has own gender protection policy which incorporates key gender equality measures. Based on Gender analysis tailored activities for women, men, girls and boys including persons with disability are designed considering the different needs, roles, vulnerabilities, capacities, access and control over resources. Thus, the project will prioritize service to special group’s especially female-headed households, Pregnant and Lactating Women, under 5 children (vulnerable to malnutrition), Person with Disability and Elderly during implementation of the proposed projects interventions. Reasonable support based on the situation for different people provided as first priority. Gender equity formula will be implemented in every step of the proposed project activities. The program will initiate to aware community people on GBV, violence against women and child and related PSEA issues.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

ACF maintain Sex, Age and diversity Disaggregated (SADDD) data for targeted beneficiary in order to address the specific needs of the each beneficiary group. ACF abide by the local laws and by applicable international instruments, including the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child and International Labor Organization conventions. The activities will be enabled for age group 18+ under the project. ACF discourages child labor and any violence against children. Inclusion of child in any CFW activities, distribution program is strictly prohibited. Additionally, ACF will ensure breast feeding corners for lactating mothers in every distribution point and a nursing corner will be placed in project office for female participants with children. HHs with pregnant and lactating women will be prioritized during selection of persons of concern. The project will essentially include awareness and sensitization session on child labor, child trafficking, violence against child, child marriage.

3.4 - Diversity

ACF will provide equal opportunities to everyone and meet the range of different needs that a diverse community needs. The participants and location selection, implementation modalities and in every cycle of project will made consideration for people of different gender identities, sexual orientation, race, ethnicities, class/caste, people with disabilities and other marginalized groups. In this project ACF will cover at least 30% women participation at Camp and Host Community under Ukhiya Upazila and 100% Female participation at Subrang under Teknaf Upazila. ACF will also cover 2% PWD participation in all project implementing areas.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project beneficiaries will be actively engaged in the assessments conducted by ACF like post distribution monitoring (PDM), food security surveillance survey (FSS). The beneficiary selection will be done by community consultations and the affected people will decide to select beneficiaries as per criteria of project interventions. HHs with women, PLW, person with disability, children under 5 and elderly are prioritized in the criteria of selection of beneficiaries. Communities also will provide their feedback on ACF led project activities formally through the complaint and feedback Mechanism (CFMC) and informally through meetings and individual interactions based on which actions will be taken to rectify the project activities if needed.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

project will facilitate to select CFW schemes for host community from the CRA (Community Risk Assessment) and RRAP (Risk Reduction Action Plan) of concern Union Parishad. Project will also assist UP for reviewing CRA & RRAP. Project will select CFW schemes from Camps by the need assessment of CiC & site management dept. This project will introduce climate smart agriculture for homestead gardening such as vertical garden, sac/bag/hanging gardening, use of organic manure etc. To address the environmental issues and climate change ACF will closely coordinate with othe NGOs/stakeholders and will supoort through CFW activities for tree pantation in road side, hilli slope and also in cleaning the cannel. Moreover, All the participants under the training activities will receive disaster risk reduction and disaster preparedness training. Also, for ensuring environment friendly approaches, ACF encourage and monitor beneficiaries to not to use any plastic materials for any purposes.
