Project Summary

HNAP is a joint-UN assessment initiative, which tracks displacement and return movements, conducts sector and multi-sectoral assessments, and monitors humanitarian needs inside the Syrian Arab Republic. The project will contribute to the coordination sector by providing effective coordination support at hub and WoS levels, facilitating a timely and evidence-based response informed by enhanced needs assessments and information management with a focus on strengthening the response capacity of national humanitarian actors.

4 - Needs

To date, the humanitarian needs and locations of the affected population inside Syria have been clearly identified and prioritized geographically, demographically and by access status. Resident population, IDP, returnee and humanitarian needs datasets are available and regularly updated at community-level across Syria (8,000 communities). Over the past year, a significant part of the data accessible to partners working in Syria to guide operational planning and inform needs analysis has been provided by HNAP. HNAP remains critical in providing independent and impartial assessments which inform a needs-based approach to the delivery of humanitarian assistance in Syria, with regards to the following: • IDP and population data (critical for the humanitarian community to be able to monitor population movements on a monthly basis and have updated population figures monthly as well as twice a year to guide programming), • Conducting multi-sectorial needs assessments, providing sectorial-relevant data that is disaggregated by sex, age, and disability as well as by other indicators of vulnerability. • Supporting the Humanitarian Program Cycle, with data on specific thematic areas, such as the intentions of IDPs in Syria or socio-economic conditions of returnees within a given year. • Support to sector-specific needs assessments (e.g. WASH) • Ad-hoc assessments in areas affected by critical developments – for example on-set displacement in north-west Syria or north-east Syria including displacement due to man-made conflict and natural hazards. HNAP has been the most widely-used sources of data to inform the humanitarian community’s strategic planning and response. This project will ensure the continuation of the collection of time-sensitive, impartial, geographically-specific and independent data that will be shared with UN clusters and humanitarian partners, at all levels of the response.

5 - Implementation of activities

HNAP conducts regular assessments and surveys using primary data collection methods, including direct interviews with local key informants (KIs), household surveys, and direct observations at community level throughout the Syrian Arab Republic. It is now the sole source of information for around 80% of communities inside Syria (compared to approximately 70% last year). The following activities will be conducted as part of this project: • Daily, monthly, quarterly and needs-based data collection across all communities in Syria (100% coverage);• Data entry and cleaning;• Analysis of collected data;• Production of reports based on collected and analysed data; • Circulation of products with humanitarian partners, including OCHA, UN agencies, NGOs and donors via email and through HNAP’s newly created password-protected website;• Providing IDP and Population taskforce with regular updates on different population movements, as well as baseline figures disaggregated by population group. HNAP shares raw data with the taskforces on a monthly basis (pertaining to movement), on a quarterly basis (pertaining to baseline figures), and on an annual basis HNAP provides sex/age disaggregated data to the population task-force which is used for the HPC cycle.• Coordination with humanitarian partners on a regular basis;• Conducting needs assessments, as well as sector and multi-sectorial needs assessments in order to feed the HNO and HRP• Training of field staff and humanitarian partners on needs assessments, household surveys and thematic or location-specific assessments; • Focus on returnee data, with emphasis on developing regression model to better predict return movements. • Where applicable, collection of data is disaggregated by sex and age, and specific PSEA questions are incorporated into surveys.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks: 1. Physical safety of respondents and of humanitarian staff in asking sensitive questions and working in dangerous locations 2. There is also risk to the staff from being exposed to primary and secondary trauma without any resources or training for self-care.3. Risk of receiving distorted information due to communities' expectations of a specific type or level of response, as well as the communities’ perceptions of humanitarian staff expectations of the information required to be received.4. Compromised quality of trainings due to the lack of opportunities for face to face trainings and measuring the impact of trainings which are delivered remotely; 5. Lack of awareness of the importance of protection and GBV mainstreaming among the staff; 6. Protection concerns of individuals due to collection of personal data during interview/assessments. Mitigation Measures: 1. Do No Harm considerations will be integrated into survey/assessment questions e.g. consultations communities on effective delivery of messages. IPs to conduct daily security briefings informing enumerators of high-risk areas and areas to avoid.2. Training of staff in survivor-centered approach and basic psycho-social first aid (PFA), and duty of care policies for local partners developed and budgeted for.3. Enumerators to continue to be transparent and provide consistent information to communities on the scope of the assessment to make expectations and intent clear; 4. Delivery of trainings and capacity building initiatives will be strengthened by training key resource people to act as trainers/facilitators inside of Syria. 5. Enhance the sensitization on Protection and GBV mainstreaming through mandatory trainings. Ensure adequate assessment approaches and interviewing techniques are applied 6. Providing personal information is always optional; respondents always have the right to not answer any questions; HNAP data is always anonymized and no personal information shared

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: 1. The security situation in Syria is the most prominent risk for this project. Changes in the situation and actors controlling some areas might affect the access to some locations and could also impact the personal security of the field staff. Assumptions: 1. Security conditions in Syria will continue to be conducive for enumerators to carry out assessments without facing personal security risks. 2. The security conditions will also continue to be conducive for communities to engage in answering assessment questions and feel safe to do so. 3. Key informants will continue to provide information which is representative of the needs of all community members including men, women, boys, girls, persons living with disabilities and elderly persons. 4. Effective engagement with communities and completion of assessments will contribute to a more appropriate and effective humanitarian programming for the WoS response. Mitigation measures: 1. The security situation will continue to be actively monitored by team leaders in coordination with the field coordinator of implementing partner with close coordination with program management in Gaziantep.2. Data is always encrypted to minimize risk faced by enumerators. Kobo and other tools are used to avoid dependence on paper forms and there is no storage of data in any form at the field level.3. All newly hired enumerators are given self-care training to build their capacity in dealing with issues arising from being exposed to primary and secondary trauma. This training is included as part of the induction and provided by the field coordinator, team leader and support staff in Gaziantep. For current staff, refresher courses are also provided.
