1 - Needs

Along the contact line in Donetsk and Luhansk Oblasts (GCA), conflict affected populations, both those who never left and returnees, have experienced/are at risk of experiencing violence due to armed hostilities. OCHA estimates 220’000 persons living along the line of contact in GCA. Hostilities and the establishment of the contact line have separated regions, villages and families, caused large scale damage to housing and infrastructure, has seriously impacted the delivery of services and the capacity of the population to meet their basic needs. Given price increases, the high unemployment rate and lack of income opportunities, conflict affected households in small and isolated communities have no regular access to general household items. In parallel, given the winter climate of eastern Ukraine combined with rising gas and electricity prices and restricted access to solid fuel it is expected that the most vulnerable IDPs and conflict affected households will still endure difficulties in heating their accommodation during the winter period. Moreover the state support program on providing subsidies to the vulnerable population is restricting its requirements resulting in the situation when significant number of households in need will stay with harsh winter conditions one-on-one. Above all, conflict affected populations still experience periodic military confrontation despite the ceasefire, including shelling, as well as an alarming presence of mines and unexploded ordnances in the immediate vicinity of the villages. NRC has also observed in small communities in proximity to the contact line a high and generalized degree of vulnerability with an average household income of around 80 USD (2,000 UAH) that is more than twice lower than the national minimum per household member (4.173 UAH) and is one the same level as the current subsistence level calculated by Ministry of Social Policy of Ukraine (1,936 UAH), which all together reflect the general economic insecurity of IDP households. Similarly, a significant portion of the population depends on pensions as a first source of income, with up to 70% elderly population in the most rural areas along the contact line. The shelter cluster estimates that the minimum recommended amount of fuel for the 4 winter months is around a minimum of 280 USD/hh and recommended is 560 USD/HH. With the average pension of around 3,000 UAH (around 120 USD which still is not the case for the significant number of elderly population), the monthly cost of minimum fuel amount during winter represents more than 50% of the average monthly pension for single person. The NFI programme provides assistance to conflict affected, vulnerable groups of all ages and gender. In our area of operation, the ratio is approximately 60% Female and 60% Elderly. This is not by design, but rather the census of the population who are unable to move away from the line of contact.

2 - Activities & outcomes

NRC will provide winterization cash and/or directly distributed NFI assistance to 600 vulnerable families in the conflict affected area of Luhansk and Donetsk oblasts (GCA). Families will receive multipurpose cash, given at the beginning of the winter months. This will allow families the choice of how to increase their winter durability, would assist families from having to make a choice between food security and heating, and would be easy to measure as to what the funds were spent on as the season changes. While multi-purpose cash, or cash based programming may be the preferred modality in the majority of instances, some settlements still do not have solid or unrestricted access to markets to purchase the necessary materials to ensure their heating and winter durability needs are met. In this case NRC would be in position to provide specific goods, through in kind distribution, after consultation with beneficiaries and mutual agreement on the most suitable modality.
