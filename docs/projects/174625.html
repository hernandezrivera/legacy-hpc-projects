Project Summary

The purpose of the project is to improve food security, economic and social well-being of vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host families in the target area.
NRC Cameroon’s Livelihood and Food Security (LFS) programme will target displacement-affected people based on vulnerability criteria and not their displacement status only (out-of-camp refugees, IDPs, returnees (former IDPs, and host communities). NRC will apply social and economic vulnerability criteria to identify vulnerable households most at risk to food insecurity. The social vulnerability criteria applied will include female-headed households, child-headed households; households, pregnant and breastfeeding women; elderly people (60 and above); households with a person with physical or mental disability or debilitating chronic disease. For the economic vulnerability criteria, NRC will use as a reference: poor or limited food consumption score; no food stock, number of meals per day, no productive assets, and loss of income. For agricultural restoration activities, NRC will also include the following criteria: having at least one able-bodied person in the household who can farm land. NRC’s LFS programmes will also target as far as possible vulnerable youth and women for vocational training and economic empowerment.
To address the intermediate needs of affected populations NRC will use cash, vouchers or in-kind assistance based on the results of market analysis and cash feasibility studies. To maximise the chances of success in this activity, NRC will as far as possible couple livelihoods interventions with food assistance to meet basic food needs. Moreover, NRC will implement complementary activities such as training and awareness raising sessions to support target households. Programme activities will gradually scale down and phase out so that by the end of the projects, communities have the skills and resources needed to be more resilient to shocks and crises in the medium and long term. NRC will work in coordination with local partners such as local technical and administrative structures and will collaborate closely with them so they can provide technical support during programme implementation and continued follow-up after the programme ends.
The LFS programme will include cash assistance, livelihoods promotion and enhancement applying a market-based approach (MBA) that refers to local traders so to improve access to income and revive/strengthen the local economy. The intervention will be geared towards supporting the most vulnerable displacement-affected households and at the same time building their capacity in food production, food transformation and marketing.

1 - Besoins / Needs

People forced to flee because of the crisis have often lost their productive assets (seeds stocks, agricultural tools, livestock) thus hindering their capacity to restart their main livelihood activity. Moreover, they have also lost access or have limited access to farmlands due to enduring insecurity. Some productive assets may have been sold as part of coping mechanisms, thus further decreasing their capacity to meet their basic needs. According to an assessment conducted by NRC, less than 30% of respondents have sufficient income to cover their basic needs. The data also shows also more almost three out of four households spend more than 80% of their income on food expenses. Conflict negatively affects livelihoods in several localities of the departments of Logone-et-Chari, Mayo-Sava and Mayo-Tsanaga . Agricultural production has been affected by the following shocks: insecurity, flooding late rains and drought, plant diseases and higher prices for agricultural inputs due to COVID-19. In the region, livestock activities were also affected by the crisis with a significant number of livestock being stolen or killed. Animal production also suffers from drought and unusual diseases. Some epizootics have resurfaced because of the interruption of animal disease prevention activities, due to the insecurity created by Boko Haram. Furthermore, the combined insecurity situation and the structural poverty jeopardise the take-off of local economic initiatives. In Logone-et-Chari and Mayo Tsanaga divisions, the acquisition of food in markets represents a great challenge for those who lost their livelihood during displacement. Before the crisis, the Cameroon-Nigeria border used to be a major commercial trading axis. Due to insecurity and COVID-19 prevention measures most of the exchange links between communities on both sides of the border have been disrupted. This has led to limited access to manufactured products (traditionally coming from Nigeria) and limited possibility to sell local livestock on the Nigerian market. All of this has resulted in less commercial activity in general, and increased poverty among traders. In addition, Boko Haram attacks have weakened the local trade fabric, impoverishing thousands of traders who depended on trade with Nigeria and worsening the already precarious economic situation of the inhabitants of this ecologically fragile region. The unemployment rate of IDPs and youth is very high in the region, unoccupied all day, they are at risk of recruitment into armed or criminal groups. Women are also affected by the loss of their means to carry out their petty trading activities (selling staple commodities, fabric, etc.). The practice of these activities dropped sharply after displacement, as a result of loss of access to land, cattle theft and the loss, looting or sale of productive assets. The economic activity most practiced by the displaced populations is currently Unskilled work in exchange for daily wages.The already precarious situation of women and girls has deteriorated further during the crisis, in an environment where they do not have access to land because of insufficient availability of arable land combine with traditional system of farming whereby management of natural resources favours men over women. NRC’s September 2020 assessment revealed the reality that COVID-19 affects the daily life of the population and their family members: 67% of the sample population reported that the restriction of movement and confinement measures had an impact on their activities and livelihoods, given that they rely mostly on trade, agriculture and animal breeding. In addition, they reported that border closure has led to an increase in the cost of living and, yet, the monthly income of families has remained the same or has dropped. Thus, this pandemic will have short, medium, and long-term consequences on household's food security and economic opportunities.
