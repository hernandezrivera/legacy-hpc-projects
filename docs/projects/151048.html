Project Summary

The proposed project is intended to improve access to immediate food needs of the target population and expand economic opportunities among the vulnerable urban-rural IDPs, and host communities affected by prolonged droughts, recurrent floods, evictions as well as other environmental disasters in South Central regions. The proposed intervention is set to benefit as much as 112,734 beneficiaries comprising of both host communities and IDP settlements. Improving access to food availability through support to agricultural production in Luuq, Bardhere, Cabudwak, Kismayo and Afgoye Districts and Support to traditional and non-traditional livelihoods, Capacity building of various community groups, associations for livelihoods.
This plan also involves improving the local community’s livelihoods through diversification and investments in livestock support through animal vaccination and treatment, fodder production as well as appropriate actions aimed at ensuring improvement in productive community infrastructures/assets such as irrigation canals, water catchments and feeder roads through Cash for Work approach. The project will further promote conservational agriculture through the application of Climate Smart Agriculture technology that aims at increasing agricultural productivity and supporting equitable increases in farm incomes, food security and development. This will further ensure adaptation to and building resilience of agro-pastoral households in the target project locations.
The project beneficiaries will be given inputs (Seeds, Fertilizers and other farm equipment) to improve their farm yield and improve their food security status. Farmers will also be capacity built together with the respective line Ministries such as Agriculture, Livestock of the Federal Government of Somalia as well as of the Regional Governments. ADA will also strive to ensure access to early warning systems to ensure faster dissemination of information in times of shock to enhance early preparedness and mitigation measures.
The project is in conformity with the FSC Objectives, Cash Working Group transfer modalities as well as the Somalia New Deal Compact.

Needs

Recent climatic improvements and large scale humanitarian assistance has led to the improvements in the food security situation of Somalis. Still, acute food insecurity, malnutrition and livelihood needs remain. The residual impact from the 2016/17 drought, combined with the recent floods, displacement, evictions etc., continue to undermine opportunities for food security improvements especially for the most vulnerable Somalis in South Central Somalia. Despite recent improvement, an estimated 1.5 million people in Somalia are expected to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3). In Southern Somalia, 2018 Gu season cereal production is estimated at 147 200 tonnes, including 7 200 tonnes of off-season harvests expected in October. This level of production is 17 percent higher than the long-term average (1995-2017) and 58 percent higher than the five-year average (2013-17). Poor households in the crop-dependent parts of southern Somalia (mainly riverine) currently have little to no food stocks and limited farm labor opportunities, due to localized below-average production, flooding, and the extended lean season. Beneficiaries facing acute food security Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse urgently need humanitarian assistance and livelihood support to prevent food security deterioration. Livelihood support is also required for people categorized as Stressed (IPC Phase 2). Routine field monitoring and assessment by ADA indicate that acute food insecurity remains prevalent among vulnerable populations affected by drought and riverine flooding in April-May 2018; and others experiencing large-scale, protracted displacement due to conflict and natural hazards. These groups are expected to face food consumption gaps or engage in unsustainable coping strategies to meet their food needs, hence the need to prevent further deterioration of their food security and nutrition situation.

Activities

The Proposed project activities include: Through Cash for Work Approach ADA will reach 3124 HH spread across the project locations using Mobile Money Transfer which is more efficient and reliable. This will be done through rehabilitation/construction of productive community assets in Cabudwak, Bardhere, Luuq, Afgoye and Kismayo Districts. Each beneficiary will be paid a sum of $6 per day for 10 days a month for 2 consecutive months of Cash for Work activities. Provision of agricultural inputs (seeds, fertilizers, farm equipment) to 3600 farmers in Afgoye, Bardhere and Luuq Districts Capacity building to 360 farmers on good agricultural practices. Pilot of fodder production to 700 farmers in Afgoye, Bardhere and Luuq Districts. ADA will liaise with the respective federal and regional government departments in having more than 2100 pastoralists have their animals vaccinated and treated for various diseases in Bardhere, Kismayo, and Cabudwak Districts.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

ADA will oversee the implementation of the project and ensure that displacement does not occur before the necessary mitigation measures are in place. In the event that the displacement cannot be avoided ADA will offer displaced persons and communities priorities for restoring and or rebuilding their livelihoods or standards of living. ADA will further ensure that the proposed activities provide opportunities to displaced persons and communities to derive appropriate development benefits from the project.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Lack of enough community participation and Empowerment: This will be mitigated through the following mitigation measures.Involve and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to FSL needs. Different criteria may affect the power dynamics. • Consult women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, chronically ill, older persons, pregnant and lactating women and marginalised persons to collect accurate information about their specific food and nutritional requirements and preferences for food distribution points and livelihood services. • Engage the community and committee representative to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response • Reinforce the capacity of the community - encourage collective livelihood projects over those that benefit individuals. Collective practices contribute to community protection and resilience, particularly in rural community economies which are closely linked with social networks, community structures and cultural practices. • Coordinate with civil society specializing in working with persons with impaired mobility or disabilities to help identify such individuals and use them as a resource to improve service delivery, train staff and for the referral of cases.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Safety and Security concerns: There is likelihood that project beneficiaries experience safety and insecurity during project implementation. This is likely to affect service provision among the targeted vulnerable populations. This will be solved by establishing and or strengthening community level safety and security measures.
