Project Summary

The intervention will support potatoes crop for Autumn seasons in Idleb. 400 beneficiaries will receive potatoes seeds(tuber), irrigation costs and other agricultural inputs which will be enough to 2 dunums of potatoes. SAED technical team will provide 3 training sessions to beneficiaries in each targeted villages. The training will be divided into three steps during the project implementation according to the activities implemented and knowledge needs of the beneficiaries at different stages of the crop growth season to train 400 beneficiaries as it is expected that most beneficiaries will transfer knowledge gained to members of the community. This intervention will strengthen the production of potatoes crop widely used in the diet of Syrian people to address food insecurity while protecting and gradually restoring agricultural-based livelihoods and strengthening the capacity of people to manage and recover from shocks. this intervention which will help alleviate the scarcity of basic food commodities and reduce dependency from imports and food aid. Support to household potatoes activities production will give to crisis-affected people including internally displaced persons (IDPs) and their host communities will help to protect and restoring food security, employment, economic growth, natural resources, and social cohesion while at the same time launch of efforts based on the fact that recovery of agricultural production, diversification of income-generating activities and employment creation can begin to rebuild food security and livelihoods and contribute to stabilization. The proposed action will contribute to the strengthening of the resilience of the targeted crisis-affected people and their host communities. Through the primary surveys and consultations made with experts in the agriculture inside Syria the following villages are nominated (Taliha – Abu Kansa – Efs – Tel Krateen – Taftanaz – Ram Hamdan – Zardna – Shaliekh – Ketan – Binsh) in Idleb. The intervention will target three or four villages from the above mentioned (which are well-known for potato crops) these villages will be selected after conducting needs assessment for the ten villages as it will be focused to select the villages with vulnerable households (having access to 2 to 10 Donums) and access to arable land and irrigation resources. In addition to giving priority to the women-headed households, households hosting IDPs, households with infants, disabilities, chronicle diseases households who did not leave their houses due to poverty. We will target the potatoes farmers who they meet selected criteria.

Needs

Entering the ninth year of the crisis, the scale, severity, and complexity of needs across Syria remain overwhelming. Conflict continues to be the principal driver of humanitarian needs, with the civilian population in many parts of the country exposed to significant protection risks which threaten life, dignity and wellbeing on a daily basis. Before the crisis in Syria, the famers situations were good in general, livelihoods were average and the inputs were accessible (from the government) the local markets were available especially for the strategic crops. Also, the agriculture guidance and support centers were available. In general, the agriculture situation was acceptable. After the crisis, starting from 2011 up to day these services started to diminish due to the conflict. Gradually the government services started to vanish. By the start of 2012 all the Agriculture Directorates (related to regime) has stopped working in the non-regime area, which resulted in decreasing the living situation of the farmers, increasing the prices of the agricultural inputs and losing some, increasing the irrigation fees due to the high prices of the fuel and the electricity outages which stopped most of the machinery used for agriculture. The markets became limited. Moreover, low crop production combined with import constraints, insecurity, and high transportation costs reduced the availability of food and pushed prices up. As more families adopt negative coping strategies and dependency on food aid, the risk of malnutrition increases as their ability to supplement the food aid with fresh food decreases. The most vulnerable groups are young children, pregnant and lactating women, women of child bearing age, the elderly, the chronically/acutely ill. SAED team conducted many visits to (Taliha – Abu Kansa – Efs – Tel Krateen – Taftanaz – Ram Hamdan – Zardna – Shaliekh – Ketan – Binsh) villages which located in Idleb countryside, according to HNO 2019, 122494 Food Insecure peoples and 36006 from peoples at risk in this area. SAED team conducted needs assessment through many meetings with specialists in the agricultural sector and many stakeholders in addition to local councils’ members through focus group discussions. The visits results show the need to support the agricultural sector where these villages are depending heavily on potato crop cultivation. The people of these villages rely on agricultural activities especially potato cultivation as basic sources of income.

Implementation of activities

SAED field team will Sign the agreements with the local councils in the targeted villages. The project will be advertised in the public places in the targeted villages, all potential beneficiaries in the village they will have details on the support and the conditions to apply for receiving assistance under the project. This process will be conducted in each village in the houses and/or the offices of elders and local council’s headquarters. The field staff will start registering the beneficiaries’ names. The registration process will be conducted in the houses of the village elders and local council’s headquarters which also included the Monitoring and Evaluation team in order to monitor the registration process. Beneficiary Selection Criteria will be as follows:- Previous experience in potato cultivation- Availability of irrigated land and water source- Agriculture is the main source of income for the household- Farmers that affected, directly/indirectly, by the on-going crisis- The vulnerable farmers and residents in the areas that targeted by this intervention- The farmers who lost their harvest or cannot afford the agriculture inputs cost to farm their land /crop and vegetable plots due to ongoing crisis and high prices- Households should have members able to work on agriculture- Beneficiaries farmers as breadwinners of their households- The beneficiaries’ farmers should not be government employees and should be without a steady source of income- Women-headed households and families where women play an important role in food production or household food security. During the period of beneficiaries’ registration and verification, the procurement committee (logistic, finance, and one of the agriculture specialists) will start to review the markets in the area in order to procure the potato seeds and fertilizers from the vendors to ensure the best quality of seeds. At the same time that the required agricultural needs are purchased, the designer at Gaziantep office will design and print the vouchers according to the required number of beneficiaries (400 beneficiaries) and send them to SAED office in Idleb to be filled out with the beneficiary data and their personal identification numbers. During the distribution, SAED team visits the beneficiaries in their homes, giving them the vouchers and informing each beneficiary about the distribution date, time and place. Each beneficiary will receive according to his/her voucher distributed between the dates of 15 July 2019 to 31 July 2019 all the allocated materials to cultivate 2 dunums of potato. Voucher ($50) will be distributed at the same time as the cost of the first irrigation (pre-farm irrigation), after about 20 days of the distribution of seeds, fertilizers and pesticides. For the second irrigation, $50 will be distributed to the beneficiaries during September 2019. The third irrigation will be distributed $50 also to the beneficiaries during October 2019 to complete the necessary irrigation for the potato crop. Therefore, the distribution of agricultural inputs; seeds, fertilizers, pesticides and irrigation costs will be distributed over the months (Aug – Sep – Oct).

Protection Risk Analysis

The following consideration will be taken into account with regard to protection- :Prioritization for safety, dignity, and ‘do no harm’ principles will be our main concerns in this program; for this project has been designed to prevent and mitigate any negative unintentional effects of intervention that might physically jeopardize people (like violence, offence, coercion, and environmental risks), or psychologically threat them (like disrespect, lack of privacy, and disengagement). This will be done through adopting voucher system instead of direct distribution -. Equal and unbiased access to assistance and services: Assistance and services will be provided basing on the needs with no discrimination. Enough quantities and types of agricultural inputs will be available and provided equally to beneficiaries with a consideration of the geography of the targeted locations. Priority will be given to the vulnerable segments. Type of assistance will be announced about in the advertisements at the local councils. Consequently, local councils will communicate with potential beneficiaries, and physically and financially facilitate their access to have the assistance easily and safely.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: - Sending the money to Syria using PTT centers only and using the Turkish Lira which will led to lose money due to the instable of exchange rate.- Ensuring the quality of the production- Security change regarding the location - Procurement problems/challenges (trader’s correlations, procurement inputs availability, quality of materials…) - Marketing challenges - - Price changesMitigating measures: SAED will organize and plan to use the best times for sending the money- Hiring the experienced persons and specialists - Sanction list check, market survey, proper and suitable technical specification, neutral evaluation and organized process for procurement - Expand to other neighboring markets - Buy from nearby or distant markets
