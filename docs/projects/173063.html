Project Summary

HOPE is planning to work on improving access to health services for the Vulnerable population in localities of Um Dukhun (CD), As Sunta (SD), Gharb El Galabat (W. EL Galabat) (Gadaref), and Abu Hujar (Sennar) that will contribute to 3 HRP Health sector objectives for 2021.
In this project, HOPE will (1) Provide essential primary and secondary health care services, including trauma care, EmONC, and referrals mostly disaggregated to 49% male, 51% female, 55% children, 35% adults, and 8% elderly, including conduction occupational visits to provide essential primary and secondary health care services for 90,000 individuals, support trauma consultation for 3,000 individuals, do mental health consultations for 1,500 individuals, do physical rehabilitation sessions for 300 individuals, do antenatal care sessions for 12,000 adult females, attend (skilled attendant) vaginal deliveries for 3,000 adult females conduct cases delivery for 300 individuals, conduct postnatal sessions for 3,000 adult females refer cases for specialized treatment (between levels of care across Sudan) for 3000 individuals, and do inpatient treatment for 600 individuals.
Also, HOPE will (2) support health authorities to carry out timely response to emergencies and disease outbreaks in the 4 targeted localities, (3) strengthen the capacity of health care providers and community health care workers to provide essential health services targeted 300 SMOH staff and 150 community health promoters, (4) Increase access to health services by rehabilitating and refurbishing of 4 health facilities with the provision of medical equipment, (5) Expand the reporting capacity of the early warning systems in the 4 targeted localities, and (6) strengthen capacity to investigate and detect disease outbreaks in Sennar and Gadaref states.
HOPE will focus on target Locality based on poor indicators concerning PHC, Sanitation, Hygiene, RH service provision, patient/service provider ratio.
HOPE will consider accountability to affected people through full community participation, strengthen the current community mechanism/ committee as well as do follow-up for all benefits targeted separately to assess the project, identify challenges and provide give recommendations to improve the project outcomes.
HOPE is participating in the health cluster meeting from 2017. However, in 2020, conducting 2 projects of COVID-19 response in states of Khartoum, SD, and CD funded from UNDP. Usually, HOPE submits the 4Ws but didn't make it regularly. starting from Q4 2020 HOPE will be committed to submitting the 4Ws regularly/ quarterly.

1 - Needs

According to HNO of Health Sector, the Health severity for 4 localities is 4 or 5, the People In Need (PIN) are 154,800 in Wadi Salih (CD), 116,842 in As Sunta (SD), 21,672 in Gharb El Galabat (W. EL Galabat) (Gadaref), and 25,683 in Abu Hujar (Sennar). The PIN/ target disaggregated to 49% male, 51% female, 10% elderly, 55% children, and 35 adults.HOPE will focus on 4 localities based on poor indicators with regard to PHC, RH service provision, patient/service provider ratio by current service providers. Many health facilities have been destroyed, effectively leaving these places without government health services. Inadequate provision of and access to PHC services especially for women/ girls and children across target locality remains the main constraint as these same areas have high mortality rates for infants and mothers as results from pregnancy convergent, the reasons for this deterioration of health services are lack of equitable access to life-saving services and non-accessibility during rainy seasons. The main communicable diseases and outbreaks in the target localities are Malaria, AWD, Diphtheria, Fever, and Schistosomiasis as well as all localities at risk for COVID-19 due to the bad health system, lack of communities practices in term of handwashing and wearing face-masks in a crowded area such as a market or when visit health facilities. Also, there is a high risk to expand the outbreaks of Chikungunya and Dengue Fever due to victor is living in the surrounding localities.Also, Sanitation and Hygiene interventions are very weak, deterioration of sanitation & hygiene; no latrines in most villages; open defecation is widely practiced increasing, drinking water is suspected to be contaminated meanly in the rainy season, no vector control was in place; increasing of flies and mosquitos were reported. In Darfur, the inter-communal, inter-tribal conflicts, and violence that is happened in the last years which are affected the safety and security of people exacerbating the risk of sexual violence especially when displaced. Women and girls continue to face the risk of gender-based violence including sexual violence when they leave the villages. Fear of reprisals from attackers often prevents rape survivors from seeking medical help. The conflict in Darfur has also increased the number of women who are the sole providers for their families. West EL Galabat of Gadaref is suffering from poor health services and a deterioration in security due to the frequent attacks of the Ethiopian Shifta armed gangs to Al Fashaga and the border of the locality especially in the agricultural season and in October 2020 the arrival of Ethiopian refugees fleeing the fighting in Ethiopia began, as well as hosted more than 15,000 refugees and expected increasing the new refugee's arrival, so there is an urgent need to improve the health services of the refugees and host communities. Abu Hujar of Sennar is also suffering from poor health services, in last year registered the highest cases of Cholera and AWD due to a deterioration of health services, lack of sanitation, and hygiene practices by community people.Sudan also faces issues with more chronic levels of malnutrition with one in every three children under the age of five years affected. These children will suffer from more long term developmental, Health and WASH impact resulting in both a shorter stature than non-affected peers as well as a significant impact on the development of the brain resulting in long term impact on IQ, learning potential, educational outcomes as well as poorer health and WASH in the immediate and longer-term future. As a result, these children will have lower lifetime earnings, and lower productivity as adults, influencing the economic growth and potential of the entire country.Due to the above situation, HOPE looks for improving access to health services.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

During design, HOPE assessed the safety and security for target areas as well as types of GBV that facing women. During implementation, HOPE will support the capacities of committee members and assist to know their rights to health and nutrition. All essential drugs at the pharmacy will be handled with the utmost care and storage - warehouses will be clean to avoid contamination. This project will include psycho-social stimulation activities for infants and young children will also be provided with psychosocial support to help mothers deal with their own distress and improve care for their children. Child protection messages on prevention and response as well as referral mechanisms will be included in activities related to health and nutrition outreach and awareness-raising. All health staff will be trained on referral procedures and mechanisms linking patients directly to experts from other agencies and the government is already supporting the health sector at a nearby facility and other secondary facilities. Adequate shelter and waiting areas of all the health centers will be provided with all basic furniture and clean water to be accessed by all seeking services. Children and women will be given priorities. Privacy during medical history taking and examination, keeping medical data without unnecessary disclosure. Awareness-raising during community mobilization on health and nutrition, education a key component that HOPE has put in place for better understanding among the beneficiaries for self-protection on various programmatic matters. HOPE also will develop a system to collect feedback from beneficiaries to measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints.
