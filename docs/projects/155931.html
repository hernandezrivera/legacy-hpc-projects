Project Summary

The proposed action aims at providing assistance to conflict affected rural underserved vulnerable communities in Syria, in Eastern Ghouta, rural Hama and Dara’a. Through its life-saving component, the action responds to WASH needs through the provisory provision of safe water pending the completion of the rehabilitation of damaged water facilities, within an approach combining emergency response with resilience building. WASH-in-Schools activities are also planned, responding to both WASH needs and WASH-related Protection issues, in particular with regards to gender. The proposed action will complement TGH current activities in rural areas of Hama and Damascus, allowing filling identified gaps and building and scaling up from TGH achievements to date, benefiting to a broaden humanitarian response in Syria. In addition, by targeting the Hama governorate, the action will contribute to the larger contingency plan anticipating potential large return movements from the neighbouring governorate of Idleb. The targeted locations have been selected with regards to TGH current operational capacity, which guarantees an immediate start of the action complying with the demand for rapid answers to the identified needs. The targeted locations however had been initially selected due to their high vulnerability with regards to their recent history in relation to the conflict. Most targeted locations have a 6 score on HNO’s vulnerability scale, and those with a lesser score (3) are in the vicinity of areas prone to conflict and potentially source of new displacement thus humanitarian needs. TGH aims through the hereby proposed action to implement a comprehensive project through WASH activities, meeting basic needs in the sector, but also protection issues faced by the targeted population. Outcomes have been designed with the concern of integrating protection components to the planned activities.
To respond to the most acute WASH needs identified during the assessment, TGH considers relevant to continue water trucking (WT) activities in the localities where it already operates and where no alternatives exist yet (EG). In accordance with TGH principles, WT must be envisaged as a last resort. The action has been designed in line with this principle and defines a clear exit strategy limiting WT to an emergency response phase. The provision of safe water via trucks will be implemented in support to longer term alternatives such as boreholes and water network rehabilitations – the latter falling within local stakeholders’ responsibility. Following the different coordination mechanisms developed during the assessment period with other humanitarian actors involved in the WASH sector in order to avoid overlaps, TGH identified among its current localities of intervention 3 localities where it will ensure the rehabilitation of safe water sources, and 7 other where other identified agencies will carry out similar interventions. WT is also planned for those locations where no humanitarian agency has plan for restoring the access to water, for a limited period of a maximum 5 months during which TGH, as an active member of the WASH cluster, will inform WASH partners and line authorities of its plans and advocate for their intervention. In Hama and Dara’a, TGH will rehabilitate water systems damaged by the conflict without being engaged in WT, as authorities and private entities already ensure the trucking of water, a situation towards which local communities could develop some resilience. In Hama as in EG, the proposed intervention will allow responding to the current widely spread consumption of unsafe water, pumped in unsafe sources with no treatment systems.

Needs

Access to sufficient quantities of safe water remains limited, with increased water quality assurances and support to water systems rehabilitation, operation and maintenance unconditionally needed. At least 10 per cent of garbage is left in the public areas, which could leave people in some areas at increased risk of contracting infectious diseases such as Leishmaniasis. As a result of the crisis at least 70 per cent of sewage is untreated and at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional, exposing communities to significant health risks The three governorates targeted by the action have experienced long and violent sieges, causing the depletion of populations deprived of basic goods and services as well as destructions and casualties.. The demographic upheaval and damages to infrastructure have been less severe in the targeted area of the Hama governorate. Needs are however dire in this rural underserved area which witnessed population returns and is prone to new ones. All targeted areas have so far received little humanitarian assistance in comparison with the scale of needs. Humanitarian interventions have often been limited to emergency responses, with poor impact in terms of durable restoration of basic services. WASH facilities of the three targeted areas have been severely affected by the conflict, be it due to intensive shelling or to sabotage from armed groups. This is confirmed by the figures of the 2019 WASH HNO, according to which the target areas include 3 310 000 PIN, out of which 1 455 000 in acute need (984 000 in Eastern Ghouta, 432 000 in Dara’a and 39 000 in Hama). Water is indeed a priority need. According to TGH survey (Nov – Dec 2018) 31.4% and 68.4% of interviewed households in Hama and EG respectively pointed out water as their second priority need, right after food (TGH assessment). Water trucking (WT) is the main source of safe water, for 44% in the targeted locations of Hama and nearly 100% in these of EG, which mobilizes a significant share of the monthly income (8%) although humanitarian agencies (including TGH) ensure the bulk of WT in EG. WT however neither suffices to cover water needs nor is sustainable. In addition, water is being distributed through public water tanks which are barely accessible for persons with reduced mobility, bringing in protection concerns. The overall water scarcity leads people to resort to harmful practices, such as reducing water consumption for hygiene purpose, with tangible impacts such as the high prevalence of lice observed in the schools of EG and Hama. The resort to unsafe sources is also common. In eastern Ghouta, the water provided through WT is commonly completed by water fetched at shallow wells which water tests have proved polluted both chemically and bacteriologically. In the targeted locations of Hama, water proved either turbid or polluted due to damages caused to adjacent sewage and water pipes. In Dara’a, in areas where boreholes are not functioning, the population only relies on WT, which is expensive. As a consequence, the targeted populations’ current access to 100% clean water is poor, ranging from 0 to 10 liters per day and capita (TGH data). WASH in schools activities are direly needed to provide children with a dignified healthy learning environment granting protection. This includes functional latrines offering privacy and protecting girls from gender-based harassment, the lack of privacy and the insufficient number of toilets being a common reason for dropping out, in particular for girls as reported to TGH by interviewed local key informants. TGH Field assessments evidenced in addition that SPHERE standards are far from being reached in most schools, as typified by the situation in EG – an average one toilet for 139 pupils and an average availability of water representing less than 1 liter per day and pupil for 75% of them (TGH survey).

Implementation of activities

TGH will collaborate with DAWSA in Rural Damascus and water authorities in Hama and Dara’a in addition to SARC to complement interventions, respect national standards and avoid duplication with regards to WASH facility installation / improvement in targeted areas. 1- Provision of clean water through water trucking (WT) pending the completion of the works planned towards more durable solutions: TGH will ensure the provision of clean water through WT in the localities of Eastern Ghouta where access to water remains a critical issue and which are not served by the two other agencies involved in WT (namely UNICEF and ICRC). 2- Rehabilitation of 19 selected water systems damaged by the conflict: The following upgrades and rehabilitations of water systems aim at expanding and improving the access to water supply services and protected water sources in returnee and IDP settlements where access to safe water is below the 15 l/p/d threshold. 3- Provision of clean water at each targeted school through water trucking 4-Rehabilitation of existing toilets at schools and installation of additional units: This activity is limited to schools targeted by education activities. Existing toilets at schools will be rehabilitated when needed. Flushing systems are excluded from the proposed rehabilitation in most cases, as it requires schools being connected to the functional water networks, or would require significant increases of the volumes supplied through water trucking, which are here calculated on the basis of pour-flush toilets. Additional units of squatting toilets will be installed at the vicinity of schools, aiming at reaching at least minimum acceptable standards - i.e. 1 toilet for 30 girls and 1 toilet for 60 boys.. The proposed intervention includes washing facilities at the near rehabilitated / constructed toilets. This activity aims at responding to the observed very poor situation of schools with regards to access to water, in which 75% of schools ensure less than 1 liter (including water for school cleaning purpose) per pupil and per day, and 25% less than 2 liters. 5- Organization of HP sessions at schools, including the provision of hygiene items: This activity will complement the HP activities ensured in TGH ongoing projects in Rural Damascus and Rural Hama, for which implementation arrangements with SARC volunteers are already in place. HP materials, which are common to most humanitarian partners, will be provided by SARC volunteers. Since hygiene practices often vary depending on the area, TGH will tailor the hygiene promotion messages to each specific context and public. In Addition, TGH and SARC volunteers will use the frequent questions and remarks raised by the audiences to adjust the approach and messages or to modify the planned topics to be addressed. The methodology has been developed by SARC volunteers and TGH team and consists in reading stories to children, involving children in drawings and diffusion of video clips and images through video projector, as well as focus group discussions. 6- Implementation of waste management activity. The needs with regards to sanitation are huge, often too important to allow designing a relevant response with tangible impact (e.g. in Eastern Ghouta where destructions are massive). Some locations may however be suitable for interventions in that sector. Upon the condition that they are considered as a relevant support by local authorities – what in most cases depends on the availability of a dedicated truck, waste containers will be provided. In addition, some locations have been identified where sewage systems require minor repairs (including with the aim to avoid the contamination of water due to connections between water and sewage lines caused by damaged pipes), or technically simple interventions (e.g. desludging and/or unblocking).

Protection Risk Analysis

Risks linked with activity 1: Inequality in distribution: this risk is mitigated by the geographical dispatch of water tanks and the coordination with other agencies involved in WT. a close management and monitoring system is already in place (complaint mechanism ). The proposed WT is proposed along with an exit strategy towards the restoration of water supply services granting communities with an equal access to water. Risks linked with activity 2: Unequal distribution of services: this risk is mitigated by the selection for rehabilitation, jointly with key local stakeholders, of pre-existing water systems which used to guarantee an equal access to the resource. The design of the planned works takes into account the demand for an equal coverage of the targeted locations (connection to networks possible, gravity system). inadvertently empower or strengthen the position of armed groups or other actors & Risk of theft of WASH rehabilitation/construction materials such as generators and construction materials: this risk is very low (formerly besieged locations now controlled by the GoS and far from armed groups).The transfer of materials and equipment will not imply crossing risky areas. Given the critical situation with regards to water and the demand of communities and authorities alike for interventions, the risk of looting of equipment is considered as low; it will be lessened by the coordination with Water Authorities and by a final formal handover. - Risk of theft of chemicals which may be attractive to groups inside Syria: while the proposed water system design includes chlorination systems,the rehabilitated systems will be transferred to local entities upon completion. As mentioned above the targeted areas are not anymore prone to conflict and characterized by the presence of armed groups. Risks linked with activity 3: Inequality of distribution: this risk is mitigated by the proposed compliance with the SPHERE standards, as well as by the planned close monitoring including direct ties with key stakeholders at schools. Risks linked with activity 4: Toilets represent GBV risk: Mitigation measure: the construction of toilets in number allowing complying with the SPHERE standards.. Risks linked with activity 5: Inequality in distribution: distribution will be organized following HP sessions at schools.. The proposed sessions are also part of TGH strategy to attract to school OOS children, whose families will therefore not be forgotten by the planned distributions. Community-based committees are currently being established in support to TGH education activities, and will further guarantee that all access the proposed assistance. Gender insensitive contents verities of kits with inconsistent items: this risk will be mitigated by TGH’s work in compliance with standards. HK will follow the recommendation of the cluster in terms of contents, and will address the specific needs of women bur also, to all possible extent, persons with specific needs (i.e. elderly). Risks linked with activity 6: Movement of trucks in congested areas and reliability of services, potential exposure to explosive hazard, health risk associated with garbage removal. Mitigating measures are to consult community regarding services modality / frequency, truck routes and schedule; establish clear routes and schedule for trucks that use wider access routes; avoiding school areas during the time children travel to / from school; conduct safety training with truck drivers / operators; ensure staff involved in solid waste management activities wear appropriate Personal Protective Equipment.
