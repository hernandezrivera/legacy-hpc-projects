Project Summary

The Health and Nutrition cluster partners estimated that 1.3 million people are in need of humanitarian health interventions across the oPt, of whom two out of every three are in Gaza. Preparedness of the health system and communities to respond to potential outbreaks of communicable diseases has been identified amongst the key humanitarian needs by, the Health cluster. The overall purpose of this project is to ensure that the health system and vulnerable communities in the oPt is ready to respond to potential outbreaks of high-threat and new/emerging communicable diseases. In this project, the WHO will target an estimated 250,000 affected people in the West Bank (150,000) and Gaza (100,000), including 480 health workers with community awareness, capacity building and hygiene promotion interventions (collectively with the WASH cluster). Furthermore, the capacity of the health system to respond to potential outbreaks of communicable diseases will be supported, focussing on high-threat pathogens and new/emerging diseases such as acute watery diarrhea (AWD), Ebola Virus Diseases (EVD), new strains of influenza, etc.

1 - Needs

The occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) has experienced significant outbreaks in the past, including H1N1 outbreak in 2009 when 1,568 people were infected. Recently the re-emergence of Measles and seasonal influenza outbreaks both within the oPt and in the region (Israel, Jordan, etc) posed an additional burden on already overstretched health systems, especially in Gaza. Despite declaring high immunisation coverage rates, there were several cases of Measles registered in both the West Bank (74 cases) and Gaza (5 cases) in 2019. Another challenge that significantly influences the occurrence of outbreaks in the oPt is the availability of safe water and appropriate sanitation. In 2019, contaminated water was identified as a source of an outbreak of the intestinal disease with more than 1,000 people affected in Marda and Beit Foureek. The HNO 2020 identified, according to the Palestinian Hydrology Group, almost 250,000 in the West Bank and Gaza in need, including in around 50 communities in the West Bank with a shortage of safe water and the very old sewage net which needed replacement. In Gaza, the HNO further suggested that over 96% of the water from Gaza aquifers is unfit for human consumption and over 100,000 cubic meters of wastewater flowed in the sea every day. This may pose a significant risk for the emergence of high-threat pathogen communicable diseases such as acute watery diarrhea (AWD). Lack of safe water and sanitation and inadequate hygiene practices are also considered to be risk factors for the transmission of influenza, including any new strains of influenza. Additionally, in July 2019, WHO Director-General declared the Ebola virus disease (EVD) outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC). This required all Member States, including oPt to implement a set of key preparedness measures to save lives in case the EVD is suspected within the borders of the country.In order to be able to manage the potential outbreaks of high-threat pathogen communicable diseases, the following key health system and community strengthening preparedness measures need to be in place: 1) Capacity of the MoH to diagnose and respond to the outbreak, including availability of test kits, training for first-line health staff, risk communication, availability of protective equipment, coordination with other sectors, epidemiological surveillance, and good health information system; 2) Community awareness and health education on hygiene and other behavior change that can minimise the transmission; 3) Work with other sectors, such as WASH to facilitate better access to safe water sources and sanitation. WHO plans to support the MoH (Ramallah and Gaza DFA) in addressing the above-identified needs and strengthening the capacities of the health authorities in oPt to be prepared and to respond for potential outbreaks of communicable diseases with a focus on high-threat pathogens and new/emerging diseases.

2 - Activities

WHO as the lead agency for health is mandated to support the MoH in preparedness for all-hazard scenarios. This is done through the Country Cooperation Strategy agreed by the MoH and WHO. However, the emergence of new high-threat pathogen communicable diseases such as Ebola Virus Disease (EVD), requires extra preparedness measures to quickly save lives including capacity building for diagnosis and clinical management of cases and procurement of required testing kits, personal protective equipment and WHO-recommended treatment courses for possible patients to ensure decreased fatality rates. WHO will also support the efforts of the MoH and other partners, especially WASH, with community-based awareness interventions to influence the transmission of high-threat pathogens.The project will have the following objectives:1. The health system in the oPt is ready to detect and manage the outbreaks of high-threat pathogen and new/emerging communicable diseases (EVD, Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) and/or new strains of influenza);2. Most vulnerable communities in oPt are aware of the risks, mitigation and community-based response measures in case outbreaks of high-threat pathogen and new/emerging communicable diseases.WHO will implement the following preparedness activities in close collaboration with the Ministry of Health in Ramallah and DFA health authorities in Gaza. WHO will plan its activities based on the national MoH preparedness plan and the Strategic Plan for prevention and control of communicable diseases in emergency situations (2018). WHO will target an estimated 250,000 affected people, including an estimated 127,500 women and 122,500 men in the West Bank (150,000 total) and Gaza (100,000 total) in 50 most affected communities, including 480 health workers:1. Support the capacity of the national system to timely and effectively respond to outbreaks of communicable diseases caused by new and emerging diseases and high-threat pathogens. This activity will focus on the following:•Training of health workers on diseases outbreak guideline, including clinical management for emerging and high-pathogen diseases (AWD, EVD new strains of influenza);• Procurement and delivery of lab equipment for early diagnosis, protection of health workers and the treatment courses for patients (if needed) as recommended by WHO; The procurement will largely depend on the epidemiological situation in the neighboring countries as well as any alert issued by WHO for new and/or high-threat pathogens. WHO will coordinate its procurement with the MoH, WHO EMRO/HQ and partners in oPt.• Development of 24/7 mechanism for early detection & notification for any of the above risks that has the potential to cross the borders.2. Support community awareness on outbreak prevention and control measures and hygiene promotion. This activity will focus on:• Development of key messages, including social media messages;• Printing and dissemination of health and hygiene materials for the public;• Organisation of community awareness sessions in the communities, including simulation exercises (where and when appropriate).3. WHO will work closely with the WASH Cluster/Sector and other stakeholders to actively identify and address key community concerns regarding the availability of and access to safe water and adequate sanitation by sharing information and coordinating the development of hygiene messages and other activities.This project will complement the ongoing WHO's effort to support the Palestinian Ministry of Health to strengthen its core IHR capacity.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

WHO places centrality of protection at the core of its response by identifying who is at risk, how and why, taking into account the specific vulnerabilities that underlie these risks, including those experienced by men, women, girls and boys, and groups such as Palestinian refugees, internally displaced persons, older persons and persons with disabilities. This allow WHO to plan, collectively with the affected population, the set of interventions that are culturally appropriate, based on the needs, relevant, timely and are in line with humanitarian principles. A feedback mechanism will be established to ensure that assistance is delivered in adherence to the principles of “do no harm”.
