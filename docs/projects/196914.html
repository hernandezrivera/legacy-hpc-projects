Project Summary

The proposed project targets Azaz sub-district,
GAN aims to enhance the resilience of affected communities in Mare and Aghtrin through the provision of livelihood opportunities and to have community resilience strengthened.
GAN aims to support the livelihoods of vulnerable crises- affected communities, by starting with Market mapping which aims to identify market gaps and needs, and then announcing and selecting participants according to eligibility and vulnerability criteria and giving priorities to women-headed HHs, PWDs when possible and later provide vocational training for 150 persons on topics that will be identified according to the market mapping with giving priorities to vocations related to PPEs production and other locally produced general covid19 protection related items such as hygiene items.
After finishing the VT, GAN will deliver SME training for the successful applicants considering their attendance percentage, tests results, and trainers’ evaluations according to which each participant will submit a business plan which will be evaluated by a formed committee.
Each successful business plan will be supported with cash grants of 1200$ for the first 50 best plans to be paid on three tranches. While the remaining 100 participants will be supported with job-related kits of 400$ each to help them engage in the market and work.
These participants attended all the vocational training and the SME training, but their business plans could not get high scores to be funded will be supported with job-related kits.

3 - Needs

Throughout 2021, the macro-economic context continued to deteriorate sharply and is accelerating into 2022. The combined effects of currency depreciation, soaring prices, reduced fiscal revenue increasing domestic debt, widespread job loss. The long-standing needs of an estimated 6.9M IDPs remain staggering, particularly for over 2M in camp. With a disproportionate impact on female-headed households, older persons without family support, PWD, and children. An increasing number of women and adolescent girls are forced to work in order to provide for their families, either as sole breadwinners or contributing to the family income.Combined with the economic downturn, the COVID-19 pandemic pushed more Syrians into unemployment. The income gap has continued to widen, only 10%of households have an income above the cost of SMEB.Rising debt has been observed across Syria and across all population groups since 2021, with price increases and loss of income as the two top reasons.Detrimental and negative coping mechanisms continue to prevail. 85,5% of households continue to use borrowing as the main coping strategy to close the income-expenditure gap, followed by remittances for 49% of households and savings depletion for 37,4% of HHs.Limited participation in community life and decision processes from females and youth is observed. The ones taking decisions on behalf of the community are “senior males”. Female and youth representation in decision-making accounts for 43,1% in NWS.

4 - Implementation of activities

The goal will be done by starting with Market mapping to identify market gaps and needs according to which the VT training topics and domains will be identified, then start several advocacy and community sensitization activities that aims at engaging the communities, explaining the merits of the intervention and the idea of SMEs, raising awareness of the importance of sustaining small businesses, and clearly communicating the selection criteria in order to reduce the risk of tensions among communities, then the selected beneficiaries will be verified and invited to join the VT which will target 150 persons and last for two months on topics that will be identified according to the market mapping, then GAN will deliver SME trainings that will last for 1 month, the participants will learn about basis of business establishment, marketing financial study, feasibility, competition evaluation.. etc., then each will submit a business plan which will be evaluated and approved by a formed committee for this purpose.The final stage will last for 2 months of coaching period including support with cash grants of 1200$ to the first 50 selected projects.While the remaining participants will be supported with job-related kits of 400$ each to help them engage in the market and work. These participants attended all the vocational training and the SME training, but their business plans could not get high scores to be funded will be supported with job-related kits.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

GAN support equal and safe access to life-saving assistance without discrimination of any kind. pay particular attention to not exclude women, girls, people with disabilities, and other people belonging to disadvantaged or marginalized groups Within this operating context and COVID-19,a key Do No Harm principle is to avoid exacerbating additional tensions and conflicts in communities already competing for finite resources Beneficiaries may face problems particularly with sexual assault and robbery including at official or unofficial checkpoints while going to and from a training halls, so the halls will be inside the communities, during daylight hours and safeguard that there is appropriate segregation for women to create a culturally sensitive and safe operational environment GAN team will include female members to make sure that women feel able to comfortably discuss their needs and constraints, the feedback mechanisms will be available and accessible for all persons with disabilities with specific regard to persons with communication difficulties, where will be providing written, verbal and assisted modes for complaints and feedback GAN intends to prevent abuse by implementing procedures to protect beneficiaries through best recruitment practices, staff induction, creating an open and aware culture and assigning clear management responsibilities, ensuring that victims are supported, and perpetrators held to account. 8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Aid workers or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they should be serving, particularly children. The risk may be heightened for children with disabilities, those unaccompanied, orphans and younger children. mitigation measures: GAN is a member of the PSEA network, attending a monthly meeting and following the guidelines, so to mainstream the protection from sexual exploitation and abuse in the project, GAN has developed a policy stating standards of conduct, including acts of SEA, and a work plan to implement the policy in place, staff members will sign the policy and received systematized and refreshed training on PSEA. GAN Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policies are in place, practiced and monitored. Polices cover standards of behavior for the organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and include accountability measures. The Polices address risks at the organizational level and the program level.GAN will provide awareness sessions about the organization’s policies and procedures.GSN ’s staff and targeted communities will have access to safe and effective reporting systems including knowing when they will get a response to their report and having the right to report anonymously.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: Collaboration from ED and local authorities, Markets are functioning properly and Trainers are available. The trainer kits' contents are available in the local market Risk1:Robbery or loss of cash by the FSP. M1: Payment after distributions: GAN only transfers to the FSP after the cash is distributed Risk2:Due to social norms,girls’ parents may not allow them to attend as planned; the training activities and the mini-grants may lead to increased risk Mitigation:announcing and community sensitization for the project in the name of GAN under the umbrella of Killis ED will increase level of trust on the project and will allow women to participate safely Risk3:Targeting a limited number of beneficiaries,could create tension in the targeted community between beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries and between the participants themselves for getting the min grants Mitigation:Sharing the complaint and feedback communication lines with targeted community members that allow all beneficiaries to send their complaints,comments, and feedback Sharing the selection criteria with the local community before starting with the implementation Delivering the mini-grants based on beneficiaries’ scorecard result Risk4:grantee receive first payment and doesn't continue the project establishment Mitigation:the second payment will not be received before confirming that the first payment was consumed for project activities.
