Project Summary

Impact Health Organization (IHO) proposes the Provision of Emergency Health Care Service project to be implemented in Magwi and Fashod County in the year 2019. This project will deliver a package of Integrated Health services activities in an effort to ensure continued access to minimum, and responsive integrated essential healthcare service that meet the needs of the vulnerable populations (IDPs, host community and returnees) in Magwi County, Eastern Equatoria and Fashoda County Upper Nile state by focusing on major causes of morbidity and mortality.
IHO conducted a needs assessment in both Fashoda October 2018 and Magwi County June and October 2018, and concluded that the health systems in the two locations were not only challenged with limited access to human resource medicine and other supplies but also limited access to blood transfusion services, obstetric care service, mental health services but also limited access to WASH services but the referral system is hampered by poor roads. The community has limited access to health education, Immunization coverage is still low. The leading causes of morbidity include Malaria, Pneumonia, Watery Diarrhoea among children, HIV/AIDS and Tuberculosis. The IPC analysis September 2018-March 2019 classified Magwi County malnutrition level in crisis stage IPC 3 classification and the Smart Survey conducted in Magwi County in 2017 reported the GAM rate at 15.2% slightly above WHO emergency threshold of 15%, a serious indication that malnutrition rates might increase in the near future.
In this project, IHO will provide lifesaving quality health service to 10000 Individuals (5040 Male and 4960 Female) of 21% whom shall be children under five years and 47% under 15 years of age part of health cluster target in the Magwi and Fashoda Counties by undertaking 1 mobile team, 1 PHCU and 1 PHCC and for fashoda County. IHO will work closely with communities to identify the problem affecting them and the community will greatly participate development and implementation of activities and community shall provide feedback on the progress of the interventions. The project will build capacities of the communities through trainings and awareness campaigns designed to halt the spread of communicable disease. IHO will address unique health needs of women and girls, particularly respond to GBV, including clinical management of rape. Finally, IHO will work closely with WHO, health cluster and MoH in ensuring provision of timely live saving health care services.

Needs

The Republic of South Sudan faces considerable humanitarian problems, which have increased substantially since the outbreak of violence across the country in December 2013. Among the mostly affected states is Upper Nile State and security situation in Upper Nile state remains uncertain with armed conflict in almost all parts of the state. Among the most affected Counties in Upper Nile state is Fashoda County. In addition, a major crisis erupted in July 2016 and intensified in the Greater Equatoria region which further aggravated the humanitarian situation, producing secondary or third displacement with populations under stress lacking access to basic services and constraining humanitarian access. The situation has been deteriorating with ongoing conflict in the targeted counties as the health care systems is challenged with limited access to safe water, inadequate human resource, poor waste management and infection prevention and control measures, poor infrastructure, potential risks of disease outbreak ie cholera, Ebola etc.Impact Health Organization Assessment conducted in Magwi County June 2018, showed that most locations in the county did not have access to timely lifesaving health care services among other basic services. The health facilities were not only challenged with limited access to human resource medicine and other supplies but also limited access to blood transfusion services, obstetric care service, mental health services but also limited access to WASH services. The referral system is hampered by poor roads. The community has limited access to health education, Immunization coverage is still low. The leading causes of morbidity include Malaria, Pneumonia, Watery Diarrhoea among children, HIV/AIDS and Tuberculosis. In another assessment conducted by IHO in October 2018 showed that access latrines remained a big challenge in most the health facilities as 19 out of 25 Health Facilities need new latrines or rehabilitation of existing latrines and 15 out of 25 Health facilities had no access to hand washing stand facility. The IPC analysis September 2018-March 2019 classified Magwi County malnutrition level in crisis stage IPC 3 classification and the Smart Survey conducted in Magwi County in 2017 reported the GAM rate at 15.2% slightly above WHO emergency threshold of 15%, a serious indication that malnutrition rates might increase in the near future. Just like Magwi County IHO needs assessment in Fashoda October 2018 showed The health facilities were not only challenged with limited access to human resource medicine and other supplies but also limited access to blood transfusion services, obstetric care service, mental health services and limited access to WASH services. Malaria was the leading cause of morbidity and mortality, followed by Diarrhea diseases. Majority of the population walk long distance to access health services, and household lack limited access to mosquito nets, latrines, safe and clean water and health education. The IPC analysis September 2018-March 2019 classified malnutrition level in Fashoda County at IPC 4 classification (Emergency Phase) and Smart survey May 2018 reported GAM at 16.1. In response to the above needs, IHO seeks to implement PHCU, PHCC and Mobile services in Both locations to meet the health needs of the communities and save lives.

Activities

IHO will implement PHCU, PHCC and Mobile Clinic Activities:PHCU, PHCC and Mobile clinicThrough this Heath integrated programming approach;IHO will improve access and scale-up responsiveness by establishing 1 Mobile clinic, support 1 PHCU and 1 PHCC in Magwi County and 1 Mobile Clinic, 1 PHCU and 1 PHCC in Fashoda County. These will provide OPD service including screening of women and children for malnutrition, management of uncomplicated cases and referral of complicated cases., Immunization of children under five and pregnant women especially emergency Measles vaccinations, malaria testing and treatment, provision of CMR services, Basic management of SGBV survivors (PEP, treatment for STI, wound care ), undertake Ante- and postnatal check-up, conduct normal deliveries at community level, and provide family planning. IHO will prevent, detect and respond to epidemic prone diseases by training staff on IDSR and EWARs, submitted reports in areas where interventions are undertaken and integrate WASH response at all identified health services. To improve resilience among vulnerable populations, IHO will conduct health education to increase demand for uptake of mental Health & Psychosocial services and GBV services and to ensure integrated response for health/Wash/Nutrition/ food security and protection, IHO will build capacity building of Nutrition staff on standard procedures and guidelines for screening and referral of SAM children with medical complications (Malaria RDT training), Water quality testing and Monitoring in health facilities to mitigate disease spread, undertake integration of Health education messages across sectors on risk communication and work with protection partners on Mapping of SGBV services and referrals.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

The project shall provide information information to the community through monthly community meeting which shall be held every month at all service delivery points. In the same regard, information share provided in print media place on the notice board or walls in all the service delivery centre as where necessary information shall be printed in Local language. In addition information shall be share on community radio especially on health education and availability of mobile clinic services. Lastly, the addition information shall be shared one on one depending on the needs for example during management of rape, IHO shall promote confidentiality.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project is a result of the needs assessment, therefore based on the needs identified IHO will develop message to suit the targeted population for example In the baseline survey conducted by IHO in Fashoda County showed that girls age married between 15-17 years. When girls get married their bodies and mind are not ready for marriage and pregnancy exposed to life threatening conditions such Obstetric fistula. Therefore, working with protection partners, IHO will undertake education session at the health facilities and Mobile clinic promoting girl’s education and delay of pregnancy among girls. other message shall include, care of child, breastfeeding, complimentary feeding, ANC, delivery at the health facility, PNC, HIV/TB prevention, treatment and care, Malaria prevention and treatment among others.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

IHO has a policy of zero tolerance of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) by IHO staff members and the employees or any other persons engaged and controlled by IHO Contractors. All IHO staff members are trained on PSEA Awareness-Raising. The organization staff or contractors or suppliers are prohibited from exploiting beneficiaries for exchange of services for sex or any form of abuse. All IHO staff members have a duty to report in good faith any SEA allegations, concerns or suspicions, or retaliation related to SEA and SEA of affected populations constitutes gross misconduct and can result in disciplinary action, including summary dismissal and referral for criminal prosecution, where appropriate.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

IHO will promote transparency during the project implementation by directly involving the community in every stage of the project to ensure clear understanding of objectives of the project, expectations and stakeholders. IHO has incorporated the Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations (CAAP). IHO conducted a needs assessment using involving a number of methods including the structured questionnaire, Key Informant Guide and Focus discussion guide. The structured questionnaire was administered at household level targeting returnee, host and IDP households, the focus discussion involved men, women, boys and girls of different background and the Key informant involved the community chiefs, county health department staff, and state ministry of health staff.
