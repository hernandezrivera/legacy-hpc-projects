Project Summary

In its eighth year, the conflict inside Syria shows all the signs of a complex protracted humanitarian crisis. The project is aiming to support vulnerable IDPs and host community members to contribute towards increased resilience of vulnerable households and support the community cohesion and engagement. The project target is: VT: 500 individuals
IKG: 200 individuals
CFW: 200 individuals
Infrastructure/ economic rehab: 10000 individuals (women - men - boys - girls)
The project sub-districts are:
Al Bab – Azaz – Afrin - Idleb - Bennsh - Maaret Tamsrin – Ariha - Ma'arrat An Nu'man
This will be applied through the following interventions:
1 – Vocational Training provision: a sustainable and restorative socio-economic interventions that starts with identifying the most needed professions in the targeted area, then support them by selecting the willing beneficiaries to learn and work in these professions, train them technically, and prepare them to be able to open their own businesses through Business Development Service and Life Skills training, then providing trainees with practical training through paid internship by contracting with business owners, in addition to distributing IKGs related to VT’s subject, with the support of the economic unit through follow up and providing consultation.
2 – In-Kind Grants provision: An income generating activity starts with identifying careers that many people have the required skills, but don’t have the financial capacity to start. Then, creating selection criteria to enroll beneficiaries, providing Business Development Service and Life Skills training to prepare beneficiaries for developing their own business plans, then distribute IKGs related to the proposed BPs, with the support of the economic unit through follow-ups and consultations.
3 - Community supporting projects: infrastructure/ economic-supporting projects that uphold and advocate the social cohesion and the engagement of all community categories in the targeted area with durable and sustainable impact, starting from addressing needs and infrastructural/ economic damages, then creating Community Representatives Board and train them to be able to prepare projects’ proposals and mobilize social resources. Moreover, consulting local engineering experts and local authorities will be required to be ready for implementing field activities, after the implementation is being done, a proper handover will be made to local representatives to undertake project’s management afterward. CFW schema will be included in this intervention, by enrolling daily waged-beneficiaries to work in the field implementation activities.
At the beginning of the project Takaful will conduct a meeting with local community and local actors to
ensure their participation in the project, explain the project goals and take their suggestions and notes
to develop the project activities and implementation.
Takaful will coordinate with ER cluster and with other organization who works in the same sector and field to reach the right beneficiaries and avoid any duplication.

Needs

In 2018, Syria has been witnessing rapidly changing dynamics of the eight years long conflict since additional areas have been retaken by the Syrian Government.Inside Syria, over 13.1 million Syrians are in urgent need of humanitarian assistance. Estimates of people living below the poverty line vary between 65% to 84% of the resident population. The living conditions of Syrians have significantly eroded, severely increasing their vulnerability and undermining the ability to cope with the consequences of the existing crisis as well as future shocks. The after-effects will continue for many years, especially for women and girls due to the entrenched complexity of the issues they continue to face every day. Hence, the response to the still very high needs must also evolve into longer-term recovery intervention able to rejuvenate social and economic dynamics. The return and sustainable reintegration of the displaced will necessitate an enhanced availability of basic and social infrastructure and services, a secure environment, and viable livelihood opportunities.(Early recovery HRP 2019).Takaful Alsham focuses in its early recovery projects to contribute in improving the crisis affected people’s and communities’ ability to cope with the shocks and to reduce their dependence on emergency supplies and services as such we conducted multi-sector need assessment included early recovery and livelihood. The purpose of the assessment is to assess the spontaneous efforts in the targeted communities attaining decent livelihoods opportunities, people's access to basic services and the most needed vocational training provisions for the markets. The assessment conducted in 13 sub-districts in Aleppo and Idleb Governorates, the most important findings as follow: All service facilities witnessed a severe decline in their performance, but the solid waste disposal service witnessed a more moderate improvement than it was before the crisis. The damage in the infrastructure facilities is 39%. The percent of the unemployment rate is 64%. The most needed support to create income-generating activities were developing the infrastructure, supporting agriculture and livestock activities, supporting cash grants and establish livelihood opportunities. The percentage of women participation declined in all sectors compared to men participation before the crises. While the provisions needed in the markets were household electricity, car maintenance and computer maintenance. The most constraints affected the markets were the need of maintenance the markets' infrastructure to restore local economic recovery, lack of markets assets like food and NFIs and security difficulties in accessing. Protection Risk Analysis

- Biased targeting of beneficiaries or biased selection of response activities.Mitigating Measure:- Ensuring that a comprehensive needs analysis informs the response formulation and that this is openly documented and transparent.- Establishing community mechanisms to identify needs and arriving at a consensus as of who should be assisted.- Communicating the planned response to the local authorities and communities concerned. - Efficiently delivering on the obligation of ‘Accountability to (Affected) Populations’ by keeping the local authorities and communities concerned regularly informed of the project progress.- Making sure that, some process of receiving feedback is put in place.- Escalation of the security situation. Mitigating Measure:Follow up on current security situation day-by-day, and prepare emergency plans.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1- Assumption: Supplies will be delivered on time, Risk: Delayed supply due to road cuts or border closures, Probability: High, Impact: High, MS: An alternative plan of insurance will be developed from more than one source.2- Assumption: Equipment required for VT are available and affordable locally, Risk: preparing training lab. For VT may require a variety of unavailable equipment locally, Probability: Medium, Impact: High, MS: An alternative plan will be set to train the trainees in Takaful Al-Sham VT centers located in Azaz, Al- Atareb, and Albab that are easily accessible by beneficiaries and fully equipped.3- Assumption: Community Supporting Projects are directed to meet the local needs and requirements, Risk: Projects are not suited to existing needs and experience required for implementation, Probability: Low, Impact: High, MS: An independent need assessment will identify the needs of the community, the existing expertise and the resources needed, then according to that a plan and study of the projects will be developed and executed.4- Assumption: Suitable number of beneficiaries will apply to be enrolled in CFW schema, Risk: Large numbers of the applicants may apply to participate in CFW schema, Probability: Medium, Impact: Low, MS: Selection criteria will be established with no chance of personal- judgment, taking into consideration the participation of the local representatives as a reference.
