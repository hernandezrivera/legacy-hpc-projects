Project Summary

In collaboration with the State Ministry of Health, the project implemented by ALIMA has for objective to reduce the morbidity and mortality for vulnerable host and displaced populations, including women and children, affected by the crisis in South Kordofan.
It will be achieved through increasing the immediate access to comprehensive health services by vulnerable host and displaced populations affected by the crisis in South Kordofan, including women and children. The project will also increase access to comprehensive quality primary health care, including nutrition, sexual reproductive health and mental health services, through PHCs support and mobile clinics for vulnerable hard to reach populations in Kadugli and Reif Arshargi localities, especially women and children, and increasing access to quality life-saving secondary health care for pregnant and lactating women and survivors of sexual violence at the maternity of Kadugli Teaching Hospital. The project will also aim at strengthening the capacity of SMoH Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) to deliver minimum healthcare services to population affected by disease outbreaks and population displacement due to flooding and armed conflict.
ALIMA aims to improve access to comprehensive primary health care services, including sexual and reproductive health, GBV response, nutrition and MHPSS, through its support of 2 primary health centers whose facilities are still accessible and safe in Moutar and Al-Bardab. To ensure the access of hard-to-reach vulnerable populations to quality care, the project will support the deployment of 6 mobile clinics providing PHC in the remaining areas of Kohliyat, Mashaesh, Miri, Saraf Dai, Hajer Mach and Dara. The team will deliver primary health care for children under 5 and pregnant women, including nutritional screening. They will also ensure the provision of a comprehensive package to ensure the management of sexual violence victims.
To make the community aware of the services, 50 community health workers (CHW) will be chosen from each community to conduct awareness raising activities about sexual and reproductive health and gender-based violence, essential nutritional practices, health promotion, personal and environmental hygiene, and referrals from the community to the health facility.
Survivor-centered GBV case-management, such as clinical management of rape and MHPSS services will be offered at the ALIMA supported Kadugli Teaching Hospital to the most vulnerable patients. 2 highly skilled midwives will undergo a full capacity building training in mental health issues with ALIMA will ensure MHPSS activities; they will be supervised and their activities coordinated by a sexual and reproductive health manager. Several consulting sessions will be provided to each survivor.
ALIMA will improve access to quality life-saving secondary health care for pregnant and lactating women and survivors of sexual violence at the maternity in Kadugli Teaching Hospital. ALIMA will support the maternity ward in Kadugli Hospital to deliver comprehensive emergency obstetric and neonatal care. ALIMA will strengthen the skills of midwives and other auxiliary nurses through on the job-training to improve patient care and global management of the maternity ward.

1 - Needs

In 2022, ALIMA supported the maternity and paediatric wards in Kadugli Teaching Hospital by providing essential medicines, training healthcare workers, minor rehabilitation of the wards and providing incentive to staff as means of motivation for the extra workload. Much improvement has been made in term of quality of care, hygiene practices, medical supply and overall ward’s hygiene. ALIMA has however, noticed that the staff knowledge and skills are very low because their qualifications are equally very low. For example, most of the staff in maternity are TBAs instead of midwives, and in paediatric ward, most are community health workers instead of trained nurses. Lot of advocacy with MoH to recruit trained staff and training are needed to optimize the minimum standard quality of patient care. In the last five months (Jun-Oct), ALIMA has recorded 19 GBV cases (rape) of which nine seek care within 72 hours; this could be associated with the GBV sensitization among the staff and the PHC communities. Strengthening the sensitization and services will improve the implementation of GBV programme in South Kordofan State.ALIMA also supported two PHCUs and set up three Mobile clinics to hard reached communities. These health facilities non-functional due to lack of staff and medical supplies; by June 2022, all these facilities were up and running. The mobile clinic sites had functioning PHCUs but were destroyed in the 2019 conflict and MoH have not be able re-start healthcare provision. Although ALIMA has started implementing the emergency life-saving referral from the ALIMA supported PHCU/Mobile clinic sites to KTH, coordination among partners to roll over this referral system throughout the State is lacking. There are needs for MoH to start taking their responsibility of rehabilitate the damage PHCUs, placing permanent staff and essential medical supplies to these health facilities. While MoH is preparing for this, partner like ALIMA should continue providing basic healthcare to the people. The management of MAM and SAM cases remains challenging due to difficulty of access and lack of coverage of part of the package. Nutrition services are rather fragmented, especially in times of strikes, and referral pathways are currently limited. There are three communities (Miri and Hajer Mach in Kadugli Locality and Dara in Reif El Shargi Locality) with a total population of 30,100 inhabitants that are facing difficulty in accessing health and nutrition care. Hajer Mach had PHCU but was also destroy by conflict and are left with no healthcare. ALIMA will plan providing Health and Nutrition services for these population.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

To make the community aware of the services, 50 community health workers (CHW) will be chosen from each community to conduct awareness raising activities about sexual and reproductive health and gender-based violence, health promotion, personal and environmental hygiene, and referrals from the community to the health facility. Considering the lack of sensitization and level of stigmatization against survivors of GBV, this community-level intervention is critical to increase the number of survivors reaching life-saving health and psychological care services. This approach aims at building the confidence of communities to reduce stigma and to foster disclosure by survivors and encourage them to reach to available GBV services. Survivor-centered GBV case-management, such as clinical management of rape and MHPSS services will be offered at the ALIMA supported Kadugli Teaching Hospital to the most vulnerable patients. 2 highly skilled midwives will undergo a full capacity building training in mental health issues with ALIMA will ensure MHPSS activities; they will be supervised and their activities coordinated by a sexual and reproductive health manager. Several consulting sessions will be provided to each survivor. ALIMA has a leading role in the SGBV Referral Pathways Taskforce to strengthen community sensitization and support the coordination of referral mechanisms to link survivors with timely, appropriate medical and mental health care as well as protection services.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The projects have a feedback and complaints handling mechanism that allows the local population to provide feedback. In addition, an e-mail address is made available and communicated to all project staff. These feedback and complaint mechanisms are inclusive. Feedback and complaints are stored in a secure database. In terms of Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse ALIMA is committed to prevent any sexual exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries by humanitarian workers. ALIMA's staff recognizes the problem related to the imbalance of power in the humanitarian field and the potential exploitation that this could cause. Sexual exploitation and abuse are condemned by law and international standards and ALIMA condemns such behavior. They are absolutely contrary to the humanitarian principles and ethics to which ALIMA and its employees adhere. Measures are in place to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse: Any employee involved in a case of sexual exploitation or abuse will be subject to immediate termination and ALIMA may refer the matter to the national authorities or the authorities of the country of origin of the employee being prosecuted. Sexual activity with persons under the age of 18 is strictly prohibited, even in the event of differing local laws regarding age of consent or age of majority. Mistaken belief in a child's age will not be taken as a defense. Any exchange of goods or services, including assistance due to recipients, for sexual services or other sexual or exploitative behavior will not be tolerated. All ALIMA representatives and managers are responsible for creating and maintaining a safe environment that protects against sexual exploitation and abuse and are encouraged to pay attention to promoting the safety of women and girls and any other vulnerable population. ALIMA staff are urged to report any inappropriate behavior they may witness. Several complaint mechanisms have been defined. One of them is to send an alert to this newly created email address: alert@alima.ngo. All of ALIMA's employees are key elements in the enforcement of this policy. We are a humanitarian organization and therefore have a great responsibility, individually and collectively, to prevent abuses or violence from being committed in our name or through the positions that are assigned to us by our organization At the field level, the HR/Admin Manager is in charge of enforcing the policy, while at the country level the HR coordination, the Deputy Head of Mission as well as the Head of mission have the task to enforce the policy.
