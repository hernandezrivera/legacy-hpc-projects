Project Summary

The Shelter and Nonfood items project will address emergency shelter and non-food items needs of Internally Displaced Persons, Returnees and host community in Abyei Administrative Area, Twic and Cueibet Counties in Lake and Warrap States respectively. The modality of assistance comprises of Cash Voucher Assistance in locations where the market is functional and with the capacity to support cash intervention. In-kind assistance will be delivered where markets functionality is weak or non-existent. Out of the 2500HHs targeted in the intervention, 900HHs will receive SNFIs assistance using Cash Voucher Assistance. The SNFIs project is designed taking into consideration expressed, assessed and prioritized needs obtained during Humanitarian Needs Overview analysis, Inter Agency assessments and Integrated Phase Classification analysis for Cueibet, Twic and Abyei Administrative Area. The project will target 2500 Households (an estimated 15000 Individuals). Distribution of beneficiaries in each targeted location being as follows: Abyei Administrative Area: 700HHs, Twic: 1000HHs and Cueibet:800HHs. TGCDA will also ensure that there is proper coordination with SNFIs cluster at National and State levels to avoid duplication of efforts and assistance. TGCDA is a key member of Shelter and NFIs, Education, Wash, FSL and Protection clusters and participates in coordination meetings at the national, state and county levels. To ensure accountability, TGCDA will coordinate the project through the RRC, Chiefs and Cluster systems. Affected persons will be involved in implementation through Accountability to affected person’s committees for openness, accountability and local contribution. To ensure an integrated response, TGCDA will work with Health, Nutrition and WASH partners on joint Measles messaging, training on use and disposal of mosquito nets. Referrals of protection concerns will be made through the protection partners. FSL and Nutrition partners will be engaged at demonstration and kitchen gardens where farmers and households will be guided on proper utilization of mosquito nets. In some instances, people use the nets to build tukuls, fence gardens and for fishing which is not in line with the objectives of the assistance. Distribution and demonstration of use of mosquito nets will be done in health facilities, at demonstration farms and at nutrition stabilization and treatment centers frequented by mothers and pregnant women. To mainstream protection, PSEA messaging, referrals, robust Complaint and Feedback Mechanism and safety audits will be conducted to protect beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries from all forms of protection concerns.

1. Needs

According to Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) Analysis Report, Twic and Cueibet Counties are classified as Emergency (IPC4) and Critical Acute Malnutrition for the period September 2023 to July 2024. Due to food insecurity, households in these two counties are expected to dedicate the little money they will get to buying food rather than meeting S/NFIs needs. According to the report, more than 25% of the people residing in these counties will depend on humanitarian assistance to meet daily calorific needs during the projected period. Twic and Cueibet Counties are prone to flooding. As a result, the high water levels will constrain affected populations from accessing forests to harvest shelter materials such as wood and grass. Also, malaria and pneumonia will increase due to increased mosquito breeding and elevated levels of coldness and will most likely affect individuals with no access to mosquito nets, blankets and shelters. From 15 April 2023, armed clashes erupted between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) in multiple cities across Sudan. Clashes initially took place in cities across Northern and Khartoum states, later spreading across the Darfur and Kordofan regions. As a result, there has been an increase in the influx of returnees into South Sudan. For example, a recent Inter Agency Assessment (IRNA) conducted in the county between 10th and 11th August 2023 has identified more than 5,436 South Sudan returnees’ households currently residing the county. During the IRNA assessments, the assessment teams noted returnees living in overcrowded Tukuls (2-5 people). Returnees with no access to tukuls were seen residing in open spaces, school buildings, under trees while others were living within host community households. Assessment teams that visited local markets noticed lack of shelter, cooking and sleeping items. Where the items were available, the prices are prohibitively high and out of reach for majority of the returnees. Focus Group Discussions conducted during the IRNA noted that the returnees were also sharing available blankets and sleeping mates. Majority of the returnees reported that they were not able to collect their belongings when the conflict erupted in their localities in Sudan. Some of them reportedly sold household assets such as cooking and serving items to get money they could use to buy food. As a result, they are forced to borrow materials e.g. cooking utensils for cooking and water containers for collecting waters from the host communities. Elderly persons, pregnant and lactating women as well as children were seen sleeping without mosquito nets. As a result, malaria cases have been on the rise among the returnees, and the host community. Unfortunately, cases of miscarriages were reported among pregnant women because of malaria infection. On the other hand, Abyei has been a center of tension and violence with dozens of people having been reportedly killed in Inter-communal violence involving Twic Dinka, Ngok Dinka, and Misseriya communities in 2023. During the conflict, homes were burnt or destroyed, while displaced persons lost household items such as cooking and serving utensils, sleeping materials such as mats, blankets and mosquito nets. Displaced persons in Abyei require immediate assistance in form of shelter and non-food items to enable them lie dignified lives. Cueibet has been recently affected by prolonged dry spelled during cultivation season following by heavy rainfall resulting into flooding in the area. Most of the houses are partially submerged under flood water while grass-thatched houses/Tukuls have started collapsing upon weakening by the flood water in locations such as Tiaptiap, Citcok, Pagor, Ngap, Agangrial, Abiriu, Duony Abyei-Cok, and Mayath. Due to flooding in Cueibet, traders are unable to resupply the market with basic food and non-food items triggering scarcity and high prices of basic household items.

2. Project activities

ACTIVITY 1: Project meetings: Inception, Review and end of project meetings will be conducted with Returnees, Host Community and IDPs to provide information on project objective, duration, anticipated results, kind of assistance to be provided, category of beneficiaries targeted in the project, modality of implementation and seek community support of the project. ACTIVITY 2: Selection of 90 Enumerators (30 in each project location), out of which 45 will be females. Enumerators will conduct mobilization of beneficiaries, communication and sensitization, registration, setting up of distribution points, distribution of non-food items and post distribution monitoring. ACTIVITY 3: Selection of beneficiaries: TGCDA’s project staff will work closely with AAP committee, enumerators and SNFIs Cluster to select 2500 most vulnerable households among IDPs, returnees and host community in Twic, Cueibet and Abyei Administrative Area who will benefit through in-kind distribution and Cash Voucher Assistance. The selection criteria to be applied will include: Individuals classified as IPC 4 and 5, women headed households, and children headed households, elderly persons, severely sick persons, pregnant and lactating women, care givers of orphans, separated and unaccompanied children. ACTIVITY 4: Collection of SNFIs from Core Pipeline and distribution of the items: ACTIVITY 5: SNFIs distribution: TGCDA project staff will supervise setting up of distribution venues two days prior to distribution exercise. Each distribution point will be accessible by elderly persons, those living with disability and considered safe for women and girls. The venues will have gender disaggregated latrines, water points and safe spaces where pregnant and lactating mothers can be able to rest, feed their children. Public announcement will be made using public address systems in all distribution locations. On the day of distribution, enumerators will conduct demonstration on appropriate use of the SNFIs prior to handing over the items to beneficiaries. All distributions will be conducted during light hours to allow beneficiaries sufficient time to walk to distribution centres and return home during daytime. ACTIVITY 5: Conduct Post Distribution Monitoring: The PDM will be carried out using S/NFI’s cluster PDM household questionnaires which contains the question that assesses the appropriateness as well as beneficiary satisfaction with amount & quality of items distributed registration and verification processes. Respondents will be selected randomly among the beneficiary HHs. A sample size of 20% of the HHs who will benefit directly through the intervention will be employed. ACTIVITY 6: Conduct HLP analysis and awareness: Due diligence will be made on land tenure system to ascertain land ownership, proof of ownership and potential and underlying land and property concerns at the community level and IDPs sites. Training on HLP access issues and HLP dispute Resolutions will be ensured to mitigate HLP cases. ACTIVITY 7: Conduct rapid market assessments and market monitoring in Abyei, Cueibet and Twic to obtain information on market capacity and operational conditions for CVA. ACTIVITY 8: Identify, register and verify 900 CVA beneficiaries as follows: Abyei: 300HHs, Twic 300hhS and Cueibet 300HHs. ACTIVITY 9: Hold community meetings with women and men beneficiaries in order to establish the type of voucher assistance that is preferred and appropriate. ACTIVITY 10: Select vendors using established selection criteria and with the participation of the community members. ACTIVITY 11: Create and distribute CVA vouchers to beneficiaries for redemption. ACTIVITY 12: Monitor Voucher Redemption. ACTIVITY 13: Conduct quality assurance of redemption and PDM with voucher recipients and vendors.
