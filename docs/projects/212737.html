Project Summary

The project aims to provide lifesaving essential integrated health, nutrition, and child
protection services, covering the different communities in Aleppo Governorate, including
Qabasin and Al Bab communities in Al Bab subdistrict, Shmarekh community in Raju
subdistrict- A'zaz district, and Meidan Ekbis community in Afrin subdistrict.
The project duration will be 12 months, covering the mentioned locations throughout
2024, with CMAM, IYCF, TSFP, in addition to awareness messages about handwashing
practices, food processing, COVID-19 and cholera signs, symptoms, means of infection,
and IPC measures.
Total project targets include 79200 CU5 and PLW, in addition to the health workers who will benefit from the training. 50000 U5 children, among them, there are 5,876 children
with a disability, in addition to 29200 PLW’s, including 1,638 with a disability, as those
groups are the most affected by the poor nutrition situation across Syria.
Projects activities, locations, targets, and budget are fully coordinated with the nutrition
cluster, to ensure optimal planning, in addition, full coordination will be maintained
during the implementation, to ensure the optimal coverage of the need, without any
duplication or overlapping, and the optimal utilization of the available nutrition supplies
and other resources.
Project activities will be implemented using two different modalities:
RRT modality: 8 RRT’s will be deployed, covering the following communities: Qabasin, Al
Bab, Shmarekh, and Meidan Ekbis. Provided services include screening for malnutrition
and referral, SAM and MAM treatment, one-to-one IYCF-E counseling, IYCF-E messages,
micronutrient supplementation, FMA, screening for external visual signs of corporal
punishment and suspected abuse and violence cases and referral, screening for danger
health signs and referral to healthcare centers, in addition to WaSH messages on
handwashing practices, food processing, and dissemination of information on COVID-19
and cholera signs, symptoms, means of infection, and IPC measures.
Facility-based nutrition services: four nutritionists will be based on four health facilities
supported by SEMA, to ensure the integration between health and nutrition services,
provided services include early identification and treatment of acutely malnourished boys
and girls under five years and pregnant and lactating women (PLW), infant and young
child feeding services, micro-nutrient supplementation. The center will also receive all the
referred cases that need either nutrition or primary health care services from RRT teams
or any other partner in the area. The MAMI program involves training healthcare workers, nutritionists, and mothers to prevent malnutrition. Facilities providing MAMI services offer check-ups, counseling, and therapeutic feeding. Community engagement through awareness campaigns and involvement of local networks is also a crucial aspect. The program targets both mothers and children, providing education, access to services, and ongoing support within the community.
**Implementation plan: The goal of this project is to significantly improve access to high-quality preventive and curative nutrition services for all vulnerable individuals in the implementation area. SEMA will maintain a highly efficient coordination with the nutrition cluster team and all nutrition partners in the area to ensure maximum coverage without any duplication or overlapping of services. The referral process between nutrition partners will be organized, and all project activities will be reported to the nutrition cluster on a monthly basis through the 4W mechanism. We will conduct rigorous monitoring at every stage of the program using appropriate data collection tools tailored to the program's needs.
