Project Summary

Common Operational Datasets or CODs, are authoritative reference datasets needed operations and decision-making for all actors in Libya humanitarian response. CODs are 'best available' datasets that ensure consistency and simplify the discovery and exchange of key data. In Libya and since 2017, UNFPA has been providing UN-OCHA with COD-PS based on best available population data extending technical support to the Bureau of Statistics and Census. Over the last three years, the quality of Libya COD-PS has improved although no recent census has been conducted since 2006.
In 2019, the Libyan government has authorized the Bureau of Statistics and Census to conduct a national demographic survey however due to budgetary cuts (following oil export blockade) and COVID-19 movement restrictions, the roll out of the survey has been postponed to 2021. Upon request from the Libyan authorities, UNFPA is providing technical assistance to ensure data is produced with full disaggregation and in accordance with the Fundamental Principles of Oﬃcial Statistics.
The demographic survey will not only update the COD-PS for Libya but will also help bridging the gap between humanitarian and development data and facilitate the multiyear humanitarian planning involving local and national authorities.
The data will be fully disaggregated and geo-spatially linked using a coordinate system (especially administrative boundaries) and have unique geographic identiﬁcation codes (P-codes).

1 - Needs

Despite the peace truce concluded in Libya Since July 2020, the humanitarian crisis in Libya is largely deﬁned by an environment of deepening vulnerability for the population at large, with a rapidly deteriorating economy and public sector and recurrent pockets of acute needs prompted by outbreaks of violence, shifts in territorial control of the local power of armed groups. This complex context is rooted in a protection, basic services, economic and governance crisis that has engulfed the country since 2011. The ability of humanitarian community to assess, analyze and response to the needs of the affected population is further hampered by the lack of reliable quantitative and qualitative data that is recognized by local, national and international agencies. In absence of reliable information produced by Libyan institutions, humanitarian responders have made use of diverse sources of data to identify priority needs, and understand coping strategies to plan their interventions. On another hand, documenting protection vulnerabilities especially human rights violations – such as gender-based violence – has proved to be challenging for Libyan stakeholders. The needs of some vulnerable people including women, boys, girls and people living with disabilities needs to be reﬂected through representative sampling and protection-sensitive data collection processes. In order to ensure accountability of national and local stakeholders, stronger leadership and improved coordination, there is a need to ensure that humanitarian action is based on reliable and mutually agreed-upon sources of data and information. There are clearly limitations to the nationally-produced data (especially when it comes to protection related information, mobility tracking and migrants and refugees’ data). Hence, it's crucial to complement the oﬃcial data with qualitative and vulnerability-based speciﬁc humanitarian data for a more comprehensive understanding of people needs and Libyan context.Due to the lack of age and sex disaggregated data, humanitarian agencies tend to assume that all age and sex groups have the same needs and are exposed to the same vulnerabilities. Actually some qualitative researches have showed that women and girls, people with disabilities and some age groups such as elderly people, women of reproductive age and children face additional barriers to access basic services. Having age and sex disaggregated population data at admin level 3 level will allow to identify pocket of needs and vulnerabilities and bridge the gap between humanitarian and development response mechanisms. On another hand, and for the same accountability issue, it’s crucial to harmonize the sources and baselines of data using the same operational datasets for both population and geographic data.

2 - Activities

The project aims at providing technical assistance to the national statistical office through deployment of technical population and data experts to Libya (national and international) to strengthen BSC capacities to design and produce quality population data according to international standards and in collaboration with the international community. This include technical assistance during the design of the demographic survey, data collection and when compiling the COD-PS. The project will allow as well to support national coordination between different national data agencies in Libya (i.e. National Information Authority, Bureau of Statistics and Census, Urban Planning Authority, and statistical units in technical line ministries. UNFPA will call for meetings and help harmonize the internationally produced datasets with Libya-owned datasets working with the members of the Information Management Working Group and the assessment working group (coordinated by OCHA).Libyan authorities have agreed to finance the operation and some of the technical costs of the demographic survey while UNFPA support will focus on the technical design and quality assurance of the survey. The COD-PS will be extracted from the demographic survey using non-identifying raw data and in accordance with the international standards for official statistics.The demographic survey is aiming at providing a fully age, sex and disability disaggregated dataset up to the ADMIN level 3 (municipal level). This would allow all data user (humanitarian and development agencies) to have a common shared dataset to plan their intervention in a harmonized way.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

4.4 - Project results in Increased exposure to COVID-19 transmissionEnumerators might be exposed to or spread COVID-19 during data collection. UNFPA will work with the BSC to educate all enumerators about COVID-19 barrier measures. UNFPA will ensure as well that BSC enumerators have access to PPE during the field data collection phase. Duration of interviews will be reduced through use of tablets. 4.6 - Disclosure of sensitive personal data of affected persons, including safety and confidentiality of case management conducted in detention centers, leading to increased protection risks and further harmUNFPA and the BSC will develop a methodology to ensure that questionnaires are anonymous and raw data is not identifying. UNFPA and the BSC will agree with community leaders on mechanisms to ensure that non-Libyans and some ethnic groups are not identifiable through the questionnaire and the raw datasets. While use of tablets will improve the geo-referencing of data, the legal status and the nationality of people interviewed will be manipulated as highly sensitive and confidential data. 4.15 - Barriers for beneficiaries to report sexual exploitation and/or abuse in a safe and confidential mannerAs per UNFPA procedures, all staff working for UNFPA partners need to be trained on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (using an online platform). UNFPA and the BSC agreed to have mixed teams of enumerators (males and females) and show zero tolerance policy to harassment and GBV.As a second line for prevention of SEA, UNFPA will rely on CFM to allow people report on any abuse or misbehavior during the data collection. 4.16 - Forced eviction of persons targeted by the projectBased on recent protection reports, there is a risk that some population groups could be evicted based on their legal status, origin or ethnicity. UNFPA will work with the BSC to ensure that data produced doesn’t allow for identification of people based on their legal status, origin, ethnicity, sex, religion or nationality. The final results of the survey will be discussed with the protection sector.

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

The project will rely on the CFM (based on the SOPs validated with WFP and the ETS) as a second accountability mechanism. On another, UNFPA will ensure that the quality assurance section of the survey includes procedures to monitor and hold accountable enumerators during data collection, and follow on any misbehavior, gap or fraudulent action. As per UNFPA procedures, all staff working for UNFPA partners need to be trained on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (using an online platform). UNFPA and the BSC agreed to have teams of enumerators are mixed (males and females) and show zero tolerance policy to harassment and GBV.The project is building on UNFPA experience in conducting population surveys in Libya and producing COD-PS. During the design of the methodology, UNFPA will advocate and ensure inclusion of different population groups in the demographic survey including non-Libyans, people living in remote and conflict-affected areas and people living with disabilities. Whenever a population is not accessible, UNFPA will rely on acceptable alternative population estimation tools to ensure that everyone in Libya is counted and accounted for. UNFPA will support the BSC in adopting various actions to ensure that women, children people with disabilities and non-Libyans are accounted and enumerated such as recruitment of women, non-Libyans and people who can communicate with PwD. Experienced field coordinators will be deployed to all muhallas to facilitate access and communication with the targeted communities.
