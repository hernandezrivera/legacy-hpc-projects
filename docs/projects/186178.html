Project Summary

The project aim at enhancing restoration of livelihood amongst IDPs and affected population through agricultural, livestock and fishery support (small ruminant, local poultry, table birds, piggery, fishery and agriculture) in Mezam, Bui, Boyo, and Ngoketunjia divisions in the NW/SW region of Cameroon. The project approach include; capacity building and support with start-up materials. The project will also provide unconditional food, cash transfers to IDPs and local vulnerable population. The project primary target includes; 2000 household leaders and youths in the 5 targeted divisions in the northwest region. This project will increase community impact and built on the successes of our just completed early recovery project with UNDP, while ensuring respect for covid-19 preventive measures.
The project is going to ensure gender mainstreaming in it design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. We are going to carry out a gender analysis and collect age and sex disaggregate data. This will help us determine the food security-related needs, capacities and aspirations of women, girls, men and boys in the affected population. This will help us identify groups with the greatest food security support needs, disaggregated by sex and age.
In order to ensure accountability to affected population, the project is going to create community complaint and feedback mechanisms committees in each targeted community. This will help us learn from beneficiaries, communities and other stakeholders and be responsive to their views, opinions and complaints, so improving the quality and impact of our work
Upon completion, the project will increase productivity of small scale vulnerable farmers as a result of capacity building and support with farm inputs. The project is also expected to enhanced social cohesion between IDPs and their host communities, and restores livelihoods of 2000 vulnerable women and youths affected by the humanitarian crisis through capacity building and support with start-up materials. The project location is Mezam, Bui, Boyo, Menchum and Ngoketunjia divisions in the NW/SW region of Cameroon. The project target is 2000 households

1 - Besoins / Needs

In the North-West and South-West regions, the socio-political crisis, now entering its fifth year, has led to massive population displacements. As of August 2020, the North-West and South-West crisis has displaced over 1.1 million people, an increase of over almost 200,000 people in comparison to December 2019, when 930,000 were displaced. These displacements were accentuated by the violence that preceded the legislative, municipal and regional elections held in February 2020. As the crisis deepens, more people are leaving the insecure rural and bush areas and are moving to urban areas in the two regions, elsewhere in the country or to Nigeria. Meanwhile, a remarkable increase of returnees to the North-West and Southwest regions is observed: while 204,000 returnees were reported in August 2019, 361,000 returnees were reported in August 2020. These returnees are typically those who displace into the bush after localized conflict and return soon after, constituting a regular pendular migration pattern. Based on the figures from the MSNAs conducted in August and September 2020 (see caveat on page 2), the number of IDPs from the North-West and Southwest in the Littoral, West and Centre regions has also increased: from 224,000 in 2019 to 297,000 in 2020. A majority of the displaced are women and children amongst whom are unaccompanied and separated children. Conflict remains one of the main drivers of food insecurity. The disruption of markets and food and nutrition services due to violence and the COVID-19 epidemic impaired the quality of diets and nutrition practices. According to the Cadre Harmonisé of October 2020, almost 2.3 million people are projected to be in food insecurity phase 3 and 4 from June to August 2021, resorting to crisis or emergency coping strategies to secure household level food security. Displacement has caused a loss of resources for food self-reliance. It primarily affects the access to land to cultivate, the loss of livestock and of the families’ productive assets. Reduced access to food is a major cause for negative coping mechanisms such as child marriage and survival sex. Intimate partner violence is widely reported to service provision points, 11 tensions in the houses due to reduced access to resources as well as restrictions of movements could be among the aggravating factors exposing women to increased GBV at home.
