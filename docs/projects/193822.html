Project Summary

This proposed intervention is contributing to the Protection Cluster Objective 2: Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation that is related to strategic objective 1 “number” of people in need receiving protection prevention and mitigation services (Child Protection, MHPSS, legal aid). The general objective of this intervention by Médecins du Monde France (MdM-F), is to reinforce the resilience of Palestinian children, caregivers and adults living under occupation, exposed to critical incidents and impacted by the increase of occupation-related violence and ensure access to Mental Health and Psychosocial Support services (MHPSS) for those in need while fighting against the stigma related to mental health. In the West Bank (WB), the intervention will target individuals in hotspot areas and vulnerable communities impacted by the occupation coercive environment, including demolition and forcible transfer practices, Israeli settlers’ violence and excessive use of force by the Israeli Security Forces (ISF) in Jenin, Tulkarem, Nablus, Salfit, Tubas and the Northern Jordan Valley (NJV), Qalqilya and Ramallah Governorates. In Gaza, the intervention will be implemented in Khan Younis and Rafah Governorates.
The following outcome is expected “The vulnerable Palestinian communities affected by occupation-related violence in the WB and Gaza Strip have increased resilience capacity”. This outcome will have 3 outputs:
1. Communities are better equipped and have increased resilience capacity to cope with critical incidents resulting from the Israeli occupation.
2. The MHPSS response capacities of community key actors are strengthened through preparedness and supervision
3. Advocacy initiatives to enhance the accountability for violations of Human Right Law (HRL) and International Humanitarian Law (IHL) and the engagement of Third States Parties.
This outcome aims at providing means for the resilience building of Palestinian affected by the repetitive critical incidents and violent escalations related to the Israeli occupation in Gaza and WB, providing awareness and psychoeducation sessions, and resilience-based peer support groups.
Awareness sessions will focus on MH stigma, increase the public knowledge about common MH issues, myths, facts and misconceptions about MH, the meaning of psychological well-being, family supports during and after crisis, behavioral problems of children and how to deal with them. Psychoeducation will focus on critical incidents, their impacts on MH, addressing positive coping mechanisms, targeting the most vulnerable and at-risk groups. MdM-F will also facilitate peer support groups with children, caregivers and adults in hotspots communities in the WB that are highly exposed to occupation related violence. In addition, MdM-F will capacitate key community actors to deliver a first MHPSS response following critical incidents. In the southern part of the Gaza Strip, MdM-F will reinforce the capacity of its local partner Cultural and Free Through Association (CFTA) to respond to the MHPSS needs in Khan Younis and Rafah governorates. This will be reflected through the strengthening of CFTA human resources capacities to provide structured MHPSS services, conduct MHPSS awareness at community level and improve referral pathways. Coordination and reporting with other humanitarian actors, including NGOs (local and international), humanitarian coordination bodies, ministries, local authorities, key members within communities, as well as MdM-F ongoing projects will be ensured at community and national level.
The advocacy contribution of MdM-F will be formalized through field briefings to diplomats and international media, production of periodic reports and factsheets to reflect the impact of the violations on MH of the Palestinian communities, in addition to a lobby tour or virtual meetings with targeted diplomats in Paris and Brussels.

1 - Needs

The needs assessment is drawn from MdM-F data collection, and MdM-F field experience, both gathered through MdM-F current MHPSS outreach intervention in the North (N) WB. In addition, MdM-F conducted a comprehensive medical assessment between May and August 2022 in the Gaza Strip and NWB, including a section on MHPSS. MdM-F conducted focus group discussions (FGDs), disaggregated by gender and age, and interviews with key informants (KIIs). Quantifying the tangible physical effects over decades of occupation on the Palestinian population is a relatively straightforward process. Year on year, conditions for Palestinians living across the occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) continue to deteriorate. What is harder to quantify is the MHPSS impact of the long, sustained, and expanding level of coercion enabled by policies and practices led by successive Israeli governments, Civil Administration (ICA) and ISF, and supported by settler violence. Through its frontline work of providing MHPSS ER to victims of critical incidents related to occupation since 2012, MdM has witnessed the impact of the coercive environment in fostering a prolonged environment of fear and intimidation. MdM identified a clear increase in the number of critical incidents within the first 9 months of 2022 compared to the same period of 2021. In 2022, MdM intervened in 463 incidents that required MHPSS ER (269 Settlers related, 62 ISF related, 132 ICA related), compared to 371 incidents that required MHPSS ER over the same period in 2021 (179 Settlers related, 97 ISF related, 95 ICA related) within the same areas of intervention. There is an overwhelming feeling of powerlessness within these communities. A study on Palestinian MH under occupation conducted by MdM-F and AIDA in 2022 (“No Peace Of Mind” report) revealed that close to 60% of respondents targeted by the study indicated that they felt there was nothing they could do to protect their families. Moreover, nearly one quarter of respondents (23%) said they had no hope for the future or that they did not consider thinking about their future. KIIs held during MdM-F recent assessment also noted that depression was amongst the most common health issues that communities are facing, meanwhile noting a clear gap in MHPSS services. As recorded by MdM-F in NWB and by CFTA in Gaza during field visits, the impacts that occupation-related incidents have on the MH of men, women, boys and girls range from strong feelings of frustration, constant feelings of fear and insecurity, eating disorders, sleep disturbances, anxiety, depression and behavioral problems. MdM and AIDA study points out that sadness and bad mood was mentioned by 75% of children and 40% of men and women, as an emotional change resulting from the occupation. 50% of men indicated they experienced stomach pain in addition to headaches. Anxiety, fear and despair were also overwhelming feelings more than a 33% of men experience. Amongst other substantial impacts highlighted by women were anxiety (60% of women interviewed) and shortness of breath. Dialogue with the communities shows that parents and caregivers struggle to ensure the physical and emotional security of their children when they themselves feel stressed and anxious about the situation. In addition, field observations suggested that caregivers are not necessarily familiar with the potential trauma-related symptoms that a child can manifest, nor are they aware of the actions that can be undertaken to relieve these symptoms. Moreover, MdM faced reluctance of some people to receive MHPSS support as shame and stigma are associated with speaking of their feelings and their mental wellbeing. This behavior was more prominent in men as Palestinian society expects them to play the gendered and traditional role of protection. As such, the norm is that they cannot share their feelings as it can be perceived as a sign of weakness both as men and in resistance to oppression. All the above-mentioned findings also show a lack in knowledge on MH and emphasize the importance on deconstructing myths and persistent stigma associated with MH to increase the demand for this service and eventually have people better equipped to face potentially traumatic events. A recent study conducted by Save the Children International in June 2022 found that, since 2018, the psychosocial well-being of children, young people, and their caregivers in Gaza has declined to alarming levels. The health needs Overview (HNO) 2022 for oPt reported that prior to the escalation, suicide rates quadrupled, especially among adolescent girls. The report also stated that 82% of adolescents in Gaza reported an overall poor - very poor level of mental wellbeing. Gender Based Violence (GBV) survivors have needs related to psychosocial support, which are not being met and 28% of households reported lack of awareness of, and access to, MHPSS services in case of GBV. A recent UNRWA Health Department Report showed that healthcare service recipients in Gaza had the highest MH needs detection rates (30.6%) amongst all other UNRWA areas of operation. Existing services are unable to meet the above-mentioned increasing needs.

2 - Activities

OUTPUT 1: COMMUNITIES ARE BETTER EQUIPPED AND HAVE AN INCREASED RESILIENCE CAPACITY TO COPE WITH CRITICAL INCIDENTS RESULTING FROM THE ISRAELI OCCUPATION. MdM will target communities to ensure access to MHPSS, with focus on vulnerable groups like children, people with disabilities (PwDs) and GBV survivors. Psychoeducation and Peer Support Groups will be delivered to the community including children, strengthening the sensibility of caregivers on symptoms children might manifest and the way of dealing with them. Activities will also be a channel through which MdM will identify caregivers who need additional MH support. Sessions will be delivered at community level, and when possible, in schools to facilitate children and teenagers’ participation. To ensure safe referral, field staff will be trained within the first month of the intervention. Through WBPC, MdM participates in the drafting of SOPs, which included guidelines on referral of children. MdM will ensure PwD’s participation by coordinating with the community to identify PwDs needing MHPSS ensuring PwDs are aware of the services available according to their disability and will oversee the referral to the appropriate partner. Provision of Psychoeducation To increase the resilience of people living in hotspots areas of the WB and at risk of occupation-related violence, psychoeducation will be provided, adapted to the age, gender, and vulnerability. Social workers will (i) Provide appropriate information about MH symptoms people may experience after an incident, targeting adolescents up to elderly people; (ii) name and normalize the person’s responses and concerns to the event, their reaction and help reducing their worry about the symptoms; (iii) support beneficiaries to use positive coping mechanisms and reduce the negative (iv) encourage to seek support when needed and break the stigma around MH and MHPSS and support referral pathways. Information, education, and communication (IEC) materials adapted to the age and gender will be shared during the sessions. Peer Support Groups (PSGs) PSG is based on the idea that others who are in similar situations, or have similar challenges are best able to understand and provide support. PSGs aim at building resilience through emotional support, experience sharing, improve coping mechanisms of people facing continuous violence and strengthen social cohesion. People who benefit the most from the PSG are 1) People affected by critical incidents; 2) People involved in critical incidents referred by other partners; 3) People at risk “Vulnerable people”: proximity with settlements, military installments, demonstration places, absence of social support network. 6 to 15 people will be supported through 6 to 10 sessions on a weekly basis. Each session will be tailored based on gender and age: positive parenting topics will be covered with caregivers, while topics related to the relationship between adolescents and their parents will be covered with adolescents. Structured MHPSS interventions at the community level: MdM-F will capacitate CFTA to provide community-based activities to strengthen psychosocial resilience while improving access to MHPSS services. This includes 1) Awareness and psychoeducation to increase awareness about MH topics and symptoms. Sessions will address the needs of vulnerable groups like GBV, CP cases and PwDs. 2) Screening, assessing, managing, and referring MHPSS needs. 3) Individual and family consultations. 4) Detection, first management, and referral of GBV, CP and PwDs cases. Through protection mainstreaming at community-level activities, safe, protective, and confidential services will ensure that possible GBV, CP and the needs of PwDs are identified and managed adequately 5) Community-level ER, including PFA and low-intensity trauma recovery intervention. The capacitation process for local MHPSS teams will ensure enhancing their knowledge, skills and preparedness to respond to MHPSS needs. It is important to mention that this intervention will be an expansion of the targeted population, of already ongoing intervention jointly with NRC, Mercy Corps and HI under the Gaza protection consortium. OUTPUT 2: THE MHPSS RESPONSE CAPACITIES OF COMMUNITY KEY ACTORS ARE STRENGTHENED THROUGH PREPARDNESS AND SUPERVISION Capacity building of the local community and CBOs on MHPSS first response MdM-F will map MH reliable community actors, such as CBOs, village councils and community leaders to provide capacitation on the delivery of MHPSS and PFA. Training volunteers (selected based on capacity to communicate, basic education requirements and willingness to support the community) and who are seen as a focal point in the community will support the community to be the first responder and being deployed after an event to provide PFA. Strengthen capacities of the local partners (CFTA) to detect, manage and refer the most vulnerable individuals in need of MHPSS support, with a specific focus on children MdM-F will provide training to the local partner to ensure the quality of services. These training include topics as PSGs, structured psychoeducation, MHPSS awareness and sensitization. The capacitation will be sustained through bi-weekly supervision to ensure the adequacy and quality of the services all along the project. OUTPUT 3: ADVOCACY INITIATIVES ENHANCE THE ACCOUNTABILITY FOR VIOLATIONS OF HRL & IHL AND THE ENGAGEMENT OF THIRD STATES PARTIES MdM collects quantitative and qualitative information to raise awareness of public opinion and mobilize decision makers to effectively act according to HRL and IHL standards. The activities include: (1) Production documentation, reports and testimonies about the impact of critical events on MHPSS wellbeing (2) Contribution to joint reports (Protection Cluster, MHPSS AoR, AIDA, UN mechanisms) (3) Organization of ﬁeld tours and contributions to decision-makers brieﬁngs (4) Lobby tour in Europe

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

All the implemented activities rely on the modality of assistance “service delivery”. MdM will reinforce the local capacities to respond to MHPSS emergencies and will directly provide MHPSS emergency response directly by MdM staff in WB and through a local partner in Gaza. Based on more than 11 years of field experience in the NWB and Gaza, this has been identified as the best modality to ensure the access of victims or potential victims of critical incidents to MHPSS ER services and reinforcing the local community coping mechanisms to better cope and protect themselves from critical incidents through psychoeducation and PSGs implementation, taking into consideration the different access barriers (stigma, lack of public transportation, financial affordability of transportation cost and closures, check points, areas close to settlements and scattered distribution of communities in area C). Through the mentioned modality of assistance, the impact of critical incidents in WB and waves of violence in Gaza on the communities will be mitigated and the community resilience and positive coping mechanisms to deal with critical incidents will be reinforced.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

MdM accountability system is based on sharing information in a timely manner with the different actors involved in the project as well as regular reporting to the Health Cluster. For this proposed project, the presence of MdM teams in the field makes this flow of information continuous and bidirectional, establishing a system that allows the participation of the PHC staff at MoH village councils and targeted community members in the definition and adaptation of MdM activities. In the same way, regular communication is kept with the International Cooperation Department (ICD) and PHC and Mental Health Directorates of the MoH to ensure full information sharing, participation, and feedback on MdM interventions. When feedback or complaints are received through any of the mechanisms in place, an immediate reply will be provided to the user with a reference number so the user can follow-up and track the process. The complaint will be processed first by the focal point who will categorize the feedback into 2 main categories: regular or sensitive feedback/complaint. Regular complaint is a non-sensitive complaint which relates to programmatic issues. A sensitive complaint includes issues related to abuse/inappropriate behavior with beneficiaries involving verbal, sexual abuse (PSEA), physical assault, or discrimination. Based on the details of the complaint, MdM will take up the feedback internally or with the partner, depending on the circumstances of the complaints. The committee will gather information and conduct hearings and visits. Users who provide feedback will be continuously updated about the process through email or phone, and final decisions and actions will be officially communicated through official mechanisms. All complaints will be registered anonymously by the focal point in a protected database and analysis will be done regularly. Feedback will be used to improve service delivery, re-orient approaches, identify training needs.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

MdM is currently using its Feedback and Complaint Mechanism (FCM) with all its activities beneficiaries, community leaders, stakeholders and suppliers, all of which are updated and reaching MdM through the distributed FCM card. The FCM cards are leaflets which highlights that feedback provided by any service user, whether positive or negative, will all be taken into consideration and addressed by MdM independently from the project team and be dealt with respect for the principles of confidentiality and privacy, without affecting the possibility to receive services in the future. The FCM cards are age-appropriate and contain safeguarding messages, tailored to adults and children. It indicates the different feedback modalities including a specific hotline and WhatsApp number to receive calls or text messages, a specific email address to receive online feedback, the cards clarify that feedback could be also received through verbal feedback to the field team or by visiting MdM office. The cards specified the working day and hours to submit feedback. MdM teams distribute the FCM cards and explain it to the beneficiaries targeting all the individuals who benefited from all the above-mentioned activities including the local partners activities, village councils and community leaders with whom those activities were coordinated. The different modalities used, enabled the access of persons with vulnerabilities and access barriers as children, women, PwDs , elderly and LGBTQ to provide feedback to MdM when needed. Following the provision of Peer support group sessions, satisfaction and feedback questions are usually implemented at the end of the session.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The MdM team, providing MHPSS to potential victims and victims of occupation-related incidents as well as vulnerable community members, is a well-trained, supervised and gender-balanced team, who is updated about the cultural, religious, context and security situation in MdM operational area of coverage including towns, villages, and Bedouin communities. In Gaza, through its local partner CFTA , MdM will reach the beneficiaries directly in the premises of the local partner and directly in the community through its outreach team. In WB, MdM interventions are provided directly in the community (in their houses or village council, Bedouin communities) to reduce the exposure of the victims to harm and provide equal access, including to PwDs. MdM security department and team are continuously assessing the locations of the activities conducted in the field to ensure safety and security of beneficiaries, partners staff and MdM team. MdM team is coordinating the activities with the village councils and community leaders and cooperates with stakeholders to avoid community tension. Confidentiality and privacy of MHPSS interventions is a crucial principle applied by all MdM team. When conducting interviews, data collection and referring beneficiaries to other services, MdM follows a clear referral protocol developed by MdM and Ministry of Health (MoH) in 2015. MdM is always ensuring that individual sessions are conducted in privacy, including from same family members. MdM MHPSS team is programing the interventions based on gender needs: Beneficiaries will have the choice to be supported by the person they feel the most comfortable with among MdM gender balanced team. During the first contact over the phone, the social worker highlights the gender balance characteristic of the team and provides the beneficiaries the opportunity of indicating with whom they feel the most comfortable with, from a gender perspective. During the visit in person, the beneficiaries is again asked if he/she feels comfortable with the social worker. The same choice is also offered to children during the field visit. Moreover, they are reaching community members in appropriate time, intervening with women within the morning hours, with children after the school time and with men after working hours. Within its case studies and advocacy documentation, MdM protects the identity of victims by not using identifiable information to avoid the identification of cases. Written consent is received from the beneficiaries for use of testimonies in published materials (even when remaining anonymous). Within its awareness raising activities, psychoeducation activities, MdM is reinforcing the local community’s positive coping mechanisms and mobilizing the community resources towards community protection. MdM is counting on functional feedback and complaint mechanisms, beneficiary satisfaction survey and is working closely with beneficiaries and community representatives involving them in the design, implementation, and monitoring of the action. MdM team shares the protection concerns of the individual and communities targeted within its interventions with the relevant protection actors, and in close coordination and cooperation with Protection cluster. GBV and CP are mainstreamed within MdM outreach activities. In addition, MdM is ensuring all its staff are sensitized and oriented to our policy on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), sexual harassment, moral harassment, and abuse of authority.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Impartiality, non-discrimination, gender, and inclusion of persons with disabilities are adopted by MdM. All MdM MHPSS activities are implemented through an outreach approach reaching the community members in their households, or another location of their choice, to ease access to the service (workplace for example). Beneficiaries will have the choice to be supported by the person they feel the most comfortable with among MdM gender balanced team. During the first contact over the phone, the social worker highlights the gender balance characteristic of the team and provides the beneficiaries the opportunity of indicating with whom they feel the most comfortable with, from a gender perspective. During the visit in person, the beneficiaries is again asked if he/she feels comfortable with the social worker. The same choice is also offered to children during the field visit, respecting the culture and traditions of the local community and visiting them in proper timing, such as visiting the mothers in the morning hours and fathers after working hours. Using the outreach approach, MdM is ensuring the access of all community members including women, elderly and PwDs to the MHPSS services and reduce the risk and community security concerns while moving to obtain the services in other locations. Within its psychoeducation activities, MdM is conducting separate sessions for men and women. The content of the session is adapted based on the age and gender of the targeted community members visited. MdM reinforced the capacity and supervision of MdM field staff on gender, GBV and inclusion of PwDs, same approach is used with MdM local partners. A specific SOP was developed to address the needs of GBV and PwDs cases identified and ensure proper referral, follow up and documentation of these cases. Moreover, identification of CP, neglect and abuse was reinforced within this intervention, and a refresher training was conducted in 2022. A clear referral system for CP is established with the Child Protection Networks and other NGOs under the umbrella of the CP/MHPSS Area of Responsibility (AoR). MdM is addressing the needs of the local community with a gender-based approach (gender specific health), for instance, developing different IEC for women, men and children, developing different protocols based upon gender and addressing all the barriers preventing PwDs to access services. Through its outreach program, MdM and its local partners can reach most of the victims affected by the critical events, with the support provided by a well-prepared logistic department. MdM team can reach the scattered Bedouin communities, farmers and shepherds in their communities and place of work. To ensure basic and protection needs, MdM is using several referral approaches through the Protection Cluster and its AoRs, by liaising with the local authorities or by mobilizing the community resources in cooperation with CBOs and village councils, ensuring access of community members in need for such services. By having an outreach program, MdM MEAL process takes into consideration the monitoring and documentation of needs per age, gender, vulnerabilities and targeted locations. All beneficiaries referred to any external service are documented and tracked accordingly.
