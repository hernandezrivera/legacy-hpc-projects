Project Summary

In response to the COVID-19 pandemic, DRC proposes a 9 months integrated Site Management (SM) and Site Development (SD) project with the aim of reducing the spread of the virus in the targeted camps. DRC’s proposed project will build on the existing SM activities in camps 8E, 8W, 11 and 12 as well as SD activities in camps 2E,2W, 5, 6, 8E and 8W. 210,942 individuals residing in the mentioned camps will be targeted by the project.
In SM, DRC will provide burial kits to Imam committees to enable them carry out safe and dignified burials. In coordination with the CiC, DRC’s SM team will continue to provide safe and dignified burials.
Twelve market shading facilities, two per camp, will be constructed by DRC’s SD team to contribute in maintaining social distancing. DRC will construct the shading facilities in line with existing customs and will ensure their are separate sections for women and men.
DRC’s SD team will construct graveyards/burial sites (50 by 10 square feet per camp) individuals will be laid to rest in a dignified manner in the event casualties as a result of the virus are reported in the camps. DRC will support in the maintenance of IOM supported quarantine facilities to ensure they remain operational even during the monsoon season. DRC will assess and include persons from households with elderly members in cash for work programme in order to contribute to the increment of their income especially during the shutdown which has affected many livelihoods in the camps.
Through implementation of the proposed activities, DRC will contribute to access to COVID-19 related services and continuity of humanitarian assistance to all camp residents, through strengthening existing coordination mechanisms and supporting new COVID-19 response processes. DRC will ensure "DO NO HARM" principles are followed in the project and will incorporate the existing complaint response mechanism in the monitoring, evaluation and learning system.

Needs

Since the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic and the reporting of the first case in Bangladesh, 80,971 people (DGHS) are in home and institutional quarantine, with 29,749 being in Dhaka while 13,364 are in other districts. The virus has spread to 60 out of 65 districts with only 1 testing facility being available in Cox’s Bazar. According to the update NGO platform published on 26th April 2020, 5 cases have been reported in Cox’s Bazar with 67 and 722 people currently in institutional and home quarantines respectively. No cases have been confirmed in the Rohingya camps yet with 451 persons (WHO week 16 Epidemiological highlights) being in institutional quarantines. In the camps, where currently over 855,000 Rohingya’s (UNHCR camp population data) reside in 34 overcrowded camps, the risk of the virus spreading is high and could overwhelm the existing systems if proper measures are not put in place early. Despite the governments efforts to minimize the spread by introducing preventive measures such as limiting access to the camps by only allowing essential activities to be carried out; data and trends from other locations with confirmed cases indicate that this measures are not adequate and should be complemented with other WHO recommended measures to increase the chances of controlling the spread of the virus successfully. Lack of adequate market shading facilities and burial sites inhibit social distancing practices as well as burying of people who have passed on as a result of the virus. Many humanitarian actors have been providing services in the camps and all the gains made are now at risk hence urgent COVID-19 response activities are required to ensure the virus do not increase the burden on the Rohingya refugees who are already vulnerable to different shocks.
