Project Summary

The objective of the project is to address the basic needs of most vulnerable communities through the provision of NFI kits, shelter rehabilitation grants and multi-purpose cash grants. Both in-kind and cash modalities will be used in order to ensure a quick and flexible response to immediate needs of IDPs and vulnerable host community members.

4 - Needs

Conflict continues to be the principal driver of humanitarian needs, with the civilian population in many parts of the country, including project locations, exposed to significant protection risks which threaten life, dignity and wellbeing on a daily basis. The humanitarian situation is incredibly complex and constantly evolving, with population movements difficult to predict. Thousands of families in project locations face protracted and multiple displacements. Acute winterization needs remain high for IDPs and families living in damaged houses, requiring large scale timely response to address their immediate needs. IDPs living in open areas, where displaced people are staying without covered shelter for a period of longer than one week, are likely to face a further layer of vulnerability in the absence of adequate shelter and services. Needs are especially acute during the cold winter months, where people’s survival is directly at risk in the absence of an immediate shelter solution and assistance. The number of IDPs residing in IDP sites has increased by 28 per cent from 2018 to 2019 and the number of people in need of shelter assistance has increased by 20 per cent to 5.65 million. Shelter conditions for IDPs remain poor with most households residing in improvised shelters which will need replacement/repair in the next year. Conditions for IDPs living with host communities are no different, most shelters require repair or rehabilitation while services are strained.

5 - Implementation of activities

* Vulnerable IDP households have core and essential needs met through the distribution of NFI kits and cash top up grants. The contents of the kits will be agreed with sector working groups and will follow Sphere standards. The small cash top up will enable households to purchase extra items to supplement kit contents, for example fuel/clothes. The NFI kits will be distributed in camps and informal settlements and will be provided to vulnerable households that fulfill one or more of the following criteria: Recently displaced; Houses hosting IDPs; Large household size with young children; Elderly or female headed households; and Households caring for one or more disabled persons. Caseload: 5000 HHs (30,000 people). * Vulnerable households with limited or no source of income have their supplementary needs met through multi-purpose cash (MPC) grants. The project will target the most vulnerable IDP and host-community households. The value of the grants will be agreed with sector working group and will be in line with SMEB value and cluster recommendations. Where possible, households will be targeted to receive 3 rounds of monthly MPC transfers. Caseload: 450 HHs (2700 people) = 15% of MPC target for 2020.* Vulnerable households living in damaged and substandard accommodation will receive cash grants to enable rehabilitation. In line with working group recommendations, MoUs (between project, local authority, occupant and shelter owner – if applicable) will be signed to ensure beneficiary rights are respected and there is no change in rental conditions for 12 months; additional support in rent-reduction will also be negotiated for tenants conducting repairs on rented homes. Regular follow up of households will be conducted by the project team to assess household progress and provide technical guidance and subsequent tranches of the grant. Particularly vulnerable households may request in-kind contracting support (of similar value). Caseload: 1000 HHs (6000 people).

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members or partner staff members. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High.Mitigation: Project staff will communicate regularly and clearly with the community about what services are available and who services are for. Regular training will be provided to project staff on the Code of Conduct, Child Safeguarding and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). A complaints box will allow staff, partners, or community members to raise complaints confidentially. These will be addressed in a timely and appropriate manner by project or management staff depending on the nature of the complaint. Protection Risk: Increased risk of inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across membersLikelihood: Medium. Impact: HighMitigation: 1. Agree to minimum assistance package across sector; 2. Assess and validate any changes before they occur; 3. Identify and exclude from variance any items particularly prone to create tensions.Protection Risk: Housing Land and Property issues (Eviction of tenants after completion of works, confusion between owner and occupant)Likelihood: Medium. Impact: MediumMitigation: Shelter Rehabilitation interventions will be done in coordination with the local authorities who will be in charge of verifying the ownership of the shelters in which the rehabilitation works are carried out. Through the signature of an MoU (between project, local authority, occupant and shelter owner – if applicable), the responsibilities of all parties will be agreed upon before any rehabilitation work begins. The MoU notes that should the occupant of the shelter be a tenant, the owner commits to allowing the tenant to remain rent free for at least 3 months and in the shelter for at least 12 months with no increase in the rent.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Safety and security risks impact project implementation, causing delays and changes in operational areas, and also threaten the safety and security of project staff and beneficiaries. Assumption: Access to project locations remains safely accessible for suppliers and staff.Mitigation measures: The organization has a designated security focal point who actively monitors the security situation in project locations, sets up security SOPs and procedures, and conducts regular security-related training and briefing for staff. Project management remains flexible and changes project locations and modalities as and when required. Hibernation, relocation and evacuation plans are in place. Should the security and access situation deteriorate, the project will remain flexible and will shift locations to areas where it is safe for staff and beneficiaries. The project will temporarily revert to remote management modality should this be required.
