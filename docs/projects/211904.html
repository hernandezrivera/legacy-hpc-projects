Project Summary

The project aims to respond to 8,400 HH with a total of 44,000 individuals disaggregated roughly as(9,500 men, 10,500 women, 12,000 boys, and 12,000 girls) of the most vulnerable conflict-affected IDPs, and host communities in Idleb and Aleppo governorates, and prioritizing households headed by a female, child, disabled, or elderly, then households with severely ill or disabled members, children below 5, elderly, pregnant or lactating women; with particular attention to those recently displaced or living overcrowded.
The project contributes to providing dignified shelter & NFI assistance through the following activities:
1- provide 6,000 HH with seasonal and supplementary NFIs and assistance, including winter clothing, stoves, and fuel in Al-Bab and Mare' sub districts and camps. Each household will receive cash assistance with a value of 180$ (one-off $180 cash assistance as recommended by cluster and CWG) and prioritize its needs accordingly.
Each beneficiary with a cash voucher can obtain the cash from FSP.
2- Provide and installation of dignified shelters in two IDP sites (Dana, Maaret Tamsrin) in the Idleb governorate. Covering the needs of 400 HHs of IDPs. The activities will include installing 400 newly designed fabricated shelter units (of steel structure and sandwich panel) with associated infrastructure in line with the designed shelter guidance.
3-Conducting site improvement works (Infrastructure in IDP sites) for 2000 HHs in (Dana, Maaret Tamsrin) in Idleb governorate, including construction and maintenance of drainage systems, roads, sewage networks, and installing lighting devices. This project will also contribute towards reinforcing an enabling protection environment and community cohesion by improving housing and related communities through
4- Rehabilitation of 400 of damaged or unfished housing with an average cost of 1500$ per house in (Harim, Salqin, Kafr Takharim, Dana) in Idleb governorate, (Azaz, AlBab, Suran, Daret Azza) in Aleppo governorate.
In all activities, SDI considers the protection of the beneficiaries, so the distribution centers and the shops will be prepared with functional righting equipment, and brochures of safety and security practices will be distributed to beneficiaries. Moreover, a female staff will be recruited and trained to ensure more protection from exploitation and sexual abuse for girls and women and to deal with any GBV cases they may encounter during the project.
Most importantly, SDI pays excellent attention to people with disabilities, elderlies, and people with special needs, and for those, assistance will be delivered to home. HLP rights of shelter sites, beneficiaries, and house owners will be considered throughout all shelter repair interventions. An agreement will be signed to guarantee the rights of the beneficiaries. Coordination with the SNFI cluster and other active members is essential to ensure geographical separation and avoid overlapping/duplication.
Banners were posted at meeting points to raise beneficiaries against COVID-19, complaints mechanism, PEAS…etc. M&E staff will monitor the activities implementation and continually give feedback and comments to the project manager; the surveys will include questions on the teams’ performance, beneficiary satisfaction, and complaints.
The SDI plan for the 2025 budget amounts to approximately the same amount as the 2024 budget. It aims to provide seasonal intervention, establish dignified shelter units, improve the infrastructure in the camps, and rehabilitate damaged homes and unfinished buildings.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Syria faces worsening humanitarian and economic conditions, marked by collapsing basic services and worsened by climatic shocks. The situation has led to a significant rise in people needing assistance. Shelter support is critical, and the population is living in inadequate conditions. Economic challenges, including high construction costs, exacerbate the housing crisis. Security environment, economic downturn, and currency depreciation increase the need for NFI support.SDI will engage with LCs through meetings and FGDs. For rehabilitation, SDI will open a registration office, verify HLP documentation, inspect shelter units for safety, and supervise contractors to ensure quality work. To provide seasonal and supplementary NFIs to 6,000 HHs, SDI will verify beneficiary eligibility through visits and deliver assistance via cash. In site improvement works targeting 2,000 HHs, SDI's engineering team will monitor implementation to ensure compliance with specifications.Tension among host communities and IDPs poses a high likelihood and impact. Mitigation measures include ensuring distribution based on needs, transparent selection criteria, and informed groups.Crowded living conditions, lack of lighting, and gender-segregated sanitation facilities heighten the risk of GBV, with a high likelihood and impact. Mitigation involves advocating for alternative accommodation, gender-segregated mass shelters, adequate sanitation facilities, lighting around shelters, and inclusive consultations.Local authorities' influence on selection criteria can lead to nepotism and exclusion of the most vulnerable, with a high likelihood and medium impact. Mitigation entails standardizing assistance packages, uniform selection criteria, and community engagement.Beneficiaries are vulnerable to sexual misconduct and often hesitant to report incidents due to various barriers, such as lack of knowledge about reporting mechanisms, fear of retaliation, stigma, and illiteracy. SDI's mitigation strategy includes announcing hotline and email contacts through multiple channels. Provide PSEA training and implement CoC for staff and suppliers. Conducting community awareness sessions emphasizing reporting confidentiality. Installing complaint boxes. Utilizing PSEA checklists. Ensuring accessibility for PwDs to complaint mechanisms.A1: Assumes accessible and secure project location. R: Armed conflicts may disrupt access. Likelihood and effect: medium. Mitigation involves security monitoring, alternative route recommendations, and coordination with authoritiesA2: Assumes availability and verification of HLP documents. R: Potential for fake or lacking documentation. Likelihood and effect: medium. Mitigation includes staff training, engaging local authorities, and community verificationA3: Assumes IDP beneficiaries are housed for free. R: Possibility of eviction or rent demands. Likelihood: medium, Effect: high. Mitigation involves signing rent-free agreements and community oversight.
