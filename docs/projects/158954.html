Project Summary

The project will ensure protective environment for children and develop positive coping strategies in the targeted communities through provision of structured, inclusive and integrated child protection mental health and psychosocial support. This will be acheived through the counselling, case management and resilience-building interventions employing the best practices in community based Child Protection (CP) mechanisms for the most vulnerable boys, girls, and adolescents. This project will build on ongoing Child Protection in Emergencies interventions and the capacitated Emergency Response Teams in the West Bank and Gaza, delivering emergency CP interventions through structured PSS and recreational activities and case management and referral pathways services as well as national and international advocacy efforts.

1 - Needs

The situation in the oPt has been characterized as a protection crisis with humanitarian consequences. Palestinian families and communities are exposed to a wide range of severe socio-economic shocks and protection risks associated with the Occupation, defined as a situation of armed conflict, and periods of escalated conflict in Gaza. In the West Bank, the population with the highest vulnerability is found in Area C, East Jerusalem, H2, and the “Seam Zone” (West Bank land located behind the Israeli Barrier and the Armistice Green Line). Children and their families living in the West Bank, in particular in Area C including H2 area of Hebron, East Jerusalem, the Jordan Valley, and refugee camps suffer from ongoing military violence, settler violence, access restrictions and the constant threat of displacement, combined with other associated violence such as continuous harassment and the arrest and detention of children and youth. In West Bank, an estimated 350,000 Palestinians living in close proximity of the settlements are at risk of settler violence, including physical assault and attacks on lands and houses. The occupation therefore severely impacts the civil, political, economic, social and cultural rights of the Palestinian people. Jerusalem Communities mainly Essaweyeh, Silwan and Old city of Jerusalem witnessed waves of escalated conflict violence and home demolitions resulted in high number of displaced children families and children, night raids, invasion and attacks on schools including imposing closure flying checkpoints. A number of schools are facing demolition orders or have already been destroyed, thus reducing the space available to provide education. There is an increase in the number of homes demolition, particularly in East Jerusalem where 111 Palestinian-owned structures were destroyed in the first four months of 2019 alone. In the West Bank and East Jerusalem, children are unable to access education in a safe manner due to a number of reasons. H2 area of Hebron suffering on daily bases from the settlers’ attacks, harassment on checkpoints, attacks on schools and restriction on movements these incidents increased after the termination of TIPH in Hebron H2 area. The ongoing deterioration of security and humanitarian situation in Gaza has left children with a deep sense of insecurity for their future, hopelessness and despair. This deterioration is driving the on-going mass demonstrations at the Israeli-Gaza perimeter fence. Since 30 March 2018, the Gaza Strip has witnessed a significant increase in Palestinian casualties in the context of mass demonstrations taking place along Israel’s perimeter fence with Gaza. Between 30 March 2018 and July 2019, 311 Palestinians, including 60 children, have been killed by Israeli forces in the demonstrations and other circumstances. Over 24,000 Palestinians have been injured, including over 5,800 wounded by live ammunition and including 3,648 children requiring hospitalization.The psycho-social consequences of the ongoing blockade and flare-ups of violence has resulted in an estimated 210,000 people or over 1 in 10 people suffering from severe or moderate mental health disorders. Mental Health Psycho Social Support (MHPSS) needs are widespread with over 47,000 children only in Gaza Strip requiring urgent support. MHPSS assessment “A Decade of Distress” conducted by SC in 2018 in Gaza Strip revealed that 95% of surveyed children reported depression, hyperactivity, preference for being alone, and aggression; 68% reported sleeping difficulties. The CP Rapid Assessment carried out by the CP Working Group (WG) in Gaza Strip in 2018 has found that there has been an increase in psycho-social distress among boys and girls including aggressiveness, bed-wetting, violence against younger ones and unwillingness to go to school. Significant funding shortfalls have also led to Child Protection and MHPSS interventions being closed or scaled down.

2 - Activities

The project will directly support the Protection cluster Strategic objective 2 of putting in place protection mechanisms to prevent and mitigate the effects of occupation and conflict-related violence. SCI secured financial resources to respond to the needs of the children affected by armed conflict the West Bank in 2019 but we will have major Gap in 2020. The response will focus on conflict-related risks interventions by giving children and youth positive alternatives in the “hot spot” areas where there is a high probability of friction and confrontation taking place over the summer break from schools. This will include the activation and expansion of Child Friendly Spaces (CFSs) including summer activities. Children will receive supportive psycho-social first aid (PFA) and referrals when there is a need for further follow up. These activities are targeted the most vulnerable communities, identified through a vulnerability matrix by OCHA, with a total population of more than 150,000 people. These communities are concentrated in east Gaza, Jerusalem and H2 Area of Hebron and were severely impacted by previous wars and escalation of military and settlers’ violence.In the Gaza Strip, the response will reach conflict affected children from ongoing Great March of Return (GMR) events. With the summer break from schools, children are more dispersed than usual and more likely to become engaged with risky activities. Support to the children and areas most affected will be provided, including, positive alternatives through CFS in communities as preventive measures to avoid exposure to risk of GMR activities. Selected children affected from distress or injuries will receive Psycho-social First Aid (PFA) and follow on MHPSS support and referral if needed. Existing CFSs will be complemented by programs, materials and technical support to provide needed support to target children.The project will target a total of 7,000 boys and girls from targeted communities at Gaza and West Bank including Jerusalem needing CP and MHPSS support through a range of activities. Effective case management and psychological first aid (PFA) will be provided through emergency home visits by the existent emergency response teams, and through group and individual counselling. The project will prioritize children who are most vulnerable and exposed to conflict-related violence in the context and children at risk of employing negative coping mechanisms or their families with consequences that directly impact girls and boys.Thirty CFSs each receiving 500 children will be rehabilitated and equipped to ensure accessible, inclusive and protective environments where children can play, socialize and overcome traumatic events. CFSs will provide them with age and gender responsive structured PSS interventions and contextualized child and youth resilience workshops and workshops for parents/caregivers (Child Resilience Manuals). Child-friendly Spaces (CFSs) will serve as hubs for CP and community based psycho-social support interventions (integrated HEART and TeamUp Manuals and Sport and Arts Based low intense PSS Interventions) and identification and referrals of children (girls and boys) with critical child protection risks and concerns. The most at risk boys and girls of child and adolescents age with protection violation cases and concerns (including, violence, exploitation, sexual abuse, GBV, non-accidental physical harm, neglect, attempted suicide, school drop-out and child labor) will be referred through case management and referral pathways to specialized service providers. In addition to children, the project will also target 3,000 parents of affected children will benefit from structured PSS services using field-tested and contextualized modalities. At the same time parents (fathers and mothers) will participate and lead activities with family centers social workers in order to provide them with new knowledge and skills.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

SCI will ensure “Do-no-harm” through our project intervention in terms of mitigation of potential social tensions in communities resulting from community members feeling excluded.Interventions are based on needs assessments and transparent selection criteria. Appropriate targeting of beneficiaries will be the result of quality assessment of design and planning processes, coordinated through regular meetings with the partners and communities during the course of the implementation.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

• To ensure the participation of the targeted people and the equitable access to the services provided, a series of discussion groups and consultation meetings will be conducted at all project stages with girls, boys, women, men, elders and people with disabilities. This will enable communities to raise their concerns and together come up with solutions to ensure that all the interventions do not exacerbate risks or create new risks.
