Project Summary

NRC has adapted its EiE activities during the COVID-19 pandemic, with the following two objectives:
- Providing information on preventive measures and spreads of COVID-19 among learners, teachers and SBMCs
- Mitigation of impact of school closure on learning and learners wellbeing, teachers and other education personnel
NRC will focus on providing child friendly information to learners through local radio stations and the use of posters and pamphlets, in line with guidance from education sector with close collaboration with SUBEB and Ministry of Education. Children of school age are at higher risk of abuse and exploitation, during the pandemic. In other to mitigate the impact of the school closure, NRC will also provide remote learning to continue supporting children during the crisis. Radio Education program will be introduced to keep learners engaged during the school closure and teachers will be engaged to facilitate radio education programming.
NRC will work closely with EiE working group, Ministry of Education and other education stakeholders and implementing partners for proper coordination and alignment with HRP. NRC will also work with WASH and CCCM teams for technical support and guidance on hygiene and adherence to DO NO HARM principles. School based management committee will also be engaged throughout the process to maintain conflict sensitivity.

1 - Needs

According to the 2019 Humanitarian Overview (HNO), 7.1 million Nigerians are in need of humanitarian assistance in NE Nigeria. Of these, 2.2 million conflict-affected children and youth are in urgent need of educational services. This includes 731,000 IDPs, 640,000 returnees and 395,000 children in inaccessible areas. In inaccessible areas, many children, especially girls, are reported to be deprived of any education. IDP children are at higher risk of lacking access to education, and schools are not yet functional in several areas of IDP return. A 2018 OCHA report indicated that about 1,200 schools have been destroyed in Borno State since 2009, and are in need of rehabilitation. The destruction of large parts of affected towns, including education infrastructure, has put a strain on public services and has restricted access to formal schooling.The Director General of the World Health Organization (WHO) declared the outbreak of the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) on 30 January 2020 a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC). On 27th February 2020 the Federal Ministry of Health announced the confirmation of the first case of COVID-19 in Lagos State, Nigeria. Since then, the case has been increasing with more than 2,500 cases across Nigeria, including in Borno and Adamawa State. On March 19th, 2020, a circular from the Federal Ministry of Education granted an approval for the closure of all school for a period of one month commencing from Monday 23 March 2020. Each state in Northeast Nigeria has contextualized this circular. In Borno State, on 20 March 2020, the Governor appointed the Deputy Governor to head the Borno response team, which directed the closure of schools in Borno State. Schools remain closed as of 5 May 2020.
