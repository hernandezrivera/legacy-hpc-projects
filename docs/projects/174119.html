Project Summary

The project aimed to improve the lives of 2000 children and 200 women beneficiaries through: establishment of child-friendly spaces (CFS) and awareness raising on child protection issues by women particularly the mothers. Key activities of the project which came into direct scrutiny by the stakeholders are: consultations with IDP leaders, religious leaders and co-identify and recruit staff and volunteers who understand local culture and speaks the local language; organize orientation and emergency training for aid workers/service providers on child protection and psychosocial support in emergency settings; conduct baseline determination of the priority child protection and psychosocial issues; identification, monitoring and responding to protection threats and psychosocial issues; provision of life skills education and psychosocial support activities to 2,000 children – with emphasis on reaching adolescent girls; establishment of safe space for children and adolescents where they can partake in age-appropriate activities, play and socialize with other children; mobilize 200 women's participation in developing interventions that protects children, adolescents and women from exploitation and abuse in emergency situations; establish referral mechanisms in collaboration with the State Ministry of Women Affairs and Social Welfare and Child Protection Action Network (CPAN); and in view of possible limited availability of referral services, social coaching to members of the IDP community and host community will be given for them to be able to cascade basic counseling skills and basic case management to their respective communities.
However the Child Safe Space will be constructed and equipped to be used by the children.

1 - Needs

An estimated 2.7 million children and adolescents (1.5 million girls and 1.2 million boys) in north-east Nigeria are in need of protection and cross-sectoral services. Threats and risks to the well-being of girls and boys include family separation, explosive remnants of war, gender-based violence including child marriage and conflict-related sexual violence, recruitment and use of children, abduction and other grave child rights violations. At least 6,000 children (more than 50 per cent girls) are estimated to have been recruited, abducted, killed or victims of violent attacks by armed groups since the beginning of the conflict. Of those, approximatively 50 per cent are survivors of SGBV. The crisis also has caused severe psychological distress among children. The conflict has not only disrupted the social fabric but has also negatively affected the ability of parents and communities to appropriately care for their children. At least 150,000 caregivers of vulnerable children, particularly female-headed households, foster careers and single caregivers with several children, have suffered from severe psychosocial distress and will require psychosocial and/or socio-economic support to assist them to better cater for children in their care.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

GREENCODE will continue to mainstream protection throughout the implementation of this project by aligning every approach with the protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity of both the beneficiaries and that of employers and volunteers. GREENCODE just like in our on-going project will continue to prioritize safety, dignity and avoid causing any form of harm to both beneficiaries and workers. GREENCODE will also strengthen already established mechanisms through which affected populations in project location in LGAs can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints. GREENCODE will continue to ensure that the target population access the service in proportion to needs and without barriers and discrimination. GREENCODE will continue to pay attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services. In the Life-skills based psychosocial support services of this project, GREENCODE will integrate GBV Risk Mitigation activities to minimize or mitigate GBV risk to beneficiaries. GREENCODE will continue to work consciously in compliance with the Sphere Humanitarian Core Standards and Imperatives. All approaches will align with the principles of protection as we remain committed to ensuring that this project does not expose beneficiaries to more risk in compliance with the DO NO HARM Protection Principle. GREENCODE will also ensure that all activities are carried out in compliance with the Nigerian Center for Disease Control (NCDC) and Federal Ministry of Health (FMoH) guidelines against the spread of, and community transmission of COVID-19. Also, GREENCODE will be intentional about maintaining an environment where there is zero sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). All staff, volunteers, contractors and vendors engaged directly or indirectly to implement this project will be given orientation on PSEA. They will be familiarized with GREENCODE policy on PSEA and they must express their unwavering commitment by signing the GREENCODE PSEA code of conduct. Also, GREENCODE will also lay down reporting protocols for the reporting of PSEA cases if unfortunately they occur. GREENCODE will also ensure that the identity of the reporters is kept very confidential. When convenient, GREENCODE will encourage that reports are made on anonymous grounds. Also, being guided by the Inter-Agency mechanism for reporting SEA, GREENCODE will ensure a systematized engagement with affected populations including confidential complaints and investigations procedures at both the system and individual agency level. As recommended by IASC Task Force on PSEA which identified inter-agency cooperation in the creation and maintenance of community-based complaints mechanisms (CBCMs) as a key component in the prevention and response to SEA. GREENCODE will continue to raise awareness to the target population that humanitarian assistance is free and never conditioned on sexual favors as well as to be informed on how to access the appropriate support if SEA occurs. Furthermore, GREENCODE will ensure that established CBCMs in target locations are effective and that there is an inter-agency coordination to ensure consistent messaging and that access to the mechanism is as broad and straightforward as possible for potential complainants.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

GREENCODE will recruit and an experienced M/E Officer that will help track project progress and gather feedback from stakeholders on the project. Secondly, GREENCODE will be holding monthly feedback sessions in the project communities to gather feedback and strengthen coordination. Thirdly GREENCODE will have a dedicated CRM phone line with a dedicated CRM that will be receiving the calls and share with project managers for response. in the same line the phone numbers will be printed and pasted at strategic locations in the communities for target groups to call or text in their feedback. Similarly, a metal opinion boxes will be constructed and position in the community for feedback collection. GREENCODE will also carry PSEA sensitization to beneficiaries via which the reporting channel for SEA related complaints to be made directly to the GREENCODE’s PSEA focal point will be communicated to all beneficiaries and community stakeholders.
