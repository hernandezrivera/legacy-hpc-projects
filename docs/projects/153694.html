Project Summary

The proposed project seeks to deliver holistic emergency GBV prevention and response in Aweil Centre county(Achana,Aroyo,Awada,Awulic & Nyalath Payams) of NBeG state through establishing Women and Girls Friendly Spaces (WGFS) to support females and GBV survivors access services such as counselling, information sharing, access to socio economic support, confidential referral pathways to medical and legal services and case management in a more discreet manner to avoid stigma. SAADO will target to reach 8,666 people (Women 5000, Girls 1700 Men 1000, Boys 966) in the selected county with GBV services.The location has been chosen because of the existing gaps in GBV programming in selected payams such as Achana,Aroyo,Awada,Awulic & Nyalath as well as the opportunities to promote girls’ protection, empowerment and resilience to mitigate GBV risks and to respond comprehensively to the needs of GBV survivors that are available. This proposed project will build upon/complement SAADO’s long experience in implementing projects in Aweil Centre county supported by Stromme foundation. Currently, SAADO is implementing a 2.5-year DFID project in NBeG state for building resilience of communities known as BRACE II which is targeting 60% female beneficiaries and this will provide an opportunity to fully integrate GBV prevention in its implementation. The project will also complement SAADO‘s Women and Girls Empowerment intervention in Aweil Centre and Aweil West supported by Stromme Foundation, which aims at skills building targeting adolescent girls and young mothers over a 5-year period. SAADO's intervention will also complement the Life saving activities carried out by other GBV partners in the selected county as well as provide integration with Education(TVET),Livelihoods(community managed savings groups)and nutrition which are all key components of the project SAADO is currently implementing with support from Stromme Foundation and is a key focus of the GBV sub cluster as well as the RTAP where SAADO is a key participant .

Needs

Despite the signing of the revitalized peace agreement on August 30th gender-based inequalities and social exclusion still remain key factors undermining vulnerable women, girls, men and boys’ capacities to cope, respond and recover from the conflict and economic crisis. Often times, women and girls face constraints that limit their capacity to respond to these 2 hazards based on traditional gender roles and the predominance of patriarchy in South Sudan. These traditional/societal norms have, for so long, led to the subordination of women, limited access to opportunities and information due to restricted mobility, limited control over economic, social and natural resources. According to UNICEF South Sudan situation report August 2018, insecurity continues to impede humanitarian efforts in many areas of the country. The impediments most commonly experienced by humanitarian actors in Aweil Centre county especially most recently during the month of May included several incidents including the unnecessary stoppage and restriction of humanitarian vehicles; the abduction of aid workers and criminal activity affecting local communities and aid efforts. In addition, the situation remains tense in the northern part of NBeG state with reported humanitarian response disruptions as a result of population displacement due to localized conflicts with the cattle herders from Sudan. Aweil Centre County also continues to suffer from a combination of food insecurity factors with a reported 150,015 children 6 to 59 months who were screened in all states with 5,808 and 19,332 children identified with SAM and MAM, respectively in August 2018 according to the IDSR bulletin September 2018 which identified the highest incidences of acute malnutrition among children in Northern Bahr el Ghazal (NBeG) at 19.8 per cent. In addition to these, cases of Gender-based violence (GBV) remains one of the most serious and prevalent protection threats to women and children as highlighted by the Gender-Based Violence Information Management System (GBV-IMS) which recorded 3,585 incidents of GBV in South Sudan with a larger portion of cases going unreported. This project will enable SAADO to respond to protection gaps in service delivery in Aweil Centre County(Aroyo,Achana,Awada,Awulic and Nyalath payams) of NBeG state where traditional kinship support structures have been weakened and the affected communities’ overall capacity to offer adequate protection and assistance severely stretched. The establishment of Women and Girls Friendly Spaces (WGFS) will strengthen structures to protect girls and women against GBV risks. Special considerations on needs of various groups, timely identification and priority assistance for vulnerable individuals and groups for emergency GBV prevention and response will be emphasized in the Women and Girls Friendly Spaces (WGFS): provision of technical expertise for referral of GBV survivor individuals to partners such as ARC and other actors with specialized skills and facilities. SAADO will ensure that those affected by protection risks and threats are provided with timely prevention and response interventions by integrating with other sectors such as Nutrition, FSL and Education to ensure that survivors needs are met when referrals are done, this will be achieved through monthly coordination meetings where a referral pathway will be enhanced. SAADO will monitor the implementation of the recommendations of the safety audits by all sectors to maximize the impact of GBV mainstreaming across sectors.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

This project will ensure that the affected populations in the targeted areas have a genuine voice during activity design and decision-making process that inform the proposed projects's priorities and modalities. As part of their orientation training and continual development, SAADO project personnel will receive instruction on why information sharing is prioritized, how it is operationalized, and what their roles and responsibilities are in making it work. In each target area SAADO will initiate an open dialogue with the community and key stakeholders. We will ensure that communication and messaging is timely, accurate, appropriate,effective and understood. Project parameters such as site and beneficiary selection will be openly discussed with beneficiaries using a highly transparentmethodology that is grounded in community participation. Information sharing will also be a key feature of the proposed project to help in avoiding overlaps between different actors in the selected county of Aweil Centre county of NBeG State as well build collaborations between humanitarian agencies and affected populations.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages will be contextualized to speak to different age groups and gender.The project will achieve this through establishment of Project Management Committees (PMCs)composed of 60% women and 40% men which will enhance community participation, ownership, and satisfaction in SAADO’s proposed intervention. Furthermore,the PMCs will include a membership that represents all subsets of society including marginalized groups and will be responsible for implementing a two-way BCA strategies identified in the project’s beneficiary communication and accountability Plan.This will include delivering initial orientation and ongoing information to communities on project activities, rationale behind beneficiary selection, and supporting the management and resolution of complaints throughout project planning and implementation. PMC's will also be involved in supporting the implementation and management of project activities to ensure that theseresources do not create a source of competition and dispute, the project will strengthen the capacity of community structures to develop and enforce context-appropriate systems for ensuring the equitable access and utilization of communal assets and resources. The project will also ensure communities are actively involved in beneficiary selection, will conduct face-to-face information,question and answer sessions, and establish a complaints system to receive and pre-emptively respond to issues with the potential to negatively impact community relationships.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project will ensure that the Women and Girl Friendly Spaces(WGFS) and training centres are safe spaces for both genders and those women with children have access to child spaces where they can play safely while their mothers attend training. During community meetings,dialogues and training sessions or any gatherings on this project, SAADO's gender and protection staff will address the people we serve/beneficiaries on issues around prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Where ever such issues are raised SAADO will link the survivors with protection implementing partners as part of strengthening the referral pathways.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Tailored transparency and feedback mechanisms such as open meetings,verbal, written and pictorial notifications of decisions and actions; consultations with women and men (separately where necessary to ensure distinct gendered voices are heard), consultations with different age groups and persons withspecial needs, and confidential complaints mechanisms (such as a complaints box) will be used in the proposed project.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project’s M&E approach will focus on accountability to beneficiaries,gathering and sharing evidence to inform programming and compliment and progress initiatives from other humanitarian and resilience actors. SAADO will work within a rigorous system that is in line with the overall guidelines for MEAL in Emergencies, along with the Common Approach to Monitoring and SocialAccountability (CAMSA) as well as contributing and aligning with the protection cluster monitoring mechanisms. Building on a rapid needs assessment, a mini baseline and end line survey will beconducted using quantitative and qualitative methodologies to measure impact and change and to set benchmarks against agreed indicators. Conflict, gender and protection analyses will be updated on a continual basis to inform program implementation. A clear monitoring and evaluation framework will be set up in discussion with women and men separately and in groups, to be monitored by implementing field teams, which will include monthly monitoring reports withclear action points. We will also ensure that a gender-sensitive community feedback mechanism (for consultation, feedback and complaints) is established to inform and change programming, including the establishment of accountability groups based on community-defined vulnerability criteria.Routine monitoring shall be conducted using a mix of participatory appraisal and quantitative methods throughout the duration of the project, engaging primary agents of change, communities, local stakeholders, and project implementing teams. At field level progress toward project objectives will be closely monitored by the Gender and protection programme manager and MEAL specialist supported by the Director of Programs. Where possible,beneficiaries and local officials will take the lead on activity monitoring, and report on progress at community learning events.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

SAADO’s proposed project will ensure protection mainstreaming to members of affected populations by ensuring that females have equal and equitable access to services and that targeted action to advance gender equality and protection is based on a gender and age analysis. All activities and results (output and outcomes) will enhance gender equality and have protectionconsiderations.The project embraces ADAPT & ACTC framework for protection and gender equality programming. For example our project (1) analyses the impact of the crises (violent conflict, shocks and economic) on girls, boys,women and men and what this means in terms of division of labour ,tasks,workload and access to humanitarian assistance and protection (2) ensures that girls and boys,women and men participate equally and equitably in the design,implementation, monitoring and evaluation; and that girls, boys, women and men are involved in decision-making positions.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project anticipates minor access barriers due to long distances in Aweil Centre County for the beneficiaries as well as localized insecurity incidences such as harassment by security organs members. In the past year, SAADO has been able to coordinate movement with other actors such as WFP to minimize these incidences thereby addressing these access challenges.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

This proposed project primarily addresses gender inequality as planned activities/outcomes are based directly on the differentiated needs of the various gender categories.Gender sensitivity including protection is factored in all activities from needs assessment, community sensitization, beneficiary selection,training, reporting and result monitoring. All project reports generated will be disaggregated by gender. The project design will ensure more than 80% of beneficiaries are females. Targeting criteria for activities will be determined by analysis on gender roles and protection issues to ensure the Do No Harm principle are applied appropriately. SAADO has a dedicated Gender and Protection Specialist who will be key at the project inception. Gender analysis will be ongoing during project implementation by incorporation into monitoring tools.
