Project Summary

The school feeding activity is implemented in the Far North region targeting the most crisis affected divisions of Logone and Chari, Mayo Sava and Mayo Tsanaga. The objective of the project is to provide school meals to 52,000 children to encourage and promote school attendance. Many children have been out of school for many years due to insecurity children in the targeted divisions,thanks to various humanitarian support some children have timidly started going to school. The project will contribute to maintain those who are already in the school till the completion of the primary school cycle. Through the project, daily hot meals will be provided to all the children during 165 days of the school year, to encourage them to attend and stay in schools throughout the school year, thus contributing to improve their nutritional status as well as to protect them and limit their exposure to terrorist groups. Some 52,000 school children will be assisted through in-kind modality.
Food commodities will be delivered to schools and meals will be prepared for the children at school. Parents and communities will complement with various items including water, fire wood and cooking services and other locally produced food. The food basket and daily rations for the in-kind assistance are; cereals (140g), Pulses (40g), Oil (15g) and salt (3g). To support the implementation of the project, capacity strengthening sessions on school feeding management, food storage, gender and Protection, will be organized for school canteens managing committees and other partners and actors involved in the school feeding project. These include members of Parent Teachers Associations, mothers’ associations and Government staff form the Ministries of Basic Education, agriculture, Health, Women affairs and social Affairs.

Besoins / Needs

The insecurity in the Far North region due to Boko Harm crisis, over 100 thousand of children have abandoned schools in Logone et Chari, Mayo Sava and Mayo Tsanaga divisions in the Far North and many schools were closed. With the displacements, children were stranded and some of them had the opportunity to register in schools in safer locations. As a result of the improvement of security situation children are progressively joining schools that have reopened. Some displaced families are returning to their villages and children are attending school but still hesitant and demotivated. Parents have limited income sources, they are mostly busy reconstructing destroyed farms and properties but they are still fearful with sporadic attacks. Most of the parents’ activities include income generation to sustain households 'feeding, some returnee families are benefiting from humanitarian assistance. Children highly need food support to keep them at school and also to improve their nutritional status as well as to protect from being enrolled or used by terrorist groups. The food assistance at school will encourage them to attend classes and stay in school throughout the school year. The school meals will serve as an incentive to maintain them in class, this alongside various other education assistance and psychological support provided by other humanitarian agencies in the mostly affected divisions.
