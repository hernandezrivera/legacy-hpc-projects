Project Summary

South Sudan continues to face deteriorating humanitarian conditions even after 11 years independence, and 4 years after the signing of the revitalized peace agreement, effects of years of conflict, violence and destroyed livelihoods have left more than 8.2 million people (about two thirds of the population) in dire need of some form of humanitarian assistance and protection according to Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO)-2023. The HNO 2023, an estimated 6 million people’s (excluding refugees) living standards and their wellbeing will be impacted in 2023 as a result of inadequate or lack of access to safe water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices in 2023. Peace Corps Organization target a total of 18,000 beneficiaries from the Tonj East, Pibor, Tonj North Counties in addressing emergency WASH support targeting most conflict and floods affected IDPs and vulnerable host communities in Warrap and Jonglei States. PCO intends to scale up water sanitation and hygiene services to these vulnerable group of people through an integrated approach in Nutrition and GBV sectors through implementation of the following key activities: Community mobilization to introduce the project clearly stating the objectives, duration and project implementing period, activities to be carried out, target population to the community and other stakeholders. Training of enumerators and hand pump mechanics who will help the community in borehole mapping and repair of the boreholes and protected hand dug wells to ensure sustainability of the project, Rehabilitation and repair of boreholes and protect hand dug wells. This will be done by trained hand pump mechanics after a GBV risk analysis through FGDs with women and girls using the in-kind supplies requested from the core pipeline, Development of new protected hand dug wells where there is no existing borehole to provide safe water to the vulnerable IDPs, Returnees and the host community. Rehabilitation of latrines will also be done in or near institutions after carrying out a GBV risk analysis and the latrines will have locks and doors to enhance privacy, dignity and security and hence reducing the risk of exploitation, such as sexual exploitation and abuse, Establishment of hand washing stations and facilities at the rehabilitated/constructed sanitation facilities to ensure communities wash their hand after defecation, Establishing and training water management committees on best ways of managing water points including guiding the elderly, sick, people living with disabilities, animals and children at the water points to ensure sustainability of the project, Transport and distribution of WASH NFIs requested from the WASH core pipeline to under 5 children SAM/MAM and PLWs and monitoring their use. This will be done by WASH staff and also Nutrition/Health/GBV staffs trained on WASH minimum packages including demonstration on usage, Selection and training of community hygiene and sanitation promoters/ volunteers to visit households with messages of improved WASH which will help influence positive behavior change to the community. The centrality of gender-sensitive and protection in all programming, which includes actions on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, mitigating GBV against women and girls will be a key pillar of all planned actions. Nutrition facilities such as OTP/TSFP/SC, health facilities such as PHCC/PHCU and protection sites such as women and girls friendly spaces and child friendly spaces will be prioritized by PCO as part of integration among the various sectors. Streamlining GBV Training to WASH staffs after being trained by the protection teams active on ground, distribution of water treatment product (PUR, aqua tab, filters), Awareness creation and sensitization of community on spread of Ebola virus disease, Training of Adolescent girls on Menstrual Hygiene Management both in schools and community level including demonstration on usage of menstrual hygiene management kits.

1 - Needs

According to the 2023 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), the highest level of needs and vulnerabilities are observed in Warrap and Jonglei states, due to multiple and recurrent shocks, targeted States are all under vulnerability IPC 4. Deterioration of the economic situation reduced ability of communities to purchase items required such as soap, purification tablets, NFIs and hygiene items. According to REACH South Sudan situation overview 2019, high proportion of HHs (74%) reporting having a water borne related diseases, with malaria the most commonly reported (74%), fever (56%) and then by AWD (20%). Majority of persons resort to unsafe open water sources that are usually contaminated with feacal material and are co-shared with livestock. Inadequate access to clean water, improved sanitation and acceptable hygiene practices within the counties of Tonj East, Tonj North of (Warrap State) and Pibor County of (Jonglei state) contributes greatly to the high levels of malnutrition, disease burdens, food insecurity as well as WASH related protection issues. Access to sanitation facilities is equally low, Access to safe and gender sensitive latrines remains a big issue during both wet and dry seasons and thus open defecation is predominant, leading to cycles of water contamination, resulting in acute watery diarrhea, cholera, eye infections, and skin diseases. Hand washing is equally low, with socio-cultural practices unfavorable to achieving acceptable WASH standards, Outbreak of Ebola (EVD) in the neighbouring Countries risking the life of the people in South Sudan. This competition and pressure on the scarce water and sanitation is a driver to conflict between the host communities, the IDPs and even animals. According to FSNM 2021, estimates that about 10% of the community have safe latrines and they use it appropriately leaving an approximately 90% of population practicing open defecation. There’s a direct correlation between low WASH coverage and high levels of malnutrition, as a result of occurrence of water related diseases amongst U5s, PLW and persons with special needs. Humanitarian needs assessment done in Pibor by PAH between 16th and 18th February 2022 indicates that a good number of the population spends 30 minutes or more to collect water from sources due to distance and high number of users. Data indicates that an average of nearly 1,200 people are served by one hand pump which is well above the international standard of 500; this also results in overuse which contributes to breakdown of the pumps and reduction in water availability. This leads not only to low water consumption (under 10 L/person/day) but also increases the burden of women and girls caretakers who are solely responsible for fetching water exposing them to some form of GBV namely abduction of young girls and boys, forceful marriages, killing and rape of young girls. Lack of spare parts and limited pump mechanics is a challenge, Lack of trained Water User Committee to foresee the borehole operation and maintenance, Lack of water treatment knowledge and practice in the institution, Water quality was carried using observation and smelling of the water in school’s containers which show un clean water containers being used for fetching water for drinking. The sanitation and hygiene situation is further worsened by the fact that most of the community does not have access to a hand washing facility, poor knowledge and practices on personal hygiene and environmental conservation practices. The operational hand pumps in Tonj East, Tonj North and Pibor Counties are overused and has led to more breakdown of pumps and increased queuing time at the viable water sources causing reduced per capita water consumption (under 15 L/person/day), hence increased consumption from unprotected water sources like untreated rivers and hand dug wells and also reduced time for other essential tasks at their homes.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will establish a range of Beneficiary Communication and Accountability mechanisms to strengthen community engagement and feedback throughout the life-cycle of the project. Safety Audits will serve as an additional Feedback and Complaints Mechanism (FCM) modality in our WASH programs and other sectors. All focus group discussions occurring as part of Safety Audits will ensure provision of an enumerator who is the same sex as the participants. The CFM will also outline strategies for soliciting regular feedback from communities (beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries).This will be done through Monthly staff meetings with first-line implementers, Focus Group Discussions with Direct beneficiaries, Individual interview form, Comment Box at Field Office Gate and nutrition facilities and Anonymous Hotline Number. As part of the MEAL system surrounding Men Group Discussions, participants will be specifically asked to share their concerns with the program and suggest improvements. The identities of all feedback and complaints mechanism (FCM) complainants will be kept strictly confidential by program management staff when provided. ). Communities will be encouraged to identify multiple information dissemination channels that leverage, where relevant and appropriate, multimedia platforms including radio and SMS. We have standard, context appropriate FCM IEC materials (posters, stickers, flyers), which are displayed at all project sites and on all project structures (e.g. equipment used by beneficiaries). IEC materials include images and text in both English and the local dialect to ensure comprehension by a maximum number of individuals. During all community meetings, trainings, and sensitization sessions, a standard script will be used by staff to inform beneficiaries on available FCM modalities. Specific responses will also be discussed bi-monthly at the beginning of focus group discussions at the site level to inform beneficiaries of what changes are being made given previous feedback, or if any concerns are being addressed at the national level. Depending on the urgency, feedback will be delivered to the first line program implementer during the monthly meetings. Urgent issues will be addressed immediately by the Head of Base and Program Managers through appropriate channels.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

PCO will adopt a consultative approach in the implementation of this project. All the beneficiaries (men, women, boys and girls) will be directly involved in the entire response cycle from inception until exit and also be informed on the duration, activities and the roles of different stakeholders in the project. This will be done by ensuring a two-way communication and dialogue throughout the project. Details on recruitment and other arising issues will be communicated in forums such as public meetings and Focus Group Discussions which will be held with the different segregated groups of people (Men, women, girls and boys) to ensure community gets timely information which enhances active involvement and sense of ownership of the project. The local leaders among other relevant authorities and other key stakeholders will also be invited during project launch and since they are the representatives of the community, they will give the community information about the assistance to be provided. PCO have developed a tool for feedback and complaint mechanism which we have been using in the previous similar projects and have always proven to be efficient. The local leaders and other community representatives will give their opinions and complaints as well as possible recommendations. PCO have locally recruited and trained Community Hygiene Promoters who conduct household visits and are able to get feedback directly from the beneficiaries and they are addressed immediately depending on the urgency. We also have sessions with the beneficiaries during our field supervisions where we discuss the challenges, project impacts and the areas which they feel requires more concern and the best approach. All the views from the beneficiaries are then screened and processed further and we use this criteria as a complaint/ feedback mechanism.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project will take into consideration protection from sexual exploitation and abuse issues to all the beneficiaries. Women and girls will be consulted through FGDs on the best site considering safety and accessibility before rehabilitating water sources and latrines so as to reduce their exposure to GBV and other forms of abuse when visiting these sites. In addition, the rehabilitated latrines will be gender disaggregated to avoid men and women meeting while protecting their privacy and exposing them to sexual exploitation and abuse. The water management committees will be trained and will discourage fetching water during the night since may also expose the beneficiaries to a lot of harm. PCO will train WASH staffs on cross-cutting issues such as GBV and protection to provide sensitization to the beneficiaries on GBV and other related abuses and ways to minimize their occurrence so as not to cause more harm to the beneficiaries. PCO has developed Protection against sexual exploitation and abuse policies which guides all the Organization staff and volunteers. PCO staff will sign PSEA code of conduct before engagement in the project. Our actions will address both basic needs that improve protection of those at risk of violations. PCO will ensure that training is conducted to all the staff members both at Juba office and field staff on PSEA before the beginning of the project implementation. The training will then be extended to beneficiaries during project inception. This training to the beneficiaries will be done by Juba based staff members and not by field teams to avoid biases, victimization or any conflict of interest. The beneficiaries will be trained on communication channels available for them to report PSEA and the contact persons. Information provided by survivors, victims of sexual exploitation and abuse or beneficiaries will be safeguarded not to be used to expose them to harm and referred to a level that they can seek help. Distribution and training of adolescent girls on usage of menstrual hygiene kits will also be done by PCO female staff so that the girls feel free and hence also protection from sexual exploitation and abuse.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

PCO have developed a tool for feedback and complaint mechanism which we have been using in the previous similar projects and have always proven to be efficient .The PMCs committee which composed of Juba staff and led by the M&E will be in-charge of receiving those feedback directly from the field locations and conducting the feedback analysis. Each feedback will be collated and grouped accordingly into areas that will ensure they are better addressed. Those that are related to programming will be addressed from the perspective of the beneficiary’s advice and lenses and they are at the centre of the project. Whether it is changing the meeting or distribution location or time, that will be changed, and beneficiaries will be informed of the changes through downward feedback from Juba office. PCO have locally recruited and trained Community Hygiene Promoters who conduct household visits and are able to get feedback directly from the beneficiaries and they are addressed immediately depending on the urgency. We also have sessions with the beneficiaries during our field supervisions where we discuss the challenges, project impacts and the areas which they feel requires more concern and the best approach will be dealt with. The M&E staff will travel to the field locations to communicate directly with beneficiaries and also notify the field staff of the changes that has been made.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

This project is conceptualized based on protection risk analysis and will main-stream protection at every level. PCO is mainstreaming protection in all its programming by ensuring that the most vulnerable groups such as women and girls and others such as PWDs, elderly, receive protection when they are providing information at any stage of the project cycle and that the whole process is confidential. PCO is a very active national NGO on GBV in the three States and will employ a protection advisor for this project to work closely with established committees to guide the process with a serious protection lens. Information provided by survivors, victims of sexual exploitation and abuse or beneficiaries will be safeguarded not to be used to expose them to harm and referred to a level that they can seek help. PCO, together with other agencies will ensure that the meeting sites or locations are safe and closer to beneficiaries. Barriers to access particularly the meeting locations will be discussed with all the beneficiaries in separate groups (constituencies) before it will be agreed on where everyone is comfortable to access. Monitoring tools PCO will use have a section of monitoring safety of beneficiaries, and other barriers to access as protection is mean-streamed in the tool and also have a section that asks questions as regards the use of the items support and whether the beneficiaries feels comfortable using them. The project activities will address inequalities and protection threats by analyzing power dynamics in the community. Local leaders will not be in charge of determining who will benefit but beneficiaries with support from our staff. PCO staff will sign PSEA before engagement in the project. Our actions will address both basic needs that improve protection of those at risk of violations. PCO is also a very active member of Protection Cluster.
