Project Summary

Education is a tool meant for all learners in any given country. In attempt to enhance access and retaining learners in schools, training of the school governing body, provision of child friendly spaces, construction of inclusive TLS, provision of dignity kits, provision of learners' and teachers' kits have been proven attracted and retained learners in schools. This project will target training of County Education Directors on their roles in quality data collection, analysis and use of collected data to make informed decisions. This will help address the vices to the access, retention and the completion of school’s circle by learners. It is also intended to enhancing teachers’ effectiveness in doing their work of helping learners acquire the intended knowledge, skills and attitude for their inclusion into their communities after they complete their learning.
The project shall target the communities of three states, Jongolei, Upper Nile, and Central Equatoria states will benefit from the project, as their dreams of Educating their children will come true. The choice of the project location is complimentary to SPEDP presence in these states for over nine years, in the field of Education, FSL, Mine risk Education, Health, Shelter-NFI, GBV and WASH.
Children with disabilities are neglected in communities of South Sudan, this project shall ensure that all the school going aged children is taken to schools and are provided equal access to resources. In the same way, girls are treated harshly and viewed as sources of wealth, and because of such stereotype believes and assumptions, the girls in some communities are not taken to school, this project shall ensure that girls are treated with equal attention just like the boys their counter parts.
SPEDP Education services will focus mainly on activities that enhance access to, retain learners in school and give them opportunities to complete their learning circle. It shall also focus on how to equitably include children with disabilities where they are available. SPEDP will establish a disability friendly environment in all the services that will be implemented under this project. 1 - Needs

The formation of the Transitional Government of National Unity at lower local government, in February 2020 brought hope, leading to increased return of IDPs and refugees into the community and country. But the prolonged war left many needs with devastating result. The 2013 and 2016 conflicts worsened the situation, affecting socio-economic, political infrastructures and education, which has been and still is badly affected. Livelihood opportunities including income generation activities that should have contributed to improving the education status are brought to zero in the aftermath of the 2013/16 war. Most of the population in the proposed project sites turned beggars. Gender equality not been witnessed and much increase in GBV risk particularly among women and adolescent girls. In this project, attention shall be on the host community, returnees, IDPs with special consideration for CWDs and girls. The country is facing a collapsing economy with high inflation, civil servants are not paid, continued tribal conflicts, cattle rustling and floods that continued to displace many people. Despite the formation of TGNU based on revitalized peace agreement, the country still faces appalling humanitarian crises, where over a million people still continue to live in IDP Camps and many are still in POCs with inadequate essen services. The Education Cluster aims to support 866,000 children to have access to safe, protective, and quality inclusive education, which will contribute to well-bein and resilience of the most vulnerable children. The cluster also aims to strengthen national and sub-national education systems, including school community representatives, parents/teachers’ associations (PTAs) and national NGOs to improve service deliver and enhance accountability to the affected people. The sectoral objective aims to improve equitable access to safe and protective education services for 866,000 crises affected and most vulnerable girls, boys, internally displaced children, children with disability, out-of-school children, caregivers and education personnel. The second sectoral objective aims to improve the quality of gender-responsive inclusive education services by increasing teachers' capacity to protect the well-being of all children and prevent the negative impact of emergencies on learning. The third sectoral objective will enhance the capacity of education system, sub-national local education actors, and communities to mitigate the impact of emergencies on learners, teachers and the overall schools at large. The Cluster’s sectoral objectives are linked to the intersectoral strategic and specific objectives of the HRP and the Ministry of General Education and Instruction’s (MoGEI) five-year strategic plan. Protracted conflict and climate-related hazards in 2022 resulted in record challenges to education, including school closures, increased numbers of schools and out-of-school children, worsening of the already low learning outcomes and difficulties in retaining children in schools, which exposed them to grave risks such as recruitment into armed groups, early marriage, and child labour. Education Cluster members in partnership with the MoGEI will support essential education in emergency services to the children traumatized by conflict and displaced by crises, children at risk of dropping out and the 2.8 million already out of school children. Locations affected by insecurity and flooding will be prioritized to ensure children are protected, continue to learn, access psychosocial support, and hope for normalcy. The cluster’s sectoral objectives require inter-sectoral commitments to address the needs and demands of school children, teachers and school communities through the provision of child protection, WASH, school feeding, school health and nutrition interventions. The cluster’s objectives go beyond emergency response to promote localization through building technical and institutional capacities of national duty

2 - Project activities

The Education Cluster will continue providing humanitarian support and effective coordination for timely education service delivery across the country. The cluster, chaired by MoGEI, will continue to provide guidance and support to education partners to overcome barriers related to access and lack of funding, avoid duplications, and use resources efficiently to deliver quality education in emergency (EiE) responses. The Education Cluster’s activities apply child-centred approaches and encourage equal participation of children and communities. The response will strengthen the capacities of SMCs, PTAs and community leaders to protect and reduce the vulnerability of schoolchildren—particularly girls—against gender-based violence. In line with findings from HNO 2023, education actors will improve basic school infrastructure by establishing and rehabilitating temporary learning spaces (TLS) and WASH facilities, distributing hygiene materials and providing essential life-saving education supplies. In addition, train teachers and PTAs on their roles and responsibility, teachers' code of conduct and address PSS, child protection and GBV issues. The Education Cluster membership will continue advocating for free education and allocating government resources for teachers’ salaries to retain qualified teachers and learners in schools. Children’s needs are multi-sectoral; therefore, the cluster advocates for multi-sectoral interventions with WASH, Protection, Food Security, Nutrition and Health clusters. The Education Cluster encourages incorporating essential components such as psychosocial support, referral mechanisms and school feeding into the package of education services. Lack of water and hygienic WASH facilities for students, especially adolescent girls retention in schools, to continue their education, so sets a critical component of the response to improve these facilities. Additionally, the cluster actively participates in inter-agency initiatives aimed at providing common service approaches to quality programming, including AAP, gender and disability inclusion and localization. The Education Cluster will continue to work closely with MoGEI, and development actors to sustain the humanitarian-development-and peace nexus. The Education Cluster will continue to lead on localization through the participation and leadership of local actors in decision-making, advocacy for principled partnerships, and institutional capacity strengthening to improve local ability and continuity of education response beyond the emergency phase. The education cluster has advanced the localization agenda by establishing a localization task team and engaging local NOG providing leadership at the national education cluster.

3 - Project indicators

The strategy will prioritize locations with the highest rates of children and youth with EiE needs, including areas that received an influx of displaced people, areas affected by flooding and inter-communal violence, food insecurity, and areas suffering from a lack of education infrastructure, services and supplies. The response will aid schools in the host community to accommodate the additional influx. The response will prioritize crises-affected and most vulnerable girls, boys, internally displaced children, children with disability, out-of-school children, caregivers and education personnel. The Education Cluster response modalities and predominant cost drivers are the provision of education services to 866,000 targeted children (52 per cent are girls) through an envelope of $54 million based on the cost/per child calculation and availability of resources, to construct or rehabilitate learning spaces, provide educational materials and supplies, construct or rehabilitate water, hygiene and sanitation (WASH) facilities, including handwashing services, health and hygiene awareness, training of teachers, building the capacity of PTAs, SMCs community leaders, state education officials, and MoGEI staff, payment of incentives for teaching facilitators, supporting the national examinations, and establishing a mentoring programme linking partners with teacher training institutes/colleges. In addition, the impact of climate change with heavy rain and floods, the influx of displaced people, and spontaneous returnees (both refugees and internally displaced people) have overstretched the education and protection services for children. 7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Through the Project Management Committees (PMCs) established in the project, communities will be encouraged to work on the principle of “leaving no one behind,” non-discrimination, and inclusiveness in the implementation of this project. This Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) created will take a special interest in identifying and collecting opinions from the physically challenged members of the beneficiaries in the project. The PMCs will deliver a mechanism that enables the most vulnerable members of the beneficiaries and community to provide feedback and seek responses in relation to activities of these beneficiaries in relation to the project in a manner that is inclusive, safe, non-discriminative, non-threatening, and accessible to all. This initiative is intended to promote beneficiary accountability and feedback and response mechanism to individuals who are physically challenged in the project. SPEDP will use this to measure the adequacy of interventions and address concerns and complaints of most vulnerable members e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls. The PMCs will also be encouraged to ensure easy access possible for returnees, IDPs, and host communities regardless of their age and sex. SPEDP plan to use multiple information dissemination channels to bring about easy access and information leverage. This will include multimedia platforms including radio and SMS. These platforms shall be identified and where necessary and appropriate used to disseminate information on inclusiveness. Mechanisms that consider opinion of women folks shall as well be consulted for their opinions for inclusion in the project implementation process. This will be done through various fora including monthly community consultation meetings facilitated by the PMCs. The Project Management Committees will be responsible for adapting and iterating implementation strategies and work plans for the inclusion of the information collected into the project.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The Education Cluster maintains a response monitoring tool/5W Matrix (Who is doing What, Where, When and for Whom)/ and partners’ self-reporting tool activity Info to track the implementation of HRP. Based on partners’ reports, the Education Cluster coordination team will track underserved groups of children, adolescents,internally displaced children and children with disabilities, and areas experiencing the highest gapsbetween severity of need, and response.The cluster also monitors the situation through its sub-national cluster focal points, as well as through an online reporting tool where partners report on incidents that impact education services. The cluster also interacts regularly with other stakeholders, including the ICCG and cluster membership, especially national actors, to monitor the situation closely. The cluster supports evidence-based response modalities through a robust information management and data collection system and updates the situation of the sector by conducting a nationwide education needs assessment. The Education Cluster is represented in the regular joint inter-sectoral missions to monitor field-level implementation of partners’ projects, and shares monitoring reports to ICCG, OCHA and implementing partners as feedback. The Education Cluster is committed to the accountability of affected populations. Sharing timely information, consultation and dialogue between service providers and affected people improves the quality and efficacy of humanitarian response. The cluster also acknowledges that national NGOs play a crucial role in monitoring.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

SPEDP adheres to the internationally accepted “DO NO HARM” and SPHERE” protection principles which ensure avoidance of exposing beneficiaries to further harm as a result of the actions of the project being implemented in the community. And, to ensure complete impartiality in people’s access to assistance in proportion to needs, no discrimination, and protection of people from physical and psychological harm arising from violence and coercion. SPEDP will ensure that people claim their rights, have access to available remedies, and recover from unforeseen effects of abuse. SPEDP will also ensure that protection risks and benefits are considered in this project to all beneficiaries. A community-based protection approach or meaningful engagement will be promoted. Ideal leadership of crisis-affected communities to identify protection risks, self-protection capacities, joint prevention, and mitigation schemes will be paramount in this project and will be linked to the SPEDP Protection Department and actors so as to seek and mainstream an age, gender, and diversity approach that aims at equal enjoyment of rights by all crisis-affected people and to integrate this into accountability framework in the project. Aware of the UN Secretary-General's Bulletin on Special Measures for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (ST/SGB/2003/13), SPEDP Project Staff Members will strengthen the PMCs and community engagement and create awareness-raising sessions around PSEA with women, girls, boys and men in the project areas as well as with other stakeholders and a feedback mechanism for receiving complaints from communities set up. SPEDP will identify and support feedback channels that communities prefer while ensuring that feedback mechanisms are sensitive to age, gender, and diversity (AGD) and accessible to women and girls, children, persons with disabilities, and other persons at high risk. Besides, SPEDP will also coordinate complaint collection and responses with other partners within these proposed project locations and establish clear tasks and duties (who will do what, where, and how).

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries feedback and information will be collected through meetings and interviews with the stakeholders in this project. The opinion of the beneficiaries will be collected during project field monitoring. SPEDP will organize committees made up of diverse representatives from the student body, PTA/SMCs, Teachers, and project staff to work together to record and handle complaints and feedback especially from the beneficiaries' regarding the quality of project implementation. SPEDP will approach and apply the principle of equity perspective and advocate for equal participation of different groups of beneficiaries. This will be based on the cluster reporting system or mechanisms. The project will put in place a monitoring and reporting system, progress, best practices, and lessons learned will be presented in cluster meetings based on the 5Ws formats developed by the cluster. The Project Management Committee (PMC) formed will be briefed on their roles and on the keeping of confidentiality. Regular meetings with staff and beneficiary representatives shall regularly be conducted to review records and in deciding on action needed. Feedback collected will immediately be addressed and included in the implementation of the project. That is, the Feedback Project Management Team will be responsible for adapting and iterating implementation strategies and work plans accordingly in real-time. Information relating to the project’s response to feedback will be fed back to communities by the PMC through scheduled consultation meetings, which in so doing will ensure functional two-way communication. Project Management Committees will also publicize and manage complaints and response mechanisms which will allow communities to provide feedback between scheduled community consultations, and anonymously report grievances and complaints. The Project Management Committees will use the project-specific risk-based protocols to respond to any complaints.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The widespread flooding in most part of the country, ongoing violence followed by displacement continued to deepen humanitarian needs in South Sudan. Since May 2021, an estimated 850,000 people have been affected by floods. Some of them fled their homes, taking refuge on higher ground in a communal structured movement. The physical access remained a major challenge to humanitarian response, despite the ongoing efforts to address the immediate needs of flood-affected people, the level of need is high and varied. Women are also unreasonably harmed by flooding and related displacement, they typically bear a greater responsibility for tasks that made it more difficult, including sourcing food and water. Displaced women are also disadvantaged in many humanitarian recovery efforts, and may face greater difficulty in accessing assistance. This has often affected education of children of the affected population, and others are critically in need of access to safe drinking water, anti-malarial and other basic drugs, and plastic sheets for construction of temporary shelters for families. It is likely that the lost crops and other sources of livelihoods will cause longer-term humanitarian needs and reduce food security in 2022. The current situation with COVID-19 pandemic and restrictions impedes immediate response. There is a need to provide immediate support to the flood affected communities. Shelter and essential household items (HHIs), Health, Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH), Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), Protection, Gender and Inclusion (PGI) activities are required for an integrated response. SPEDP vision to facilitate rehabilitation of basic social amenities such as latrines, hand pumps, health facilities, farms and nutrition centers.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

Education is the most extreme exhibition of gender inequality in South Sudan and a central human rights violation based on some cultural ways of life, coupled with the protracted crises that have dragged the country into aggravated devastation negatively impacting on all the social sectors including education. This project shall be implemented to prevent and response to integrates and collaborates with other sectors within its operational areas to ensure equitable access to, retention and completion of education circle. SPEDP uses available Quality Assurance Tool to provide quality, timely and inclusive education services. The primary aim of the project is to support access to, retention in schools and completion of education circle by learners. The project will emphasize on the provision of and access to holistic services by strengthening the school governance structures.
