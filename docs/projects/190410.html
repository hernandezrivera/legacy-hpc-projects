Project Summary

As September 2021, the Ethiopia National Displacement Report 10, highlighted an increase to 4,239,636 displaced individuals nationwide due to several factors, such as conflict (85%), drought (7%) and seasonal displacement (3%). In 2021, the triggers of humanitarian needs in Ethiopia were armed conflict, inter-communal tensions, climatic shocks, desert locusts and disease outbreaks. In 2022, the humanitarian situation for Ethiopia is still characterized by severe shocks, including the conflict in North of the country, drought conditions and persisting inflation (34.5% in January 2022). The project will target both conflict affected population and those climate shocks to reduce the risk of morbidity and mortality through the provision of integrated WASH services. The project will support the response to the gaps identified in: Afar, region is highly prone to climate shocks and human made disasters: desert locust infestation, floods and droughts have severely impacted the food and nutrition security. Ethnic tensions between Afari/Somali and Afar/Oromo have caused violence and displacement. Since the spillover of the conflict in Tigray in Afar, more than 336,000 IDPs in 11 sites in the region require water trucking every day to fill the minimum standard of 10l/p/day (OCHA). 29 woredas of the region face serious water shortage due to damages, electricity down, unavailability of fuel and non-functionality of water supply and 164 water supply schemes are out of service in the region. Low coverage of sanitation and bathing facilities in IDPs sites represent a huge risk for disease outbreak. COOPI will target the Zones 1, 2, 3 and 4. Amhara: from August 2021 the spillover of the Tigray conflict in the region resulted in a huge displacement of population including North Shewa and Oromia Special Zone, already affected by ethnic conflict. COOPI, based in Ataye Town, conducted an assessment in North Shewa and Oromia Special Zone (February 2022): as result of the conflict, a huge number of water supply systems are damaged, Water Offices looted, health facilities destroyed, sanitation facilities of education facilities used as shelter in need desluging, among others. Further displacement is reported also in Awi Zone, where COOPI is present in Chagni, as result of ethnic tensions. iii. Oromia Region: COOPI will focus on both conflict and drought affected areas in Guji, East Bale and East Hararghe Zones. Both East Hararghe, where COOPI is present in Chinaksen, and Guji where COOPI works since 1995, are affected by clan clashes and the presence of UAG. Tensions between UAG and ENDF have resulted in 190.000 IDPs in Guji. Due to the extremely low precipitations in October to December 2021 (deyr) in southern and southeastern pastoral areas combined with two consecutive failures in the rain season in late 2020 and early 2021. The drought has deepened humanitarian needs both in Guji, East Bale, Bale and Somali Region. Specifically in Somali, currently, 3.4 million people are facing water shortage and need urgent water intervention with around 1848 sites (84 Woredas). In accordance with the WASH cluster's objectives the proposal will contribute to: a) deliver conflict-sensitive essential life-saving WASH services to affected people and b) enhance vulnerable households’ ability to withstand recurrent and future shocks in Ethiopia through early recovery and resilience interventions. The project will provide maintenance and repairs of 39 water schemes (10 in Afar, 8 in Ahmara, 4 in Benishangul, 12 in Oromia, 5 in Somali); deliver water trucking in Somali, improve access to sanitation and hygiene with construction of latrines and delivery of WASH NFIs kits, improve capacity of WASHco and restore the functionality of WASH services in HCFs. The project will mainstream protection and gender&age approach as well as PSEA. The project targets in total 453920 individuals (22% male; 23% female; 26%boys; 25% girls; 2% elderly men; 2% elderly female).

1. - Projects activities

1.1 Maintenance and repairs of 39 water schemes (10 in Afar, 8 in Ahmara, 4 in Benishangul, 12 in Oromia, 5 in Somali). The project will target non-functional/partially functional water schemes through quick maintenance and repairs. The project will also take into consideration the possibility to substitute generators and/or submersible pumps, in the event that maintenance or repairs of the electromechanical equipment are not feasible, due total failure of the same. When possible, alternative solutions, in terms of providing pumping equipment that rely on renewable source of energy (solar), will be taken into consideration based on a scheme-by-scheme situation analyses (number of beneficiaries, quantity of water needed, total head); this technical solution would reduce the burden on the running costs of the schemes.The activity will guarantee also water availability at health facilities and schools; this will be made through extensions of pipelines to health facilities and schools.1.2 Maintenance of hand pumps (30 Ahmara, 20 Benishangul Gumuz, 30 Oromia, 10 Somali)The project will target non-functional hand-pumps installed on hand-dug wells or shallow depth boreholes.1.3 WASHCo trainings (Afar, Ahmara, Oromia, Benishangul Gumuz, Somali) (ii) 39 WASHCo, in charge of rehabilitated schemes, will be trained in managing, operating and maintaining the targeted water schemes, to enhance the capacity of the members to properly carry out their duties. The training, based on national and regional rules, will be delivered in collaboration with the Woreda Water Offices. 1.4 Delivery of emergency sanitation facilities (Afar, Ahmara, Oromia, Benishangul Gumuz, Somali)The project aims to reduce the exposure to disease outbreak through the construction, management, decommissioning of 100 blocks of emergency trench latrines of 5 stances (standard: 1 stance per 100 persons). COOPI will mainstream gender, age and disability.1.5 Hygiene campaigns (Afar, Ahmara, Oromia, Benishangul Gumuz, Somali). COOPI will carry out initial and end-line KAP survey. The campaign will include the production of illustrative and easy-to-understand materials and the training of community facilitators in the areas of intervention in charge of dissemination of key hygiene messages at household level. COVID-19 related messages will be dispersed. This activity will give special attention to pregnant and lactating women, elders and people with disability. 1.6 Water trucking (Somali). The water trucking will address the water shortage at health centers and schools, in Somali Region. The project will guarantee that National standards in terms of water quality and quantity are respected. 1.7 WASH NFI kits distribution (Afar, Ahmara, Oromia, Benishangul Gumuz, Somali). Beneficiaries‘ identification, selection, verification and registration.The composition consists of: water treatment chemical (3 months), Jerrycan (2/HH for 6 months); Bucket (1/HH for 6 months); Body soap (1/p/month for 3 months); laundry soap (1/person/month for 3 months); washing basin (1/HH for 6 months); flashlight (1/HH); dignity kit (1/female). This activity will accompanied by post distribution monitoring to assess: assess the quality of the process and the degree of satisfaction of beneficiaries regarding the following dimensions: quality control of process (assessment, verifi cation, distribution)/ effectiveness of the response; accountability to affected population (Information provision/Sharing,participation, feedback and complaint mechanism); distribution process; timeliness of the response; Protection; quality control of the items, appropriateness, usage and satisfaction.1.8 Insitutional WASH in Health Centers (3 Ahmara, 2 Benishangul Gumuz, 3 Somali).The activity will support improving water supply, waste management, sanitation facilities, in HCFs respecting Sphere and National Standards

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

COOPI adhere to the humanitarian accountability principles and all COOPI Ethiopia staff is trained of the 5 commitments of the Accountability of the Affected Population. COOPI will make sure that benefciaries know they have a right to equitable and safe assistance, where and how to obtain it.Following COOPI Standard Operating Procedures, to ensure informed decisions COOPI staff will involve partners, communities, local authorities, beneficiaries and especially vulnerable groups in the planning, implementing, monitoring and evaluating of this project. This includes engaging beneficiaries at the design stage by conducting focus group discussions and individual interviews with both men and women in various age groups, and ensure that assessments include the most vulnerable, including elders and people with disabilities. During the action, beneficiaries at all levels will take a leading role in the formation/consolidation of the committees described in the proposal (selection committees). These committees, jointly with local authorities and project staff, will co-lead in the selection of the recipients and in the monitoring of the action.COOPI will provide opportunities for women, people with disabilities and older people to hold positions of responsibility and be represented on community-based committees including as community focal points for assessment teams. An accessible, well understood and confidential mechanism will be set up for receiving suggestions and complaints, with a clear follow-up system on all reports.COOPI’s feedback and complaint mechanism includes information sharing on:• Project objectives, outcomes and outputs, implementing agency and donor.• Selection criteria and mechanisms to report issues for beneficiaries;• Composition of the ES-NFIs kit/entitlements. The validation and agreement with the main stakeholders on the selection of sites and beneficiaries of distributions will take place through meetings with community members, local authorities and cluster representatives.COOPI Project management team will strengthen local capacities, foster local knowledge and community coping strategies, as well as enhance protection mechanisms providing where possible psycho-social services to the community and beneficiaries.COOPI management team will make sure that field teams are sensitized and trained on the importance of makingassessments tools age-friendly, by including questions on the needs and capacities of elderly and vulnerable people.Particular attention will be provided on PSEA. COOPI will provide training on its internal policies to the project staff and main stakeholders. The policy includes the concepts related with PSEA, preventive measures, referral mechanisms as well as feedback and complaints mechanisms. Besides that, COOPI will brief the staff also using the Ethiopia PSEA Network Training Package.COOPIwillset up a feedback and complain mechanism to allow benefi ciaries’ participation,improving monitoring and accountability reporting and promptly adjust theimplementation of the project. Mechanisms to report issues will be done through:•Provision to local stakeholders of main contacts for COOPI staff (phone and email),especially for the SMT program staff• Provision of help-desk contact(help@coopi.org) • Establishment of complaints and feedback box and notice panel atthe fi eld (depending on level of literacy of benefi ciaries)• Participatory selection offocal points at community level, for oral complaints/feedback.COOPI will record all possible complaints and feedbacks. According to the severity of the issue COOPI SMT will respond, in at least 4 days, involving different staff at different level:• Minor complaints/ request of information: social promoters/wash advisor/emergency lead•Major complaints: Programme Manager/Emergency ResponseLead/CountryAdministrator/Head of Mission/HQ
