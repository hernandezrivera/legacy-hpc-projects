Project Summary

Somalia is facing a Triple Threat of Desert Locust, flooding’s and the global pandemic COVID-19 contributing towards the deterioration of production outputs, social economics increasing vulnerabilities of households. These culminations result in an impact on the states of acute malnutrition situations affecting children and women. Already, vulnerable communities due to persistent climatic shocks, locust infestation, and the COVID-19 pandemic, are facing severe food and water scarcity and are at risk of deadly communicable diseases such as cholera outbreak and acute watery diarrhea (AWD), With limited resources and funding, the humanitarian agencies in Somalia are overstretched and in need of urgent support to provide a timely and deliberate response. The communities living in the riverine areas along Juba and Shabelle rivers are at high risk of flooding. As heavy rains are expected to continue in November and December, scaling up of livelihoods, improving and supporting health services, and WASH facilities is vital in order to prevent further deterioration in food security and escalation of the public health crisis.
In response to WASH gaps identified during the joint need assessment in Wanlaweyn led by OCHA in September 2020, the proposed project actions under this request are meant to meet the dire needs of the flood-affected communities who are at risk of acute watery diarrhea AWD/Cholera and WASH-related diseases in Wanlaweyn district of the Lower Shabelle region. The proposed project is providing life-saving and life-sustaining assistance to people in need, prioritizing the most vulnerable women, girls, boys, men, and people with disabilities (PWDs). Specifically, the project will enable IP to fill the WASH gaps through the provision of an emergency and sustainable WASH assistance package that will be based on needs identified in allocation strategy and the local context of the target communities. The overall objective of this response is to provide emergency life-saving and life-sustaining WASH services which are crucially appropriate to the needs of the most vulnerable communities living in the 35 flood-affected villages of Wanlaweyn district to improve access to safe and sustained water., sanitation, and hygiene educations, reduce acute watery diarrhea AWD/Cholera incidents and WASH-related diseases through rehabilitation of strategic shallow wells protected and installed with water pumps, establishment, and training of hygiene promoters on personal and environmental hygiene. The project will also focus on the construction of handwashing facilities, hygiene promotion activities, and provision of hygiene kits to flood-affected households, continue with acute watery diarrhea AWD/Cholera awareness and distribution of water purification tablets to acute watery diarrhea AWD/cholera suspected areas as emergency response, institutional hygiene promotion, training of WASH committees on hygiene domains, operation, and maintenance, households water treatment. The proposed intervention will involve people with disabilities (PWDs) in WASH assessment to identify risks and WASH access barriers they face, ensuring that persons with disabilities and their families are actively involved in identifying barriers that impede their access. The project will involve PWDs in decision-making and will ensure that persons with disabilities are fairly represented, taking into account the various forms of disability as well as age, gender, and diversity. The project will make sure that women, girls, men, boys, and people with disabilities are involved in monitoring activities and their feedback improves future response.

Project Activities

The project will prioritize the implementation of the following activities to support the flood-affected communities of Wanlaweyn district of lower Shabelle region:- 1) Rehabilitation of 35 strategic shallow wells in the target 35 flood-affected villages of Wanlaweyn district in order to reduce the burden of the women and girls fetching water from long walking distances and ensure households have access to safe drinking water. 2) Training on water treatment at the household level and distribution of water purification tablets to households of the 35 target villages. This will ensure the safety of water from the source to the mouth. 3) Training of 35 water committees established for the 35 rehabilitated shallow wells on water management, operation, maintenance, and sustainability. 4) Hygiene awareness and promotion for villagers (men, women, boys, girls, and PWDs) of the project target areas. 5) Provision and distribution of hygiene kits to the households of the 35 flood-affected target villages and recruit community hygiene promoters who are based at the community level to improve safe hygiene practice at critical times leading to disease control, targeting the needs of women, men, girls, boys and people with disabilities. 6) Construction of 140 handwashing facilities at public and market places of the 35 target villages.7) Training of community hygiene promoters on personal hygiene, environmental sanitation, behavior change of handwashing practices. 8) Construction of shared 250 VIP latrines for the target population in the 35 target villages. Every five households will share 1 latrine. at least 15% of constructed latrines will be designed to accommodate people with disabilities. 9) Project monitoring to track the proper implementation of the activities, identifying and mitigation of challenges and evaluation of project impact.

Mitigation Measures

Mitigation measures for the Risk of women, girls, and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance:-1. Project will ensure the location and access routes to WASH facilities and distribution points are safe. 2. Project will ensure the mode and frequency of distribution minimize exposure to safety threats protecting beneficiaries from face theft, intimidation, sexual assault, and extortion whilst taking their hygiene kits home. 3. Project will ensure all Designs and construction of WASH facilities must preserve the safety and dignity of its users.4. Project will ensure preventing the improper use of cleaning chemicals and chlorine. The project will also ensure proper storage and that labels and instructions on their usage are in a language or pictorial form that is understood by users. Mitigation measures for risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, girls, and boys do not participate in consultation or decision making process for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities:-1. Project will ensure that women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, older persons, and marginalized persons are consulted to collect accurate information about their specific WASH requirements and preferences for location and design of WASH facilities.2. Project will include people with disability in WASH assessment to identify risks and WASH accessbarriers they face. 3. Project will ensure that persons with disabilities and their families are actively involved in identifying barriers that impede their access, and the project will also ensure Involving PWD in decision-making. 4. Project will engage in consulting with women, girls, persons with disabilities on identifying their needs, solutions, and participate in the decision-making process.5. Project will ensure that persons with disabilities are fairly represented, taking into account thevarious forms of disability as well as age, gender, and diversity. Mitigation measures for Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflicts over access to resources:- 1. Project will involve representatives from the target groups ( women, girls, men, boys, PWDs) participate in the project beneficiary process and make sure impartial selection is avoided. 2. Project will ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable - Identify and prioritize the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups 3. Ensure people with disability are involved in monitoring activities and their feedbackimproves future response.4. Project will set up accessible, confidential, and well-understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment, and assist in detecting misconduct. Eg. place feedback surveys in hygiene kits, complaint & feedback box at WASH facilities, Hotline numbers. 5. Sensitize target beneficiaries (women, girls, boys, men, and PWDs) on key processes and activities of the project in order to have a full and clear picture of the intervention and avoid speculations.
