Project Summary

Through the GiHA WG, UN Women and UNHCR continue to support gender responsive humanitarian action across and within humanitarian sectors throughout the humanitarian programme cycle. More specifically, UN Women leads the GiHA WG in providing technical inputs from a gender perspective to the needs analysis, sector strategy and consolidated response plan formulation and ensures that gender equality and women’s empowerment is integrated across the humanitarian response. UN Women provides training based on the IASC Gender Handbook, IASC gender marker and other GiHA tools and guidance such as the ISCG sector gender tip-sheets, and promotes and provide technical support to all sectors to apply gender-sensitive approaches in their projects to ensure investment decisions are gender responsive.
By regular meetings GiHA provides space for the sectors and organizations gender focal points to share the information, identify capacity development needs, advocate and initiate the joint interventions. The series of trainings will take place and at least three joint activities during 2019. The gender briefs, profiles and other reports developed by Gender Hub will be shared and promoted through members and organizations to the first responders. The monitoring of the gender provisions under JRP 2019 will be done practically, under the guidance of Gender Hub, that will be established very soon in order to ensure comprehensive approach and strengthen the collaboration between GIHA, GBV, PSEA and Protection working group.

Needs

A year after the influx of Rohingya refugees started in Bangladesh, it was decided to collectively take stock of progress made in mainstreaming protection, promoting gender equality, empowering women and girls, and, preventing and mitigating risks of gender-based violence (GBV) across the response on the occasion of the mid-term review of the JRP. Beside efforts of GIHA, PWG and GBV subsectors the report shows that many humanitarian actors in Cox’s Bazar do not recognize that the various programmes they implement can contribute to the overall protection of refugees; they do not consider gender and protection as central to the response , and gender mainstreaming is not well understood, in general there is a knowledge gap. Gender sensitive language and data disaggregated by age, sex and disability are not widely used and there are persisting gaps in inclusion and access to services across camps and sectors for persons with specific needs, including in particular persons/children with disabilities, older persons, chronically or severely ill persons, and persons living with HIV/AIDS, LGBTI persons, adolescent girls, female headed households and unaccompanied and separated girls and boys. An inclusive and more participatory approach to assistance and services throughout the response is required in order to adequately address the differentiated needs of every woman, man, girl and boy enabling them to exercise their basic rights and enjoy their entitlements to equal humanitarian assistance. More specifically ther is a need for consistent capture of how women, girls, boys, and men are affected by the crisis, the empowerment of women and girls, in particular through the expansion/diversification of cash for work schemes and skills development programming, Inclusive and effective consultation with and involvement of women and girls at all stages of intervention, to ensure their meaningful participation in managerial groups/decision making and for capacitating of actors across the response to mainstream gender and protection through training. Humanitarian assistance must develop mechanisms and modalities that enable and empower women and girls to mitigate real or perceived risks; to monitor and address rights violation issues; and to strengthen their roles as first responders, leaders and decision makers. Such interventions in refugee response efforts build women and girls’ participation and empowerment and ultimately their community’s resilience. In addition, gender-responsive humanitarian assistance is proven to be more effective. GiHA WG is critical for cross-sectoral mainstreaming gender in the response but still do not have capacities to address all above issues and limited collaboration between, GIHA, GBV, PSEA and PWG raised the need to establish a Gender Hub that will bring together all these mechanisms and support their efforts on mainstream Gender and protection across the response.
