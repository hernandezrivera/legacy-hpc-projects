4 - Needs

Daraa: The United Nations (UN) and humanitarian partners have significant concerns for the civilian population impacted by recent increase of hostilities in Daraa Governorate, with the HG military campaigns, ex-non-state armed groups (NSAGs) use of IEDs, assassinations and other activities against the army positions and personnel, where at many occasions it involves civilians casualties. The current socio-economic status is deprived with the forced sanctions over against the majority of the government sources of income, especially fuel. This had created severe shortages in basic resources (gasoline, gas, diesel, etc.) across the country and its turn led to an inflation and vast increase of prices, including the decline of the Syrian Pound value and instable fluctuations. With the COVID-19 global pandemic, the health services deteriorated with the poor response, also, the access to health services is very limited across the governorate due to the lack of qualified medical staff. Unexploded ordnance (UXO) and other explosive hazards are present in Daraa governorate too, with repots of civilians’ casualties. Returnees are exposed to a particularly high risk of ERW. Alraqqa Across SDF-controlled areas of northeast Syria there still presence of IDPs and refugees. The count of IDPs had decrease with number of IDP/refugee camps, after the 2019 Turkish with NSAGs incursion against SDF in the north and northeast part of the country. While returns continue to occur towards Ar-Raqqa from all sites, new arrivals during the month have largely come from Deir-ez-Zor. There continue to be significant concerns for the situation of IDPs in Ar-Raqqa, many of whom are reportedly not adequately equipped for winter.

5 - Implementation of activities

Project Objectives and Implementation Objective: Provide adequate security support for the UNCT and to enhance the security risk management measures in order to ensure the safety and security of UN personnel and continuity of humanitarian programme delivery in Ar-Raqqa, and Daraa Governorates. Implementation for each of 2 locations mentioned above: Field Security Coordination Officers (FSCOs) and Local Security Assistants (LSAs) are essential to maintain and create the security risk management mechanisms and procedures as well as defining strategies and apply mitigation measures to enable the presence of humanitarian and development organizations and the International Community. Security Communications Centers are instrumental to enhance the awareness of the UN system in a location and to quickly respond to security incidents or emergency situations on the ground. They are part of the established Emergency communications System, integrating Smart phones, radios and satellite phones into a seamless 24/7 command and control room that can help to start the right reaction to contingencies in a minimum time.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The security situation in Syria continues to remain extremely volatile as hostilities continue throughout the country. With an increasingly complex security situation and heightened risks for UN staff and assets, there is a need for adequate security support for the UNCT and to enhance the security risk management measures in order to ensure the safety and security of UN personnel and continuity of humanitarian programme delivery inside Syria. UNDSS is responsible for providing oversight and operational support for the security management system in Syria, and plays a crucial role in supporting UN operations. Furthermore, it is crucial that UNDSS maintain presence in Alraqqa, and Daraa to provide 24/7 security-related operational and analytical support to the UN Agencies and implementing partners and strengthen risk-based humanitarian delivery to the most vulnerable in Syria while keeping humanitarian personnel safe. In addition, UNDSS will continue to undertake awareness and training sessions (SSAFE, First Aid, ERW, Defensive Driving, ETB, etc.) for UN agencies' staff and humanitarian actors.
