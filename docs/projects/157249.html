Project Summary

The main purpose of this project is to improve the access and utilization of quality primary health services, and to improve the nutritional and FSL status of IDPs and the most vulnerable households in North Darfur.
The health program mainly provides the minimum package of essential health services including consultation of communicable, reproductive health services (ANC, Deliveries, and referrals), EPI vaccination, and referrals to secondary units, minor trauma care, pharmacy services, and health education. The nutrition program mainly targets children six-to-59 months and pregnant and lactating women (PLWs). The program delivers preventive and curative services which include screening, referral, admission and treatment of SAM children in OTP; and MAM children and PLWs in TSFP. OTPs form the basis of the CMAM approach aimed at addressing and treating cases of severe malnutrition without complications. SAM patients will be rehabilitated using RUTF “Plumpy Nut” along with medical treatment. MAM patients will also receive supplemental feeding in the form of pre-mixed dry rations consisting of highly improved fortified Super Cereal Plus food. A key intervention in nutrition also include IYCF interventions, which includes food demonstration, promotion of vegetable gardening, and breastfeeding counseling to mothers.
The FSL component is a preventive and complementary component to the nutrition intervention through increased access to nutrition food and household incomes. RI will support households to access quality animal health services, which improves the household dairy consumption to reduce malnutrition, particularly among the most vulnerable groups ( <5 children and PLWs). It also improves the households’ resilience through alternative livelihood options during times of shocks.

Needs

Communicable disease morbidity and disease outbreaks are a serious concern in North Darfur. In 2018, cases of acute watery diarrhea (AWD), measles and malaria were reported across the proposed target locations. More than 219 cases of measles were reported in Zamzam Camp (B) (RI facility data, 2018), and due to heavy rains in 2018 RI clinic data showed a dramatic increase in malaria cases with 1,157 cases reported between May-October 2018 compared to 493 cases for the same period in 2017. From May to October 2018, there were 66,877 consultations in RI-supported facilities, with Zamzam and Al-Malha averaging 11,146 per month. Despite the high demand for service, the health system is weak and over-burdened and SMoH has inadequate financial capacity to improve access and availability of services. The HNO reports that nearly 36% of primary health care facilities in Sudan are not fully functional due to staff shortage or poor physical infrastructure, medical supplies and equipment.In North Darfur, the nutrition record shows a high number of admissions of children to feeding sites in Zamzam Camp and Al-Malha Locality. OTP admission data from January to October 2018 indicates a total of 5,679 children < 5 years were admitted to RI-supported nutrition sites. This admission rate indicates high levels of malnutrition. The joint MUAC assessment carried out by RI and SMoH in August 2018 in Al-Malha and Zamzam revealed that out of a total 31,108 children screened, 704 SAM cases and 3,055 MAM cases were identified representing a proxy GAM of 12.1% and SAM 2.3%. Relative to the rest of the country, North Darfur has even higher rates of GAM (27.9%) as per S3M assessment conducted in August 2013 with children and PLWs most affected. The attributing factor for the poor nutrition status includes household food insecurity, lack of dietary diversity and poor hygiene and IYCF practices.The household food security are affected by pertaining factors including devaluation of the currency, and food transport limitations due to shortages of fuel , which continue to place more risks to vulnerable families. Livestock is an alternative means of income to substantial number of households’ as well animal products constitutes 20% of the household food consumption in North Darfur. A recent FGD conducted by RI in Umkededa and Umgedebo Villages identified the veterinary service as poorly developed with limited access to animal health service. A post-harvest assessment conducted by the Ministry Livestock also confirmed diseases and poor veterinary services as factors (76%) affecting the livestock production in the different localities. Similarly, an FGD with Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) indicated constraints, including physical access to veterinary services, absence of veterinary supplies, poor veterinary infrastructure, and lack of support by the livestock department are constraints for provision of accessible veterinary service in the villages.

Activities

RI proposes activities in El Fasher (EF) and Mellit (Mt) Localities based on needs. RI estimates 47% male & 53% female participation. Health: RI will support clinics on primary health & RH services to 24,201 people. RI will train & support health staff (EF: 62; Mt: 36), CHWs (8 in Zamzam (ZZ) & 3/clinic in the rest), VTMs (25 in ZZ & 2/clinic in the rest), VHCs (32 in ZZ & 3/clinic in the rest), and 40 WSGs in ZZ. RI will also support clinic rehabilitation; provision of supplies & pharmaceuticals; emergency referrals; & disease surveillance/reporting/response. Nutrition: Prevention through: integrated BCC, CMAM, & IYCF with positive deviance to tackle social norms (EF: 24,000; Mt: 201); kitchen gardens for food access; & cooking demonstrations for care practices. Screening, referral, follow-ups, & BCC will be done by trained CNVs (EF: 142; Mt: 12). To manage existing cases of malnutrition, RI will continue to support OTPs (EF: 3; Mt: 7) & 1 SC in EF for CU5 SAM cases (EF: 953; Mt: 201). TSFPs (EF: 3; Mt: 3) for CU5 MAM cases (EF: 2,562; Mt: 201) & PLW MAM cases (EF: 519; Mt: 201). RI will distribute MNPs under SFP activities to malnourished CU5 that do not qualify for the TSFPs (EF: 9,614; Mt: 201). FSL: Targeting HHs with SAM & MAM cases who lost livestock due to conflict, RI will support restocking (10 goats/HH) (EF: 157 UK: 693) with livestock production and small business skills trainings. Using the One Health approach, RI will train government livestock extension workers, CAHWs, (EF: 7 UK: 31) on common disease identification & treatment; zoonotic disease control awareness campaigns; & outreach services & supplies.

Impact on environment/natural resources

Medical waste will be managed under WHO guidelines to minimize any solid waste impacts. The kitchen gardens would have minimal potential impacts. RI will ensure that the type of seeds are suitable to the local area and culture, and will not promote chemical inputs or irrigation efforts beyond hand watering/micro-irrigation.In order to minimize potential impacts from livestock programming, RI will support restocking with small ruminants that will have less impact on climate change, and land and water impacts through grazing and feeding. As well, veterinary support will be in line with the Government of Sudan standards on livestock pharmaceuticals and chemicals; any required disposal of livestock vaccines is the responsibility of the Government.
