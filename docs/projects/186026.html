Project Summary

The proposed intervention seeks to address the capacity strengthening of the Demand and Supply-side actors (Justice Sector Institutions) to ensure timely arrests, documentation and prosecution of perpetrators, establish safe spaces to provide MHPSS and Livelihood empowerment to reduce the heightened risk of negative coping strategies, including sex for survival. The project will also involve conducting of advocacy to relevant stakeholders to influence policies and programs aimed at preventing and responding to SGBV, increase awareness campaign on SGBV in Humanitarian Settings and sustained IDS-SGBV Telephone Helpline for survivors’ confidential counselling/referral pathway, and develop SGBV Specific Services Guide Booklet and Mobile App to help survivals and their relatives identify service providers within their communities. Other activities will include community engagement, risk mitigation and resilience-building.

1 - Needs

The prolonged Boko Haram insurgency in Borno State and other northeastern states of Nigeria has led to heightened levels of displacement, sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV). According to the recent IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM-July 2021), 2,184,254 IDPs are currently residing in camps or in host communities across Adamawa, Borno and Yobe states of Northeast Nigeria. The disruption in the socio-economic activities has increased the risks of sexual violence, intimate partner violence (IPV), early marriage and other forms of violence. Women/girls and men/boys from marginalized populations face even greater risk, based on intersecting dimensions of inequality such as disability, sexual orientation and gender identity. SGBV is a serious and life-threatening issue and poses significant safety and protection risks. The 2019 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) indicates that about 3 million people need SGBV prevention and response services in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states. Sexual and Gender-Based Violence (SGBV) is a significant global human rights issue that has recently received greater consideration in conflict and post-conflict environments, predominantly as a crime against women and girls (Carpenter 2006, Sivakumaran 2007, Linos 2009). The global SGBV crisis requires a prevention, protection and survivor-centred approach in all humanitarian activities. The spike in SGBV since the outbreak of COVID-19 has been called a ‘shadow’ pandemic by Pramila Patten, the UN Special Representative to the Secretary-General on Sexual Violence in Conflict. Human rights experts, including the UN Special Rapporteur on Violence Against Women, have labelled the situation a pandemic in a pandemic and have called for ‘peace at home’, imploring States to eliminate gender-based violence (GBV) through post-COVID-19 recovery plans.

2 - Activities

1. CAPACITY BUILDING FOR DEMAND AND SUPPLY ACTORS: The activity will involve capacity strengthening of social actors targeting CSOs, the Media and the justice sector institutions (The Police, NSCDC, Magistrates, Social welfares and Traditional/Religious Leaders). Others are the Ministries of Women Affairs, Justice, Health, SEMA, NHRC and NATIP on the importance of SGBV prevention, responses and Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). Others include case documentation, management and their role in facilitating timely arrest and dispensation of justice against perpetrators in the society and to ensure survivors are free from all forms of injustice in the project targeted locations. 2. SGBV RESPONSE SERVICES: These activities will help social actors and justice sector institutions (JSI) to take a client-centred approach to their work and provide a platform for coping with the impacts of conflict, COVID-19 and reduced exposure of women, girls, boys and men to SGBV through the following sub-activities. (a). Telephone Helpline, (b). Establishment of Safe Spaces, (c). Livelihood Support for SGBV Survivors, (d). Research and development of Service Guide Booklet/ Mobile App: IDS will conduct an operational research and map up services providers to develop SGBV Service Guide Booklet and a Mobile APP for free download with detailed information, guidelines and directions on where SGBV survivors, relatives, friends and other concern community members can access justice, MHPSS, economic support services and referral pathways or focal points within their communities for SGBV prevention and timely responses. 3. ADVOCACY AND AWARENESS RAISING CAMPAIGNS: IDS seeks to, as part of its implementation, conduct community advocacy on key stakeholders across Government Lines Executives, the Parliaments, Traditional/Religious Leaders, CSOs and Development partners to influence policies and programmes aimed at preventing and responding to SGBV in the society. All the project proposed activities will be implemented with strict compliance with WHO and Nigeria Center for Disease Control (NCDC) protocol on COVID 19 prevention and containment. IDS will also distribute personal protective equipment (PPE) and encourage the use of facemasks, sanitisers, handwashing and maintaining of social distancing.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

IDS will be responsible for the collection of feedback and reports directly to the Project Director/Focal person. Reports can be made through IDS GBV Helpline: 08172702242 or info@ids.org.ng, The phone number and email address that receives and responds to enquiries, complaints, feedbacks and reports of safeguarding issues. This phone number and email address are managed by the trained staff to maintain confidentiality and promote the rights of the complainant. IDS have a complaint and feedback box in all areas of intervention where individuals, community members and beneficiaries can anonymously drop their complaints, feedback or report any safeguarding concerns. The complaint and feedback mechanism focal persons manage the boxes at the community level while the gender officer manages them at the organizational level, IDS protection manager, gender officer and complaint and feedback mechanism (CFM) focal persons in communities of intervention are trained to receive, handle and manage reported safeguarding cases, these staff are also trained to recognize and respond to safeguarding issues. All reports are treated as soon as possible efficiently and in confidentiality. Reports are assessed by the protection manager then case management for the survivor (if need be), the investigation is launched, and the outcome of the investigation is used to make decisions and also used to inform the disciplinary action to be taken against perpetrators. Information on the investigation is shared on a need-to-know basis and with the consent of the complainant. The Email and complaint box mechanisms can be used for anonymity, the whistleblowing policy (procedures) protects people with serious concerns. Depending on the magnitude of the case IDS makes referrals for survivor to have Justice.
