Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in Zimbabwe is deteriorating, with the economic crisis (rising food prices, under-employment, high unemployment among the economically active group) compounding the impacts of erratic rainfall which has been characterised by recurrent El Nino-induced droughts and the recent Cyclone Idai. The 2018/19 agricultural season was severely affected by these weather phenomena resulting in delayed start of the season which was followed by prolonged dry spell. The Eastern and parts of Southern parts of Zimbabwe was affected by Cyclone Idai. Approximately 3.58 million people (38% of the rural population) are in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse and require urgent action to reduce food consumptions gaps and save livelihoods. Approximately, 2.47 million people face Crisis conditions (IPC Phase 3) whilst 1.10 million are in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). An additional estimate of 2.68 million people faces Stressed conditions (IPC Phase 2). The prospect of significant deterioration in the health and nutritional status of the communities cannot be ruled out unless support to these communities is delivered. The results of the 2019 rapid ZimVAC rural assessment showed that malnutrition rates are still high with 8 rural districts having a GAM rate above the emergency threshold. The nutritional outcomes are expected to deteriorate as households continue to adopt negative coping strategies affecting dietary diversity. Women and children particularly girls are disproportionately affected by as they are the first to drop from school and assist bin seeking food, women carry double burden of feeding the family and caring for the children at home especially in time of crisis like the current drought and the recent cyclone idai disasters.

In response to this emergency, Save the Children Zimbabwe proposes to implement high impact nutrition interventions in 12 Districts across the 7 provinces thus Binga, Kariba, Gokwe South, Wedza, Chimanimani, Chipinge, Bulawayo, Shamva, Mt Darwin, Mazowe, Rushinga and Hurungwe districts. In Chipinge District Save the Children Zimbabwe will only support the deployment of Nutrition Ward Coordinators. The project will target the most vulnerable population thus the children under the age of five, pregnant and lactating women.
The project aims to save the lives of under-five children with acute malnutrition through improved multi-sectoral coordination and integrated emergency response. This will be realized through capacity building health workers and community volunteers on mass screening for early case identification of moderate and severe acute malnutrition, infant and young child feeding, strengthening of mother to mother support groups and supportive mentorship of health workers within the 12 selected districts for a period of 12 months. Systems will be developed in child safeguarding and Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and the programme will be connected to referral pathways.
