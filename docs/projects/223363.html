Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in Borno State is quite severe due to ongoing conflict and violence, primarily driven by non-state armed groups. Over 2.4 million people in Borno are in need of humanitarian assistance, with internally displaced persons (IDPs) making up 51% of this group. Many of these IDPs live in overcrowded camps or host communities, which strains local resources. (HNO 2024 Nigeria)
The WASH (Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene) situation in Pulka / Gwoza, located in Borno State, Nigeria, is currently facing significant challenges:
1. Infrastructure Damage: Heavy rains and windstorms have damaged Shelters and WASH facilities in Pulka/ Gwoza, necessitating urgent repairs and construction of new facilities.
2. Overcrowding: The growing population in IDP (Internally Displaced Persons) camps has overstretched the available WASH facilities, leading to a need for decongestion or expansion, previous year IOM started the decongestion plan.
3. Hygiene Concerns: There are ongoing health and environmental concerns due to inadequate hygiene practices and poor waste disposal systems
4. Recent Attacks: Pulka / Gwoza has also been affected by recent security incidents, including a suicide bomb attack, which has further complicated the humanitarian situation. (https://reports.unocha.org/en/country/nigeria/).
Efforts are being made to address these issues, including the rehabilitation of water facilities and the distribution of hygiene kits to prevent the spread of diseases.
The situation remains critical, and continuous support is needed to improve the living conditions and health of the affected population.
The project will bring improved access to complete WASH services in Pulka / Gwoza camps through water point rehabilitation, water provision in GBV safe spaces, latrines and shower constructions, menstrual hygiene management, and hygiene promotion. Water supply has been the major challenge facing most of the camps, as well as the host community. The project will contribute to the WASH sector objective by providing safe water to IDP camps for their domestic needs (cooking, drinking, bathing, and laundry), Improve the sanitation service by the construction of sanitation facilities by gender and adapted for people with disability, constructed according to the WASH sector design requirement.
The project targets a total of 10,333 individuals IDPs living in Pulka / Gwoza IDPs camps.

1 - Needs

The assessed needs in the WASH (Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene) sector in Pulka / Gwoza are significant and multifaceted :1. Water Supply: There is an urgent need to improve access to safe drinking water. This includes the rehabilitation of boreholes and the implementation of active chlorination to ensure water safety. UNICEF with the support of RUWASSA and NCA set up a Pulka Small town water supply scheme which in the long run should cover the entire locality of Pulka2. Sanitation Facilities: The construction and maintenance of sanitation facilities, such as latrines and showers, are critical. These facilities need to be safe, decent, and capable of handling the high demand from the local population and IDP camps.3. Hygiene Promotion: There is a strong emphasis on hygiene education, particularly for adolescent girls. This includes the distribution of hygiene kits and menstrual hygiene management (MHM) kits, as well as training on best hygiene practices.4. Waste Management: Establishing proper waste disposal sites and supporting sanitation control through the provision of sanitation kits are essential to prevent the spread of diseases.5. Cholera Prevention: Given the high risk of cholera outbreaks, there is a need for comprehensive cholera prevention and response strategies. This includes educating the community on cholera preparedness and response.These needs highlight the importance of continuous support and intervention to improve the WASH situation in Pulka and ensure the well-being of its residents.

2 - Activities

NCA will directly implement activities in targeted IDP camps, with prioritization of new arrivals in Pulka, and filling gaps in service provisions.NCA will employ community hygiene volunteers to promote language-tailored hygiene messages among the IDPs. The community hygiene volunteers will receive training on hygiene promotion awareness and key hygiene promotion modules, with heightened focus on cholera outbreaks. They will target as priority the new IDPs arrival in the different camps. NCA will rehabilitate latrines and showers in the camps. These sanitation facilities will be separate by gender. A focus will be placed in the design for people with disabilities to facilitate their easy access to the sanitation facilities. Filled latrines will be dislodged and NCA will strengthen WASH committee capacities on sanitation facilities maintenance and repairs. NCA will provide in kind material for latrines, showers, minor repair, and environmental cleaning kits. A team for disinfection activities will be mobilized to disinfect particularly sensitive facilities such as public latrines, health centres and sanitation facilities, to prevent the cholera outbreak.NCA has estimated that approximately 1000 meters of extension of existing drinking water networks will be needed inside IDP camps to close gaps and facilitate access to households, so that they as a rule are less than 500 m away from a water point. New standpipes will be created at the end of each extended safe water new network, with six tap heads that can serve according to the standards 250 per person per tap, i.e., 1,500 people per standpipe. As such, a total of 7,500 IDPs are targeted to reach safe water within 500 m from their dwelling. Technical capacities of operation and maintenance technicians will be reinforced to self-maintain the functionality of the water access facilities, and NCA will provide three maintenance kits after assessment of the tools of all the technician in the target IDPs Camps. NCA will specifically target new IDP arrival in the camps throughout the duration of the project. Activities will include the distribution of MHM kits to women and young girls as well as the dissemination of MHM IEC materials and the organization of regular awareness raising sessions on MHM. The mains activities are :- Hygiene kits distribution- HP campaign- Latrines and shower construction or rehabilitation- Borehole rehabilitation / water point construction or rehabilitation- Washcom engagement-Community engagement- CRM mechanisme set up
