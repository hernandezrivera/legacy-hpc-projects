Project Summary

The situation in the North-West (NW) and South-West (SW) regions remains concerning. Armed clashes, targeted attacks resulting in killings, armed extortion, and arbitrary arrests, remain frequently reported even till November 2023. The population is caught in between and exposed to violence, leading to displacement.
In October of 2023 in the Akwaya subdivision (Manyu division, SW), over 875 persons were forced to flee Kajifu 1, Kekeukessim 1, and Kekeukessim 2 villages due to targeted attacks by non-State armed groups (NSAGs). During those attacks, NSAGs killed 4 persons, injured 25 others, and burned 33 houses and 7 motorbikes. The population in these villages and most of the villages in Akwaya subdivision have fled either to villages in neighboring Nigeria, to Mamfe, or nearby bushes due to the attack and continuous threats. Also, On Monday 06 November around 03:30 AM, suspected NSAG members attacked members of the “Boki” community living in Egbekaw village in Mamfe. Over 15 houses were burnt during the attack, leaving around 20 people injured and at least 50 deaths. As a result, limited access to safe drinking water/sanitation remains problematic in such settings, an outbreak of any of the WASH-related diarrhoeal and infectious diseases in such circumstances could be more difficult to contain thus, necessitating prevention.
The conflict has a major impact on the WASH situation of the population as only 62 percent of households in the NW/SW regions have access to improved sources of water (MSNA, 2023). Almost 59% of households do not have access to functional latrines. Responding to the WASH needs of IDPs, Returnees and vulnerable host populations is necessary, as access to water is a key challenge both in terms of quantity and in terms of quality in the NW and SW.
Purpose
Respond to WASH needs and reduce morbidity and mortality of WASH-related diseases amongst IDPs, Returnees, and vulnerable host communities in hard-to-reach areas while initiating innovations to foster resilience in the NWSW.
Objectives:
Improve sustainable access of IDPs, Returnees, and vulnerable host populations to safe drinking water and hygiene services.
In 2024, the project will improve access to safe drinking water, and sustainable sanitation both at community and household levels, and empower communities and other stakeholders to respond to flood and cholera outbreaks and other outbreaks with innovative approaches that will foster resilience to climate change.
Targets
- 45,000 people sensitized on good hygiene practices
- 50,000 especially flood and cholera-prone communities educated on cholera response.
- 32,000 people utilizing improved water services from 12 constructed/rehabilitated water points and revitalized Water Management Committees.
- 45,000 people (6,000HHs) with WASH kits
- 7,000 individuals with hygiene-kits
- 2,000 HHs without latrines with latrine will benefit from latrine construction.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

- Basic WASH commodities and NFIs: These populations lack basic WaSH supplies for their daily living (Jerry cans, buckets, baby pots, hygiene and dignity kids etc) These supplies are basic especially to families that ran taking with them nothing or whose homes were either destroyed or burnt. - Hygiene promotion awareness: Gaining knowledge about hygiene and sanitation through SBC is an important aspect of health promotion. This empowers them on issues of personnel and environmental hygiene, waste management, food handling, basic first aide, etc. - Safe drinking and sanitation: They usually say water is life and without water death is eminent. Most water surfaces have been destroyed and some new settlements are working on constructing new water sources. These water can only be sustainable when water management committee have been put in place and are being followed up by the communities to ensure that there appropriate management of resources. - Flood and cholera response knowledge: Flood and cholera has become very common in these crisis zones. there is need for the population to be educated on the response to flood and cholera by education, transmission and contact based precautions.
