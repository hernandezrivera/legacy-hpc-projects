Needs

GBV continues to be a key protection and health concern in Palestine. The protracted protection crisis, and its impact on gender and family dynamics, has exacerbated GBV in all its forms, including sexual violence, intimate partner violence, and child marriage. In 2017, the GBV Sub-Cluster lead by UNFPA registered more than 32,000 GBV cases as illustrated in the GBV Information Management System of the GBV Sub-Cluster. Out of these registered GBV survivors, around 80 percent were women, 10 percent girls, 2 percent men and 2 percent boys. This clearly shows that especially women, and hereafter girls are in need of GBV services compared to men and boys. The GBV cases detected also mostly take places within the family, where husbands and other family members were identified as the alleged perpetrator. The humanitarian situation in Gaza Strip continues to deteriorate. Since 30 March 2018, the Gaza Strip has been tense with weekly demonstrations as part of the ‘Great March of Return’. According to OCHA, as of 9 August 2018, 172 Palestinians were killed in the context of the demonstrations, including 30 children and more than 17.504 were injured. Other 48 Palestinians were killed during the same period in contexts other than demonstrations. Women and girls from families affected by casualties and injuries have an increased risk of GBV, which was confirmed during focus group interviews with the affected communities. Based on advocacy paper published by GBV Sub Cluster, the electricity and fuel crisis in Gaza Strip has further intensified the situation making the living conditions of the population worse with a particular impact on women disrupting almost all aspects of life. GBV Sub-Cluster partners have reported an increased financial burden in order to guarantee the continuity of services to the most vulnerable and marginalized women and girls. This has resulted in cancelled activities and reduced working hours. The breakdown of the reconciliation process between the Palestinian National Authority (PNA) and the Defacto government has resulted in the PNA's failure in transferring salaries to civil servants of Gaza, which is fostering instability and increasing the tension within families resulting in increased GBV of the affected families. The UNRWA financial crisis has further destabilized the OPT and especially the Gaza Strip, where 1.3 million out of a population of 1.9 million are refugees and thereby receive assistance. UNRWA is expect to run out of funds in 2018 and humanitarian actors are therefore looking towards the cluster system, including the GBV actors, to step up. In West Bank, the deteriorating economic conditions, especially in Area C and related high unemployment due to the blockade and policies of the Occupying Power are among the main drivers of GBV. Additionally, risk of forced displacement and forced displacement itself increases the pressure on the family and thereby exacerbating GBV in all its forms. Vulnerable women and girls in high risk of GBV includes Bedouins, internally displaced, refugees, persons with disability and poor households. Based on the above, the project will target women, girls, men and boys affected by the recent deteriorating situation in the Gaza Strip. Especially families impacted by the mass demonstrations and poorer households will be targeted, as they are impacted greater by the current situation. In West Bank, the project will target areas identified as having a high vulnerability level in the ‘Protection of Civilian Vulnerability Analysis’ by OCHA.

Activities

Improved availability of safe and high quality multi sectorial services and protection vulnerable women and girls in the affected communities in Gaza Strip and West Bank. - Providing case management services for 5,000 GBV survivors including women with disabilities in West Bank and Gaza Strip.- Provide GBV health care and sexual and reproductive health services for 2,000 GBV survivors including women with disabilities through counselling centers or health clinics- Providing MHPSS services (psychological counselling, and individual and group MHPSS support) for 5,000 GBV survivors through counselling centers or health clinics- Provide GBV health and MHPSS services to 1,000 GBV survivors reached through mobile clinics in Area C- Refer 100 GBV survivors to mental health services and advanced psychosocial support services.- Conduct 20 open recreational days for in total 1,000 GBV survivors.- Providing dignity kits for 5,000 women and girls including women with disabilities (survivors of violence).- Provide counselling sessions and psychosocial support for service providers for 100 GBV service providers. - Provide counselling sessions for 200 perpetrators of GBV through awareness raising sessions and couple therapy- Conduct focus groups interview in to assess the survivor’s satisfaction on the provided services (communication with communities). Focus will be on the fives Governorates of Gaza Strip and areas market as highly vulnerable in the Protection of Civilian database by OCHA in the West Bank. Enhanced the knowledge and awareness of vulnerable groups of women and girls, GBV service providers and non-GBV specialists in the affected communities on the GBV, it consequences and the available services in their communities. - Conduct needs assessment in each governorate of the West Bank and Gaza Strip - with women and girls in risk of GBV to assess their knowledge about GBV and the available services (focus will be on the fives Governorates of Gaza Strip and areas market as highly vulnerable in the Protection of Civilian database by OCHA in the West Bank).- Raise awareness on GBV and available GBV services among 1,000 GBV service providers.- Raise awareness on GBV and available GBV services among 2,000 non-GBV specialists including humanitarian actors, community and religious leaders in highly vulnerable areas- Raise awareness on GBV and available GBV services among 10,000 women and girls in high risk of GBV. - Produce material to be broadcasted through TV, radio, printed material and internet on GBV issues and available services
