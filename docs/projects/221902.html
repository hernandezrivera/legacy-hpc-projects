Project Summary

Healthcare Foundation Organization (HFO) project seeks to ensure that Women and Girls live empowered lives free from Gender-Based Violence in Malakal, Ulang, Nasir and Mayendit counties by addressing the pervasive and critical issues of GBV facing Women and Girls that are exacerbated by ongoing conflict, displacement, and socio-economic instability in South Sudan through improving survivors' access to services such as case management, psychosocial support, cash assistance, legal aid, safety and protection through availability of Women and Girls Safe Spaces and establishment of GBV referral pathways.
The project targets vulnerable populations, particularly GBV survivors, women, girls, and marginalized groups who are at heightened risk of violence, including sexual violence, exploitation, and abuse.
The project will also strengthen community-based protection mechanisms, improve access to lifesaving services, psychosocial support, legal assistance, and safe spaces, and build the capacity of local actors to respond to GBV. The response plan aims to create an enabling environment for the prevention of GBV and ensure survivors receive timely and comprehensive case management.
If HFO receives direct resources to implement the project and empower Right Holders mainly the GBV Survivors in an holistic approach, Women and Girls will improve and increase their understanding about GBV; which will then result in better implementation of the project and increased self-esteem and economic income level of the house hold, increased access to life-saving services, and establishing Women and Girls Safe Spaces at the community structures through community-led concerns in South Sudan.
HFO will conduct GBV prevention to challenge harmful norms through community led program such as SASA approach, community cares program and Engaging Men and Boys through Accountable Practices (EMAP).
HFO will provide dignity kits, including menstrual hygiene material products, to help women and girls of reproductive age maintain their dignity and meet basic needs during crises. The kits promote dignity for women and girls and support to reproductive health and well-being through reusable sanitary pads, hygiene, and sanitary items tailored to the local needs of girls and women in the affected locations.
HFO will also ensure that all staff working in this project receive PSEA training within the first month of project implementation. In addition, all staffs will sign Code of Conduct and they will be held accountable for the ethical commitment.
HFO will provide community awareness sessions on PSEA including the reporting mechanisms that beneficiaries and community members can report concerns.
This project aims to protect women and girls from Gender-Based Violence and harmful cultural practices, increase access to life-saving services, establish Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSS) to provide women and girls with a safe place to gather to access services and information at the local level, and respond to GBV concerns for 12,000 people in Upper Nile state and Unity state. The project will be implemented from 1st January 2025 to 31st December 2025. During this period HFO will use the 5 Ws template to regularly report the implementation status to GBV AoR
In this project, HFO will mainstream other crosscutting topics such as Nutrition, Health, WASH, Cholera, environmental protection, do no harm principle will be ensured throughout the implementation period of the project through proper understanding of the local context and prior communication before distribution of other related project services e. g dignity kits for adolescent girls and women. With the novel cholera pandemic, HFO will ensure that key messages on the pandemic are passed to women during the time of their activities in order contribute to the prevention of cholera outbreak and reduce its negative impacts such as violence on women and girls resulting from stresses created by the global pandemic on households.

1. Needs

Gender-based violence (GBV) is critical in South Sudan, with women and children at high risk of sexual violence, harassment, and domestic violence. WHO (2018) reports that 34% of women aged 15-64 have experienced physical violence and 13.5% sexual violence in their lifetime. UNICEF (2019) found that 65% of women and girls experienced physical and/or sexual violence, and 51% suffered intimate partner violence, often starting before age 18.4 Alarmingly, GBV prevalence has persisted and is nearing epidemic proportions.Specific regions, like Unity State, reported higher incidents of GBV, with 77% involving rape or gang rape, and 23% other forms of violence such as unlawful killing, flogging, beating, sexual molestation, and forced nudity in 2021. From January to May 2024, 43% of PMS respondents reported GBV in their communities.GBV is rampant in South Sudan, driven by harmful social norms and systemic gender inequality, severely affecting the safety and rights of women and girls. The PRMS reported GBV as the main protection risk in 38 per cent of the 2,497 communities surveyed in 2024. The ISNA revealed that 36 per cent of women and girls avoid certain areas due to safety concerns including GBV, while 53 per cent of households’ face barriers in accessing vital services including response to GBV survivorsA survey of GBV experts found a limited availability of core GBV services in 35 per cent of counties. Multilayer data analysis estimates that 2.75 million people will need GBV interventions in 2025 due to increased risks to GBV. The 2025 severity classification highlights the urgent need for action, with 23 counties at Severity 4, 53 at Severity 3 and 3 at Severity 2. Women and girls in Jonglei, Unity and Upper Nile face 'extreme' conditions, with most counties labelled Severity 4. Displaced individuals, returnees and those living in poverty are disproportionately affected, enduring severe physical, sexual and psychological abuse. This situation necessitates urgent GBV prevention, risk mitigation and expanded response services.South Sudan remains a deeply patriarchal society in which custom and tradition entrench the subservience of women and girls to men. The prevailing inequalities, discrimination, unequal power relations, lack of access to resources and very limited opportunities for quality education result in women and girls being at higher risk of sexual and gender-based violence. This situation is intensified by conflicts, displacement due to flooding as well as the fragile economy that impacts gender norms and drives the harmful survival mechanisms for women and girls.GBV is widespread but goes unreported mainly due to the stigma associated with the brutality of those crimes, the dysfunctional court system and perpetrators’ impunity, the fear of retaliation, and the inability to report due to lack of awareness or disability. According to South Sudan GBVIMS data, for the first half of 2024, rape and sexual assault constituted 30% of the reported incidents in the country, while physical violence counted for 36% of the reported incidents. Data on men and boys’ survivors of sexual violence is also limited. With customary courts mainly serving as the only means to seek justice, GBV criminal cases remain essentially unresolved, failing to provide justice and protection to women and girls survivors. Evidence shows that particularly children born out of rape by security forces are marginalized within their communities and are a constant reminder of the conflict and the harm inflicted on children.This project aims to protect women and girls from Gender-Based Violence and harmful cultural practices, increase access to life-saving services, establish Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSS) to provide women and girls with a safe place to gather to access services and information, hygiene and sanitation services at the local level, and respond to GBV concerns for 12,000 people in Upper Nile state and Unity state.
