Project Summary

Since April 2020, AAH delivers health, nutrition and MHPSS activities through mobile clinics in Meme Division. This new project will complete what is currently delivered and aims at answering urgent health needs in the increasingly unstable southwest region of Cameroon, through the provision of emergency primary health care, sexual and reproductive health activities integrating MHPSS to populations affected by the crisis and particularly children under five and pregnant and lactating women.
The proposed project aims to fill critical gaps, providing lifesaving support to the most affected populations by integrating mental health and care practices in a gender-responsive health and nutrition intervention. The project will provide communities with health sensitization and active detection of acute malnutrition and the most common diseases (malaria, respiratory infections, diarrhea…), as well as a comprehensive package of primary health care services (including OTP) and psychosocial support through a mobile clinic and 2 private health facilities
that will be provided with technical and financial support. The mobile clinic (sites:Kokoboma, Talangaye, Kosala, Barombi Kang, Mabonji, Ediki, Mike 1, Dikome and Mile 40) and private health facilities (St. John Fiango-(Maternity) and St John Diongo-(PHC)) will refer complicated cases to secondary health care facilities, particularly a supported stabilisation center for complicated cases of SAM in children U5.
MHPSS activities will be conducted through the mobile clinic and health staff in supported HFs will be trained on MHPSS care practices. The project will provide psychosocial support and healthcare services to SGBV survivors and will refer survivors according to the pathway elaborated by the GBV AoR led by UNFPA in the region.
AAH aims to consolidate the achievements of previous projects by taking into account experiences to adapt the approach. A mobile clinic will be deployed in Meme's division providing primary health care to beneficiaries, with a focus on children under 5, PLW, but is also delivering services to adults for most common pathologies. AAH integrates into its intervention support for functional private structures and the implementation of a community approach through identification and training of CHWs and thus hope to perpetuate the activities of the project. The interventions will contribute to increase the availability of SRHR services through an initial technical training of the supported health facility staff on SRHR. AAH will support the creation or rehabilitation of a dedicated space for SRHR services within the identified health facility and will ensure the provision of reproductive health equipment and supplies based on available data. Counselling on HIV testing and screening, while ensuring the application of standard precautions by medical team, as well as family planning awareness will be provided to all women and girls. A specific MHPSS protocol will be developed to support adolescents (girls and boys) throughout this particular period in a context where they are particularly exposed to adversities.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Due to the crisis, more than 400 000 IDPs and 360 000 returnees were reported within the NWSW as of September 2021, of which 48% are women and 44% are children (51% girls). Meme division, where AAH is implementing a project, is the most affected division in NWSW with 66 300 IDPs (ibid). In the month of August 2022 56 PLWs (31 ANC and 25 PNC) were screened at MC sites, compared to the month of July were 56 (28 ANC and 28PNC) were screened. In September 2022, 168 children 6- 59 months were screened passively at the MC sites (compared to 328 in August). Among those screened, 1 MAM and 1 SAM cases were identified. In September 2022, CHVs actively screened 4761 children 6-59months among which 15 children 6-59 months were recorded as MAM and 00 SAM.The low figures are due to access challenges as incidents and road blockages prevented assistance delivery. Limited movements and coverage scope of humanitarian aid also prevented access to the most vulnerable populations in remote areas. According to August 2021 data, SGBV partners reached 27 448 persons including 8 738 in the SW through psychosocial support, risk mitigation, capacity building of community members, SGBV awareness-raising, women’s empowerment, MHPSS and livelihood activities for female survivors of sexual exploitation. Security challenges and limited funds are continuously hindering NGOs and local organizations to meet basic needs in hard-to-reach areas. Since Sep. 2021, an increase of Covid-19 positivity rate was recorded in NWSW. Just 1.1% of the target population has received the first dose of the vaccine, and 0.3% has received the second dose, as transportation of essential medications, including Covid-19 vaccines, to health facilities were severely disrupted. Pregnant women, children under five, patients with chronic diseases, people living with HIV/AIDS, and victims of trauma are the most affected.As of June 2021, 2.2 million people are affected by the NWSW crisis. Curative consultation for children U5, nutrition services for SAM children under 5 without medical complications, nutrition screening of all children and PLW as well as referral and MHPSS services to adults and children above 6 years will be made accessible through the mobile clinic and supported HFs. The mobile clinic will be deployed in 8 remote sites with free services in order to reduce the negative effects of limited access to healthcare.Support to HF and to a stabilization center will respectively allow OTP and inpatient admission of SAM children U5 with medical complications. In order to strengthen the use of services by women and children, community mobilizers will engage women, men and adolescents during community sensitization and awareness sessions. Gender-sensitive feedback channels will also be strengthened to manage potential complaints and protection concerns.The mobile clinic team will conduct antenatal and postnatal care for PLW and adolescents. Family planning services will also be available for men and women of reproductive age and adolescents. One of the supported secondary level facilities will manage pregnancies, including those with complications. At community level, CHVs will engage with men, women, girls and boys during community sensitization and awareness sessions in order to strengthen decision making regarding SRH, particularly among women and girls. In this context, the selection of CHVs will be gender sensitive and will engage women mobilizers. A gender sensitive feedback mechanism will also be put in place to manage complaints and potential protection concerns.Through this project, AAH aims to implement a mental health and psychosocial support program integrated in the health and nutrition intervention to provide a holistic approach to people in need with specific attention to the most vulnerable, including PLW, adolescents, and people in distress
