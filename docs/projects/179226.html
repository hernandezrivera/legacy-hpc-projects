Project Summary

In this project, ATAA is planning to target children and youth including ECCD in locations as follows:
• Idleb governorate: Dana, Harim, Maaret Tamsrin, Teftnaz, Badama, Jisr Ash-Shugur sub-districts.
• Aleppo governorate: Al Bab, Jarablus, Aghtrin, Atareb, Daret Azze sub-districts.
The schools located in Aleppo are 9 (5 off-camps & 4 in camps) whereas 28 schools located in Idleb (5 off-camps & 23 in camps). The proposed project will target children and youth from the age of 3 till 24 who are in the ECCD, Basic, Secondary Vocational Education (Pre-school, 1st & 12th grade) including CwDs to the most vulnerable. These communities prioritized based on 1) its high and med severity, 2) the concentration of new IDPs, 3) gaps in education and its infrastructure, 4) the security stabilization, 5) accessibility by ATAA 6) high rates of illiteracy and dropout, 7) affected by COVID-19.
In the proposed project, ATAA’s concern is towards the dropped-out children, those at the risk of being dropped out and the newly displaced as a result of frequent displacement and difficult living conditions. The project will provide FE, NFE, ECCD, & TVET opportunities for 15997 (Female;7661, Male; 8020, CWD Female; 127, CWD Male; 189) in addition to 670 educational personnel includes trainers, headteachers, teachers, cleaners and guards (Female; 297, Male; 374), the percentage of students (51% females and 49% males), the percentage of staff (45% females and 55% males).
The project will focus on enhancing the infrastructure by constructing 6 learning centers along with WASH facilities from prefabricated and concrete materials, the other learning spaces based on a rehabilitation need assessment will be provided with minor rehabilitation and school furniture to be friendly environments to achieve safe, equitable and protective education considering COVID-19 measures. The project will be cost-efficient as some of the targeted schools are equipped with furniture, fuel tanks, heaters, book corners, solar panels, textbook, and internet routers, etc.
FE (SIG curriculum) will be provided and NFE (ALP including Curriculum B, Remedial, Catch-up and SLP) to those who have been out of school more than one academic year based on age and period of dropping out, TVET will be given to the dropped out and OOSC who are between 16 and 24 years since they are at the risk of being recruited to armed groups, early marriage and child labour. There will be an MoU signed with ED to recognize the students who got NFE in order to be referred to FE.
CWDs will be provided with tailored activities and facilities to come back to education. School safety plans include fire extinguishers, first aid kits, evacuation maps, drill and sharing information (banner, brochures, roll-up). Teacher recruitment and incentives payment will be strictly applied by ATAA, Regular teachers’ observation, PTA and TLC will be conducted on monthly basis, and training package to teachers (TICC, BLN, CSG, PSS, SLP, Distance Education, SEL, Safe learning). Monthly running cost to schools, COVID-19 schools’ disinfection and PPE provision to each school. Education process (physically, remotely, blended, Radio Education), apply distance education modality via a digital platform, WhatsApp groups. Summer program will include Catch-up-UP and remedial classes, SEL to 1st&4th grades, Base & end-line ASER, one BTL campaign per school to decrease rate of dropping out. ATAA will invest in strengthening community by PTA (online/physical), Provide ECCD, school in box materials, recreational, teachers & students kits in addition to coordination with UNICEF. Winterization (fuel & heaters) and individual support activities will be there also. Also, visual aids, textbooks printing, recreational, ECCD and school in box materials will be provided. Recreational and CP activities will be given and tailored in the targeted locations considering the INEE minimum standards along with teacher students’ ratio will be there.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2021, an estimated 6.9 million people (97 per cent children) need humanitarian education assistance and around 2.45 million children were out of school including 1.6 million children were at risk of dropping out due to the cumulative effect of the conflict, the closure of learning facilities, and/or reduced learning hours due to COVID-19. Also, Covid-19 appearance forced all schools to move between physical, remote, and blended learning modalities. The project is in line with the three objectives: Scale-up safe and equitable access to formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children and youth (aged 3-17 years), enhance the quality of formal and non-formal education and strengthen the capacity of the education and communities.ATAA has collected secondary data in sub-districts (Atareb, Daret Azze, Jarablus, Al Bab sub-districts, Dana, Harim, Salqin, Teftnaz sub-district) to reflect how far the education status has been affected by the current conflict. The main reasons behind the non-enrolment of different school-aged children are low income, child labor and the multi displacement.The proposed project will go in line with the prioritized activities by cluster in reference to the HNO to address the requirements of education in formal, non-formal, ECCD and vocational education such as: monthly incentives, textbooks printing, school in box materials including stationery and recreational kits, school rehabilitation, WASH facilities, provision of heating materials, safety school planning including fire extinguishers and first aid kits, training, monthly PTA & TLC meetings & BTLs.

5 - Implementation of activities

ATAA will carry out the direct implementation through coordinating with all stakeholders (Education Cluster & Directorates, local communities, and parents). Different curricula: FE & NFE (SIG curriculum, Curriculum B, catch up classes) in addition to ECCD & TVET. Construction and rehabilitation will be very effective considering the schools as friendly environments for all students, especially for CWDs in which ramps, latrine will be equipped with special tools considering COVID-19 requirements & school disinfection and COVID-19 protective measures.Enrollment of students along with BTL campaigns, provide students, recreational, ECCD and teacher kits, winterization, individual support, transportation will be provided. Baseline and end-line ASER will be conducted to 10% of students. Conducting a complete assessment to the teachers and provide the needed training as TICC, BLN, CSG, PSS, SLP, Distance Education, SEL, Safe learning. Hygiene promotion to increase the awareness of both students and communities. CP activities and the awareness raising sessions will be integrated such as PSS, SEL, referral mechanism will be activated at each school. TLCs & PTAs will be activated on a monthly basis. Feedback and complaints mechanism in schools using suitable methodologies will be activated, printing textbooks based on need assessment.MEAL: Checklist to monitor the construction of TECs, WASH facilities and rehabilitation of the existing ones and to verify their quality and suitability for CWDs, school safety will be measured by school safety checklist, trainings will be measured by both KAP survey & training evaluation and PDM will be conducted after each distribution, Physical and distance education modality will be monitored.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Risk: A member of the ATAA team uses their position of authority as a method of exploiting vulnerable populations. Their actions cause significant long-term damage to this individual or group. All trust between ATAA and the affected community is damaged. ATAA face severe reputational damage and loss of funding. Mitigation: All staff who join ATAA Project are going to a thorough and transparent recruitment process. References are obtained for all staff during the recruitment process. The Project Team will sign ATAA PSEA and CSG Policies ATAA have developed PSEA, Code of Conduct, and Child Safeguarding policies and mechanisms in place to ensure every effort is made to protect all Education/WASH and Protection beneficiaries. ATAA’s PSEA Mechanism is designed around four core components: Awareness Raising (for staff regarding power dynamics, risk and vulnerabilities, etc.), Prevention (awareness regarding the existing controls which are in place and how PSEA is prevented), Reporting (how participants can report and what are the specific steps which need to be taken regarding SEA cases) and Response (how and what ATAA will do when cases of SEA are reported). A similar mechanism is available for CSG where the staff are familiar with how to approach and deal with CSG cases of child abuse, exploitation, or violence. All community centers/schools and mobile units’ staff will sign ATAA CSG and PSEA Policies to ensure a safe environment for all Project beneficiaries
