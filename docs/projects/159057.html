Project Summary

Considering the need for humanitarian response to education, the project will establish psychosocial and education remedial program at four schools within the H2 area in Hebron to counter the impact of traumatisation, low educational performance, and risk of drop-out. The project will support in establishing an after-school psychosocial and education remedial program.
PCS and its partner "Yes Theatre" will carry out a 9 month project which will contribute in reducing suffering, improve education performance, and improve children mental health in 4 targeted schools in the H2 area. The selected schools will be supported to be more responsive to potential emergency through remedial programme and psychosocial services. PCS and Yes theater will work closely with the children in psychosocial programme where the remedial programme will be under the supervision of the Ministry of Education.
Based on identified need and evidenced lack of attainment in particular subjects, students will be matched with teachers to provide remedial classes. This has been a recurring request from targeted schools, who voiced the concern that lagging students might end up dropping out from school.
Structured extracurricular activities will also be rolled out to include expressive arts activities, music, theatre and drama, and photography. These activities will be linked to outputs that the students will produce. Facilitators will train students on how to use expressive arts and photography to document violations they and their peers face in reaching schools, particularly after the TIPH departed their presence from H2 area.
Psychosocial mobilizers will be supporting students and children in coping strategies, educational catchup, and advanced informal learning. Collective outreach activities will be supported, moving away from focusing on individual clinically based diagnoses to focusing on holistic, broad-based preventive programmes that promote resilience and develop coping strategies across the entire affected group. Protection mechanism will be put in place to ensure students have a safe and comforting environment.
PCS and its partner YES Theatre are well positioned to deliver this project. From its field office in Bethlehem, PCS will be providing close and hands-on management to the project through its project coordinator. It will continue to coordinate closely with the Ministry of Education, the directorate of education in Hebron, the 4 targeted schools, and partner Yes Theatre to ensure full support to the project. Yes Theatre will deliver on major components of the psycho-social programme, assigning 6 out of the 12 drama teachers from Yes theatre pool of experts. The selection of these teachers will be based on Yes Theatre professional criteria.
PCS has excellent grassroots experience, working with the most marginalized to improve their social, economic, and mental wellbeing. Its office in Bethlehem has become a main spot for receiving and acting upon human rights violations. Over the past 7 years, PCS has worked with and established excellent working relationships with more than 20 CBOs in the Hebron district, who we aspire to be engaged based on particular needs of the project. The project coordinator will be based in the field office of Yes Theatre in Hebron, with a masters degree in Psychosocial and community interventions, and a certified trainer in using drama in psychosocial interventions.
Yes Theatre (with a Hebron-based office) is a community theatre that uses artistic performances, drama and theatre workshops, performances, capacity building training for teachers in theatre based techniques, and activities of cultural exchange for community development. Yes Theatre mission is to contribute to improve the psychosocial well-being of Palestinian children and youth through the use of Drama and Theatre. YT's activities in the Hebron district annually reach an audience of 30,000 school students.

1 - Needs

The education 2020 HNO identified a general lack of school-based psychosocial support for affected children in Area C, Hebron H2 and East Jerusalem. The first half of 2019 witnessed 112 education-related incidents in the oPt, a significant increase compared to the 118 incidents of interference with education affecting 23,188 children, in all of 2018. In the H2 area of Hebron, the withdrawal of over 60 international TIPH monitors in early 2019 has left a gap in human rights monitoring and protection especially for school children. H2 was the most affected in terms of violations against education in 2019, with thirty-five schools in the area serving 5,561 males and 7,167 females. Apart from the physical injury and related absence from school, these incidents impact children socially and psychologically by creating anxiety, trauma and harm to their sense of safety, which ultimately makes learning more difficult. Despite the fact that there is some evidence that children adapt to the aforementioned situations, this does not mean that continued exposure to violence does not have a major detrimental impact on the children’s psychological and social development as well as on their education. Nationally, there are reports showing that in the areas most affected by the conflict, 75% of adults think children are experiencing greater emotional problems and behavioral change. That finding is supported by two separate opinion polls that found that some 80% of parents think that their children’s behavior had changed (Birzeit University Development Studies Programme Survey, 2001a, 2001b; Ministry of Social Affairs [MOSA], 2001). In areas of increased tension like H2, students can be stressed and find it difficult to concentrate on their education. In H2 area, parents told counsellors that their children manifested a series of problems including repeated nightmares, bedwetting, insomnia and irregular or changed sleeping patterns. Some children also withdraw from friends and family, engage in risk-taking behaviour, rebel and reject authority, become aggressive or depressed and pay less attention to personal care. Children also lost interest in schooling, preferring to stay at home watching television, or doing their own thing. Mothers interviewed noted a marked deterioration in their children’s achievements at school. Teachers and heads also noted that the achievement of children had been much affected, and this showed up because they knew that their colleagues in H1 schools were managing to cover much more of the syllabus than they themselves were managing to do in H2 schools. The future of the Palestinian people is bound to the future of the Palestinian child; this makes the present of Palestinian childhood of particular importance. This also means that the physical, psychosocial and education structure of new generation is the core of the entire Palestinian people’s future and is what will determine the fate of the long-term struggle against the Israeli Occupation. The Israeli occupation has always made efforts to aggravate the emotional and psychosocial suffering of the Palestinian child and break his spirit through its arbitrary measures such as physical maltreatment and road movement restrictions forced daily upon children, besides what his family and friends are subject to, such as murder, injury and destruction of property. Moreover, the Palestinian child suffers from poverty and suppression due to severe closures and curfews. In other words, the Israel-Palestinian conflict is costing the Palestinian children much, so that many of them are paying the price with their lives. Children living under harsh conditions lose their childhood and their innocence, just as they lose hope and joy in their lives. In light of these circumstances, there is a dire need to establish school-based psychosocial support and remedial education for students and school staff affected by education-related violations.

2 - Activities

The loss of school days due to curfew conditions, the difficulty of focusing on education in a situation of conflict and violence, the psychological and social distress related to the daily experience of violence or expectation of violence, as well as the parents’ role in instilling coping mechanisms to support their children’s education, are all issues that will be addressed through the project activities. PCS, Yes Theatre, and the directorate of education together agreed on this responsive intervention. The project will set up an education remedial and psychosocial school-based center that will target two girls' schools and two boys' schools in the H2 area. Two school-based centers will be supported, one in a males' school and another in a female school. A needs assessment for targeted students will be carried out, both school-based and household based to identify the most pressing issues that need to be considered in designing the center’s activities. The remedial classes will address the student shortcomings and areas of student academic under-performance. The remedial classes will be in the form of educationally supportive interventions focused over a short period of time, and aimed at those students who had fallen backwards in the coverage and integration of the curriculum due to their own learning difficulties as a result of the situation in H2 area. Classes will be organized after regular school hours. Another important aspect of the educational remedial program is providing orientation to parents, providing them with tools and tips on coping mechanisms that allows for effective support to their children in pursuing their education. In addition to remedial classes, the center will provide psycho-social support programmes. These will be aim to instill informal learning in a social context marked by activities, games, music, photography and theatre that are both educationally sound and enjoyable at the same time. The project will support a total of six psychosocial mobilizers, supervised by a project coordinator from PCS. Before rolling out the services, the facilitators will be briefed in child protection issues and requirements. Also, they will be required to attend training for each specific program they would be implementing with the children and parents. The Psychosocial mobilizers will be supporting students and children in coping strategies, beside the teachers in remedial class where they will help the student in educational catchup, and advanced informal learning. Collective outreach activities will be supported, moving away from focusing on individual clinically based diagnoses to focusing on holistic, broad-based preventive programmes that promote resilience and develop coping strategies across the entire affected group. Protection mechanism will be put in place to ensure students have a safe and comforting environment. Prizes will be given as further incentives to students. Other informal education activities will also be carried out, to include trips and visits outside H2 area. Each month, a major outreach activity will be held that includes plenty of physical and recreational activities. Both modalities in the delivery of the remedial programme and psychosocial programmes will involve teachers as well as student helpers from higher grade levels (referred to as mobilizers).

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The project has done a sensitivity analysis with the Directorate of Education and school principals of the 4 targeted schools, where we applied basic requirements in do no harm principals during selecting the schools and designing the implementation process. During implementation, PCS team and Yes theater teams will apply basic requirements such as ensuring transparent selection criteria of children. Each children will voluntarily participate in the programme. All children will be treated in respectfully way without any discrimination. All the material which will be used will be relevant to and friendly used for children. The activities will be inclusive and sensitive to risk, ensuring that the location of the centers for after-school programme will include safe and protective routes. All the facilitator and project team will be accountable for doing no harm and finally all the project team will sign consent form of child protection protocol which focus on Do no harm principles and every aspect of our work to protect children and the communities they live in.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project will establish an education remedial and psycho-social programs that will target the most in need. The schools, teachers, and parents will play a leading role in identifying who are the most at-risk students.
