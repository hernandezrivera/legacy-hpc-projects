Project Summary

Throughout 2022 and 2023, Violet organization is planning to implement comprehensive livelihoods support interventions applying a variety of modalities to address the needs of the different groups with proper modality.
The proposed project will effectively contribute to supporting the self-reliance of most vulnerable crisis-affected groups in NWS and improving the livelihoods and value chains through income generation activities as follows:
1. Cash for work for crops and vegetable value chains.
2. Vocational training for agribusinesses and Food processing
3. Business grants for agribusiness and food processing
4. Agriculture inputs provision through various modalities (in kind, voucher, cash for inputs) with relevant training for farmers on GAP and climate-smart agriculture
5. Provision and Installation of solar powering irrigations systems
6. Supporting assets building and assets protection through the provision of livestock, animal feed, and fodder production support with relevant training for technicians and livestock breeders
7. Provide emergency livestock treatment and training for veterinary services,
8. Establish/strengthen the capacity for the provision of essential services for local communities including an early warning system.
Out of the total amount requested, 596,092 USD is already secured.

4 - Needs

According to the drafted 2022 Syria HNO, the food security situation has steadily worsened over the past four years across Syria. By mid-2021, an additional segment of the population became food insecure - totalling 12.8 million people – meaning two of three Syrians are in need of some form of food and agricultural assistance, with an additional 1.8 million at risk of falling into food insecurity. The increase in food insecurity is indicative of a population that is increasingly vulnerable, with many having exhausted their capacity to cope, due to a range of factors, including the degradation of livelihoods and increased cost of living. Other contextual drivers have also played a role, namely climactic and human-made shocks affecting natural resources - particularly water, continued unilateral coercive measures and annual wheat deficits which can now only be offset by increases to the price of basic food items. Needs related to agricultural livelihoods therefore remain considerable. Unilateral coercive measures have worsened matters for millions of Syrians, both directly and indirectly, by affecting the availability and quality of agricultural inputs and equipment, resulting in adverse effects on crop and livestock production. Farmers also suffer from limited access to credit, damaged infrastructure, and limited access to relevant early warning information, all exacerbated by limited and poorly distributed rainfall in the 2020/21 agricultural season.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Risk of discrimination if the service is provided to a specific group and others are excludedLikelihood: Medium; Impact: HighBefore implementation, Violet conducts assessments and selects vulnerable communities based on severity; selection criteria (eligibility/vulnerability) transparently communicated through the AAP team. AAP team has access to service mapping to communicate informed with communities about alternative services.2) PWD or at-risk groups might be excluded from assistance or distribution.Likelihood: Low; Impact: HighInformation sharing and distribution points are suitable for PWDs, conduct AAP sessions to inform about requesting special assistance (transportation/delivery) for PWD/elderly/pregnant BNFs, consultation with stakeholders to identify excluded beneficiaries, training staff on inclusion.3) Risk of GBV to and from or in overcrowded distributionsLikelihood: Medium; Impact: HighSelection of suitable distribution (time, location, accessibility), referral of special cases, avoid long queues and allow clear space for waiting, operate with diff. locations.4) At-risk group do not receive needed servicesLikelihood: Medium; Impact: MediumCoordination with relevant sectors to avoid duplications, collaborate with local protection team to ensure equitable access to services, consult BNFs in sex-segregated FGDs, systematically use Washington Group Questions as tool to include disability in selection, consider intersectionality in targeting


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Violet targets PSEA risk through a) training on PSEA for staff, BNFs, vendors; b) integrating PSEA in AAP with safe and accessible complaints mechanism; c) appointment of PSEA focal points in each location to handle reports of misconduct, d) support to survivors through case management, e) regular information of affected community on their entitlements. Potential risks: 1) Request of favors, e.g. SEA, for selecting BNFs to the trainingMitigation: clear information on the provided services in terms of quality and quantity and that nothing is expected in return in any case, emphasise on complaints mechanisms, provision of multiple channels to raise complaints, referral to inter-agency hotlines as alternative, gender-balanced staff during training, also in MEAL observation. 2) SEA misconduct from staff to selected beneficiaries or staffMitigation: ensure regular staff training on PSEA and how to engage with beneficiaries, communication of PSEA policy and complaints mechanisms to BNFs, ensure diverse and gender balanced project and MEAL teams, assign sutiable training sites with gender-segregated WASH facilities, identify at-risk households and provide special assistance to PWDs- Violet will conduct sex-segregated FGDs and KIIs to ensure the participation of women during all phases of the project, especially in the design of the activities and monitoring


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption 1: Security situation worsensRisk1: prevent the movement of Violet staff and delaying project implementationMitigation1: VO established SOPs in place, staff trained and an experienced and dedicated security team will continue to monitor trends to enable rapid and appropriate changes to implementation plans;Assumption2– Changes in local power structures.Risk2: delay of programme delivery.Mitigation2: Ongoing communication with actors operational across Violet’s area of intervention and continued adherence to acceptance strategies built on community and stakeholder understanding of Violet’s programming.Assumption3: The money transfer agent is following all Violet policies, instructions, and methodologyRisk3: Diversion of cash when using money transfer agent (MTA)Mitigation3: The MTA will be requested to provide the beneficiaries’ signatures and beneficiaries’ original vouchers alongside the invoice requesting payment. The transfer agent will not be reimbursed unless he gives the original vouchers and the signed lists.Risk4: crossing borders are closed due to COVID-19 or the suspension of the UN security council resolutionMitigation4: the project was designed in a flexible way to ensure the continuous delivery of humanitarian assistance as all project materials are available in Syria
