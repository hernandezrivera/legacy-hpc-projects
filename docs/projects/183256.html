Project Summary

In line with the FS sector-specific objectives, ACTED will provide life-saving food security and basic needs assistance to the most vulnerable Lebanese and migrant individuals being impacted by the ongoing economic crisis. ACTED has designed its intervention to complement ongoing/planned assistance across the most affected locations with pre-existing vulnerabilities sector-wide. Tapping into the overall coordination mechanisms (WFP and LHF partners), this programme proposes area-based and punctual assistance to 7,500 individuals most in need in the governorates of Akkar and Beirut-Mount Lebanon (BML) and the urban area of Tripoli. Targeting an estimated 4,125 women and girls and 3,375 men and boys, ACTED will provide:
-cash-based assistance for the acute food needs of 2,000 vulnerable households in Akkar, BML, and Tripoli
-cash-based assistance for the basic needs of the same 2,000 vulnerable households in Akkar, BML, and Tripoli
ACTED will be targeting areas of high vulnerability that lack current full coverage by the actions of other actors in the sector. Akkar has one of the highest poverty rates and food insecurity rates in the nation, with 55% of households indicating difficulty in meeting basic needs, well above the national average (40%). As of 2021, Akkar has the highest rate of household food shortages in the nation, with 71% indicating that they were forced to reduce food intake in response to acute financial need. Municipalities within Akkar will be selected based on assessment of acute need and coordination with sector actors to avoid duplication of coverage. ACTED will build upon knowledge of the context it has built from a presence in the region since 2015, which has included work such as cash assistance programing, three years of EU-funded WASH programming in 9 Akkar districts, as well as ongoing social stability and livelihoods work funded by ILO. ACTED will also target Tripoli, as it is an area of high population density with high poverty rates, particularly in the neighborhoods of Tebbeneh and Kobbe. Implementation will be aided by the experience and strong relationships with local actors ACTED has gained during previous livelihoods, protection, and basic services programming in these neighborhoods. While BML has a lower rate of food insecurity, it contains neighborhoods of acute need and high pre-existing vulnerability. ACTED was at the forefront of the emergency response to the Beirut Blast, and in its 2020 planning targeted affected neighborhoods of Metn (Bourj Hammoud, Basta, Karantina, and Karm elZeitoun) with cash assistance, cash for work, and in-kind support to damaged businesses. In 2021, ACTED will build upon this work by continuing emergency cash support to these neighborhoods, as many HHs continue to face difficulty in meeting basic necessities due to vulnerabilities predating the Blast. Additionally the Metn area is home to a substantial number of migrant HHs. ACTED will also target Nabaa, an underprivileged neighborhood proximate to the Blast and home to a high number of migrant households, and Ouzaii, a low socioeconomic neighborhood located further from the epicenter of the Blast, but which is still struggling with the compounding effect the Blast had upon its economy. ACTED has established a presence in the areas and working ties with municipal officials through its ongoing EU-funded WASH intervention targeting drinking water access. Among the 2000 HHs targeted, ACTED will be aiming to reach 500 migrant households primarily in the Metn area. ACTED will provide the assistance in accordance with sector guidelines on assistance per individual and household level, through money transfer agencies or proxies. As FSS co-chair and previous Basic Assistance co-chair, ACTED will ensure each aspect of cash programming will align with sector harmonized packages and adhere to sector guidance, and will apply a protection lens to the selection of beneficiaries and project implementation.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

1. Cash-based food assistance: ACTED will identify 2,000 HHs through referrals from local authorities/CBOs in locations of current implementation/existing relationships, WFP and FSS partners, and door-to-door outreach. Assistance will be granted to HHs not covered by WFP/ partner assistance and prioritized if Low/ No Income HHs; Single-Parent HHs (especially female-headed); with elderly dependents; with members who are disabled/suffering medical conditions. ACTED will work in coordination with IOM and other actors (Caritas, IOCC) on outreach/referral cases for the migrant HHs to avoid overlap of coverage/ maximize reach. ACTED will provide each individual in a HH 300,000LBP per month for 11 months. Beneficiaries with a valid ID will receive cash through a financial service provider (FSP). Beneficiaries without valid ID will a) be able to withdraw the cash using a proxy of their choice or b) be provided with an ACTED beneficiary ID and receive the cash from project staff. The same will apply to the beneficiaries with mobility challenges who cannot access FSPs. ACTED will be responsible for distribution, follow up, financial reconciliation, and regular Post Distribution Monitoring in accordance with the sector tools.A2: Cash-based basic need assistance: All 2000 households selected in Activity 1 will be further supported through cash assistance for basic needs. ACTED will provide the selected beneficiary households with 400,000LBP per month for 11 months via FSPs or proxies.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Protection risk mitigation measures will be integrated throughout the project to ensure the distinct needs of women, men, girls and boys are reflected. ACTED adopts a zero-tolerance approach towards sexual exploitation and abuse, or any form of child abuse or exploitation, with policies encouraging prevention; promoting detection; and setting clear investigation procedures/mechanisms of sanction. Its SEA policy applies to all staff, as well as implementing partners, sub-grantees, beneficiaries, consultants, or associated entities. Within the program, selection will prioritize female-headed households or those with members with additional vulnerability. Data collected throughout the project will be disaggregated by sex, age and gender during assessment, monitoring and evaluation. ACTED staff are trained on referrals, PSEA, protection during COVID-19. ACTED will utilize existing intersectoral referral mechanisms to channel any relevant beneficiaries to suitable protection opportunities.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

The project will promote the use of best practices in accountability and transparency of its interventions, in line with Common Humanitarian Standards principles and Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) through formal Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM). ACTED’s CRM is well-staffed and operates at a high capacity, closing 90-100% of responses within the appropriate timeframe and diligently adhering to the InterAgency Minimum Standards on Referrals. Complaints will be confidentially recorded in a country-level register under restricted access. Maximum response time to a complainant is 15 working days, with the most urgent/serious complaints prioritized (same day pathway activation). Multiple means of communication ensure that vulnerable individuals – especially those with limited mobility – can provide feedback with total confidentiality. Project teams will ensure that CRM modalities are widely communicated to beneficiaries and showcased in relevant visibility material.
