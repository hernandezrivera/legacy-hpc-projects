Project Summary

In line with the objectives of the Early Recovery Sector, the project is designed to restore access to livelihoods through emergency employment for IDP's and vulnerable host communities in Jere and Maiduguri LGA's. Restoring access to livelihoods will be reached through provision of emergency employment opportunities by a Cash for Work(CFW) program. The activities include the rehabilitation, repair and maintenance of community infrastructure such as the WASH infrastructure built by ZOA. Furthermore, Cash for Work interventions to address solid waste management in highly contaminated areas and de-sludging of toilets especially in areas affected by the most recent cholera epidemic. The project will target 1000 women or men selected based on their vulnerability and willingness to work for the community. From every HH only one active member can participate. Therefore 1000 selected women/men will be engaged through the cash for work project and through this will provide income for approximate 6.0000 individuals (average household size of 6). The targeted areas will be IDP,s in Host Communities in Bolori 1 and 2 and Shuwari 1 and 2 in Maiduguri LGA. For Jere LGA, ZOA will target the villages of Zabarmari and Gongulong were many IDP,s live in open spaces in between the houses of the host community.

Cash for work will support vulnerable people in recovering their livelihood and creates sustainability in crises affected communities. As 52% of the IDP,s residing in host communities don't have any income, Cash for Work is well suited to meet the livelihood needs for these people especially when it comes to meeting the demands on housing, food security and purchasing productive assets.
Self-earned cash income allow beneficiaries to make their own choices, yet experience shows that extremely food insecure households typically elect to spend a significant proportion of their cash on food. A post distribution survey conducted by ZOA in Gongulong 2018, revealed that 80% of the cash is spent on food, 5% on fuel, 6% in savings/business enterprise, 4% on debt repayment and 5% on health/education/NFi and communication
CFW will help the beneficiaries in having ownership in the work that they do and the money they will earn with the activity. It will also compliment the activities which are implemented by ZOA to support the communities . Special emphasis is placed on female heads of households and households with children under five, as well as elderly or disabled individuals.
Concerning PSEA, Standard operating procedures are in place for submission and receipt of complaints, reporting, investigation and victim assistance. ZOA has a focal person place at its office in Maiduguri and for the field offices. ZOA staff members have received training on how to prevent and respond to acts of sexual exploitation and abuse. ZOA is part of the PSEA network in Maiduguri. As a humanitarian emergency organization, ZOA is committed to Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) commitment by mainstreaming protection in community based interventions and taking in account the Age , Gender, Diversity (AGD) inter-sectorial guidelines. In ZOA projects, beneficiary participation, feedback and complaint mechanisms are integrated in all aspects of the program starting from the inception phase. Beneficiaries are represented in Community Committees through the project cycle which provides channels of communication with the beneficiaries to disseminate information about procedures and processes that affect them, so that they are able to make informed decisions and choices.

Needs

Humanitarian agencies have scaled up their responses to the humanitarian situation in the North East of Nigeria. However, as more agencies are setting up activities in hard to reach areas in formally inaccessible areas in the North East, a large portion of the population in better to reach areas such as Jere and Maiduguri LGA's remain in need of Early Recovery infrastructure assistance and other basic services. Many households have exhausted their stocks and are lacking the income to buy their food and other commodities on the markets. Possibilities for wage labor are limited and access to commodities and services are limited due to above average market prices and expensive public services. Markets in Jere and Maiduguri LGA's are well stocked and functioning but vulnerable households have limited household purchasing power to access these markets. Although humanitarian assistance have been delivered to Jere and Maiduguri LGA,s for out of camp IDP,s and vulnerable host populations, , there remain unmet needs. Food and other humanitarian assistance has been provided but irregularly which results in the most vulnerable of the affected population unable to recover from the shocks they experienced especially among IDPs and female headed households. Therefore, vulnerable people among the communities will still require support as the crises is ongoing and many households don't have to ability to build up a sustainable livelihood Addressing immediate needs remains an important part of the response at this stage, but the need to focus on recovery based initiatives must be a major objective to restore sustainable communities and restore livelihoods that have been devastated by the conflict. Therefore, ZOA proposes early recovery assistance in the form of Cash for Work activities for the most vulnerable through maintenance and operation of Wash infrastructure as well as solid waste and de-sludging activities.

Activities

The beneficiaries are crisis affected families, consisting of IDPs in Host Communities and returnees living in camp and non-camp settings and vulnerable host community members. ZOA will target food insecure HH regardless of their status, although the targeted areas will show that the majority of the selected beneficiaries are IDPs in Host Communities. Targeted communities will make an initial list of vulnerable households through community based approaches based on their perception of household vulnerability. The households on this list will interviewed by ZOA staff who will verify family size, actual residence and state of vulnerability. Based on the outcomes of the FCS, CSI and Socio-Economic indicators combined with a motivation on their willingness and proven capabilities/ experience to be able to enter the program successfully, a selection will be made of beneficiaries entering the programs. The proposed activities will be implemented in Jere and Maiduguri LGA,s. ZOA has a well established presence in Jere and Maiduguri through implementing WASH, food security and livelihood interventions for donors such as ECHO, UN OCHA, DRA, CFGB and FFP . Based on the needs as been outlined above, ZOA proposes early recovery assistance in the form of Cash For Work activities for the most vulnerable. Output 1: The most vulnerable conflict affected families are provided with Cash for Work activities to meet their basic livelihood needs. Activities: 1.Security assessments/ protection/ safety measures 2.Assessments of construction, repair and maintenance needs in targeted areas 3.Conduct participatory targeting of beneficiaries in affected communities including stakeholders 4.Generate CFW participation/volunteers list 5.Establish complaints handling and feedback mechanism 6.Mainstream inclusion and protection in planned activities 7.Plan for logistics, procurement, supply chain and storage facilities 9.Verification ID distribution of beneficiaries/ registration on networks for payments 11.Consistent monitoring of protection/GBV concerns 12.Continuous monitoring on progress of CFW activities 13.Receiving CFW payments 14.Milestones surveys 15.End of project survey In areas were IDPs are among host populations many latrines, bathrooms and water point have been set up during the emergency interventions which are currently unused due to lack of maintenance or neglect. Toilets are filled to an extent of dysfunctionality. Stagnant water around water points is source of contamination and waterborne disease transmission. Bathrooms are unasfe for females due to lack of doors. Uncollected waste remains a health hazard. ZOA plans to rehabilitate 200 blocks of latrines and bathrooms in the identified communities which including de-sludging , repairing lining , and construction of a new superstructure water points with drainage facilities. Hand pumps will be rehabilitated with new pipes and handles. Water infrastructure and networks will be repaired in schools, clinics and community centers A solid waste collection system will be set up and CfW staff will be trained to provide hygiene promotion messages on the prevention of waterborne diseases such as cholera.
