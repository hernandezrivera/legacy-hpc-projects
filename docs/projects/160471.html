Project Summary

NP's overall objective is to effectively respond, prevent and reduce protection concerns of conflict affected communities, as well as build peaceful co-existence in South Sudan. This objective will contribute to HRP Protection Cluster Objective 1: Ensure the provision of critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas, Objective 2: Prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience, Objective 3: Enable durable solutions for IDPs and other population, and Objective 4: Enhance protection assessment and monitoring to inform protection and overall humanitarian response. Activities are designed to increase the safety and security of civilians affected by violent conflict through deterrence, protection through presence, strengthening local protection mechanisms, community-based psychosocial support, conflict mitigation, protection mainstreaming, and community dialogues that also contributes to social cohesion. Project activities are implemented in both static locations, as well as through mobile response missions that allow National and International Protection Officers to respond to the most urgent needs throughout the country. Specific and unique needs of women, men, boys and girls are incorporated into all interventions.

1 - Needs

Following the signing of the Revitalized Agreement (R-ARCSS) in late 2018, the consolidation of peace and implementation of the peace agreement remains fragile. In this context, 7.1 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance, with the majority experiencing a complex mix of acute needs and underlying vulnerabilities. Access to services has eroded with ongoing violence, insecurity and economic decline. Rape and other types of sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) are pervasive but are largely unreported. Additionally, protection concerns related to possible increases in recruitment and SGBV linked to establishment of cantonments, especially near civilian spaces, is on the rise. In regard to the implementation of R-ARCSS, the question of peace spoilers and related protection risks are to be considered. For example, armed confrontations throughout the country including very recent clashes in Unity and Bahr el Ghazal illustrates discontentment towards the peace agreement by some factional armed groups, and presents continued protection risks to civilians, including displacement, grave child and human rights violations, forced recruitment and SGBV. Additionally, non-signatory actors remain in control of key locations in the Equatorias where continuous troop movement and armed confrontations take place. For example, recent armed conflict along the Maridi corridor, about 15 miles from Yei town, continued to displace civilians to Yei town in September. Fighting sustained for weeks and could still be ongoing as humanitarian access to the area continues to be restricted. The number of displaced communities is increasing and, as of September 2019, approx. 1600 individuals (majority women and children) are displaced. In most parts of the country, communal conflict is the primary threat to safety and security of civilians. For example, in the Upper Nile, Jonglei, Lakes State and Warrap, ethnic tensions, cattle raiding and revenge killing contributes to cyclical and sustained multigenerational inter- and intra-communal conflict, exacerbated by the proliferation of small arms and light weapons and breakdown of traditional justice mechanisms. For example, as recently as September 2019, escalating violence related to cattle raiding raised serious security concerns in Rumbek and surrounding areas, including a surge in casualties and injuries. Additionally, cattle raiders have been increasingly targeting military and police forces contributing to wider insecurity that has an upwards destabilizing impact and has led to increased protection needs for affected communities. Likewise, in WBeG, multiple episodes of violence in the beginning of 2019 between pastoralists and farmers displaced many people to locations in Jur River and Wau. As the dry season approaches, increased cattle movement is expected, with partners preparing for a rise in protection needs as a result of violence relating due to cattle raiding, with vulnerable and marginalized groups particularly at risk, including women and children. In contrast, hubs of stability in Yambio, Rumbek, Juba, Bor and Torit represent areas where post-conflict development is currently being encouraged. In these areas, as well as other pockets of stability throughout the country, such as some areas of Wau, opportunities for stabilization, social cohesion and peacebuilding are ripe. Nevertheless, some conflict dynamics and current patterns of violence contribute to the fragility of this stability and partner organizations must be prepared for augmented humanitarian needs.NP is currently operating from the following static sites: Rumbek (Lakes State), Yei, Juba (Central Equatoria), Aweil (Northern Bahr el Ghazal), Wau (Western Bahr el Ghazal), Ganyiel, Bentiu POC, Beyond Bentiu Response (Unity), Aburoc, Ulang (Upper Nile), Lankien (Jonglei), Mundri, Yambio (Western Equatoria). In 2020 NP will expand activities to: Mvolo (Western Equatoria), Bor, Pibor, (Jonglei), Torit (Eastern Equatoria).

2 - Activities

(1) Direct Protection, Prevention & Risk Mitigation for Civilians at Imminent Risk (Links to HRP Strategic Obj1,2&3)a) Direct protection for civilians vulnerable to violence, including protective presence, accompaniment & patrolling in insecure areas to deter direct attacks on civilians. There will be a particular focus on women/girls who are accessing aid or other resources to address the basic needs of their families/communities: accompanying women & girls when they travel to markets or to collect firewoodb) Identify children, predominantly boys & young men, at risk of recruitment, implement protection strategies to prevent recruitment by armed groupsc) Carry out regular assessments to identify high-risk areas & factors exposing civilians to conflict & violence. Assessment reports will be shared with the cluster & sub-cluster coordinatorsd) Conduct conflict mitigation & dialogue facilitation activities to reduce tensions & prevent violence in communitiese) Engage in timely & effective rumor control to prevent unnecessary displacement & reduce communal tensionsf) Verify & report observed grave violations of child rights through MRM.(2) Improve Protection Response Services & Community-based Psychosocial Support (Links to HRP Strategic Obj1,2&3) a) Strengthen/Support the establishment & utilization of referral pathways to medical, psychosocial & aid services.b) Provide safe space for vulnerable boys and girls through the provision of Child Safe Spacesc) Conduct protection workshops for law enforcement, judiciary, traditional & local leadershipd) Facilitate referrals & support safe access to services for individuals requiring individual protection assistance (3) Improve the Protective Capacities of Communities (Links to Strategic HRP Obj2&3) a) Conduct early warning & early response assessments and trainings in high-risk communitiesb) Develop, train & mentor community-based protection mechanisms such as Women's Protection Teams and Youth Protection Teams in all sites to facilitate community participation in peace & security. Coach teams to carry out protection identification & response activities as well as build their relationship & influence with local/traditional authoritiesc) Facilitate monthly community security meetings between beneficiaries, security actors & humanitarian actorsd) Conduct capacity building workshops for community members to strengthen local capacities for violence prevention, protection & responsee) Strengthen youth capacity for protection & response to violence, including gender-based violencef) Conduct advocacy & awareness-raising campaigns on pertinent protection & social cohesion issues with all community members(4) Enhance partner protection mainstreaming practices (Links to HRP Strategic Obj1&2)a) Provide on-the-ground protection support prior to, during & immediately following aid distributions. This includes working with agencies prior to the distribution to ensure that the layout of the distribution is set up in such a way as to minimize the risk of violence as much as possible; intervening to deescalate tensions, prevent violence & identify vulnerable individualsb) Train agencies, with a special focus on national organizations, on practical protection mainstreaming tools so that they can independently implement the measures described in the previous activityAdditionally, NP will deploy an Integrated Mobile Protection Team & Southern Unity Mobile Team to conduct assessment & response activities including: direct protection in high-risk & minimally access areas, protection awareness raising, mainstreaming, referral & SGBV prevention/response. NP will target 720,878 beneficiaries in 2020. All activities are focused on general protection, but contain elements of Child Protection & GBV Prevention & Response. NP will ensure coordination with the CP & GBV sub-cluster focal persons in all areas of operation for all CP & GBV activities & provide monthly 5Ws.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Community engagement, one of the pillars of NP’s methodology - Unarmed Civilian Protection - promotes local ownership and meaningful influence over decision making through regular dialogue, participatory planning and implementation, and ensuring activities are beneficiary-led and tailored to the context. NP will utilize a community-focused approach to ensure full participation of beneficiaries to maximize sustainability and impact of project activities as well as serve as a feedback loop for beneficiary input to the project including to ensure a Do No Harm approach. All activities will draw on the experience of NP staff and beneficiaries and are continuously adapted accordingly. Additionally, NP is committed to providing accessible and timely information on organizational procedures, structures and processes that may impact communities in order to support informed decisions and engage communities in a dialogue as part of information provision. These mechanisms will enable credible and beneficiary-led activities tailored to the context and help to ensure meaningful participation of all community members through targeted interventions such as age- and gender-specific FGDs, trainings tailored community concerns, and strengthening of local protection infrastructures. NP will also provide direct protection and mainstreaming at distributions to support vulnerable groups, dissipate tensions and ensure crowd control to reduce the likelihood of violence and disruption. NP coordinates with distribution partners by ensuring that local leaders are informed beforehand to avoid misunderstandings at the various distribution centers. NP also ensures protection is provided to the civilians as well as identifying the most vulnerable individuals and prioritizing them. For example, NP will seek to identify, prioritize and support unaccompanied minors, heavily pregnant women, lactating mothers with newborns, elderly persons, disabled persons and those persons whom are sick or injured.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

NP has a clear Community Feedback/Complaint and Response Mechanism. According to these policies and procedures, NP staff, especially Team Leaders, are trained to play a frontline role as NP official focal points who receive and respond to less complex or refer sensitive and complex feedback and complaint issues. They are responsible for ensuring that the complainant’s safety is ensured during handling or referral process of the feedback or complaint. Any feedback or complaint received can be treated directly by the NP team concerned in that community where is less complex or sensitive. Otherwise the case is reported and referred to NP leadership immediately for instructions on the response provided. NP field teams have the responsibility to receive any feedback and or complaint and record it into a feedback/complaint logbook, and these a monitored by NP leadership on a regular basis. Complaints on sexual exploitation and abuse are sent to the Head Office in Geneva for immediate response within the 48 hours. Additionally, feedback is sought systematically through monitoring and evaluation of the project.
