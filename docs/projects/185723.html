Project Summary

The HOPE Field Hospital for Women is one of the few inpatient facilities which provides 24/7 uninterrupted health services to the refugees and affected host community people with total 100 beds capacity. This is to note that the SRH services at 50-bed field hospital and COVID-19 response at 50-bed SARI unit components will be covered by our partner UNFPA. However, this proposal of HOPE Foundation is focused on the outpatient care, 12 beds inpatient care and community outreach activities for serving communicable, non-communicable, chronic, dental and mental health problems. This project is limited to only HOPE Field Hospital for Women at camp 4. It is worth mentioning that both projects of UNFPA and HOPE will work in an integrated way in terms of prevention and treating infectious diseases. This integration includes serving communicable chronic diseases (HIV, TB and Hepatitis etc.), and outbreak potential diseases (dengue, AWD etc.), if needed. The project also covers PMTCT component with coordination of UNICEF. The project targeted a total number of 7,308 households as direct beneficiaries with a total of 32,576 individuals as indirect beneficiaries including all 1211 people with special needs (PSNs) from all blocks of camp 4. Also, Refugees from adjacent camps and hosts from Rajapalong union have equal access to this field hospital for primary and secondary care. This hospital has been serving as a referral hub for other Primary Health Care Centers and Health Posts. Under the project, aged people above 59 years, adult female and male, and children under 18 years will receive health care at the outpatient department (OPD). The patients will receive consultation and medicine free of cost. A total number of 12 inpatient beds are dedicated for serving patients with acute diseases who need hospitalization under inpatient department (IPD). Among total, 10 beds are allocated for females and 2 beds are for males. All patients under IPD will get treatment, medicine and food free of cost. In addition to this 12 beds, as mentioned above, 50-bed SARI unit covered by UNFPA may support to treat infectious diseases in an integrated way with this project also. To increase the access of persons with physical disability and ageing people to the health care, basic supports of wheelchairs, stratures, alternative entrance gate access and need base transport support are given. Necessary service providers including medical doctors, nurses, medical assistants, lab technologists, ward attendants, cleaner and support staff work to ensure quality health care. Health professionals are mostly female as it is a women focused health facility. Two dedicated teams of dental and mental health care professionals are present including dentist, psychiatrist, female psychologist and counselor. Mental health activities are coordinated with MHPSS working group. HOPE has two mobile medical teams (MMT) for responding any natural or human made disasters. This MMTs will be working with coordination of the MMT working group.
The community sensitization activities are ensured by providing health information and knowledge on preventive and curative measures for communicable and non-communicable diseases by appointing community health workers (CHWs). A number of five CHWs visit the community people on a regular basis and provide counselling and referral support for their desired and needed services. They work to maintain a bridge between the beneficiaries and service providers to make sure that the affected people have easy and dignified access to the facility-based services within the shortest time. Five (05) CHWs will be included in the mapping under CH working group.
Regular reporting to DHIS-2 for all health services, to EWARS for disease surveillance, 4Ws for health sector, CHWG, MHPSSWG, MMTWG reporting are ensured.

1 - Identified Needs

Continued attention to primary and secondary health care for communicable, non-communicable, general, chronic, dental care and mental problems are in great need due to the volatile situation at camp level. The Rohingya refugees are living in a very poor hygienic condition inside the camps and they are at a heightened risk of getting affected often by communicable and non-communicable diseases. This risk even becomes higher due to arrival of COVID-19 as it influences unfavorably more than ever on perceived health seeking behaviors and on accessing of health care. It has an adverse effect on the mental wellbeing of the people along with their existing anxiety due to displacement from homeland and different forms of sexual and gender based violence. Women suffering from postpartum depression and SGBV need special attention in terms of ensuring their mental wellbeing. It is very important to ensure mental health care and wellbeing of adolescent boys and girls, and aging people as these two groups are comparatively having limited access to need base health care than others. Besides, a good number of hosts are living surrounding the camps that are prone to be impacted by the camp’s environment and need special attention too. Therefore, ensuring the health care for both communities is indispensable as comprehensive service facilities are less in the catchment area. Along with, there are a very few health facilities offering inpatient care so far for the patients who suffer from communicable, non-communicable and other acute health conditions. The Rohingya refugees are bound to take health care at the nearest facilities since they are not permitted to go outside the resident camps. As a result, this underserved and vulnerable people in the catchment area struggle to survive from serious diseases including but not limited to acute watery diarrhoea, bloody diarrhoea, acute respiratory infection, urinary tract infection, measles, dengue, mumps, contagious skin diseases, asthma, COPD, cardiovascular diseases, diabetes, hypertension, jaundice, accidents and different forms of injuries. HOPE Field Hospital for Women has sufficient capacity and strength to serve these people with support of the partners. Also, the communities have very limited access to dental care in the camp setting. This hospital could meet the demand of dental care in the area. The people targeted need more access to the emergency outpatient and inpatient care for accidents and injuries as these kinds of incidents are very common in the camp setting.In terms of community health outreach, this group of people has a lack of knowledge and awareness on health and personal hygiene issues. They are less concerned about the root causes, preventable measures, and results of communicable and non-communicable illness since they have very limited even no opportunities of learning them in the camps. The refugees have poor health seeking behavior and are reluctant to visit health facilities due to cultural norms and religious beliefs. Considering these factors, community outreach activities play a significant role to sensitize them on those health issues. The community health workers need to inform them on health updates and refer for the timely facility-based services.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: Provide outpatient care to 8094 adult female and 6433 adult male of 18-59 years old; 480 elderly female and 565 elderly male of 60 and more years; and 8053 female children and 8687 male children of under 18 years; including 294 disabled, for communicable, non-communicable, chronic, emergency and general health care. The activity is ensured with coordination of the Health Sector. Activity 2: Ensure the life savings medicine, equipment and logistics for OPD and IPD patients free of cost to 8094 adult female and 6433 adult male of 18-59 years old; 480 elderly female and 565 elderly male of 60 and more years; and 8053 female children and 8687 male children of under 18 years. Activity 3: Ensure effective referral mechanism within the facility and outside the facility for emergency non-obstetric patients. This facility will receive incoming referrals from other PHCs and HPs for secondary care for infectious and non-communicable diseases. It aims to receive and serve 50 adult female, 50 adult male, 25 female children and 25 male children under 18 years, 10 elderly female and 10 elderly male patients. Activity 4: Provide dental care including scaling, filling, extraction and root canal (emergency purpose). The activity aims to serve 1000 adult female, 1000 adult male, 400 female children under years, 400 male children under 18 years, 100 elderly female, and 100 elderly male patients for higher treatment. Activity 5: Provide inpatient care in 10 female beds and 2 male beds and provide the food free of cost. Ensure adequate female nurses and support staff to serve the inpatient beds. Also HOPE SARI ITC will be used to serve chronic communicable diseases including HIV/TB/Hepatitis. PMTCT service is ongoing under the guidance of UNICEF. Hope Field Hospital is supporting HIV testing currently. The activities aim to admit and serve 1000 adult female, 400 adult male, 200 female children under years, 200 male children under 18 years, 100 elderly female, and 100 elderly male patients for higher treatment. Activity 6: Provide mental health counselling, clinical management and psychosocial care through trained counselor, psychologist and psychiatrist. Activities are ensured under the guidance of MHPSS working group. It aims to serve 600 adult female, 600 adult male, 200 elderly male and 200 elderly female, 200 under 18 male and 200 under 18 female children. Activity 7: Door-to-door visits by five community health workers for providing health information on prevention and curative measures of communicable, non-communicable, chronic and general health problems. Arrange health education and psycho-social counseling session to improve knowledge and health seeking behavior of community people, and follow-up at community level. This activity will be ensured with coordination of CH Working Group (CHWG). It aims to cover 900 households (on an average 180 HH for each CHW) and total 3979 individuals (on an average 4.42 people per HH). Activity 8: Monitor activities based on regular collection of sex and age disaggregated data of service components. Ensure reporting rate to DHIS-2, EWARS, 4Ws, MHPSSWG, CHWG and MMTWG. Activity 9: Functioning four HOPE mobile medical teams (MMT) to response any emergencies (natural and man made disasters). The activities are aligned with MMT working group. All MMTs are expected to assist lots of people during natural catastrophe, cyclone, fire, flood and other disasters. The MMT includes doctor, medical assistant, nurse, midwife, dispenser and driver in each team. Activity 10: Physical disabled and ageing people will get basic supports of wheel chair, crutch, alternative entrance and need base transport support while they seek treatment in in the hospital. Coordination with disabled care centric organization and CHWG will be carried out to reach out, and follow-up in post hospitalization period in case they need further treatment. The activity aims 100 female and 50 male disabled patients.
