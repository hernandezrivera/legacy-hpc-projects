Project Summary

In Somalia, many factors exacerbate protections concerns, especially for women and girls. Gender based violence (GBV) remains a silent, yet pervasive issues for many women and girls. Relief International (RI) has 12 health facilities across three regions, providing a full package of primary and preventative health care, servicing both IDPs and host communities. This project seeks to address the protection gaps for marginalised women and girls living in both formal and informal settlements and provide and integrate protection services, response and referral mechanisms for victims of GBV.
According to UNHCR, drought, conflict and insecurity resulted in the forced displacement of 162,000 Somalis between January and May 2019. Women and girls living in informal settlements are at an increased risk of being subject to GBV as settlements are often insecure and without the necessary infrastructure and support mechanisms that would help mitigate GBV. Furthermore, knowledge of available GBV response services and a willingness to address the issue is smothered in stigma and many survivors do not seek the medical and psychosocial support needed. In March 2019, RI interviewed a sample group of people in Hiraan and Gedo about their knowledge, attitudes and comfort in referring survivors of GBV to relevant services. Of the 150 individuals interviewed, less than 10% knew of any available GBV response services and less than 5% reported they would feel comfortable referring a survivor to the health facility. Despite the heightened risk of GBV (and other protection concerns), few patients experiencing GBV related trauma and health concerns do not seek assistance and protection.
Relief International (RI) will improve access of conflict-affected women and girls to referral and response services, increase health service providers with technical capacity, engage women and girls and their communities raising awareness and response with community outreach programs promoting protection and service provision (reaching women and girls physically unable to travel long distances due to disability or protection concerns), increase community engagement with men, boys, religious leaders and clan leaders and conduct a GBV reporting and access to service barriers analysis. Finally, RI will also establish women and girl safe spaces which will include education around reproductive health and messaging and support for GBV victims, menstrual hygiene and empowerment.
RI proposes the project be implemented across all 12 health centres, in Gedo, Hiraan and Banadir to serve communities – especially those in informal settlements and those in remote, underserved settings.

Project Activities

RI will implement the following activities:- Improve access of conflict-affected women and girls to referral and response services; - Increase health service providers with technical capacity to address SGBV (clinical management of rape);- Engage women, in-school girls, out-of-school girls and their communities around awareness and response with community outreach programs; - Increase community engagement with men, boys, religious leaders and clan leaders;- Conduct a GBV reporting and access to service barriers analysis; - Provide cash assistance to survivors of GBV; - Establish women and girl safe spaces which will include education around reproductive health and messaging and support for GBV victims, menstrual hygiene and empowerment; - Establish men's support groups to mobilize community around GBV prevention and advocacy for women and girls.

Mitigation Measures

1b:Access to protection services will require women and girls to leave their homes.- Ensure safe locations for SGBV services- Conduct activities in daylight hours- Easily accessible – for all groups from all settlements, informal and formal- Ensure privacy and anonymity due to stigma of SGBV 2b:- In the design of the project and the implementation of WGSS and men's support groups, RI will ensure that all groups, including the most marginalized, are included in the conversations and have access to these services without discrimination by:- Having facilities in accessible areas for all and having dialogue and interaction with key community figures to involve marginalised groups (women, girls, boys, IDPs, people with disabilities)- RI health staff will seek and listen to the advice of beneficiaries accessing services, and their feedback, ensuring their needs, safety, discretion, comfort and voices were met and heard.- RI will actively use community gatekeepers to access and hear (directly and indirectly) the concerns and preferences of women, girls and boys, marginalised individuals and groups, and people with disabilities when accessing services.- Community sensitisation and community dialogue activities will be conducted throughout the project to promote decision making processes are extended from the health facilities of victims, to multiple groups within households. Changing perceptions will also ensure prevention and reduce stigma around treatment and increase accessibility by groups typically excluded from access and decision making processes. 4b:- Risk of violence from intimate partner or family members if they are the perpetrators of SGBV. Seeking assistance and treatment may be a threat in itself. Therefore, ensure services are included with other health services that can ensure its privacy and confidentiality, appearing as standard health checkup, deflecting risk and suspicion.- Promote women's and girls' safety and protection with key male figures in communities (elders, religious leaders, government officials) to help create safer access to and from health facilities.- Ensure female health staff are present during consultations and are trained to provide PSS and PFA
