Project Summary

Zimbabwe is suffering from the effects of economic crisis combined climate shocks. The price of basic commodities such as food and fuel rose steeply, while drought, increases in input prices and delayed availability of inputs are impacting on farmers. According to the Zimbabwe Vulnerability Assessment Committee (ZimVAC) results for rural and urban, about 7.7 million people are projected to be food insecure during the period from October 2018 to March 2020. The humanitarian situation was worsened by Tropical Cyclone Idai which made a landfall in Zimbabwe on the 15th of March 2019. The combination of strong winds and heavy precipitation smashed the districts of Chimanimani, Chipinge, Makoni, Buhera, Nyanga and Mutare in Manicaland Province as well as Bikita, Gutu and Zaka in Masvingo Province causing significant loss of life, damages to infrastructure and destroying livelihoods.
It is against this background that Zimbabwe Council of Churches is proposing an agriculture livelihood recovery project in Chipinge and Chimanimani districts. The proposed intervention will target 400 vulnerable farmers who will benefit from nutrition gardens and field crops inputs. The project will minimize the risk of further food insecurity deterioration by improving access to basic crop inputs, and enhancing access, functionality and availability of critical community assets for nutrition garden production. The project is also aimed to optimize the benefit of agricultural support through extension and advisory services through training of farmers to improve agricultural yields and manage crop pests and diseases in Chipinge and Chimanimani districts by December 2020.
Most parts of Chipinge and Chimanimani districts lies in agro-ecological regions i, ii and iii which receives annual average rainfall from 700mm to above 1000mm. The southern part of Chipinge district is however in natural region iv. In light to the input guidelines by Food Security Cluster in consultation with the government of Zimbabwe and various stakeholders, the project is proposing to establish nutrition gardens to address the immediate nutritional requirements at household level. The project is also proposing to support farmers with field crop maize and cow peas seeds that are most suitable in most of the geographical locations in Chipinge and Chimanimani.
The project will target people in IPC 3 and 4, these are to be selected using a participatory community-based selection process that will be agreed between all the stakeholders and the community. IPC 3 and 4 indicators, household access to land and water amongst others will be used by selection facilitators to guide the process. Special considerations will be given to marginalised groups such as people living with disabilities, chronical illness, the elderly, widowed, orphans and child headed.
Zimbabwe Council of Churches (ZCC) has extended its operations in Manicaland Province during the beginning of Cycle Idai response in March 2019. It has supported victims of the disaster with emergency food assistance and shelter support. ZCC has signed Memorandum of Understandings (MoUs) with Chipinge and Chimanimani districts to conduct livelihood recovery activities in the province.

1 - Activities

The proposed project will focus on rehabilitating water sources, establishing nutrition gardens, providing nutrition garden and field crops inputs in an environmental friendly manner (i.e no pestcides and chemicals will be used as part of agriculture inputs). A total of 8 water sources will be rehabilitated and solarised to provide water through out the year for nutrition garden production at 8 nutrition gardens that will be established. A sum of 400 farmers targeted for nutrition gardens will be supported with garden input kits comprising of 20g tomatoes, 20g carrots, 20g leaf vegetable rape and cabbage, 100g butternuts, 20 g cucumber, 20g onions and 500g beans or peas per farmer. In addition to nutrition garden inputs, the project will provide the farmers field crop inputs consisting of 10kg maize seed and 2.5 kg cowpeas. The project will enhance farmer knowledge and skills through trainings on nutrition garden production; climate smart agriculture, diseases and pest management; agriculture value chains, post-harvest handling, storage, processing and marketing. All the activities will be implemented in coordination with line government departments, local authority, food security humanitarian organisations and local leadership. Government technical department will offer technical expertise in facilitation of project activities. The project will coordinate its activities with other programs in the district to minimise duplication of efforts and ensuring that the most vulnerable people are targeted. The organisation will attend all the meetings that will be invited through the Food Security Cluster.

2 - Indicators

The success of the project will be determined by measuring the number of households receiving season sensitive emergency crops and livestock inputs, critical community assets for crop and livestock production rehabilitated and built as well as the number of vulnerable small holder farmers receiving extension and advisory services to manage crop pests.
