Project Summary

The current socioeconomic conditions compounded by the blockade, energy crisis, salary cuts for PA employees and the ongoing impacts of the Great March of Return demonstrations are affecting all aspects of life in Gaza. As a result of such deteriorating living conditions, UNRWA saw a threefold increase in the number of new applications for its Cash for Work (CfW) programme in 2019, increasing the current average waiting time for a family to receive a short-term job opportunity to more than four years. Soaring rates of unemployment and weak income are key drivers to poverty and food insecurity among the population.
In line with Strategic Objective 3 of the HRP i.e. supporting the capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome protracted crisis, and the response approach of the Food Security Sector (FSS) i.e. assisting households with limited access to income opportunities, UNRWA, through its CfW programme, aims to support Palestine refugees in Gaza coping with protracted threats and shocks, and mitigate food insecurity through offering short term employment opportunities to approximately 14,000 poor Palestine refugees (equivalent to 79,800 total beneficiaries considering the average household size of 5.7 members). These will be equal to more than 1,430,000 work days, and will inject over US$ 17,6 million into households, generating almost 5,000 full time job equivalents (FTEs). The short term job opportunities will include more than 10,000 unskilled positions, more than 3,800 skilled positions and around 170 professional positions. In order to ensure gender access to these opportunities, UNRWA will aim to provide 40 per cent of skilled and professional job opportunities to women. CfW positions will be located in a number of locations throughout Gaza, including UNRWA installations, partner Community-Based Organizations (CBOs), Non-Governmental Organizations (NGOs) and other service-providers.

1 - Needs

The continued blockade and intermittent hostilities, the ongoing impacts of the Great March of Return demonstrations, as well as the intra-Palestinian conflict with the crisis of PA salary cuts are continuously deteriorating all aspects of life in the Gaza Strip. As the land, air and sea blockade entered its thirteen year in 2019, socioeconomic factors, including lack of employment opportunities, political uncertainty and chronic energy crisis, continue to have significant repercussions on the lives of Palestine refugees. According to Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics, the overall unemployment in Gaza reached 46.7% in the second quarter of 2019, among the highest worldwide. Palestine refugees are facing even higher unemployment rate of 48.3 per cent, compared to 43.5 per cent for non-refugees. Furthermore, the current situation has a particularly severe impact on vulnerable groups, including women and young people. Unemployment is especially high among women with 68.3 per cent. In addition to lack of job opportunities, women face social barriers to employment in Gaza, particularly when this involves travel to a workplace away from home. Young people between the ages of 15 and 29 are also affected, with unemployment rate of 64.0 per cent, while young women face extremely high unemployment rate of 86.0 per cent in the second quarter of 2019. Despite being highly educated, young people in Gaza face the prospect of unemployment, remaining dependent on already strained families and isolated from the outside world, rather than creating an independent future for themselves. Consistently high unemployment rates and deteriorating purchasing power have eroded many households’ capital and coping mechanisms, rendering the majority of the population of Gaza dependent on humanitarian assistance to meet basic needs and trapped more than half of them into food insecurity.According to the findings of the latest Socio-Economic and Food Security Survey (SefSec) carried out in 2018, about 68 per cent of households in Gaza experience severe or moderate levels of food insecurity. This is despite the fact that 69 per cent of households in Gaza reported in the SefSec that they receive some form of food assistance or other forms of social transfers from Palestinian governmental bodies or international organizations. People cope with food insecurity mainly through borrowing food and/or money. UNRWA has been experiencing the influx of people desperately seeking for jobs, even for a temporary employment such as Cash for Work opportunities. Currently, an average waiting time for a family to receive such opportunity is more than four years. There are more than 260,000 applicants already included in the Job Creation Programme database, while 26,123 submitted their applications during the first half of 2019 compared to about 9,000 applications during the first half of 2018. The statistic confirms the high demand for temporary employment.

2 - Activities

Through its Job Creation Programme (JCP), UNRWA will support refugees coping with protracted threats and shocks, and mitigate food insecurity through offering short term employment opportunities to approximately 14,000 poor Palestine refugees (equivalent to 79,800 total beneficiaries considering the average household size of 5.7 members). These will be equal to more than 1,430,000 work days, and will inject over US$ 17,6 million into households, generating almost 5,000 full time job equivalents (FTEs). The short term job opportunities will include more than 10,000 unskilled positions, more than 3,800 skilled positions and around 170 professional positions. Priority will be given to applicants from households who have been assessed as living below the poverty line of less than US$3.87 per person per day. Targeting criteria will also include skills and qualifications, location, age and gender. With the aim to enable access of vulnerable groups of the population to these employment opportunities – on top of which are women and youth, as described below, UNRWA aims to provide 40 per cent of skilled and professional job opportunities to women and about 40 per cent of all CfW opportunities to youth (18 to 29 years old). In order to ensure benefits are spread widely, contracts for unskilled positions are offered for three months and up to four months, and contracts for skilled positions are offered for three months and up to nine months. Only one member of a given household is eligible for cash for work contract. This intervention will inject more than US$ 17,6 million in wages into the local economy, supporting the recovery of economic activity in Gaza. The salaries provided will remain below average wage level so as not to distort the local market, and will range from US$ 10 to US$ 19 per day. Also note that although salaries are indicated in US dollar, beneficiaries will be contracted and paid in local currency to avoid the impact of fluctuations in the exchange rate. The JCP field monitoring team will ensure appropriate placement of refugees, and will monitor work attendance and performance. The CfW programme is a gender sensitive program integrates gender mainstreaming in its planning, operations and monitoring to ensure gender equality and development of women for competing in labour market. Moreover, women working under CfW contract modality are treated in accordance with the principles of UNRWA policy on the subject of (1) Maternity (unpaid). (2) Recruitment of pregnant. (3) Pre-Natal. (4) Resuming Work- Post Natal. (5) Breast feeding working hours.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

UNRWA has a specific obligation to mainstream protection across its services and operations, as well as made a clear commitment to protect and uphold Palestine refugees' rights. An integrated humanitarian response, with the inclusion of cash for work, is directly related to a growing focus on resilience. Resilience, in this context, relates to peoples' ability to withstand and recover from crises. The cash support reduces current risks by providing relief to strained families and enhancing refugees’ sense of dignity and self-reliance. Gauging the impact of such very-short temporary assistance is impossible; especially in a volatile and chronic-emergency Gaza context. Without such cash assistance, the vulnerability of poor families will be worsened and hence this entire project will contribute – among other factors and interventions- towards “resilience”. It is worth mentioning that JCP is a gender sensitive program integrates gender mainstreaming in its planning, operations and monitoring to ensure gender equality and development of women for competing in labour market. This project aims to target 40% of skilled and professional job opportunities to women to tackle the harsh economic situation women are facing. The Agency also takes into consideration that the culturally acceptable jobs are identified for women. As mentioned in "Activity" section, the salaries provided will remain below average wage level so as not to distort the local market, and will range from US$ 10 to US$ 19 per day. Also note that although salaries are indicated in US dollar, beneﬁciaries will be contracted and paid in local currency to avoid the impact of ﬂuctuations in the exchange rate.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNRWA services are open to all Palestine refugees who are seeking for assistance. However, in terms of the Cash for Work opportunities, the priority will be given to applicants from households who have been assessed as living below the poverty line of less than US$3.87 per person per day, considered as food insecure. Targeting criteria will also include skills and qualifications, location, age and gender. UNRWA aims to provide 40 per cent of skilled and professional job opportunities to women and about 40 per cent of all CfW opportunities to youth (18 to 29 years old). In order to ensure benefits are spread widely, contracts for unskilled positions are offered for three months and up to four months, and contracts for skilled positions are offered for three months and up to nine months. Only one member of a given household is eligible for cash for work contract. With the limited prospects of employment opportunities in Gaza, the Agency through its CfW Programme, managing the Cash for Work, addresses these economic hardships and ensures that families are self-reliant, albeit the limitation of the CfW contract duration. The provision of CfW opportunities allows families to cover some of their basic expenses, to improve living conditions and contribute to their copying mechanisms and resilience. Regarding the inclusion of people with disabilities in the CfW programme, consideration is given to candidates with disabilities whenever there is a suitable position. Moreover, the CBOs and NGO entities supported through the CfW programme also regularly provide opportunities for people with disability or special needs to be enrolled in the programme .
