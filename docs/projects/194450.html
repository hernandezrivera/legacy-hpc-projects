Project Summary

The project intends to deliver life saving static and mobile services to the most vulnerable children and their caregivers affected by floods, conflict that causes displacement ,diseases and famine. This comprises of returnees, Host community and internally displaced population ( men women, boys , girls , young people and adolescents) living in Jonglei state ( Akobo and Pibor counties ) and Western Equatoria State ( Tambura and EZO counties). The total target population is 40,000 vulnerable individuals ( 15,000 girls, 15000 boys, 5000 women and 5000 men). The primary purpose of the project is to address compounding effects of converging factors i.e displacement ,severe food insecurity /loss of livelihoods, economic decline, protection threats and lack of services which contributes and aggravates the prevalence of violence against children and adolescents. This project will focus on both preventive and responsive aspects of Child protection and work closely with GBV , FSL, Health ,S/NFI WASH, Education and Nutrition partners to ensure an integrated approach for a holistic service delivery that entrenches resilience and sustainability of the services rendered to the affected children and their care givers affected by multiple shocks. The intervention will be guided by the IASC guidelines for child protection ,national SOPs and cluster strategy. The response is strategic to the attainment of Cluster objectives and aligned to activities, indicators, and outcomes of the HRP 2022.The project activities focus on mitigating child protection risks, preventing and responding to child protection cases through; CFS programming, capacity building of frontline service providers on CPiE, Case management including FTR services , Structured/non-structured PSS, community Outreach and dialogues, and safe referrals. It is expected that by the end of the project period, lives of vulnerable women and girls,young people and their caregivers will be saved, they will have increased access to multi sector services , life skills and positive coping mechanisms entrenched in them.
CIDO is able to deliver the assistance in the project areas without difficulties attributed to physical presence in the 4 locations and excellent understanding of the dynamics of Western Equatoria and Jonglei Counties.

1 - Needs

According to Humanitarian Needs Overview ,more than 5 million people, of whom 2 million are children and adolescents, including those with disabilities, are at heightened risk of violence, abuse, and exploitation and need critical child protection services. The situation for children has deteriorated over 2022, with continued incidents of recruitment, abuse, exploitation and other grave violations. According to DRC assessment of Tambura county ,over 848 children (400 girls and 448 boys) have been registered as unaccompanied, separated or missing as of February 2022. According to IRNA assessment of Akobo county conducted in October 2022 sub-national violence and floods among communities of Akobo and Pibor counties has led to displacement of children and their caregivers with over 230 cases of abduction of children ( boys and girls)reported. Akobo and Pibor are counties affected by seasonal flooding yearly and this further complicates accessibility of child protection services with increased child labour cases and transactional sex among young girls. In Akobo and Pibor, community displacement is very intensive due to a combination of insecurity and food insecurity; cattle raiding and revenge killings are, respectively, the main problem and criminal offence recorded in the county; The situation has further been exacerbated by Ebola virus fears and COVID 19. According to OCHA’s humanitarian Snapshot of October 2022 ,IDP returns continue to be reported in Ezo and Tambura with reports of scarcity of services. CIDO reports of Ezo county as February 28th reported a total of 32 UASC as a result of the displacement with several cases of sexual violence against women and young girls. The Tambura conflict caused interruption of schools routine and has subsequently contributed to a rise in sexual violence incidents affecting children. Thousands of people who had fled to Western Bhar-el Ghazal in 2021 conflict are returning to Tambura . Social structures and family bonds were breaken, children,separated from their families, and they have very limited access to basic services, which hinders their physical and mental development. IRNA assessment for October 2022 of Akobo county indicated massive abduction, revenge killings and forced early marriage of girls attributed to subnational violence and revenge attacks among Pibor and Akobo communities . Adolescent girls are particularly vulnerable to multiple forms of violence and have limited support systems and access to information. The sub national conflicts have led to displacement, children being abandoned or encouraged to live alone. In total, it is estimated that 390 UASC and CAAFAG children will need Comprehensive Case Management and re-intergration services. Children with disability and children with mental health disorders will need individual protection assistance in Tambura and Ezo counties. About 4000 caregivers and foster-care parents will also need training on appropriate and culturally acceptable child care practices. CIDO intends to coordinate with other sectoral partners like GBV, Health, Nutrition and Education to provide integrated basic and comprehensive services to children. By establishing community child protection and support groups for prevention and support of children with long-term psychosocial needs, about 160 community child protection committee members will need training on age appropriate PSS and how to report PSEA.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

CIDO will ensure all staff are trained on data management and security. Themanagement will provide lockable safe boxes in all field locations for keepingconfidential files and encourage the use of passwords to protect soft copies ofdata.HLP matters (e.g., rental/lease agreements). CIDO will also consult with allgroups within affected populations to identify most effective and appropriate methodsof awareness-raising. CIDO will be guided by the cluster safety audit whilemainstreaming protection, conflict sensitivity and AAP. CIDO in close collaborationwith Chiefs, AAP committees and Protection partners will conduct HLP messagingwhile at the same time addressing existing and emerging HLP issues throughoutproject implementation in line with the cluster humanitarian shelter and land rights duediligence guidelines for S/NFI actors in South Sudan to ensure smooth intervention.Community engagement and communication including with the local leadership in allaspects of the project will mitigate external interference likely to spark conflict. CIDOwill adhere to principals of protection and the Do no Harm policy while engaging withall stakeholders .CIDO will work closely with county security organs and local authorityto prioritize safety of staff, lives and property of community members through localdialogues and peaceful negotiations. To promote beneficiary access to CRFMmechanisms, CIDO Project team will use Community Communication andEngagement (CCE) strategies while being guided by the Accountability to the AffectedPopulation (AAP) guidelines all through the project implementation. CIDO will put inplace AAP committees composed of young people and their caregivers in all projectlocations to monitor and promote rights of beneficiaries as far as access to qualityservices is concerned. To mitigate the risk of explosive hazards in the project locations,CIDO will coordinate with Mine Action partners through sharing data in subnationaland national cluster meetings and use their expert recommendations to advise onsafety of both beneficiaries and staff.
