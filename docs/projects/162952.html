1 - Needs

An estimated 250,000 people displaced since 2013 in Darfur with trends indicating continued displacement as armed violence continues in Jebel Marra. Market inflation and economic challenges has significant impact on the Sudanese people, increasing their vulnerability to risks. In 2019, an estimated 5.6 million will encounter acute food and livelihood insecurity in Sudan based on review and analysis of various food securities information reports IPC. Lack of adequate income and their low diversity, lack of agricultural extension activities, lack of improved seeds, insecticides, pesticides chemicals, lack of mobility tools for extensionists, lack of the fruits seedlings propagation nurseries, and lack of improved vegetable and fruits demonstration plots are the common challenges. In Feb, UPO conducted a survey and found the most common constraints faced by the communities and the local authorities are lack of sufficient on farm seed production, poor agricultural production methods, lack of skilled human resource capacity at the state, locality and community levels, and lack of skills particularly among the youths. Women and youth also reported insufficient income-generating sources UPO has conducted a rapid assessment in IDPs camps and most affected rural areas with newly IDPs and host communities in July 2018 where UPO team has interviewed newly IDPs and most vulnerable returnees with involvement of men, women, boys and girls through direct group discussion especially with those who newly displaced and affected and armed inter-tribal conflicts between the periods of January –April 2016. More than 24,000 newly displaced and conflict affected individuals including women, men, boys and girls were identified to be in urgent needs for food security and livelihood coping mechanisms. Another 7,600 most conflict affected and returnees' populations (women, men, boys and girls) in ED ty were assessed to be in needs agricultural inputs, extension services, training on clay stoves and other substantial livelihood opportunities to help them possess new income opportunity. UPO has identified limited opportunities for households to generate income and produce food, with agricultural inputs and livelihood activities. In UPO, community interviews 60% of households in Darfur indicated they do not have sufficient food. The majority of respondents cited a lack of seed, and 78% indicated they have not been able to store their own seed (UPO rapid assessment in Feb). Community members identified insufficient land, due nomadic occupation, requiring improved methods to maximize output and diversity their livelihoods. Animal health is also poor, particularly amongst IDPs and host households, with only 5.6% indicating good animal health. UPO FSL intervention will focus protecting animals' health, provide quality declared drought tolerant and early maturing seed to enable their own production and ensure self-reliance.

2 - Activities / Outputs

Provision of agricultural inputs and seeds for 10,000 conflict affected farmers HHs (women, men, boys and girls) in camps, gathering and underserved areas to help them meet their food needs from own production and improve livelihood condition. 2 conducting extension trainings for 1,000 most vulnerable farmers (600 female& 400 male) on modern agricultural techniques. 3 Provision of vaccinations to 200,000 head of animals owned by 6,000 (2000 females, 4000 males) most vulnerable HHs dependent on livestock. 4 – Provision of vocational trainings and emergency livelihood start up kits for 600 for most vulnerable women headed HHs in camps and gatherings to enable them to reduce aid dependency and increase self reliant. 5 Provision of agricultural inputs and tools for 6,000 most vulnerable affected HHs (3500 female, 2500men) to enable have new income opportunity and cope with the current situations. 6 Conducting awareness for 1000 most vulnerable populations both female and male on environment protection, gender involvement and equality. 7 Conduct training for 300 most vulnerable women on fuel efficient stove production skills to enable them to possess new income opportunity and reduce environmental degradation. in addition 40 % of this program will be cash through distribution of vouchers, cash for work and cash for business/start-up kits. UPO has 3 years history of cash based intervention in Darfur. IGAs, business start-up capitals , vocational training will be partially implemented through cash transfer: UPO will establish groups/association , facilitate bank account opening for the group enable easy transfer of the cash. the intervention will be one-time off based on the activities designed.
