Project Summary

CH is proposing this project to meet immediate shelter and none food items needs of vulnerable households from host communities, IDPs and returnees in Leer, Panyijiar, Koch, Guit and Rubkona Counties. The project will build on the strong foundation CH has, working with various partners in southern Unity as a static member of Shelter and NFI. CH has worked and jointly intervened with Concern worldwide several times in the northern part of Unity State particularly in POC, Rubkona, Bentiu town, Guit and Koch County. In Southern Unity, CH works very closely with INTERSOS, MEDAIR, PAH and t World Vision and other partners. With good experience and understanding of the cluster tools, context, well-trained staff, CH is at vantage position to launch successful response to support vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities in targeted locations.
According to HNO 2019, “7 million people or about two thirds of the population in dire need of some form of humanitarian assistance and protection, 4.2million people to ﬂee their homes in search of safety, nearly 2 million of them within and 2.2 million outside the country. About 1.5 million people live in areas facing high levels of access constraints – places where armed hostilities, violence against aid workers and assets, and other access impediments render humanitarian activities severely restricted”
HRP 2019 indicates that 7million people are in need of food, while 5 million women and girls need protection.
The severity of shelter and NFI situation is conﬁrmed by the recent IPC reports where more than 55.4 % of the 6.21 million populations in needs are in phase 4 and 5, which is a worrying situation.
The project will support 15,000 beneﬁciaries directly with Shelter and NFI solutions as aggregated by gender herein: Women =5,000 Girls =4,250, Boys =3,250, Men = 2,500.
This project is designed to meet the immediate and medium to long-term shelter and NFI solutions of vulnerable households which include; returnees, IDPS and host community as supported by CH internal assessment reports and partners in Panyijiar, Leer, Mayendit, Guit where the families assessed lacked essential shelter and NFI. The Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) report by IOM placed the registered IDPs as at March this year at 1,420,189. Furthermore, the REACH reports describe the situation in South Sudan generally and Unity State in particular as a “complex humanitarian crisis, restricting humanitarian access and hindering the ﬂow of information required by aid partners to deliver humanitarian assistance to populations in need”. More than 50% of the population interviewed reported concerns like lack of shelter and NFI and insecurity as their major concerns. In the recent months, CH has registered signiﬁcant number of returnees coming back to their homes from neighbouring counties and countries. This project will support and improve their shelter and NFI needs to enable rebuild their homes.
CH will strive to link up NFI beneficiaries to other cluster partners operating in the area by providing information about the services and support they are providing like nutrition services and OTP /health centers, health service providers in supported health facilities and mobile clinic centers as well as refer these beneficiaries to the facilities already supported CH in the project area. Further linkages will include CCCM to effectively coordinate the S/NFI deliverables in a coordinated manner without duplication of services.
Since beneficiaries of S/NFI are mostly vulnerable persons, they will also be linked to FSL to be assisted with FSL livelihood kits to increase food security. The beneficiaries will further be linked to WASH partners for assistance in WASH needs including sanitation and hygiene promotions GIK and others. There is a proposed humanitarian Hub to be established in Leer town and this will provide network to improve communication and networking. Supplies are 100% in kind support from core pipeline.

1 - Needs

South Sudan in general has been going through multiple crises and former Unity State in particular was worse hit. There is a huge gap in shelter and NFI needs among the most vulnerable members of IDPs, returnees and host communities within the counties CH is targeting. While priority will got of vulnerable IDPs AND returnees, host communities have equally suffered due to inﬂux of IDPs, vulnerable returnees stretching these communities’ limiting coping mechanisms, hence need for support. The prolonged instability in the targeted counties since the December 2013 war has caused multiple crises including internal communal conﬂicts, revenge killings, cattle raiding, serious gender basis violence including rape, sexual assault, high level of diseases outbreaks and malnutrition due to broken health system to lack of food and no functional markets. Shelter and NFI tops beneﬁciaries concern in all the assessments as they have lost everything during the conﬂict. While the ceaseﬁre that was declared in August 2018 has largely held among the warring parties, there has been localized conﬂict threatening IDPs and returnees. Internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees continue to be faced with many challenges including lack of shelter and NFI solutions in their new homestead abandoned for many years. There has been small return to some targeted locations but beneﬁciaries return to nothing in their former homesteads and wanted to rebuild their lives. Therefore humanitarian assistance shall remain the only option in 2020. According to the IPC reports released in September 2019 more than 55.4 % of the 6.21 million populations in needs are in phase 4 and 5, which is a worrying situation. This information is higher than the projection for 2019. The above has been corroborated by our internal CH assessment reports and partners in Panyijiar, Leer, Mayendit, Guit, Rubkona Counties. According to reports prepared by the IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM), the number of registered IDPs is at 1, 420,189 (March 2019). Furthermore, the REACH reports describe the situation in South Sudan generally and Unity State in particular as a “complex humanitarian crisis, restricting humanitarian access and hindering the ﬂow of information required by aid partners to deliver humanitarian assistance to populations in need”. More than 50% of the population interviewed reported lack of shelter and NFI as one of their major concern. These reports conﬁrm dire humanitarian needs and urgent support to the most affected population with limited humanitarian assistance, especially for those outside the POCs. TheAccording to HNO 2019, “7 million people or about two thirds of the population in dire need of some form of humanitarian assistance and protection, 4.2million people to ﬂee their homes in search of safety, nearly 2 million of them within and 2.2 million outside the country. About 1.5 million people live in areas facing high levels of access constraints – places where armed hostilities, violence against aid workers and assets, and other access impediments render humanitarian activities severely restricted”HRP 2019 indicates that 7million people are in need of food, while 5 million women and girls need protectionInaccessibility and closure of many markets where basic shelter and NFI items could be sold has further exacerbated the situation in the former Unity State in general and the counties targeted in particular. The reports from our protection monitoring and assessments by CH in Unity where returnees and IDPS were interviewed expressed concerns like food, shelters and NFIs and WASH related. The targeted counties have most the households lacking proper shelter and non-food items. Continued inter-communal displacements and multiple threats looming, provoked demands for appropriate shelter and life sustaining non-food items other than 15,000 people in the targeted 6 locations.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes,the project is basically going to provide support new and old displacements in the categories of IDPs,host and returnees within the location where the project shall be implemented. All vulnerable members of the community regardless of their status shall benefit.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

This is pure humanitarian project and do not link to government programmes. However,the project is linked to community who are in needs.The project is designed in line with humanitarian response plan as well as the Shelter and NFI cluster objectives.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is designed an aligned with national Humanitarian response plan for South Sudan and targets IDPs,returnees and host communities in the respective locations identified of priority needs.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

While the project is primarily going to meet short and emergency response needs of vulnerable households affected by the conflict,it is expected that it will toward contributing to longer-term durable solutions to IDPs,returnees and host communities shelter needs. The project will support community transiting from the current crisis level to more stable position so that they can rely on their own coping mechanisms to provide for their emergency shelter and non food items needs.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes,the project will provide community members with basic training and capacity building skills in order to provide for their ES/NFIs including market strengthening and use of locally available materials for construction and other household uses.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

During the implementation of this project, we may face some risks as mostly affecting women, children and people with disabilities. In our running interventions (GBV, WASH and Health) we have already noticed that women and girls have limited rights on decision about housing, land and property ownership. They are the most affected category in regard to GBV. We will therefore be guided by HLP approaches that have been used and tested in other countries with humanitarian challenges to address some of the expected risks. Furthermore, following the HLP guidelines we will engage all community platforms including; local leadership, administration structures, as well as lowest level of community (household) to ensure all people have the right of having shelter, safety and the ability to secure a livelihood free from forced eviction. This project will focus on protecting women girls, boys and men even those with disabilities from any discrimination regarding housing/shelter, land and property rights. The already existing community structures in Ganyliel, Tayar, and Yai have been used to train and engaged women and girls under GBV/GP program. The same experience will be transferred to the new locations with the hope to address HLP related risks. This project will provide the opportunity to people with disabilities to communicate with freedom on the distribution of S/NFI commodities. Their views will be considered as guided by the GBV pocket book on GBV related risks. This project will be integrated with GBV to provide appropriate conditions for the GBV survivors to be treated with dignity.. The GBV survivors will be provided with Psychological First Aid (PFA) and appropriate referrals will be made to ensure effective assistance. S/NFI services will be brought nearer to the pregnant and lactating women so as they have limited interruption in their usual household work which at some point results into GBV from their husbands in case of delayed meals.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

This project is designed in line with shelter &NFI strategic objectives SO1, SO2, SO4 which thoroughly define this intervention as coordinated response to persistently address shelter &NFI needs with durable solutions. Discussions with women and girls will be held to analyze shelter & NFI related GBV risks during the needs analysis missions and distribution sites. CH will continue to train its staffs and volunteers on PSEA, GBV and other sexually related behaviors that are not encouraged by our professional code of conduct. Share with community to improve flexibility in component of society norms that drive early marriages, abduction and sexual violence. This will involve consultations with representatives of the people living with disability, people living with HIV&AIDS, women representatives, cultural leaders, Elderly people and religious groups so that each member living in the community is assisted and protected from any shocks related to shelter and nonfood items. Distribution of shelter and non-food items such as blankets, kitchen sets, sheeting will be conducted in distance discussed and decided by the affected populations. Distribution and needs analysis will not be carried out in area where security situation is extremely volatile. Local authorities and OCHA will be consulted to brief the team about the security situation of the area intended for the distribution of shelter and NFI before the will go in for distribution. Weekly and daily reporting about the weather updates and security situation will also help the team to know the status of the area a head of intervention. People living with disability: The project will consult people living with disability through their representatives in all the 6 counties targeted by this project. These consultations will be carried out in line with international standard of disability (Washington Groups short set of question WGSS) which defines inclusive disability clearly.
