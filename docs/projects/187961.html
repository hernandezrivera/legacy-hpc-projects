Project Summary

Takaful has designed this program aims to support the food security of the most vulnerable IDPs and part of the host community in Idleb and Aleppo governorates,
Main interventions:
First: providing most vulnerable IDPs and HCs standard food baskets on monthly basis, targeting 8000 HHs 40000 individuals (8640 men -9776 women – 10260 boys -10524 girls ) in Idleb and Aleppo governorate, The distribution will be conducted at specified food distribution points (FDPs), safe FDPs will be chosen, away from the military centres with enough and safe distance and beneficiaries have easy access to them. For beneficiaries who have difficulties to reach to distribution points such as families headed by child or Disabled persons, the baskets will be transferred to their location using light vehicle. The HHS who live in camps will receive baked bread in. the HHS which will receive the bread will be 1000, 5000individuals (men 1080 - women 1222 - boys 1320 - girls 1378). Second: providing newly IDPs with Ready to Eat rations (RTE) which enable them to provide their families with required food for the first week of the displacement. This intervention targets 5000 HHs 25000 individuals (5480 men - 6332 women - 6420 boys 7068 girls ) The identification of these IDPs is the responsibility of the Takaful MEAL team, who track the movements of the IDPs, collect their data, and verify these data. Third: Cooked Meals: In this intervention, Takaful Al-Sham will provide 5,000 cooked meals to 5,000 individuals (1296 men, 1466 women and 1653 girls 1584 boys), The new IDPs from the areas that have been displaced by the recent military operations will be targeted. The meal will contain the calories agreed to in the standards set by the Cluster. Takaful will coordinate with the food security cluster and with other organization who works in the same sector and field to reach the right beneficiaries and avoid any duplication. The project will ensure equal access for both genders with the priority for women, children headed household, disabled person, sick, PLW and elderly in addition the project staff will include female and male members trained on the protection principles and gender equality
We already secured 2.6 M $ though BHA fund.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2021, 14.2 million people in Syria are in need of food and agriculture (68.6% of the population). at least 12.4 million people are estimated to be food insecure, of which 1.3 million people are severely food insecure. The majority of IDPs is located in the Northwest of Syria are in Dana, A'zaz, Maaret Tamsrin, Jebel Saman, Salqin, Idleb, Afrin, and Al Bab, sub-districts. According to Care International Org research in the Northwest of Syria in 2021; 43% mentioned that the high price of food creates a challenge facing people in accessing food, 44% mentioned the lack of money to buy food.TAS carried out a NA in 2021 in the subdistrict of Atareb, Azaz, Dana, Harim, and Maaret Tamsrin. It was found that main concern of residents according to HHs and KIs is that no income to buy food, and the insufficient food, and they adapted coping strategy as reliance on cheaper items 25%, decreasing the number of meals 23%, decreasing the consumption of food of HHs 22%, decreasing the consumption of food for adults to feed children 10%.Aleppo (A’zaz, Al bab, Jabel Saman, Jarables, Afrin); Total Pop 3450539, IDPs 1183956, PwD 22.2%, (0-17 G) 21.5%, (0-17-B) 25.9%, (18-59, W) 26.2%, (18-59,M) 20.6%, Elderly (6.6%), PiN 1701405, Severity Scale 3.4.Idleb (Ariha, Jisr Ash Shugur, Harim, Idleb); Total Pop 2809367, IDPs 1837835, PwD 23.6%, (0-17 G) 23.2%, (0-17-B) 26.8%, (18-59, W) 24.0%, (18-59,M) 22.2%, Elderly (4.8%), PiN 79844, Severity Scale 3.5 [Pop 2021 - HNO 2021].

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk 1: The vulnerability of children and women in conflict circumstances highly increases and places them in higher risk of being exposed to exploitation, abuse and rights violations.Mitigation strategy: 1- Staff personnel will sign code of conduct and child protection policy.2- Most of the management and field staff had attended workshops to increase the awareness of PSEA cases.3- the compliance mechanism will be defined and shared with beneficiaries.Risk 2: There are not enough humanitarian staff (both male and female) at the distribution site monitoring the distribution process Mitigation strategy: 1- Ensure oversight of distributions by aid workers who are trained on PSEA, Codes of Conduct and how/where to report misconduct.2- Ensure field level PSEA Focal Points in distribution teams and/or that staff involved in distributions know how to report or refer any PSEA allegations received.3- Deploy gender balanced teams to support projects for disaster-affected communities.4- Consider wearing clearly marked organization logos on shirts/jackets/caps for ease of identity by beneficiaries.Risk 3 : when challenges and issues arose during distribution, they are not handled well, and there is no female employee if the beneficiary was a female1. Train field workers on PSEA2. Ensure female aid workers are present and involved in the resolution of the challenges3. Sensitize affected communities on PSEA and who/where to report
