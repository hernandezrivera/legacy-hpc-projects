Project Summary

The overall aim of the project is to contribute to strengthening prevention, mitigation and response to GBV issues as well as strengthening quality of care for GBV survivors. This will build on the already gained milestones from the 2018 that has already enabled AYA to provide community based women and girls friendly spaces in Mundri Payam, Kotobi Payam in Mundri West and Lakama’di and Kedi'ba in Mundri East. Also continuous provision of case management services especially in Lui, Mundri, Kedi’ba and Kotobi. However, these established structures of the centers remained poor, limited W&GFS staffs and increased number of IDPs in the targeted locations.
AYA intends to continue run and equip Women and Girl’s Friendly Spaces established in Kedi’ba, Mundri, Kotobi and Lakama’di. There will be establishment of one additional women and girl’s friendly space in Lui.
Women and girls are the primary target of the project. Community outreaches, psycho-social support services, case management. Young male and young female engagement that will support discussion around the GBV and women rights will be conducted. This will create change in communities that the project aims to support.
AYA will also create awareness to the women and girls during women and girls friendly spaces sessions on the available services for GBV, small income generation as part of psychosocial support, recreational activities with women and girls in women and girls friendly spaces and support building capacity of women and girls especially most vulnerable women and girls in vocational activities for improvement of livelihoods.
There is already existing referral pathway in Mundri West. This project will enable AYA to train the members of GBV Working group on the referral pathways and how to use to support the survivors of GBV and there will be quarterly update of the referral pathways. AYA will create one referral pathway for Mundri East., and update it regularly.
The organization through the previous project, have already trained and positioned its staffs in GBV issues including case management. Therefore, the only refresher training to the staff will be on discussion around child survivors of GBV. This will be done to sharpen their skills on supporting child survivors of GBV.

Needs

South Sudan is first and foremost gradually becoming protracted protection crisis. Since 2013, the population has been exposed to repeated deliberate attacks on civilians and other violations of international humanitarian and human rights law (UN OCHA HRP “2018). Though the signing cessation of hostilities was conducted in December 2017 and signing of revitalized peace agreement was conducted on 12th September 2018, the fighting in parts of South Sudan continued. A country of more than 11 million people has faced numerous, overwhelming challenges since conflict broke out in December 2013. During the fighting, homes were reportedly looted and burned, children were reportedly killed and forcefully recruited in the military ranks, others were reported missing or separated, and cases of GBV were reported in an unprecedented high figure. The majority of people displaced by the fighting were women, children and the elderly, including persons with specific needs. Fighting in Kedi’ba headquarters of Mundri East County in December 2016 displaced nearly 5000 individuals who short refuge in Mundri IDP camp. These people in the last two years depended on humanitarian assistance since the security could not permit them to cultivate outside Mundri. In Bangolo, Medewu and Kotobi, communities faced tremendous challenges due to lack of available services. This categorically forced women, children and elderly people to seek refuge in Mundri town (AYA FGD- April 2018). 8000 IDPs forcefully migrated to Mundri town. In late April 2018, forceful displacement of communities from seven locations in Mundri East happened. The displaced IDPs were settled in ‘Buagyi Boma in Lozoh Payam. Conditions of IDPs deteriorate on everyday basis. This left many traumatized since the only source of livelihood was left back home. Rain washed children and pregnant mothers and elderly people and persons living with disabilities were left behind in bushes (May 2018 Assessment). In May 2018, inter communal fighting happened in Dari and Lesi displacing considerable number of people to Lakama’di and Mundri (NP protection assessment August 2018). Inter-communal fighting also erupted in Lakama’di on 26th August 2018. This also led to loss of lives, houses burned and considerable number of people displaced to nearby villages and they live without essential services. According to the series of assessment reports, displaced populations reported incidents of forced recruitment and violence against civilians, including gender-based violence (GBV); women also explained how economic insecurity coupled with high levels of poverty resulted into pressure at household level and increased GBV when women were not able to bring home sufficient food. Although insecurity along the Mundri- Juba road seems to have decreased compared to 2017, women in all areas assessed report cases of harassment and rape when they travel to Mundri town from villages for food assistance or to access the market. In addition, women also reported cases of GBV when they walk long distances to the forests to fetch water, firewood or look for wild fruits. In places like Kotobi, women have to walk 15 miles to access good health services in Mundri PHCC. The displacement cause food insecurity which exacerbates harmful gendered roles, increased risk of a variety of forms of gender‑based violence including but not limited to: sexual violence in the form of SEA and survival sex; child, early and forced marriage; and unequal access and control of resources for women and girls. However, AYA believes that each form of GBV listed above requires case management interventions which are life‑saving. Therefore the need for GBV humanitarian assistance in Mundri West and Mundri East remains dire especially with regards to Psycho-social Support (PSS), and GBV case management as rape survivors are mostly labelled and stigmatized, including needs for promotion of gender relations and promoting active participation.

Activities

Provide basic counseling, case management and referrals to 1000 vulnerable women, girls, boys, men, elderly persons and persons with disability in groups or as individual, update and disseminate referral pathways. Establish women safety committees and train 15 men, 15 boys, 20 girls and 20 women in PFA, and women protection networking methodology including GBV guiding principle. Continuous mentoring of the trained groups for participation in protection coordination structure, community level mediation of conflict and peaceful coexistence to promote community peace and security. Running and strengthening capacity of 3 women and girls friendly spaces AYA is running and including child friendly space activities in all the centers. Conduct community awareness through house-to-house visitation and 6 open air community campaigns, 12 community forum dialogues on gender equality and gender relations including women participation in leadership. Conduct 1 weekly outreach to remote villages. This is to have discussions about services available, gender analysis in communities.

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

There will be established referral pathways. These referral pathways have information about the service providers and type of services offered. During consultation, the information was provided to the beneficiaries. This does not compromise the issue of managing the expectation.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

In women and girls friendly spaces, messages for adolescent girls differ from messages for women in the group. The messages to be passed to each category of beneficiaries is designed to suit their situation. The project will also target the men and boys to discuss about musclinity. This message will differ from messages communicated in daily activities in Women and Girls Friendly Spaces. Messages to be passed in Child Friendly spaces will differ from messages in Women and Girls Friendly Spaces.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project staffs have signed the code of conduct, there is already PSEA Focal Point trained in AYA and doing his job and all the staffs are aware of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. AYA also acts continuous drama on Sexual Exploitation and Abuse to let community know certain acts are not acceptable.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

The consultation was conducted from various women and girls friendly spaces. With the members of the center, the volunteers of the center and another consultation was conducted with the management of the center. Women and girls were involved in decision making regarding to support to be given to them. Including PSS support that geared towards empowerment. Youths were also consulted during youth group activities. They clearly mentioned which type of support to be given. Especially the youths talked about the behavioral change communication initiatives that will enable them change from the negative tradition to support the discussion towards ending GBV. Consultation was also conducted with community leaders. They specifically talked of youth engagement in accountable practices, women's rights discussion and discriminatory behaviors that promote violence in the community.
