Project Summary

Medair’s proposed action will provide PLWMI with access to interventions at community and household-levels to 588 target beneficiaries (excluding overlap between distribution and referral beneficiaries), building on the experience, expertise and relationships from its previous project cycles to reach the most vulnerable and in-need, and with sustainability at the core of its programme through support of inclusion for PLWMI in family and community life.

Needs

The prevalence of disability in Syria is highly likely to have increased in recent years due to war related injuries. War related injuries such as amputation or spinal cord injuries lead to life long impairment for nearly half the survivors (WHO, 2011).The prevalence of mobility related disabilities was found to be as high as 29% in Aleppo and 22% in Idlib (PRDWG survey, 2018). Half of these cases were a direct result of the conflict. Mobility impairments were counted as 12% of People With Disabilities in this survey. Medair conducted assessments in May 2018 among Eastern Ghouta IDPs who arrived in the areas of At Tal, Maraba and Hafir Taghta in Rural Damascus, and reviewed data received from DoH distribution of Medair supplies in the collective shelters through DoH. As well as disability due to war related injuries, these assessments found high numbers of suspected cerebral palsy cases, possibly reflecting poor access to intrapartum health care, and high rates of disability due to old age. The recent PRDWG survey in Idlib, Al Raqqa and Aleppo found the highest prevalence of disability among young men, which is in line with Medair’s finding among IDPs from Eastern Ghouta in May 2018 that 61% of people living with mobility impairments were male. Medair data showed 53% of assistive device beneficiaries are in the age of 19-65, 30% are over 65, 15% are between 6-18 years, and only 2% are under 5. Of all the people with disabilities in the PRDWG Survey over 60% stated that the impact of their disability was exacerbated by the crisis with lack of access to healthcare, including a lack of assistive devices, given as the most common reason. Other reasons are the environmental change and stress created by conflict, in addition to the creation of new impairments from ongoing violence (PRDWG, 2018). Health facility staff as well as protection staff report that people living with disabilities and their families in Syria are often highly vulnerable due to discrimination, stigma, exclusion and poor livelihood opportunities affecting them as well as their families potentially exposing them to additional protection risks such as child marriage, child labour and exploitation. People with physical disabilities often have limited mobility and limited care available. This leads to increased morbidity, mortality and social exclusion. Healthcare facilities are often inaccessible for people with physical disabilities, for example due to stairs, raised entrances and a lack of rails. Housing, public buildings and infrastructure are unsuitable for people with disabilities. A limited number of humanitarian actors in Syria currently support people with mobility impairments. DoH as well as MOSAL in their priorities describe a continued need for support for people with mobility impairments in all of Medair’s project areas. After Medair’s assistive device distribution in Dara’a, community leaders requested further distributions of assistive devices in other neighbourhoods (December 2018).

Implementation of activities

The activities both extend Medair’s established multisectorial programming in Rural Damascus and Aleppo (accessed from 2015 and 2017 respectively) and target newly accessible areas, Dara’a and Deir Ez Zour (accessed from late 2018). People with mobility impairments will be identified in supported communities by the community health workers (CHWs) or during household assessments by the Shelter/NFI team. Medair prioritises communities based on the following: - Hard to reach areas and newly accessible areas (≤3 months), - HNO overall severity scale ≥3, sector specific severity scale ≥4, - ≥25% of the population is IDP or returnee that have arrived to location within the last 12 months - Recent or sudden population movements (≤3 months), - High and acute population needs with few to no actors (i.e. a lack of community services like lack of running electricity, lack of access to health care, lack of access to safe water, lack of access to schools, lack of access to safe shelter), - Population has low income opportunities and a lack of availability of food, - Recent outbreaks of communicable diseases. Within these communities, any person with a mobility impairment will be assessed for suitability for an assistive device; no individual prioritisation criteria are used for this. The CHWs are part of Medair’s Health intervention of rehabilitation of PHC facilities and the implementation of a community health program. The CHWs primarily provide health education through household visits, but also identify households with PLWMIs that are supported through caregiver training, assessment for assistive device provision and possible referral to physiotherapy or other services in the area covered by the project (incl. PSS and/or community centres). Medair’s scope for this project is limited to support of people with mobility impairments. Appropriate assistive devices including wheelchairs (children/adult sizes), elbow crutches, rollators, canes, raised toilet seats, therapeutic wedges, foldable beds and chairs will be distributed depending on identified needs. Special hygiene kits that include adult incontinence pads and barrier cream will be distributed if continence needs are identified in assessments. Kit contents have been adapted in response to beneficiary feedback after previous actions. The special hygiene kits are gender neutral; however, Medair also provides female hygiene kits for women of reproductive age that support menstrual hygiene needs (female hygiene kits are covered in the NFI submission). Post distribution monitoring will be carried out to monitor acceptability of items provided and beneficiary satisfaction with the distribution process. Medair’s field staff have been trained on PFA principles to provide basic psychosocial support on site, as well as to recognise when more specialist PSS is needed. Medair will continue working with protection actors to map local referral pathways and facilitate referrals for PLWMI and their families to other services, including PSS support providers, livelihood projects and community centres. However, referral practices remain challenging if not facilitated through the appropriate approvals.Medair will support referrals for specialized physiotherapy input if clear vulnerability and selection criteria (including the potential to benefit from specialist input) are met. Budget provision for referral support includes the cost of specialized treatment, physiotherapy from external provider (although usually free services are provided) and transport to and from appointments. Number of cases of physiotherapy referral for people with mobility impairments will total 112. Supported PLWMIs will be re-evaluated by Medair disability officer to document progress and assess whether further therapy is indicated.

Protection Risk Analysis

General• Staff are not aware of protection issues and do not develop programmes to mitigate risks. Internal training undertaken across all staff (male and female) to ensure awareness of protection issues and to improve the project team’s ability to identify and mitigate protection risks. • Confidentiality of sensitive beneficiary data is breached, risking stigmatisation of individuals/households. Data collected using Open Data Kit (ODK) is encrypted; awareness raising among staff of data protection principles; staff to ensure privacy during consultations.Beneficiary Selection• Perception of or actual discrimination or unfair selection of beneficiaries for household-level interventions. Clear community sensitization on details of the intervention (targeting people with mobility impairment only); independent and targeted Medair assessment or verification by Medair staff of any beneficiary assessment provided by 3rd parties; explanation in advance to beneficiary communities; use of sign boards during distributions or work phases, including contact info for feedback; post distribution or post project monitoring directly by Medair staff.• General aid diversion. Mitigate by ensuring local context well understood and all stakeholders mapped in assessment. Establish strong links with authorities and operators; joint planning and written agreements on intended beneficiaries with the MoH rehabilitation department. Medair does not exclude patients that do not have civil status documents, but based intervention purely on needs.Gender• Head of house reluctant to accept household interventions, including the support of PLWMI. Medair teams (including CHWs, surveyors, physiotherapists) are predominantly female (85%) to achieve acceptance for HH visits.• GBV towards community volunteers or risk of accusations against volunteers: Always make HH visits in pairs, where possible team of male and female CHWs. All Medair staff and short term staff are trained on PSEA. Reporting systems are in place internally, both in the Medair Syria structure and a confidential email reporting hotline allowing all staff to report concerns directly to the GSO (Global Support Office).Disability• Inequity of access by people with disabilities: Training for facility health workers and CHWs. • Culture and stigma around members of families who have disabilities: there is a risk that those households/families will not admit that they have a disabled family member. Community workers build trust with communities and families and have been trained to sensitively engage and contribute to inclusion of the disabled.• Further stigmatization of families with disabled family members once those family members have been identified and access services because of prevalent cultural perception: Include variety of community stakeholders and the wider community in inclusion and advocacy training/awareness raising activities• Distribution of assistive devices without adequate assessment and beneficiary/carer education may inadvertently cause harm, e.g. if devices are used inappropriately. Assessments will be made by trained CHWs and reviewed by a physiotherapist; an external disability consultant will provide training and on the job supervision to ensure quality service delivery. Post distribution monitoring of beneficiary feedback.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

ASSUMPTIONS- No significant change to the political and security situation inside Syria and the GoS continue to hold control over recently-retaken governorates- No excessive fluctuations affecting value of the Syrian pound and any inflation/deflation will be able to be offset against hard currency (USD)- Acceptance and participation of authorities, local leaders and communities during interventions- Technical agreements, including health facility design specifications, and/or Memorandums of Understanding (MoU) with PHCs and other relevant actors are adhered to- Equipment in specifications listed by Ministries will be available at the time of procurement- No major deterioration in logistical conditions, supply chain for essential supplies remains reliable, necessary goods approved for transport- Referral facilities with appropriately trained staff and adequate equipment are accessible from the targeted communitiesRISKS- Harsh weather conditions temporarily reduce access or delay implementation- Temporary suspension of programme if insecurity status is too high, delaying project delivery- Medair cannot secure approvals from ministries within the timeline outlined, delaying project delivery- Medair staff or contractors submit fraudulent expenses or do not comply sufficiently with procurement guidelines leading to a loss of financial resource. MITIGATING MEASURES:- Conduct robust, regular context analysis as part of security management- Keep security planning up to date and train a crisis management team for each location - Beneficiaries to be well informed of the level of support Medair provides to manage expectations- Daily, careful monitoring of security situation (INSO, UNDSS, National Staff etc.) including close liaison with local stakeholders on security issues - Proactive coordination with Ministries,SARC, other agencies and UN to avoid duplication- Maintain good contact with SARC, Ministries, local authorities, communities, UN agencies and other stakeholders- Balance activities for IDPs, returnees and host population to avoid unnecessary tensions, noting that lead factor for beneficiary selection is vulnerability not status- Complete training for all new staff on procurement guidelines, systematic internal and external audits to assess adherence to financial and logistic guidelines, spot checks for any fraudulent activity.- Training on PSEA of all Medair staff and short term staff to guarantee adequate sensitisation.
