Project Summary

Zimbabwe is currently experiencing a widespread emergency caused predominantly by drought and aggravated by poor farming practices, poor economic conditions, pests and diseases that has led to extensive food insecurity. The drought has devastated food production, depleted pastures, disrupted markets, and left 5.5 million people food insecure . Due to the population’s overreliance on agriculture and farming, and a prolonged dry period, much of the country has inadequate access to food and needs emergency assistance. Low food production has inflated the prices of a staple crops which has further increased food insecurity. The WFP Global Hunger map (Jan 2020) classified Zimbabwe as experiencing very high prevalence of insufficient food intake (above 40% of the population). In typical deficit-producing areas, Crisis (IPC Phase 3) outcomes are expected in the post-harvest period due to below normal harvests and limited humanitarian food assistance. A FEWSNET Report shows that the prolonged dry spells between Oct to Dec 2019, is ranked the driest on record in most parts of the country and the prospect of a good field crop harvest is very low in most marginalized areas- leading to high food insecurity.
World Vision (WV) will specifically address factors that exacerbate farmers’ vulnerabilities during this time of drought. This include pest and diseases, and post-harvest losses. Pest, including the fall armyworm (FAW) and African armyworm (AAW), specifically destroy maize and sorghum, staple foods which reduce yields. Post-harvest loss is largely a consequence of storage facilities/methods that do not protect against rodents and insect pests, which can cause more than double the normal losses. Other drivers of postharvest losses include poor timing of harvest, moisture management, and threshing or shelling techniques. Given that pest damage and postharvest losses are already existing vulnerabilities in Zimbabwe, WV expects the drought to severely impact the emergency needs of households (HHs) in Zimbabwe. The ongoing COVID-19 pandemic will exacerbate food needs since it limits alternative livelihood options.
WV will focus its efforts on increasing agricultural production and improving food security to address ongoing emergency needs as they relate to the current drought and to increase community resilience to future droughts. By increasing agricultural production among 3,820 small holder beneficiaries facing climate change induced droughts in Nkayi, Mberengwa, Gwanda, and Umguza provinces, WV will tackle food insecurity which will increase the supply of food for HHs. HHs can sell surplus in the market which will increase supply of food in markets and potentially reduce market prices in target areas. Additionally, the trained farmers will be able to carry skills learned into the future and mitigate risk to future climate change induced droughts in Zimbabwe. WV will accomplish this by integrating preparedness and disaster risk reduction into activities to increase resilience so HHs can withstand the impact of future droughts.

1 - Activities

1.1.1. Support the rehabilitation and establishment of horticultural/field crop infrastructure: The project will rehabilitate and establish 8 irrigation schemes and 32 nutrition gardens to benefit 1,120 farmers. The irrigation schemes will also support hygiene infrastructure to also prevent the spread of COVID 19 through tap water provision at garden and irrigation sites.Support will vary depending on farmer needs, but the main support will be fencing material, horticultural seeds, garden equipment and construction of gravity canals (irrigation schemes). New infrastructure will be restricted to nutrition gardens. This infrastructure mitigates the impact of the drought and prevents communities from experiencing the worst effects of future droughts. 1.1.2. Field crop and horticulture training: In response to knowledge and skills gaps and the need for efficient water management techniques as a result of climate change and increased rainfall variability; the project will train 120 lead farmers on improved farming methods, with a particular focus on Conservation Agriculture (CA) and promoting drought resistant crops suitable for the target areas such as sorghum, finger millet, short season maize variety, pearl millet and cowpeas. The 120 lead farmers will cascade the training to 1,680 farmers in Farmer Field Schools (FFS). Garden and irrigation scheme beneficiaries from Activity 1.1.1. Will receive horticulture training with a focus on high value crops, to improve their production and produce marketing capacity. Additional training focusing on targeted field crops will be provided to irrigation scheme beneficiary farmers. 1.1.3: Establishment Demonstration Plots and Farmer Field Schools: The project will organize the targeted 1,800 farmers into 60 Farmer Fields Schools (FFS) consisting of groups of farmers getting together to study conservation agriculture, organic agriculture and soil husbandry, providing opportunities for learning by doing and teaching basic agricultural and management skills that will make farmers experts in their own learnings. The lead farmers trained in 1.1.2 will coordinate day-to-day operations of the FFS will technical assistance from AGRITEX extension and project staff. 1.1.4: Facilitate the establishment of 40 Asset Management Committees (AMC- asset based): each rehabilitated/created asset will have an AMC, which will be responsible for coordinating the maintenance of the asset. The activities under this sub-sector aim to improve food security of 1,800 farmers most affected by drought through increasing capacity in field and horticultural production.2.
