Project Summary

In Syria, 14.9M people require health assistance (HNO/HRP 2024 Health) amid deteriorating services and minimal development investment. Ongoing conflict, airstrikes, and shelling impede humanitarian access. Overall, 25 attacks on health facilities resulted in the death of 5 civilians and 20 injuries (including 5 health care workers) from January to December 2023. In October 2023, hostilities displaced 120,000 in northern Syria, impacting 40 health facilities. WHO reports low functionality in health facilities, a 70% healthcare worker brain drain, and a 29% disability rate. The multifaceted crisis in Syria creates continuous cycles of vulnerability, disproportionately impacting those most in need, including individuals with disabilities. According to HNAP, in 2022, 69 per cent of households with at least one member with disability reported lack of meaningful access to health services and higher spending on health care and medical expenses compared to other households. s. The 2023 MSNA in northern Syria shows that 44 per cent of households with at least one member with disability reported health services as an unmet need and 42 per cent reported medicine as a top unmet need. Moreover, there are 3,185 individuals registered as persons with disabilities across 10 IDP sites in north-east Syria, however most of the infrastructure and basic services on sites are generally not “accessible”, therefore potentially excluding persons with disabilities from key services, including health services.
There are no accurate statistics about access to rehabilitation services in Syria. However, WHO estimates that over 50% of individuals in low- and middle-income countries lack necessary rehabilitation services, with only 10% globally having access to required assistive technology. In Syria, internal data analysis indicates that while 60% of women and 55% of men face barriers to receiving essential rehabilitation support, only 8% of women compared to 12% of men have access to assistive devices. Additionally, it underscores the life-threatening consequences of limited or delayed rehabilitation access, especially for women with conflict-related injuries, who face higher mortality rates compared to men. There is a clear correlation between the lack of rehabilitation services and the increased secondary complications for individuals with chronic disabilities. Data from Raqqa National Hospital for 2023 reveals prolonged wait times, with beneficiaries with chronic conditions waiting a minimum of 40 days for their first physical rehabilitation session due to lack of physiotherapists.
On another level, these prolonged crises have exhausted the coping mechanisms of millions of civilians and exposed them to mental distress, in a country where MHPSS remains scarce, and mental illness highly stigmatized. The INGO’s needs analysis (2024) shows that in Syria, almost two in three families are psychologically distressed about their life conditions, living with the fear of conflict and hostilities, displacement, destruction of property, and injury or death of family members. Signs of psychological distress were reportedly higher in female-headed households, with a 15% increase compared to male-headed ones. Moreover, households headed by an individual with disability or having members with disability show a higher prevalence of psychological distress (20% and 25% respectively), indicating the intersectionality of gender and disability in exacerbating mental health challenges.
The intervention prioritizes multidisciplinary rehabilitation and psychosocial support. The INGO supports local health partners with tailored capacity building and inclusion specialist guidance. Operating across Syria, including GoS areas, it focuses on developing multirehabilitation services in underserved regions like Idlib and Aleppo governorate in NWS; Hassakeh, Raqqa and Deir Ez Zor in NES and Homs and rural Damascus in GoS.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Implementation of activities:The intervention includes the provision of physical rehabilitation sessions, Assitive Devices, Prosthesis &Orthosis devices. Health service aim to prevent long-term impairment, promoting independence.The intervention includes the provision of mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services to people suffering from psychological distress with or without having physical rehabilitation needs. MHPSS services include psychological first aid, psycho education, and individual and/or group session therapy.The INGO provides support to its local health partners through technical, financial and managerial support, to improve the quality of services and ensure the sustainability of impact of the project. The capacity building support is tailored to the needs of partners. This project provides services to Syrians within hospital and rehabilitation centers, but as well as mobile teams in NWS, NES, and GoS to increase access for IDPs, underserved communities and persons who are incapable of reaching the service centers. Gender-balanced teams are offering the servicesProtection Risk Analysis: INGO prioritizes staff safety with low visibility, employing a Duty of Care policy during healthcare attacks. PSEA policy is applied, ensuring accountability through investigations. Training addresses vulnerabilities of persons with disabilities, older individuals, and girls/women. Protection and referral task forces optimize mainstreaming. Healthcare provision emphasizes training for staff on interactions with vulnerable groups. Safe facility closure and data protection measures are implemented. Implementing partners undergo thorough assessments, with reporting, monitoring, and feedback mechanisms in place. PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy The INGO is committed to combating sexual exploitation, abuse, and harassment. Robust policies and a Code of Conduct are in place, with mandatory and refresher training. Recruitment includes background checks, and PSEAH policies apply to staff, partners, and accompanying individuals. Information sessions for beneficiaries, complaint mechanisms, and gender balance in staff address cultural sensitivity. Any partner involved in abuse faces case follow-up and termination unless a drastic behavioral change is ensured. Risks, assumptions and mitigation measures risks: Risks in the intervention include cross-border resolution uncertainty, security deterioration, incidents involving staff, aid diversion, and currency depreciation impacting budget. The Syrian pound depreciation and essential goods price surge affect livelihoods. Mitigation involves conflict analysis, contingency planning, security guidelines, advocacy for border access, stock securing, donor communication, and capacity building for local staff. Effective coordination with authorities and diversified pre-selected suppliers helps avoid price fluctuations.
