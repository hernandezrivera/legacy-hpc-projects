Project Summary

This project aims to inform the revised 2023 HRP and focus on addressing lifesaving multispectral assistance and protection services in line with HRP Strategic Objectives (SO) 1 and 2. through the timely provision of lifesaving and protection interventions from Shelter/NFIs, GBV and WASH sectors for the newly internally displaced person and the most affected vulnerable populations in BN, Gedaref and Kassala States. Sudan has been in crisis for years, and in mid-April 2023, fighting between the Sudan Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) escalated into a conflict of alarming scale. The conflict began in Khartoum and quickly spread to other areas across the country, resulting in over 12,000 fatalities. The conflict has caused massive displacement, with over 6.1 million people forced to leave their homes in search of safety elsewhere. Of these, 4.85 million were internally displaced, and over half were children under 18. Sudan currently faces the most significant internal displacement crisis in the world, with 3.8 million IDPs. This war resulted in a deteriorated situation for many people who have lost their livelihoods and urgently need lifesaving protection, essential services and support to restore their lives. This project will provide an integrated multi-sectoral lifesaving response, including protection assistance (GBV) to 28,200 PoC, NFI kits to 6,000 PoC and WASH services to 30,000 PoC. The project targets 64,200 vulnerable IDPs in 6 localities in the three targeted states.
GBV Protection interventions will cover persons with specific needs to help them access proper and safe assistance. Throughout the project, protection monitoring will be conducted in each target locality to identify and track protection concerns and to inform a consolidated protection cluster monitoring at the state and national levels. 3000 Adults will receive group-based mental health and psychosocial support services so parents and adults can have improved psychological well-being by reducing levels of psychological distress, improving daily functioning and ensuring effective coping strategies. Community-based structures will be supported or established to strengthen the community resilience mechanisms. Large-scale public community awareness-raising campaigns on GBV will be conducted through 12 campaigns targeting 6000 people. Providing 3,000 dignity kits to women and girls of reproductive age will help them during their menstrual and improve their hygiene.
Shelter/NFI interventions will be done by providing timely NFI assistance to the most affected people, such as 300 tents and 1000 nonfood item kits, targeting 6,000 individuals.
WASH interventions include the provision of 3000 multipurpose bar soap to households, constructing 60 hand washing facilities, providing 3000 hygiene kits for HHs, conducting six hygiene campaigns targeting 3000 individuals and conducting six vector control campaigns targeting 6000 people.
All these multi-sectoral activities will be implemented in close consultation with target communities and with due consideration to the local capacities to ensure equal and representative participation and information sharing with all stakeholders. Engagement of local communities, civil societies, line ministries, sector/cluster focal points and humanitarian actors at the field level will be prioritized.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Practical Action acknowledge, adopts, and adhere to the protection principles in humanitarian context. PA recognizes that enhancing people's safety, dignity, rights, and avoid exposing them to further harm is a fundamental protection principle and integral part of our programming, design, and delivery. PA also will ensure people's access to impartial assistance according to the need and without discrimination. Furthermore, PA will assist people to recover from the physical and phycological effects of threatened or actual violence, and help people claim their rights. Practical Action ensures understanding and commitment to the safeguarding policies package by all employees, partners and contractors, which states Zero tolerance to all types of sexual exploitation and abuse especially against children, women and vulnerable adults. Complaints system will be put in place to ensure safe and effective communication channel is active for all beneficiaries to raise their concerns. The system includes different tools including hotlines, emails, complaints boxes and others. As communities will be well-oriented and engaged in initial consultations and need assessments, Practical Action will ensure inclusion of the needs of different community groups including women and disabled people to assure that no one is left behind! Doing No harm principle will also be maintained to ensure no negative implications will arise from the project interventions that violate people safety, security, dignity or create damage to the resources.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

To improve and adapt activities, the project will use community feedback in thefollowing ways: 1. The project will work with communities and humanitarianresponders to identify the barriers that prevent feedback from being listened to andacted on. This stage is essential to understand where the problems lie, including lackof staffing, poor communication channels, non-working technology, inadequate datamanagement, and confused information flows. 2. The project will build on existingstaff capacities and work with established services to ensure sustainability. Theproject will identify which activities are already underway, build on these, and identifywhich community members, partners, and staff they can work with. 3. The project willengage with communities through channels they like and trust. The project willconduct an Information and Communication Needs Assessment to identify whichchannels different groups and individuals prefer. 4. The project will have clearcommitment and agreement on roles and responsibilities, both internally and inter-agency. The project will draft Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs), which indicatethe agreed commitments, follow-up timeframe, and different agency/individual roles.5. The project will design its mechanism to collect structured data that enables it tomake decisions and take action at the right time. The project will also engage in adialogue and joint analysis with people about the feedback they provide. This isessential in identifying solutions and managing their expectations about whatprogram adjustments can be made.
