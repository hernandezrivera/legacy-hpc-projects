Project Summary

The proposed project will work towards increasing access to basic and specialized services for the most affected population of persons with disabilities living in area C of the West Bank, and persons with injuries due to great return of March (GRM) in Gaza by providing multidisciplinary rehabilitation services; through outreach and in home services, including but not limited to, basic chronic rehabilitation services, post-operative and psychological services. The package of services will be based on pre-identification and assessment of person’s needs. The project will adopt the interdisciplinary approach safeguarding the combination of well trained and skilled rehabilitation professionals. The project will promote accessibility through the implementation of home adaptation for the beneficiaries who developed permanent disabilities, and also for preidentified primary healthcare centers to increase and ease access of persons with disabilities to those centers to benefit from the services as their peers. This ultimately will improve community participation for the targeted population through progressing of their independency, and regain well recovery. The project will support preparedness efforts via prepositioning of assistive devices and hygiene kits in terms of crisis eruption. Besides, increase rehabilitation mobile teams’ knowledge on safety practices to support mitigation of health attacks (includes movement restrictions) in particular in area C of the targeted governorates of the West Bank. Nonetheless, the project will work on a holistic approach to ensure proper and better access to other needs beyond project deliverables for the targeted beneficiaries through the coordination mechanism of local referral networks. This also will support the implementation of a rehabilitation task force under the health cluster management to ensure that rehabilitation minimum standards are well promoted and at least three local/international actors are adopting those standards through HI intensive follow up and technical support. At the level of prevention, and detection the project will increase the awareness raising on disability mainstreaming for healthcare professionals. In addition, to increase mobile teams’ competencies on gender-based violence Gender based Violence (GBV) detection including boosting referrals mechanism for persons with disabilities to increase their access to the GBV services required as per their identified needs and demands.

1 - Needs

Based on HI and partners’ observation and field experience, persons with disabilities became more neglected in the oPt. Through a rapid analysis to the current collected database, HI’s partners were able to approach 400 persons with disabilities between July and September 2019 in the whole five governorates of the Gaza Strip, and other 180 persons with disabilities on the pending list, waiting their turn to receive post-hospital and rehabilitation care. Currently, partners’ teams used to receive around 15 cases of Cerebrovascular accident. Besides, since the eruption of great return of March (GRM) crisis, HI has been responding and is delivering emergency and post emergency rehabilitation and psychological services to the affected population of persons with injuries. As per HI collected and current database, between June and September 2019, 2,900 persons were approached, including 600 persons with injuries who re-admitted again to receive further treatment as per their individual assessment and wound complication and progressing. As per health cluster report (August 2019), the accumulative figures showed that the final total of persons with injuries stands for 31,338. Of whom 15,588 treated trauma stabilization points (TSPs) and discharged immediately home; while 18,549 casualties arrived at the emergency departments (ED) of ministry of health (MoH) and non-governmental organizations (NGOs) hospitals. Of them, 1,221 were children. Out of the hospitalized patients, only 7,545 cases were gunshot injuries; and including them almost 6,590 were limb gunshot injuries. Those numbers are concerning especially for whom in need intensive care and follow up which stands to 21% from the total proportion of the persons with injuries. The report continued to say that around 149 persons with injuries were lost their parts (122 were lower limb amputations and 27 upper limb amputations). Besides 24 paralysis cases and 15 eye injuries suffered permanent loss of vision. HI expected around 4% of the total persons with injuries to sustain and develop a permanent disability. Being an actor mandated in the field of vulnerability and disability, HI launched an in-depth sectorial assessment (DSA) in early September 2019. The aim of the assessment is to provide evidence to address the challenge of comprehensive rehabilitation needs, including rehabilitation, MHPSS, wound care, assistive devices provision, and referrals, and to analyze and provide discussion on the percentage & types of disability amongst persons with injuries related to (GRM). The study will further nurture the existing knowledgeIn the West Bank, many communities, mainly in Area C and Bedouin communities, are facing difficulties accessing to basic health and rehabilitation care/services due to insecurity, restrictions on movements and checkpoints and violence. A quantitative survey conducted by Bethlehem Arab Society for Rehabilitation (BASR) in area C south of the west bank in 2019, shows the extended needs of persons with disability to access health services and other social services. The respondents indicated that their movement is restricted due to the in accessible transportation, and the political situation of area C. The respondents also stressed on the lack/ absence of interventions for persons with disabilities in area C, the limited role of the government in these areas, beside the weak coordination among other stakeholders in matters of persons with disabilities. The survey was conducted with a total of 262 persons with disabilities, among them 88 with physical impairments, 230 persons who has transportation restrictions, at least 186 are in financial catastrophic, and a total of 186 restricted to access services due to the absence of service providers.

2 - Activities

Output 1: Persons with injuries and people with disabilities have increased independency and have improved access to basic rehabilitation, health, and psychosocial services through a multidisciplinary outreach and center-based services in line with the continuum of care. Activities: 1.1. Provision of multidisciplinary outreach and center-based rehabilitation services support for 1,500 persons with disabilities (PWDs) and 500 persons with injuries (700 females and 1,300 males) in Gaza and WB targeted governorates; 1.2. Training and follow up of at least 2,000 family members and caregivers (1,200 females and 800 males) on physiotherapy, occupational therapy services and psychological activities to support PWDs and persons with injuries in Gaza and West Bank targeted governorates; 1.3. Technical support to partners’ rehabilitation teams though monthly technical trainings, quarterly exchange meetings and monitoring field visits in Gaza and West Bank targeted governorates; 1.4. Purchase and donation of 450 assistive devices, and 450 hygiene kits “including diapers” for the targeted beneficiaries of PWDs and persons with injuries as per their identified needs in Gaza and West Bank targeted governorates; 1.5. prepositioning of 200 assistive devices, and 200 hygiene kits “including diapers” to support affected population of PWDs and persons with injuries in any crisis in Gaza targeted governorates; 1.6. Purchase medical materials and nursing disposables (100 kits) to support post-operative care and wound dressing services for PWDs and persons with injuries in Gaza; 1.7. Support independency through reasonable accessible works to 40 houses of PWDs in Gaza to enhance mobility and daily life activities; 1.8. Support and improve access to healthcare services for persons with disability through implementation of basic accessibility work in 10 Primary health Care Clinics (PHCCs) in the West Bank. Output 2: Support continuum of health care through referral. Activities 2.1: Support Rehabilitation task force for coordination and collaboration with active rehabilitation and post-operative actors to standardize rehabilitation intervention and boost referrals for persons with injuries in Gaza targeted governorates; 2.2: Conduct awareness raising sessions on disability mainstreaming for healthcare professionals (UNRWA, MoH, Mobile clinics) in collaboration with disability working group (DWG) in Gaza targeted governorates, and in coordination with the partner (BASR) in West Bank targeted governorates; 2.3: Provide technical training to rehabilitation mobile teams on safety practices to support mitigation the risk attacks on health workers(includes movement restrictions) in Gaza and West Bank targeted governorates; 2.4: Provide training for mobile outreach, and center based Rehab teams, on GBV detection & referral for persons with disability in collaboration with GBV working group in West Bank; 2.5: Provide awareness raising on disability for the targeted community on how to interact with PWDs, and create disability focal points in each community through advance disability training in the West Bank targeted communities; 2.6: Support vulnerable people to access acute center-based health and rehabilitation services through covering transportation and service cost in Gaza and West Bank targeted governorates

3 - Indicators

The following indicators will measure the progress and impact of the project: Outcome: At least 75% of outreach and center-based teams' practices are respected and in line with intervention agreed quality criteria; Output 1.1 : I1 2,000 PWDs and persons with injuries (700 females and 1,300 males) will be provided with rehabilitation and psychosocial support comprehensive rehabilitation sessions in the targeted governorates (400 women, 200 girls, 100 elderly women, 300 boys, 800 men, and 200 elderly men); I2: At least 75% of targeted persons with injuries and PWDs receiving comprehensive rehabilitation and psychosocial support report satisfaction on the services received; I3: At least 80% of beneficiaries that received assistive devices’ beneficiaries report an improvement in their movement independency and participation in daily life activities; I4 At least 40 houses of PWDs and persons with injuries are adapted as per their identified needs; I5 of PHC centers benefitted from accessible works; Output 1.2: I1: At least 75% of trained healthcare professionals and rehabilitation workers report an increase of knowledge of identification and referral of PWDs; I2: At least 50% of detected PWDs survivors of GBV successfully referred by mobile teams in coordination with the GBV working group; I3 At least 50% of detected PWDs survivors of GBV have successful referrals by mobile teams in coordination with the GBV working group. Apart of the planned activities abovementioned, and in order to build partners capacities towards better and effective response; HI had conducted a comprehensive capacity assessment to project identified partners in Gaza and West Bank in APR19. The whole process resulted of development to a Support Plan that reflected the areas in need for improvement with partners consultations. Those needs were prioritized and timely planned by partners with HI support. The main reason behinds this aimed at providing partners’ organization with the necessarily knowledge and skills that will develop and elevate partners capacities in the field of the intervention. HI maintains good collaboration with humanitarian actors in the field of the intervention, where checking of assistive devices service provision shall be verified through different means such as available database within partners organizations, and communicating with local referral networks members.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The ‘Do No Harm’ principles have been considered at different levels in relation with both the services and the individuals, by considering gender differences, by promoting local community ownership of the project through local partnerships. A psychologist will be part of the rehabilitation service team and will be trained to adequately answer psychological needs of affected persons. The tools used for the need’s assessment will be tailored to suit both Gaza and West Bank context and specificity of persons with injuries and persons with disabilities.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project will be implemented in support to local organizations within partnerships agreements. HI has developed strong local partnerships supporting access to beneficiaries. Some criteria of partners’ selection were the location of partner’s centers in order to cover the identified areas. The local partners' teams will allow access to remote areas and will be recruited within their communities, ensuring though fine understanding of the context and public acceptance.
