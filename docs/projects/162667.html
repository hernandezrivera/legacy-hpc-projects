Project Summary

The IRC plans to deliver an integrated emergency health program of primary healthcare, nutrition and WASH activities, with an expanded essential package that ensures delivery of sexual, reproductive, maternal and newborn health, child health including immunization, non-communicable diseases, mental health and psychosocial support, and communicable disease control, including where relevant outbreak surveillance and response. For nutrition, the IRC will deliver a complete CMAM package. IRC will set up and/or strengthen existing case management services for moderate and severe acute malnutrition for children and pregnant and lactating women (PLW). The IRC will ensure the existence of active Stabilization Centers, OTPs, TSFPs, and community activities to raise awareness, and screen and refer malnourished children and PLW. The IRC’s WASH program will provide lifesaving WASH services, minimizing hazards related to seasonal flooding, and responding to water-borne and vector-borne disease outbreaks. WASH activities will be delivered at the health facility and community level comprising of safe water supply, sanitation, waste management, hygiene promotion, and vector control.
FSL - The IRC will provide emergency food and agricultural assistance as well as longer term agricultural and non-agricultural livelihoods support. The IRC will consider modalities of cash, vouchers, in-kind and training packages (for agricultural support). The IRC will layer immediate food assistance needs with agricultural support focusing on those in IPC classifications 3 and 4. In smaller villages the IRC will assess 100% of the population in need and consider a blanket distribution assistance approach to prevent creating tensions within the community; while in urban areas the IRC will work with local relief committees / local organizations to support in the identification of vulnerable households.
Child Protection - The IRC will work on community-based child protection systems, expanding access to and quality of specialized services, including case management services, with focus placed on UASC and CAAFAG. IRC will establish Safe Healing and Learning Spaces (SHLS) and Supporting Adolescent and their Families in Emergencies (SAFE) program spaces for adolescents. SHLS is a secure, caring place where children and adolescents can learn and be protected, for stronger social and emotional skills and reductions in violence in the home. Both programs use a Parenting Skills Intervention which improves parents’ stress management. IRC will also train local actors on child protection minimum standards.
Protection Rule of Law - IRC will establish community-based protection (CBP) programs which integrate participation, empowerment and resilience. The project will seek local capacities, perceptions of problems and ideas about solutions, and work with community actors to support, activate and expand the capacities for self-protection. The IRC will build the capacity in protection core concepts and safe identification and referrals, protection risk analysis, non-violent conflict resolution, and participatory action planning. The IRC will establish community structures and training of focal points.
GBV Prevention and Response - The IRC will deliver services to meet women and girls’ needs in response to violence working with health and social service providers, and providing direct case management. The IRC will train GBV caseworkers in guiding principles and survivor-centered approaches, and will establish private spaces to receive survivors. The IRC will ensure safe, confidential storage of all client information. IRC will also do service mapping and develop referral pathways as well as psychosocial services and prevention activities. The IRC will work with women’s groups for provision of basic psychosocial services. The IRC will establish safe spaces for women and girls to exchange information and get support.
All projects will work toward a RRR approach over time.

1 - Needs

Needs across Sudan are multi-sectoral and interlinked - none can be looked at in isolation. Poor nutrition outcomes result in health and protection issues, etc. It is thus important to ensure close links.Health needs are massive, reflecting weaknesses in both responses – to outbreaks, natural disasters or conflict – as well as in basic service delivery. Preparedness is weak, leading to reactive responses, while service provision is hampered by poor infrastructure, supply chain and staffing/salary gaps. Vaccination rate across Sudan is extremely low, around 60%, and many areas remain hard to access. The underlying gaps reveal a deep need for health system strengthening approaches to move away from a reactive implementation mode. The nutrition situation is dire, with food insecurity, poor WASH conditions, and health system collapse leading to over 2.4M children suffering from acute malnutrition. SAM and MAM treatment is required and children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women are at particular risk; micronutrient deficiency is common. WASH programming needs to be prioritized alongside health and nutrition to address underlying causes of illness – there are high rates of oral-faecal contamination and the rapid spread of vector-borne diseases could be slowed with improved water and sanitation provision. Health facilities often lack proper WASH support, and disposal of medical waste needs urgent attention. FSL needs are as high as ever, with a record number of people in IPC 3 and 4. Millions of people are not able to meet their daily food needs, and the economic crisis is worsening the situation. People lack capital and assets to purchase or cultivate enough nutritious food. Farmers in many areas struggle with access to their land due to conflict and natural disaster; while the harvest was relatively good this year, other factors are outweighing this. Families need access to food, vouchers/cash, and agricultural/market inputs to meet daily needs and reestablish productive livelihoods strategies. The protection status of those in need is extremely precarious – decades of conflict, neglect and changing environment have left people with poor and unequal access to services; many people are unable to achieve even basic rights. In addition, community trust and cohesion has suffered as a result of conflict and suffering, so there is a need to rebuild the social contract, where people are empowered to ask for and demand their rights, and service providers are equipped to deliver. There are also basic gaps in documentation, and large challenges in the area of HLP. There are a number of interventions required to address child protection. As armed conflict draws to a close, children associated with armed forces and armed groups (CAAFAG) need tailored support to address stigma and post-traumatic stress as they return to their families and try to resume schooling. Children and adolescents lack safe spaces and professional facilitation to address toxic stress through social and emotional learning. Decades of conflict and displacement mean there are large numbers of unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) who need support in reunification or alternative structures. Gender-based violence is a huge problem across Sudan, with very few actors positioned to respond. Until recently, there was little acceptance of such programming. Issues range from child marriage to attack by armed groups. Domestic violence increases during times of conflict, meaning that programming to reduce intimate partner violence (IPV) is essential. Women also have little access to safe spaces where they can come together and express their experiences to work toward shared healing. As peace negotiations continue and Sudan enters a new era, where possible projects will incorporate and/or work toward relief, resilience and rehabilitation. 2019 already saw a rise in returns for both IDPs and refugees, and RRR work will be important to enable durable solutions.

2 - Activities / Outputs

The IRC will implement a robust response including health, nutrition, WASH, FSL and protection to ensure full aspects of life-saving and well-being needs are addressed with a protection lens. The IRC, where possible, prefers to address health in the broadest sense, taking into account primary health, reproductive health, WASH, nutrition and health system strengthening. This blended approach will help address both preventive and curative needs, for example improving water quality can reduce vector-borne diseases, which then improves health outcomes. Adding protection ensures that quality of life and specific challenges around access to and equity of services can be taken into consideration. Activities will be implemented using a mix of direct implementation and work with local partners. Local partners will be supported with organizational capacity strengthening alongside technical support. For health the IRC will use a combination of mobile and static services, and will include technical support to the MoH/health systems, including a rapid response capacity. Activities will include:• Support to clinics, including staffing, medical supplies and medicines• Life-saving curative and preventive care• Newborn and child health• Outreach and mobile clinics as needed• Communicable and non-communicable disease interventions• Obstetric care• Outbreak surveillance and response• Care for survivors of gender-based violence; consider links with WPENutrition will include:• Full CMAM package• Case management services for moderate and severe acute malnutrition for children and PLW• SCs, OTPs and TFSP• Community activities to raise awareness and screen and refer malnourished children and PLW WASH:• WASH response at health facilities and community level• Safe water supply• Sanitation• Waste management• Hygiene promotion• Vector control For FSL, activities will include:• Cash/voucher programming • Provision of immediate life-saving materials• Provision of agriculture inputs• Food and cash for assets• Specialized support to women, including female-headed households, according to their specific needs Protection:• Engage community in community-based protection and establish structures• Conflict sensitivity and reduction awareness and trainings• Conduct community-led protection risk analysis• Provide individual protection assistance• Support community-level micro-project interventions• Legal officer to deliver conflict resolution trainings and support• Engagement on issues of access to justice, civil documentation, and opportunities for paralegal training in follow on actions. Child protection:• Improve community-based child protection systems and structures• Case management for UASC and CAAFAG• Establishing Safe Healing and Learning Spaces (SHLS) and Supporting Adolescent and their Families in Emergencies (SAFE) program spaces for adolescents. • Facilitate/teach social and emotional skills and reading and math• Provide Parenting Skills programs • Train local actors on child protection minimum standards and safe identification and referrals. Gender-based violence: • Train GBV caseworkers in guiding principles and survivor-centered approach• Establish confidential spaces to receive survivors• Establish a case management system and safe, confidential storage of all client information. • Mapping of available and viable case management services• Development of age-appropriate referral pathways• Development and dissemination of key messages on available services.• Collaboration with RH program on CMR, CCSAS (clinical care for sexual assault survivors)

5 - Environment Marker

B+: Medium environmental impact with mitigation

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Please note the IRC just recently returned to Sudan and will conduct environmental impact assessments prior to beginning programming, with support from UNEP. Globally, the IRC uses best practice to ensure light footprint programming, using local materials where possible, and with a focus on sanitation and waste management for any sector of programming. IRC's conflict analysts conduct pre-project stakeholder mapping, including considerations of environmental factors and changing climate, and then develop context-appropriate strategies. Given the high impact of climate change on Sudan already, this will be carefully considered. All programming follows SPHERE standards and any agreed country-wide guidance.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The IRC, as a global protection lead actor, ensures a protection lens across sectors, and through directly implementing protection programming alongside other sectors. Do No Harm and Social Network Analyses are done prior to programming, and all staff are trained on protection, GBV, and child protection core concepts. PSEA training is also done.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Protection of our beneficiaries and the integration of gender equality is of paramount importance to the IRC- particularly as it may be perpetrated by humanitarian workers, including IRC employees, volunteers, interns, Board Members, incentive workers, partners, and all those that work on behalf of our mission (hereby the “Actors”). IRC is guided in its preventive efforts by the UN Secretary-General’s Bulletin on “Special Measures for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Sexual Abuse,” published in 2003 and incorporates the Bulletin’s six core principles, in addition to a prohibition on all types of exploitation and abuse of our beneficiaries, in IRC’s Beneficiary Protection Policy. Additionally, in accordance with Articles 3 and 19 and of the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child (UNCRC), a Child Safeguarding Policy is in effect to ensure all Actors do no harm to children, and to ensure that safety risks and cases of misconduct are identified, reported, and addressed in an appropriate and timely manner. Further, the IRC’s core values are entirely opposed to the trafficking in persons and considers such action to be a fundamental and grave violation of human rights. The Combatting Trafficking in Persons policy and associated compliance plan affirm the IRC’s commitment to safeguard its operations and ensure that its staff are not victims of or engaged in the crime of trafficking and compliance with statutes and regulations aimed at combatting trafficking in persons.
