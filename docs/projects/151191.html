Project Summary

The present action aims to contribute to addressing the healthcare needs of vulnerable conflict-affected population residing in Eastern Ukraine. Core activities are (1) the provision of essential medical equipment to 40 medical facilities targeting 15,000 individuals, and (2) targeted cash assistance for transportation to medical facilities for 2,500 people in vulnerable situations (elderly, people with disabilities, low income households and large families with three and more children).

Needs

According to the REACH Multi-Sector Needs Assessment in the Non-Government Controlled Areas (NGCA) of Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts, October 2017, even though the majority of health facilities are functioning and accessible in NGCA, infrastructure is damaged due to the protracted conflict in Ukraine; Since the beginning of the conflict there has been an increase in infectious diseases such as tuberculosis. According to the Multi-Sector Needs Assessment in the Non-Government Controlled Areas (NGCA) of Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts, October 2017, these diseases continue to be on the rise due to the lack of treatment. Moreover, the rise of diseases such as flu and cold especially during the winter period contribute to dramatically increased queues in hospitals; Deferring from going to the doctor is the second most used coping strategy (reported by 48% of respondents) due to lack of money after purchasing cheaper and less preferred food, according to the post-distribution monitoring of IOM’s cash programme carried out in June 2018. Moreover, as observed during home visits, even though some feldsher points continue to operate in limited capacity in locations close to the contact line, there is a large number of individuals requiring specialized medical assistance which cannot be provided at the feldsher points. Due to lack of resources, individuals often choose not to travel to the doctor; According to the Situation Overview: Winter Assessment of Government-Controlled Areas within 5km of the Line of Contact presented by REACH in February 2018, Thirty-eight per cent (38%) of rural households reported the need to travel more than 25km to access multi-speciality hospitals; in addition, forty-two per cent (42%) of the households within 5km of the contact line face difficulties in accessing health care. Longer distance and difficulty of travelling to health facilities are main challenges vulnerable households faced with.

Activities & outcomes

Outcome: IDPs, returnees and members of host communities have improved access to essential health care services through provision of medical equipment and cash assistance for transport to medical facilities. Output 1.1. Medical facilities in the targeted communities in Donetsk and Lugansk regions (NGCA) have improved capacity and sustainability to address the healthcare needs of the vulnerable population - Activities for Output 1.1.: 1)Select medical facilities and assess infrastructure needs in coordination with relevant de facto authorities in Lugansk and Donetsk regions (NGCA); 2) Carry out procurement and distribution of the essential medical equipment among the medical facilities located in NGCA; 3)Conduct post-distribution monitoring (PDM) of distribution of essential medical equipment to assess the efficiency and effectiveness of the assistance provided. The list of essential medical equipment will be discussed with each targeted medical facility separately. During the focus group discussion that took place in the medical institutions where the WASH rehabilitation works conducted under previous IOM projects, X-ray machines, photo roentgenographs, cardiographs, microscopes, autoclaves, hot-air sterilizers, ultrasonography machines, hemodialysis systems, etc. were mentioned as the most requested one. Output 1.2. Vulnerable households benefited from conditional cash assistance to cover their transport costs to specialized health facilities - Activities for output 1.2: 1) Identify beneficiary households in need of cash assistance to access specialized medical institutions defined in coordination with local counterparts, stakeholders and humanitarian actors within the cluster system; 2) Disburse targeted cash assistance to selected beneficiaries based on the visit performed to specialized medical institution; 3) Operate dedicated hotline for the registration of beneficiaries, consultation during the project implementation and feedback; 4) Conduct PDM of the cash assistance to assess the efficiency and effectiveness of the assistance provided. The calculation taken for cash assistance to cover the transportation costs to specialized health institutions is 10 USD per case. However the actual compensation amount will be calculated based on the distance between the location of beneficiary residence and medical facility visited. The amount of assistance is solely targeting compensation of transportation costs for vulnerable health cases since limited access to medical health facilities has appeared as the result of the ongoing conflict.

Indicators & targets

Outcome: IDPs, returnees and members of host communities have improved access to essential health care services through provision of medical equipment and cash assistance for transport to medical facilities - 1) % of beneficiaries reporting that the action has contributed to increasing their access to healthcare services (Target: 75%).Output 1.1: Medical facilities in the targeted communities in Donetsk and Lugansk regions (NGCA) have improved capacity and sustainability to address the healthcare needs of the vulnerable population: 1) # of medical facilities provided with equipment (Target: 50); 2) # of beneficiaries in medical facilities benefiting from the provision of essential equipment (Target: 15,000 individuals including 9,000 female and 6,000 male as well as out of them 1,500 children, 4,500 adults and 9,000 elderly). Output 1.2. Vulnerable households benefited from conditional cash assistance to cover their transport costs to specialised health facilities: 1) # of individual supported with cash assistance for vulnerable health cases (Target: 2,500 individuals including 1,500 female and 1,000male; as well as out of them 250 children, 750 adults and 1,500 elderly); 2) % of surveyed beneficiaries satisfied with the procedure and the modality of the transfers (Target: 75%) Main target groups in this project are those meeting the following vulnerability criteria: elderly, persons with disabilities and families with 3+ children, and single-headed households with children. The project will ensure that both men and women benefit equally; it aims to target not only female single headed households, but also male single headed households where possible. Also, beneficiaries living with chronic illnesses will be targeted by this component; priority will be given to members of vulnerable households that are in need of medical examination or treatment that is not accessible in the location of their residence. Moreover, the project intends to address the crucial needs of medical facilities both in urban and rural areas regarding essential equipment (such as cardiographs, microscopes, autoclaves, hot-air sterilizers, ultrasonography machines, haemodialysis systems, etc.), prioritizing facilities situated along the contact line, with high importance to the communities and located in remote rural areas with a significant number of population. In addition to the regular monitoring of project actions, targeted PDM will be carried out after conditional cash assistance disbursement through phone surveys (10% of beneficiaries) and household visits (5% of beneficiaries) to collect sex and age disaggregated quantitative and qualitative data. This will enable IOM to extrapolate information on the level of satisfaction among and the project’s impact on beneficiaries – particularly women, girls and other vulnerable categories.
