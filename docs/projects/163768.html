Project Summary

It is now more than eight years since the fall of the Gaddafi regime. Libya continues to suffer from political division and stalemate that increasingly leads to armed conflict and economic suffering for its population. Residential, Agricultural, and Industrial areas in Libya are affected by large amounts of Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) resulting from repeated armed conflicts since 2011. This engenders a direct threat to local communities, prevents the safe return of Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), and impedes reconstruction and livelihoods. Thus, the prompt response of Humanitarian Mine Action (HMA) actors to conduct clearance of ERW and raise awareness is vital to the safety of these communities, especially once they return and seek to rebuild. There areas most affected are Tripoli and surroundings, Benghazi, Tawergha, Sirte and Derna. In this project 3F will conduct Explosive Ordnance Disposal, Battle Area Clearance and Risk Education activities according to the national priorities as decided by the Libyan Mine Action center with Teams based in Tripoli, Misrata, and Benghazi, with the ability to deploy across West, Central, and East regions.
Through ERW clearance and Risk Education, the project aims to achieve the following outcomes:
• Outcome 1: Expedient safe and sustainable return of displaced communities in Tripoli and surroundings and facilitation of immediate rehabilitation efforts and return of basic services after the conclusion of the conflict
• Outcome 2: Increased Safety and Security for Targeted communities

1 - Needs

Residential, Agricultural, and Industrial areas in Libya are affected by large amounts of Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) resulting from repeated armed conflicts since 2011. This engenders a direct threat to the physical safety of local communities, prevents the safe return of Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), and impedes reconstruction, basic services, and livelihoods. Moreover, the presence of ERW can hinder Aid groups as they seek to address needs in other sectors. These physical threats need to be removed to lower the risk of injury and facilitate recovery efforts, while at the same time Awareness raised in the community to understand the threat and learn how to avoid it. According to available data, from the IMSMA Database at LibMAC about mine and ERW accidents in Libya since 2011 more than 90% of victims are male, with young men being the most likely victims. Additionally school age girls are the most likely female victims.Furthermore, 3F seeks to take into consideration the differing needs of men, women, and children when planning its interventions. In all communities where 3F works it will seek to understand the differences in behaviour of different groups to target the most at-risk individuals. Although available data suggests that women and girls are at lower risk of injury from ERW, this could be a result of under-reporting due to several factors (conservative culture or failure by mine action actors to reach them). Specific local behaviour patterns may put women at higher risk.

2 - Activities

3F has existing open task orders for Benghazi, Misrata, Tawergha, Tripoli, and Gharyan for Explosive Ordnance Disposal (EOD) Spot tasks.EOD spot task refers to the detection, identification, evaluation, render safe, recovery and disposal of explosive ordnance. For the proposed project, 3F teams will conduct EOD spot tasks to clear single items of ordnance using a number of different techniques depending on the type and condition of the ordnance. Ordnance are located through liaison with the community and via 3F’s hotline. This is often through meetings with local notables or during Non-Technical Survey (NTS) or Risk Education Campaigns. 3F employs local volunteer mobilizers in each community it works in to deliver school-based or community-based risk education sessions to their own communities. Local mobilizers, as members of the community, are better positioned to understand the specific needs of their community and how to build trust and encourage reporting about ERW than employees coming from other cities or neighborhoods. NTS refers to the surveying and categorizing of suspected hazardous areas through mapping and community liaison to determine the type of contamination and the appropriate clearance response.Additionally, the teams can be combined or be expanded by recruiting additional three to eight searchers in order to conduct large-scale surface Battle Area Clearance (BAC) tasks. 3F policy is to always encourage recruitment of additional BAC searchers/deminers from the affected community to ensure community participation in and acceptance of its work.BAC refers to a systematic and controlled clearance of a defined polygon of land where hazards are known to not contain mines. BAC can be surface or sub-surface depending on type of contamination, type of soil, vegetation, and infrastructure. 3F will deploy 3 multi-tasking clearance teams under this project to work alongside existing Community Liaison/Risk Education Officers and volunteers mobilizers.
