This project will have three core components.
1.Improve access and scale up Primary Health Care and Sexual and Reproductive Health with a focus on vulnerable populations, children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women in Aweil North.
PUI is currently implementing an Essential Health Care Package in PHCUs and through mobile outreach activities in 15 sites in Aweil North, integrating health, nutrition and FSL services targeting children under 5 and PLW with a specific focus on preventive care and health promotion.
In 2019, PUI plans to continue its intervention, with a view to expanding its capacity and cover other communities in the area, as a recent assessment revealed that demand for health services outweighs PUI’s current operational capacity.
PUI will continue to build on existing programs providing to communities an essential health care package. PUI will also aim to reinforce service uptake and utilisation through community mobilisation, advocacy on health seeking behaviour –encouraging and incentivizing pregnant mothers to come for their ANC visits-, offer a proper immunization status of sick children coming for PUI’s nutrition services and reinforcing defaulter tracing for both EPI and ANC.
2.Implement a health response for the population in need, in the greater Baggari area where most of the health facilities have been looted.
As the communities have little to no access to health care in the area, PUI plans to provide a basic package of curative and preventative health and nutrition services through 1 PHCC and Mobile outreach at PHCU level with a specific focus on CU5 and PLW. CHWs will be trained for assessments, including identifying danger signs and treating Malaria, Respiratory tract infection and Diarrhea for children, referral criteria and reporting. For PLW, PUI will start with ANC, PNC and Family Planning counseling through a mobile outreach team. This will include active community mobilization, promoting 4 ANC visits, safe deliveries by skilled birth attendants and post-natal care for mother and child; PUI will also focus on sexual health rights, including the right to family planning.
A full package of primary health care will be provided at PHCC level, including Basic Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (BEmONC) – which is one of the key activities expected for Wau health services in the Health 2019 HRP.
3.Establish an emergency response team who will conduct assessments and respond to emergencies through an integrated approach in the Greater Bahr el Ghazal region.
PUI will work together with partners for Initial or Rapid Needs Assessments (IRNAs); depending on the situation, PUI will participate in coordinated or joint assessments where and when an emergency arises. Given the limited time-frame and scope of IRNAs as well as the rapidly changing humanitarian landscape in South Sudan, PUI will continuously monitor and collect more in depth information, in line with 2019 HRP objectives for health and nutrition.
Depending on the nature of the identified needs, PUI will develop an emergency program. The most foreseeable needs include the prevention, detection and response to endemic and epidemic prone diseases (key Cluster activity for the health HRP). In any case, vulnerable populations such as CU5, women of reproductive age, IDPs, the elderly and communities in underserved areas will be particularly considered.

Needs

Despite the signing of the September 2018 Revitalised Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in the Republic of South Sudan, the country still suffers from a weak economy, poor health infrastructures and faces high maternal and child mortality and morbidity, as well as widespread hunger and malnutrition. The latter is a main public concern, with GAM rates consistently above 15%. The majority of health facilities in the country are supported by NGOs but often face drugs and staff shortages. PUI has established its presence in the field level in Aweil North (Northern Bahr el Ghazal) and provides Health, Nutrition and FSL services through an integrated approach. PUI has also conducted several assessments in the Greater Bahr el Ghazal region. The Greater Baggari area in Wau is of prime concern: the area is or has recently been affected by violence, civilians attacked, women and girls facing sexual violence, homes, schools and health facilities looted, resulting in massive civilian displacements, as recently reported by the Human Rights Watch (24 October 2018 reports). The latest IPC Report for September 2018 puts Wau and Raja in IPC Phase 4/emergency and Aweil North in IPC Phase 3!. PUI will be conducted a SMART Survey and health assessment in Raja, Western Bahr el Ghazal, in November 2018. Aweil North has 5 Primary Health Care centers (PHCC) and 25 Primary Health Care Units (PHCU), supported by the MoH and the HPF implementing partner. Première Urgence has supported the PHCC in Majak Kaar until May 2018, when PUI had to hand over the PHCC to the CHD due to reduced funding.However, this left behind quite a gap as the State Ministry of Health has limited resources: Majak Kaar had been the only PHCC with continuous available quality service, reaching an average of 7,000 consultations per month for all age groups in the area. While 49 out 76 health facilities in Wau State are reported to be functional, only 1 in 20 facilities is functional in Baggari and Beselia Counties. This is mainly due to the insecurity in the area: Mboro, Farajalla and Ngissa used to be functional PHCUs before the conflict began and were then supported by MSF at the onset of the crisis. However, since late 2017, only one facility is still accessible (Ngodakala, supported by ICRC) as others have closed due to lack of supplies and human resources. Due to insecurity, the support provided by NGOs has been a challenge, leaving consequent humanitarian gaps. Baggari County has indeed no Stabilization Center for the management of severely-malnourished children with complications and there are no referral mechanisms between Baggari’s lone health facility and Wau Tow. Most people in the area rely on volunteer health workers, who manage to get some supplies from NGOs in and around Wau town. To respond to those gaps, PUI is thus preparing to provide an integrated response in health and nutrition as well as setting up an emergency response capacity.

Activities

• Provision of Essential Primary Health Care including preventive care for children under 5 and women of reproductive age, integrating Health and Nutrition:- Through mobile outreach and in PHCUs in 15 locations in Aweil North;- Through mobile outreach and 1 PHCC in Greater Baggari in Wau; • Carry out rapid needs assessments, in coordination with partners and clusters in locations with newly-identified emergency needs in the Greater Bhar el Ghazal area; • Provide emergency and essential health care to address immediate health needs of persons impacted by acute emergencies (disease outbreaks and –or man-made disaster) in the Greater Bahr el Ghazal area.

Consultations with affected people

Yes

Provide breakdown by women, men, boys and girls

To ensure quality programming and good services, PUI prioritizes needs of the communities. They have the opportunity to influence decisions in the different phases of the program cycle management, especially during assessments and needs identification, as well as strategy planning and monitoring and evaluation. All views of the different targeted groups are taken into account. Première Urgence Internationale has the support of the County Health Department (CHD) in Aweil North for its 15 nutrition sites and concretely involves local communities through the Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs), Health Workers, community meetings, Mother-to-Mother Support Groups and exchanges regularly with the Boma Health Committee. In September 2018, PUI organized its strategic workshop in Aweil North and invited all relevant stakeholders, including the CHD, the Boma Health Committee (30 people) and organized focus group discussions directly at community level (170 people). The groups varied from one village to the other, as some involved women part of the Mother to Mother Support Groups, mainly aged from 15 years old to 40 years old; others included the Boma Health Committee of Majak Kaar (more men involved) and farmer’s groups set up for PUI’s food security and livelihood activities (a majority of women attended the discussion). These discussions are instrumental to assess local perspectives of community capacities and vulnerabilities as well as to outline local priorities in addressing their vulnerabilities. Such consultations will not only help PUI to fine-tune and improve its programs, it will also reinforce the community’s acceptance and ownership of future activities.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

The community members have the opportunity to influence the whole project cycle management. They are indeed consulted for feedback on the current services and give some recommendations to PUI for new activities in 2019. Then, once a project is starting or about to start, PUI shall organize a kick-off day to celebrate the project and inform the community members on the forthcoming activities. During the project launch phase, appropriate sessions will be organised with all stakeholders to provide information on the project's objectives, the implementation methodology, the planned activities, the selection criteria of the beneficiaries, etc. The health and nutrition workers, the Community Nutrition Volunteers and the Mother to Mother Support Groups are all groups on which PUI can rely on to provide the beneficiaries with information. While implementing the project, a number of feedback mechanisms will be put in place to acquire the relevant data which will enable PUI to tailor its activities to the needs of the populations. Some of these mechanisms include monitoring visits, community leaders meetings and key informant interviews.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

A key aspect of PUI’s program is its integrated approach, which underscores the importance of awareness-raising on primary health care, nutrition and hygiene. In each nutrition site, Première Urgence Internationale has a minimum of 8 Community Nutrition Volunteers, equipped with a bike and a backpack containing Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) sensitization materials. During their home visits or at the nutrition sites, the CNVs explain basic hygiene practices, sensitize caretakers on the importance of using mosquito nets to prevent malaria and emphasize why pregnant women should come for ANC visits or practice safe MIYCN. Discussions are also facilitated with the Mother-to-Mother Support Groups –PUI has established 56 groups-, who raise those issues with their communities and spread basic health and nutrition messages, aiming to improve the general nutrition and health status of the households. These Mother to Mother Support Groups have already showed great results, as mothers are increasingly open to information, eager to share experience and show the positive impact of MIYCN practices; the linkages with the health facilities have also improved. Moreover, PUI has painted sensitization messages on the walls of its nutrition sites in the villages to reach as many community members as possible, showing safe child feeding practices, mothers using mosquito nets and washing hands with their children before a meal. Furthermore, if messages are shared on paper, they are written both in English and in Thuangjang, a Dinka dialect. Throughout the years, these actions have helped foster a greater acceptance of the activities and greater proximity between PUI and community members – which helps adapt messages accordingly.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

PUI employees are all bound by PUI’s policies, including the Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse Policy (PSEA). Staff are trained and informed on the protection policies and sign a certificate to agree with the protection policies. Beneficiaries in the communities are informed of PUI’s protection policies and are asked to report any violation. PUI has also endeavoured to recruit more female staff but the challenges of low literacy, especially for women, is a hindrance, especially when qualified staff is needed to provide quality life services. Moreover, awareness activities related to basic human rights and Gender based violence will be integrated into PUI programs and target both men and women adjusted to their specific roles in the community. PUI integrates basic (S)GBV awareness activities in its nutrition and health programs, during health promotion sessions through story-telling, which has shown very positive results and fostered engaging discussions. While it is true that women in general are more vulnerable to (S)GBV, PUI engages both men and women in the promotion of gender equality, streamlining inclusivity in all its activities. In order to break the cycle of silence and stigma, different group sessions are held to allow more space for women and men to freely express themselves.Given PUI has been working for 3 years with the communities and is well respected for the given services and the NGO’s reliability, PUI also plans to do an initial assessment to have greater visibility on needs, practices, attitudes and knowledge of the communities on (S)GBV matters. Depending on the outcome of the assessment, (S)GBV awareness activities will be readjusted to the needs of the community.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

PUI aims to include all relevant stakeholders in its consultations: traditional community leaders, local authorities (ie, the CHD, Commissioners, etc.), the Boma Health Committee and community members. The latter are represented through the Community Volunteers, the Community Health and Nutrition Workers and the Mother to Mother Support Groups: the aim is to regularly consult the populations to adjust services and ensure PUI responds to their needs.

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

PUI will discuss and decide the feedback mechanism adapted to the needs of each Boma, taking into consideration relations within the community, gender and modes of communication.Options considered will include:- Feedback through a specific committee; - Feedback through community Nutrition Volunteers and PUI staff; - Evaluations through focused group discussions.Première Urgence Internationale is also expected to respect and safeguard the dignity of the beneficiaries. Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

In 2019, PUI plans to establish Project Monitoring Committees in all its sites of intervention, aiming to enhance ownership of the program in the community. PUI will start with an initial discussion and explanation of the role of the project committees and discuss with the community and local authorities the selection process of its members. The selection will consider specific criteria to provide equal opportunities for all and ensure equal participation of women and men. The community members involved in the Project Monitoring Committees will then ensure that activities run smoothly and that the services are provided as planned, all while providing feedback to PUI’s staff. This system will be paramount to improving capacity building and increasing ownership of the activities.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

As the South Sudanese health system is barely functioning, if only for the support of the humanitarian community, it is crucial to save lives, reduce acute needs and protect the most vulnerable populations. PUI, through its essential health care package, will target children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women. Moreover, it is crucial to understand the needs of these population groups, as well as their behaviours. As PUI has been involved in Aweil North and has established close links and relations with the community, it remains extremely important to change health seeking behaviour, as most seek care once they are sick, and not before, impacting immunization rates and antenatal care: ramping up 2018 activities, PUI plans to increase mobilizations and improve defaulter tracing, both for children and PLW, relying on CHWs, CNVs and Mother to Mother Support Groups. Distance and access to sites is also a key factor for the communities, which is why PUI constantly tries to increase its mobile outreach program and thus, health coverage.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

PUI is aware that some areas in Aweil North are hard-to-reach and often necessitate several hours of trekking by foot for the beneficiaries. To ensure there are fewer barriers to access services, PUI has steadily increased the number of nutrition sites (to 15) and has expanded its mobile health outreach activities since starting its activities in 2015, including two sites in the disputed 14-mile area – Kiir Kang Bek and Malek Miir. For instance, the latter are facing specific challenges due to access constraints and the fact that it is, by definition, an internationally disputed area. The number of IDPs is higher in both Bomas than elsewhere in PUI’s catchment area, with 37% IDPs in Kiir Kang Bek and 16% in Malek Miir, as Arab nomadic tribes migrate depending on the seasons. Given the access constraints, there are no other NGOs or public services. By opening more sites and organizing mobile outreach, PUI thus tries to reach as many vulnerable populations as possible to deliver quality primary health care.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Protection is a major concern in South Sudan related to the conflict, ethnic tensions, violence, abuse, violation of fundamental human rights and poor rule of law in the country. In the Greater Baggari, due to the ongoing conflict, there are increased protection risks. Women and children face specific protection risks, including gender-based violence, family separation, forced and early marriage and forced recruitment. From observations during an assessment in early June 2018, PUI saw armed children and women. It can be assumed that risks of SGBV are also high and needs for psycho-social aid will also be significant. PUI plans to start with Psychological first aid, within the health care and develop a referral system to enhance mental health.
