Project Summary

Proposed project areas cover some of the most needy with vulnerable host communities and IDPs. Kismayo and kahda have some of the most vulnerable and protracted IDP in Mogadishu, most facing multiple displacement. Most households have limited access to health services and poor WASH status exacerbating disease and malnutrition. GAM rate is above emergency threshold (FSNAU,2018). Birth assisted by skilled attendant is still low with few projects offering focused antenatal care thus presenting missed opportunity to strengthen IYCF at an early stage.
Hobyo and Jariban nutrition situation is critical for respective districts with aggravating factors. The areas were greatly ravaged by 2017 drought with animals dying,scarcity of water, very limited access to toilets leading to increased AWD /Cholera cases,low vaccination coverage,limited access to health services and a critical Food security situation. The situation expected to deteriorate for all areas if nothing is done especially if rains fail again.
SAF UK will therefore focus on life saving, Resilience building, protection activities in hard to reach areas and most vulnerable populations e.g children under 5(Boys and girls), women, elderly,HIV/TB patients, IDP and remote rural population. Activities that support full recovery from effects of drought and resilience building for affected population will remain a primary focus.
This will be done through prevention and treatment of acute malnutrition with a strong community component that will promote community participation and Information sharing, to improve access to those in need and provide a platform for surveillance. SAF UK has developed strong links with communities and conducts biannual community conversations with affected population including them in programming in all stages of the program cycle. The organization has developed community structures that ensure services are devolved to clusters of 15 households especially IDP, in line with organizations community strategy and Accountability to affected population (AAP) Framework.
Programs will integrate with health, WASH and food security and livelihood activities.This will include integration with basic health and maternal services, including Expanded program for immunization (EPI), BSFP, hygiene promotion including hygiene kits.The project will also seek to improve health seeking behavior though community mobilization.
Significant others to the target beneficiaries, especially Men and grandmothers, will be targeted for health promotion. Peer support groups will also be formed to promote peer learning. Involvement in multi cluster coordination, including attending coordination meetings and related activities will be high on our priority.
The project will employ and train staff (Men and women) and include community workers(Men and women) through a transparent process.

Needs

Kismayo town has continued to experience rapid population growth following 2017 drought disease outbreaks, food and civil insecurity ravaging neighbouring villages and districts. IDP camps have become overcrowded reducing program coverage with many unwilling to return to their homes. The current GAM in IDP deteriorated to 14.4% with aggregating factors. Health seeking behaviors is very poor with poor WASH, high morbidity especially among children at 68% including AWD Cholera, low vaccination coverage and poor IYCF indicators. Kahda IDP hosts some of the most vulnerable and protracted IDP in Somalia, most facing multiple displacement. Most households have limited access to health services with poor WASH status increasing diseases. GAM rate is at 16.7% with 6.6% SAM, among the highest in the country. The IDP population continues to increase with in migration from neighboring districts (SAF UK,2017) increasing violations that demand for protection interventions.Hobyo and Jariban nutrition situation is critical,with GAM of 9.8% and 2.1% and 11.2 and 1.5% respectively with aggravating factors. 2017 drought greatly ravaged the area with animals dying,scarcity of water, very limited access to toilets leading to increased AWD /Cholera cases,low vaccination coverage,limited access to health services and a critical Food security situation. Though the rains and humanitarian interventions contributed to decrease in GAM, more needs to be done considering current food insecurity and disease prevalence. SAF UK will therefore focus on integrated life saving activities,Resilience building and project sustainability with protection mainstreamed in hard to reach areas and among most vulnerable and marginalized populations e.g children under 5(Boys and girls), women, elderly,HIV/TB patients, IDP and remote rural population. Activities will include primary secondary and tertiary prevention with a strong community component that promotes AAP.

Activities

Activity 1:Regular identification of acutely malnourished children(Boys and girls) and PLWs including active case finding. Activity 2:Therapeutic feeding support for treatment of acute malnutrition among children 6-24 months (Boys, girls) and Pregnant and lactating women Activity 3: Micro nutrient support for vulnerable groups e.g Vitamin A and micro nutrient powders for children under 5 (Boys and girls) , Multiple micro nutrients (MMN) for lactating women and Iron folate for pregnant women. Activity 4:Food based and non food based preventive actions including nutrition sensitive activities for communities (Men , women,Youth, boys and girls) including linkages to food and cash programs, kitchen gardening and promotion of least consumed available nutritious foods. Activity 5:Integrated mutisectorial nutrition, health including treatment of common illnesses and vaccination ,treatment of common illnesses, hygiene promotion with distribution of hygiene kits, agriculture-livestock and fishery promotion and protection mainstreaming including GBV referral for communities (Men , women,youth, boys and girls)

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

In all proposed areas the environment is sufficiatly safe to enable implementation of resilience activities. All our projects have a good working relationship with the government and other relevant stakeholders. Two of our facilities were handed to the organisation by the government and work under strict suppervivion of the same. In all states, project involve government entities in all activities and receive several monitoring and supportive supervision visits. the organisation is also not only registred by government to operate in the various states but also have Memorandum of understanding wioth each state stating terms of work, relationship with government, commitment to serve and deliver, allegiance to total transparency and accountability, and commitment to deliver positive outputs and outcomes. The organisation also has strong working relationship with communities we serve working closely with all age groups but more closely with community elders who are involved right from needs assessment. These not only represent the community but also provide leadership and guidance. some of our facilities are donated by the communities in support of our programs. Sustainability is one of our focus areas for 2019 and sustainability component is being developed in our policy to cover the component over a period of time.All our beneficiaries are informed of their entilement to quality service delivery and praccess to utilities as per project requirement. This is entranched in our policies and APP framework. Thesea are also currently operational with a strong and functional feedback mechanism in place. Beneficiaries and communities are also informed of their entitlements and rights through community programs and project banners.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

For IDP in Mogadisho the main risk is displacement. for this the organisation has maintained a database for all beneficiaries with telephone contacts and that of relatives. For those in rural Mudug the risk is spontenious clan clashes. While it is difficult to deal with this, we have ensured that services are provided for the different clans in their areas equitably. Interclan clashes therefore have minimal effect our programs.in all location the organisation will seek to provide safety nets by implementing BSFP . Victims of GBV will also be provided with an organisational GBV focal point who will link survivors to appropriate assistance. Programs will also ensure IDp have access to basic services in an equitable and dignified manner with equality also observed in job allocation in projects serving IDP population.Among IDP the risk of exclusion based on tribe is real. Most IDPs in Kismayo are from a certain community who found it hard to access services in the recent past based on their tribe and skin color. The organisations' policy does not allow exclusion and actually reaches out to all community members to receive services. Recruitment of staff is not based on tribe but professional competence. This has produced staff from a hybrid of cultures who have learned to work together well. Some positions are also reserved to the target communities and community groups that are marginalized like IDPs and women so that equity is achieved.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

All gender will be awarded opportunity to benefit in equitably. Women and youth who are mostly left out in recruitment will be considered in all projects and positions will be awarded equitably. Needs assessment will include all community groups so that an all conclusive, agreeable and fair assessment is reached. both men and women leaders with an additional youth representative will be part of community health board as per organisational policy. This will ensure decisions made are all inclusive. SAF UK policies have sought to put men at the helm of needs affecting their wives, daughters and female relatives. The organisation's APP framework seeks to ensure men participate through affirmative action for men ensuring that men are not excluded from interventions targeting women beneficiaries. Children who are often left out will not only be seen as beneficiaries but their views on service delivery ad need will occasionally be sought by a professional child specialist so that the whole community participates in program design and implementation. Community diagnosis, community dialogues, evaluations and assessment will seek to include all community groups to ensure all are reached.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Both in Mogadisho and Kismayo security risk is minimized by ensuring all staff go through the internal security vetting every quarter and present a clearance letter from ministry of internal security. They must also get reference from a local leader like an elder or imam who is accountable. These must also be known by local elders from their tribe who pledge accountability. This ensures that our staff are not related to any non state actors planning violence.For those in rural Mudug, the risk is spontaneous clan clashes. While it is difficult to deal with this, we have ensured that services are provided for the different clans in their geographical areas equitably. Inter clan clashes therefore have minimal effect to our programs. However after conversations with elders, it was agreed that all clans are served by the same staff regardless of their clan background and without discrimination and threats to ones life and property. This is slowly but surely contributing to cohesion among the various clans and a realization of each others strengths.
