Project Summary

Given the growing humanitarian needs in Lebanon which have been further compounded by the COVID-19 pandemic, Beirut Port explosion and continuing political and economic instability, NCA continues to build on its existing gender-based violence (GBV) response including its GBV emergency response to the Beirut Blast, to target women and girls from all community groups, including both refugee and host communities and to ensure vulnerable Lebanese women and girls are reached specifically. The current crisis in Lebanon has had significant impact on all communities, increasing household’s vulnerabilities, limiting access to basic items and services, with women and girls being disproportionately affected in terms of reduced protections and increased risk of GBV. Global studies, current programming and NCA’s recent context analysis study on Lebanon (March 2021) continue to confirm that women and girls are disproportionately affected in terms of increased risk to GBV. Data has shown that women and girls are at greater risk of violence because of COVID-19 and subsequent lockdowns, are carrying additional burdens in relation to managing their households and mental health and are at risk of exploitation and abuse due to an increased reliance on negative coping strategies.
Despite the availability of safe spaces/GBV services in refugee and host communities, findings show that these are not necessarily reaching vulnerable Lebanese women and girls or that they are unaware of such services. GBV service providers including ABAAD reported increases in calls to their GBV hotlines, reporting a 9% increase of Lebanese GBV survivors accessing case management in 2020. Given the further deterioration of the situation in Lebanon, it is likely that this figure will continue to increase as needs grow. In response to these changing needs in Lebanon, NCA and its partners will ensure that community outreach targets vulnerable Lebanese women and girls in key locations, and will provide them with safe spaces whereby they can access lifesaving GBV services and psychosocial and emotional support focusing on healing, empowerment, and recovery. To complement GBV response interventions, GBV risk-reduction and prevention strategies which aim to address harmful social norms and systemic gender inequality will be implemented particularly amongst vulnerable Lebanese communities with a focus on raising awareness about GBV, information dissemination about available services and referral pathways, GBV/PSS key messages, distribution of dignity/hygiene kits and information dissemination about protection of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA).
Through NCA’s partnership with the Middle East Council of Churches (MECC), Lebanese women and girls alongside other community groups can visit MECC’s Our Lady Dispensary (OLD) centre in Mount Lebanon to access PSS and participate in activities such as vocational training and life skills activities. These group activities aim to empower women by reducing their stress and anxiety, support them by building their capacity to protect themselves and their families from GBV and establish relationships and networks with other women in a safe environment. In partnership with ABAAD, NCA ensures that women and children have access to multi-sectoral life-saving specialized GBV through its midway house in Mount Lebanon, three Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSS) in Beirut and Mount Lebanon (Msaytbeh, Sin el Fil and Jbeil) and ABAAD’s Men Center. Whilst these spaces already exist and are frequented by many community groups including refugees and host communities, NCA and partners will conduct outreach in areas where vulnerable Lebanese communities reside in order to strengthen engagement, build trust and link them to the services provided in the different centres.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

NCA’s GBV programme provides support to women and girls including survivors of GBV and those at risk of violence, to empower them and assist them to cope with the impact of GBV. Through GBV response and prevention activities, women and girls are provided with safe access to multi-sectoral, lifesaving and specialized GBV services including survivor-centred case management, health services including clinical management of rape, specialized MHPSS, legal support, cash assistance, support with small-enterprise/livelihood opportunities, vocational training/life skills training, emotional support, dignity/hygiene kits, structured/non-structed PSS including to caregivers and adolescent girls. Through community outreach activities, women and girls are provided with information about available services and referral pathways to empower them to seek safe and confidential support and provided with awareness-raising and key messages on GBV, PSS and PSEA to build their capacities and individual resources and tackle the normalization of GBV amongst communities. In parallel, and as part of NCA’s risk mitigation/ GBV prevention strategy, partners work with community members including community leaders, men and boys to promote social stability, prevent violence and support the reintegration of GBV survivors, at the community level (and contextually relevant & culturally sensitive) and challenge traditional beliefs that perpetuate trauma and harmful practices, allowing safe reintegration.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

NCA ensures that its PSHEA policy and key principles are integrated into all programmes. NCA has also developed cross-cutting checklists and capacity-builds partners on these in order to ensure key issues around accountability, do no harm and gender/age/diversity are integrated and reported on throughout the project cycle. To mitigate and respond to SEA, all NCA projects have complaint response mechanisms in place for both NCA and partners, include regular capacity-building of staff on CRMs, and community engagement to mitigate the risk of SEA and to ensure reporting channels are developed in line with the cultural/local context. NCA’s GBV projects, integrate PSEA through strong community outreach and sensitization on PSEA and reporting mechanisms as part of wider awareness-raising on GBV and available services. NCA’s GBV projects also respond to SEA directly, through the provision of survivor centered GBV services including case management.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

NCA is Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS) certified; NCA’ complaints and response mechanism in line with its commitment to the CHS and aims to ensure feedback, transparency and learning for strengthening transparency and accountability. Beneficiary participation is integrated during programme design, implementation, and M&E stages of the project. NCA supports partners to strengthen the integration of cross-cutting issues such as accountability, do no harm, gender/age/diversity and the UNSCR 1325 in all projects. NCA also supports partners to develop M&E tools including feedback and satisfaction surveys to measure the quality of services and conducts assessments to ensure that NCA and partners are able to understand, respond and learn from the safety and protection concerns of women and integrate them into programming. Finally, all awareness-raising materials are developed in collaboration with the community to ensure key messages are culturally and contextually relevant and appropriate.
