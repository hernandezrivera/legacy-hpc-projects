Project Summary


For 2021 is 13.4 million in need people in Sudan need some form of humanitarian assistance. This includes over 1 million refugees and asylum seekers, the majority of whom are largely dependent humanitarian assistance. Further the situation is exacerbated by the ongoing macro-economic challenges resulting in continuing high inflation estimated as more than five-fold increase compared to one year ago. This has led to shortages of fuel and food, increase in prices of key commodities and medicines and subsequently reduced household purchasing power & livelihoods opportunities in particular for children, women and girls, people at risk, and host communities remain exposed to protection risks. According OCHA Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview 2020 (January 2020); after a year of civil unrest and political change, humanitarian needs continue to rise. Some 13.4 million people as mentioned – 23 per cent of the population – will need humanitarian assistance in 2020. The transitional government is prioritizing peace and ending the economic crisis, issues closely intertwined with the drivers of humanitarian need in the country. While incidents of fighting have reduced considerably in recent years, the situation of people displaced due to decades of conflict remains unresolved. Some 1.87 million IDPs and 1.1 million refugees and asylum seekers continue to need humanitarian assistance and protection support, both in and out of camp camps and within host communities. Pockets of armed conflict continue in Darfur, and sporadic inter-communal conflicts also continue.
Across Sudan, basic services are lacking, and natural disasters, like floods, affect people each year. Moreover the deepening economic crisis, following years of stagnation and little investment in already-weak public services that is driving worsening food insecurity, deteriorating healthcare, and other needs across Sudan. Recently COVID-19 made the life worthier in Sudan.
Throughout Sudan, most people – 58 per cent of households – cannot afford a basic daily food basket. Over 2.7 million children suffer from acute malnutrition. Medical facilities across the country are not functional due to lack of essential drugs. Moreover, rape, or violence incidences in and around camps are reported. As UNAMID reduces its presence in Darfur, protection is one of the key areas of concern, which will require close monitoring during 2020-21.
Through this project Al Massar with its partners (inline ministries & COR) will respond to an urgent and critical emergency for people affected by the conflict in South Kordofan, White Nile, North & East Darfur states, targeting vulnerable, IDPs, refugees, undeserved children, teachers, PTA's, and host affected communities. More than 60% of the children in the target locations are out of schools & the other 40% receiving poor basic education services. Therefore; the proposed education activities will contribute to access of better basic education services.
About 2.5 million children under age five suffer from wasting annually, out of whom close to 700,000 suffer from severe acute malnutrition (SAM). The Government has scaled up response in line with the National Nutrition Strategic Plan 2014-2025, and the number of children who have access to treatment of SAM has doubled over the past five years.

1 - Needs

An estimated 6.3 million people across Sudan were experiencing Crisis (IPC 3) or worse levels of acute food insecurity from June through August, according to a September IPC analysis. Civil unrest and poor economic conditions—including cash shortages, elevated food and fuel prices, and high inflation—are factors cited as contributing to the high prevalence of acute food insecurity. The Famine Early Warning Systems Network (FEWS NET) indicates that IDPs and poor households in Jebel Marra—a mountainous region encompassing parts of Central Darfur, North Darfur, and South Darfur—and in parts of South Kordofan are experiencing Emergency (IPC 4) outcomes during the ongoing lean season. Prices for key staple foods continued to increase sharply in August to approximately double the prices from August 2019 and three times higher than the five year average, according to FEWS NET. While the start of the harvest in October may improve food security outcomes in some areas, Crisis conditions are likely to persist in parts of Blue Nile, Kassala, North Darfur, North Kordofan, Red Sea, and South Kordofan states, as well as in Jebel Marra through at least January. This, in addition to the recent Ethiopian conflict that pushed more than forty thousand Ethiopian refugees to Kassala & Gedaref States according to OCHA first December 2020.According to the national program of the Federal Ministry of Health to reduce malnutrition diseases especially among under five years children, where there were many interventions, studies, and surveys to reduce malnutrition diseases and to include nutrition services to primary health services.Al Massar organization was one of the organizations which included nutrition services to its health centers at Zawia Gara and Mustariha in Kutum and Serif localities and government clinics in East Darfur, White Nile & South Kordofan. Since the necessity to include nutrition services to those centers / medical units because these areas were considered as areas of high priority according to HNO 2020. An elevated number of Sudanese are facing poor nutritional outcomes, due to the increasing cost of the basic food basket. Around 2.4 million children ages five years and younger are acutely malnourished, with approximately 700,000 suffering from severe acute malnutrition according to the UN. The beneficiaries of this project included IDPs and Refugee are the most severely affected people who live in the high priority areas as described according to HNO for 2020. The targeted areas has been classified as highly needed and they lack the basic gender-sensitive sanitation services in many sectors.Therefore, Al Massar ORG is targeting IDPs Camps, refugees, returnees and host communities in the targeted States & Localities. The target population is in urgent need of health, nutrition, and caring support. The target area suffering from the prolonged conflict and currently both chronic & acute malnutrition increased amongst the population in addition to COVID- 19. This increase has resulted in reaching the international threshold in wasting 15%. The beak of malnutrition in previous years has to be around the rainy season (May- August) & thus could hunt at increasing malnutrition during the 2020 dry & rainy season above 15%. Al Massar response strategy to address the target population needs will include implementation of active case finding, screening and referrals of SAM/MAM& SAM, IYCF and complementary feeding covers the overall PLW population, this in addition to the provision of treatment services.
