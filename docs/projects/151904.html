Project Summary

INTERSOS will develop a comprehensive and multi-sectoral service model to address the multiple needs of vulnerable migrants, refugees, IDPs, returnees and host community children and their families, linking interventions in different sectors including: education, Child Protection and GBV. The aim is to make available to the different groups a number of services including but not limited to, psycho-social support, life skills and CP/GBV case management, information sharing and awareness raising, through the establishment and operation of the community centers (PEACE Centers).
The proposed intervention is targeting four different areas, Tripoli, Misrata, Sirte and Zawiyah. Currently, INTERSOS is in the process of establishing the first center in Tripoli funded by UNICEF targeting vulnerable children, the center will be a startup point to obtain a better understanding of situation and best practices to implement the activities in the Libyan context. Field assessments for the other Mantikas will be conducted in parallel with the establishment in order to assess the Mantikas’ needs and the organization needed capacity to implement. To avoid overlapping with other service providers working in the same Mantikas, INTERSOS will ensure, at the beginning of the project, coordination with IOM, NRC and CESVI as providers of similar services to avoid duplicating efforts and covering a wider geographical area within the same Mantika.

Needs

The ongoing crisis in Libya is negatively impacting the population and still worsening the living conditions of the different vulnerable groups. Refugees, returnees, asylum seekers, migrants, IDPs and vulnerable host community members are suffering from lack of access to, if not all, different services. There are serious protection concerns, hardship resulting from multiple displacements and loss of a protective environment puts children in Libya at higher risk of abuse, exploitation, and violence. According to Libya HNO 2019, it was highlighted that, in an unpublished study by UNICEF on violence, children are facing protection risks at schools, homes and also within the community. 35% of migrant children are unaccompanied and at risk of domestic violence, recruitment by armed groups, sexual and gender-based violence, abduction, unlawful detention, torture and other forms of ill-treatment, as well as killing or maiming due to presence of unexploded ordnance or explosive remnants of war.Migrant and refugee/asylum seeker children in urban areas, although less visible, are equally at risk with the ones in detention centers. All vulnerable boys and girls require sustainable, holistic prevention and response measures, including but not limited to case management, medical referral and rehabilitation assistance, and mental health and psychosocial support for them and their caregivers, which are currently very limited throughout the country.The same Libya 2019 HNO confirmed that risks of GBV are exacerbated by the weak national laws and limited specialized services for GBV survivors. Women, girls and boys are the most at risk of GBV though the need for psychosocial support is estimated to be high, women and girls reportedly fear accessing such services out of fear of being recognized as a GBV survivor. In addition, it is difficult for women and girls to access services without the permission of their husband or male relative. Libyan adolescents and youth are among the most vulnerable population groups in the country as a result of risks related to the conflict. Out-of-school adolescents and unemployed young men, in particular, are at risk of being forced or coerced into joining armed groups or militias. Young migrants and refugees set out to escape harm or secure better futures face staggering risks in the process. Their journey is marked by high levels of abuse, trafficking and exploitation. Adolescents and youth are at greater risk than adults on the migration routes.Migrant and refugee children are often denied access to education within the national educational system and rarely attend school with Libyan children, especially those who are non-Arabic speakers coming from Sub-Saharan African countries. Available data reveals that out of 61,882 school age migrant and refugee children registered in the country, 52,682 children are in need of support to access education services. The same unpublished UNICEF report highlighted that 100 per cent of interviewed unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) had no access to education in LibyaIn conclusion, a safe environment that provide women, girls, men and boys with safe and dignified access to specialized services is highly needed. Children, adolescents and youth need to have access to education and livelihood opportunities depending on their age. Parents and caregivers are in need to be sensitized on better parenting, protection risks, human and children rights. Vulnerable communities living in hard-to-reach areas and cannot reach services providers due to different reasons such as insecurity, fears, lack of legal documentation, lack of transportation or simply by not being aware of the availability of services are in need to be outreached and receive services without the need to move.

Activities

Establishment of PEACE centerProvide WGBM from the different vulnerable groups with a safe space and environment where they can express themselves, identify challenges, gaps and needs, and to determine the best solution for their situationOnce the center is established, INTERSOS will raise awareness about the center and services available to both the Libyan and non-Libyan communities, this will be through sessions, flayers and the conduction of events. In reaching urban refugees and migrants, INTERSOS will draw upon out previous experiences in different missions. Additionally, all messaging clearly includes that the center and all services will be available to all groups, regardless of status will be ensured. Volunteers will be also selected from the migrants and refugees’ community. This, in addition to overcoming the language barrier, will help in outreaching the targeted groups Child protection and GBV mechanism providing general and specialized protection services• The center’s vision for specialized child protection and GBV services is aligned with international guidelines, and includes: Protection of all children from all forms of abuse, neglect, exploitation and exposure to violence• Provision of strategies and approaches that are child-centered, rights-based and considering children within family systems• A system of child protection that is coordinated, coherent, integrated and holistic• A range of adequately resourced services for prevention, intervention and recovery/treatment• A society with social norms that value children and encourage positive parenting Engage children in structured activities within the centersThe center will provide in a secure learning and socializing environment psycho-social activities. Effective free families’ participation in daily activities within the centres will be promoted, and structured psychosocial activities will be provided within the center to different age groups. Some activities will be open to the families, and through dedicated parents’ group, the caregivers will be directly involved in assess needs, possibilities and capacities The definition of activities will take place in an open participative process, that will involve children and caregivers, promoting child participation and empowerment as part of the possible PSS structured activities. Apply a comprehensive module for the non-formal education Basic courses in literacy and numeracy will be carried out for children and youth (primary and secondary levels) by trained staff. These courses will provide children with basic education support and informal skills that represent essential requirements for academic achievements Implementation of gender appropriated Life Skills education activities for children aged 10-18:Considering the emergency context, the proposed life skills education activities are meant to be considered as Life skills training workshops, and all the activities will be conducted in line with the UNICEF life skills guidelines and by using UNICEF’s life skills training manual, maintaining the focus on one of the five basic areas of Skills Building Targeted community participate actively in Community based Committees for active prevention, identification and to response to child protection and GBV issuesThe community mobilization activities will be activated; committees will be established aiming at improving the referral system through the identification and training of dedicated focal points, which will be coached and supported on regular basis by the Social Workers. CP/GBV awareness campaign will be incorporated, contributing to advocate for the protection of human rights Response to needs of vulnerable communities living in hard to reach areas through mobile approachIn order to ensure that the most vulnerable children living in host communities are identified and assisted, INTERSOS is planning the implementation of a set of mobile activities for children and their families

Access, safety and dignity of beneficiaries

INTERSOS has recently updates policies against PSEA and the Child Safeguarding Policy. INTERSOS always guarantees involvement of the targeted population as part of the decision making process in its programs. This ensures a non-discriminatory intervention, and also fosters social cohesion, acceptance of the interventions and sense of ownership. In this specific response, INTERSOS guarantees full involvement of targeted communities in terms of: identification of general needs as well as at risk cases; sharing, discussion and full endorsement of typology and methodology of the planned activities; Beneficiaries and key stakeholders in the target areas will be involved in the different phases of the action to the greatest extent possible. During the inception phase, awareness sessions will be conducted for community members on the scope of the action; criteria for selection, modalities of assistance and steps in the implementation, including the verification and the monitoring exercises, will be shared in advance.Accountability towards beneficiaries will be guaranteed through the application of internal tools already available, such as PSEA Policy, Child Protection Policy, Code of Conduct and Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs); as for the latter, INTERSOS SOPs on Data Protection and SOPs on Complaint Mechanism will be particularly relevant for the current proposal. These tools will also ensure vulnerable groups with equal and impartial access to the services provided. Selected community members will be specifically trained to act as focal points in case of any problem or concern. The training will try to foster community members’ capacities in referring those cases to INTERSOS or in encouraging beneficiaries to use the set complaint mechanism. Beneficiaries will be provided with the updated contact details of INTERSOS focal points, and informed about the complaint mechanism available.
