Project Summary

Access to food in Gaza remains a challenge for a majority of refugees struggling with deteriorating socio-economic conditions. As a result, UNRWA expects that in 2020, around 1,000,000 refugees – with around 50 per cent of them being females – will require emergency food assistance. Hence, in order to mitigate food insecurity, UNRWA is planning to provide food assistance to approximately 200,000 Palestine refugee families including persons with disabilities and female headed households. This intervention is aligned with Strategic Objective 3 of the HRP i.e. supporting the capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome protracted crisis, and the response approach of the Food Security Sector (FSS) i.e. addressing basic food needs of households that are suffering from limited access to food.
The beneficiaries will be surveyed through UNRWA specific poverty based targeting system, that identifies families living on less than $3.87 per person per day. Gender access to food assistance will be taken into consideration, as approximately 50 per cent of the beneficiaries will be females and as around 17,000 female households will be targeted by the intervention. Eligible refugee families will receive a food basket every quarter that includes a range of basic food commodities. Through its strong monitoring system in place regarding the food distribution process, UNRWA will continue to provide nutritionally improved food baskets while also guaranteeing a fair, equal and consistent distribution.

1 - Needs

The prolonged Israeli blockade of Gaza, the ongoing impacts of the Great March of Return demonstrations, and internal political divisions coincided with the crisis of the PA salaries cuts and the electricity shortage continue to have a devastating impact on the socioeconomic conditions for Palestine refugees, severely restricting the movement of people and goods and crippling the once vibrant local economy. The socioeconomic context continues to be characterized by widespread unemployment and stagnating low incomes, continuing to trap the majority of the population in the Gaza Strip, 70 per cent of whom are Palestine refugees, into poverty. According to Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics, the overall unemployment in Gaza reached 46.7% in the second quarter of 2019. Unemployment reached 48.3 per cent for refugees, compared to 43.5 per cent for non-refugees. The moderate boosts to the economy generated by the post conﬂict reconstruction activities after 2014 have proved to be transitory and have not been enough to balance the salary cuts imposed on Palestinian Authority’s employees and the cuts in energy supply, as well as the protracted effects of the land and sea blockade which entered its thirteenth year in 2019. These trends have had a particularly severe impact on vulnerable groups, including women and young people. Unemployment is particularly high among women with shockingly high 68.3 per cent. In addition to lack of job opportunities, women face social barriers to employment in Gaza, particularly when this involves travel to a workplace away from home. Young people between the ages of 15 and 29 are also particularly affected, with unemployment rate of 64.0 per cent, while young women face extremely high unemployment rate of 86.0 per cent in the second quarter of 2019. Despite being highly educated, young people in Gaza face the prospect of unemployment, remaining dependent on already strained families and isolated from the outside world, rather than creating an independent future for themselves. Consistently high unemployment rates and deteriorating purchasing power have eroded many households’ capital and coping mechanisms, rendering the majority of the population of Gaza dependent on humanitarian assistance to meet basic needs and trapped about half of them into food insecurity. Persistently high levels of food insecurity in Gaza are not due to the unavailability of basic commodities in the local market, but rather to households’ lack of economic access to food, which, if unaddressed, would have lasting consequences on both the extent and severity of food insecurity. If to date hunger and undernourishment are not widespread in Gaza, it is because of the assistance provided by UNRWA and other humanitarian organizations. UNRWA projects that more than one million refugees in Gaza will continue to require emergency food assistance in 2020, an estimated 98,000 of whom are chronic poor and will be assisted through the Agency’s core, human development programming. The increase in emergency needs is dramatic, almost 13 fold increase on the 80,000 refugees who required assistance at the start of 2000. UNRWA identiﬁes refugees in need of assistance through a Poverty Assessment Survey (PAS), conducted by UNRWA social workers during home visits to each household.

2 - Activities

UNRWA will provide food assistance to Palestine refugee families surveyed through the Agency speciﬁc poverty based targeting system, identiﬁed as living on less than $3.87 per person per day. UNRWA estimates that about 1,000,000 refugees (around 200,000 families), including persons with disabilities and female headed households, will require emergency food assistance in 2020. Within this caseload, around 620,000 refugees living on less than $1.74 per person per day are classified as abject poor, and around 380,000 refugees living below the $3.87 poverty line are classified as absolute poor. Each eligible refugee family will receive a food basket every quarter which include basic food commodities. Quantities of each item are determined by the poverty status category and by the actual household size. Rations will be distributed at the 10 UNRWA distribution centres across the Gaza Strip. In 2020 UNRWA will continue to provide nutritionally improved food baskets, while also guaranteeing a fair, equal and consistent distribution. Community is engaged through effective consultation with refugee communities to ensure accountability. In order to ensure gender access to food distribution and since women are one of the most affected groups when it comes to unemployment and vulnerability – as described above – approximately 500,000 females i.e. nearly 50 per cent of the beneficiaries – will benefit from food assistance. In addition and in order to support female who face particular socio-economic challenges, approximately 17,000 female households will also benefit from food assistance.

The UNRWA Monitoring and Evaluation team, in close coordination with the Relief and Social Services Programme (RSSP), will continue to regularly monitor the quality of the food distributed to beneﬁciaries, ensuring continued feedback from the community through focus group sessions and regular communication with beneﬁciaries. Targeting is based on a proxy means testing system, introduced in 2009 and adjusted in 2015 to better reﬂect the rapidly changing socioeconomic realities in Gaza. This methodology, based on data collected during systematic home visits conducted by trained UNRWA social workers, determines a household’s economic status through interviews during which information is collected on proxies such as housing conditions, asset ownership, education, employment or health status. Any family can obtain updated information on its eligibility status at the nearest Area Relief and Social Services Office.Families who are not satisﬁed with their eligibility classiﬁcation can appeal if (i) their ration was reduced or interrupted (if the family is reclassiﬁed from abject poor to absolute or non-poor or from absolute poor to non -poor) and (ii) they were found non-poor after a new application or reapplication. This process ensures accountability and promotes transparency with the community. The food eligibility system takes into account the speciﬁc cases of wives from polygamous marriage, divorced women and separated or abandoned women (not officially divorced), both to guarantee that their poverty status is assessed fairly and that they are assisted with dignity. Particular attention is given by the social workers to the identiﬁcation and assistance of “abandoned” women and their children who, besides social stigma, are also exposed to speciﬁc risk factors. Not being formally recognized as divorced, they do not receive any regular ﬁnancial support from the husband and often have to rely on ad hoc and weak social networks. UNRWA will make sure these vulnerable families do not fall through the system and are able to be assessed to receive food assistance independently from the husband.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

The Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) Unit, in coordination with the other implementing units, prepares internal quarterly food monitoring distribution reports (including, for instance, number of families received food, quality of food assistance and beneficiaries’ feedback) as well as communicate to Relief and Social Services Programme the results of Poverty Assessment Survey (PAS) applications re-visits (PAS Post- visits) to take corrective actions if required. The reports, which include any obstacles encountered, are sent to the Agency’s management for information, evaluation and corrective action as required.The collection of beneficiaries’ feedbacks through both UNRWA M&E and RSSP units contributes to continuously improve the food distribution mechanism at different levels. As an example, based on beneficiaries’ feedbacks collected through focus groups, committees, outreach and meetings, new food items were incorporated to the distributed food basket in 2016.Beneficiaries’ feedbacks on food distribution access, which are collected through the PAS and / or home visits, are also used to provide specific assistance to these beneficiaries – in particular widow or divorced women as well as people with disabilities – in order for them to be able to collect their food baskets like the rest of the beneficiaries (further details provided in section 9.2).In addition, and as described above, an appeal mechanism as well as a committee feedback system are also in place for families who were initially rejected from the intervention based on the results of the Poverty Assessment Survey. Based on the social workers’ reports from the first visit, this can lead to a re-assessment of their poverty status by UNRWA RSSP teams which can – depending on the outcomes of the new survey conducted – lead to a change of the initial decision.

8.6.b - If 'Other', please specify

UNRWA Relief Social Services Programme (RSSP) regularly conducts focus groups to discuss key items such as the Poverty Assessment Survey (PAS), the quality of the food assistance, the quantity of the food basket and the process of food distribution. UNRWA-RSSP has a complaint system in place to receive beneficiaries’ feedback. An appeal mechanism as well as a committee feedback system are also in place for the families who were rejected from the intervention based on the results of the Poverty Assessment Survey. UNRWA RSSP replies to the families regarding their poverty status are shared with them based on their complaint or appeal.UNRWA Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) unit is also involved through regularly conducting meeting with beneficiaries across the 10 distribution centres or at their homes to collect their feedback regarding the quality of the received commodities, the behavior of the staff in charge of distributing the food and any other issues related to their satisfaction.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

UNRWA has a specific obligation to mainstream protection across its services and operations, as well as made a clear commitment to protect and uphold Palestinian refugees’ rights. Distribution centres and social services are regularly monitored against the four main protection mainstreaming pillars, as defined by the Global Protection Cluster and echoed by UNRWA, the assessed principles are: (i) Prioritize safety, dignity and do not harm; (ii) Meaningful access to services; (iii) Accountability; (iv) Participation and empowerment of refugees. During the food distribution cycle, UNRWA staff based at the food distribution centers including social workers and M&E teams, ensure that the targeted beneficiaries receive their food assistance with dignity and respect. In addition and as described above, regular monitoring work is conducted to collect beneficiaries’ feedback on key items of the programme e.g. the quality of the food distribution process, the content of the food basket, the overall quality of the food distribution or the behavior of the staff involved in the food distribution. These feedbacks help to continuously improve the intervention.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNRWA is targeting all Palestinian refugees living under the poverty line with food assistance, without any discrimination and following the result of a Poverty Survey conducted by Relief and Social Service Programme (RSSP). Information to beneficiaries, eligibility criteria and other services are available to all refugees where application and claim can be made to RSSP, often located in the same area of the distribution centres. Location of distribution centres takes into consideration safety concerns, especially for women and girls, as well as catchment area, distance and potential risks to reach the area for different groups of population. The distribution takes into consideration specific vulnerability concerns and adopted specific SOPs to provide meaningful access and prioritize assistance to vulnerable groups (abandoned women, pregnant women, persons with disabilities, elderly, etc.). A specific follow up system is also in place whenever assistance is not received in order to identify and follow specific concerns and potential protection cases. Further, distribution centres are clearly marked and visible, respecting UNRWA neutrality policy. Staff in the distribution centres, whom is regularly targeted by capacity building activities including on protection mainstreaming and gender, are able to refer specific protection cases to social services for follow up. A crowd control system is in place and a specific calendar is set up to avoid and limit potential incidents and/or disturbance. Finally, a complaint response mechanism is accessible in RSS offices and mechanism to provide timely feedbacks are available through specific response committees. In order to ease the process for targeted persons with disabilities to receive food assistance, there is a delegation system in place that enables those people to delegate one of their family members to receive the food assistance on their behalf. The delegation is set during the home visits of the social workers and following their reports’ recommendations. The Poverty Assessment Survey (PAS) system is continuously improved to enable vulnerable groups such as widow or divorced women as well as people with disabilities or anyone not able to go to the distribution center to be provided with specific assistance in order to receive their food basket.
