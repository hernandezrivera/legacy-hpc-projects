Project Summary

Two years after the signing of the revitalized peace agreement, its implementation has not reduced the humanitarian needs of the South Sudanese people. South Sudan remained a protection crisis in 2020. Lack of durable peace and limited investment in basic services are holding people back from stability and sustainable development.(HNO2021)
In 2020, communities were hit hard by the triple shock of intensified conflict and sub-national violence, a second consecutive year of major flooding, and the impacts of COVID-19. Some 1.6 million people remained internally displaced and another 2.2 million as refugees in the region. (HNO 2021).
The health situation has further worsened due to last year’s intensified conflict and sub-national violence, a second consecutive year of major flooding, and the impact of COVID-19. In 2021, the Cluster will target 5.2 Million People in Need (as per 2021 HNO).
According to OCHA’s Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for 2020, there are over 51,000 people with significant humanitarian needs in Mayendit County, representing approximately 95% of the county’s total population, according to population estimates listed in the HNO. Mayendit is listed as one of nine counties in most “dire” need of health services (HNO 2020).
Mayandit was among the areas that were vastly affected by flooding in Unity State. An inter-agency flood assessment mission that was conducted comprising of a CCCM team revealed 1,652 individuals displaced by the current flooding in Mayandit were coming from different Payams including Madol, Malkuer, Pabuong, and Bathjiop.(IOM CCM Sep 2020).
Mayendit is Vast with very poor communication, road and referral network. The area is flood prone during the rainy season.
CASS is the current implementer of primary health care services in the County with a network of 12 health facilities and one outreach site with Funding from HPF.
CASS with funding from health cluster intends to strengthen primary health care services, emergency preparedness and response in the county by reaching, 100% of the IDPS (12,066) and 100% of Displaced (12,066) and 100 % of the host community (31,128) a total population of 55,260 (Male 27,995, Female 27,265)
This will be done through provision of primary health care services at 1 PHCC (Mayendit), 2 PHCU’s (Paboung, Madol) and 1 mobile team.
Manyo County borders the South Kordofan and White Nile regions of Sudan to the north and west respectively, Renk and Melut Counties to the east, and Fashoda County to the south.(HNO 2020)
The cumulative impact of this interruption to services has been a significant humanitarian burden. OCHA’s Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for 2020 estimates that there are over 52,000 people with significant humanitarian needs in Manyo County. This represents 41% of the estimated population of the county reported in the HNO and reflects an increase compared to previous years. (CRSF 2020).
There is limited access to health and Nutrition services in Manyo County. No physical presence of humanitarian partners apart from CASS. Access to health facilities especially during rainy season is by speed boat which is very expensive.
CASS is the current implementer of primary health care services in the County with funding from World bank. CASS support a network of 6 health facilities (3 PHCC and 3 PHCU’s).
CASS with funding from health cluster intends to strengthen primary health care services, emergency preparedness and response in the county by reaching, 100% of the IDPS/ Displaced (6,947) and 50 % of the host community (1,269) a total population of 8,216 (Male 4206, Female 4,010).
During rainy season access to most of the facilities is through water transport using speed boat, the facilities are about 3 to 4 hours apart. The support from Health cluster will help improve referral, boost immunization services and access to primary health care services through 1 PHCC (kaka), 1 PHCU(Thorgwang) and a mobile team.

There are no other means for movement during this period making referral very challenging. Some facilities do not have cold chain equipment like Thorgwang PHCU and Mobile services are difficult to operate because of high cost of the speed boat.Apart from the health partner CASS, there are no physical presence of other humanitarian partners in the County. World vision provides nutrition services through mobile teams from Renk.The support from Health cluster will help improve referral, boost immunization services and access to primary health care services through 1 PHCC, 1 PHCU and a mobile team.

2 - Project activities

• Support static primary health care services at 2 PHCC’s, 3 PHCU’s and 1 Mobile team. • Establish 2 mobile teams, 1 in Manyo and 1 in Mayendit. • IPC in health facilities through regular assessment using score cards, provision of supplies and protocols and continuous monitoring of WASH in health facilities• Immunization against vaccine preventable diseases and procurement of outbreak response commodities and emergency antigens for immunization and cold chain commodity.• Provide technical and operational assistance to heath facilities through supportive supervision visits to ensure quality provision of health services• Views of the communities are taken into consideration in the provision of health service.• Provide services for SAM with medical complications at Kaka PHCC. • Improve the Wash facilities in the health facilities by providing safe water, provision of cleaning materials, general infection prevention and control. • Provision of CMR services at Kaka and Mayendit PHCC’s. • Provision of BeMONC services at Kaka and Mayendit PHCC’s. • Recruitment of skilled midwives, CO’s at all the facilities to be supported. • Training of skilled birth attendants. • Provision of routines outpatient consultations at the PHCC’s, PHCU’s and the mobile teams. • Training of health workers on CMR, IPC, IMNCI, disease surveillance and response. • Provision of Rape kits at Kaka and Mayendit PHCC’s. • Provision of sensitization and referral services of SGBV cases from all the supported health facilities and mobile teams. • Sensitization on MHPSS services and referral of cases.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Children aid will ensure that members of the vulnerable groups of the communities will be consulted right from the design and during implementation through making sure they are represented in community structures such as village health committee, Boma Health committee etc.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project design was informed by the feedback from the communities to be served. The beneficiaries of the service will be engaged from inception, regular meetings will be conducted with different stakeholders from the communities to understand their feelings/needs, the intervention will be evaluated from time to time and corrective measures shall be instituted as appropriate.Complaint box will be established in all the project sites to ensure the communities can voice their concerns about the intervention and actions will be taken accordingly. The populations will be informed of the duration of intervention and expected date of closure, hand over will be done in a transparent manner with the involvement of different stakeholders and development partners in the respective locations.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

During project review were all stakeholders are present concerns from feedback are noted and immediately in-cooperated into the next Phase of months of the Project.
