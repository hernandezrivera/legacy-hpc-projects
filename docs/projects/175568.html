Project Summary

This project is in-line with health Cluster objective and the Strategic Objectives of South Sudan Humanitarian Response Plan 2021.The project will meet emergency primary health care service to the most vulnerable population in the communities both returnees, IDPs, and the host communities of Panyijiar, Koch, Maiwut, Leer, Mayendit, Pariang, Akobo and Longechuk counties.
This project will integrate health service delivery with WASH, Protection and Nutrition. The targeted counties have convergence of high needs related to health service, food insecurity, protection, and water, sanitation and hygiene, with the estimated 81% of people in need of humanitarian assistance with 310,000 people are refugees.
The latest humanitarian needs overview 2021 (HNO), indicates that 8.3 million people of which (women 24%, girls 26%, boys 28% and men 22%) are in need of humanitarian assistance representing 81% population in South Sudan who are IPC phase 3, 4 and 5. Relief Agency is targeted 8,750 beneficiaries disaggregated by gender as Women 3,061, Girls 2,187, Boys 2,187, Men 1,313 . Prolonged effects of 2013 -2016 crisis as well as the COVID 19 pandemic, inter-communal fighting and the revenge killing are the factors that have triggered the humanitarian’s crisis in addition to the recent floods in the country particularly in Jonglei, Upper Nile and Unity states. This has caused the negative impact on the life of vulnerable people in the target counties. The project priority will be placed on women, children, elderly, men and the people with special needs such as people with disabilities.
The emergency primary health care service provision will improve the health status of vulnerable population through the provision of , 5 Primary health care unit (PHCUs) and 3 mobile clinics in all the 3 states in South Sudan. It will also provide long term lifesaving intervention through building the capacity of health staff for the preparedness of taking the health facilities through the counties health department (CHD) after the project period as a sustainability strategy. . For the project to achieve its overall objectives, RA shall mainstream across the various elements from the various clusters to achieve a broader result. Relief Agency will co-ordinate and collaborate with other partners to meet necessary needs. RA will expand its outreach to work hand in hand with other humanitarian partners in order to achieve cluster and overall strategic objectives. This project will address meaningful support to the vulnerable members among the communities in the hard to reach areas as well as providing basic support to the vulnerable communities. Relief Agency will provide health care service including the services of the people living with disabilities by identifying their needs and reported to relevance services providers.
The project will address the reduction of mortality and morbidity in South Sudan through provision of OPD consultations, ANC and PNC services, Reproductive health services, malnutrition screening and referrals, mental health and SGBV services.
The health service delivery will ensure that the most vulnerable populations cut off from health service delivery are reached through mobile health services thus contributing to improving access to health services to the vulnerable and affected populations.

1 - Needs

As per the recently assessment report by IOM DTM team, at least 10,745 people have been displaced in Akobo County by the flood with over 1 million people affected in South Sudan, leaving them with no medical services, WASH, NFI and other basic services. Decades of protracted conflict, chronic vulnerability as well as weak essential services continues to put the three states into a humanitarian crisis. It is estimated that more than hundreds of thousands of people will need humanitarian assistances in early 2021 as a result of crisis and other humanitarian emergencies including COVID 19 pandemic. Over 8.3 million people are projected to face severe acute food insecurity in 2021 according to humanitarians need overview. An estimated 1.3 million children aged 6-59 months will need health care service. Unity, Upper Nile and Jonglei states have charmingly become epicenters for humanitarian crisis due to its heightened vulnerability to climatic shocks, including inter-communal violence and limited access to basic services. The main humanitarian needs in these three states include access and scale up of both preventive and curative primary health care service, Nutritional services; access to safe drinking water. A report by REACH and IRNA assessment by Relief Agency and other partners of Oct. 2020 found that widespread destruction of food stocks, decimated livestock and destruction livelihood assets including health facilities had been destroyed by flood which led to the disruption of health service and limited access to health care service and increase in market food prices is affecting food availability and accessibility which led to malnutrition among the children. Access and utilization of health services in those three states remains significantly poor. Also the devastated floods damaged the few health service facilities available which led to high morbidity and mortality with the increasing trends of diarrhea and acute respiratory infections and other water related diseases. This project is aimed at delivering emergencies lifesaving that will contribute to overarching objectives of reducing morbidity and mortality levels as well as promoting protection services that address vulnerabilities in women and children in Unity, Upper Nile and Jonglei states. This project intends to deliver quality essential Primary Health Care (PHCCs) support through both static and mobile response modalities in order to improve access to lifesaving for over 102285 vulnerable populations. The nutrition component will scale up services and implement a full CMAM package covering both TSFP and OTP which will further increase and deepen coverage and access to 15,841 people in the counties of Jonglei, Unity and Upper States. Health services are needed particularly in hard to reach areas where the other partners are not reaching or limited/ no health facilities being operated there. RA will adopt an inter-cluster approach in delivering its activities and ensure program quality through accountability to affected populations, gender mainstreaming and effective mechanisms for humanitarian protection during this project implementation. RA will Integrate MHPSS and SGBV including services in health facilities and provision of MHPSS services.

2 - Project activities

• Provision outpatient and Inpatient services that will include management of uncomplicated malaria, acute water diarrhea, uncomplicated pneumonia and other diseases through routine consultation. • Provision EPI services to infants (aggregated by age and gender) for 6 preventable communicable diseases through vaccination. • Provision of basic ANC through safe and clean deliveries attended by skilled trained health personnel • Provision Nutrition services by doing screening, deworming, management and Referrals. • Conduct community based outreaches and community mobilization through awareness raising to ensure the dissemination of health messages • Provision of health education and community social mobilization on health preventive measures • Provision of integrated diseases surveillance responses service ( IDSR) and control including outbreak detection, investigation and response. • Training of Health facility staffs in IPC & Case definition for COVID 19 • Integrate MHPSS and SGBV including services in health facilities and provision of MHPSS services. • Provide reproductive health service both at the PHCCs and PHCUs.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

PSEA messages are part of the information package shared and disseminated to the project beneficiaries during the consultations and information sharing sessions and even throughout the project implementation PSEA messages shall be disseminated and beneficiaries are encouraged to report with immediate effect any form of such acts during the project implementation in a confidential fashion as possible through the established feedback mechanisms

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project team will adhere to inclusivity principle throughout the project implementation so as to hear from all gender groups inclusive of people living with disability and impairment, sign language will be paid attention to read and address beneficiaries unsaid concerns expressed in the form of body language as well as strengthening outreach awareness to reach vulnerable groups to ensure feedback and gather their views regarding the project assistance. The team will also ensures to bright the language gaps for illiterate beneficiaries using local language to gather the information that may help to measure project effectiveness.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project team will carry out information sharing sessions not limited to regular group discussions and key informant interviews with different groups of community member including, women leaders, church leaders local authorities and the county health department (CHD) to disseminate the project key information and the roles of the different stakeholders, existing complaint and feedback mechanism in the project and encourage beneficiaries to submit their complaints/feedback through any practical effect channel to the project team during and even after the implementation. RA team is flexible to give the beneficiaries choice of languages they could speak to provide feedback comfortably.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Relief Agency project team will collect beneficiaries’ feedback and use it in such way as when facilitating decision on the assistance in order to avoid harm against of the beneficiaries targeted for this support. Feedback shall be used as well to address complaints about the intended support so as to achieve optimal positive outcomes than negative and what changes did communities got from this project.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

The project will identifies, address and monitor environmental, attitudinal and Institutional barriers that are unable the participation of people living with disabilities, older persons so that they will be part of the project owners. The project team with consultation from the association of people living with disabilities will dedicate the budget for removal of these identified barriers. The Relief Agency will coordinate referrals and involve local authorities to ensure safety is guaranteed to the patients and their caregivers. MHPSS, CMR and health packages will be integrated for effective case management of mass causalities/Injuries and referrals. CHWs and hygiene promoters will be train and mentor for health basic skills essential for management of common diseases such as malaria, AWD and ARI. The South Sudan Boma Health Initiative (MoH) module will be used for this purpose as recommended by MoH. During the social mobilization for health. Relief Agency will integrate peace messages to all social groups to promote social co-existence and harmony. The project team will also participate in active protection threats monitoring, referrals to the protection care givers, referrals of suspected cases to peace committees, forums and local authorities as well as to the neutral partners operating in the counties Relief Agency has a PSEA policy that will be implemented by all the project staff to ensure safety of beneficiaries and especially women and girls as they access health services
