Project Summary

The continue conflict between Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) since April 15th,2023 in Khartoum more than 7.4 million people have fled their homes.
More and new displacement took place due to clashes in Gazira, 611,000 people displaced in Aj Jazirah. About 205,500 on safe locations within Aj Jazirah, and another 304,336 IDPs fled to other states across Sudan, including Gedaref (64,551 IDPs), Sennar (60,000 IDPs), Red Sea (50,035 IDPs), White Nile (40,750 IDPs), River Nile (30,000 IDPs), Kassala (30,000 IDPs), Blue Nile (15,000 IDPs), and Northern (14,000 IDPs) states.
The internally displaced persons (IDPs) are sheltering in 6,282 locations across all of Sudan’s 18 states, an increase of 343 locations over the past month. The expanding of fighting has driven a significant increase in humanitarian needs during the harvesting season development is expected to lead to considerable deterioration in acute food insecurity in the southeast from what was previously expected, worsening an already dire situation, according to FEWS NET’s Sudan - Food Security Outlook Update, outcomes expected in the heavily conflict-affected urban areas of Khartoum, greater Darfur, greater Kordofan, and Khartoum
Disease outbreaks are increasing due to the disruption of basic public health services, including poor disease surveillance, functioning public health laboratory and rapid response teams. In addition, insecurity, displacement, limited access to medicines; medical supplies, electricity, and water continue to pose enormous challenges to delivering health care across the country. About 65 per cent of the population lack accesses to healthcare and between 70 - 80 per cent of hospitals in conflict-affected areas are no longer functional. Meanwhile, 7,725 suspected cases of cholera, including 216 deaths, have been reported from nine states as of 15 December, according to the Federal Ministry of Health (FMoH) and the World Health Organization (WHO). Other disease outbreaks are ongoing in several states, including measles, malaria and dengue.
NPO established in 2014 as a response to the call raised by communities, who are complained the urgent needs in IDPs camps North Darfur. NPO is responding for Humanitarian and development program with main office in Khartoum and six State-office in El-Fashir, Gadaref, South Kordofan, Gazira, River Nile and Kassala. NPO offices well equipped with qualified staff in Humanitarian work and technical lead in Protection, WASH, Health, Nutrition. FSL and Education. NPO has financial system (ODOO) linked the head office with other states offices.
The proposed integrated Health and Nutrition will establish and run 23 health facility in three states, 13 in Khartoum state – Jabel Awlia locality, 5 in Jazira – Almanagel locality and 5 in Kassala state. Those 23 HF will provide full package of primary health care and nutrition services with medical supplies through OTP sites and refer SAM cases to nearest SCs.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

During implementing this integrated Health/Nutrition and WASH project NPO will take all kinds of design consideration especially in the construction of sanitation facilities, latrines so as not to cause any kind of risk to women, girls, boys and people with special needs. NPO will maintain more number of sanitation facilities keeping reasonable distance from the IDP families, this way will minimize the risk that women and girls potentially face during night time while walking to sanitation facilities, and also will enable children to access latrines at night in close vicinity of the house. In addition awareness raising sessions on gender based violence will be conducted for women, men, boys and girls. While running health facilities the hygiene promotion sessions will conducted , the community (especially women) will be given awareness on HIV/AIDS to increase their on the risks that might result from SGBV if no timely treatment was sought.

NPO has accountability core principle in its program it is comply with what have formalized by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee’s (IASC), Accountability to affected people AAP is about the humanitarian programming cycle to ensure that the community have a voice and a hand in the decisions that affect on program implementation and then impact on their life. This will be through working with primary changes agents (the beneficiaries) from the start of program; in needs assessments, program design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. To realize AAP standards, NPO designed its interventions with a concrete steps include respect, transparency, and a willingness to work with affected communities, and also be influenced and judged by them through establishing open channels of communication for feed information sharing. NPO apply this not only because it is fundamental to humanitarian principles, but also a practical means to improve the quality and effectiveness and sustainability of its program. Incorporate explicit AAP approach elements in it program, NPO usually committed to practice the following steps in all its projects, also will consider, staff and volunteer capacity building to ensure that they well aware, skilled and sticking to AAP elements. in addtion to during the project design the consultation and engagement of with targeted communities from the assessment along the implementation and up to evaluation of the project will be in place. NPO starting with orientation kick off meeting involving all related government sectors and targeted community explaining project component and implementation plan .- Conduct routine coordination meetings and discussions at project sites involving project targeted groups. - Establishing feedback and complaint mechanisms including (hot line and feedback boxes) to seek participation from the less visible/more marginalized members of the community with clear care not to put them in risks and get information not only about project implementation but also about staff and agency behavior while implementing the project - Assigned staff for collection analysis, following up and acting on the feedbacks - Sharing of the lessons from community feedback with agencies and bring the information back to the sectors and ensure partnerships are used to leverage AAP issues.
