Project Summary

This project is aimed at improving Sexual Reproductive Health Services to host communities,IDPs and returnees in emergency through capacity building of health workers,facilitates referral and treatment of affected persons.The focus location will be Lamurde,Mayo-Belwa and Numan LGAs of Adamawa State.The project tends to build and extend on previous efforts of the state ministry of health and other partners in improving access to SRH services in the areas.The focus will be on key activities as outlined in the Inter Agency Working Group (IAWG) Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) for SRH in humanitarian settings. However,special efforts undertaken to address the early recovery and development needs of the health system within the framework of the Humanitarian Development Nexus that will enhance its responsiveness to the needs of the communities as they transition to full recovery.A mix of quick gain interventions based on more resilient outreach services that will utilize outreach tents, specialized centers for adolescents and young people and medium to long-term investments in equipment, capacity building used in this project. Establishing strong referrals system and network between mobile outreach locations and contiguous health facilities for Clinical Management of Rape (CMR)Syndrome STI Management,HIV/AIDS prevention care and treatment,emergency obstetric services and prevention of Mother to Child Transmission of HIV (PMTCT) strengthened because of the demand created from previous SRH/MH outreaches.Provision of Emergency Reproductive Health (ERH) kits for the health facilities will ensure Basic Emergency Obstetric care & CMR services are available at the primary care centers.FSACI will work closely with the State Ministry of Health, Supporting the SRH sub sector to ensure no duplication of services. The project will focus on the LGAs of Lamurde , Mayo-Belwa and Numan where needs for returnees IDPs, refugees and host populations continue to be high. IDP camp based facilities in these locations will be the prioritized as per recommendations of the SRH sub working group.FSACI will work closely with partners on ground in these locations
The trained community volunteers, hygiene promoters, community-based workers and collaboration between FSACI and State Ministry will help in sensitizing and awareness creation in all the communities and settlements within the selected LGAs on COVID-19.Since the pandemic is looming and creating panic and also that so many do not believe in it, it is part of the responsibility of FSACI to make sure quality awareness is done to effect a positive impact for the responding organization
The SO1 is to provide life-saving and life-sustaining humanitarian health assistance to the affected IDPs, returnees and host population, this objective will be achieved by strategically incorporating FSACI proposed project which is to improve Sexual and Reproductive Health Services to host communities, IDPs and returnees in emergency through capacity building of health workers,facilitate referrals and treatment of affected persons in Lamurde, Numan and Mayo-Belwa L.G.As of Adamawa state and contribute to alleviating the sufferings of over 1 million people in critical need within the state
Increasing access to health services for women and girls in Lamurde, Mayo-Belwa and Numan will be achieved through health education and promotion, outreaches, awareness creation, supporting safe deliveries and improved access to health services in prioritized areas with critical needs through a strengthened referral system.
The goal of the project is provision of life-saving and life-sustaining humanitarian health assistance to the affected IDPs, returnees and host population.
The objectives will be:
1. Improving sexual and reproductive health services in emergency.
2. . Support the LGAs health systems to provide basic emergency obstetric and neonatal care services to significantly reduce maternal and neonatal mortality and morbidity

1 - Needs

.9 million People, of which 23% and 57% are women and children respectively are in need of humanitarian assistance in north-east Nigeria in 2020. This is as a result of a crisis that is now in its eleventh year. The crisis, which is fundamentally a protection of civilians’ crisis, has largely been triggered by an ongoing regionalized armed conflict, characterized by widespread abuse against civilians, including rape and other sexual violence, abduction, forced displacement, child recruitment, killings, burning of homes, arbitrary detention, and the use of explosive hazards, including deliberate attacks on civilian targets.Fundraising efforts to implement the Health Sector projects in the Nigerian Humanitarian Response Strategy 2020 has been continuous and relentless. An appeal for $86.2 million for submitted projects to be implemented by humanitarian actors was to provide life-saving aid and alleviate suffering in the three most conflicts affected states in Northern Nigeria. $2.0 million have been received, representing 2.3% of the required funds, according to the Financial Tracking Services (https://fts.unocha.org/appeals/869/summary). Despite the contributions made so far to address the humanitarian needs of this population, there exists still a huge spending gap in health. Although this information does not show specifically the disaggregated expenditures and funding gaps for SRH services, however, it is well documented that Humanitarian agencies usually focus on basic needs and care of infectious diseases to prevent deaths and disabilities. The reproductive rights of host communities, refugees, returnees and internally displaced people are often left behind or at most move at a slow pace. This leaves little or no opportunities to exercise people’s sexual and reproductive rights or care for women’s reproductive health needs. While maternal mortality and morbidity is a common cause of death among women of reproductive age living in resource poor setting, having the extra stress of living outside their usual stable environment into desperate conditions in camps or other settlements, exacerbates their vulnerability and make delivering a child even more difficult and potentially life-threatening. Breakdown of pre-existing family support networks and the loss of traditional sources of information, assistance, and protection aggravates their vulnerability.Yet despite the significant need for specific sexual and reproductive health services in (Lamurde, Mayo-Belwa and Numan) Adamawa state, there is no significant and adequate presence of health partners in the above mentioned LGAs with Reproductive health activities. It will be a great collaboration and support from the sector to the State Ministry to have FSACI in the listed LGAs responding to the SRH needs of the populace (IDPs, Returnees and Host Communities)
