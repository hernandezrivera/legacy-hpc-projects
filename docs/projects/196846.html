Project Summary

The project proposed interventions seek to improve the humanitarian situation for the war-affected population by providing secured and dignity shelter to provide more security and privacy by targeting the most vulnerable people depending on the gap analysis from the SNFI cluster the targeted locations will be selected, then the field team will conduct beneficiaries’ registration and verification by coordination and coordination with LC in targeted areas to get the final list of the beneficiaries according to vulnerability criteria.
The project will provide 1200 shelter caseload for 1200 HHS of the most vulnerable IDPs in Al-Bab and Afrin camps to transfer them from camps to secured and dignity shelters.
•ONSUR will Construct1200 shelter units with all infrastructure needed. Furthermore, these building will accompanied by service facilities including (mosque, schools (primary and middle school), health center, playground, gardens) in in Al-Bab and Afrin areas. The square for per housing will be m2 includes 2 rooms, kitchen, bath room, WC, balcony.

3 - Needs

As outlined in the Shelter and Non- Food Iteams SNFI HNO 2023, Syria remains a complexhumanitarian and protection emergency characterized by over 10 years of ongoinghostilities and their long-term consequences including widespread destruction of civilianinfrastructure, explosive ordnance contamination, and the largest number of internally displaced people in the world. More than ten years of crisis have inflicted immense suffering on the civilian population, who have been subject to massive and systematic violations of international humanitarian and human rights law. More recently, the accelerating economic deterioration and impacts of climate change have increasingly become additional key drivers of needs, compounding vulnerabilities even further. In 2022,14.6 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 1.2 million from 2021. Despite receiving only 46 percent of the required funds for the 2021 HRP, Syria remains one of the largest humanitarian responses in the world, with assistance delivered to 6.8 million people per month in the past year. The long-standing needs of an estimated 6.9 million IDPs remain staggering, particularly for over two million people in 1,760 informal settlements and planned camps, often hosted in inadequate shelters and with limited access to basic services.
