Project Summary

The project will contribute to supporting the most vulnerable population in NWS through; financial support to small and medium enterprises and provide short-term employment opportunities through cash-for-work support. Additionally, the project will provide the most vulnerable HHs with market-based assistance. The project will promote the usage of renewable energy by supporting farmers with solar-panelled irrigation solutions.
SR will provide financial support to a total of 500 new entrepreneurs to start their first entrepreneurship by providing small/medium cash grants. The grant will be delivered in 3 instalments according to the business plan. The completion of each stage will trigger the release of the following instalment, SR will measure the progress of the business plan implementation through multiple evaluation methodologies, including; field visits, review of business cost/benefit analysis and reviewing the remaining period plan to ensure business success.
SR will enable 2000 workers (12000 individuals) to meet their basic needs by providing Monthly wages over 6 months. Each HH will receive (5$ unskilled and 8$ semiskilled daily) on an average 130$ per month (as per ERL cluster recommendation). Due to the deteriorating status of both IDPs and host communities, SR will target both groups. To ensure that the most vulnerable HHs selected, SR will conduct 100% door-to-door registration and verification and select the workers according to SR eligibility and vulnerability scoring system.
The project will provide the most vulnerable groups (female-headed households mainly), on average 1000 HHs, with seed funding to start their own business in their homes, which will enable them to earn their income and become self-resilient. SR will conduct community consultation sessions to identify common skills for females in the targeted locations and potential ideas to be implemented under this activity and will construct the vulnerabilities and eligibilities together with community representatives.
Additionally, the project will enable 3000 farmers (15,000 individuals) to improve access to irrigation by providing solar-panelled irrigation pumps, which will reduce the cost and improve the yield quantity/quality and will be reflected in a higher profit for farmers.

3 - Needs

According to ERL HNO section, The assessments and analyses carried out across the country continue to show a general deterioration in access to livelihoods, ability to meet basic needs, availability and affordability of infrastructure and services, threatening the resilience and social cohesion of households. Inflationary trends on key goods and services, widespread low productivity of inputs, low wages, and lack of employment, among other factors, continue to impact households’ priority needs and exacerbate the income-expenditure gap across the country. Data on labor force participation at the household level shows that there is a marked difference in the different areas of control. In NWS, it reaches 21 per cent. Moreover, considering that approximately 90 per cent of households in Syria consistently experience a gap between their income earned and expenditure required to meet basic needs, severe economic vulnerability poses increasingly higher risks of reliance on negative coping mechanisms, which include begging, illicit economic activities, corruption, child labor, the recruitment of men and children by armed groups, as well as risks of increased GBV.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

SR will do many actions to mainstream protection and gender across all project stages as following: SR conducted a rapid needs assessment by using Focus Group Discussions (FGD) with males and females separately as well as the most vulnerable groups such as disabled and PLW to ensure the program design meets the exact need for beneficiaries to avoid doing harm in the implementation stages. Female staff will be hired for distribution/redemption and MEAL to ensure equal access for all beneficiary categories including females headed of household, girls and widowed. Beneficiaries will be clearly informed that all distributed value-based vouchers are free and that cash distributed is provided with no obligations on their end. Options for home delivery of the needed food commodities for vouchers which will be redeemed on behalf of vulnerable persons (e.g., PWD who cannot access the markets, elderly, etc.) confidential complaints and protection reporting mechanism system will be designed and beneficiaries will be clearly informed where to refer or report incidents or violations. The day, time, and location of the distribution of the vouchers will be discussed with different community members and leaders to determine the safest most accessible location to allow all individuals including women, children, and the elderly to access the service with dignity and without exposure to further risk of harm; moreover, the list of contracted markets, allowed food commodities, and the redemption


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Protection Risks: 1-SEA risk against beneficiaries especially women 2-GBV Risk against beneficiaries 3-Not equal access for services from marginalized groups such as disabled and women (Bias and discrimination in distributions process) Mitigating Measures: All staff and vendors will be trained, acknowledge, and sign a code of conduct for sexual exploitation and abuse, moreover, SR has zero-tolerance against SEA. PSEA messaging and awareness-raising will be done with beneficiaries through one or more of the following (PSEA posters will be distributed to beneficiaries, PSEA banners will be placed at the distribution site, PSEA messaging/videos and hotline information will be shared with beneficiaries through SMS/WhatsApp). All project staff will be trained on GBV prevention and response in emergencies, to ensure full adherence with the Do No Harm principle and to stress that PSEA and GBV issues will be dealt with firmly and expeditiously. Female staff will always be present at the distribution/ redemption places and involved in the distribution process All markets populated by beneficiaries will have access to educational posters/banners for the appropriate use. Ensuring safe and equal access to inclusive, accessible, and non-discriminatory list of markets to get their food assistance. Protection cases including GBV will be referred to the respective focal point NGOs in the respective locations and sensitive data will be handled and kept confidentially with the PSEA field FP.
