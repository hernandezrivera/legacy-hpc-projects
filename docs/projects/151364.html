Project Summary

In this project, TUOS is planning to implement integrated emergency PHC services through 2 fixed health facilities and 4 mobile teams targeting 17500 people (including 10000 female and 7500 male) with equal access to women, girls, men and boys. This project will seek to reduce health impact within the IDPs and host communities in Dhusamareb district. Project activities includes; 1. Provide Quality Integrated health services to IDPs and affected rural population through 2 Mobile team and 2 fixed health facilities to serve 17,500 people (Female: 10,000, Male: 7500) Children (under 18): 8000, Adult (18-59): 9000, Elderly (above 59): 500 2. Establish trauma centers to respond to counselling services to the affected population. 3. Provide basic training on reproductive, maternal, newborn and child health in emergencies to 20 (10 men, 10 women) service providers and 25 (12 men and 13 women) community health workers 4. Support MoH in targeted locations on emergency preparedness and response to outbreaks of measles and Acute Watery Diarrhea 5. Strengthen referral services for complicated and emergency cases to the referral hospitals 6. Support the provision of immunization services through health facilities and mobile clinics to serve 15,000 children (8000 girls and 7000 boys). 7. Conduct regular supportive supervision to improve and sustain quality health service delivery 8. Support data collection, analysis and use for action in targeted districts for routine use and for emergency response 9. Conduct Community mobilization, and education on health, nutrition, hygiene, and sanitation. Project target group will include 17500 people; IDPs (11000), host community (6500), Female (10000), Male (7500), Children (8000), Adult (9000), and Elderly (500). To carry out this intervention TUOS is seeking USD 609,000 for a one year project under this financial appeal. TUOS will collaborate with other stage holders and partners in the region in an integrated manner to bring health services closer to the vulnerable community. TUOS will work closely with local administration and community leaders to see the success of the project. Monitoring will be carried out jointly with the Ministry of Health and principal partner. TUOS will conduct mobilization before the project and register vulnerable group with the help of community leaders and conduct verification exercise to make sure that the most vulnerable and disadvantaged members of the community benefit.

Needs

The lack of comprehensive and reliable epidemiological and disease information from all regions has made it very difficult to ascertain the extent of gaps in service provision and burden of disease across the country (HNO, 2019). Gu 2018 nutrition survey results reported GAM prevalence of 14 percent and SAM of 1.6 percent indicating sustained Serious levels of GAM. Morbidity remains high among IDPs (41.7% in November 2018 compared to 45% in Deyr 2017). The Gu 2018 CDR (0.93/10 000/day) is an improvement to Serious compared to 1.06/10 000/day in Deyr 2017. However, U5DR has sustained Serious levels 1.27/10 000/day in Gu 2018 compared to 1.51/10 000/day in Deyr 2017. The acute malnutrition, high morbidity and high mortality situation among IDPs in Dhusamareb call for integrated support interventions in order to improve the protracted situation that has persisted in these population groups. Morbidity incidences was reported to be the highest in the region with 41.7%. Low measles immunization and vitamin A supplementation status were reported among IDPs in Dhusamreeb (FSNAU 2018). These factors combining creates the perfect condition for increased morbidities and propagation of disease outbreaks in the affected population which will ultimately lead to increase in the number in need of emergency health services. TUOS rapid assessment in the target villages indicated that the target communities lack access to primary healthcare due to an inadequate number of health facilities and poor physical and technical capacity of the few existing ones, majority of women have no access to primary health care services, including family planning, antenatal care (ANC), micro nutrient supplementation and clean and safe delivery services. In this project, TUOS is planning to implement integrated emergency PHC services through 2 fixed health facilities and 4 mobile teams targeting 18000 people (including 10000 female and 8000 male) with equal access to women, girls, men and boys.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Dhusamareb district has enjoyed a relatively calm condition with less clan conflict over a couple of months now. Though in some recent events, the district has experience high influx of IDPs which may protract clan conflict. However this will be managed by the community elders and local government. The project is in line with Somalia 2017-2020 durable solutions strategy in place. The project will also support the long term government intervention on health programming which aims at reducing negative health impact in the region. Training of community health workers as part of the project activities will help in sustaining health intervention in the health facilities thus promoting sustainability of health and nutrition activities in the district.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

To avoid any form of exclusion in the project, TUOS will employ a Do-No-Harm approach principle and help the community to select project committees which will comprise of all the target group representatives. Beneficiaries targeting and selection committee will be formed from each group, that is host community and IDPs. A selection criteria will be agreed upon before the project starts and discussed upon with the donor for approval. Emphasis will be given to the most vulnerable individuals and households such as IDPs regardless of their age, gender and clan. TUOS will conduct due diligence of the list provided by the selection committee to ascertain the inclusiveness of the target beneficiaries. Periodic monitoring and evaluation exercise will be conducted with the aim of checking the changing dynamics in the target area and need for changes in the project implementation.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Health interventions may fail to reach the most vulnerable and, in some cases, may actually lead to further harm. The risk of exclusion based on gender stereo type and age is prevalent in the community. The project targets women, children, girls, boys and men of all ages. The selection committee will be trained on the selection criteria as such to promote inclusion of all gender and age group in the target community. Pregnant and lactating women and children under five years of age have special health requirements and suffer severe consequences from undernutrition. Experience has shown, however, that all groups need to be involved in health and nutrition programmes as well to ensure that assistance for women and children is effective. Male family members have a strong influence on the feeding practices and eating habits of mothers and children which affect their health status. They also influence the healthcare practices and beliefs of mothers and other caregivers. Health facilities will keep data disintegrated with gender and age. TUOS also will mobilize the community to understand and basis of the project and the target group.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Women and children are the most vulnerable group during clan conflict and violence in the target area. To mitigate the effect of being adversely affected by the conflict when it occurs, TUOS will be working with community security team to protect women and children from attack. Also health services will be provided at the community level or IDP centers thus avoiding the need to travel for such services through mobile teams. Also in times of conflict, TUOS will set up temporary hold up warehouse in the community to avoid supplies cut off and sustain the health services to the beneficiaries. TUOS will also be working with community elders in the target areas such that the community elders themselves will be negotiating among the warring groups to allow services to continue if any of the target areas will be affected.
