Project Summary

In response to identified priority needs of protection risk mitigation and protection service provision, DRC will deliver Sexual and Gender Based Violence (SGBV) prevention and response services for conflict and displacement affected communities. DRC will implement SGBV prevention and response activities in a variety of locations including hard to reach locations through both static and mobile modalities. In doing so DRC aims to contribute to the overarching intersectoral strategic objectives for the 2021 HRP through contributing to the four-protection cluster strategic objectives:
- Ensure the provision of critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas
- Prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience
- Enable durable solutions for IDPs and other population
- Enhance protection assessment and monitoring to inform protection and overall humanitarian response
DRC GBV program activities will ensure GBV prevention and response services are available to women and girls, boys and men, those who are survivors or at risk of GBV. DRC prevention activities will include risk mitigation through protection awareness raising and information dissemination, distribution of dignity kits, capacity building for duty bearers and strengthening of community-based protection capacities. Gender-responsive action will include mobilising communities in the prevention of violence against women and girls through the SASA! community mobilization program, working with both men and women to challenge the negative consequences of gender inequality and harmful power dynamics. SGBV response activities will include case management for survivors of SGBV and focused non-specialized individual and group-based counselling for women and girls. DRC will also support where appropriate the establishment of community based safe shelters and/or safe homes. DRC will strengthen or support the establishment of safe referral pathways in areas of both static and mobile operation. DRC currently has eight Women and Girls Friendly Spaces (WGFS) and/or Multi-Purpose Protection Centres (MPCs) located in Unity State and Upper Nile state. DRC will continue to provide services in these DRC established, women and girls owned, spaces. SGBV response services will be mainly provided in these static locations given the crucial need for confidentiality, privacy and ability to follow up when delivering such activities in order to avoid doing harm. DRC mobile SGBV services will focus mainly on prevention activities where response services are available. DRC currently has 2 Mobile Protection Teams that operate in hard to reach locations that have limited access to services. These mobile response teams will provide GBV prevention services where there are already response services available. As such the locations listed as targeted areas for intervention include those locations where DRC has static presence as well as locations where DRC may respond as part of the country wide mobile protection response team capacity.
DRC activities in the sectors of SNFI, FSL and other sub sectors of protection including HLP and General Protection will complement SGBV prevention and response services and allow for multi-sectoral service delivery in response to multi-faceted protection needs.
Overall, DRC aims to reach approximately 14,290 vulnerable individuals affected by displacement and conflict, including approximately 2,010 men, 7,290 women, 1,420 boys, and 3,570 girls.

1 - Needs

South Sudan remains a protection crisis, with 8.3 million people in need of humanitarian assistance (HNO 2021) and 60% of the population facing severe acute food insecurity in 2021 (IPC Report). A triple shock of increased sub-national violence, two-years of severe flooding and COVID-19 pandemic has severely impacted people’s physical and mental well-being, living-standards and deteriorated coping mechanisms.SGBV remains one of the major protection concerns and remains underreported due to unequal power imbalances between men and women, social stigma, fear of retaliation, lack of access to justice and services and multiple layers of harmful traditional norms. An analysis of the 2020 GBVIMS data indicates that 99% of GBV survivors were women and girls, with 55% of them experiencing violence by an intimate partner. Prolonged localized conflict has resulted in a large number of female-headed households; however this position is in conflict with traditional gender roles that relegate women to relatively subservient roles. In assessed areas pressure on limited resources is expected to increase as IDPS and refugees return to the state. Based on a GBV SC safety audit conducted at the end of 2020, women and girls are subsequently increasing their movements to the bush to collect firewood and soil to support household income generation, placing them at higher risk of assault. As evidenced by DRC protection information analysis, in 2020, COVID-19 has significantly heightened protection risks faced by women, particularly adolescent girls, as restrictive movement, closure of schools and decrease in livelihoods opportunities have contributed to an increase in sexual exploitation, child marriage and early pregnancies – as well as increased intimate partner violence. The voice of women also remains largely unrepresented in leadership structures despite being well placed to strengthen community self-protection. Recent GBVIMS data indicates continued prevalence of violations across states of South Sudan, particularly Unity and Upper Nile state highlighting the continued need for a flexible approach consisting of both static and mobile service delivery.

2 - Project activities

The specific detailed activities that DRC will implement include:Provision of survivor centered psychosocial support that is focused on empowerment and recovery. Through the WGFS that DRC will continue supporting under this award, DRC will provide a space for women and girls to engage in group and individual non-specialized focused PSS through participation in women led initiatives such as kitchen gardening, baking, basket weaving and other skills building activities based on women and girl’s preferences. DRC will continue to support through capacity building, mentorship and material support the existing Women’s Network groups in Upper Nile and Unity State. These groups play a crucial role in supporting the psychosocial wellbeing of women and girls in their communities. DRC will provide comprehensive quality survivor-centred case management services to survivors and individuals at risk of GBV in line with best practice such as the Inter-agency GBV Guidelines for CM. GBV service mappings will be conducted, in target locations and in consultation with GBV SC, to map out available services and enhance referrals. GBV response staff will work closely with other sectors such as health and legal to strengthen the referral pathway, including coordination to ensure PHCs offer CMR services, PEP kits and ECs. Where necessary, as part of case management, GBV survivors will receive IPA, cash or material support, to address an immediate protection risk. DRC will conduct information dissemination and awareness raising on available services, the prevention and mitigation of GBV risks identified in the community and relevant rights and entitlements informed by protection risk analysis and GBVIMS trend analysisDRC will distribute dignity kits given women and girls consistently express the need for hygiene and sanitary items and other items to preserve dignity. DRC will increase accountability of men and boys in SGBV prevention through engaging communities with SASA!. The SASA! methodology aims to address the power imbalance between men and women, through mobilizing community to generate change. DRC will establish and/or strengthen support to community based safe shelters to provide immediate security, temporary refuge and support GBV survivors who are escaping violence or abusive situations. The provision of safe shelters has been a gap for GBV survivors especially those experiencing intimate partner violence. To better support a safe, dignified response DRC will also provide capacity building on GBV guiding principles and core concepts for frontline service providers including the survivor centred approach, the rights of women and the GBV pocket guide. DRCs capacity for a multi-sectoral approach will ensure holistic GBV prevention and response programming. DRC will support women and girls with the provision of fuel efficient stoves to further mitigate the risk of SGBV particularly given the continued reporting of sexual violence during firewood collection. This activity will be integrated with DRCs FSL work, with DRC FSL team assisting in creating market linkages. DRC will refer at risk women to services such as lifesaving DRC SNFI services. DRC will also ensure that at risk women who would benefit from engagement in IGAs are safely referred, and prioritized, for inclusion within DRC FSL activities. In coordination with DRC CCCM colleague’s women and girls engaged in DRC GBV services will be supported to conduct safety audits of their communities and recommendations will be channeled to the appropriate internal DRC services as well as clusters. The Women’s Networks and WGFS will also serve as a structures through which other female DRC sectoral staff can consult with women and girls to ensure their project activities are safe and response to the needs of women and girls.

5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Cash IPA is based on market functionality as cash is only provided, instead of in-kind, when the support required to meet the protection outcome is feasibly able to be purchased (eg cash for transportation services where such services are present).

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash will be provided as IPA support for survivors engaged in case management where referrals to other services is not feasible and where in kind support will not address the immediate protection risk and achieve the intended protection outcome. Cash will be provided only if no other actor can provide support, and the protection risk can only be resolved or reduced through this means (for example providing cash for transport to a life-saving service which the person would otherwise not be able to access), deemed safe for the person in need to move to a safe space/location or access life-saving services but does not have the means to do so. The cash assistance is provided on an individual basis and only following a thorough risk analysis.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

DRC conducts regular awareness raising with beneficiaries on PSEA, including reporting mechanisms, as well as provides information on PSEA to beneficiaries in multiple formats.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

DRC is implementing a Feedback, Complaints and Response Mechanisms in all project locations, which would be capable of handling complaints related to any of its activities, and will have several entry points for lodging complaints comprising of in-person office visits, Let us know box (suggestion box) and through community visits (either orally at or formal written complaints, at both the FCRM desks or to other DRC staff), and through intermediaries working with DRC e.g. implementing partners and community representatives. Having several mechanisms allows for beneficiaries to choose a method that is most comfortable and practical for them and ensures there is at least one mechanism in place in all locations. DRC Staff have been trained to handle personal complaints and inquiries in a confidential manner, and protection issues are referred to DRC protection team or to protection partners to ensure sensitive issues are handled by qualified staff only.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

DRC has IEC materials available in multiple formats that are displayed/shared in order to raise awareness on the feedback and complaints mechanisms. DRC conducts regular awareness raising with communities on the presence of the complaints and feedback mechanisms.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

DRC has a functional Feedback, Complaints, Response Mechanism (FCRM) that allows beneficiaries to give feedback and complaints regarding the activities being implemented in their respective locations. The DRC F-CRM desks are set up to receive complaints and questions from beneficiaries and follow-up with feedback, including referrals to external service-providers or to the DRC protection desks when appropriate, either immediately or within a reasonable timeframe, depending on the nature of the complaint. DRC has a standardized FCRM form where all feedback and complaints are captured. All data captured on the FCRM forms is then uploaded onto a password protected database where all feedback and complaints are tacked until they are closed. Furthermore, beneficiary complaints and feedback will also be captured when conducting post distribution monitoring (PDM). Beneficiaries will be able to provide their opinions on how DRC interventions can be improved and they will be given the opportunity to provide feedback or make a complaint.
