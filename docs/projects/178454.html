Project Summary

The HALO Trust will continue to deliver an integrated Mine Action approach in northwest Syria involving Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE), Explosive Ordnance Disposal (EOD), Non-Technical Survey (NTS) and Victim Assistance (VA), delivered both directly and through three implementing partners.
HALO and its implementing partners will provide EORE to men, women, boys and girls, with a focus on community members most at risk of accidents (including children, teenagers, IDPs, agricultural workers and rubble removal workers). HALO will also continue to deliver ‘Training of Trainer’ (ToT) workshops to NGO workers and Community Focal Points to improve the relevance, integration, and reach of EORE messaging across all communities and relevant humanitarian sectors. Incorporation of COVID-19 messaging to EORE sessions will continue throughout 2021 and HALO will provide COVID hygiene kits to EORE beneficiaries.
In Q4 2020, HALO completed its contamination impact survey work in all accessible communities in northwest Syria, improving HALO’s awareness and understanding of explosive ordnance (EO) contamination in the region. This has created opportunities for HALO to advance its survey activities to improve its Mine Action response in the region. Disposal teams will dispose of all accessible surface and sub-surface EO items (excluding minefields and IEDs) in accordance with priorities determined by the HALO Operations room. The primary method of disposal will be via thermite flares, with the outcome being the detonation or burning of EO items in a controlled and safe manner by suitably qualified field staff, mindful of team safety and the safety of local communities. EOD permanently removes the lethal threat posed by EO. At this point in time, disposal activities do not extend to minefields or IEDs, due to the technical nature of these items.
NTS activities will validate previous explosive ordnance survey data with an emphasis on suspected minefields, IED fields, and multiple EO-contaminated areas (notably sub-munitions). NTS provides information for disposal and clearance operations and informs where Mine Action resources should be prioritised. Lastly, NTS provides local authorities, humanitarian actors, the Gaziantep cluster network, and the donor community with a greater understanding of EO threats, including specific locations.
HALO will also expand its EOD activities, through its Turkish implementing partner, to all accessible districts in Turkish-administered northern Aleppo, including Afrin. This will permanently remove the threat of explosive ordnance contamination to community members and IDPs, preventing further accidents and allow land, infrastructure and resources to be used safely and productively. HALO will also incorporate into operations full clearance activities to tackle minefields identified and mapped during prior NTS work.
The support to survivors of EO accidents will continue through a Victim Assistance (VA) component implemented by HALO and one of its partners. This will include the provision of prosthetics and orthotics, physiotherapy, nursing, psychosocial services and multi-purpose assistance delivered by three rehab centres between January and February, and two centres between June and December 2021. HALO and its other partner will also provide victim assistance case management services, providing referrals to specialised services for survivors of EO accidents.
HALO acknowledges the requirement to avoid activity duplication within the MASC as well as addressing gaps in providing EORE (to children, adults, CFPs, and NGO Focal Points), NTS and EOD. This will be achieved through ongoing and regular coordination with other members of the MASC, as well as engagement with local authorities, communities, and other NGOs throughout the implementation of the project. Both HALO and its implementing partners are active MASC members (and HALO is co-chair) and regularly submit 4Ws to the cluster.

4 - Needs

As Syria marks the 10th anniversary of conflict, staggering levels of humanitarian need persist. The ongoing crisis has resulted in widespread contamination of communities and agricultural land by all forms of explosives including landmines, cluster munitions, rockets, mortars, artillery projectiles, and IEDs. NW Syria has been hit particularly hard and extensive survey conducted by HALO between 2018 and 2020 confirmed the presence of explosive contamination in 41% of all accessible communities surveyed, with an estimated 1.6 million people living at risk from EO. Overall, 4.2 million people (including 2.7 million IDPs) are living in overcrowded conditions in northwest Syria. This puts pressure on available agricultural land and natural resources, further increasing the chance of casualties of unexploded ordnance. 67% of people living in these contaminated communities are already identified as PiN by the UN. According to HALO’s extensive contamination assessments, from 2018 to 2020, there were 3,500 explosive-related accidents in NW Syria, resulting in approx. 4,500 casualties. In 2020, over 600 casualties have been reported. According to UN OCHA, 25% of all recorded EO casualties in 2020 were children, with 40% killed from these explosive accidents.There is a pressing need for EORE, as the 2021 HNO reported that 70% of key informants had not attended EORE sessions in 2020.Only through the continuation and expansion of survey, disposal and clearance activities is the threat from explosive contamination to community members and IDPs permanently eliminated and future rehabilitation possible. These activities will prevent further accidents and allow land, infrastructure and resources to be used safely and productively.

5 - Implementation of activities

HALO will conduct activities both directly and through implementing partners. EORE activities will involve the delivery of EORE through community-based sessions, with flexible options including interactive school-based sessions, social media campaigns and usage of cartoons and comic books. Considering the different risks that certain gender, age and occupation groups are exposed to, materials tailored for specific groups will be used during sessions. NGO workers and Community Focal Points will be trained through ToT sessions. HALO will prioritise PwDs as ToT participants and continue to explore ways to guarantee the inclusion of PwDs in EORE activities.NTS will confirm hazardous areas by validating previous EO survey data with an emphasis on suspected minefields, IED fields, and multiple EO-contaminated areas. All collected data will be shared with UNMAS. The information on disposal and clearance operations would inform where Mine Action resources should be prioritised.EOD will dispose of all accessible surface and EO items, accordance with priorities determined and agreed upon by HALO and partner technical staff. The primary method of disposal will be via thermite flares to destroy EO in a controlled and safe manner.VA will include provision of services, such as physical rehabilitation, and in-kind assistance and referral to specialised services. MEAL Activities: HALO and partner MEAL staff will compile monitoring reports and feedback forms, while HALO’s field monitors will conduct regular spot checks to validate project implementation. They will attend all activities, including trainings, and complete QA forms. Key informant interviews and focus group discussions will also assess the impact of projects.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Familiarisation may cause the civilian populace to become overconfident to threat of EO(Likelihood: medium; Impact: high): Emphasis on “Stop, Warn and Report” messaging in EORE and introducing EOD team hotline for further interventions. Difficulties accessing girls and women(Likelihood: medium; Impact: high): Current teams already include female staff. HALO will seek to achieve gender-balanced recruitment for field staff to facilitate the consideration/implementation of appropriate activities for inclusion of girls and women. Trained local EOD capacity may become overconfident and injury to team members &/or local community members during survey/disposal tasks(Likelihood: medium; Impact: medium): Staff trained to IMAS standards and supervised by Technical EOD Advisor through regular quality assurance checks. Teams will deploy with an ambulance, first aid equipment, and communications. Risk that local authorities may dictate mine action priorities are not in line with humanitarian objectives(Likelihood: high; Impact: medium): Recruit local staff to build trust and to promote community participation. Highlight humanitarian priorities by using contamination data while engaging with local authorities. Teams perceived as having a biased affiliation, exposing them to harm(Likelihood: low; Impact: high): Partners are well known/trusted in their areas of operation and coordinate with local authorities to facilitate safe access. Escalated dependency on assistance by VA beneficiaries(Likelihood: low; Impact: low): Early health intervention, referrals, and self-reliance promotion through peer support activities will increase beneficiary socio-economic participation and reduce humanitarian assistance dependence.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

HALO and its implementing partners have zero tolerance of abuse of human rights in any part of the organisation or its supply chain and partner organisations. HALO conducts due diligence checks on partners, contractors and suppliers to ensure that correct processes and practices, values and standards are in place to ensure the protection and safeguarding of staff and communities. Gender-balanced recruitment, training, and code of conduct requirements for project staff will be applied to ensure appropriate protection-sensitive interventions. Staff exposed to, or guilty of, GBV, sexual exploitation, and abuse(Likelihood: low; Impact: high): Mitigation - all project staff are trained in PSEA, gender mainstreaming, and 'Do No Harm'. Activities are designed to consider the needs of men, women, boys, and girls. Each beneficiary has access to feedback and complaint mechanisms. Ongoing M&E through field visits. Safeguarding incident in particular gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse during activities(Likelihood: low; Level of impact: High): All project field staff will receive initial and refresher safeguarding training which includes PSEA, GBV, gender mainstreaming, and the principles of 'Do No Harm'. EORE activities are designed, taking into consideration the needs of children and adults. A leaflet including the feedback mechanism (WhatsApp number and an email address) will be provided to each beneficiary.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Access. Regular contact is made with local, governorate and national authorities to ensure any concerns are addressed quickly and avoid withdrawal of approvals. Official protocols have been signed between partners and Turkish governor offices and verbal approval has been given by the President of Turkey’s office to conduct disposal & NTS across Turkish-administered territory. If COVID restrictions prevent standard EORE activities in Idleb, HALO will conduct Indirect EORE activities, including videos via social media and adjusted community engagement.Under performance against project activities. A mobilisation phase builds relationships with relevant local authorities and provide refresher training. Conservative beneficiary targets are established for project activities. Project design is based on experience in NW Syria and in southern Syria. Official sub-grant agreements are established between HALO and partners. Formal project kick-off meetings and monthly project reviews will occur and strong MEAL mechanisms will be in place.Recruitment & retention of essential field personnel. HALO and partners have demonstrated the capacity to recruit good quality staff. The maximum number of suitable staff from any concluding projects will be recruited for the next project and competitive salaries will be offered.Security situation deteriorates preventing/restricting project activities. Daily security situation monitoring informs work plans. Activities can be quickly diverted to respond to changes in the security environment, with the principal security challenge being widespread security deterioration. In this case, HALO and partners will suspend activities and review to identify areas where work can be safely conducted.
