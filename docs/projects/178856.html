Project Summary

As part of the proposed project, TGH will directly contribute to Objectives 1 and 2 for Syria through providing safe and equitable access to qualitative non-formal education (NFE) to out-of-school children (OOSC) and children at-risk of dropping out within public educational institutions. This proposed project will be part of an Education, Protection, WASH and Shelter integrated programming under 2021 HRP.

The response proposed by TGH is based on its 3-year experience in implementing ECHO, OCHA and AICS funded education projects in Syria TGH is thus intending to carry on its integrated approach and replicate its successes in sub-districts of Rural Damascus, Hama, Idlib and Dar’a. These sub-districts were selected based on their level of accessibility and the severity of needs as per the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) (with all the localities being rated over 3), TGH 2021 field assessments and coordination with municipalities SARC, DoE and other NGOs. In addition, these areas are all witnessing important returns and/or displacements movements thus increasing the level of needs.
Through the proposed intervention, TGH will provide access to non-formal education (NFE) to a total of 5,180 children in Duma and Nashabiyeh (Rural Damascus), in Sinjar (Idlib), in Karnaz and Ziyara (Hama) and in Da’ra and Sheikh Miskine (Dar’a). Boys and girls will be both encouraged to participate in the activities through back-to-learning (BTL) campaigns. A specific focus will be made on supporting the reintegration of children with disabilities, early married girls and child labour cases into the formal education system.
The proposed intervention will include:
- Rehabilitation of 7 schools, including gender-sensitive and disability-sensitive WASH facilities;
- Provide schools with school furniture;
- Provide OOSC and children at-risk of dropping out with NFE programs (self-learning programs (SLPs), curriculum B and remedial education);
- Provide TVET for adolescents in safe spaces provided by the communities;
- Conduct BTL campaigns;
- Provide students with school supplies;
- Provide maintenance and running costs (such as cleaning material, stationery for school personnel) in schools;
- Provide cash for education given the priority to OOSC;
- Provide professional development to teaching staff and education personnel;
- Provide teachers and education personnel with teaching resources;
- Provide children with supplementary learning materials.

4 - Needs

As indicated in the HNO 2021, due to COVID-19 measures and the closure of education facilities and/or reduction of learning hours, the number of OOSC and children at risk of dropping out tend to increase. After thorough needs assessment in January 2021, TGH found that a large percentage of educational facilities were still either destroyed or partially damaged in the targeted areas. As a result, most of the schools are congested and overcrowded resulting in poor quality education. Moreover, most of the families reported unaffordable high costs related to education. Coupled with unaffordability of transportation, this is further dissuading parents from sending their children to school. COVID-19 outbreak and the ongoing unprecedented economic downturn have profound impacts on the welfare and livelihoods encouraging child labour. Other limiting factors, such as early marriage and violence both at school and home, are further limiting children access to education. In this context, girls and female adolescents are particularly subject to gender-based violence both on their way and inside schools. They also suffer from the absence of sex-disaggregated WASH facilities. The situation is even more problematic for children and youth with disabilities who face challenges to access education due to the lack of inclusive facilities, adapted transportation and education pedagogy. Reliance on unskilled and unspecialized teachers with limited financial remuneration due to the lack of teaching staff is impeding learning outcomes of students in public schools. Finally, the lack of textbooks, teaching and learning materials impairs learning in the classrooms and demotivates children to continue education.

5 - Implementation of activities

As part of Objective 1 of HRP Response framework, school rehabilitation will be conducted as per INEE, Syria Education Cluster and MoE standards and will take into account gender sensitivity and accessibility for children with reduced mobility i.e. access ramp, seated toilet and grab bar. Besides, TGH will provide NFE to OOSC and children at risk of dropping during the academic year in the targeted schools combined with PSS and mine risk education. TVET for adolescents and numeracy/literacy classes for adolescent will be delivered in safe spaces provided by the community. Aiming at favouring the return to education of OOSC, TGH will implement BTL campaigns in line with UNICEF’s and MoE’s strategy through community based approach that will include the involvement of teachers and parents. TGH will provide multipurpose cash for education aiming at enrolling the most vulnerable OOSC in the targeted areas. A part of Objective 2, teaching staff will be provided with training on Active Learning, Curriculum B, Inclusive Education, life skills, citizenship education, PSS and RE and will be supported by TGH trainer on monthly basis. The project will be directly implemented by TGH in coordination with DoE, MoE and SARC based on its experience (see “Summary”) in these new targeted locations. M&E will be conducted through pre and post-tests. TGH will ensure strong monthly monitoring using Child Monitoring System approach tool. Participatory approach, through a complaints and feedback mechanism, will be undertaken to identify needs, implement and evaluate programs to ensure the views and protection of all stakeholders are considered.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

- Teachers or education personnel and students participate in physical punishment or bullying of students. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Code of conduct in learning facilities; training and prevention in schools; professional development training on dealing with bullying, training toward teachers and monthly follow up, PSS activities on daily basis. - Aid workers use their power of influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they serve. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Application of Child Safeguarding and PSEA measures, transparency in recruitment process; training of all staff and associates who engage with children on child protection, identification of children victims of violence, first line support and safe referral pathways, safeguarding policies and procedures; signature of code of conduct also posted on walls; establishment of child-friendly complaint mechanism; awareness sessions on children's rights and GBV; sufficient number of adult women working in the learning environment to protect girls attending activities.- Members of the community or local authorities try to over-influence the type of support provided to education services or beneficiaries. Likelihood: low. Impact: medium. Communication with local education and other relevant authorities and community members about the project. Satisfaction survey will be implemented. - Danger for children travelling to and from skill training/learning facilities. Likelihood: low. Impact: high. Training on RE, collaboration with relevant authorities to identify safe locations and ensure their support in the safety of the project implementation.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Inflation rate increases prices. Likelihood: High. Impact: medium. Mitigation: TGH included inflation rate in the budget; in case of continuous inflation TGH will adjust the BoQs accordingly.- Overcrowded schools cannot accept new coming students who benefited from SLPs and have a negative impact on attendance and dropout rates. Likelihood: medium. Impact: medium. Mitigating: in last resort, TGH will seek approval from MOE to implement additional sessions in two shifts on the week end. - Parents/caregivers discouraged from sending their children to NFE in school or attending awareness sessions. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: BTL campaigns through door-to-door will be organized. A dedicated TGH team will visit the families and provide them with the necessary information about the importance of sending their children to school, and supporting them at home.- Unavailability of qualified manpower resources in the targeted locations. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: TGH will introduce campaigns and new methods of recruitment to reach quality staff from the same project locations or close area. For Rural Damascus, field persons as PSS facilitators will be hired from Damascus.- Learning facilities could be attacked. Likelihood: low Impact: high. Mitigation: strengthen learning facilities; safety protocols in place; provide preparedness training.- COVID-19 lockdown prevents conducting activities. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. TGH has already set up alternative learning modalities through remote learning (online lessons) through WhatsApp and home-based classes with limited number of students.
