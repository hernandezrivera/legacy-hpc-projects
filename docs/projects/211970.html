Project Summary

Context analysis: The provision of education services at Tel-Samen Camp in Ar-Raqqa City has faced substantial disruptions due to the severe impact of the Syrian conflict. The intense fighting and widespread destruction resulting from the military campaign against ISIS in 2017 forced the displacement of around 300,000 civilians and caused extensive damage to 80% of Ar-Raqqa. posing additional challenges.Tel Samen Camp was established to provide shelter and support to a significant population of internally displaced persons (IDPs). The essential educational requirements in Ar-Raqqa encompass various aspects. These include the restoration and enhancement of schools and learning environments, the implementation of mental health and psychosocial support services for both students and education personnel.
In Tel Samen Camp, the REACH report of August 2022 highlights critical needs in early childhood development services. Shockingly, only 3% of children between the ages of 3 and 5 have access to early child education, indicating a severe lack of provision in this crucial area. Furthermore, there are distressing reports of child labor within the camp. Both girls and boys are engaged in domestic labor and work for others.
The Camp Civil Administration offers formal education for children aged 6-17, but the coverage remains limited. Ultimately, the goal is to ensure access to quality education for all children in the camp, regardless of their age, gender, or background. The program interventions aim to facilitate the preparation of children for formal schooling and enhance their transition into the formal education system. Acknowledging the existing barriers that hinder a smooth transition into formal schooling .
Provision of Non-formal Education Pathways: This component addresses the need for educational programs that cater to (OOSC) or those at risk of dropping out, enabling them to stay engaged in learning and acquire essential foundational and life skills required for their continued education or transition to the workforce. our efforts will be made to identify new (temporary) locations and repair existing spaces as necessary. Consideration will be given to accessibility issues and compliance with the Inter-Agency Network for Education in Emergencies minimum standards for safe learning spaces during light repairs on educational facilities. Al Raed will adapt its Safe School Common Approach to promote the safety of children in and around non-formal learning sites, protecting them from violence, and everyday conflicts. The Safe School approach adopts an all-inclusive, all-hazards strategy to uphold children's rights to safety, encompassing action at four different levels: A) Policy and Systems , B) school safety management , C) safe school facilities , D) teachers and children . Reaching out-of-school children: Three interventions will target groups of particularly underserved children: namely school-aged children out of school, those of pre-primary age and children with disabilities.use PSEA Network-developed field observation checklist
Activities: 1. Accelerated Non-formal Education: Adolescents form a significant portion of the out-of-school population and lack access to structured learning opportunities. Non-formal education plays a critical role in providing a pathway for these adolescents to reengage in learning and acquire essential skills for their future. To address this gap, the program will align with the MYRP and offer integrated learning spaces that provide adolescents with foundational, transferrable, and vocational skills.
.2. Early Childhood Education (Ages 3-5): To promote access to (ECD) opportunities, the program will expand non-formal(ECD) programs using a community and inter-sectoral approach. These programs will cater to boys and girls who are out of school or lagging behind in their learning. The primary aim is to ensure the acquisition of foundational skills in literacy and numeracy, as well as (SEL) skills.
