Project Summary

According to the IRNA, TOCH inclusive in Tonj North an estimated 15,700 HHs or 94,200 (54%), 90,283 (80%) people have been affected by the flood August 2022. This is equivalent to 54% of the total 165,000 (population census 2008). While security situation remains fragile, torrential rain and flood has severely affected many payams in Tonj North this year. Many families have been displaced from their villages to higher ground grounds. The flood submerged crops, affected public facilities, and forced many cattle to leave. Partners described the current situation as crises.
In Tonj North incident of mysterious fire outbreak in Alabek and Akop Payams that burned total of 69 houses and displaced 648 IDPs, of these majority are children and women whose fathers/husbands were killed thus remaining in a devastated situation as the struggle to survive. 67 houses affected comprised of 402 individuals out of which majority are women and children who are highly at risk of diseases including malnutrition resulting from food shortage since all their belongings were destroyed by the fire. All children 6-59 months and PLWs are at greater risk of malnutrition due to poor hygiene, diseases and complete lack of food and 103 children under five (boys 65 and girls 38) are among people affected by crisis.
In June 2022, armed confrontation between armed youth and SSPDF in Rualbet triggered massive displacement of people from Rualbet to other payams in Tonj North and other counties. This has disrupted livelihood and deteriorated the vulnerability of hundreds of people in Tonj North. This increase is largely driven by triple of recurring of conflict, widespread flooding, deepening food insecurity situation, inflation and high food prices, and lack of access to basic services. In addition, Partners described the current situation as crises compare to 8.9 million in needs in 2022.
The deeply rooted beliefs and attitudes about how women conduct themselves or with other gender still negative consequences where girls are prevent or not allow to school, cases of child and forced marriage, sexual exploitation and abuse and child pregnancies continue to increase despite efforts partners and government are putting with limited resources. Other forms of GBV such as physical violence, denial of resources and emotional abuse are also widespread in this context. This highly patriarchal society has become even more unsafe for women and girls due to lose of livelihoods caused by flooding, continue conflict and enrollment of children to school still lower particular in most areas affected by flooding and continue in mentioned locations of Warrap State.
Conflict related sexual violence and gang rape, intimate partner violence and sexual assault, are also common in this areas and are strongly connected with insecurity and instability, Cultural norms for example competition over one girl to marry and practices of composing songs to provoke other communities and some individuals.
According to IRNA and education cluster august 2022, As a result of torrential rain and flash flood, education services in Gogrial East, Tonj North and Tonj East have been badly affected, total of 26 primary schools have been submerged and closed more than 6000 girls and boys including child friendly spaces. Many girls have been forced into early marriage by either their parents or situation during that period.
There are gaps in understanding the nature, scope and magnitude of CP, GBV and provision GBV prevention and response services. 1 - Needs

The cumulative effects of inter communal violence and flooding in South Sudan continue to destroy livelihoods leaving more than 8.9 million people in need including 8.54 million South Sudanese women, men, girls and boys and 328,000 refugees and asylum seekers. Two million people are internally displaced; of these 1.4 million require urgent support, and 1.8 million are returnees, of these 1.2 million have urgent needs. In addition, there are 6 million vulnerable host community/ non-displaced people, of whom some are older persons or infirm, or persons with disabilities in 2022, compared to 8.3 million in 2021. (HNO 2022) This increase is largely driven by compounded shocks triggered by continued conflict, widespread flooding, deepening food insecurity situation, inflation and high food prices, and lack of access to basic services. In addition, an estimated 2 million people, including 1.3 million children under the age of 5, and 676,000 pregnant and lactating women, are expected to be acutely malnourished in 2022.The deeply rooted beliefs and attitudes about how women should behave have had negative consequences where girls are not taken to school, cases of child and forced marriage, sexual exploitation and abuse and child pregnancies have increased. Other forms of GBV such as physical violence, denial of resources and emotional abuse are also prevalent in this context. This highly patriarchal society has become even more unsafe for women and girls due to lose of livelihoods caused by the pandemic and child did not manage to return to school during the schools reopening in May 2021 as they became family breadwinners during lockdown. Conflict related sexual violence and gang rape, intimate partner violence and sexual assault, are also widespread in this areas and are strongly connected with insecurity and instability and displacement of communities and separation from families. COVID-19 pandemic have had negative impacts on women and girls both in Warrap state. According to Education cluster, in May 2021 there were up to more than 600 girls pregnancies' for those pupils and students who were enrolled in 2020 before COVID outbreak and subsequent lack down sand closure of schools. Many girls have been forced into early marriage by either their parents or situation during the outbreak and post COVID era. There were no specific response designed to respond to such problem. There are gaps in understanding the nature, scope and magnitude of CP,GBV and provision GBV prevention and response services. Women and girls at the reproductive age at risk of gender-based violence due to the fighting and the subsequent displacement. More so there is need to work at community level to support communities identify ways in which GBV prevention can be embraced and communities are safer for women and girls while also providing safe referrals to services. There is limited reporting of cases due to the stigma that survivors have to face, limited services or lack of survivor centered services and lack of awareness in the community. The main threats faced by women and girls in the community include;• Risks of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA)• Different types of sexual violence including intimate partner violence• Psychosocial distress form the violence experienced including emotional abuse, threats of violence and denial of opportunities• GBV including child marriage, forced marriage, teenage pregnancy and all other forms gender related violence.• Missing and unaccompanied children• Stress and trauma of children during inter communal conflict and women and girls whose loved ones were lost

2 - Project activities

1. Establish 9 community based child friendly spaces in 3 counties to provide psychosocial support to children affected by inter communal conflicts and flooding.2. Conduct community based awareness creation on Child Protection, GBV response services, risks and prevention through mapping out on available GBV services to review, update and strengthened referral pathways and provide training on its GBV survivors.3. Provision of dignity kits for 3,000 households 4. Construction of Women and Girls Friendly Space programming (WGFS).Construct 6 Women and girls friendly spaces 2 in Tonj North,2 in Tonj East and Gogrial East counties to reach 10,000 women and girls with PSS and life skills sessions and raise awareness on GBV, information on available services for GBV survivors and the functional referral systems in Tonj North, East and Gogrial East counties5. Continuous provision of psycho social Support Services in all the targeted Counties of Tonj North, Tonj East and Gogrial East Counties targeting 6,000 beneficiaries both women and girls. 6. Protection and GBV awareness raising on prevention messaging targeting GBV survivors and the community as a whole of Tonj North, East and Gogrial East. Awareness sessions and community outreach on Gender-Based Violence for children, adolescents and women will be carried out as continued support to affected persons to ensure necessary support is provided. Topic in the sessions and outreach shall include child marriage, domestic violence, polygamy and marital rape among others will be included. A total of 900 beneficiaries (women, girls, boys and men will be reached).7. Support comprehensive case management in form of individual/Group psycho social support and referrals for at least 200 beneficiaries. 8. Establishment of Women and Girls Friendly Spaces and supplies in 3 locations to support 9 groups of 30 each individuals group. 9. Provide training and mentoring of 50 staffs and local service providers to strengthen and utilize the existing GBV referral pathways in communities for coordinated services to target population. 10. Legal awareness, assistance and counselling to 500 beneficiaries 11. Identification, Train and support women business: It is essential to give GBV survivors livelihood opportunities that decrease their vulnerability to violence, builds their confidence, teaches them skills on business management, marketing, financial literacy skills and provides the small capital grant of $30 for 30 women to start up business that lift their families up from "living on the edge" of hunger, disease, lack of education, and the unending cycle of extreme poverty. 10 women from each county of the project location.12. Training of school girls on Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM).Menstrual hygiene management (MHM) refers to what females can do to manage their monthly periods in a safe, private, and healthy manner. Women need adequate water and safe spaces for washing with dignity and in privacy, clean material to absorb menstrual blood, facilities to properly dispose soiled materials and the use of soap and water for washing the body as required. TOCH shall conduct identification of six school 2 each county to promote good practice and champion campaign of adolescent safety, access to services including reproductive health and basic medical services.13.Capacity building of front-line service providers on specialized GBV topics such as GBV case management.80 service providers trained on updated referral pathway (GBV SOP) including the GBV guiding principles, PSS, Case management to ethically handle survivors of GBV referred for additional assistance according to the standard 14. provision of integrating Cash and Voucher Assistance as well as livelihood support into GBV programming to 30,000 to improved access to GBV prevention and risk mitigation interventions 15. awareness through radio talks, Production of IEC, early warning information GBV, dialogues, assessment.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

TOCH will use verbal communication to those who are not literate to communicate key concerns and how to support them during project implementation. Rights of children, women and girls will be considered separate and given attention throughout the implementation.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

TOCH Gender officer who is a focal point on feedback complain mechanism who works with project staffs through physical meeting, reporting line and during implementation, community feedback sessions and provide road of reporting in the community. in addition, volunteers, constructed spaces and key protection staff support this. Both child help desk and women and girls are set up in the W&GFs/CFS to provide platform for beneficiaries to share their complains with TOCH staffs. There are also numbers and email address to use beside suggestion box to provide information sharing to the beneficiaries who would like to contact TOCH for complain. Leaflets with contact numbers are also posted in the communities for easy use by the beneficiaries. Awareness raising on how to use, location of these facilities conduct timely by TOCH staffs at the centres and in the community where TOCH operate and project locations.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

TOCH will conduct training to the new staffs of this project to ensure that they know about information on PSEA.TOCH will also conduct safety audit in the project locations and this report will inform the management on the risks which they need to be aware before and throughout project implementation.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The feedback mechanism shall be presented during project inception workshop with communities and local authorities so that proper work plan is adapted for the implementation.
