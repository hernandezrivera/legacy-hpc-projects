Project Summary

The project will provide nutrition services for 8,400 PLWs and 18000 children under five years (1800 PWDs). Through this project, MWL is going to:
*Scale-up and continue the integrated mother and child-centered nutrition life-saving interventions
Scale-up and continue supporting the optimal infant feeding and caring practices in an emergency
Fill the current service gaps in high needs through providing emergency integrated FSL (by providing free bread and voucher of vegetables for the pregnant and lactating women, nutrition, and child protection rapid services through15 of Rapid Response teams (RRT) by referral services of women and girls suffer from violence. Also, the two protection centers will specify one room for RRTs to provide nutrition services.
Continue and strengthen the existing program for managing acute malnutrition among children under five and PLWs.
The scope of interventions will include:
1st: Scaling up integrated nutrition response support through the Rapid Response teams which will be dispatched to the areas of high IDP flow ( Afrin, AlBab sub-districts in Aleppo governorate, (Dana and Salqin sub-districts in Idleb governorate), to provide the following activities:
- 700 PLWs and 1200 Children will be referred and follow up.
- Ensure early identification through scaling up the family MUAC approach (FMA) , training mothers on MUAC measure to identify if child u5 suffer from SAM or MAM and reported.
- Provision of IYCF-E with recommended feeding practices awareness session, Hand Washing awareness session, in addition to one-to-one IYCF-E counselling, nutrition communication, and social mobilization messages.
- Distribute micro-nutrients/nutrition supplements to 350 for children under 5 for preventing anemia and malnutrition and for pregnant and lactating women in in (Afrin, AL Bab sub-districts in Aleppo governorate, (Dana and Salqin sub-districts in Idleb governorate).
- Secure referrals of malnourished cases, Children U5 with dangerous signs and medical problems, PLWs with Medical problems, disabled beneficiaries, Identified visual signs of abuse/violence against childU5/PLWs to the respective treatment centers.
- Identify visual signs of abuse/violence against childU5/PLWs during Nutrition screening.
2nd: : Continue functioning of the OTP-SFP services through protection centers in Afrin and Salqin sub-districts Through a room designated for the RRTS in Salqin and Afrin, where OTP-SFP services for cases suffering from severe acute malnutrition (SAM) or moderate acute malnutrition (MAM). In addition to MUAC measurement, odema measurements and appetite test. etc. These rooms will be fully equipped to provide the service in good manner.
3rd: Capacity building of 50 frontline nutrition services providers on IYCF-E and CMAM programs in the context of COVID-19.
As well as, MWL will train the caregivers on MUAC and edema screening, referral, recognize signs of deterioration.
- The rapid response team will provide MUAC screening for children (Boys and Girls) under the age of 5 and for pregnant and lactating women in (Afrin, AL Bab sub-districts in Aleppo governorate, (Dana and Salqin sub-districts in Idleb governorate). The RRT will apply CMAM protocols for managing the respective groups of the population. It is also expecting that a pregnant woman who is malnourished will received the same services.
If SAM condition is revealed then treatment will be assigned, however, if SAM child has medical complications, he/she to be referred to hospital treatment immediately.
- If MAM cases is revealed then treatment will be assigned,
MWL MEAL staff will follow-up regularly on project activities .MEAL staff will set an M&E plan as per to project log-frame. Regarding the reporting, the M&E plan would develop outlines data collection methods (interviews, Focus Groups Discussion and observation, data sources, timeframe, responsibilities, and dissemination procedure). MWL will send regular reporting to the nutrition cluster.

4 - Needs

In 2022 according to HNO , 14.6 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 1.2 million from 2021. Syria remains one of the largest humanitarian responses in the world, with assistance delivered to 6.8 million people per month in 2021. The substantial harvest losses combined with a chronic economic deprivation have contributed to the mounting food insecurity as 12.8 million people, or almost 60 per cent of the population, are food insecure– a 57 per cent increase since 2019 and the highest number ever recorded in the history of Syria. In line with the rise in food insecurity, malnutrition levels have increased. Compared to 2020, stunting increased by four per cent, severe acute malnutrition (SAM) increased by 29 per cent and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) increased by 45 per cent. The health impacts related to this are well studied and should be seen as direct contributors to the fact that Increases in waterborne diseases are a reality in 2021, and Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates have more than doubled since last year. In conjunction with the rising food insecurity in Syria, the prevalence of malnutrition is a major public health concern as levels increase across the groups it disproportionally affects (infants, children under 5 and PLW). The number of children under the age of five in need of life-saving nutrition assistance stands close to four million (3.8m) and PLW far surpassing one million (1.7m).

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

PSEA Risk: Violate PSEA or CSG, CoC principles. mitigating measures: - Signing PSEA and child safeguarding MWL’s policy by all staff. - getting training on PSEA for all staff. - PSEA awareness through brochures, videos. - Assigning complaint mechanism for SEA issues. - policies of Code of conduct, PSEA, child safeguarding is mandatory for all staff and vendors working with MWL. Accountability Officer in the field will ensure all beneficiaries are aware of MWL complaints and feedback mechanism, which will be in place. The mechanism will include fixed and mobile complaint boxes, WhatsApp number and hotline that will be handled by Accountability Officer. The officer will compile all complaints/suggestions or feedback and share them with the related Project Manager to be addressed. Urgent complaints will be shared with PM in 24 hours. CSG and PSEA complaints will have a different channel as there are two dedicated emails through which the CSG and PSEA Focal Points in Turkey Office can be reached directly. In addition, the Accountability Officer will introduce the project activities to local communities so that they will be aware of Project implementation time, locations, types of Project services and selection criteria.
