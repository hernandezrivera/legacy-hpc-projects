Project Summary

The explosion that hit Beirut happened during very tough times that Lebanese are economically living. It left people homeless, internally displaced and there are also those who remained in their destroyed houses where electricity and water infrastructures were also ruined. In addition to the trauma that everyone has to deal with, costs of rehabilitation are huge and most probably not affordable by people living in somehow deprived neighborhoods. Knowing that the economic crisis led to inflated prices in the market, food items currently cost a fortune. In addition, cooking utensils, plates and cutlery are no longer available in most of these houses, whereas those who still have them, are in a bad shelter condition where cooking or setting up a fire is truly dangerous.
As such, and since IOCC was the first NGO to initiate the community kitchen model and the delivery of hot meals to vulnerable Syrian and Lebanese households since 2013, developed this modality when it co-chaired the FS sector since February 2016 until 2019 and has been part of drafting the hot meals modality with the Global Food Security Cluster; IOCC is proposing to activate a community kitchen in Beirut in order to be able to cater nutritious hot meals to the families. The modality will consist of joining forces with a local NGO in Beirut that has the premises of an equipped kitchen where women will be recruited by the local NGO for the cooking activity, enabling them to access income-generating activities. IOCC team would set a weekly diversified menu and deliver the meals to the identified families. The activity of locally purchasing the needed ingredients also contributed to an improved economic cycle within the community.
The distribution will happen on a door-to-door basis rather than having the beneficiaries queue in order to obtain the meals. This modality will ensure respect to the families’ privacy and will preserve their dignity.
Meals are to be placed in re-usable, heat resistant food containers that will be kept at the families’ premises and collected at the second distribution date in order to have it cleaned and washed again at the kitchen so that food safety is sustained and hazards are eliminated.
Every family meal also includes a bread parcel and is tailored for a family size of 4 people. However, meals can be adjusted when families are bigger or smaller, trying to include all those who need this assistance in the program.
The total target for the hot meals food assistance is equal to 2500 Households
Knowing that all food-service chains like restaurants, snacks and pubs were severely damaged due to the heavy explosion that hit Beirut, IOCC acknowledges that livelihoods within this specific sector were conjointly affected. The economic crisis is not making it any easier whether to employees or to business owners to cope with this sudden situation that has been worsening since a while. IOCC is suggesting to support the most affected through the reconstruction and the rehabilitation of the infrastructure as well as by providing and installing water filters systems within their kitchens for improved food safety.
IOCC aims at helping 6 food businesses recovering their status pre-explosion.

1 - Needs

The overall goal of the rapid needs assessment conducted by IOCC is to determine the pressing needs of the families affected by the explosion in the target areas. The collection and analysis of the quantitative and qualitative assessment data will ensure that IOCC’s emergency response will be adequately designed and implemented in accordance with the determined needs. The assessment also helped to guarantee community engagement and acceptance of the planned intervention.The overall observations from the rapid assessment show that families are living in modest households with minimal belongings and are dependent on assistance. In most cases, their homes were old and in poor condition even prior to the explosion. The families were unable to afford the cost of repairing their homes, and were limited to cleaning up debris, which they could do themselves.On the days of the assessment, the families were welcoming despite the stress they have experienced. Some families’ pride was holding them back from requesting help. They were asking for less than needed and sometimes asking to shift any support to more vulnerable neighbors. All of the assessed households sustained certain level of damage. The severity of the damages varied between neighborhoods. The assessment results reflected that food remained among the ultimate needs to households that were affected by the Beirut Port Blast and that were originally vulnerable to the situation in Lebanon. Additionally, families reported receiving some kind of food assistance but claimed that it was in an inconsistent manner. Most of the families also reported that they have lost their means of cooking, if not the cooking pots and the kitchen utensils, but also the appliances that they could use whether to cook or to heat food. It is also worth mentioning that the majority of the families discussed the inflation rates of the food in the market and how they had to cope on shifting to a less diversified menu within their lifestyle as they could no longer afford to pay for fresh ingredients. Since distribution of cooked food is largely used in humanitarian contexts to respond to immediate food security needs, targeting the most vulnerable such as the dispossessed, landless/homeless people, migrants in transit camps, people in quarantine facilities, internally displaced population, and more generally those that have no means to store, transform or utilize dry foods. In some cases cooked meals can be also used to help preventing and reduce the prevalence of malnutrition for certain groups (e.g. children, the elderly). Therefore, nutritious food came as one of the priorities of the response in this case where people have lost their cooking and eating utensils, their kitchen appliances, and the purchasing power of raw ingredients. On the other hand, and due to the economic crisis which led to inflation and increased unemployment rates, individuals within the food sector were heavily affected whether by losing their jobs or by losing their business. Restaurant, snacks, mini markets, and pubs in the affected areas provided income-generating opportunities to a considerate number of youth. 2 - Activities

Providing Direct Food Assistance:IOCC will conduct a food security assessment in order to identify the families to be targeted through the response. IOCC, in collaboration with Saint Lazarus Association will also activate a community kitchen in Ashrafieh that will cater nutritious and diversified hot meals to identified households three times per week. This assistance will be detailed as follow:- IOCC will assess available equipment for cooking and storing food in the community kitchen and will purchase needed material/equipment (big pots, refrigerator, freezers, cooking and pouring spoons, chopping boards, re-usable food containers, etc...)- IOCC will cover the incentives for the cooking team and drivers in the kitchen whom shall be recruited by the local NGO- IOCC will set a weekly diversified menu to ensure a proper dietary diversity- The Local NGO (Saint Lazarus Association) will be responsible of purchasing the ingredients freshly for each meal.- IOCC will monitor the cooking procedure and ensures food safety standards are respected throughout the entire process (starting by preparing the ingredients until the delivery of the hot meal to the families)- Meals will be placed in re-usable and heat resistant food containers that will be collected every second day to be washed again at the kitchen premises- IOCC officers will deliver meals to selected beneficiaries in a door-to-door modality- Every meal will be accompanied by a bread parcel.- Meals and Portions are adjustable according to the family size.- Every family will receive 3 diversified and cooked meals per week - This activity will cover 2500 families Providing Infrastructure Assistance to Damaged Food Businesses:- IOCC will assess and identify 6 food businesses in need for rehabilitation of infrastructure - IOCC will develop Bill of Quantities for each place separately - IOCC will draft a list of equipment that were damaged and need to be replaced- IOCC will solicit a request for quotation and would choose the best contractor offering the best price to proceed with the rehabilitation of the infrastructure, of the kitchen and of the water chambers of each place- IOCC will also provide kitchen equipment and water filters- IOCC will provide small grants to business owners to re-launch and recover their food-industry business.
