1 - Needs

Over 2.1 million Palestinians across the oPt, 60 per cent in the Gaza Strip and 40 per cent in the West Bank, experience or are at risk of conflict, violence and forcible transfer, and will be in need of protection assistance in 2021. 2020 has seen a profound deterioration in the severity of protection risks in the oPt. This deterioration has been driven by the outbreak of COVID-19 and related movement restrictions, school closures, job losses and socio-economic distress. The administrative vacuum, as a result of the PA halt in coordination with Israel, has introduced additional challenges. In the Gaza Strip, the longstanding Israeli blockade and related limitations in economic opportunities, recurring escalations, and the intra Palestinian divide are the main drivers of a protracted protection crisis. The already limited freedom of movement afforded by Palestinians in Gaza has been further reduced by the pandemic: only a minimal number of medical emergency cases have been permitted exit via Israel, the crossing with Egypt was shut down (with a few exceptional openings), and internal movement and business operations within Gaza curtailed by a lockdown imposed since late August. The overall deterioration in the living conditions has had a disproportionate impact on children, youth, women and persons with disability (PwD). It has increased the risk of poor mental health, stress anxiety and depression, lower educational outcomes, child labour and domestic violence. Vulnerability remains high also among those seriously wounded or traumatized during the ‘Great March of Return’ demonstrations in 2018-2019, IDPs from the 2014 conflict, refugees, and poor households, in particular those at risk of eviction due to their inability to pay rents. In the West Bank, the coercive environment facing many Palestinians in Area C, East Jerusalem and the Israeli-controlled part of Hebron city, H2, continue to generate a risk of forcible transfer. Related policies and practices include discriminatory planning, demolitions and confiscations, forced evictions, access restrictions and lack of law enforcement on violent settlers. A range of settlement expansion projects, currently underway, threaten to further encroach into Palestinian land, increase fragmentation, and heighten the risk of displacement. In 2020, there has been an increased application of military orders permitting the expedited demolition or summary seizure of structures in Area C, significantly impeding access to legal recourse, while the introduction of greater punitive measures against unauthorized construction in East Jerusalem led to a spike in self-demolitions. Access to farmland in the ‘Seam Zone’ behind the Barrier continued to deteriorate, following amendments to the permit system. Throughout the West Bank, Palestinians have been exposed to excessive use of force by Israeli forces, including in the context of search and arrest operations, and during protests and ad hoc clashes. Meanwhile, the changes in regulations and the pressures on civil society organizations by both Israeli and Palestinian authorities pose a threat to the ability to deliver protection services to those in need. A broad range of assistance to respond to rising needs is required, including: child protection responses; prevention of gender-based violence (GBV) and support for its survivors; mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) interventions; and legal aid to protect housing, land and property rights, and secure access and movement. The monitoring and documentation of violations of international law, as well as advocacy to prevent further violations and end the cycle of impunity, as well as mine action, continue to be central. PwD are one of the most disadvantaged, vulnerable and marginalized groups in Palestinian society in need of protection services: in addition to the aforementioned pressures, they face institutional and attitudinal barriers, which impede their access to appropriate multisectoral responses. PwD experience lower educational outcomes; poor health; high rates of unemployment; high rates of poverty, and greater risks of violence, stigma and discrimination. Females with disabilities are significantly more vulnerable to these risks than males. Children exposed to conflict-related violence have remained a most vulnerable group requiring various protection responses. Between July 2019 and June 2020, Child Protection partners have documented 516 violations, affecting nearly 13,000 children Against this background, the Protection Cluster is working towards increased respect for IHL and HRL and accountability for violations; strengthening adherence to protection principles and standards across clusters in the humanitarian response; and ensuring that protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict-related violence, as well as shocks and emergencies such as the COVID-19 pandemic or escalations in hostilities. OHCHR, as PC lead, has overall responsibility for ensuring coordinated protection responses. The Protection Cluster Working Group (PCWG) meets in Ramallah and Gaza City. The PC has the following sub-groups: Child Protection and Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) Area of Responsibility, Legal Task Force, and GBV Sub Cluster. OHCHR is responsible for ensuring that all protection responses are coordinated taking into account gender and diversity, and the different protection needs of various groups within the population. Effective coordination between the sub-groups of the cluster is essential and is achieved through the Strategic Advisory Group, chaired by the Cluster Coordinator which also focuses on achieving a one Palestine or national approach to the protection work. Gender considerations are integral to the protection response. Notably, the need for strengthening both gender analysis and gender-sensitive responses across the Cluster's sub-sectors.

2 - Activities

Given the large membership of the cluster and the broad scope of the issues coordinated under the cluster, sufficient human resources are essential to ensure leadership and coordination, as detailed in the project budget. In line with below-outlined activities, and working with the chairs of the Protection Cluster sub-groups and with the Cluster members, the coordination team will: 1. Provide vision and leadership for the Protection Cluster, based on consultations with members and stakeholders; 2. Undertake day-to-day coordination of Protection responses, including convening meetings, information sharing and ensuring Cluster inputs to humanitarian processes; 3. Co-Chair the Legal Task Force in the West Bank, 4. Ensure coordination with the work of the PC sub-groups and AoRs. Related activities include ensuring coordination between working groups to ensure complementarity 5. Identify and propose action to address gaps, including in information gathering, management, analysis and coordinated response to priority protection concerns, with a focus on increased analysis on gender-specific vulnerabilities and gender-specific impact of ongoing violations of IHL and IHRL, and persons with disability; 6. Strengthen detection and referral for protection cases; 7. Strengthen Protection Information Management under the protection cluster and feed into inter-sectoral response monitoring including 4Ws, demolitions and settler violence response trackers; 8. Coordinate joint protection monitoring and information analysis; 9. Coordinate joint human-rights based advocacy for affected populations under relevant priorities of the HCT Advocacy Strategy, including through liaising with UN human rights procedures and mechanisms, and link Protection Cluster advocacy to the work of the HCT Advocacy Working Group; 9. Through the Legal Advice Group, coordinate the discussion of questions of international human rights and humanitarian law, and the provision of international law analysis to the HCT; 10. Through the Legal Task Force, which OHCHR will co-chair with NRC, ensure that up-to-date information, legal advice, and analysis on emblematic cases, trends and protection risks is available to the ICCG and the HCT; 10. Work with the other clusters and sectors to mainstream protection, in order to ensure that Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) commitments are fulfilled and that protection is made central to the humanitarian response in all stages of the HPC. Activities in this regard include trainings and technical support to the provision of guidelines, checklists, and coordination of operational responses to protection issues; 11. Build capacity of cluster members to strengthen communication with and accountability to affected populations (AAP), including through beneficiary feedback on protection responses, and specifically, by establishing a tracking tool to monitor progress on AAP throughout the Humanitarian Program Cycle for all funded HRP projects under Protection; 12. Needs assessments: work to identify baseline data and needs of persons with disabilities in the context of humanitarian action; support the efforts of working groups to address assessment gaps as identified in the HNO; 13. Under the lead of the Protection Cluster gender focal point, provide support to cluster partners in gender mainstreaming all their activities, including by providing capacity-building support to partners to enhance gender sensitivity in the planning, implementation and monitoring of projects submitted to the HRP; 13. Support resource mobilization by cluster partners

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

All Protection Cluster partners are required to follow the relevant AOR's Minimum Standards in Emergencies’ thereby prioritizing safety, dignity and do no harm, providing meaning full access, ensuring accountability mechanism as well as participation and empowerment of disaster-affected communities. The Protection Cluster will continue to provide capacity building to partners on these principles including by organizing training and information sessions o protection mainstreaming.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Protection Mainstreaming will continue to be central to the Protection Cluster Coordination work during 2021, the second key principle of Protection Mainstreaming, “ensuring meaningful access”, has been identified as a focus area for PM capacity building throughout the cluster membership, with as a special focus on people with disabilities and other left-behind groups.
