Project Summary

Secours Islamique France (SIF)’s proposed intervention will give access to vulnerable communities to long term dignified livelihood activities on Rural Damascus, Dara’a, Rural Damascus, Hama, Homs, Al- Hassakeh, Dier- Ez-zor and Aleppo Governorate through (1) the Rehabilitation of Bakeries, (2) the Rehabilitation of a Health Centre and (3) the Rehabilitation of commercial Markets (4) Debri Removal. The targeted sun districts are selected based on their level of accessibility and the severity of needs as per the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) Syria, SIF field assessments and consultations with local authorities and municipalities of each targeted sub-district. All selected sub-districts localities are at level 3 or above on the severity scale. A total of 148,858 beneficiaries are estimated to be reached though these interventions.
The project aims to promote early recovery and strengthen the resilience of affected communities. The implementation phase will create short term employment opportunities in the region and support entrepreneurship and businesses from the local community, which includes the creation of jobs for construction workers and professional laborers. The rehabilitated Health centers, commercial market and bakeries will create long-term employments (e.g. nurses, health care workers, merchants, owners, employees, distributors, local farmers, etc.) benefiting the local community in large and improving the living conditions in the targeted areas.
The rehabilitation of these livelihood infrastructure will also contribute to addressing food security and health issues in these communities by providing enhanced access to basic food items and health facilities. The project intervention will focus on early recovery, stabilization of livelihood and planning for durable and long term solutions primary targeting areas with high number of IDPs and returnees and areas with anticipated significant inward displacement. The project will directly contribute to the Livelihood sector’s priorities in some of the highest severity ranking sub-districts in the targeted governorates.
SIF ensures participatory approach in all project cycle phases including the planning, project design, implementation and the monitoring phase. The MEAL team will closely monitor the intervention and will put in place community based complaints and feedback mechanism.

4 - Needs

According to the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) Syria, there are 11.6 million people in need of early recovery and livelihood support including 5.9 million women, 5.3 million children and 3 million people with disabilities across Syria. The long standing Syrian crises has adversely effected the social and economic situation of the country, with the economic crises in Lebanon adding to the depreciation of the Syrian currency and causing additional inflation. Coupled with the aftermath of the new trade restrictions in June 2020 and the problems caused by the Covid 19 pandemic has further deprived Syrian people from livelihood opportunities and basic needs. Unemployment was estimated around 50% of the working age population by the end of 2020, forcing people to use negative coping mechanisms and creating further stress on the socio-economic situation of the country (HNO 2021). With the end of the armed conflict in the target locations, many of the areas are left with massive destruction. SIF conducted a needs assessment in December 2020 in coordination with SARC, local authorities and municipalities. Many livelihood infrastructures in the targeted communities were reported to have been partly or completely destroyed, impacting the access of affected local communities to basic food items, who are then often forced to travel several kilometres to access basic necessities. Destruction of infrastructures like health centres, bakeries and butchers have not only deprived people of livelihoods but also disrupted the supply of basic necessities like access to food and health.

5 - Implementation of activities

The livelihood infrastructure rehabilitation will include the provision and repair of mechanical equipment, the installation of doors and windows as well as the rehabilitation of WASH facilities. SIF will rely on a contractor who will be selected based on SIF Standards of Procedures (SoPs) in order to ensure transparency and accountability of the selection process. SIF will coordinate with Ministry of Trade, local authorities, and SARC to conduct the assessments, select beneﬁciaries, obtain the necessary approvals, and issue oﬃcial acceptance certiﬁcates for hand over. A period of guarantee will also be provided to each owner and public facility in case some repairs would be needed after works are carried out. The project will be complementary to Shelter, Education, Protection and WASH interventions in the targeted areas which will aim at providing the communities with functional sewage network, rehabilitation of water systems, rehabilitation of houses, remedial classes and protection activities in order to foster communities’ early recovery and resilience and long term sustainable growth.The activities will be monitored on a daily basis by SIF through ﬁeld visits, data collection and reporting to SIF coordination team, HQ and donors to report progress, highlight areas of concerns and ensure good completion of the works. Through monthly project and budget follow-up, SIF will ensure to abide by standards of project implementation and that resources are being effectively used. Participatory approach, through regular structured focus group discussions will ensure that any protection concerns highlighted are discussed and mitigated. The MEAL team will put in place a mechanism for complaints and feedback.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Not getting approvals from national security to enter the area. Likelihood: Low. Impact: high. Mitigation: SIF will target easy-to-reach areas and establish a constant dialogue and follow-up with the authorities. Risk 2: Risk of increased prices due to heavy inflation. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation: (1) SIF has already accounted for the increased inflation rate in the budget, (2) in case of continuous inflation SIF will adjust the BoQs accordingly. Risk 3: The security situation deteriorates and effects the implementation of the activities. Likelihood: low. Impact: HighMitigation: (1) regular monitoring of the security situation by SIF Logistics and Security Manager (2) ensure updated contingency plans are in place at field and coordination level (3) ensure the preparedness of SIF team through regular briefings and regular updates on any change in the situation Risk 3: Risk of Froud and misuse of resourcesLikelihood: MediumImpact: HighMitigation: (1) Ensure that complain and feedback mechanism are at place to report any fraud. (2) Close monitoring by MEAL department (3) Random spot checks (4) Ensure procurement procedures are transparent and in line with SIF and Donor policies (5), Insure that staff is hired in a fairly adhering to SIF procedures. (6) Ensure SIF’s Anti-Fraud policy is strictly followed to detect, prevent and address cases of fraud.
