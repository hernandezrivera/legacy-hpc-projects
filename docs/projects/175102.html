Project Summary

The project will support vulnerable households population composed of returnees, IPDs, and host communities who are at the risk of survival due to the high hunger, malnutrition rates, high dependency, loss of food assets accelerated by the recent floods, high economic hardships, continued displacement and the prolonged effects of inter-communal conflicts and crisis. The project targets 5,333 beneficiaries HHs, in Leer and Panyijiar counties in Unity, Akobo, Bor South and Duk counties in Jonglei and Manyo county in Upper Nile state. Through this project, Relief Agency will provide emergency livelihoods support to various vulnerable groups to meet their needs and strengthening their capacities to overcome the long-term food insecurity caused by protracted crisis. This emergency livelihood assistance will increase access to food and market linkages that enhance household farmers’ in restoring, strengthening and sustaining food production. The achievement would be attained by providing farming inputs such as vegetable, crops and fishing kits including other agricultural tools to increase food production and improve community livelihoods situation. This lifesaving intervention will constitute provision of fast maturing vegetable kits to the population that are mostly affected by the floods, covid-19, and hard economic situation to improve nutritional and dietary needs. To achieve the overall objectives of the project, it is linked to other clusters to mainstream sectoral approaches to achieve a broader results. This project will provide basic lifesaving support to improve food security on market oriented agricultural activities with the farmers to enhance food production and meet the most immediate food and nutritional needs to avert the high rates of malnutrition burden and hunger incidences that may worsen the situation in many counties of greater upper Nile region (Jonglei, Unity & Upper Nile States). The project is intended to contribute to achievement of strategic objectives under CO2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19 (compulsory use of new SOP & guidelines);
In order to achieve the above objectives, the project shall undertake the following activities;
1.1.0. Carrying out project launch and rapid need assessment in the project areas to identify the existing community requirements and available services, with participation of 25 participants per county through FGD, KII and direct observations to identify target vulnerable household population and agreeing on selection criteria.1.1.1. Conduct community mobilization and sensitization of 5,333HHs in targeted counties sharing project key messages on PSEA, complaint and feedback mechanism, introduction of project scope to the relevant stakeholders, key stakeholder roles and responsibility, beneficiary selection criteria. 1.1.2. Conducting beneficiaries’ identification, registration, verification and distribution of quality livelihood kits to 5,333 HHs in the targeted counties. 1.1.3 Conducting a farmer lead groups training compose of 100 as (TOT) in Leer, Panyijiar, Akobo, Bor South, Duk and Manyo counties on identification and early warning system of fall army worm surveillance and reporting. 1.1.4 Training 150 Agricultural extension workers, 25 each per county on pest and crop disease control e.g. detection, prevention & eradication. 1.1.5 Providing 5,333 vulnerable household farmers with emergency livelihoods support such as fishing kits and fast maturing vegetable seeds (65% women and 35% men).
The project will require an estimated financial support of 266,667 USD for project successful completion in a period of twelve (12) months. This project will assign to support implementation of this project that shall be 80% recruited locally.

1 - Needs

Jonglei, Unity & Upper Nile states are the most affected states during the 5 years of conflict with most of the population displaced to Protection of civilian sites in Bentiu, Malakal and Bor. However, some have fled to remote hard to reach areas specially swarms, neighboring countries such as, Ethiopia, Uganda, Kenya and Sudan. The host communities depend entirely on humanitarian assistance which sometimes could not be regular and also not enough for living. Six counties in three states will be targeted in this project because of the identified dire needs of humanitarian situation. Given the indicators of the dire situation, the key identified factors of the humanitarian needs are related to COVID 19, effect on income activities, flooding that destroyed crops and affected livestock, insecurity, high food prices due to hard currency inflation rates and markets that are not functioning efficiently. Spontaneous returning of returnees from neighboring countries and inside the country from the displacement sites increases the needs of the communities hence increases shortage of food for the population registered by IOM and other Agencies for assistance rations. The recent IRNA assessment in the mid of January 2021 in upper Nile and Jonglei states shows that, over 36,000 individuals are identified with limited access to basic services and many more are displaced due to communal violence.In reference to the recent humanitarian needs overview, 8.3M are in need of humanitarian assistance in the whole country while 776,000 of population in Unity state and Ruweng administrative area are classified as food insecure at crisis and emergency. The highest number of populations in unity state is food insecure as categorized in (phase 3, and phase 4 of IPC). Upper Nile State, it is categorized in either Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity across all the periods, with the lean season projection period being the worst. During the lean season period between April and July 2021, the food security situation will deteriorate with an estimated 1,034,000 people (70.8% of the population) likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 688,000 people will be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, and 346,000 people will likely be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. The continuation of the conflict reported by IRNA between the communities in Upper Nile state reduce chances of access to livelihoods activities and bring economic crisis, dozens of civilians are reported to fled their households with fear of revenge repercussion. In Jonglei state and Pibor administrative area, the projection between April and July 2021, food security situation is expected to deteriorate further throughout the entire State. Humanitarian needs overview indicated an estimated 1.68 million people (84.7% of the population) likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 876,000 people will likely be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, 792,000 people will likely be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, and 11,000 people will likely be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Akobo County. Relief Agency project is therefore designed to contribute and narrow the huge needs and gaps identified during IRNA assessments of Oct to Nov 2020 and other findings by other partners that have responded through rapid response fund (RRF). In line with FSL cluster objectives, HRP 2021 and OCHA scope paper, RA will mitigate the risk of being food insecure and avert the emergency situation deteriorating to catastrophic level. This intervention seeks to contribute to reducing dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen household's abilities to absorb shock and enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable, crop seeds, fishing and livestock support for populations in IPC 3, 4 and 5 counties targeted.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

PSEA messages are part of the information package shared and disseminated to the project beneficiaries during the consultations and information sharing sessions and even throughout the project implementation PSEA messages shall be disseminated and beneficiaries are encouraged to report with immediate effect any form of such acts during the project implementation in such a confidential fashion as possible through the established feedback & complaint mechanisms.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Project team will adhere to inclusivity principle throughout the project implementation so as to hear from all gender groups inclusive of partners with disability, sign language will be paid attention to read and address beneficiaries unsaid concerns expressed in the form of body language as well as strengthening outreach awareness to reach vulnerable groups to ensure feedback and gather their views regarding the project assistance. The team also ensures to bridge the language gaps for illiterate beneficiaries.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Services providing team will carry out regular group discussions and key informant interviews with different key stakeholders (women, girls, boys and men) to find out their views about the services and how would RA improve the strategies on services delivery. Different FGDs with women, girls, boys, men and various stakeholders will be conducted separately to introduce the project scope to the stakeholders. Inclusive mobilization will be carried out to explain the background of the exercise so that people will be free to speak and share their experiences without fear of being victimized. RA team is flexible to give the beneficiaries choice of languages they could speak to provide feedback comfortably. Relief Agency team will adopt feedbacks and lessons leant from beneficiaries to evaluate how best the project is implemented and measure the satisfaction of beneficiaries then incorporate the lessons learnt into our project framework development to achieve adequate and satisfactory assistance.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Relief Agency project team will collect beneficiaries’ feedback and use it in such a way as when facilitating decision on distribution sites in order to avoid harm against the beneficiaries targeted for this support. Feedback shall be used as well to address complaints about the intended support so as to achieve optimal positive outcomes.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

1) Recruit distribution teams to adopt, timing, schedule (especially for people walking more than 2 hours to go first) to avoid risk areas/hotspots for women/girls. Where women/girls have to walk more than 2 hours one way to get items, ensure that there are distribution points provided closer to current locations to avoid risks. Work with local authorities and community leaders to agree on the protection of persons travelling to/from distribution suites. Ensure at least 50% of the livelihood inputs distribution teams are females. Ensure crowd control and separate queuing lines for women and girls from men and boys are recruited. Separate latrine block (temporary) for men & women with internal locks. Signs with pictures and writing in the appropriate language to indicate the male/female structures are posted on each latrine at the livelihood distribution site. Set a timeframe in different days during registration with specific number of beneficiaries to reduce bulkiness of crowd in one place to mitigate the violent. 2) Provide a hotline number or accessible number for sensitive complaints and separate hotline for SEA reports. Take the services closer to the disable groups who have limited access to the site of distribution.3) the project team will established precautionary measures teams to protect and to safely escort the children from the distribution sites to their home to safeguard the security of beneficiaries.4) Provide information for the hard to reach beneficiaries about explosive and hazards (make sure information bears signs and symbols of danger of explosive hazards). Sensitize beneficiaries on where referral services for GBV survivors.5) During registration of beneficiaries, identify persons who will not be able to easily collect their livelihood inputs from the site, and may need extra assistance, including people with disabilities, elderly, female-headed households, child-headed households, pregnant and lactating women and chronically ill.6) Set up a complaint desk with staff trained to handle SEA complaints to help women and girls report the issues related to collection of inputs, and cases of abuse (women staff). Ask these persons with special needs what arrangements could be made with other family or community members to collect and provide on their behalf. Be aware that giving through chiefs represents a risk of SEA, and is not encouraged.7) Provide information for confidential complaint in line of approaches (make sure information and complaints are not just done through local leaders, make sure there is a mass meeting with women and with people with disabilities and their careers present)8) Provide information to beneficiary communities about their entitlements, the selection criteria, humanitarian code of conduct (on fraud and PSEA). Provide information to community leaders, men and boys on contents and requirements for use of all inputs materials, with the fact that, some of the materials are sex-specific.9) Under take consultation with local authority on the land disputes and conduct a comprehensive meeting with inclusion of local chiefs and representatives from the community to avoid insecurity in the targeted project area.10) Form the awareness team to sensitize the beneficiaries on risks to explosive and hazard, post IEC posters that bear symbols of dangers.
