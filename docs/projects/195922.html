Project Summary

The goal of this project is to provide emergency non-food items for the IDPs in Aljehad, Alsalam, Al Wafaa, Reef Aleppo Al Janobi, Jabal Al-Hos, Ghetaa Al Rahma2, Nour Al Houda, Al Forkan, Al Sufara'a. Al Amal and Al Khan) located in Harim Sub district. This will bene t approximately 14933 individuals (2800 HHs) in total in the targeted 12 camps The rural households in NWS have suffered from the ongoing con ict which almost entirely displaced the residents of the southern villages and towns. After security was re-established in those villages, the IDPs returned to their original homes which were either partially damaged or destroyed, the returnees also lost the houses' items which were in their houses, and their farms were adversely affected by the long period of abandonment. For these reasons, the households are unable to afford the minimum basic life-needs, such as basic house furniture, cutlery, clothes, winter clothing, fuel, winter-specific shelter upgrades; or summer/flood assistance etc in winter. These households lack secure access to market whether because of physical obstacles, lack of means of transportation, or other concerns. Women, children, elderly and people with special needs are the most vulnerable groups. Therefore, we will work through our “NFI Distribution” project to provide them with the basic needs which support both returnees and IDPs by distributing a variety of items which can be manufactured as necessary such as: emergency kits (Plastic Sheeting, Jerry Can, Solar Lamps, Mattresses, Blankets, Hasira in summer and Carpet in winter and other items as per technical SNFI cluster guidance)
The project is designed to bene t all population of the camps including women and girls. The aid will be provided to all households without discrimination. For the older and persons with a disability, the aid will be taken to their place of accommodation. The project should reduce the risk of vulnerable women and girls from being exploited in anyway due to lack of basic life-saving aid.
Basic needs will reduce domestic violence within the family and tension within the camp. Women will be able to stay safe in their own tents and feel less vulnerable. In general, SDH will ensure that there is su cient monitoring conducted by SDH to avoid any kind of harm done to the bene ciaries in the distribution. Proper measures will be in place to ensure there is no abuse of power by any members of the implementation team, the volunteers or the IDPs themselves. SDH will involve the women participants in the operation to decrease the protection risks in dealing with gender sensitive matters. Also,
there is no risk of tension between inter-IDPs or with host communities as all bene ciaries are IDPs and there is no host community involved. SDH also have a strict PSEA and CP code of conduct for their staff to adhere to in the course of the operation.
This project follows the WHO guidance on prevention and response to COVID-19, in a ‎rigorous effort to prevent the infection spread, protecting staff and beneficiaries from this hazard. Social distancing, ‎restrictions in gatherings, and hygienic measures are in place within the project.‎

3 - Needs

According to the 2023 HNO, with the crisis in its 12 years, staggering levels of need persist for people throughout Syria. Whilst there has been a reduction in violence in many parts of the country over the past year, an estimated 11.8M people require multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance. Amidst ongoing hostilities and signi cant protection needs, the population continues to look for safety. According HNAP’s MNM report of JAN/2023, Dana sub-district witnessed 71,315 IDPs moving in. In Maaret Tamsrin, 39,487 IDPs have moved till OCT 2022. Idlib subdistrict has seen 31,520 IDPs in ux so far. Dana, Maaret Tamsrin, and Idlib feature in the Top 5 sub-districts experiencing new arrivals recently. Of IDPs, 61% are in inadequate housing; 41% of whom are in emergency shelter. The project will begin with a RNA activity to determine the needs. SDH plans to reach approximately 14933 individuals (2800 HHs) in 2023 as per the current IDPs in Aljehad, Alsalam, Al Wafaa, Reef Aleppo Al Janobi, Jabal Al-Hos, Ghetaa Al Rahma2, Nour Al Houda, Al Forkan, Al Sufara'a. Al Amal and Al Khan. Those camps are not covered by any NGO and is desperate need of basic items. The project will take into consideration their protection needs as well as participation in the implementation to address the gender sensitive issues.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

By following the cluster guidelines, the project will address potential protection risks with mitigating measures appropriate to the intervention modality of this project. SDH assesses the risks to be in following areas- A: Inter-IDPs tension due to varied selection of items – Med/Med Since the aid is provided through cash/voucher approach, and all HHs getting the same amount of aid, and the content is based on cluster’s guidelines and standards. B: Tension between Host and IDPs – Low/Med The camps are sufficiently distanced from the nearest host communities. C: Abuse of authority by distribution actors – High/High SDH applies Code of Conduct policy/procedure on all staff along with complaint mechanism to prevent abuse. D: Risk of Physical Harm distribution points – Low/Low Intervention is based on cash voucher hence greatly minimises such a risk E: Risk to humanitarian nature of the intervention by armed actors – Med/High Strong coordination with local civil authorities, camp management, and community actors to prevent interference by armed actors. F: Lack of documentation may exclude certain IDPs – Med/Med Close coordination with Camp management authority to ensure no IDPs are excluded. Alternative documentation will also be considered depending on the camp policy and processes. Reconciliation will be done between data provided by the camp management and our own assessment from data collection/analysis.
