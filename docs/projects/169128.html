Project Summary

Within the emergency response to Beirut explosion, NRC aims at strengthening the protection of the affected families through: 1) Provision of tailored legal services to the affected families, on legal, civil documentation and housing, land and property (HLP) rights. 2) Cash assistance to vulnerable families

1 - Needs

The explosion at the Beirut port on 4 August has significant consequences in terms of loss of lives, physical injuries and property destruction that will have an impact in the short and longer term. This incident comes at a time when Lebanon is already severely affected by a socio-economic crisis and the COVID-19 emergency. Amid instability, growing unemployment rates and challenges in accessing basic services, the Beirut explosion exacerbated the vulnerabilities of the affected families, in particular in areas with pre-existing socioeconomic inequalities. In the aftermath of the explosion, many families were obliged to seek temporary shelters (300,000 persons were reported displaced) and several remained in their damaged houses. The preliminary data of an ongoing rapid assessments conducted by NRC and other shelter actors show that approximately 12% of the buildings in the affected areas assessed, sustained severe damage, 32% sustained moderate levels of damage and 56% have minor damages. This situation will generate legal protection needs related to housing, land and property (HLP) among the affected families. These emerging challenges include disputes between tenants and owners on the property damages and repairs, security of tenure, concerns regarding compensation mechanisms and eviction threats, in a context where the pressure on the housing market is increasing. Specific difficulties can be faced by tenants who have leases stipulated before July 1992, renewed automatically every year, assuring security of tenure with very low rental fees that can be questioned in the current situation. Moreover, a number of persons are expected to have lost or damaged legal and civil documentation as a result of the explosion. Some families may also require support regarding death registration processes.The explosions caused considerable trauma to thousands of people belonging to the Lebanese and non-Lebanese communities. Vulnerable groups including elderlies, persons with disabilities, persons with serious medical conditions, female heads of households, and other persons with specific needs, are among those most impacted by the explosion and the existing overlapping crises in the country. As a result, the most vulnerable cohorts are exposed to increased protection risks, including forced evictions, increased levels of gender based violence, and risks of exploitation and abuse. Trauma and increasing mental health issues were reported among the affected population particularly in Karantina and Nabaa neighbourhoods. According to information shared by child protection actors, approximately 100.000 children are currently in need of humanitarian assistance.. In terms of children’s needs, rapid needs assessment conducted by UNICEF shortly after the Beirut Port explosions indicated that 50% children were showing changes in behaviour or signs of trauma or extreme stress. University of Tromso and NRC identify two broad categories of children have been affected by the Beirut explosion. 1. Directly affected children: those living in close proximity to the explosion, those who have lost family members, seen people being injured and /or experienced loss or damage to property. 1. Indirectly affected children: those whose family members, friends and relatives have been directly affected; children living in Lebanon who have been watching high-intensity media coverage with grotesque scenes. Vulnerable children: How severely affected the children in the two categories are may depend on their individual vulnerabilities. Children are in general vulnerable; those with previous trauma exposure such as war experiences are in particular vulnerable.Children are more likely than adults to be affected by disasters, and more severely so. On average, children and young people seem to be less well equipped than adults to cope with disasters. The cognitive abilities and lack of life experience of children may reduce their ability to handle acute stress.

2 - Activities

• Expand pre-existing emergency and protection cash-based intervention programmes to address urgent blast-related needs. NRC will ensure that the target includes vulnerable refugee and host communities for the provision of emergency cash, The outreach activities and cash assistance intervention will target single-headed household, elderly-headed household, household with a member with disability, household with a chronically ill member, household with persons injured in need of medical care, household with two children under five years old. The emergency cash assistance modality will be used to cover specific vulnerabilities linked to a protection incident/situation, and is provided as a one-off support measure. The provision of cash assistance will aim to offset the risk of individuals and families resorting to negative coping mechanisms.• Provision of tailored information, counselling and legal assistance services to affected groups (including Lebanese, refugees and migrant workers). The legal aid service delivery will aim at responding to the emerging legal protection issues on legal and civil documentation (including replacement of lost and damaged documents), and housing, land and property (HLP) rights, focusing on due diligence support, security of tenure, rights and responsibilities of tenants and owners regarding property damages, collaborative dispute resolution interventions and awareness raising on available compensation mechanisms. The legal service provision will be carried out in coodination with other relevant protection, legal actors and UN agencies engaged in the emergency response, in particular UN-Habitat, UNDP and OCHCR.
