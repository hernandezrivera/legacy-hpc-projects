Project Summary

This project aims to provide sustainable lifesaving food assistance and Livelihoods support through cash and voucher, direct inputs distribution and trainings to most vulnerable 20,000 HH (120,000 individuals)(51% Female, 49% male) in Jonglei, Lakes, Eastern Equatoria and Central Equatoria state.

Needs

"South Sudan is undergoing a serious political, economic , social and environmental crisis. These multiple factors have caused serious economic, social and psyschological suffering through loss of assets, displacement , deaths , lack of health, education services. All these are compounded by high poverty levels among the people caused by many years of war that led to deprivation, marginalization and neglect. Due to all these factors the vulnerability of many people has increased and therefore their coping strategies have shifted from normal to abnormal coping strategies, thus affecting their resilience to most of the shocks and stresses. The shocks and stresses have affected the populations differently. Children , women , the youth , the disabled , people living with HIV/AIDS and the elderly have been worse affected.acording to September 2018 IPC update, It is projected that the food security situation is likely to deteriorate with 6.1 million people severely food insecure (IPC 3,4,5). this will come to 5.2 Million in the January - March 2019 projection. This figure is likely to rise during the July-August 2018 lean season. Reliance on subsistence rainfed agriculture exposes farmers in Eastern, Central and Lakes States to local climate related shocks such as delayed/erratic rains, drought, soil degradation and flooding. Poor access to inputs , training and financial services particularly for women contributes to low production. In 2017 in the States (lakes , Jonglei, Eastern Equatoria and Central Equatoria, faced threat of climate , economic , health and conflict related shocks , including delayed rains , prolonged dry spell , Fall army worm infestation that affected the maize crop, high in food prices of above 500% and violent conflict that drove widespread displacement, blocked farmers from crop-fields, and scorched farms.Cattle raiding in Greater Kapoeta, Jonglei (Pibor /Boma) and Lakes states have led to loss of livelihoods assets. The slow implementation of the August 2015 peace agreement is likely to lead to increased insecurity, and therefore, continued people displacement, loss of assets, disruption of livelihoods including market and adaption of destructive copping strategies such as cutting trees for fire wood and charcoal, child labour, abandonment and recruitment into warring parties. In order to prevent a dramatic deterioration of food insecurity, malnutrition and associated social crisis and child protection risks, the need to provide timely and appropriate resilience building humanitarian and livelihood assistance in 2018. Based on post distribution monitioring in our project locations communities and discussions with other NGOs and local government the following needs were identified: 1. The immediate need is to continue providing safe access to food through Cash/Voucher to the most vulnerable housleholds. 2. Getting support to protect and rehabilitate their livelihood assets through provision of Livelihoods inputs to enhance their capacities to cope with recurring shock and reduce their reliance on food aid. In particular the following needs have come out from the discussion and the experience so far with Plan intervention: In all project areas, community assets such as community access roads, schools are at best very much limited. The need to expand and strengthen rehabilitation of community assets using cash for work model is a viable option. 3. Communities and youth needs to be food secure and build sustainable livelihoods through skills training for the youth, formation of SACCOs and VSLAs and business start-ups." The needs gaps have been identified by a Rapid assessment carried out by Plan International in May 2018 in Kapoeta and Pibor and Inter-Agency Assessment carried out in Torit and Magwi.

Activities

This Project targets 20,000 HHs (120,000 individuals) and is broken down down as follows per objective/activities. Objective 1.provide sustainable lifesaving food assistance through cash and voucher to most vulnerable 2,000HH (51% Female, 49% male) in Jonglie, Lakes, Eastern Equatoria and Central Equatoria state. (Response to cluster objective 1) 1.1 Distribute cash or voucher to purchase food food to 2,000 HHs vulnerable Individuals in Lakes, Jonglei, Eastern Equatoria and Central Equatoria state. Objective 2. Protect and stabilize livelihoods for 15,200 vulnerable HHs (female/male 51/49%) through provision of Livelihoods inputs, Cash/Voucher transfer activities. (Response to cluster bojective 2) 2.1 protect livelihoods through distribution of seeds and tools for crop and vegetable gardening plus creatinon and rehabilitation of assets throuch cash for work acitivities for 9,200 HHs in Jonglei, Lakes, Central Equatoria and Eastern Equatoria states. 2.2 Train 1,000 HHs on Appropriate agronomic practices in Jomnglei, Lakes, Eastern Equatoria and Central Equatoria states 2.3 support provision of veterinary servies through community based intitiavives to 5000 HH in Eastern Equatoria, Lakes and Jonglei states Objective 3: Communities and Youth become food secure and build sustainable livelihoods through creation of access to finace and markets for 2,000 people (51% Female, 49% Male). 3.1 train 800 HHs on speciff skills as per the local needs in Eastern Equatoria and Central Equatoria states. 3.2: form 30 VSLA and 4 SACCOs targetiing 1000 HH (51% Female, 49% Male) in Central and Eastern Equatoria. (Plan International own funding) 3.3: support mentorship and business incubation for 1000 HHs youth and VSLA members."
