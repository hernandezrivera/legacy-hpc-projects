Needs

"As of 30th September 2019, UNHCR population fact sheet indicates 55% of the total Rohingya population being children, majority of which lack child protection services. Despite improved services over the last 2 years, the CP needs remain enormous and new risks are emerging for children, adolescents and families. Children are experiencing high levels of distress because they witnessed extreme violence in Myanmar and have been exposed to stressful and uncertain living conditions which affects their psycho-social, mental health and development. The joint participatory child protection assessment with Rohingya adolescents, 2019 indicates children to be at risk of child marriage, physical abuse, sexual abuse, and emotional abuse, as well as denial of access to services. Children are further exposed to kidnapping, trafficking, and sexual harassment, particularly for girls as 41% households reported fear of their girls experiencing sexual violence. As of Sep 2019, a total of 7,689 (to be updated) unaccompanied and separated children have been registered and are at heightened risk of child trafficking, abuse and exploitation in the camps. Girls, who represent a larger proportion (57%) of this vulnerable group, are particularly at risk of child marriage, sexual exploitation, abuse and neglect. As of 30th September 2019, UNHCR population fact sheet indicates 3% of the total population with specific needs to be Unaccompanied and separated children. Children continue to experience high levels of distress after witnessing extreme violence in Myanmar, as well as being exposed to continued stressful and uncertain living conditions upon arrival in Bangladesh. This is coupled with feeling unsafe, risks of child marriage, physical abuse, trafficking, sexual abuse and denial of access to services especially education. The monsoon and cyclone seasons equally exerts emotional distress to children. In the previous two years, community based child protection mechanisms have been established. However, for them to function and ensure community participation in protection of children, there is need to strengthen systems and mechanisms including capacity building in the form of training and awareness so as to address the existing child protection concerns. Boys and girls, women and men are equal partners in addressing this gap. Adolescents represent one of the most neglected group in accessing child protection and education opportunities. only 15% of the total adolescent are accessing learning opportunities of which only 2% is girls. 36% reported child marriage as a barrier, 23% cultural reason, 20% needed at home and 21% think education is not useful. Child marriage has become a coping strategy as families look at it as a protective measure in ensuring their social, economic and physical security. This often exposes adolescent girls to early marriage, pregnancy, sexual and physical abuse from their husband as well as abuse from the community and higher rates of divorce which increases stigmatization. For boys’ child labor, drug abuse and engagement in criminal activities is the alternative in the absence of Education. Many households in the camps rely on the unpaid household labor, including caring for younger siblings, cooking meals, collecting water and firewood, among other household chores. The MSNA 2019 reports indicates 5% of household reporting a child working to earn income, 5% household reports presence of early marriage and 2% reports children working under hazardous working conditions. There is also need to address community perceptions and knowledge gaps on child protection, education, PSEA, and adolescent participation. This will address child protection concerns such as child marriage, child labor, trafficking, sexual harassment, physical abuse, access to education, peace buildin and participation among others. Activities or outputs

"The project goal will be to strengthen the protection of refugee women, men, girls and boys by providing specialized child protection services for Rohingya children and adolescent at most risk. The project will contribute to objective 2, 3 and 4 of the protection sector strategy 2020 while working in collaboration with the child protection sub sector. Output 1: Promote a community-based approach to support community self-protection mechanisms and facilitate meaningful access to specialized services for persons at heightened protection risk, including girls, boys, women and men of all ages and with diverse needs and vulnerabilities, with the ultimate aim of mitigating exposure to protection risks, strengthen the resilience of affected communities, and place communities at the center of the response in line with AAP principles. The project will apply an inclusive community based approach of bring beneficiaries at the center of implementation considering access, safety and dignity for boys, girls, men and women including persons with disabilities. Project activities will include 1.1: community access to information through awareness campaigns and meetings targeting boys, girls, men, women, faith leaders, community leaders among other stake holders at the established multipurpose centres, community public places like mosques, community kitchens among others while working closely and in partnership with community leaders, children leaders, influence women, camp level coordinating platforms such as CiC coordination meeting, protection and child protection coordination meetings among others. Materials developed through the child protection sub sector will be prioritized in addition to other developed by children and the organisation1.2: strengthening community based child protection mechanism by working with Community based child protection committees, faith leaders and center management committees. Training on roles and responsibilities, prevention and response to child protection concerns as well as referrals will be conducted. Regular meetings and engagement in general child protection situation monitoring at camp level will be conducted in consultation with children and other stake holders. This will equip with knowledge and tools to be able to participants in prevention, response and reporting of child protection concerns. 1.3: As a way of empowering communities to respond to child protection needs during monsoon and cyclone seasons among other DRR eventualities, Parents and community protection groups will be trained on monsoon and cyclone preparedness to ensure propositioning and monitoring the child protection situation across respective camps. The child protection sub sector DRR response framework and strategy will be used to guide DRR activity engagement with children, parents and communities. 1.3: Adolescent led intervention. The multipurpose child and adolescent centers approach will be used to provide a package of hands on skills training, peace building, PSEA and Child safeguarding skills training for adolescent boys and girls between 15-18 years of age. Training and orientation for staff, facilitators and instructors will be conducted for men and women. Field based session will be prioritized to ensure Rohingya men and women facilitator’s participation. Output 2: Support system strengthening, by supporting Government and local partners, promoting peaceful coexistence and enhancing social cohesion within and between refugee and host communities. 2.1: Integrate peace building activities for boys, girls, women and men through adolescent led initiatives such as outdoor games and sports, adolescent club led innovations on social cohesion, dialogues with faith leaders, parents and community leaders.
