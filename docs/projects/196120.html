Project Summary

LEAP is informed by proven, evidence-based approaches, programmatic lessons and
consultations with stakeholders, including girls, boys, women, men, community leaders,
partners and government actors. The project duration is 36-month and projected
budget is $6.8 million for whole project and all working areas (CXB Sadar, Ramu, Ukhiya, Teknaf and 3 Camp- 1W, 3 and 4).But under the provision of JRP, the project
will cover 7 locations- Teknaf- Sabrang & Teknaf Sadar, Ukhiya- Jaliapalong & Holudiapalong, Camp-1W, 3 and 4 of Ukhiya. The project
will employ a rights-based health services , gender-transformative (GT) and three-pronged
strategy to: i) improve the individual and collective agency and resilience of adolescent
girls and young women to exercise their SRH and protection rights (1100); ii) strengthen
health systems to provide sex- and age-responsive quality health and protection services
(1200); and iii) enhance the capacity of local women's rights organizations (WROs),
including girl-led organizations (GLOs), and youth-led organizations (YLOs) on evidencebased advocacy for GARI health and protection services while advocating for long-term
change for evidence-based GARI SRH, SGBV and MHPSS services and programming
(1300). Improved, integrated SRHR outcomes for girls have shown to contribute to better
lifelong outcomes in education, protection, livelihoods and overall wellbeing (Brookings,
2016). LEAP’s multi-sectoral and feminist approach works across health and protection
sectors to strengthen horizontal linkages while integrating women and girl's agency
throughout and engaging WROs/GLOs and YLOs as influencers to improve accountability
for GARI SRH and SGBV services and policies.
Aligned with national and local humanitarian, health, protection and GE policies and
strategies, as well as the SDGs and Canada’s Feminist International Assistance Policy
(FIAP), LEAP will contribute to poverty reduction by targeting marginalized groups in
Rohingya camps and host communities facing multifaceted dimensions of exclusion. In
partnership with four local partners where three implementing partner working in demand side and one key technical partner working on supply side, LEAP will directly reach 97,774 people, where 60,578 female + 37,196 are male including four Unions of
Ukhiya and Teknaf sub-districts, as well as three Rohingya refugee camps (37,529 where 21,462 is in 4 Host Union + 16,067 is in 3 camp).

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Cox’s Bazar is one of 20 districts in Bangladesh lagging the national average fordevelopment indicators, with approximately 33% of the population of 2.29 million (2011)living below the poverty line compared to the national average of 31.5% (Care, 2018; WorldBank, 2016). The influx of Rohingya refugees since 2017 has brought new andcompounding challenges to the already weak local health and protection systems(UNHCR, Policy Brief, June 2020.) Approximately 949,000 people live in host communities(49.6% women and girls) and 860,000 Rohingya refugees live in camps (51.7% womenand girls), most of whom face deteriorating situations in SRHR and protection (UNOCHA,2020 COVID-19 Response Plan – Addendum to the JRP: Rohingya Humanitarian Crisis,Apr-Dec 2020). Families, especially women and girls, are exposed to threats from criminalgroups, trafficking, violence, child abuse and neglect and child, early and forced marriage(CEFM). The incidence of CEFM has increased during the pandemic due to the disruptionof education and economic activity and rising food prices. There are still gaps in theoutreach of safe and quality GBV services and referral mechanisms (SAFERWORLD, June2021). Mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services are non-existent. Poormental health can be associated with risky sexual behaviour and substance abuse,resulting in negative impacts on the SRHR of adolescent girls and young women,including heightened vulnerability to unintended pregnancy, unsafe abortion, sexuallytransmitted infections (STIs) and SGBV. Harmful social gender and patriarchal norms,attitudes and behaviours present in both host community and camp settings disempowerwomen and girls. Unequal power relations, compounded by the impacts of COVID-19,confine women and girls with the burden of unpaid care work, increase their risk ofintimate partner violence (IPV), hinder their access to and control over resources, excludetheir participation in family and community decision-making and limit their mobility,which makes them vulnerable to GBV and restricts their SRHR (SAFERWORLD, June2021). The contraceptive prevalence rate is only 34% compared to 62% at the nationallevel (Family Planning 2020, BDHS 2014) due to widespread, deep-rooted stigma,misconception and societal expectations of women and girls. This is especially true foradolescent girls who face unique barriers in accessing GARI SRH and SGBV services dueto lack of awareness of their rights and unresponsiveness of services. In Teknaf, 82% ofadolescents felt that health providers did not address their issues (Cox’s Bazar CPSS,REACH, Child Protection (CP) Assessment, May 2020). To respond to these interrelatedbarriers, Plan International's proposed LEAP (Lifting Healthy, Empowered and ProtectedGirls and Women) project will advance the realization of SRHR of the most vulnerableadolescent girls and young women in all their diversity in refugee and host communities.LEAP will be implemented in partnership with four local partner NGOs for whole workingareas but there will be three partner NGOs : 1) Samaj Kallyan O Unnayan Sangstha (SKUS)and 2) HOPE Foundation and 3) Partner's in Health and Development within the scope of JRP. This ultimate outcome is aligned withthe 2023 Joint Response Plan (JRP), emphasizing building the capacity of both Rohingyarefugee and host communities in Cox’s Bazar District. Specifically, pillar 3 of theprotection framework for the humanitarian response, guiding the 2023 JRP, focuses onfostering the wellbeing of host communities in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazilas and refugeecamps to promote alternatives to negative coping mechanisms and promote peaceful coexistence.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project was in the JRP of 2022 (RBGD22-HEA-185777-1) and aligned with health sector’s strategic plan (SO1. To strengthen the health systems capacity to deliver effective and efficient quality health care services, SO2: Maintain equitable access to essential health care (Preventive, Curative, Rehabilitative, Palliative health care) in a sustainable manner and of acceptable quality at all levels (Community, Primary and Secondary Health Care Levels) community engagement to promote and sustain their health, protection mainstreaming and Gender-Based Violence (GBV) Mainstreaming. According to the reference #3 - Adopt an integrated approach, and # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuty the project will not establish any new facility but work on capacity building of the service providers of enrolled health facilities on Adolescent Friendly Sexual and Reproductive Health Service (AFSRHS), SGBV (reporting protocol also) and MHPSS. In each camp the project will collaborate with one health facility (total 3 by three camp) and will contribute to the facilities by refurbishment support (need based). Dignity Kit supply for adolescent and young women and materials supply to the health facility. As per Health Facility Rationalization Results- SAG Recommendation of Health Sector, primarily the project is decided to work with following health facilities: In Camp 1W - 1. HF_008 GK/RTMI/UNHCR/UNFPA- Primary Health Center, in Camp 3, HF_033 PHD and in Camp 4, HF_029 Hope Foundation/UNFPA/IPAS.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

To protect children, adolescents and young people the project works to ensure health rights specially SRHR including sexual and gender based violence related issues. The targeted beneficiary will learn SRHR, SGBV, MHPSS and Life Skills which helps them to be sensitize and claim their demand in the mentioned aspects. Again, to ensure enabling envioronment for the children, adolescents and young people, the project included their parents and different stakeholders under good parenting and community sensitization activities. To provide adolescent and youth friendly service on SRHR, SGBV and MHPSS the project has taken capacity building initiatives for service providers and refurbishment activity for facility. The project is also working to settled an effective referal linkage system in this purpose.

3.2 - Gender

LEAP is applying a gender-transformative approach. In line with this approach, the following are aimed to be achieved: 1. Empowering young women and adolescent girls’ individual and collective agency. 2. Engaging family and community level powerholders and men and boys to create an enabling environment promoting GE and supporting women and girls’ access to SRH services. 3. Ensuring availability of gender responsive and adolescent friendly SRH services and strengthened advocacy capacity of WROs around GARI SRH services. To achieve the mentioned aims, the project builds on Plan International’s existing, proven interventions. Girls and young women’s networks will be engaged in advocacy by creating linkages with and support from Women’s Rights Organizations (WROs), Girl-Led Organizations (GLOs) and Youth-Led Organizations (YLOs). 3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

Child safeguarding is in the core of this project. The project conduct safeguarding risk assesment for each activity and take necessary steps in this regard.

3.4 - Diversity

Inclusiveness and openness is an approach of this project. It is welcoming the people of different religion, culture, ethnicity, economic status, host community and refugee, people with disability (except blind and deaf), gender and sexual identity within the target audience.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

To maintain and ensure the pillars of AAP, the project has interventions of Feedback Mechanism, Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) and Community Reflection Meeting. The M&E Team of the project will monitor the process of ensuring accountability to the affected people through out the project period. For transparency and financial monitoring, Finance Team of the project is respoinsible. Overall all the relevant issues like participation of relevant stakeholders, access to information, capacity buliding and compliance, the project team will ensure appropriate practice among the team members.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes. LEAP involves the carrying out of few of potential to cause negative environmental effects. In this purpose, the project has Environmental Action Plan which already submitted to GAC and approved by them as well. The EAP defines how Plan is going to mitigate the potential negative effects of the project while taking into account climate change and how they will contribute to build awareness on environmental and health linkages when possible. For LEAP, this will be mainly related to minor refurbishment works undertaken at selected government health facilities and medical waste management activities. The EAP also outlines the mitigation, monitoring, and institutional measures to be taken during LEAP’s implementation and operation to avoid or minimize potential adverse environmental effects while maximizing environmental benefits, and the actions needed to implement these measures.
