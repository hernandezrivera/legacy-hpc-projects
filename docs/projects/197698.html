Project Summary

The ongoing project will continue to support the GBV SUB sector by providing GBV prevention and response service to crises affected women and girls in Pulka (Gwoza LGA), Mashamari (Konduga LGA) in Borno State North-Eastern Nigeria. Malteser International will provide GBV case management programming including caring for survivors of GBV as well as immediate needs of women and girls formerly associated with armed groups & forces/trafficking, Mental health, and Psychosocial Support Services (age-appropriate) Including Counselling, psychoeducation, Art therapy, adult literacy section, recreational activities and referrals (one-stop centers) will be equipped to strengthen the capacity of case management for the immediate response to GBV related cases. Malteser International will provide and distribute standardized, culturally appropriate dignity kits to address the critical needs of survivors who are either affected by displacement-related issues or domestic violence. The program will support and proactively participate in field-level coordination, periodic service mapping, and update location-specific referral pathways. Peer support, mentoring/coaching, skills building, and empowerment targeting adolescents and adults as well as relevant capacity building and engagement with stakeholders and security actors will be integrated during program implementation. The program will establish linkages to meaningful and protective livelihood activities and promote resilience by organizing specialized skills-building and empowerment actions as per market demands. The project will integrate GBV risk mapping/safety audit into GBV response to reduce the risk of GBV in the community such as early child marriages, survival sex, SEA, etc, and mainstream it into the WASH program to mitigate risks of GBV at related water and sanitation facilities. Conduct awareness and capacity building for staff, community leaders, gender champions, and non-GBV staff including the water user committee on GBV services and how to make safe referrals to appropriate services.
The project will support the ongoing WASH sector activities by providing a comprehensive WASH approach in implementing complete WASH packages and accountability to affected people in the hotspot locations of Gwoza LGA in Pulka specifically in (Camp C and sector 5 in the host community), Konduga LGA, Jere LGA, MMC, Mafa LGA in Borno State . MI will provide WASH life-saving services through improving access to safe and sufficient drinking water supply, safe/adequate and dignified sanitation facilities, hygiene supplies, and information to mitigate risks related to public health while mainstreaming protection, gender equality for vulnerable IDPs, returning refugees, and host communities that meet the differing needs for men, women, boys, girls, and persons with disabilities. Activities will also include cholera preparedness and response, construction of water and sanitation infrastructure for displaced persons and members of host communities, operation and maintenance of existing WASH infrastructure including water supply systems, latrines, and bathing facilities, solid waste management and distribution of hygiene kits, which are in line with the WASH sector strategy.
Malteser international will ensure linkages between the WASH and GBV programs in implementation areas through awareness creation on personal/environmental hygiene. Beneficiaries will be provided with house-to-house sensitization on menstrual hygiene and access to GBV services. 1 - Needs

Malteser International identifies humanitarian needs and responds accordingly by relying on the primary source of information through conducting its own needs assessment and secondary source of information from the Humanitarian Needs Overview conducted by UN-OCHA as well as relevant information from WASH/Protection cluster humanitarian gaps mapping and multi-sectoral need assessment. According to the Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) between 2021 and 2022, the BAY states experienced an increased number from 3 million to 5 million people in need of WASH humanitarian assistance, across internally displaced persons (+29%), returnees (+113%) and hosting communities (+95%). The highest WASH needs were even found in the catastrophic (very critical) situation for host +communities and IDPs out of camp in some LGAs in Borno and Adamawa states. In 2022, access to safe water, dignified sanitation, and hygiene awareness were impacted by heavy flooding, camp closures, and the movement of IDPs and returnees. This has led to negative coping mechanisms including drinking water from unsafe sources, increased open defecation, and poor personal and household-level hygiene, resulting namely in an unprecedented cholera outbreak. Further displacement due to conflict has led to poor access to safe water, and congestion in camps has resulted in limited access to safe sanitation with partners resorting to manual dislodging facing heavy flooding, moreover with some challenging access to the dumping sites. Hygiene awareness and personal hygiene at the household level have been impacted by the high cost of living which has resulted in a lack of soap at the household level, leaving vulnerable populations, especially the elderly and children under 5 at high risk of diseases.All forms of GBV are mostly underreported. In particular, reporting an incident of sexual violence (rape or sexual assault) is hindered by the risk of stigmatization, retaliation, shame or marginalization, and oppression from the community, as well as a lack of survivor-centered service provision after referrals. Physical assault, sexual violence, denial of resources, opportunities, and services, and psychological and emotional abuse are among the violations most frequently reported, and recurrently perpetrated by intimate or former partners (domestic violence). According to protection monitoring reports conducted in the first quarter of 2022 by UNHCR shows that the 5 top priority needs highlighted by 2,568 IDPs and Returnees interviewed remain Food, WASH, livelihood, shelter, and financial support. Limited access to food and the high cost of food items continue to push IDPs and returnees to desperation making them susceptible to exploitation and negative coping mechanism. The limited access to livelihood opportunities and financial independence has continued to lead to dependency syndrome in these locations while others have adopted negative coping mechanisms such as forced/child marriages, children being forced to beg, and hawk to sustain their families and caregivers. Despite the concerted efforts of humanitarian actors and the government, unmet gaps remain and there is a need to continue to empower IDPs and returnees to mitigate unmet needs while continuously advocating for improved security in return areas/LGAs to enable returnees and host community members to access their farmlands without fear of attacks or abduction by NSAG members. Assessment conducted by Norwegian Church Aid in Pulka shows that there is an increase in exploitation and abuse of women and girls because of increased vulnerability forcing married women and FHH to engage in sexual intimate relationships with men to provide for their families. Incidents of such has made FHH and women getting pregnant, yet their husband died or in prison this also increase violence with in- laws. Cases of rape, FGM and child marriage are rampant but are underreported because of fear.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Malteser International is currently implementing protection services at Pulka, Gwoza LGA, and Mashamari, Konduga LGA providing psychosocial support, GBV case management, and skill acquisition/empowerment to GBV survivors. Aligning with its protection program Malteser International will ensure streamlining of overall protection strategies in its WASH activities to reduce risks to vulnerable populations through its designs and activity implementation. Malteser International Nigeria will strive to reduce the risks from exposure to GBV and protection issues by creating a safe, dignified and friendly environment for women and children making facilities safely accessible to individuals with special needs. Malteser International will adhere to the guidelines set by the IASC for Integrating Gender-Based Violence mainstreaming in humanitarian action and the Humanity and Inclusion guidelines in the design of facilities and services for people with disabilities. To ensure safety and dignified access to facilities for women, children, and people with disabilities, Malteser International will undertake the following program approaches • Construction/rehabilitation of latrines which are gender-segregated for women and men and constructed at suitable locations and distances which will be discussed and agreed upon with community members. • Setting up separate women's and men’s committees in Focus Group Discussion (FGD) to provide spaces for the participation of and accountability to the community about the design, implementation, information sharing, and opportunity for feedback. • A gender-sensitive approach to MEAL will be adopted to ensure that the program is having the intended impacts to meet the needs of women, men, boys, and girls while addressing sexual exploitation and abuse. • Installation of strong locks from inside doors of latrines to ensure privacy and protection while latrines are used by girls, boys, and women. • In each block of 5 latrines rehabilitated, 1 latrine will be designated for people with disabilities. This latrine will be provided with a door that is wide enough to ensure access to those with limited mobility and their caregivers where suitable; handles and other utensils to support mobility will also be installed to facilitate the use of the latrine. To ensure the project enables equal and impartial access to WASH services with consideration of people with special needs, MI will undertake the following activities: • Hygiene promotion volunteers will be dispatched to homes of people with limited mobility and elderly people to present the content from the community level hygiene and sanitation activities as well as obtain their perspectives for the design of the services and facilities to be more accessible to their special needs. • Hygiene kits and other items such as chlorine tablets will be delivered to individuals with disability/ limited mobility to their homes, rather than to general distribution sites for the general population • Hygiene promoters will use IECs for special presentations of hygiene practices to individuals with impaired hearing, if any, in order to ensure equal/ equitable access to messaging.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

In ensuring integrity and accountability in our work as per the Core Humanitarian Standard, feedback from beneficiaries will be received via the following channels; •Community Volunteers will be the primary channel through which MI receives feedback from the camp about its Services. Community volunteers are functional in all camps where MI is rendering WASH Services. •Complaint Desk: The M&E will station a complaint desk at the distribution site to record requests, complaints, and feedback from beneficiaries during NFI distributions. M&E officers will resolve simple complaints at the distribution spot and refer a complex one to the team after distribution. •Feedback boxes (suggestion boxes): will also be used to gather feedback from beneficiaries and other stakeholders. The boxes will be located inside the camp. Complaints/suggestions will be retrieved at the end of the month by dedicated MI staff in the presence of the Camp Leaders. •Protection caseworkers will use WGSS to facilitate educational and recreational activities and collect feedback from survivors after rendering services to ascertain the survivor's satisfaction with the support provided and welcome suggestions if any. •Additional informal channels for capturing feedback, requests, and complaints from camp and host communities include periodic community meetings and focus group discussions conducted by staff from the (WASH, WGSS caseworkers, and M&E department), regular post-distribution monitoring visits provide additional opportunities for IDPs to share feedback. Awareness and Information will be communicated to beneficiaries through community meetings, FGDs, or face-to-face interactions with volunteers and staff. The Feedback Response Mechanism operates on the principle of confidentiality, meaning that complaints made through the mechanism are not shared beyond people who need to know about them to address them.
