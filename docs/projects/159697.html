Project Summary

Mercy Corps intends to conduct emergency repair and/or rehabilitate for eight critical existing water wells across the Gaza Strip. Gazans are facing worsening public health hazards due to a lack of capacity by local water and wastewater utility providers. This lack of capacity is primarily caused by the energy shortages, lack of availability of funds to properly maintain the existing infrastructure, and difficulty of importing parts and materials necessary for periodic repairs. Unfortunately, this means that the main utility operator, the Coastal Municipalities Water Utility (CMWU), is in a continuous emergency intervention mode that is hindering it from reaching a preventive maintenance operation scheme. Maintenance is an essential part of infrastructure utility operation. Without it, water and wastewater utilities start to fail causing widespread public health hazards and negative environmental impacts.
A WASH cluster initiated and Mercy Corps led assessment proved that there is a critical need for improved water and wastewater services for the people. Moreover, CMWU has endorsed Mercy Corps’ initiative to rehabilitate eight (8) water wells across the Gaza Strip and has supplied a BoQ detailing the required emergency works.
This emergency intervention will be conducted in coordination with the WASH Cluster, PWA, and CMWU with the goal of meeting the needs of vulnerable households and sustainably building the capacities of local utility providers. All Project proposed activities, designs, and budget estimations were closely coordinated with CMWU.

1 - Needs

The Gaza Strip is a 365 km2 semi-arid strip of land located on the Mediterranean that has been under land, sea, and air blockade since 2007. The 2 million residents of Gaza suffer high levels of poverty, unemployment, and lack of adequate access to food, water, fuel, electricity, medicine, and construction materials.Gazans are facing worsening public health hazards due to a lack of capacity by local water and wastewater utility providers. This lack of capacity is primarily caused by the energy shortages, lack of availability of funds to properly maintain the existing infrastructure, and difficulty of importing parts and materials necessary for periodic repairs. Unfortunately, this means that the main utility operators, the Coastal Municipalities Water Utility (CMWU) and the municipalities, are in continuous emergency intervention mode that is hindering them from reaching a preventive maintenance operation scheme. Maintenance is an essential part of infrastructure utility operation. Without it, water and wastewater utilities start to fail causing widespread public health hazards and negative environmental impacts. Unfortunately, the Palestinian authorities are unable to adequately fund the essential and live-saving maintenance activities that the utility operators are supposed to perform. Hence, many utilities have already failed or are the brink of failure, subjecting hundreds of thousands of Gazans to health hazards and environmental disasters that have led, and will continue to lead, towards a humanitarian catastrophe.For example, the lack of energy and capacity at the wastewater treatment plants in Gaza have resulted in untreated sewage flowing directly to the sea making it inaccessible for recreational use, or ponding the sewage in random ponds in highly populated areas introducing disease-carrying insects, odors, and health risks for the population. Hence, and after a call has been made by CMWU, the WASH cluster partners in Gaza’s attention has been directed towards the need to prioritize projects related to the repair and rehabilitation of essential utility services across the Gaza Strip to alleviate the suffering of the communities directly affected by the failing infrastructure.A meeting at CMWU was held on July 22, 2019, where a list of high-priority required emergency repairs was shared with the WASH cluster partners. On September 10th, 2019, a Rapid Assessment report was produced by Mercy Corps under the umbrella of the WASH cluster and with the involvement of a number of partners. The assessment concluded that intervening in the repair and rehabilitation of these critical utilities is of vital importance because of the vast humanitarian suffering felt by the Gazans.Mercy Corps hence has coordinated with CMWU to target eight (8) water wells across the Gaza Strip. CMWU has already supplied a BoQ detailing the required emergency works. 2 - Activities

The project activities Mercy Corps intends to carry out are straight forward and to the point. First, Mercy Corps intends to consult with the facilities operator (CMWU and Municipalities) to define the scope of work to be done and identify the exact repair and rehabilitation needs for the targeted water wells. Mercy Corps will also conduct public consultation meetings with the residents of all the target areas to ensure that any missed concern they may have is taken into consideration. Then Mercy Corps will finalize the tender documents and BoQ with CMWU, then our procurement team will issue a call for tenders where different local contractors will submit bids to repair/rehabilitate the target water wells. Mercy Corps will continuously supervise the repair works and will establish a feedback mechanism to solve and mitigate any issues or complaints. We will ensure that the contractor adheres to the specifications and we will conduct quality checks throughout the installation process. Once the contractor has finished their required works, and all items have been installed correctly and thoroughly tested, Mercy Corps will hand over the project to the operator. Mercy Corps’ Monitoring Evaluation and Learning (MEL) unit will conduct a number of quality checks to ensure that works are being completed up to standard. Moreover, daily records, photos, and weekly meetings minutes will be kept on record as part of the project’s indicators mean of verification.The target water wells are located in Bani Suhaila , Beit Hanoon, Khanyounis, Al Shokah, Al Naser –Rafah, Al Bureij, and Beit Lahia.

3 - Indicators

Mercy Corps (MC) uses adaptive management techniques to constantly monitor and drive changes needed to improve implementation and respond to changing conditions on the ground. Continuous project monitoring and evaluation is part of these adaptive management techniques and will be conducted by MC Monitoring, Evaluation and Learning (MEL) expert team on an ongoing basis. The information collected during monitoring is fed back to program teams and is used to inform managerial and strategic decision-making in order to make ongoing adjustments to implementation strategies and ensure maximum impact. Moreover, MC MEL strategy is built based on four key pillars that attain to achieve for every single program: 1. Relevant and effective measurement to understand and track progress across targets and indicators; 2. Constant reflection and iterative learning to ensure that processes and program approaches are thoughtfully refined and improved; 3. Local ownership to promote participatory learning and sustainable community impact. 4. Flexible systems and adaptive management to enable us to pivot intentionally and measure appropriately with the dynamic changes in the Gazan context. PMP and MEL tools will be developed in collaboration with the program team, and as necessary with the solar power contractor and CMWU, ensuring consistency in data collection and documentation and facilitating analysis for program reporting and learning. A participatory MEL approaches will be adapted and customized to enable the project team to effectively track, discuss, and understand progress and review feedback from partners and beneficiaries. Throughout the project, MEL team will ensure that qualitative and quantitative methods will be used to monitor and evaluate the programs in the following way: 1. Regular, joint, and spot visits to the targeted WASH facility will be conducted before, during, and after the construction to ensure that the designs, BoQ, construction quality and schedule, and overall quality is up to par. Photos of project activities will be taken for documentation and reporting, a brief on the field visits will be reported on a timely manner as part of the project reports; 2. MEL team will also conduct interviews with beneficiaries to verify their satisfaction and elicit their feedback in regards the provided services. Any issues arising will be handled promptly by the project team and followed up by the MEL team; 3. To determine the impact of the provided service and its contribution to improving the targeted households’ resilience, an exit survey will be conducted with a representative sample of beneficiaries. 4. Lesson Learned session will be held to capture impact and practices. 5. Data Quality Assurance (DQA) and Internal Controls will be conducted for the project activities to ensure data quality and procedures are in place during implementation. As part of our accountability to beneficiaries, Mercy Corps maintains a “Community Accountability Reporting Mechanism – CARM” system, that creates accessible, and secure reporting mechanisms to all beneficiaries; including a special private channel for girls and women to report unlawful situations or inappropriate conduct. 9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Absolutely, the nature of the project is a wide spread benefit for all residents to have improved and more reliable access to domestic water. Hence, due to the auxiliary nature of the project, safety and dignity of the beneficiaries will not be impacted at all, and hence no harm will be done. The nature of our approach is that our intervention cannot be used to fuel further conflict, disadvantage any particular social group, or lead to increased human rights violations. This is because it is centralized benefit to all people. Our project will improve the lives of all impacted people by providing a more reliable source of domestic water. Absolutely no harm will be done because no harm can be done.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The nature of the project is a wide spread benefit for all residents to have improved and more reliable access to domestic water. The water will use the existing water networks which are connected to all benefiting households, hence, the access is impartial and equal for all. The project ensures that men, women, girls, and boys will all have equitable access to the service. Once the water wells are rehabilitated, all will have improved access in their homes. The service is equitable to all groups, all ethnicities, religious groups, etc.
