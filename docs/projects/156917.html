Project Summary

The proposed project aims at restoring rural and urban livelihoods for conflict-affected communities in Idleb governorate, Northwest Syria. This will be achieved through a combination of different interventions at the individual and community-level. In rural areas, at the individual level, crop farmers will be provided with a voucher to redeem seeds, fertilizers and tools and training on climate-smart agriculture and best practices, while breeders’ farmers will be provided with vouchers to redeem fodder for their livestock. ACTED will provide as well cash grants and training to small and medium size agricultural entrepreneurs to enable them to start their own business. At the community level, ACTED will rehabilitate communal productive assets, including bakeries, mills, silos, but also rehabilitate and perform canal drainage and cleaning. These interventions will benefit both farmers (who will have increased access to water for irrigation through canal rehabilitation) and also communities (who will be able to access good quality bread through bakeries rehabilitation, for instance). In urban areas, instead, ACTED will support existing and new small-scale businesses with cash grants and training, enabling them to restore and expand their business. Finally, ACTED will provide vocational and educational training classes to men and women through an Educational Vocational Training Centre (TVET), in order to provide them with a wide range of skills to increase their employability. This project is estimated to benefit 6,420 individuals.

Needs

In 2019, food insecurity remains high in Northwest Syria. In Idleb governorate, the food security severity ranking qualifies as major in 4 out of the 5 districts (Idleb, Harim, Jisr Ash-Shugur, Ariha), and moderate in the remaining one (Al Ma’ra). Out of the 2,379,650 individuals living in Idleb governorate in August 2018, 638,377 were considered to be food insecure, and 472,913 were considered to be at risk of becoming so. 40% of the population is represented by internally displaced persons (IDPs). (HNO PIN Severity 2019). A Whole of Syria assessment coordinated by the Food Security Cluster found that 33.4 percent IDPs and 41.2 percent of returnees are food insecure. 29 percent of returnees adopt emergency coping strategy as compared to 18.7 percent reported across all groups at national level. Moreover, an estimated 40 percent of Syrians spend more than 65 percent of their expenditure on food, 57.2 percent of food insecure households have outstanding debt and 65 percent of food insecure people adopt negative coping strategies. With an 83 - 89 percent people living below poverty lines, and with cumulative GDP loss of $226 billion between 2011 – 2016 out of which $16 billion in losses and damages attributable to agriculture sector alone, the relationship between poverty and food security is becoming inseparable. A monthly food ration with staple items costs at least 80 percent of an unskilled laborer’s monthly salary and 50-80 percent of a public service employee’s monthly salary. Finally, unprecedented livestock asset losses estimated to about half of the pre-crisis situation affecting livelihoods leading to irreversible coping strategies. (Food security sector narrative – HNO version). The agricultural sector has been particularly affected by the crisis, especially in Idleb governorate. Households living in rural areas depend on agriculture as their main livelihood. Over 75% of rural households grow food for their own consumption and more than 1/3 of the rural households rely on their own production for over a 1/4 of their food requirements. However, due to higher production and marketing costs, and constrained purchasing power, 90% of HH spend half of their income on food, against a 25% pre-crisis. While prior to the crisis 60% of HH had access to finance from any source, this % now amounts to 25. Rural farming and herding have been the most affected by the crisis. The destruction of vast areas of agricultural land reflected in a shortage of inputs for farmer, who can no longer afford to purchase good quality seeds, fertilizers and fuel to power irrigation pumps due to high prices. In addition, agricultural assets, including processing facilities, were damaged and are in need of rehabilitation. The sector which accounted for the highest proportion of damage was the livestock sector (manifested in the value of livestock death). The loss of livestock by breeders’ households is one of the main concern of the sector. (Food security sector narrative – HNO version). In spite of all this, the agricultural sector remains a safety net in a context of insecurity, market closures and disruptions. However, it needs to be supported. Key priorities for farmers to resume their agricultural production remain: 1) input provision; 2) credit, marketing and processing support and asset repair. Therefore, it is crucial to build farmers’ capacity to sell their own production through value chain approaches (post-harvest management, food processing, preservation and marketing) and to promote the development of income-generating activities. (Agriculture in Syria after 6 years of crisis, FAO, 2017).

Implementation of activities

The proposed intervention aims at strengthening the early recovery and enhance resilience of conflict-affected populations in Northwest Syria through a restoration of rural and urban livelihood. This will be achieved through the following: Restoration of agricultural livelihood opportunities through individual and community-level interventions • Distribution of restricted commodity vouchers agricultural vouchers to mid-size crop farmers. Vouchers will be redeemed in exchange of good quality seeds, fertilizers and tools. Farmers will also take place to theoretical and practical training on climate-smart agriculture, best agricultural practices, including through Farmers Field School. • Distribution of three rounds of fodder vouchers to livestock owners. The value of the voucher will be proportionate to the livestock held by the farmers. Each voucher will cover the fodder value for two months. Livestock holders will also take part in trainings which will focus on how to prepare fodder in order to support a more sustainable and self-sufficient agricultural sector that does not have to rely on expensive, inaccessible or internationally funded inputs to operate. • Support to agricultural entrepreneurs through cash grants and training. In additional of a cash grants, ACTED will provide ongoing mentorship to agricultural entrepreneurs in order to help them creating connections within their own community to foster the growth and development of their start-up business. • Rehabilitation of community-based productive assets (bakeries, mills, nursery plants, seeds laboratories). Agricultural and productive assets will be identified through a participatory process which will involve the targeted communities. • Rehabilitation of irrigation systems. Through this activity, ACTED aims at addressing one of the most critical challenges faced by farmers, which is accessing water for irrigation. This in turn contributes to increase farmers’ yield. Restoration of urban livelihood opportunities • Support to small and medium-size existing businesses through cash grants and training aimed at enabling them to restore or expand their business, including by purchasing new furniture but also by learning new marketing techniques. • Provision of vocational training to men and women through the TVET centre. Training will be gender-disaggregated and include: mechanical, electronic subjects for men and tailoring, office package, administrative classes for women.

Risk: Low participation of women in the projectLikelihood: Medium – Impact: HighMitigation measures: The proposed intervention has been designed and will be carried out in accordance with ACTED gender strategy. All along the project implementation, ACTED team will ensure the dignity and safety of women is at all-time respected and ACTED will strongly encourage the inclusion of women in the activities. For all activities, the inclusion of vulnerability criteria will enable encourage the participation of female-headed households where they wish to participate in this project. The specific needs of female-led MSME owners and entrepreneurs have been well assessed through ACTED’s past and current Livelihoods programming, and have been fully integrated into the design of this project. It was clear from the past ACTED program that ACTED has to ensure that both genders fell comfortable in the business training classes, and therefore ensure that the trainings will be conducted by both a male and female trainer/training assistant. The main objective is that women feel at ease asking questions of their trainers and safe working on their 'action plan' with them directly one on one at their desks. Women entrepreneurs will be supported for a wide variety of businesses, and not just those types that are stereotypically known to be well-suited for women. However, ACTED will support existing businesses and therefore it is impossible to say at this stage what type of business these women own. It is to be noted that from past programs support MSMEs, the majority of women owned a tailor shop, a hairdressing salon, or a clothes shop. Risk: Lack of meaning full access for the most vulnerable groups Likelihood: Medium – Impact: HighMitigation measures: The special needs of women and girls, elderly and people living with disabilities will be taken into account throughout the implementation of the activities, and in particular during the phase of beneficiary selection, conducted by Programme Team and ACTED AMEU. ACTED will ensure that distribution and training sites are safe, easily accessible. During distribution, separate lines will be arranged for women. In addition, the particular necessities of pregnant and lactating women, female head of household, and elderly or disabled women will be taken into account, and should they be unable to reach the distribution sites, they will be able to obtain the kits by proxy.
