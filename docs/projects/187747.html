Project Summary

The conflict has widely disrupted markets and livelihoods, with negative consequences worsening year after year as the conflict ensues. Typical livelihood strategies – in particular agricultural systems – have been eroded or completely destroyed. Many families have been left without traditionally male breadwinners, leaving female heads of household to enter the labor market – in many cases for the first time, with lack of access to education, training and experience, and conservative social norms limiting opportunities. The main changes at HH level identified by participants were an increase in HH members contributing to HH income, including negative coping strategies engaged in by women and children. PIN will support communities in Northern Syria to enhance market systems and empower vulnerable, conflict-affected households to launch viable income-generating activities to meet their basic needs through activities including Cash for Work (CfW), vocational training, apprenticeships, and business grants targeting at-risk households, including those resorting to negative coping strategies. Cash for work approaches empower beneficiaries to work for the cash assistance received, promoting resilience and dignity while supporting communities and stimulating local markets. PIN favors high-value CfW activities that support broader communities by providing services, infrastructure, and/or production of products for sale or donation. PIN’s cash for work approach covers the most vulnerable HH for a minimum of three months, supporting them to meet their basic needs while minimizing resort to negative coping strategies. Initiatives supported will include public infrastructure rehabilitation and activities explicitly intended to support women, including education-oriented, knitting and sewing activities. Furthermore, PIN will implement integrated Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET), Small Business Grants (SBGs) and Apprenticeship placements with local businesses. Target populations will include vulnerable male and female heads of household within host, IDP and returnee communities. PIN aims to provide the most needed assistance to such groups by employing a wide spectrum of different activities to meet distinct needs, establish beneficiary selection criteria to prioritize the inclusion of those most vulnerable and adapt its assistance delivery to promote access for the most vulnerable people. PINs programmes are carried out in coordination with local authorities at various levels – from cross-border to hub, to district and village level. PIN engages in regular dialogue with authorities to ensure transparency, accountability, and protection mainstreaming. This includes consultations and briefings on needs, humanitarian principles, PIN procedures, and beneficiary selection at the planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation stages.
Furthermore, PIN will implement financial grants and comprehensive skills building support, aimed at creation or restoration of livelihoods. Target populations will include vulnerable male and female heads of household within host, IDP and returnee communities, while taking into consideration the sustainability of support, by multi-faceted targeting incorporating also business-aptitude of targeted beneficiaries.

4 - Needs

Recent PIN's assessments across Northern Syria highlighted that works for infrastructure repairs remain a significant priority across all assessed communities, ranking Cash for Work (CfW) in the top 3 priority assistance modalities. In addition, beneficiaries need to start their own business and income-generating activities and acknowledge the need for employment opportunities. PIN engages with the private sector actors to identify the employment opportunities in different sectors and adapt the activities to respond to these needs. The lack of capital, high raw materials prices, and poor living conditions are the main barriers to business creation and income generation for men and women. Apart from the overall strengthening of infrastructure and markets and equitable access to essential services, access to raw materials and supplies is highly needed at an individual level. In addition, promoting community acceptance of women's economic empowerment is recommended, facing additional social barriers obligations and child-care preventing participation in income-generating activities. This problem is particularly severe in Raqqa, where there are high rates of widowed/divorced women, and FHH depends on support from the community or child labor to survive. PIN focuses on the inclusion of host, IDP, and returnee communities equally and based on objective needs and vulnerability criteria will diminish potential social tensions.

5 - Implementation of activities

PIN aims to strengthen access to livelihood by creating income-generating opportunities and improving access to production and market infrastructure to restore local economic recovery through Cash for Work, Business Grants, TVET, and Saving Groups. CfW targets the most vulnerable in activities based on eligibility, ensuring the inclusion of PwDs, and activities designed for women specifically. The workers are employed for 12 weeks (60 days), 5 days/week, according to local labor market trends. The TVET cycles will last 3 months, 4 hours a day, 5 days weekly; an equivalent of a part-time job, allowing beneficiaries to maintain other income-generating activities and domestic or child care responsibilities.The best TVET graduates will be selected for SBGs, prioritizing the SGBs that thematically match the vocational training, combining skills building (Business Planning and management, financial skills, marketing skills. A call to submit business proposals will be announced, and a committee will select the best candidates. The apprenticeship component is designed to partner with potential employers and foster opportunities and a start-up kit. PIN plans to integrate saving components into the business creation support, to enhance sustainability and self-reliance of the supported beneficiaries. Standalone self-help and saving groups will also be created for women, specifically.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

PIN’s protection mainstreaming strategy for 2019-2021 carries out regular protection risk assessments, focusing on all assistance modalities. These protection risk assessments feed into the development of protection risk and mitigation matrices. The following major risks and respective mitigation measures (MM) were identified: 1)R: Most vulnerable are not selected for assistance, 1)MM: targeting criteria communicated with the communities and LCs; advertisement in targeted public places; use of Washington groups Questions and advocacy for inclusion of PwDs by the local councils.;100% verifications of the beneficiaries; FGDs with representatives from a cross-section of the community, comprehensive advertisements and communication channels for PIN’s CFRM, 2)R: intergroup tensions over assistance; 2)MM: Community stakeholder meetings, harmonization of the packages across partners, Provision of assistance to host communities and IDPs. 3)R:PSEA 3)MM: Training of all staff and trainers; Gender balanced teams, Clear process to address PSEA case swiftly, Signs are posted throughout project sites stating that assistance is free. 4)R: Project sites are not accessible/safe, 4)MM: distribution of protective equipment for CfW, collaboration with Security and Access teams for timing and place of CfW activities, Accessibility adjustments of TVET centers to ensure access of PwDs.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

PSEA considerations are integral part of PIN protection mainstreaming strategy. PSEA trainings are conducted to all new staff as part of the protection mainstreaming trainings. PSEA is also integral part of the Code of conducted signed with staff, local authorities, daily workers, and other stakeholders involved in PIN projects. Protection risks and mitigations matrix is developed for all the activities implemented by PIN in order to ensure that PIN has well-thought identification of possible gaps and standardize the precautionary and preventive measures required.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

(A1/R1): Security situation in target areas remains conducive to implementation;(MM1): Suspend/relocate activities to areas where access is upheld; and/or switch to remote management, potentially through pre-identified local partners. (A2/R2) PIN’s staff, offices and warehouses in Syria are not subjected to targeting/abductions/raiding by armed actors. (MM3): rigorous security monitoring and management mechanism. If specified risks occur, PIN temporarily suspends activities; conduct internal/external investigations; advocate for dialogue with actors, inform counter-terrorism agencies/authorities/partners of incidents; and seek to relocate the intervention;(A3/R3): LCs/others continue to collaborate in line with principles. (MM3): Temporarily suspend activities; engage in dialogue to re-sensitize on principles of engagement. If such action fails, PIN relocates resources to other areas. (A4/R4): Armed actors present in areas of operation facilitates safe humanitarian access; (MM4): Suspend operations, advocacy with local authorities and armed actors (jointly with UNOCHA, NGO forums, Working Groups) to restore free and unhindered access. If such efforts were unsuccessful, relocate activities to areas facing acute needs; (A5/R5): Local markets remain functional; (MM5): If border crossings to Syria were closed/restricted or PIN was prevented from transferring goods, advocate with UNOCHA/local authorities to facilitate access; purchase locally if possible; shift to Cash programming
