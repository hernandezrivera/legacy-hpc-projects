Project Summary

In most societies, women have limited space to meet, and public spaces are often inhabited largely by men. Traditionally, women’s responsibilities include taking care of children, cooking, carrying out household chores, and generally looking after the family. While these roles may change during crisis, where women may find themselves working or becoming the breadwinner, they remain responsible for the household nevertheless. For many girls the opportunity to move freely in the community becomes limited at the onset of puberty . Parents often keep their daughters inside the house, protected from any contact with males. This unofficial restriction on female mobility tends to persist throughout life.
In the Syrian context, women have become more isolated as a consequence of the crisis. Their mobility has been curbed significantly. Women and their family members reported having limited movement of women and girls outside the home due to fear of sexual violence, harassment, and indiscriminate attacks
women and girls reported perceived as using community resources to which they are not entitled, makes them particularly vulnerable. Further, they noted that women had to take on roles they had not played it before, such as working or going out to get aid. While freedom of mobility was somewhat limited for many women and girls prior to displacement, increased fear of sexual assault and harassment has placed even further restrictions on displaced women and girls.
The Safe spaces GAN seeks to build will helps to reduce risks and prevent further harm during acute emergency responses. These spaces provide women and girls with a safe entry point for services and a place to access information. Safe gathering points also offer them an opportunity to engage with each other, exchange information, and rebuild community networks and support. In this way, safe spaces can be a key way of building women and girls.
The center will be attended by approximately 500 women and girls divided by age groups (10-17) Adolescents who are least likely to enroll in formal education and most vulnerable to forced marriage Early marriage, extreme recruitment and adult girls (18-24) are most vulnerable to health problems, domestic violence, (25-60) who are most vulnerable to reproductive health problems and problems of family planning and child-rearing.

Needs

The conflict in Syria has claimed the lives of hundreds of thousands of people, displaced half the population and left 13.1 million people in need of humanitarian assistance. Half of the displaced, and half of those in humanitarian need are youth. Amid the chaos, young women and adolescent girls become particularly vulnerable to economic deprivation, Gender Based Violence (GBV) and the devastating consequences of limited mobility and isolation brought about by the breakdown of community structures.According to the findings of the available assessments, the protection environment in Syria remains characterized by many of the protection issues chosen for analysis, with frequency of occurrence across sites and population groups, as well as some fixed patterns. In 48% of the assessed communities, the consulted population reported at least four protection cases. In parallel, qualitative sources of protection are a crucial source of information to illustrate the interrelatedness of different protection issues, and should therefore be addressed in a comprehensive manner.Assessments indicated that gender-based violence, particularly sexual harassment, early / forced marriage, domestic violence (including intimate partner violence and other forms of domestic violence against women and girls) continued to dominate the lives of women and girls in Syria from home and abroad, Very few places where women and girls feel safe. The long-standing crisis and deep social norms, combined with the exacerbation of lawlessness in some regions, lead to the normalization of this violence, resulting in harmful social psychological well-being and the continued erosion of the rights of women and girls. The fear of sexual harassment (on the way to school, the market, distribution sites, etc.), as well as sexual violence, often associated with abduction, is a concern for women and girls who are consulted and contribute to psychological stress and social constraints. Their movements. In addition, different technology means - for example. Using text messages and social media - for sexual harassment and facilitating early / forced marriage. Restrictions on the freedom of movement of women and girls deny access to services and humanitarian assistance and ultimately reduce their rights, while widows who suffer from divorce, widowhood and adolescent girls are disproportionately affected. Shame and shame Sexual violence means that survivors do not often talk about violence when it happens. Families arrange marriages for girls, believing that they will protect them and reduce the financial burden on the family. Young girls were reportedly married, with early / forced marriages - sometimes involving girls aged 10 or 11 - as a protectionist strategy. The socio-economic situation, the lack of livelihood opportunities and the eventual increase in poverty increase the vulnerability of women and girls to all forms of violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse.Safe spaces for women and girls serve as a place to seek safety and access to basic life-saving services (health, psychological, social and legal support) and other necessary services and skills training. 2019 MSNA: respondents in 45% of assessed communities reported early marriage to be occurring for adolescent girls’; 29% of respondents in assessed communities reported early marriage for adolescent boys. In 6 governorates respondents in over 80% of assessed communities reported the occurrence; in 9 governorates over 60%.

Protection Risk Analysis

Given the sensitivity of addressing GBV in the country and the protection risks related to backlashes from sections of the community and on GBV messaging, GAN will continue to engage with the community to mitigate this risk and to build acceptance. By engaging with the community, GAN can ensure the GBV messaging is appropriate and sensitive to the context and culture. Although GAN’s strategy is primarily focused on empowering women and girls, men and boys will also be targeted. IEC campaigns and outreach activities focused on building trust with men and boys will be developed to minimize the risks of women living in male headed households being refrained from accessing the WGSS. Moreover will be Provision of training and awareness to men and boys on basic concepts of GBV. The unequal power structures between men and women and notions of traditional gender roles in Syria means there is a risk GBV programming can create tensions within communities and families, especially between men and women. Risk can originate in family or community backlash towards women participating in recreational, vocational or empowerment activities, in the form of stigmatisation, reprisals and further trauma. To mitigate this risk, GAN conducts reassessments of activities, including safety audits and FGDs regarding Protection and GBV, as well as community and individual coping mechanisms, mitigate the risks of further harm within existing services and referral systems. Specific staff members will be training as key resource points, as well as a remote monitoring system, will ensure training and service quality will not be impaired.Persons with disability have difficult accessing GBV servicesProvide transportation support for persons with disability Breach of confidentiality putting at risk SGBV survivors due to lack of capacity on information management of service providersMitigating Measures: Training of service providers on principles of safety and confidentiality, including strong awareness on the consequences of the potential information’s disclosure; adequate follow up of measures to keep confidential the information; establish good protocols for any data storage. Exploitation by service provider or by other actor in the community.Mitigating Measures:- Ensure beneficiaries know services where to disclose if violence occurs- Raise awareness on PSEA and other types of exploitation and where to report Pressure on the service provider from the local authorities on who should or should not receive the service.Mitigating Measures: Establish good relation with local authorities, clarify humanitarian principles and stick to them.
