Project Summary

The proposed project will provide immediate food security and livelihoods assistance to 2000 IDPs representing 12,000 women, girls, boys and men in stress and crises in Banadir Region to access to essential food items through food vouchers for three months. In addition, the project will provide Restocking to 400 rural HHs and farm input to 2000 HH farmers and pastoralists in Lower Shabelle and Hiran regions for recovery and resilience, benefitting around 14,400 women, girls, boys and men affected by floods and droughts to protect assets and generate income for their households. The project will also train 300 farmers and 100 pastorals for the best farming technologies and animal husbandry.
WARDI will rehabilitate through CFW 25km feeder roads affected by floods in Hiran and L/Shabelle regions, benefiting 1000 HHs or 6,000 women, girls, boys, and men to rehabilitate productive assets; in Hiran and Lower Shabelle regions.
During the project implementation, WARDI will maintain regularly: The visibility of the project activities; communication and coordination mechanisms with the FS Cluster partners in the target districts, the Local Authorities and with the FS cluster and OCHA and Keep the project documents in safe custody with good quality. WARDI project staff will undertake supervision and monitoring of the project activities on daily basis during the project implementation.

Needs

Recent climatic improvements and the humanitarian assistance have led improvements in food security. Still, acute food insecurity, malnutrition and livelihood needs remain. Recent FSNAU seasonal assessment indicates severe levels of acute food insecurity and malnutrition among IDPs. The residual impact 2016/17 droughts, combined with floods, displacement, evictions, conflicts, etc., in Hiran, Banadir and L/Shabelle regions continue to undermine opportunities for food security improvements. The forecasted El Nino phenomena and associated abnormal rainfall in last quarter of 2018 is expected to risks of shocks. The expected above normal rains may cause the deterioration of IDPs situation that are already in a precarious situation. The assessment also indicated that: (1) Hiran: 41,000 people are IPC 3 and need urgent humanitarian assistance and livelihood support. 114,000 people who are IPC 2 also require livelihood support. 11,000 people are (IPC 4), while 2000 in Belet Weyne IDPs & urban are in IPC 5 and need urgent food security and livelihood support. (2) L/Shabelle the food security is improved, but around 123,000 people are IPC2 and require livelihood support. (3) Banadir: 189,000 and 456 people are IPC 3 and IPC2 respectively and need urgent humanitarian assistance and livelihood support, while 30,000 people are (IPC 4) and 5,000 IDPs in Mogadishu are in IPC 5 and need urgent food security and livelihood support. In general, rural livelihoods in Hiran (riverine, agro-pastoral) and IDPs in Mogadishu are in need of urgent nutrition and health support interventions. Under these circumstances, the recent food security improvements, risk deterioration if the humanitarian assistance is not sustained particularly among vulnerable population groups. Assistance should be sustained including livelihood support to make lasting improvements to the food security situation.

Activities

1. Provide Food Vouchers to enhance the food security to 2,000 IDP & Host Community HHs in Banadir Region for 3 months, benefitting around 12,000 men, women, girls and boys. With community and Local Authority support, select and register the 2000 beneficiaries with full details. Identify & select Food Vendors to supply planned food items for the IDPs near their settlements and ensure the location and access routes to shops are safe with enough. Distribute the planned food items equivalence to US$100/HH to the 2,000 beneficiaries at the selected vendors’ shops on monthly basis. Undertake monitoring & supervision of the food distribution events at each shop. 2. Distribute farm input to 2000 farmers in Hiran and L/Shabelle, benefiting around 12,000 men, women and children to start their farming activities destroyed during the recent flooding and not received any external assistance and provide 2000 ha machinery land preparation (3hours/ha) to the target 2000 farmers. 3. Restock 400 HHs, who lost their animals during drought and floods (200HH in Hiran & 200 HHs in L/Shabelle regions with shoats, benefitting around 2,400 men, women, girls and boy (5 heads/HH).4. Conduct training to 300 farmers (100 men and 100 women) and 100 pastoralists ( 50men and 50 women) on best farming technologies and methodologies and animal husbandry respectively. 5. Rehabilitate 35 km flood affected feeder roads in L/Shabelle and Hiran regions with CFW through voucher to 1000 skilled and unskilled workers (500 in Hiran and 500 in L/Shabelle). Each unskilled worker will receive US$5/day and the skilled workers will receive US$10/day for 3 months. The beneficiaries will take directly from the nearest money vendor to avoid risk of looting.WARDI will maintain regularly: The visibility of the project activities; communication and coordination mechanisms with regional FS Cluster partners, the Local Authorities and OCHA; Keep project documents in safe custody with good quality

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The project activities include rehabilitation of productive assets like irrigation canals destroyed by recent floods in Hiran to boost crop production in the area as well as clearance of roads linking villages to the main market to improve access to traffic and human movements in Lower Shabelle. The rehabilitation of canals and roads with CFW contribute to income injection to the communities to improve their livelihoods and benefit from their potentiality to address their own needs in a productive and sustainable manner. Both canals and roads are communally owned, used and maintained. After the project ends the communities will sustain the interventions in a participatory and supportive manner by contributing individual capacity in kind or in cash. The project will also boost the livelihood assets for resilience and recovery to the farmers and pastoralists through provision of agricultural and livestock support. This will support the beneficiaries to start their livelihood activities and not depend any more to external support. WARDI will also improve their capacity to manage the small resource they received through training, in order to adopt the new technologies and methodologies for farming and animal husbandry.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Insecurity and conflict due to insurgency and clan conflict is now one of the main causes for displacement. Drought, floods and cyclone are also the root cause of the displacement in Somalia. Somalia faces a dire humanitarian situation, having endured drought and famine as well as continued fighting and heavy rains, all of which have aggravated the conditions of IDPs. People denied the rights of the IDPs as human being and they are facing evictions, rape and looting due to the fragility of the central government who was under their responsibility to protect them. Most of the IDPs left behind their livelihood assets and displaced to the big towns with empty hands. Thus, the IDPs have the right to receive necessary service, such as protection, FS, shelter, education, WASH, health and nutrition services in order to safe their lives. This project will give great allocation to benefit the resources to the IDPs. Unfortunately, conflict sometimes is preventing humanitarian agencies from delivering assistance to the IDPs. In this project, WARDI will try to reduce the burden of IDPs (women and children suffer most) and bring the FS and livelihood services near to their settlements, in order to reduce the risk of GBV against women/girls for searching needed services. The project will increase to support the livelihood and food security to the IDPs. WARDI will also include the IDPs (giving women IDPs their share) to the established committees for the project implementation to get their views and to be heard their complains if arise. The IDP rights will be availed in the course of the project implementation.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Target communities always compose of different categories or groups of people whose needs are different, including men, boys, women, girls, elderly, disabled persons, etc. During WARDI intervention, WARDI will consider the needs of these people's rights in an equitable and fair manner, because each and every person has full right to address his/her human rights to live in peace, prosperity with dignity. When targeting the project beneficiaries, special attention will be given to the Elderly. In many cases, also unaccompanied ones by any others; female-headed households, pregnant and lactating women (PLW), families with large number of children, widows with no partner/husband, orphans families, as well as disabled persons, male or female members of the target households will be given an account. WARDI will take these actions during the project implementation:1. Prioritize safety & dignity and avoid causing harm2. Promote equitable and impartial access to all3. Ensure accountability to affected populations, and 4. Strengthen participation and empowerment of the target beneficiaries.Hence, regardless of age, Gender, status, origin, or clan or sub-clan of the beneficiaries, resources will be availed accordingly, in order to let them meet their own rights as HUMANS. Failure to do so, means lack of Transparency, Accountability & Justice for all.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Security in Somalia is dynamic and the districts security focal point will analyze security situation and advice the frontline staff the situation every morning. It is anticipated that the security will remain calm and allow smooth implementation of activities within the project time frame. WARDI will identify the risk of conflict in the project area and immediately avert this risk, as food and CFW based humanitarian intervention is important to the drought/flood affected community as conflict of interest starts in the course of the selection of the beneficiaries, WARDI will closely engage the local leaders, government officials and the community including women representatives to partake the selection process, in order to reduce the risk of conflict. WARDI will adhere the principles of "do no Harm" through not creating any partial, nepotism, conflict oriented and sensitive issues within the project beneficiaries. WARDI will adhere to these principles of “do no harm” at all times throughout the project cycle by involving all categories of the community. WARDI is currently implementing WASH, food security, Education, Nutrition and Health projects in Hiran, Banadir and L/Shabelle regions with out any security problem, although sometimes there are security issues in L/Shabelle region. Safety and security for the project staff and beneficiaries are our top priority, there will be security briefing every day during staff meeting to minimize risk. The project will be implemented in accessible villages/camps to reduce risk of walking long distance to women and girls. Rapid needs assessment and technical assessments were done in the target locations by WARDI staff without security challenges.
