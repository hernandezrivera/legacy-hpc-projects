Project Summary

The situation in Gaza is known as being one of the most prominent protracted crises. The Gaza strip is characterized as having one of the highest population density in the world; according to the United Nations Relief and works agency (UNRWA), Gaza is home to approximately 1.9 million people, out of which 1.4 million (73.6%) are Palestinian refugees. The Gaza strip has been under land, sea, and air blockade since 2007, imposed by Israel.
The health system in the Occupied Palestinian Territories is under severe pressure because of the occupation, the blockade, rapid population growth, lack of adequate financial resources and shortages of basic services. Restrictions on the movement of people and materials, including medical resources, have led to a serious deterioration in the availability and quality of health services, according to UNOCHA. Access to medical care is therefore difficult for all patients - for example, there are 2.2 doctors per 1,000 inhabitants in Palestine, which is assumed to be less in Gaza-Strip. The political, social, and economic context of Palestine is considered major underlying causes for the lack of national comprehensive health care in Palestine. Years of socioeconomic decline, conflict and closure have left the health sector across the Gaza strip lacking adequate physical infrastructure and training opportunities. Facilities are overstretched, and service is frequently interrupted by power cuts. These challenges combined with the restricted movements of the population moving in and out of Gaza impose threats on the health of the population.
Eye care provision to the Palestinians has been hugely affected by the Spread of Covid-19 and the full lockdown in addition to the strict restriction of movement by Palestine and Israel; ophthalmic patients have been unable to access any of the eye care services nationally for more than six months, which have led to huge suffering of patients who could lose their sight being unable to access treatment services. The Ministry of Health has been unable to provide eye care for its own patients since the outbreak of COVID-19 and is now fully reliant on St. John, as its main referral, to tackle the urgent unmet needs of its vulnerable patients.
St. John Eye Hospital Group, despite being the single largest provider of eye care services to the Palestinian, has also experienced a dramatic drop in the number of patients at its 4 facilities in East Jerusalem (main hospital), West Bank and Gaza. The first lockdown had resulted in more than 18,000 patients missing their scheduled medical appointments for examination and clinical treatment; much worse, over 1,200 major sight‐saving surgeries were cancelled. These numbers are only representing SJEHG, and it is expected to be much higher on a national level. Many of those patients are under risk of losing their sight, either partially or fully.
The proposed one-year project which is within the objective of the Humanitarian Response Plan for 2021 is a co-fund of a larger three-year project funded by the Federal Ministry for Economic Cooperation and Development (BMZ). The project aims at strengthening the resilience of the Gazan’s vulnerable population by establishing a sustainable, free of charge in country provision of ophthalmic services to prevent avoidable blindness that will occur if timely interventions did not take place. This will be accomplished through enhancing the hospital’s surgical capacity and securing primary eye care for the Palestinians during the time of crises, taking into consideration the economic and financial burden caused by the spread of Covid-19 in Gaza and resulted in a larger poverty rates and severe shortage of essential services. Therefore, urgent aid assistance is required to respond to the urgent and essential needs of the most vulnerable groups and refugees, especially as St. John is currently dealing with thousands of uncovered patients.

1 - Needs

Israel’s nearly 13-year blockade of the Gaza Strip has severely impaired local health care. As Gaza’s collapsing health care system is grappling with a shortage of medicine, physicians, equipment and medical training, it is unable to offer many forms of treatment. Gaza’s health system was already underequipped to cope with a large outbreak of Covid-19. It is anticipated that thousands of patients under risk of blindness and visual impairment haven’t been able to receive their treatment services since March.Economically and financially, the outbreak of COVID-19 has been exhausting the financial resilience of the people, the “state” and NGOs equally, all of which has resulted in a much larger poverty rates and severe shortage of essential services. In reflection on eye care, the Ministry of Health and other referral parties have not been able to fulfill their financial obligations towards the bare minimum of the Hospital cost for referred cases, and the vast majority of patients are not able to contribute their minimal share. The Hospital, being the single largest, and the only charitable, provider of comprehensive eye care services to the Palestinian people, is under huge financial pressure to fulfill the unprecedented demand on charitable eye care service. The SJEHG is seeking support to help in treating people who will otherwise become unnecessarily blind. Despite the all the tireless efforts of St. John to absorb the needs of the people, lack of financial resources in such circumstances would exhaust the ability to provide services to all in need.On the other hand, St. John Eye Hospital Group had also witnessed a great drop in patient’s attendance to the Hospital Group’s facility in East Jerusalem, West Bank and Gaza due to the full lock-down. This has also resulted in cancelling thousands of medical appointment and around 1,200 surgical interventions cancelled. The Hospital re-opened its doors gradually following the full lockdown and it is experiencing a huge load of demandThis project targets UNRWA registered refugees and vulnerable groups as a primary target group that are not financially covered by UNRWA and they face the risk of losing their sight if timely interventions did not take place thus leading to a severe deterioration in their health status. Studies show that Visual impairment increases the risk of mortality directly and indirectly through its adverse impact on mental well-being. Prevention of disabling visual conditions remains a public health priority along with more aggressive diagnosis and treatment of depression and other mental health conditions in those living with VI, for this reason early diagnoses and treatment for the causes of avoidable blindness will decrease the likelihood of death or severe morbidity, such as disability or deterioration of physical or mental health if timely health care services were not available. Coordination between the SJEH and UNRWA will be conducted during the whole project in order to have specific and clear criteria for the selection process. Although all population groups in Gaza suffer the consequences of longstanding blockade and its socio-economic sever effects, UNRWA refugees are amongst the most vulnerable and underserved population in Gaza. In addition, the UNRWA refugee population group makes up 73.6% of the 1.9 million population of Gaza, and therefore, the project targets the largest population group. Based on the results of the RAAB study that was conducted by the SJEH to assess the Eye Health Care situation in Gaza women and people with disabilities will be prioritized within this project. SJEHG is implementing a "Gender and Disability Analysis". This analysis will be done at national level and will include data from other ophthalmic services to define a catalogue of measures and strategies. To secure inclusive and gender-sensitive activities, Women's associations, OPDs and other community programmes play a central role in the implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the proposed project. Discussions with relevant partners will determine the type of cooperation and how exactly the referrals can be secured, what barriers exists and what services to be made accessible for all. As of September 30th, the waiting list has over 900 patients waiting for surgical services only, let alone the other clinical diagnostics and treatment services, which is quite serious. Most of the patients (over 75%) are UNRWA registered patients. The Governmental hospital in Gaza, which also receives UNRWA referred patients for some geographical areas based on the agreement with UNRWA, is now receiving more COVID-19, which is another factor that will increase the waiting list further and the pressure on St. John.Until currently, we have identified and agreed with 22 CBOs representing the most vulnerable in addition to UNRWA special needs facilities. We are constantly working to identify further similar CBOs. We are also very receptive for suggestions of the Health Cluster or any of its health partners.

2 - Activities

1. Screening of patients during outreach and referrals to SJEH in Gaza cityThe outreach assignment with the mobile clinic will be carried out free of charge three days a week over a period of 12 months to provide basic ophthalmological care for women, the elderly and people with disabilities who have no access to alternative healthcare. The SJEHG assignment team will examine between 60 and 80 people per day. The outreach mission will be carried out by an ophthalmologic consultant and trained ophthalmic nurses who will receive the patients, take their medical history and visual acuity and examine them. SJEH will implement the outreach at the location of the partner (DPOs, women's centers, senior centers or rehabilitation centers). To reach the partner, the outreach team will use an equipped outreach van. The mobile outreach team will examine the patients and distribute, for example, eye drops. All patients will receive individual information sessions, which are embedded in the individual screening time. In cooperation with and with the active participation of women and organizations of people with disabilities (DPOs), SJEHG will develop questionnaires for satisfaction surveys to be distributed to 15% of the target group reached. The aim is to involve the beneficiaries and collect data on their satisfaction and opinions about the accessibility of the services offered. The data will be analyzed and, depending on the results, appropriate measures will be taken together with the community organizations. For the mobile outreach activities, a proportional budget is set for one opthalmologist and two nurses, one of whom is responsible for the registration and distribution of the questionnaires.1.1 Medication (eye drops and ointments such as Viscotears, Dexamycin, Maxitrol, Fucithalmic, Timoline, Refresh, eye pads, gauze cotton sticks and gloves) will be purchased and provided to treat patients during outreach visits.2. To examine and treat 34,863 patients at the SJEH in Gaza City during the project period with priority to Diabetic retinopathy patients.Patients register and are referred to the general eye clinic, where clinical nurses provide medical care and begin initial examinations (e.g., visual acuity tests). The results are recorded in the patient file. In a third step, patients are referred to a (general) ophthalmologist and a (subspecialized) senior ophthalmologist for initial eye examinations. After the examinations and the diagnosis, the medical staff determines the further treatment for each individual case. For the diagnosis of simple eye diseases (e.g. allergies, short-sightedness), the ophthalmologist writes prescriptions for medication or glasses for the patient. In the case of a serious eye disease, the patient is referred to the appropriate department within the hospital for further treatment. During the entire project 34,863 patients are examined. The patients treated in the hospital are patients referred by women's and disabled rights organizations or the UNRWA health centers and DR screenings, this will ensure that the patients treated are within the project criteria. The following medical personnel are budgeted for the treatment of patients:- Patient registration staff, - OCT/FAA technicians,- clinical nursing staff,- ophthalmological nursing staff, - nursing staff, - surgery nurse and- Accountant.3. Provide advanced diagnostics procedures, clinical & surgical treatment to patients in SJEH Gaza in 1 yearsDepending on the diagnosis, a total of 3,863 patients from 34,863 are referred for further diagnostic services such as fundus fluorescein angiography (FFA), eye coherence tomography (OCT) and electroretinography (ERG). The services are performed by OCT/FFA technicians and optometrists. They diagnose each case and determine the following necessary treatment accordingly. The treatment spectrum for the positively diagnosed patients is wide and includes Avastin injections, phacoemulsification (cataract surgery) and vitreo-retinal surgery, clinical and surgical treatment of glaucoma, corneal surgery, multi-laser therapy and others. All clinical procedures are performed by ophthalmic nurses and all surgical procedures are supported by surgical nurses. After surgical interventions, the visual acuity is checked again. In case of a visual impairment that could not be corrected or if necessary, internal referral to the Low-Vision Clinic and/or to appropriate rehabilitation partners is made. The surgical team consisting of a specialist, VR surgeon, ophthalmological consultant and general ophthalmologist are budgeted.3.1 Procure equipment supplies and instruments for examination and treatment at SJEH GazaIn order to be able to examine and treat patients properly, SJEH must procure equipment, consumables and instruments for examination and treatment. For the project bpsw. phaco packages, Avastin injections as well as VR instruments and other disposables and medicine are budgetedThrough this project, equipment purchased by SJEHG for usage during this project will continue to be used after the end of this project thus ensuring the sustainability of the project objectives for on the long run. 4.The Hospital has identified the employee, who will participate in the SEA committee, and a separate email will be sent to PSEA Coordinator to assign the PSEA focal point.

8 - Community engagement
8.1 - Project needs assessment

No

If not checked, please explain why (narrative text)

For this type of medical intervention, UNRWA is considered the official party representing the targeted population. According to the criteria UNRWA, jointly with St. John, will select the beneficiaries. Direct individuals in such an emergency and purely medical intervention will not specifically be involved in the decision making, but their medical files and categorization will determine their participation in an indirect way. UNRWA doesn’t provide any primary, secondary or tertiary services to its patients, and it relies solely on the provision of services of the partnering hospitals and clinics UNRWA has agreements with, including St. John. Now, for the primary and secondary services, UNRWA patients heads directly to St. John without referrals by UNRWA, and UNRWA doesn’t cover any of the cost for such services. Once patients are diagnosed, they head back to UNRWA stating their need for tertiary referral for St. John. Briefly, our current agreement with UNRWA is very limited, due to UNRWA’s on-going financial difficulties, in 3 ways; 1) it covers a very limited clinical and surgical treatment services and only for a certain geographical areas of Gaza and therefore the majority of cases for the other key services, despite being UNRWA registered refugees, are not entitled to receive coverage by UNRWA and eventually are covered by St. John, with or without a minimal contribution of patients. This places a significant amount of pressure on the financial system of St. John; 2) what makes it much more limited is that even with the services that are covered by UNRWA, the monthly ceiling/maximum of coverage by UNRWA is $5,000 (currently temporary increased to $10,000 as they got a bit of extra funding), which means that the waiting list will keep growing because we are limited by the agreement, in the number of cases we can receive. However, as a charity and a humanitarian organization, we tackle as much as we can from those major cases and we treat them, surgically and clinically, without any coverage from UNRWA, which again puts a high pressure on our financial resilience, 3) even with the limited list of approved services, the covered referrals cover less than 1/3 of the actual cost of service, which also further puts a great deal of financial burden on our charitable budget. The current system is too complicated, but eventually, St. John is taking the major bulk of treatment cost for patients and the patient contribution is almost nil or very very minimal (i.e 100 NIS out of 3200 NIS service). In order to sustain the essential services to the refugees, especially with the current COVID-19 and its economic and social consequences, we urgently need support. With such support, we will provide the services for totally uncovered patients free-of-charge, whereby no contribution from patients or UNRWA is available. To confirm again, patients will eventually receive the care free of charge.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

St. John is working in Gaza since 1992 and the local staff as well as UNRWA are aware of the social and cultural norms and the proper protocols. Based on the do-no-harm analysis the project will take into consideration the following points: • Everybody will be received in the hospital based on SJEHG mission and vision• Priority will be given to refugees, people with disabilities and women who have no alternative access to health. Information about services is therefore distributed among communities and organisations equally, to make sure that awareness about available services is adequality distributed and known• Sensitisation of SJEH staff towards inclusive services through training them on inclusive eye care and low vision to make these services inclusive for people with disabilities.• Our services will be provided in a very safe environment abiding to the Health Authorities and international standards of disinfection and social distancing to minimize infection of patients and staff. Hygiene kits are distributed in all the hospital facilities, and temperature measures are taken for every patient and staff member Following COVID-19, the Hospital Group has taken all the international standard protection and cross-infection measures in its operations, not only in Mobile outreach, but also in all our clinical settings and facilities in Jerusalem, the West Bank and Gaza. For the mobile outreach, we have set a prevention protocol/policy to protect patients and staff from infection.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In order to support vulnerable groups, a permanent system of cooperation and referral between the providers of eye care and rehabilitation services and other community-based organizations will be established, which is currently lacking in the health sector. The access to eye health services and to necessary rehabilitation services can thus be ensured for patients in the long term. The established two-way referral system (from and to SJEH) will be the first step towards establishing closer cooperation between the programs, aiming to maintain a lasting and sustainable cooperation after the end of the project.Through our 139 years of service, St. John focuses on enhancing gender equality as we consider women to have a major role in the society and have an integral part in building our future generation. In addition, wihin the larger project scope the Hospital is currently implementing a national Gender and Disability assessment, a first ever study that research obstacles and barriers that prevent or limit proper accessibility to eye care services for women, girls and people with disability. The results of the study will enable St. John to identify barriers and improve accessibility and involvement of women and people with disability in the service delivery process and cycle.
