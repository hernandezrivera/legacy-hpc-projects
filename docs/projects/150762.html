Project Summary

The proposed action is fully in line with 2019 WASH Cluster Objectives 1 and 3. The action also contributes to the Shelter Cluster Objective 2.
The action aims to work at two levels:
- Community level through life saving emergency WASH response interventions and preparedness to the most vulnerable communities through improvement their drainage water systems, domestic water quality monitoring and reducing hazards for private/public hygiene. This will also enhance service provider’ preparedness capacity and support communities’ resilience building in face of protracted threats and chronic vulnerability and especially in terms of Health and WASH.
- Household level through rehabilitation and/or upgrading of shelters, HH wastewater connection and hygiene promotion, which will reduce the exposure of targeted beneficiaries to both man-made and natural disasters, weather conditions and risk of water borne diseases and health problems, by enabling them to live meeting adequate standards.
Lack/poor drainage water infrastructure place thousands of families at a risk of displacement and destruction of their properties. This problem has even worst consequences when stormwater mixes with garbage and/or HH cesspits, polluting the surrounding environment and enhancing the existence and spread of insects/rodents causing serious illnesses. Sicknesses of HHs’ members imply an extra burden for women as family’s caregivers, as per their traditionally imposed role.
Providing adequate shelter will contribute to reduce and mitigate the effects, vulnerabilities and risks associated with living in access restricted area (ARA), especially women, children, elderly and people with disabilities (PwDs). Shelter rehabilitation and upgrading activities, will allow to advance in the fulfillment of the objectives of empowering communities, in terms of coping capacities and protection response mechanisms, enhancing their capacities to respond to protracted crisis and reinforcing the local capacities as mitigation and preparedness to environmental and man- made hazards.
The project will address the needs of acutely vulnerable HHs living in substandard overcrowded housing units, improving the accessibility to adequate and safe shelter through providing increased thermal comfort, protection from the effects of extreme climate during the summer and winter, and promoting safety, privacy, and hygiene. Particularly, the project will provide comprehensive upgrades and repairs alleviating its immediate effects, promote an adequate standard of living and create a safer environment to cope with environmental threats.
The proposed intervention will target the North Gaza Governorate, specifically Bet Lahia, Bet Hanoun, Jabalia, Jabalia Camp al Umm Al Nasser, directly benefiting 1,544 HHs, meaning a total of 9,293 individuals (2,459 girls, 2,569 boys, 2,112 women and 2,153 men) and 377,125 people in an indirect way. As mentioned above, in the target areas there is an urgent need of strengthen WASH and Shelter preparedness and response capacities to emergencies at both HH level and community level (service providers).This action is in line with the PWA Storm water management policy and is considered as high and urgent priority by CMWU, ensures access to essential services strengthening the population to cope with protracted threats, and is seen as an emergency time critical response, which is not currently addressed by CMWU and PWA intervention due to funding constraints.
The project includes the following activities based on the protection egg model:
Responsive (50% of the budget): Connection of households to the municipal wastewater system; Enhancing of operation of 16 municipal water wells
Remedial (38% of the budget): Repairing/upgrading of HHs main structures facilities (WASH and Shelter); Hygiene and environmental health awareness campaign and distribution of hygiene kits
Environmental building (12% of the budget): Provision of protective clothes and tools; Cleaning and dislodging of 15 wastewater pumping stations and wastewater networks. The shelter component aims at responding to their needs in terms of shelter and protection, and indirectly WASH and Health, and it complies with the minimum SPHERE standards for adequate housing, Shelter Cluster, minimum agreed standards, gender, and adequate access to housing by people with disabilities. The beneficiaries of this action are selected among the most vulnerable households in terms of socioeconomic and protection related vulnerabilities which are connected to inadequate shelter. The shelter rehabilitation will target 100 HHs through different techniques, to improve the shelter conditions, including protection from natural hazards and climate, and to ensure privacy and safety, to contribute to self- recovery, to reduce the risk of GBV and other protection-related risk. The assessment carried out during the proposal elaboration, highlighted the following issues: presence of old shelters in urgent need of rehabilitation, including severe electrical damage, persistent dampness, and leaking and caving roofing, and many other critical needs; incapacity of the shelters to guarantee proper summer insulation and proper hygiene conditions; need for installation of doors and windows to prevent the entrance of animals, need for privacy (especially for women who suffer from lack of privacy in their rooms and in the WASH facilities inside their homes and for boys and girls to facilitate their homework); unhealthy and unhygienic WASH conditions due to old sewage systems in their shelters. The assessments shows that the inability of the community to improve structures is due mainly to economic deprivation. The action contributes to the Shelter Cluster Objective 2 and will be implemented in coordination with stakeholders involved in the WASH & shelter sectors, including the PWA, CMWU and MoSD. Women CBOs- local implementing partners, will be responsible for HH field surveys and soft activities such as distribution of NFI, awareness sessions etc. The average cost for rehabilitation is estimated at 2,700 USD and winterization 300 USD per household

Needs

GVC conducted a WASH assessment (published in 2017), targeting both women and men, in the Gaza Strip, including ARA locations, analyzing the critical WASH needs of 3,977 HHs. It shows that in North Gaza Governorate 13% of the HHs are unconnected to wastewater networks, thus forced to use cesspits, generating black water infiltration directly to groundwater. In addition, 93%, 52% and 48% of HHs suffers from storm water accumulation/floods around their houses in Um Al Nnaser, Beit Lahia and Beit Hanun respectively.In coordination with stakeholders involved in the WASH & shelter sectors, including the PWA, CMWU and MoSD, GVC jointly with local partner women CBOs, conducted the following assessments: a) Complementary field survey on WASH and shelter conditions and needs to confirm/identify winterization preparedness needs and prone to flooding areas due to water/wastewater vulnerability, analyzing men’s and women’s situations, conducted thorough community engagement and with relevant stakeholders such as CMWU, municipalities in North Gaza Governorate, Oct 2018; b) field survey on Shelter and WASH conditions and needs in 113 HHs in North Gaza governorates, May 2018; c) Field survey on Shelter and WASH conditions and needs in 201 HHs in Um Al Nnaser, Jan 2018. Main findings are:•Winter storms and localized flooding continues to impact HHs, also caused by poor and deteriorated infrastructures•Several HHs and schools suffers from undrained stormwater due to poor drainage systems•Wastewater networks and pumping stations are only cleaned in a case of flooding or through specific funded projects•Al Zaytoon neighborhood (Beit Hanun) is unconnected to wastewater network and depends on cesspits for wastewater disposal, which cause both public health and environmental problems due to high frequent floods•HHs of 42 locations (Beit Lahia and Jabalia) are connected to overloaded cesspit•Municipalities cannot afford the increased operating costs of water wells due to the current electricity crisis•94% of the assessed HHs are extremely vulnerable in term of Shelter and WASH and are still needs shelter upgrade to the minimum standards, such as, bathroom and kitchen, water supply and storage, poor ventilation, safe electric fittings, PwDs access, and use curtains as partitions or doors for privacy.•75% of the assessed HHs live under roofs are made of rusted old sheets of galvanized/Asbestos and in need for protection from natural (harsh weather conditions/ summarization/ winterization)•89% of the assessed HHs are affected by last war 2014.•58% of the assessed HHs are connected to overloaded cesspit•88% of the assessed HHs suffer from bad hygiene conditions, bad hygiene practices and the insufficient availability of hygiene and cleaning materials•32% of children, 29% of women, and 51% of elderly HHs members rely on face insufficient and unsafe sources of drinking water•74% of sanitation facilities visited do not guarantee privacy and protection for women and childrenThe consequences of continuous cesspits overflow are 1) increasing of pollutants, 2) contamination of groundwater by increasing of nitrate in domestic water; 3) forcing HHs to continue paying the cost for cesspits vacuuming. People living in substandard housing units are remain vulnerable to a range of protection threats, particularly children, female-headed HHs, PwDs, the elderly and the chronically ill, as well as pregnant women. Women represent a vulnerable group, given the patriarchal culture that stresses women’s role in the domestic sphere, they suffer from improper and unsafe living conditions and they are more vulnerable to diseases. The same applies for children, elders and PWDs. Floods affect men, women boys and girls in different ways: delays in reaching work and school mainly for men and children, and increased workload for women with the social imposed role of taking care of hygiene of children. PwDs are also heavily impacted.

Activities

The project intends to work at two levels, strengthen the WASH & Shelter preparedness and improve response capacity to emergencies for HHs (A.2 and A.3) and for service providers (A.4). Assessment (A.1) will be conducted as monitoring tool in the initial and ending phases of the project, while awareness campaigns (A.5) will enhance the sustainability of the action. GVC will sustain coordination with the PWA, CMWU, WASH and Shelter clusters and other organizations working in the target location to join efforts and avoid overlappingA. 1. Pre/post needs assessmentConduction of a rapid assessment in order to verify the exact needs, security situation and expected objectives of the project, including: a. Technical survey for planning and designb. HHs field visits and separate focus groups with women/girls and men/boys to collect feedback and incorporate their needs in the action, most probably each group will identify different places where the problems are due to their different roles and tasks. A. 2. Connection of households to the municipal wastewater system2.1 Meeting to inform HHs’ members about the project, involving women’s participation and inputs of project activities2.2 Preparing Bills of Quantities and technical specifications along with CMWU for the construction of 7,200 meters of sewer pipeline including all required manholes and fittings2.3 Installing of 1,144 HHs’ wastewater connections and all the associated works benefiting of 8,660 peopleA. 3. Repairing/upgrading of HHs main structures facilities (WASH and Shelter)Repairing/upgrading of 100 (633 people) substandard housing units for acutely vulnerable households affected by harsh weather and flood risk including at least 10 females headed HHs. The repairs/upgrade is based on the shelter cluster minimum standards which include items to ensure physical and climate protection, privacy and dignity, improved access to HH WASH services, safety and security and reflect gender and protection concerns and special needs: house roofing, external walls and door, bedrooms, separation of bathrooms and kitchens; separation of sleeping and living spaces; access ramps and suitable installations for PwDs, handrails for stairs, LED emergency lighting; smooth and cleanable floor and wall surfaces; proper ventilationA. 4. Improving of service provider’ capacities4.1 Provision of protective clothes and tools for 100 labors required by CMWU for their operational teams for winterization prevention and responses4.2 Cleaning and dislodging of 15 wastewater pumping stations and wastewater networks in the North Gaza Governorate for prevention and responses to potential floods. Positive impacts are (i) reducing risk of temporary displacement (ii) reducing the risk of destruction of HHs’ assets4.3. Maintain the operation of 16 municipal water wells through provision and repair of chlorine dosing pumps, general maintenance of wells such as hydrocyclone and monitoring of domestic biological and chemical water quality at HH level4.4. Practical training for 12 municipality’s operators ensuring and maintaining a high level of disinfection throughout the water networksA. 5. Hygiene and environmental health awareness campaign and distribution of hygiene kitsConducting 120 public awareness and hygiene training activities and distribution of 300 hygiene kits benefiting 1,800 people. The aim is to reach the shared responsibilities and behavioral practices of the vulnerable women, men, boys and girls living in the targeted areas, promoting both sexes’ participation and to promote social mobilization and inclusive behavioral change. In the initial phase of the project a specific project committee will be established, including the participation of the women CBOs, PWA, MoSD and CMWU responsible for planning, monitoring and following up the implementation of the activities. Women CBOs, will be responsible for HH field surveys and soft activities (distribution of NFI, awareness sessions)

GVC will give priority to women notably female headed HHs, girls, boys and men needs in the rehabilitation process. GVC will consider concerns of different age categories of females and males living in the HH. Project information is ensured to male and female family members in each HH and in community sessions to ensure equal access to information. GVC’s will develop M&E plan at the beginning of the project to track performance against indicators (outputs and outcomes), timeframe and encompass key criteria for baseline/end-lines and other related studies. GVC’s community-based protection approach together with a quantitative data analysis, act as the core part of the project’s M&E system to track changes and progresses of technical implementation and beneficiaries’ (women, men, girls, boys, elderly) acceptance, knowledge and practices. Gender and protection pre/post monitoring surveys are carried out to follow up, assess the impact and incorporate mid-course corrections, if required. Data collected will be gender sensitive and disaggregated by sex for both qualitative and quantitative, using different tools, such as: 1) Baseline and end-line surveys to monitor the impact of the project.2) Monitoring visits to follow up the progress of project’s activities and their compliance with the project workplan.3) Monthly technical progress reports.4) Hygiene promotion activities will be specially monitored by pre and post KAP questionnaires.5) Focus groups discussions with different stakeholders, including, but not limited to, children, adults, elders, PwDs, community leaders and partner CBOs.6) Complains and feedback mechanism will be established at preparation phase with well-known access by targeted beneficiaries to ensure GVC accountability and participatory approach.
