1 - Needs

TDH conducted a rapid health assessment regarding the COVID-19 response and the preparedness of different hospitals and clinics across four Libyan districts (Aljabal Algharbi, Azzawya, Misurata, and Tripoli). The assessment results are based on Key Informant Interviews (KIIs) carried out by the TDH Health Unit. In total, 11 hospitals/clinics were consulted. The surveys used to facilitate the KIIs were designed to gather data on the following key characteristics of the hospital/clinic’s COVID-19 response and to identifygaps in training and supplies needed, including: 1) Number of staff and training received regarding COVID-19; 2) Number of beds, isolation rooms and intensive care facilities and equipment; 3) Protocols to avoid the spread of infection; 4) Telehealth adoption; 5) Screening methods and capacities; and 6) Availability of drugs used for treatment. The assessment results showed there was increasing concern over the lack of supplies, which included PPE, medications for symptoms relief and screening equipment. Although therespondents did not specify which types of drugs or screening equipment that were most in need, there was a general demand for CT and rapid kits for testing, despite concerns of the ecacy of non-PCR testing. The survey results also reected the scarcity of resources, which has also been stressed by the World Health Organisation (WHO). According to WHO, the Ministry of Health had received some supplies. However, limited information had been shared with the WHO regarding the distribution plans at the time of the assessment.Health workers had reportedly refused to go to work due to lack of PPE. There were also concerns regarding the mixed responses and management of funds across different municipalities, where some accepted the money, and others rejected the fund allocation by the government. Most of the hospitals/clinics surveyed showed interest in receiving the training packages outlined by TDH in the consultations. The rapid assessment also documented the importance of the National Center for Disease Control (NCDC) as a focalpoint for referrals, but also in leading health education training and/or managing different aspects of COVID-19 response such as telehealth depending on the capacity of the hospital/clinic. The overall capacity of the Libyan health sector remains extremely limited, as, in some areas, 90 percent of the healthcare facilities face a lack of resources or arecompletely closed (OCHA). The following project summary outlines the necessary interventions designed to meet the needs of the sites assessed while also allowing for additional sites to be supported based on referrals from the MOH/NCDC.

2 - Activities

Output 1: In alignment with Pillar 3, TDH will provide training to 11 hospitals and clinics across Aljabal Algharbi, Azzawya, Misurata and Tripoli that were identied within a rapid assessment conducted in April/May 2020, and will additionally serve health institutions designated by the NCDC for the technical training of staff (including MHPSS/PFA modules to enhance patient and staff care), with priority for rapid response teams. In addition to training packages, sites assessed and referred will receive critical pharmaceuticals and medical devices required to ensure rapid response teams can deploy and operate with no material constraints. PPE training and supply management guidance will be provided ahead of PPE distributions to maximize the use of the quantities distributed. Output 2: Under Pillar 2, TDH will work with the Health Sector, MOH, NCDC, MOE, MOSA, hospitals/clinics, and local councils to support and implement the national risk-communication and community engagement (RCCE) plan for COVID-19. This will include brief consultations to understand key target audiences, perceptions, concerns, inuencers, and preferred communication channels. Additionally, TDH will participate incoordination meetings with national/local authorities on risk communication strategy and activities. TDH will then prepare local messages and briey consult with the targeted communities through a participatory process, explicitly targeting key stakeholders and atrisk groups. Output 3: Under Pillar 6, TDH will work with sites identied in the rapid assessment and in-partnership with the MOH and NCDC to determine which hospitals andclinics to support in establishing isolation wards/departments. This activity will complement Output 1 and include additional training packages, pharmaceuticals, medical devices, and PPE.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

4.1Information and understanding on how complaints handling systems and feedback mechanisms function is the primary barrier as of 2020. Focused efforts will be made to educate stakeholders and beneficiaries on the function and process of these systems to encourage utilization. 4.2 Relevant accountability activities and communications as well as necessary visibility efforts will be implemented to prevent/mitigate misperceptions of inequality in service provision. Community engagement and participation and inclusive programming throughout the project cycle is another critical step that TDH utilizes to mitigate this protection risk. 4.3TDH utilizes an inclusive and participatory process with stakeholders and beneficiaries and makes efforts to ensure a balance in demographic reach as resources allow. 4.4Guidance and protocols from TDH, the cluster system, and local authorities will be used to ensure that increased exposure due to the necessity of programming does not result in harm to beneficiaries nor staff. 4.6TDH will be engaged in CM activities based on assessed/referred need and therefore will be privileged to possess sensitive information. Agency safeguarding measures and data protection policies will be adhered to at all levels internally, and agreed upon confidentiality and data-sharing procedures determined by sectors and sub-sectors will be followed. 4.8TDH will work with local authorities, stakeholders and partners to support durable solutions for person in forced displacement. 4.9TDH will maintain impartiality as required by using agency standards for assessing and determining need, and escalate with local authorities, OCHA, donors and the Libyan INGO Forum when necessary to eliminate external interference. 4.14 - A "do no harm" approach will guide our emphasis on programming acceptance, which will be achieved by working with community leaders, school staff, and parents and help to mitigate any adverse responses to activities that endanger the target population. Child safeguarding measures, including agencies procedures for responding to protection issues, will be put in place and align with sector and sub-sector standards. Membership on the Case Management Task Force will guide interventions requiring case management. Training for staff and education personnel participating in the activities will include education on these protection issues and how to support mitigation, prevention and intervention.
