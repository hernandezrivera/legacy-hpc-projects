Project Summary

based on the HNO 2022 and a comprehensive needs assessment carried out during 2020-2021 for beekeepers in North West Syria (NWS), Seeds of Hope (SOH) aims to protect and develop the livelihoods of 400 Households working in beekeeping (in Aleppo/Al-Bab & Azaz districts) by providing them with:
1- Vocational Training to enhance productivity (5 Training workshops)
2- Cash Assistant to cover operational expenses, (25$ for 400 beekeepers for 5 months)
3- Productive Assets (Beehives) to restore production rates (400 beehives / 40 Beekeeper)
4- Organize the sector by registering beekeepers (400) and tagging beehives (7000)
to create feasible income that saves their dignities and sustains their businesses and protect them from negative coping strategies under the stress of conﬂict. The project includes the study of the Honey value chain in the local context and ﬁnding solutions for main barriers hindering this social/economic activity from reaching its maximum limits. The project will provide the following services for the targeted beekeepers: 1- Support food security and operational costs for beekeepers 2- Organize the profession locally 3- Provide productive assets.
Agreement is signed with the donor, 15% of the project fund is secured, the remaining 85% will be released/transfered based on the project progress.

4 - Needs

Seeds of Hope based on HNO 2022 and in coordination with donor carried out needs assessment for beekeepers in 4/5/2021 in Aleppo Northern areas (Al bab, Azaz, Jerablus, Sharan), the survey covered sample of the beekeepers in the area, who reported the following needs:1- Covering part of the operational expenses (especially after fuel price increase which affected transporting beehives to pastures)2- Restore assets destroyed because of the conflict and other natural crisis (displacement, uncontrolled agricultural pesticides application)3- Provide training on special subjects important for local beekeepers (pest control, queens breeding)4- Organize the general practice in coordination with local councels.5- Provide beekeeping tools and honey marketing solutions for the beekeepers.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project activities will be implemented by professional agricultural engineers specialized in beekeeping, and utilizing reliable connection and relationship in the local communities targeted by the project, and according to the FSL Cluster framework to standardize the intervention. The project Intervention (activities) will focus on the following: Expert beekeeper (Consultant) will evaluate the beekeepers businesses/livelihoods from two aspects: 1- Professional practices of beekeepers in utilizing existing beekeeping assets in production (to be followed by Vocational Training whenever necessary). 2- Supporting small beekeepers to increase their production and generate suitable income, (to be achieved by distribution of production assets such as "beehives", "queens"...etc. or by providing cash assistance to cover operational expenses)

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

PSEA Risk/Threat 1: beneficiaries sexually harassed or abused in exchange for project aids, Likelihood: low, Impact: high, Mitigating Measure: Consult community throughout program cycle to determine the safest mechanism for handing over the cash/in-kind assistance, Monitoring: Enhance communication: (i) distribute brochures before/during aids distribution including hot-line numbers for reporting PSEA incidents (ii) avoid 1 to 1 connection with beneficiaries (SOH front-end team will consist of 1 male and 1 female at least), and (iii) provide training for SOH field staff, and ensure they commit to humanitarian principles and code of conduct signed after recruitment.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The project took into consideration the following risks (beyond the project's control): 1- Mass displacement from Idlib to Aleppo based on conflict escalation (SOH contributes actively with local stakeholders during crises and has plan B to shift operation to Turkish/Syrian borderline). 2- Markets may be un-accessible because of insecurity (modalities will be changed if necessary according to local security conditions/challenges). 3- Some humanitarian actors may conduct uncoordinated assistance for beekeepers without informing SOH (coordination with the local council and FSL cluster will take place before implementation).
