Project Summary

In the context of deplorable humanitarian situation in Somalia, AVORD has established this proposal that focuses on both urgent life-saving approaches for the acute phases and post-emergency options for resilience and recovery. The overarching aim is to improve the living conditions of the most vulnerable IDPs and host communities by rehabilitating 30 shallow wells while constructing 10, supplying emergency water to households struck by sudden emergencies and by constructing 500 emergency and permanent latrines where demonstrated needs exist. AVORD will also undertake 10 integrated hygiene promotion campaigns involving door-to-door sensitization, installation of posters and distribution of hygiene promotion leaflets. The campaigns will focus, among other themes, disaster risk reduction, environmental sanitation, proper hand-washing with soap and menstrual hygiene and will be conducted following distribution of WASH kits containing basic hygiene items such as female sanitary towels and soap. The process will be further complemented by emergency environmental cleaning and management through drainage and/or removal of stagnant water ponds and garbage piles. 10 WASH committees will be trained on a range of topics including community mobilization and sensitization on safe sanitation and hygiene practices.
Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), environment, gender and protection issues will be mainstreamed throughout the project cycle. On DRR, WASH facilities will be designed and constructed while considering existing disaster risk and vulnerabilities e.g. building of flood proof latrines, enhancing communities' disaster risk awareness and mitigation as part of hygiene promotion campaigns. Strategies that AVORD will adapt to promote protection levels for target beneficiaries include establishing of WASH facilities in central locations that are not more than 500 meters from settlements and provision of adequate lighting, undertaking distributions in safe and accessible locations and in daylight hours, ensuring that the hygiene kit size is appropriate for the beneficiaries to be able to carry home with no additional physical or financial burden and without being dependent on others to carry it for them and building WASH facilities in a way that can preserve the safety and dignity of users, among other strategies. AVORD will also work in coordination with protection actors to put in place measures to prevent and respond to physical/sexual violence arising from use of or lack of WASH facilities.
The design of activities will always be informed by context-specific need and protection analysis and will be completed in close coordination with target communities to promote ownership and ensure sustainability. The effectiveness and quality of the aid package will always be evaluated through monitoring exercises, field visits, and other evaluation methods thus ensuring that beneficiaries access their services safely and in a dignified manner and that anticipated outputs are realized. Elaborate feedback and complain mechanisms will be setup to ensure accountability to the effected population.

Needs

Repetitive natural disasters and conflict continue to sustain humanitarian crisis in Somalia. In addition to vulnerable host communities, the country is home to one of the largest Internally Displaced People (IDPs) in the world. According to a recent UNHCR bulletin, there was an estimated 2.6 million IDPs in Somalia by end of August 2018. The number continues to grow due to conflict, forced evictions and natural disasters. In August 2018 alone, up to 53,000 people were newly displaced.Both vulnerable host communities and IDPs live under deplorable living conditions characterized by chronic lack of basic necessities. Data from JMCNA that was carried out in 48 districts indicates that 47% of assessed households have insufficient water for household purposes, as measured against the SPHERE standards of 15 litres per person per day; 41% rely on unprotected water sources that are more susceptible to contamination which can increase the risk of transmission of waterborne diseases. Only 13% of assessed households across Somalia reported that they treat their drinking water and over 52% have indicated that they wash their hands with water only. Open defecation is practiced by as many as 39% of the population due to lack of access to sanitation facilities and awareness. This is one of key factors driving cholera outbreaks. As per the health cluster, 39 new cholera cases has been reported during the week of 13th – 19th August 2018 of which 72% were children below the age of 5. This puts the number of recorded cholera cases to 6,169 including 41 deaths since December 2017. Lack of access to sanitation facilities does not only contribute to diseases outbreaks but also exposes women to harassment and different forms of gender-based violence as they walk to distant locations for defecation. Due to poor awareness, IDPs often don't follow basic hygiene practices which further contribute to widespread disease outbreaks in settlements.

Activities

AVORD seeks to improve the living conditions of 15,000 vulnerable DPs and host communities in Somalia by undertaking the following activities:1) 10 new shallow wells and 30 rehabilitated to enhance access to water and promote the resilience of affected population. The process will engage target communities in cash for work schemes to have the ripple effect of enhancing food security among the target populations.2) Emergency water supply for 3,000 IDPs and host communities in humanitarian crisis. Where market conditions allow, AVORD will employ a cash-based approach enabling beneficiaries to use vouchers to source water from local vendors. Each household will receive 15 liters of water per day for survival during emergencies.3) Emergency environmental cleaning and management through drainage and/or removal of stagnant water ponds and garbage piles.4) Construction of 500 dislodgeable latrines with hand-washing facilities. Women and girls will be involved in the technical design of latrines to address their unique needs for privacy and protection. Separate latrine blocks will be constructed for men & women with entrance on opposite sides and clearly marked signs in the Somali language. Consultations will be made with key local stakeholders to ensure that latrines are built in sites where eviction is unlikely. Latrines will be lockable from the inside with adequate lighting to further enhance protection levels for IDP woman and girls. 5) AVORD will also undertake 10 integrated hygiene promotion campaigns involving door-to-door sensitization, installation of posters and distribution of hygiene promotion leaflets. The campaigns will focus, among other themes, disaster risk reduction, environmental sanitation, proper hand-washing with soap and menstrual hygiene and will be conducted following distribution of WASH kits containing basic hygiene items 6) Training of 10 WASH committees, Community mobilization and sensitization on safe sanitation and hygiene practices.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

This proposal is humanitarian in nature focusing on provision of life-saving WASH assistance to IDPs and host communities in humanitarian crisis. However, it also contributes to building the resilience of targeted communities by rehabilitating and constructing water supply systems including shallow wells in strategic drought affected locations and/or cholera hot spots which contribute to overall improvement in living conditions and accessing livelihoods hence leaving beneficiaries more resilient to future shocks. Sustainable sanitation facilities will promote reintegration of protracted IDPs. To maximize impact and sustain project outcome, AVORD will seek integration with other clusters providing response in areas where proposed activities will be implemented. Details pertaining to project activities will be shared through relevant clusters and 4W reporting matrix to avoid duplication of effort. Local communities will play a key role in different phases of the project to create sense of ownership which will contribute to sustaining of project outcomes. AVORD will strive to build local capacities thus ensuring that various community leaders and committees are able to sustainably maintain resources following project completion and handover. Disaster risk reduction and environmental sanitation will be mainstreamed in project cycle to ensure that proposed interventions build communities' resilience and coping mechanisms to future shocks. The Do-No-Harm principals will be adapted through continuous contextual analysis and exploring how various activities interact with target communities to avoid un-intended negative consequences.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

IDPs may face exclusion from gate-keepers who try to hamper their access to the assistance provided by humanitarian actors. Besides, certain power dynamics within IDP settlements may sometimes lead to the exclusion of IDPs from marginalized communities. AVORD will prevent this by undertaking direct identification and registration of beneficiaries without use of middle men or gate keepers. AVORD will also apply systematic beneficiary identification and collection of adequate household profile before the intervention to ensure that only the most vulnerable and marginalized IDPs are registered for assistance. Forced eviction remains a constant threat facing the IDPs in Somalia. To mitigate this, AVORD will advocate for the construction of permanent shelters only in public land or in areas where land tenure is secured for a minimum of 10 years and obtain relevant legal documents e.g. land titles. AVORD will also work closely with local authorities to address the impact of eviction on IDPs while identifying the groups who could be more prone to eviction during need assessments.Finally, there is a likelihood of IDP and host community tensions resulting from the intervention. This will be mitigated by being transparent with the target communities and providing them with timely information on the selection criteria, organisational procedures and processes that affect them. Joint IDP-host community committees will be established to participate in project design and implementation.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Exclusion based on gender and age could happen in IDP settings if adequate preventive measures are not taken. For example, in an environment of male dominance, women and girls could be easily left-out or disproportionately registered during beneficiary identification and selection processes. To prevent that, AVORD will adapt a systematic approach to beneficiary selection with a clear criterion to prioritize the most vulnerable in target communities including unaccompanied children, pregnant and lactating mothers, single headed-households, persons with chronic illness or psychological condition and persons at risk as identified by protection officers. Data collected to inform beneficiary selection will be desegregated by gender to ensure that no group is left-out. Other strategies used to mitigate gender based violence include 1) Establishing WASH facilities in central locations that are not more than 500 meters from settlements and provision of adequate lighting. No facilities will be established near military installations or high risk areas. 2) Undertaking distributions in safe and accessible locations and in daylight hours thus ensuring that beneficiaries have sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. 3) Ensuring that the hygiene kit size is appropriate for the beneficiaries to be able to carry home with no additional physical or financial burden and without being dependent on others to carry it for them. 4) Building WASH facilities in a way that can preserve the safety and dignity of users. For example, separate toilets and bathing facilities for males and females, with clearly marked signs in pictorial form for illiterate users and ensure they are used by the indicated sex. 5) Working in coordination with protection actors to put in place measures to prevent and respond to physical/sexual violence arising from use of or lack of WASH facilities.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Repetitive flare-up of armed conflicts in Somalia may hinder access to areas targeted by this project. AVORD will mitigate that by continuously assessing local dynamics and making plans accordingly. Where necessary, relief items will be pre-positioned in strategic locations where feasible to respond to humanitarian emergencies in the event of restricted access. To prevent exposing beneficiaries to risk, AVORD will ensure that access routes to distribution points and services are safe with enough space for orderly and safe distribution. The Do-No-Harm principals will be observed at all times to prevent un-intended negative consequences.
