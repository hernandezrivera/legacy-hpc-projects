Project Summary

Opportunities and needs for improved Early Recovery intervention on the ground are growing. This necessitates additional efforts to improve collaboration at the national and sub-national levels by allocating human resources to help the growing membership of the Early Recovery Sector in undertaking comprehensive Early Recovery assessment and planning as well as ensuring successful coordination in sector partners’ collective response. Furthermore, given the wide range of Early Recovery initiatives across many line ministries and the sectors they serve, it is critical to continue advocate for the early recovery approach within the local administrations, at the relevant decision-making and operational levels to ensure continuous knowledge exchange on assessed needs and objectives, as well as to expedite clearances needed for implementation.
The ERL sector has built this sector coordination and interface capacity in the governorates where UN hubs are currently located. Furthermore, coordination for cross-border ERL activities has been established at the Syria-wide level. Coordination at the field level proved critical in bringing the ERL partners under one umbrella to improve context-sensitive early recovery efforts on the ground, prevent duplication and discrepancies, and achieve synergies in interventions.

3 - Needs

Thus far, the progress made through continuous coordination efforts at the Syria and WOS levels, and consultations with sector partners, and several workshops conducted in collaboration with UNOCHA and other sectors reflected the critical importance of improving sector coordination at all levels. This project is focused on the fact that the need and scope for an Early Recovery and Livelihood response are increasing significantly, necessitating a greater need for ongoing planning efforts and more in-depth needs assessments and programming support work that can help deliver a more integrated and context-sensitive early recovery response in prioritized areas. Secondly, this project aims to strengthen ties with decision-making partners as well as civil society, as clearance and program implementation would suffer without them.Thirdly, the project will address the partners’ ongoing need for a forum where they can share information, including context analysis, and get an independent and unbiased picture of the needs in Syria.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

In its attempts to effectively mainstream and operationalize protection in its operations, the sector seeks to go beyond the definition of “Do No Harm” to “Do Good.” This will mean programming to avoid exacerbating pre-existing inequities, conflicts, and tensions and fostering social stability, community capital, and mutual confidence by promoting grassroots collective action.In practice, this will imply ensuring that response prioritization is solidly focused on sex- and age-disaggregated needs and behaviors that are – to the greatest degree possible – embraced by all parts of the affected beneficiary populations. This, in turn, will necessitate robust input and data from all sector stakeholders in order to ensure fully informed decision-making and prioritization; accurate monitoring; consultative processes with recipient groups in defining response options and priorities; and, fall-back strategies and alternatives in the event of program reorientation and/or a changing operating climate. The sector will ensure a proper contextual analysis as a fundamental step in elaborating area-based early recovery plans to underpin planning and programming activities. The analysis will equally facilitate the identification and mitigation of any negative consequences from protection, gender, and social perspective.

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

As a result, the ERL Sector will work closely with other sectors to foster synergies and prevent overlap and duplication as it implements its multifaceted community-focused strategy. To further enhance local and national coordination, a common integrated area-based approach, as a cross-sectoral integrated strategy would contribute to achieving a more comprehensive local resilience plan for prioritized communities. The approach will contribute to shifting ERS resilience interventions from project-based local support to a more integrated approach that links up initiatives, helps harmonize actions of local actors, and more effectively addresses the multiple, interlinked drivers of local conflict and cohesion. Effectively supporting area-based is only possible where there is a continuous, timely, and coordinated presence in communities. The sector is working to improve and strengthen the mainstreaming of resilience and early recovery methods in the planning processes of various partners through resilience-focused discussions with stakeholders at sector meetings and lobbying for resilience-oriented initiatives to achieve better resilience of the Syrian people to cope with the effects of the crisis and prevent their further slide into destitution.
