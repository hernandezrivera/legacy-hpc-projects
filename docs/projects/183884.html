Project Summary

This main objective of the project is to provide life-sustaining services to children affected by conflict, natural disasters and extreme poverty by providing them a safe and dignified environment through the creation of access to basic education. By the end of 2018, an estimated 4.2 million school-aged children (6-16 years) were out of school, many of whom live in the most vulnerable or conflict-affected communities. To this effect, Save the Children's education project will consider supporting children in both formal and non-formal settings (OOSC in the ALP centres) through an integrated approach of education with WASH, Child Protection as well as provision of school meals. Targeted states are associated with a high number of vulnerable children (including IDPs) and are often affected by conflict (Central Darfur, West Darfur, North Darfur, Blue Nile, South Kordofan and North Kordofan states, plus Khartoum for school meals). Save the Children will have an impactful education intervention through the complementarity approach, targeting localities with high severity and low fund accessibility, and the organization has provided education support such as the locked areas in South Kordofan.
The intervention aims at responding to the most acute needs of the communities in the targeted localities through providing life-saving quality education services, by improving the learning environment within the school and the quality of teaching through training and support to teachers. The intervention mainstreams gender, WASH, and child protection into the education. This will ensure children have access to WASH facilities in school (including gender-segregated and disability-appropriate latrines, hand-washing facilities, and safe water supply) and can access child protection related services through referral pathway. Hygiene awareness sessions on good hygiene practices will be conducted both at community and school level to reduce in the long term the risk of water-borne diseases. Education activities will aim at improving sustainably learning environment and children's resilience through classroom rehabilitation, teachers training, psychosocial Support (PSS), and referral activities in schools, protection awareness campaigns conduction in communities or capacity of community-based CP structures strengthening.
Save the Children will implement school feeding programmes in Khartoum (Jabal Awlia locality), Central Darfur (Shamal Jabal Marrah, Wadi Saleh, Zalingie) and North Darfur (Tawila, Kutum, Kelemando). School feeding, supports improved nutrition and hygiene status of school-aged children, increased access to learning opportunities for the most vulnerable children and in particular an incentive for families to send girls back to school and help girls stay in school.
Save the Children in Sudan will implement its activities in coordination State Ministries of Education, and education clusters in different targeted states. The implementation team will work with humanitarian actors in the localities.

1 - Needs

According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022 (HNO), there are 14.6 million people will be in need of humanitarian assistance across Sudan in 2022. Needs are driven by an extended economic crisis characterized by sustained high inflation which reached more than 340% in May 2021, the crops destruction during 2020 and low level of rains in some part of the country 2021 have exacerbated the economic suffering. The weak public service provision and a volatile political context; with repeated shocks including intercommunal conflicts, mass displacement, flooding, and disease outbreaks including the COVID-19 pandemic. All of these have adversely affected the Education Sector in Sudan. The Sudan Education Sector has identified more than 3.1 million children who need life-sustaining humanitarian support to continue or resume their formal education (2,917,717 vulnerable residents; 616,598 IDPs; 241,499 refugees; 165,840 returnees). By the end of 2018, an estimated 4.2 million school-aged children (6-16 years) were out of school, many of whom live in the most vulnerable or conflict-affected communities. In 2020, the education of 9.6 million additional children was affected by COVID-19 when all schools were closed in March, with most schools not reopening until early 2021. The learning losses from this prolonged school closure will be felt for many years to come, and some children may not return to school at all unless there is an increased commitment to support Education in Emergencies and the overall wellbeing of school-aged children in Sudan. The targeted localities and states are among the mostly severe localities in the country namely, Central Darfur: Gharb Jabal Marrah, Sharg Jabal Marrha, Wasat Jabal Marrah and, Azum, West Darfur: Geneina, Sirba South Kordofan: Rashad, Abu Jubeiha, Blue Nile: Wad al Mahi, Kurmuk, Geisan, Bau, the locked area SK (Delami, Heiban, Um Durein), North Darfur (Tawila, Kelemando, El Fasher). While in North Kordofan also the following localities are among the most severe localities, the State Ministry of Education are highly pushing SCI to support due to their limited capacity to response to the education needs in these localities, namely, Gharb Bara, Sheikan. All of these localities have a severity rating for education of level 4, apart from Um Durein in South Kordofan (at severity 3) and Gebrat el Sheitk in North Kordofan (at severity 2). Access to education for children with disabilities and adaptation of schools for children with disabilities stands out as a current major gap. Across all schools in the country average of only 4 or 5 children with disabilities enrolled per school. According to JENA 2021 average of 62% attendance overall for children with disabilities, compared to 95% for girls and 91% for boys. According to the same source, this is likely related to the fact that transportation issues were very commonly reported as a main barrier to accessing education in this region. The next most commonly related barriers were financial (19% of school KIs), considering that the cost of additional support needed for children with disabilities cannot always be met by families, as well as a general lack of specialized support for children with disabilities such as wheelchairs, hearing aids, visual aids (18% of school KIs).

2 - Activities/Outputs

The over all objective of the project is to improve living conditions of populations affected by conflict or natural disasters by providing them a safe and dignified environment through creation of access to basic Education and integrated in-school Child Protection services in Central Darfur, West Darfur, North Darfur, Blue Nile, South Kordofan and North Kordofan states. In order to achieve the above mentioned objective, the following outputs are expected by end of the project:1. More vulnerable children estimated at 92,540 children (half of them are girls) have access quality education. 2. Strengthening the capacities of education actors and improving pedagogic and positive disciplining means. 3. The access to safe and healthy learning environments for vulnerable populations is enhanced. These three outputs will be achieved through the implementation of the following activities: 1. Construction and rehabilitation of 56 classrooms and TLSs, using Ministry of Education standards.2. Provision of Education and learning materials for 92,540 children studying in different schools of the targeted localities. 3. Provision of teaching tool kits for 52 targeted schools. 4. Provision of schools seats and furniture for teacher offices.5. Conduct school based hygiene campaigns along with distribution of hygiene materials. 6. Provision of recreational kits. 7. Establishment of referral pathway for existing services and services providers in at least 52 schools in the target localities. 8. Train teachers in child protection, child safeguarding, positive discipline, teaching methodology.9. Ensuring schools are adequately supported for prevention of spreading of coronavirus pandemic within education facilities. 10. Train PTAs and mother supporting groups in child protection and child safeguarding issues and referral system. 11. Train PTAs' members in school management and School Grant Management and School Improvement Plans. 12. Provide grants for schools and monitor the implementation of school improvement plans (SIP).13. Provide hot school meals to 32,989 children to improve improve nutrition and hygiene status of school-aged children and increase access to learning opportunities. Furthermore, for sustainability, efforts will be made to support national capacities to design and manage national school feeding programmes, mainstream school feeding into national policies, establish stable funding and budgeting, and build partnerships for coordination and community ownership.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The climate crisis is an intergenerational on child rights crisis, and the greatest threat to children's survival, learning and protection. Save the Children has an Environmental Sustainability and Climate Change (ESSCC) Policy, and is committed to minimizing the negative environmental impact of our programmes and operations, contributing positively to environmental sustainability and climate change action, and implementing interventions which reduce vulnerability to climatic shocks. Save the Children will include environmental considerations into all interventions. In particular, the sourcing (local, national, international) of all supplies and materials, especially those used for construction and rehabilitation of infrastructure, will be carefully considered to select the option with the least negative environmental impact, considering both local dynamics (e.g. deforestation, brick manufacturing processes) and contributions to global carbon emissions caused by freight and production processes. All construction works will be done to limit negative environmental impact on the soil and its surroundings. The project will contribute to resilience and a sustainable use of natural resources through involving and encouraging children and their communities to implement better practices which support and better environmental protection and a safe local environment. In this regards, children school clubs will be established and educated and trained in how they can keep their school clean and green. Moreover, School Improvement Plans may include planting activities within the school as part of improving the learning environment for children. In this regard, Save the Children field offices will coordinate with Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry Resources to ensure adequate plants were distributed to schools. Plants that can contribute in reducing the carbon dioxide and can grow properly considering different weather and environment features of localities where SC Sudan operates. SC Sudan will also communicate publicly about the impacts of climate change and environmental degradation in Sudan.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Through all programming, Save the Children aims to increase safety, uphold dignity, and ensure equitable access to services, including through addressing the concerns of individuals and groups with specific needs. Save the Children will ensure that girls and boys, women and men (of all ages, abilities and backgrounds) are consulted in a meaningful way in programming at all stages of the project lifecycle, and have regular opportunities to provide feedback and concerns. Gender, age and disability sensitive measures will be adopted, including through adapting meeting/service schedules and meeting venues to ensure equitable participation, using single sex discussion groups, considering ways to support the dignity and privacy of beneficiaries, and ensuring facilities are physically accessible to all, and adopting child-friendly methodologies. Save the Children will provide timely and accessible information (in local languages and culturally appropriate mediums) to affected communities on proposed interventions and deliverables, expectations of staff behavior especially with respect to our Child Safeguarding policy and Code of Conduct, and how to raise concerns or provide feedback. SC Sudan has a well-established Complaints & Feedback Response Mechanism, including a telephone hotline, and multiple further modalities accessible to those with varying levels of literacy and for those that prefer anonymity; and a clear system for handling feedback of all natures. Programme design and implementation is informed by context analysis including local power and conflict dynamics. Save the Children will work closely with multiple stakeholders (community members, government partners, local NGOs) to avoid unintended negative effects on individuals or exacerbation of community tensions, and will seek programming which safeguards, protects, and capacitates vulnerable and marginalized individuals, and reduces tensions. SC Sudan Protection staff support the effective mainstreaming of protection across all programming including though training on referral pathways, conducting protection audits, and providing technical advice. A Gender Equality Specialist supports with improving the gender sensitivity of programmes, and all projects are assessed through gender equality marker. Teachers, members of Parent Teachers Associations, and other stakeholders will be trained on child protection risks, how to identify vulnerable children, and appropriate prevention and reporting actions. Child protection activities will be mainstreamed throughout, and the project will seek as far as possible to address the challenges faced by children in schools, and that the most vulnerable children are supported to enrol, attend and achieve at school. Awareness campaigns in the schools and communities will be undertaken to encourage enrolment and ongoing attendance, and to emphasise the importance of education especially for girls and prevention of early marriages.
