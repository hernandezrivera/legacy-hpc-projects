Project Summary

"Background: Engaging Communities through Youth Leaders Network for Promoting Life-saving Behaviour, Accountability and Social Cohesion (ReLnet) project is designed on the outset of changing dimension of Rohingya crisis at Cox's Bazar district and promoting access to services and accountability manifesto CwC WG to the effective population.
Youth group consists one third of the total population in FDMN camps and host communities of Cox’s Bazar who are among the most vulnerable population and will be the most affected population in the near future. This group from both communities are in need of proper attention and guidance at this context. The key focused of the project will be engaging youth groups to promote access to sectoral services and accountability for the refugee and the host community people.
JRP 2020 strategy and project design:
- Based on the experience of implementing the largest community mobilization networks in Rohingya camps from the last two years, BRAC C4D has initiated the youth Leaders Network project to engage and capacitate young people from Host and Rohingya community for implementing CwC activities following the strategy of CwC to disseminate life-saving messages and promoting accountability. The project is designed based on the JRP strategy of 2020 for the CwC WG.
- The project will mobilize and train 80 host community young people
- 200 Rohingya youths volunteers to be trained on cascade basis to disseminate service information and behavioural messages to Rohingya and Bangladeshi people on multi-sectoral approach, conduct accountability mechanism to affected population and facilitate social cohesion.
- Access to complaint and feedback systems will be ensured within the host community.
- The project will be implemented under CwC Working Group and ensure its full cooperation as and when needed and vice-versa.
- All messages, materials and activities will be cross checked through gender lens. Rights of elderly people and disability inclusion are key consideration of the project.
- The project will coordinate with BRAC Sectors in camps and host community areas and seek support as and when needed. Besides, it will engage the youth from BRAC development programme called Palli-Somaj and BRAC Innovation Lab.
- BRAC Operation team has 13 offices surrounding camps which will support the project partially ensuring operational and administrative compliances for them.
Gender review: The project will target youth groups as a channel for disseminating life-saving messages as well as the most vulnerable groups including woman and ethnic minorities will be poetized and tagged audience. Moreover, it is designed following the sector objectives and key performance of CwC WG activities.
- All project staff will be provided trainings on Safeguarding Policy of BRAC, Gender awareness and Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) as mandatory.
- Project has been design is such way that it will be able to answer the questions asked during midline or end line evaluation of JRP 2020.
- Project has followed age and sex disaggregated indicators in identifying beneficiaries and target audience; Activities will be tailored accordingly.
- Youth Leaders network will be consisted of at least 80% women as well as facilitate their appropriate participation in trainings and activities.
- There are some ethnic and minority groups like Chakma, Marma (in Hnila area), Garo and Borua and Hindu will get preference in staff hiring process."

Needs

"There is significantly a growing need for CwC intervention in host community. According to the Mid Term Review of JRP 2018, there continue to be critical gaps in how partners can communicate with refugees and the host community. It is also mentioned in the document that Communication between Host communities and Rohingya is doubly important as host community needs and concerns continue to grow, and social cohesion remains limited between the refugee and host populations in certain areas.Ensuring accountability using cross-cutting issues: In host communities, ensuring equality of access to information throughout the response is one the major identified gaps. Marginalized people and those with limited mobility and literacy are heavily reliant on others for needed information and as such are at protection risk of denial, exploitation and other forms of abuse and violence. A consistent, meaningful, acceptable and transparent feedback and remedial mechanisms to demonstrate accountability to affected population and service users (Rohingya and host community) is not yet concretely in place in all Sector and in several camps. Finally, there continues to be a need for minimum standards for data collection and data sharing mechanism across all sectors and partners to ensure a more streamlined approach to providing services and collecting feedback from affected populations. Meeting grand bargain commitments: As per the information needs assessment conducted by Internews, alike the Rohingya community, almost 80% of the population of host community does not feel like they have enough information to make appropriate decisions for themselves and their families. Almost 60% people from host community prefers to receive information from face-to face communication like, from friends and families, community and religious leaders etc. Alignment with the macro-settlement development plan: Considering the context BRAC C4D designed the project with CwC activities for host communities with the aim to meeting the need for more accessible, community preferred means of information. The project will focus on to enhance Youth Leader capacity in regards to focus to reach most vulnerable groups of people. Based on the need for coordination mentioned in JRP 2018 review, the project incorporated enhance leadership mechanism, community dialogue, organize study group. This will ensure effective coordination with sectors and CwC WG. Besides, a common and more effective way of feedback mechanism of ISCG will be used for collecting and analyzing host community feedback.Ensuring coverage: Based on the experience of the Interpersonal communication session BRAC C4D has been conducting successfully with the Rohingya communities from the last two years, in collaboration with IOM with the evidence from needs assessment through internews, ensuring the following population coverage free of doubt. Contingency for natural disaster: A certain percentage of budget allocation is already justified for the contingency for natural disaster. Align with CwC WG mandate for the Rohingya Community as well as host community this project will implement 72 hours plan. Nevertheless, the project is designed to enhance access to information, access to complaint and feedback mechanism, social cohesion within the communities both in Rohingya and host. However, as it is a need based program and if the budget is not fully funded, reaching a large number of beneficiaries will be difficult and they will be deprived. Moreover, the whole process of engagement will be hampered due to the reason. "
