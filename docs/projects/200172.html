Project Summary

Aligned with ERP Health SO1, SAMS will support in-patient hospital care for at least 720 vulnerable Lebanese citizens, and non-refugee migrants whose ability to access life-saving treatment has been eroded due to the pandemic and Lebanon’s deteriorating socio-economic situation. The decision to include Lebanese and migrant populations stems from the increasing numbers of Lebanese and migrants who can not meet their basic needs and access secondary healthcare.
In 2022, the Health sector was further strained by the pressure of the ongoing financial and economic crisis that has been ranked by the World Bank as one of the most severe globally since the mid-1800’s. The multifaceted crisis started late in 2019 with country-wide protests in response to a deteriorating socio-economic situation. It got coupled in 2020 with the Corona Virus Disease (COVID-19) outbreak, and the devastating Beirut Port explosions. In October 2022, the first cholera outbreak in nearly three decades was declared in Lebanon following the detection of the first laboratory-confirmed case.
Moreover, according to findings shared in health sector coordination meetings held in April 2022, some hospitals have reduced operating capacity by as much as 50 percent due to fuel shortages that lead to electricity cuts. The availability of hospital beds has also become more limited because of dwindling financial support from the MoPH. In addition, some advanced medical conditions are not approved for financial support by the ministry. For these reasons, SAMS will support life-saving hospital care for the most vulnerable patients on an as-need basis for beneficiaries where insurance scheme doesn't cover them.
SAMS will provide patients full financial coverage to support hospital admission and treatment for urgent, life-saving care. SAMS will screen referred cases against vulnerability criteria to determine whether or not they qualify for full coverage. This criteria is based on prior coordination with MoPH, MoSA, IOM, ICRC, Caritas and other key stakeholders. Concerning payment modality, there will be no transfer of funds to patients. Rather, a network of around 30 contracted hospitals across the country that SAMS has previously vetted will submit invoices to SAMS for review and payment of bills of up to $1,800 per Lebanese patient, $1,500 per migrant patient, and $1,300 per psychiatric patient. The cost per patient was determined in coordination with Health Sector leads, IOM, and based on SAMS’ prior experience.
As an immediate result: 720 patients experiencing severe economic hardship will receive the medical care they could not afford, while alleviating any protection concerns associated with hospital admissions (such as document confiscation and refusal to admit/detention in hospitals due to a lack of payment). While only an estimate, the anticipated disaggregation is as follows: 450 Lebanese and 270 migrants.
As a long term result: Hospitals will benefit from additional income and patient bills paid.
Having implemented hospital referral programming for refugees, Lebanese, and non-refugee migrants over the past four years, SAMS has built relationships with relevant ministries, UN agencies, and other vital stakeholders, alongside a network of contracted hospitals across the country. SAMS has also developed a robust outreach plan that has involved reaching out to community centers, leaders, and volunteers to increase access to local communities and supplement its work with regular privately funded medical missions.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

In coordination with relevant working groups and key stakeholders to establish medical and socioeconomic vulnerability criteria and raise awareness of available support, SAMS will target 720 vulnerable Lebanese and non-refugee migrants across Lebanon to support with hospitalization costs, allowing patients cannot otherwise afford the remaining bill to access life-saving surgeries and urgent care. SAMS will receive referrals for 3 population groups: 1) Lebanese unable to cover the full cost of interventions (not covered by any ensuring party) will be fully covered by SAMS up to $1,800 per patient; 2) Migrants unable to cover the full cost of interventions will be fully covered by SAMS up to $1,500 per patient (in coordination with IOM and relevant agencies); 3) Psychiatric patients (Lebanese and Migrants) who are unable to cover the cost of care (who are admitted without any other support) will be fully covered by SAMS up to $1300 per patient.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

During program design, SAMS consulted with the PSEA Network and SGBV WGs to ensure top-up contributions are equally accessible to men and women, including persons with special needs. The project will decrease protection concerns for patients, including the confiscation of ID and legal documents, the long separation of patients from their caregivers, refusal of the hospital to admit or discharge the patient because of medical debt, and the refusal to release the bodies of deceased patients through MOUs with hospitals that name SAMS as the responsible party for payment rather than the patient. Specifically, with regard to PSEA, trained Safeguarding Focal Points will be responsible for ensuring that hospitals display reporting mechanisms, and will be ready to support survivors of sexual exploitation or abuse with consent-based referrals, as needed. Based on needs, project staff are also trained by a Country Security Officer on SAMS Safety and Security Policy.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

In keeping with the Core Humanitarian Standard on Quality and Accountability commitments, SAMS will welcome feedback from beneficiaries and react constructively to concerns promptly. Recognizing that individuals have access to different means of communication, SAMS utilizes various methods for receiving confidential feedback/concerns through the following methods:1. Receiving feedback over the phone or through hotline numbers advertised in PHCs, flyers, WGs, and other places SAMS accesses beneficiaries.2. Receiving feedback through satisfaction surveys/key informant interviews.3. Receiving feedback from online forms shared with project participants subsequent to conducting follow-up calls with them.3. Advertising that SAMS services are free of charge. SAMS will ensure consistent and effective communication with patients through pre-procedure phone calls, post- procedure calls, and satisfaction surveys.
