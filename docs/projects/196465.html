Project Summary

The project's fodder and sheep distributions will encourage Northeast Syria (NES) sheep breeders to maintain and increase their sheep flocks. Supplying agriculture inputs for drought resilience fodder sources and livelihood assets for sheep by-products projects will strengthen livestock value chains and family incomes.

3 - Needs

In the 2021 and 2022 season a report revealed that "18 sub-districts across NES recorded losses in harvested crop area of over 75% – 13 were in Al-Hasakeh Governorate, including 7 with over 90% losses – affecting an estimated 60,087 people in agriculture-based livelihoods - 50,474 of which are farm owners – and an estimated 228,496 total people in agriculture-dependent households." Livestock farmers have begun to adopt negative coping mechanisms by destocking through sudden death or selling their poorly nourished sheep for low prices. Recent Key-Informant-Interviews (KIIs) in several sub-districts in the Al-Hasakeh governorate have revealed that some sheep breeders have sold or lost more than 50% of their sheep herds. Then, they only keep between 5-20 sheep to keep just enough for home consumption and reduce the fodder cost burden, losing their traditional source of livelihood. These same local experts expect that the remaining sheep breeders across Al-Hasakeh will sell their remaining livestock and mass migrate if the crisis continues.

4 - Implementation of activities

The program intervention is established on the four emergency livestock programming principles and objectives: 1) minimizing immediate livestock losses, 2) protecting livestock assets, 3) restocking sheep assets for long-term livelihoods, and 4) building strong family and community cohesion for cooperative livestock value chain development. The program will focus on minimizing livestock losses through immediate fodder ration distributions to achieve the first three objectives. Livestock assets are protected in two primary ways. Program veterinarian services help to stabilize overall sheep health. Secondly, through fodder rations, a healthy incentive is given to beneficiaries to not resort to negative coping mechanisms such as selling their sheep to acquire more fodder or quitting their livestock-based livelihoods. An additional 55 HHs are targeted in this program with two pilot components to achieve the fourth objective. First, 25 HHs will be given agriculture kits to cultivate approximately three donems of green fodder of their choice (corn, azula, green barley, clover etc.). Finally, a subset (30- HHs targeted) will be selected based on business plan viability, leadership qualities, animal husbandry best practices, and proven adoption of healthy coping mechanisms from the 2022 beneficiaries for further livelihood support in 2023. These beneficiaries will receive a cash grant (averaging about $3,000) to share in a group of a minimum of 3 HHs.
