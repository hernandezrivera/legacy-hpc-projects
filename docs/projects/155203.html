Needs

Cox's Bazar is an economically disadvantaged district due to low productivity, food insecurity, and high poverty rates. Livelihoods for over 2 million local population are predominantly based on small scale subsistence farming, fishing, and unreliable and sporadic daily labor wages. In addition, the Rohingya influx that has put a strain on resources for host communities. Almost a quarter of a million of host community members rely on government social welfare support, many youths receive daily wages, and high number of new graduates are seeking employment. Despite the comprehensive humanitarian assistance, the Rohingya populations remain vulnerable. There is a strong correlation between self-reliance and improvements in food security, self-confidence and protection awareness among the refugees. According a WFP VAM Survey, it is estimated that at least 80 percent of the overall refugee population is highly to entirely relying on life-saving assistance and that Rohingya often unsustainable coping strategies such as selling a portion of the assistance received, borrowing money and buying complementary food, including vegetables or condiments, on credit in order to meet basic needs including health-related costs. The lack of self-reliance opportunities has been exposing refugees, especially women and adolescent girls, to protection risks and potentially harmful coping mechanisms such as trafficking, exploitation, survival sex, child marriage, and participation in illegal drug trade. IOM has documented the hidden talents and skills that can be nurtured into lifelong employability potentials. The influx of refugees has exacerbated pre-existing tensions of host communities against the Rohingya. Host communities perceive that refugees are responsible for the deterioration of living conditions, high costs of living, and insufficient government response to their plight. Recent surveys have reported a fall in wage of 50%. Financial resources are not sufficient to meet all food and non-food related needs for the vast majority of Bangladeshi living in areas with a high presence of refugees. WFP classified on average 10% of the host community population as highly vulnerable to food insecurity while 21 to 30% were seen as being vulnerable. Similarly, the host community households headed by women are more vulnerable to food insecurity (45% are vulnerable or highly vulnerable, especially due to cultural practices inimical to women's self-empowerment) than those headed by men (35%). Having collaborated with other partners (FAO, WFP) in the support to over 25,000 refugees, IOM will build on the achievements with a more strategic approach to livelihoods in 2019. In camp settings, the approach to self-reliance capabilities will be oriented on skill development to serve as a protection and empowerment mechanism and for nurturing relevant skills and knowledge. The Rohingya communities, youth, girls, women, elderly will be engaged to prioritize those traditional crafts, upholstery, community service practices, environmental knowledge and practices and life skills. These trainings and diversified cash-for-work incentives are used as entry points to keep refugees engaged, motivated and promote social cohesion, a sense of self-empowerment for the future. An additional package will be provided in consultation with host community business sector, authorities on potential interlinkages for some of these products on exhibition, and talent displays which may create markets for the future.

Activities or outputs

IOM's livelihoods support in camp setting will be tailored to building capabilities for self-reliance. Rohingya refugees (women and youth) will receive incentives and skills training. On food sufficiency, Rohingya beneficiary households will receive high quality skills training, continued mentoring, inputs for homestead gardening techniques (plant physiology, irrigation, soil fertility, etc) as well as orientation on food preparation for the refugee markets. Individuals will be selected to develop their skills and transfer knowledge through day to day technical advice to their communities and will receive a certification. On the creation of temporary income opportunities for women and youth, the project will incorporate small scale labour intensive works that will, at the same time, allow the transfer of portable life skills and the provision of functional literacy, technical or vocational trainings for people with interest in specific technical knowledge that is sought after in the camps (dress making, kids clothes, electronics, carpentry, etc). Livelihood support in the host communities will create an environment for innovations, alternatives, and market centred production approaches. In coordination with WFP and FAO, farmer groups and women groups interested in fish processing will benefit from extension services, assets, grants and market linkages. Grants will be provided to Women vendor Groups and facilitation of small number of collection centres for selected agricultural produce, including vegetable crops, fish products generated through the project. These collection centres will have linked to the Camp supplementary food supply chains. In line with the Strategic Objective 2 of the Food Security sector, IOM will support community collective actions to rehabilitate environmental systems (water sources, solid waste, through job creation). Finally, Youth Employment innovations will be supported based on market opportunities and assessments.While the above will contribute indirectly to reducing tensions and building social cohesion, IOM will also address risk to social harmony at three Levels: (1) building community cohesion through communal works and social networks of engagement (such as culture, sports, trauma healing, advocacy, (2) leadership training and collaborative learnings in Camps and host communities (traditional leaders, Government, Youth, women), and (3) community safety, peace and security mechanism (role of law and order, relations with police institutions, community dispute resolution and referral systems. This support will feed into the anti-trafficking Response. Strengthening the use of knowledge systems is an integral part of adaptation and resilience. because these systems have been insufficiently exploited in host communities, the capacities to adapt new practices for livelihoods remain mundane. IOM will complement Government, FAO, WFP skills development in extension, with new centres (the Community Integrated Knowledge Centres (CIKCs) that will provide comprehensive studies, mentoring services and production of Information Education and Communication (IEC) materials for community production systems. In the Camps, IOM undertake study on transformative hands-on-services to life-long skills in the context of the Rohingya refugees. The total target population is 25,000 Households
