Project Summary

The project aims to improve the health and wellbeing of both the Rohingya refugee population at Camp:04 and the host communities in Ukhia, Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh, by focusing on access to essential healthcare, disease prevention, mental health support, and community empowerment in an inclusive and equitable manner. Special attention will be given to addressing the needs.
Objectives:
1. Enhance healthcare access for both Rohingya refugees and host community members through integrated healthcare services.
2. Strengthen disease prevention efforts to reduce the incidence of communicable diseases and non-communicable diseases.
3. Promote mental health and psychosocial wellbeing for individuals affected by trauma and stress, focusing on vulnerable groups such as women, children, and the elderly.
4. Improve health literacy and empower communities with knowledge on hygiene, sanitation, and disease prevention practices.
5. Build local capacity by training healthcare workers, volunteers, and community leaders from both the Rohingya and host populations.
Key Components:
1. Integrated Healthcare Delivery:
Establishing Health post by trained healthcare professionals to serve Rohingya communities, providing Emergency & general health services, Non Communicable diseases (NCD Services) , Lab facilities , Maternity and SRH Services ,MHPSS & GBV Referral Services ,Dental Service. Free medicine distribution according to Prescription. Maternal &childcare and management of chronic Condition.
Referral Systems will ensure that patients requiring specialized care are referred to appropriate healthcare facilities by Own ambulance.
We are conducting a total of 6 health camps in our project in the host community of Ukhia Upazila, one camp every month. In these health camps, the people of the host community are getting health services and free medicines as per the prescription.
2.Mental Health and Psychosocial Support:
Mental Health and Psychosocial Support
will be available for both refugees and host community members, with a focus on addressing trauma, anxiety, depression, and stress-related issues provided by this health post and health camp in both Communities.
3.Capacity Building and Community Engagement:
Training Programs will be provided to healthcare workers, community health volunteers, and leaders from both populations to enhance local healthcare delivery and ensure the sustainability of services.
Joint Community-Based Initiatives will encourage collaboration between refugees and host community members, fostering social cohesion and collective responsibility for health outcomes.
4.Partnerships:
Collaborating with local NGOs and international organizations to leverage resources and expertise.
Target Population:
The project targets the over 34 thousand rohingya refugees in camp :04 and the host communities in ukhia , Cox’s Bazar, focusing on vulnerable groups such as children, women, the elderly, and individuals with disabilities.
Expected Outcomes:
1. Increased access to healthcare services for both refugees and host communities.
2. Decreased rates of preventable diseases due to improved health practices and vaccination efforts.
3. Enhanced mental health and wellbeing within both communities through counseling and psychosocial support services.
4. Strengthened local health capacity, ensuring the sustainability of health services for both populations.
5. Improved social cohesion between refugees and host communities through collaborative health interventions.
Conclusion:
The integrated health initiative in Ukhia, Cox's Bazar aims to enhance the health of Rohingya refugees and host communities by offering high-quality healthcare services, illness prevention, and mental health assistance. The project's goal is to promote long-term health equity and resilience for everybody engaged by strengthening local health systems and encouraging collaboration between the two communities.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

OBAT Helpers Health Program in Ukhiya, Cox's Bazar, aligns with the suggested actions in the 2023 MSNA Plan Guidance Note on Protection Mainstreaming. The project incorporates protection measures into various aspects of its implementation to ensure the well-being and safety.OBAT Helpers recognizes the importance of female representation and will make efforts to recruit female staff members both within the project and the organization as a whole. This step aims to enhance female participation and ensure diverse perspectives. To establish effective feedback mechanisms and address concerns, OBAT will introduce a complaint/advice box. This box will serve as a confidential channel for beneficiaries to voice their complaints, provide advice, and make suggestions. The implementation of this approach aligns with the Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Safeguarding policies ensuring a safe and respectful environment for all.To maintain community engagement and receive feedback on project activities, OBAT will continue involving parents and local leaders such as imams, Head Majhis, and sub-Majhis in the evaluation of learners. Their participation provides valuable insights and strengthens community ownership of the project. Moreover, OBAT will collect information from the complaint/advice box allowing beneficiaries to share their thoughts, concerns, and recommendations. Here is how the project addresses the issues;• Community-Based Surveillance and Monitoring by the CESG• Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS)• Sexual and Reproductive Health and Rights (SRHR)• Volunteer Team for Education-related Awareness to Parents and students• Observation of National and International DaysIn brief, OBAT Helpers Health Program demonstrates a comprehensive approach to protection mainstreaming by integrating protection measures across various components of the project.

2.3 - Age & Disability

OBAT Helpers Health project aims to provide access to 34980 persons to attain health services from Health Post in Rohingya camp as well as from the Health Camps in the Host communities including 18590 women & girls and 1520 Elderly population. The Health services have been provided in line Health Sector guideline. OBAT Helpers is dedicated to promoting inclusivity and ensuring the participation of people with disabilities (PWD) and children with disabilities (CWD) during the implementation of the project. As part of this commitment, OBAT Helpers will strive to include a minimum of 5% of PWD and CWD within the project, based on their availability within the targeted population. By actively including PWD and CWD in the project, OBAT Heleprs aims to provide them with equal opportunities and access to the project's activities, services, and benefits. This inclusive approach recognizes the rights and unique needs of individuals with disabilities and seeks to create a supportive and empowering environment that promotes their well-being and development.OBAT Helpers will ensure that appropriate measures and accommodation are in place to facilitate the participation and engagement of PWD and CWD. This may include providing accessible infrastructure, adaptive learning materials, specialized support, and targeted interventions to meet their specific requirements.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

OBAT Helpers strongly feels that the impacted people should have the chance to raise concerns, offer input, and receive a response, so it made sure that this initiative has the appropriate mechanisms in place. The comments gathered from the impacted populations will be examined during project implementation, and necessary measures will be implemented on a regular basis. In this project, possible PSEA hazards in program delivery contexts will be evaluated and mitigating actions will be determined. OBAT helpers also provides copies of the PSEA Code of Conduct to all employees, as well as other safety precautions, and reminds them of their responsibilities. Through the project outcomes framework, the project focus is tasked with keeping an eye on AAP-related outputs and indicators. To ensure active engagement and a successful community feedback system by the facility, OBAT Helpers also includes affected populations in the monitoring process to ensure community feedback through facility based CESG & Parents’ meeting. OBAT Helpers will also collect feedback in ‘Complaint & Suggestion box’ in each of learning center.
