Project Summary

An innovative approach using sports for social change and development will contribute enormously in breaking barriers, reducing stigma, enhancing prevention, and building resilience. The project seeks to enhance education and prevent COVID-19 infections mitigating the socio-economic impact of both health problems, provide psychosocial support to vulnerable, infected and affected people, and mainstream proximity education and sensitization. The project will put in place functional peer and community educators programs using football activities amongst youths and young adults between 7-24 years in 30 primary secondary/high schools in Dschang, Mamfe, Bamenda. Prolonged unrest in the North-West, South-West compounded by lack of resources have left national health systems profoundly ill-prepared to fight an epidemic such as COVID-19.
The project will build on the existing strong partnership with the different actors such as Ministries of Basic and Secondary Education, Health, Ministry of Sports, WHO, UNICEF, UNDP, OCHA, CDC, the Red Cross, ALIMA, MSF, COVID-19 Task force, the CSO networks and platforms where HEDECS is a team member. HEDECS’ partner Coaches Across Continents will accompany us technically in the process. Creative thinking on how to address COVID- 19 and conflict together could play a game-changing role in improving on health and empowering the communities in the North-West, West and South-West regions of Cameroon. It is against this backdrop that this project seeks to contribute in health and education promotion as well as mitigating the negative impact of the diseases and humanitarian crises using sports as a medium of social change and development.
An inclusive approach will be used involving the beneficiary communities and key stakeholders in the project design, implementation and monitoring and evaluation. Gender will be mainstreamed to include adolescent girls and young women (AGYW), youths who make up the majority of the population, people living with disabilities, and juveniles. Stakeholders including and health sports institutions buy in will be a prerequisite and central. Sports will be the medium in breaking barriers, mobilizing communities and building resilience. Monitoring and evaluation will be in integrated in the project life cycle. Age appropriate modules/workshops will be conducted for primary school ages and another for secondary school ages
Overall Objective
To build community resilience for education and health promotion while mitigating the socio-economic impact of COVID-19 on AGYW/youths through sports.
Specific Objectives
To strengthen the community response to education, health promotion and disease prevention on COVID-19 in the North West, South West and West regions of Cameroon.
To enhance risk communication and community engagement on coronavirus prevention and vaccination promotion.
To provide psychosocial support to infected and affected communities through the youth empowerment center
To build resilience in infected and affected communities.
To use football as medium for social change and development.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Since 2016, the English-speaking regions of the North West (NW) and South West (SW) of Cameroon have experienced social instability punctuated by violence. The escalation of tension and the multiplication of hotbeds of hostility between the armed groups and the defense forces severely affected the civilian population, leading to the displacement of many people. into other Regions like the West. The number of households who have left their villages or regions in search of more secure areas is increasing rapidly and steadily. The violence has weakened the education and health system and has increased gender based violence. Mostly affected internally displaced persons are children and youths. Children have difficulties accessing education, gender based violence is on the increase. Mental health challenges further increases risks of the vicious cycle of education, diseases and trauma. The 15-24 age group accounted for more than 30% of new infections, and the 0-14 age group for about 20% (3,308), mainly from mother-to-child transmission. These are essentially the results of the implementation of PMTCT programs, combined prevention programs for key and vulnerable populations and, of course, the expansion of access to care, including ARVs. In the 10-19 age group, girls recorded about six times as many new infections (2,097) as boys (351), a differential observed since 2004, while in the 15-24 age group, the ratio was reduced to 1 boy to 3 girls. The appearance of the very large differential between boys and girls in the 10-19 age group (sex ratio of 1:6) can be explained by the early and disproportionate acquisition of infection through sexual transmission in young girls aged 15-19 (frequency of inter generational sex, particularly transactional sex; earlier entry into sexuality; low condom use in this age group). Only 50% of girls in this age group reported consistent condom use, compared to 72% of boys (DHS 2018.The 2018 mapping estimated the number of youth and adolescents in vulnerable situations at 201,653 [155,615-247,691] nationwide. These are people who have dropped out of school and are most often living with "hotspots" or with a partner from key populations.The situation became exacerbated with the COVID-19 pandemic. Since March 2020 there has been an increase in the number of cases, and the occurrence of community transmission of the disease. The epidemiological situation on July 22, 2020 is characterized by an exponential trend of recorded COVID-19 cases. Since the start of the pandemic, Cameroon has recorded 16,708 confirmed cases, and 385 deaths. The health districts of the West and North West regions are among the most affected. Cameroon's health system is weak, characterized by the lack of equipment / infrastructure as well as the weakness of the community response to health problems. The country faces a significant existing health burden due to other diseases such as HIV. The HandiVIH-ANRS/IFORD, ANRS 12302 study conducted in 2016, showed a prevalence of 3.9% in the general population versus 6.8% in the handicap population. Women with disabilities were more often involved in paid sex and were at increased risk of sexual violence, proven risk factors for HIV infection. (Mid-term review of Cameroon's National Strategic Plan to Fight AIDS 2018-2022).Access to reproductive health services for youth under 21 years of age is required by law to have parental permission. Practices such as early marriage and forced marriage exacerbate the vulnerabilities of adolescent girls and young women by limiting their autonomy to seek and use services. In the absence of laws sanctioning these practices, efforts to eradicate them have struggled to produce results. Ministry of Women's and Family Promotion strategy fights against gender-based violence. The role and importance of sports and physical activity for health maintenance are not sufficiently rooted in lifestyles.
