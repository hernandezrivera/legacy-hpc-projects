Project Summary

Humanitarian situation has deteriorated in South Sudan after the outbreak of civil war in the neighboring Sudan in April 2023. The United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian affairs estimates that 9.4 million people which is 76% of the total population, will need assistance in 2023 through 2024. This is far greater than the number projected in 2023 and previous years. The drivers to this situation stems from cumulative and compounding effects of years of conflicts, sub national violence among communities, food insecurity, climate disasters and public health difficulties. The worse affected are the border areas receiving unprecedented influx of returnees and refugees from Sudan. According to IPC projection for the lean season April – July, 2023, 7.8 million were estimated to face crisis or worse food insecurity with Ayod, Duk, Twic East, Aweil East and Aweil North classified in phase 4 while Bor South was in IPC 3.
The project proposes to implement activities integrating WASH, FSL, Health, Protection, and Nutrition programming at households, institutions, and community levels while mainstreaming GBV, prioritizing the immediate provision of integrated lifesaving FSL and nutrition services to the most vulnerable and food-insecure populations in IPC 3, 4 and 5. The project will support 26,410 people in (Ayod: 2,641, Bor South: 1,321, Duk: 1,321, Twic East: 1,321, Aweil East: 9,244 and Aweil North: 10,564) where boys, girls, men, and women are 6,603, 9,244, 4,754, and 5,810 respectively where 43% are males and 57% are females. The project seeks 200,000 United States Dollars to implement the integrated services in 2024.

1. Needs

Humanitarian situation has deteriorated in South Sudan after the outbreak of civil war in the neighboring Sudan in April 2023. The United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian affairs estimates that 9.4 million people which is 76% of the total population, will need assistance in 2023 through 2024. This is far greater than the number projected in 2023 and previous years. The drivers to this situation stems from cumulative and compounding effects of years of conflicts, sub national violence among communities, food insecurity, climate disasters and public health difficulties. The worse affected are the border areas receiving unprecedented influx of returnees and refugees from Sudan. According to IPC projection for the lean season April – July, 2023, 7.8 million were estimated to face crisis or worse food insecurity with Ayod, Duk, Twic East, Aweil East and Aweil North classified in phase 4 while Bor South was in IPC 3.According to IRNA conducted by partners in Northern Bahr el Ghazal in March prior to the Sudanese conflict in April, there were recorded 1,256 households or 7,536 individuals returning to Mangok payam of Aweil East county of Northern Bahr el Ghazal from Sudan or displaced elsewhere. By October, 317,993 refugees, asylum seekers and returnees have been registered to have crossed into South Sudan with 9,463 in Aweil (October OCHA). The vulnerable people comprised of women, children, and the elderly are exposed to bad weather and protection challenges, including limited water supply, shelter, sanitation services (open defecation) and lack of food etc. While provision of critical services to the affected population is constrained by poor infrastructure, lack of roads, flooding and funding constraints to respond to the needs of both new arrivals and receiving communities. According to the HRP 2023/24, Aweil East and Ayod are priority one counties while Aweil North is a priority two county and Bor South being a priority 3. GBI has been providing humanitarian responses since 2019 in Aweil East, Aweil North, Twic East, and Duk among other areas and will therefore; use its networks, experiences and establishment to provide assistance to the vulnerable communities affected by conflict and natural disaster through 2023/24.

2. Project activities

Activities: 1. Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity, and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3). 2. Emergency food production: main season agriculture seeds and tool distribution; fishing, vegetable and crop kits. 3. Improve livestock intervention; livestock disease surveillance, Community Animal Health Workers Training and inputs support, cold chain establishment and maintenance, prevention & treatment especially zoonoses. Provide livestock veterinary services to curtail livestock losses, improved meat quality, and milk yield, thereby stabilizing and safeguarding rural and urban livelihoods through livestock vaccination and treatment on common diseases as well as surveillance of diseases. 4. Training and capacity building: both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production (farmer/agro-pastoral field schools, demonstration plots), income generation (business skills), vocational training, etc. 5. Multi-sectoral models: livelihoods, nutrition-sensitive food security, hygiene, sanitation 6. Provide conditional assistance in the form of cash/ vouchers or food. Cash and voucher-based transfers – beneficiaries meet their immediate cash needs in locations with functional markets while ensuring that all households are receiving training in financial literacy to make informed and effective decisions relating to their financial resources, including on how to generate, invest, spend and save money. Training will be delivered jointly to male–and female–household members, as a means of promoting joint decision-making over household budgets. Indicators: 1. Number of beneficiaries receiving food, non-food items, cash transfers, and vouchers. Number of Cash and voucher-based transfers beneficiaries – meeting their immediate cash needs in locations with functional markets while ensuring that all households are receiving training in financial literacy to make informed and effective decisions relating to their financial resources, including on how to generate, invest, spend and save money. 2. Number of beneficiaries receiving agricultural inputs (vegetable, crop & fishing kits). 3. Number of kitchen gardens set up at Nutrition facilities. 4. Number of colds chained procured and livestock vaccinated/treated. As a capacity-building activity in livestock, Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) will be provided with drugs and trained on disease investigation, vaccination and treatment, and best management practices to increase output and minimize livestock losses. 5. Number of people benefiting from capacity building & training, asset building, and market support.
