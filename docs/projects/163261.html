Project Summary

Emergencies typically exacerbate existing conditions that contribute to food and nutrition insecurity. The nutritional status of other vulnerable groups, beyond young children, can deteriorate rapidly in an emergency setting. Pregnant and lactating women (PLW) and people living with HIV (PLHIV) are disproportionately affected by emergencies. The response in Zimbabwe requires comprehensive support to restore food and nutrition security. While scaling up general food assistance is essential, it is equally critical to scale up WFP’s emergency nutrition response. The intervention will complement existing UNICEF-supported acute malnutrition treatment services that support children under-five. WFP's nutrition component will target malnourished PLW and PLHIV/TB with treatment support to promote recovery, reduce acute malnutrition rates and long-term consequences of malnutrition include increased risk for mortality. Targeting PLW is in line with the first 1,000 days, ensuring that the intergenerational cycle of malnutrition is broken.

1 - Activities

Malnourished PLW and PLHIV/TB will be identified and treated through health facilities. Screening will be done using mid-upper circumference (MUAC) and all individuals with MUAC <230mm will be enrolled for treatment. The acute malnutrition treatment intervention includes a specialised nutritious food (Super Cereal) and will be complemented with nutrition education/counselling as part of a comprehensive package of care integrated within the routine system for management of PLW (antenatal care services) as well as treatment for PLHIV/TB. The treatment duration is a minimum of 60 days. WFP will also strengthen community outreach through its existing platforms at community-level for example, the general food assistance programmes, to raise awareness and promote health seeking behaviour among PLW and PLHIV/TB.
