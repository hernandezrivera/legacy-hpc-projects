Project Summary

This project aims to support the olive sector and vulnerable olive farmers in Azaz sub-district and Afrin district of Aleppo and support the resilience of workers' groups through the provision of agricultural services and inputs in addition to the income generation activities through cash for work related to supporting agriculture.
The project’s interventions will include both IDPs and host communities with a focus on targeting females headed households and child HHs in addition to HHs including PSNs and unaccompanied elderly.
Sadad well-trained and experienced technical team in cooperation with local authorities and the agricultural units will focus on improving food security and supporting livelihood by providing two main components:
1-Support for best agriculture to increase the productivity of 1000 hectares of Olive trees (2000 HHs, most of them females headed, to support 5 dunums per household) through providing agricultural services as follow:
- conducting agriculture services; ( autumn and spring land plowing, harvest service, two rounds of leaf-nutrition (high K and high N), technical pruning service) to the targeted 1000 hectares.
- Providing the targeted 2000 olive farmers' HHs with 2000 olive-oil extraction vouchers (each voucher will be used to extract olive oil from 1250 kg of olive fruit, the estimated production of five dunums of olive trees) and then starting the process of olive oil extraction in the mills.
-Conducting awareness-raising seminars for 700 farmers on integrated olive-cultivation management, controlling olive tree pests, olive trees' services calendar and how to avoid negative methods related to olive cultivation.
2-Improving livelihoods of crisis-affected groups by creating short term cash for work including vocational training linked to conducting agriculture activities (2415 beneficiaries 915 IDPS / 1500 host community) through providing cash for work related to agricultural activities as follow:
-200 male beneficiaries will be hired and provided with cash for 50 working days to conduct the project's related services and activities, in addition to a three-day training workshop on the integrated olive -cultivation management, and an introduction to minimum standards in humanitarian action including how to deal with beneficiaries according to the humanitarian standards.
-1400 beneficiaries will be hired, divided into groups and provided with cash for 25 working days to conduct olive fruit harvesting service, in addition to vocational training on technical olive trees pruning
-450 male beneficiaries will receive 7 days of training on technical olive trees pruning and then will be provided with cash for 36 working-days to perform technical pruning to the targeted hectares.
-365 Males' beneficiaries will be provided with cash for 30 working days to conduct Spraying service, two rounds, for the targeted 1000 hectares of the olive.
- Mainstreaming General protection through conducting 36 awareness-raising sessions on; GBV related risks، child labor and child marriage، awareness on mine action issues. This will include distributing awareness-raising brochures and leaflets.
As a result, the project will contribute mainly to enhance food security by supporting olive agriculture and means of income in addition to supporting the resilience of the vulnerable groups within the targeted communities.

4 - Needs

Nine years into the crisis, the food security situation in Syria has deteriorated compared to last year, with a total of 9.8 million people in need; of which 569,477 people are estimated to be severely food insecure; 7.3 million estimated to be moderately food insecure while 1.9 million people are estimated to be at risk of food insecurity. The number of food-insecure people has also increased from 6.5M (2019) to 7.9M (2020), an approximately 20 percent increase from last year. Unemployment rates are believed to be at 50 percent, with only 16 percent of women working in the labor force. About 216,000 hectares of wheat and other crops such as barley, fruit trees, Vegetables, and grazing areas (17 percent of national areas planted) were affected by a number of shocks including floods, fires, frost, hailstorms, high temperatures, and plant diseases and pests specifically yellow rust. HNO 2020Sadad conducted an agriculture needs assessment in 30 villages in Azaz and Afrin districts between November and December 2019, which are classified as high severity areas (4-6). Respondents reported the importance of olive trees which contribute in household’s income (41-to 60%), KIs have reported that quality services of olive trees haven't provided during the past years due to difficulties related to farmers economic situations and external factors; lack of agricultural inputs, high transportation and agricultural services (land plowing, harvesting, oil extraction, leaf nutrition, and pruning). Besides the prevalence of pests and insects, lack of pesticide control causes decreasing in olive production.FSS stresses the need to combine medium-term approaches through enhancing primary production capacity and incomes, especially for livelihoods-based agriculture, and providing inputs to improve the agricultural services sector to improve local availability of food (supply) with short term measures through food assistance. HNO 2020

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will be implemented through Sadad offices in the Azaz and Afrin by Sadad technical agricultural team who have previous experience with Sadad in implementing two previous olive projects. The implementation process will be done with the collaboration of local councils and the agricultural units/offices in the targeted villages, after introducing them to the project’s activities and signing MOUs with them.•Meeting will be held at Sadad HQ Turkey to discuss and prepare the implementation plan. After that, announcements of the project's job opportunities will be done, then signing the contracts•After that, conducting deep need assessment to select beneficiaries according to specific criteria.• Contracting with 200 service workers, they will be trained on the integrated management of Olive cultivation and how to deal with LCs and BNFs according to the humanitarian standards.•Contracting with pruning workers and providing them with training on techniques of olive pruning to conduct pruning service of the targeted 1000 hectares of olive trees.•Purchasing leaf-nutrition materials (high K and high N). Then contracting with spraying workers who will conduct leaf-nutrition (high K and high N) service to 1000 hectares of olive cultivated lands.•Contracting with tractors and related plowing equipment then starting Autumn-spring land-plowing for1000 hectares of olive cultivated areas.•Contracting with1400 males and females harvest-workers to conduct harvesting for 1000 hectares of olive cultivated areas, then MOUs will be signed between Sadad and 8 mills.•Distributing plastic bags, gallons to beneficiaries and olive-oil extraction vouchers.•Conducting workshops for 700 farmers on integrated olive-cultivation management.•During implementing the whole project’s services, Sadad protection team will conduct awareness-raising sessions about common protection issues. And get feedback about beneficiaries’ satisfy / complaints about services.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Sadad during implementing the activities of the project may face these risks according to the project's activities. Protection risk 1: Community tension arising from the selection. Likelihood: high / Impact: High. Mitigation: sadad will consider the most vulnerable female-headed household, males with PSNs HHs, considering the children situations and difficult within these HHs (highlighted to HHs including males and children with disabilities or chronic diseases and unaccompanied elderly.), to receive services during this project. Sadad has collaborated with local leaders (to make them aware of the whole project’s activities and interventions) in designing the project to minimize harm and support local capacities to build peace. Protection risk 2: Risk of harassment of women and girls by the humanitarian workers. Likelihood: medium, Impact: high. Mitigation: establishing a confidential complaint mechanism and enhancing accountability mechanisms besides training the staff to deal with beneficiaries in addition to providing workers with training on humanitarian standards and signing code of conduct

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The security situation within the targeted villages is estimated to be safe to ensure the best implementation and provision of services as the villages related to Azaz sub-districts and Afrin district both of them are considered to be safe after the spread of Turkish monitoring points and located under Turkish government control areas (Olive Branch areas and Euphrates Shield areas ).Risks of insecurity situation comes from airstrikes, clashes, the attack of regime forces, etc. Likelihood: high / Impact: High Mitigation and Management: However, there will be continuous risk assessments and safety and security plan and activities may be suspended and delayed if there is shelling in addition to putting in place an emergency response plan and a contingency plan which allows alternative solutions to implement activities in emergency situations.Risks of Community tension arising from the beneficiary selection. Likelihood: high / Impact: High Mitigation and Management: Sadad will identify clear selection criteria and will share these criteria with local authorities and the beneficiaries
