Project Summary

The GBV prevention and response program of International Medical Corps (IMC) in South Sudan aims to empower communities and build resilience through promoting social and behavioural change to address the root causes of GBV in the country. Leveraging on the vast experiences, gains and successes of IMC in implementing GBV prevention and response interventions through FCDO-HARISS program, this project will be continued to implement in the states of Western Bahr el Ghazal (Wau IDP Camp and County), Upper Nile (Aburoc IDP camp in Fashoda County, and Malakal PoC and County), Unity (Nyal County), and an additional location in Jonglei (Canal/Pigi County). It will directly benefit about 27,146 vulnerable women and girls across the target locations.
One of the project’s key priorities is to further strengthen core services to GBV survivors and vulnerable women and girls in the aforesaid intervention sites. These services include quality case management using a survivor-centered approach, along with psychosocial support to enhance healing and recovery. IMC works to ensure that survivors’ holistic needs are met by providing multi-sectoral services, referring to health centers for clinical management of rape, to legal justice services and to other kinds of support based on the needs of survivors.
IMC, together with the Women Center Management Committees, will further strengthen the existing Women and Girls Friendly Spaces in Aburoc, Malakal, Nyal and Wau, and establish new spaces in Payams/Bomas, which are far from the existing spaces of IMC within the said counties. Empowerment, awareness and recreational activities ranging from age-appropriate PSS, life skills and soft skills activities as well as curriculum-based GBV awareness sessions on GBV core concepts and information on services to integrated awareness sessions are being provided by IMC’s trained GBV staff, in cooperation with the medical and MHPSS teams.
Similarly, temporary spaces/tents will be set-up across in Canal/Pigi County for psychosocial support services, including Psychological First Aid and counseling for vulnerable women and children. Due to volatile security, continued displacement and weather-constrained access of many areas in Canal/Pigi, IMC will form and deploy four Mobile Protection Units, which will be integrated in the Mobile Medical and Nutrition Units of IMC, which will rotate in various remote areas on a pre-determined schedule, providing an integrated health, nutrition and protection services.
IMC will also scale-up its implementation of holistic community engagement strategies on the promotion of long term positive change in the targeted communities. IMC will use the GBV community mobilization tool called SASA! to help communities rethink about “power” and the positive use of power, and EMAP to ensure men engagement with accountability to prevent GBV.
Furthermore, IMC aims to support more vulnerable women and GBV survivors with economic empowerment and livelihood activities through building their business skills, helping them to start small scale businesses, and encourage them to save though strengthening of existing and/or forming new Village Savings and Loans Association groups. These are proven effective strategies to fully realize the economic potential of women.
Finally, in order to ensure sustainability of the project and ownership by the community, especially of the WGFSs, IMC will continuously train Women’s and Adolescent Girls’ Committee Members on key Protection Concepts, GBV principles and referrals as well as basic knowledge on MHPSS and PFA. Bi-weekly consultations on specific topics, as well as joint quarterly safety audits, will serve to inform programming, community adaptions of safety measures and support as well as advocacy through coordination fora with key stakeholders to improve the safety measures in the target communities.

1 - Needs

Violence against women and girls is an endemic problem in South Sudan, exacerbated by ongoing conflict and chronic displacement. The full magnitude of the problem is unclear. However, studies conducted by UNICEF (2019) indicate that some 65% of women and girls have experienced physical and/or sexual violence in their lifetime; 1 out of 4 reported cases of conflict-related sexual violence is a child. Some 51% have suffered intimate partner violence, and some 33% of women have experienced sexual violence from a non-partner, primarily during attacks or raids. The risk of child marriage remains constant due to conflict, the country’s economic situation and harmful social norms that perpetuate issues of GBV. The GBV Sub-Cluster’s Strategy for 2019-2021 underscores the persistent and serious problem of GBV, which affect women and girls disproportionally. According to UNFPA (2019), though GBV remains pervasive throughout the country it goes largely unreported; GBV survivors have little to no legal redress. This finding is corroborated by the multi-sectoral needs assessment conducted by IMC in Fashoda County in December 2020, and by IMC’s GBV safety audits in Nyal, Malakal and Wau from 2019-2020. Individuals interviewed in Fashoda reported that women and girls are at high risk of sexual violence; poor economic conditions further compound this situation. Access to critical services such as healthcare including reproductive health; to women or girls-friendly spaces, and case management for GBV survivors were generally low. Women and girls who have experienced violence may not access services due to fear of being identified as survivors, and distance to facility remains a continuing issue. Furthermore, IMC is the only partner providing GBV services in Aburoc IDP Camp. Hence, women and girls reported walking long distance, as far as 10km in order to access services at the spaces. The situation is similar in Panyijar, where sexual assault against women and girls was reported to be prevalent. Poor economic conditions made girls especially vulnerable to sexual exploitation and abuse. Women and girls have limited access to opportunities such as education due to limiting social norms. Participants interviewed in Malakal during an assessment conducted by IMC in 2019 reported that GBV is widespread. Physical and emotional abuse, rape and sexual assault are among the most reported acts of violence. Perceptions of individuals who experience these forms of violence are generally negative as survivors are stigmatized or blamed for the violence that they have experienced. However, in Wau a significant barrier in accessing services is the distance to service points especially in Wau North where there are no GBV services. Meanwhile, in Jonglei State, UNFPA in 2019 reported that one in five of the reported GBV survivors experienced sexual violence (rape or sexual assault). Women and girls who have been displaced by the conflicts often reside in the camps or IO-controlled areas, where they face significant dangers collecting firewood, fuel, water, food and shelter materials. NGO and UN assessments have found that sexual violence is widespread in these sites. In 2020, REACH reported that domestic violence is the main protection concern among assessed women (25%); while early marriage was the main protection concern for surveyed girls (26%). Currently, there are no protection partners in Canal/Pigi. Overall, across all the target project sites, GBV continues to be prevalent while high barriers to reporting and services still exist due to sensitivity of the topic as well as structural and socio-economic reasons. Therefore, continuation of GBV prevention and response, including case management services is essential to ensure quality of care and comprehensive support to survivors.

2 - Project activities

IMC will implement the following activities using different approaches including community engagement and sensitization, specialized facility-based and mobile case management and PSS services provision, provision of dignity kits/material support, and capacity building of local communities and other service providers. Below are specific activities to be implemented: Increase access of GBV survivors to timely and quality response and referral services, through the provision of sex and age appropriate, confidential and survivor-centered case management, counselling and PSS, including referral to health care for clinical management of rape and other type of services. Internal case review sessions will be conducted on a weekly basis with all caseworkers for the purpose of supporting each other in self-care and in addressing special cases. To address the needs of survivors in a timely manner, individual case review sessions will also be conducted with each caseworker and facilitate proper follow-up and documentation. When need be, case review meetings will also be organized with relevant partners to further assist survivors facing various hurdles in accessing services. Implement GBV risk mitigation activities, through further strengthening of WGFSs programming to provide services, information and activities that promote healing, well-being and empowerment. IMC will also carry out holistic community engagement, such as Engaging Men in Accountable Practice (EMAP) and Start, Awareness, Support, and Action (SASA!) activities. IMC community outreach teams will conduct awareness raising activities through community awareness campaigns, street and radio drama/talkshows production, distribution and posting of SBCC materials. Existing drama groups of IMC will be tapped and trained to produce dramas and talkshows to be performed in strategic locations in the communities and/or to be aired in radios. IMC will also organize activities for the International Women’s Day, 16-day of Activism and Menstrual Hygiene Day. Improve participation of vulnerable women, including GBV survivors, in economic activities, through the provision of need-based Income Generating Activities (IGA) opportunities to at least 250 vulnerable women and GBV survivors across the 5 target locations. Each IGA woman will receive training in business management and leadership, supported to develop a business plan and will be given a seed fund to start and/or expand a business coupled with further technical support to ensure sustainability of their businesses. Furthermore, IMC will encourage and promote voluntary group savings (VSLAs) to support economic empowerment activities and as a way of ensuring the continuity of the IGAs. Each VSLA group will be provided with all the basic supplies required to start a functioning VSLA, such as cash safe with lock, calculator, record keeping journal, member savings passbook, ink pad, rubber stamp, ruler, savings bowls, padlock, etc. Each group will undergo weekly VSLA trainings, including sessions on business and leadership management, literacy and numeracy. Enhance capacities of frontline workers, key community leaders and community-based organizations on GBV prevention, mitigation and response. As a way of ensuring sustainability of this project, IMC will continue to provide various targeted trainings to key GBV actors – the frontline responders such as the GBV case workers, law and justice sector staff (security enforcement, customary law/local courts, prison wardens), clinical- and non-clinical health workers; community leaders; women’s committee members, among others on GBV basic concepts, and on quality GBV prevention, mitigation and response.

IMC has a well-established monitoring and evaluation system. At the field level, the Program Managers use the developed work plan that will guide implementation of the program. At Juba level, the Technical Coordinators, working closely with the M&E Coordinator, will provide technical support to the field teams on data collection, reporting and analysis pointing areas that need extra efforts to improve quality of services. Using the existing reporting tools (i.e. checklist, daily, weekly, monthly reporting forms, GBVIMs, etc.), program training reports and supplies consumption reports will be shared internally and externally with the Health Cluster, donor and other stakeholders. In addition to the formal reporting system, field program team will conduct quarterly joint supportive supervision, as well as review meetings to review the project’s progress against planned activities.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

IMC in South Sudan has developed country-specific guidance to protect beneficiaries from exploitation and abuse in humanitarian crisis. This Guidance complements IMC’s global safeguarding policies. All IMC employee has to undergo a training on PSEA, and sign a pre-employment commitment against sexual exploitation and abuse, as well as report any suspected cases. IMC will conduct regular awareness raising sessions with the community on PSEA through QAAPCs and CHWs. The project team will incorporate PSEA messages into CBFRM activities, especially during monthly AAP meetings with the community. The project team will also ensure posting/distribution of IEC materials at strategic locations in the community, including how to report PSEA cases. Regular sessions at the health facilities and other services point will be done on a monthly basis. Community focal points will be empowered through trainings to be able to pass on key PSEA messages, jointly with IMC project teams. For sensitive cases that arise, communities will be sensitized to report to the field site manager/designated focal point. The field site manager is responsible to log in the complaints into IMC reporting system, Ethics point, for further investigation. Communities will be sensitized to report any sensitive cases directly to Ethics point reporting systems for investigation. Ethics point contacts will be shared during community sensitizations.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

IMC will further strengthen and/or put in place various CBFRM mechanisms that will ensure the most vulnerable members of the community are catered for. IMC ensures accountability to affected population is incorporated from the design phase by consulting affected communities, with equal representation from women, girls, PWDs, elderly, etc., on their specific needs and vulnerabilities, that should be adapted by the project. During project implementation, the project will use different channels, such as AAP monthly meetings, suggestion boxes, hotlines, house-to-house visits, etc., to inform affected communities on their rights and entitlements, and mechanisms available to them to share feedback and complaints. The project will also consult communities on their preferred feedback modalities, and support set up of these channels where they are not functional. The mechanisms will be robust to ensure collection of all forms of complaints including cases of PSEA. Feedback and complaints coming from the various channels will be analyzed, and patterns identified to inform program adaptation where necessary. In addition, regular community consultations will be conducted to ensure participation of communities in the ongoing implementation and M&E activities. These will include assessing level of satisfaction with services provided, unmet needs, identification of lesson learned and recommendations for project improvement.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

IMC utilizes community-based feedback and response mechanisms (CBFRM) to solicit feedback and receive complaints. IMC’s CBFRM procedures provides guidance on: (1) effective community consultations – building upon community preferences on how to raise concerns and provide feedback; (2) major steps on managing feedback and complaints and how to explain our actions and decisions to the communities (including vulnerable and marginalised people); (3) Clear informed consent - IMC will provide all necessary information on how to manage feedback and complaint process, explain people’s rights and entitlements, as well as key steps that IMC will take to keep confidentiality of sensitive complaints, and (4) Routing and management of sensitive complaints, including SEA and safeguarding, including, protection measures and investigation pathways. CBFRM is aligned closely with the organization’s broader safeguarding and accountability policies; together, these procedures help ensure the systematic use and management of the feedback and complaint handling process across all field locations and programs. Building on the existing feedback and complaints mechanism, IMC will ultimately promote community participation on management and use of the feedback and complaints through community learning meetings, community Help desks, Post distribution Monitoring, AAP meetings and field registers as a safe and user friendly channels to collect, process and respond to the complaints according to IMC and community’ preferences, as well as disseminate information about the project. During program implementation, IMC will continue to enhance these already existing community accountability structures in the interventions guided by IMC South Sudan CBFRM country local procedures. IMC will share relevant program information including information on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure meaningful participation. Regular information on the program performance and monitoring results, staff code of conduct and PSEA reporting lines staff will also be shared. Communities will be sensitized on existing AAP structures as a means of sharing complaints and feedback. During these sensitizations, communities will also be consulted on the most context-appropriate and safe avenue to share complaints and feedback. The information will be used to strengthen the CBFRM in the implementation sites. Further, the existing AAP committees will be trained on receiving, handling and responding to complaints and feedback on the project interventions.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback and Complaints arising from the communities will be processed, analyzed and discussed during program management meetings. The meetings will develop responses and action plans to address the complaints and feedback raised. These responses will be provided through the AAP committees in each project location. In addition, the information will be keyed into a complaints and feedback response database for analysis and learning. During monthly/quarterly program review meetings, the information from the database will form part of the meeting reflections and will inform necessary programmatic corrections.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

IMC incorporates protection principles in its all its programs and its daily work by prioritizing the safety and dignity of all beneficiaries served. Consultations and other activities will take place in secure areas that are also easily accessible for groups who are extremely vulnerable and/or with limited mobility. IMC healthcare workers will be trained with a focus on gender-based violence prevention and survivor-centered treatment core concepts (clinical management of rape), considering the challenging environment that poses high barriers to vulnerable people, especially women and girls, in terms of reporting GBV cases, as well to counter the general lack of information regarding this topic. This proposed project will integrate GBV medical and MHPSS services to survivors, including counselling, management and proper referral for survivors of GBV, including rape survivors. All staff will be trained on protection, GBV Core Concepts and safe referrals. Service mapping and medical referral protocols are in place across IMC’s health facilities and project locations to ensure meaningful and safe referrals, including ensuring patient information shared with third-party service providers is only done so after informed consent of the patient is secured. Furthermore, IMC will continue to provide PSS interventions, including psychoeducation, support groups and peer support sessions for the most vulnerable groups, including women, children, GBV survivors and PWDs, at the WGFSs, household and community levels. These are conducted through small group discussions, tea sessions and community outreaches with the aim of bringing vulnerable individuals and group of people together, create social opportunities for their families and care givers of persons with psychosocial disabilities, and the wider community structures, which are tailored to ensure promotion of psychosocial wellbeing is achieved and the human rights of socially disadvantaged vulnerable persons are promoted. In addition, IMC will implement/pilot Self-help -plus (SH+) in all its project locations. SH+ is a stress management intervention based on Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) principles, for use by persons and group of people experiencing psychosocial distress and other stress-related conditions.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

Through an integrated and comprehensive health, nutrition and protection services delivery, and through working with partners, IMC will ensure optimal and sustainable service quality for acute malnutrition management, as well as improving IYCF practices through nutrition education, counseling, facilitation of support groups, complementary feeding and kitchen gardening at the women and girls friendly spaces. A sustained delivery of such interventions will then significantly contribute to the decrease of proportion of populations facing IPC 3, 4 and 5.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

IMC will conduct a Vulnerability Analysis to understand the needs, roles and dynamics of women, girls, boys, men, PWDs and elderly in different age groups. The project scope will also include coverage surveys and bottleneck analysis to identify barriers to accessing different interventions and identify solutions to overcome these barriers. IMC will work closely with protection actors to incorporate community awareness to address inequalities or protection threats. Furthermore, IMC will adopt SBCC approaches to eradicate stigma and norms against women, girls, PWDs and older persons. Regardless of community social norms, women and girls will be engaged through the program activities and all women and girls will have access to community feedback and complaint mechanisms to share their needs and issues regarding access to services. IMC will put in place safe and friendly systems for women to respond to their needs. IMC will also mainstream gender equality in program review and planning through deep analysis of the program results to assess impact of health activities to target beneficiaries, particularly women and girls. This proposed project is also designed and will be implemented to account for the challenges that may be faced by PWDs and Older People. IMC will promote accessibility of the project’s services by defining cross-impairment indicators for monitoring and ensuring that support is given to people with different impairments. All IMC staff and volunteers will receive protection training that highlights the special needs of these groups. Risk communication activities will also emphasize the role of the community on ensuring protection for PWDs and older people, and their immediate referrals to health facilities in case of any illnesses. IMC will use the IASC Guidelines on the inclusions of PWDs in Humanitarian Actions.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

IMC will adopt a multi-sectoral approach in services delivery in all its target locations, integrating health, nutrition and protection services. IMC directly implements nutrition-specific and nutrition-sensitive interventions, as well as strengthening the enabling environment for nutrition to address the immediate, underlying and basic causes of undernutrition through provision of both curative and preventive nutrition services. In areas/facilities where IMC does not implement nutrition programs, IMC will closely work with nutrition partners on the ground to establish a well-coordinated and coherent referral systems. Through this, IMC will contribute to decreasing the incidence and prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 and PLWs.
