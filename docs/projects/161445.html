Project Summary

REACH’s mandate is to support the information needs of the humanitarian community through coordination mechanisms such as OCHA, the clusters and technical working groups. REACH will provide support to the FSL Cluster (FSLC) and Needs Analysis Working Group (NAWG) through providing technical support to partners, participation in FSNMS training and data collection, and IPC Analysis workshops for Acute Food Insecurity, collection of perception based FSL data through the Area of Knowledge (AoK) remote monitoring system, and provision of ad hoc trainings such as ODK/mobile data collection upon request.

1 - Needs

Timely and accurate information on nutrition information and prevalence of acute food insecurity is necessary in South Sudan for the effective identification of areas in need, and prioritization of resources. Currently, food security and livelihoods (FSL) information needs are met primarily through the biannual Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS) data collection, however given cost and access constraints there is often large information gaps on the population levels of food insecurity throughout the country from month to month. REACH aims to continue providing support for high quality FSL data in South Sudan, as well as address FSL information gaps in hard-to-reach locations. REACH has been collecting multi-sectoral information through our Area of Knowledge (AoK) remote monitoring system since 2013, assessing the needs of hard-to-reach settlements and summarising indicative needs through monthly sectoral factsheets. In order to address FSL information gaps in hard-to-reach areas, REACH has developed and implemented the Integrated Needs Tracking (INT) System, in collaboration with the Needs Analysis Working Group (NAWG). The system uses data from REACH and partners (FSNMS, IPC, WHO, etc.) to assess risk factors in order to improve information gaps and to highlight potential hotspots for prioritisation and follow up with rapid assessments and verification missions. In a further development to the INT system, REACH will introduce the Shocks Monitoring Index (SMI) in early 2020, which aims to provide monthly tracking of shocks along several dimensions for both timely response and early warning for acute needs. REACH has been providing technical support to the FSL Cluster and partners from 2018 and throughout 2019 via direct technical support to partners for FSNMS Surveys, participation as Food Security technical trainers for the FSNMS, and participation in Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) Food Insecurity analysis workshops. To continue to support the high quality FSL data collection and analysis, REACH would continue to support these activities in 2020, as well as any other support that the NAWG, FSL Cluster and/or FSNMS/IPC may need in terms of technical support or capacity building of partners.

2 - Activities

Provide all activities • Incorporating FSL modules on REACH rapid assessments, including but not limited to coping strategy, livelihoods zones, and shocks modules • Providing technical support to partners for high quality and cost-efficient FSNMS surveys, on an in-kind basis • Supporting technical training both at the ToT and state level for the Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS)• Providing FSL technical support for the Integrated Phase Classification for Acute Malnutrition (IPC AMN) Analysis Workshop• Production of monthly FSL factsheet products for the humanitarian community and FSL partners using Area of Knowledge FSL data, and if directed by the FSL Cluster or Needs Assessment Working Group. • Provide other ad hoc technical support for the FSLC or NAWG as requested, for instance providing trainings on ODK mobile data collection, analysis of FSL or other data for the FSNMS/IPC process • Further development and maintenance of the Integrated Needs Tracking System for the NAWG
