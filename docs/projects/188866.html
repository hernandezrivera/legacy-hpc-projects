Project Summary

The goal of the proposed intervention is to reduce morbidity and mortality and to improve the
living conditions, resilience and dignity of affected populations in North-East Syria (NES).
This aligns with BHA’s mission by providing multi-sectoral emergency and early recovery
support to vulnerable people affected by the complex and protracted crisis in NES. The
integrated approach taken by LEARN in Phase III utilizes and builds on local assets and
capacities, mitigating the impact of current and potential future shocks. As well as contributing
to immediate lifesaving outcomes, this intervention lays the foundation for recovery at household
and community levels.
LEARN III also contributes to the three key objectives of the 2020 UN Humanitarian Response
Plan (the most recent validated version at the time of writing):
1. Save lives and alleviate the suffering of the most vulnerable people
2. Enhance the prevention, mitigation and response to protection needs
3. Increase resilience, livelihoods and access to basic services
These objectives are interlinked and interdependent, meeting acute humanitarian needs while
also building capacity and resilience in target communities; this holistic approach is reflected in
the design of LEARN Phase III.
Theory of Change
If the interlinked economic, health, shelter and WASH needs of conflict-affected populations are
safely met, then beneficiaries will experience improvements in physical, social and mental health
and wellbeing, will be better protected, and will be more able to increase their resilience, develop
positive coping strategies and thrive.
Purposes
As this intervention takes a holistic approach, all sectors are interlinked. For the sake of
simplicity and transparency, one multi-sectoral purpose is given here, and others are shown in
their respective sectors.
WASH: Reduce WASH related morbidity, mortality and public health risk for 110,514 people
(47,747 IDPs) from conflict-affected populations.
LEARN will:
Support solid waste management in conjunction with local authorities and communities.
Rehabilitate water and sanitation infrastructure, while also building the capacity of water
departments and communities for ongoing maintenance.
Provide appropriate toileting and bathing facilities in collective centers and informal
settlements (this will also be part of private shelter repairs in the Shelter section), considering
gender, age and disability.
Provide community-level WASH messaging, including solid waste management, hygiene
practices (including handwashing, menstrual hygiene management and COVID-19 prevention),
latrine usage and cleaning, and safe water storage and use.
Distribute essential hygiene materials, again appropriate for age, gender and disability.

4 - Needs

Inadequate solid waste management is due to low capacity of local authorities, as well as lack of infrastructure and community-level understanding. This leads to piling up of waste in neighborhoods and camps, creating conditions for transmission of diseases such as Leishmaniasis. Drainage, water supply and sanitation infrastructure have been damaged by conflict or fallen into disrepair. Households lack functional, dignified latrines and washing facilities, and many need to adapt or improve hygiene and water management practices, which are currently inadequate to prevent water-borne disease transmission. This is exacerbated by lack of access to essential hygiene materials.Healthcare facilities have inadequate WASH provision, including lack of handwashing facilities, toilets, bathrooms and access to water and sanitation networks.

5 - Implementation of activities

LEARN will continue addressing IDP and host community households’ (HHs) basic WASH needs in Al-Hasakeh, Ar-Raqqa and Aleppo governorates - including in ISs, villages, collective centers (CCs) and informal camps - through a comprehensive approach focusing on sustainable solutions. LEARN will develop its comprehensive and integrated WASH program consistent with WWG approaches, local authorities’ and communities’ needs. LEARN will ensure that services are provided in an equitable and dignified way by following Sphere & Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP) standards. Partners will use their expertise and experience to address WASH needs of IDPs and at-risk host communities in Al-Hasakeh, Ar-Raqqa, Deir-ez-Zor (WASH in Health) and Aleppo governorates, in continuing and new locations. Areas are selected based on low access to WASH, high level of water-borne disease (WBD) morbidity , collaboration with other sectors e.g. through the expanded CBA, and LEARN’s experience in the area. Access to essential WASH services will be improved in informal settlements (ISs), villages (IDPs & host communities) and collective centers (CCs with cross-sector integrated approach to effectively and efficiently address WASH needs and reduce health risks and morbidity cases due to WBD). LEARN phase out from some locations by handing over, transitioning to a sustainable service (e.g. from WT to rehabilitated water supplies) or completion of activities (e.g., after sanitation intervention).

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks include: • Inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across locations • Tension between host communities, IDPs and returnees due to varied assistance packages • Abuse of power by staff distributing goods or delivering services to vulnerable populations. • Road accidents, physical harm • Restricted access (due to insecurity of COVID-19 lockdowns) • COVID-19 public health concerns.Mitigation measures for the identified risks are integrated in SI’s implementation strategy, starting with transparent selection criteria agreed upon in coordination with the relevant clusters. The risk of abuse of power is mitigated by thorough vetting of locally recruited staff, who all abide by the SI Code of Conduct. SI also has a Feedback and Complaints Response Mechanism (FCRM) in place that allows for real-time feedback from beneficiaries and other stakeholders. The FCRM is an accountability tool that simultaneously facilitates the collection of lessons learnt that will be used to improve future interventions.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risks:Gender-based violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), tends to increase during times of crisis. Risks of this occurring further increase in situations of aid dependency and power imbalances. SI is cognizant of the inherent risks related to service delivery, notably for cash and in-kind distributions. To ensure that assistance delivered is safe, relevant and timely, SI has accountability systems and procedures in place.Mitigation and prevention measures:SI is determined to prevent the sexual exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries by humanitarian workers and reaffirms its goal of achieving full implementation of the six core principles adopted by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee Task Force on Prevention and Response to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. SI's PSEA Policy complements the Code of Conduct and Protection Policy, both of which state that it is strictly forbidden for any employee i) to use its position to obtain any favor, including sexual favors, and ii) to have sexual intercourse with minors. SI’s contracts and partnership agreements include a standard clause requiring sub-contractors to adopt policies that prohibit SEA and to take adequate measures to prevent and respond when required. A Whistleblowing Policy and a Feedback and Complaints Response Mechanisms Framework are key pillars of SOLIDARITES INTERNATIONAL Policies package. SI has PSEA focal points to identify and address any PSEA concerns and implements a referral system when require


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:• Access to vulnerable population and vendors is not further reduced by additional border-crossings restrictions • Operations are not further limited by regulatory restrictions inside Syria • Safety and security of SI and partner staff is not compromised • SI is able to appropriately manage the risks of aid diversion to designated terrorist groups or through corruption and bribery • Exchange rates and material/service costs remain reasonably stable throughout implementation period of the project • Sufficient materials and skills required for the output are available in the targeted geographic areas• Construction companies with sufficient capacity are available inside Syria• Labour with sufficient technical skills is available inside Syria• Security situation allows regular sharing of information between stakeholders• Communication channels remain open and enable relevant monitoring methods• Payment mechanisms remain open for the reimbursement of suppliers• Inter-agency coordination mechanisms minimize the risk of duplication and variations in levels of assistance. Risks: Non-materialization of the assumptions mentioned aboveMitigation: • Use money transfer system (hawala) for operations in North Syria including for the payment of its staff and contractors • Cooperation with Turkish authorities and capacity to work Cross-Border from Turkey. • SI maintains a representation in Gaziantep in Turkey with its main coordination office in Amman, Jordan.
