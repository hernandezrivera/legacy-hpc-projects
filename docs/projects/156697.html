Project Summary

Mercy-USA in this project will provide the most vulnerable IDPs in Ra’a camp in western rural Aleppo with garden seed kits for agriculture, tool kit, water trucking, and training, in order to support their livelihood as kind of exit strategy from the camp. Targeted beneficiaries 152 HH can be broken down as following (Men 138, Women 167, Boys 388, and Girls 337).

Needs

According to the CCCM cluster IDPs Sites Integrated Monitoring Matrix (ISIMM) of November 2018, there are 428,138 IDPs living in 325 sites in North West of Syria (NWS). The 325 IDPs sites are running 400 per cent beyond their capacity. Between July 2017 and January 2018, the satellite images on Idleb show an increase of 120 per cent in shelters in the main populated areas in northern IdlebAlthough IDP sites (including planned camps, informal sites, transit/reception centres and collective centres) are designed to act as an option of last-resort, the past year has witnessed an increase by 16 per cent in the number of IDPs seeking shelter in these sites. Against the backdrop of overcrowding, there is limited absorption capacity in IDP sites. Some sites are running by more than 400 per cent above their actual capacity. (HNO 2019)According to Intentions survey of IDPs in Reception Centres in Northwest Syria which was conducted by joint effort from HNAP, IOM and CCCM, some key findings are:-63.1% of IDPs interviewed stated that they intend to remain in the RCs and the main reasons given for intending to stay in the RCs were: insufficient money (51.2%), access to food in the site (22%), and access to humanitarian assistance in the site (9.8%).-21.5% of IDPs interviewed stated that they intend to move to another location in Syria and the main reason given for intending to move was work/livelihood opportunities (85.7%) in other areas. moreover, Intentions Surveys have shown that economic conditions and food scarcity are the two top factors influencing the intention of IDPs on whether to move out from the sites (or not)Based on the mentioned information, it is super important to start implementation integrated projects with other sectors in camps such as FSL, SNFI and WASH, which will play key role in support resilience of those IDPs, in order to be start point as exit strategy from camps and informal settlements in northwest Syria.Mercy-USA is focal point for Ra’a camp in CCCM cluster and distributes monthly food baskets and winterization assistance for almost three years, so piloting this project needs collaborative IDPs and good relations between people and the NGO which is exactly the case of Mercy-USA with Ra’a Camp.

Implementation of activities

Target is 152 HH (1030 individuals).Mercy-USA will conduct training sessions for IDPs in Ra’a camp to give them knowledge about this project and goal and outcomes behind it, moreover, all beneficiaries will be given:a. Printed instructions, and gardening guide.b. Community based technical training sessions (including pest and disease management, organic composting, food preservation).then MOU will be signed with beneficiaries including all agreement details. To this end, Mercy-USA will distribute agriculture tool kits:a. Garden rakeb. Garden hoec. Planting hoe for transplantingd. Watering cane. Hosepipe f. Hand compression sprayer 10 Lg. Plastic sheeting/filmsh. Sticky board traps (pest control) As well as spring seeds in line with FSL recommendations include:a. Tomatob. Cucumberc. Eggplantd. Zucchinie. Parsleyf. Beansg. Cowpeah. Onioni. Garlicj. Cabbagek. Chilli (hot pepper)l. Green/ Red pepperm. Okran. Lettuceo. RadishFurthermore, Mercy-USA will provide. weekly water trucking.Mercy-USA will follow up implementation on regular basis by technical staff for resolving any problem might be faced, and with MEAL staff for following up activities delivery within the proposed timeline. Mercy-USA will use lessons learned from this project to be applied for other IDPs sites with collaboration with CCCM, in order to achieve IDPs resilience and exit from informal settlements. Mercy-USA will engage the most vulnerable households including Men, women, youth, adolescents, boys and girls in order to maximize the benefit as much as possible.

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk/Threat:Livelihood projects are diverted to only benefit landlords and might also be perceived as an encouragement for the IDP to stay in the settlements whereby they will be considered as durable solutionsLikelihood: MediumImpact: HighMitigating Measure:1-This project will be implemented in Ra’a camp where there is strong participatory management of the camp.2-This project will focus on skill sets than exclusively on livelihood infrastructure.3- Continue ensuring that the ultimate objective is to strengthen the IDPs to seek durable solution out of camps
