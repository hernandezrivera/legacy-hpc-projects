Project Summary

Starting on 15 April 2023, fighting between the SAF and the RSF, the number of people in need (PiN) of humanitarian assistance has increased from 15.8 million, estimated in November 2022, to 24.7 million in May 2023, representing a 57 per cent increase.
The primary objective of this project is to safe live through supporting the newly displaced individuals who are the most vulnerable and marginalized people at risk through multi-sectoral lifesaving intervention. This project will prioritize activities that ensure life-saving assistance and protection to people affected by recent crisis primely and also people at risk of epidemics and floods, and other vulnerable residents - in order to reduce mortality and morbidity in the targeted areas. The proposed action will address 350000 (IDPs-175000(F-85750, M-89250), Returnees- 105,000(F-51450, M-53550), Vulnerable community-70,000(F-34300, M-35700)) crisis affected peoples. The WASH component will respond to the most urgent needs through Operationalization of water treatment plants in Khartoum, Water trucking in areas where supply is disrupted including health facilities and the establishment of distribution points, including in gathering and transit points; Essential WASH supplies and NFI emergency sanitation and handwashing facilities will be provided in hotspot locations; Hygiene promotion and household water treatment practices will be promoted as an immediate alternative, especially for those reliant on surface/river water. Following the immediate lifesaving response (stabilization and restoration), Rehabilitate, operate and maintain water supply schemes, replenish supplies and strengthen technical and management capacity for sustained service delivery will continue. This component will be complemented by Education, Child Protection FSL and ES/NFI activities: it will include provision of NFI kits, establishment of rapid-response communal shelters, distribution of tents for swift deployment in mass displacement scenarios. Additionally, upon local market and banking functionality, cash assistance for rent, emergency shelter construction, shelter repair, distribution of Emergency Shelter Kits (ESKs) will be included based on local market availability; Identify acutely vulnerable boys and girls via using outreach methodologies, provided with specialized child protection response services, identification of specific sources of threat and/or vulnerabilities of children, provided with gender & age appropriate (structured) psychosocial support and life skill, and communities are mobilized to support children through activation of child protection networks and community volunteers.
The FSL project will focus on Cash/Vouchers Based Transfer (Full Ration), Cash/Vouchers Based Transfer (Half Ration) related to food activity while the livelihood part will support on agricultural Support (Seeds, Tools), livestock Support (Distribution, Treatments, Kits), and livelihood Cash/Voucher Support. The Education project will supporting the most vulnerable and marginalized girls and boys at risk of exclusion from education. TGH will also strategically responding in the hotspot zones (conflict zone – Khartoum, Darfur) through local NGO and CBOs who have strong partnership with TGH, including AORD, & AL Massar. The education response programme aims to prioritize immediate critical life-saving education needs. The response will include awareness session to children age between 5 to 18 years, provision of life skills in emergency training to teachers, ALP Facilitators and PTAs, distribution of Education in Emergency supplies to both schools and safe learning spaces, support for the facilitators in the learning centers, providing psychosocial support through counselling and support to equip children with the skills they need to cope with trauma. By focusing on the same area where the education sector is being targeted, the WASH and child protection response will be delivered and integrated with it.

1 - Needs

The number of people in need of humanitarian assistance has increased from 15.8 million, estimated in November 2022, to 24.7 million in May 2023, representing a 57 per cent. (HRP 2023). In Darfur, violence has escalated, particularly in Geneina and Zalingei and with the involvement of civilians between local RSF-aligned Arab militias and members of the area’s non-Arab Masalit group. All 86 gathering sites throughout Ag Geneina have been burnt down as per OCHA. Sudan: Flas Update No. 13. 21 May 2023 and forcing more than 85,000 people into secondary displacement, mainly staying in the open and in public buildings. Thousands of people keep arriving in Gedaref and Al Jazirah according to UNHCR. Sudan situation. 11 May 2023., along the Khartoum-Madani Road, fleeing Khartoum where the shelling continues and is widespread. After over a month of fighting, Sudan is facing shortages of food, water and medicines, mainly due to the lack of fuel and market disruptions, while access to communications and electricity is limited in many parts of the country, and supply chains are down due to the closure of the aerial space. Education-Estimated 285,000 school-aged children have been displaced since the start of the conflict. 208,000 school-age children, 106,000 girls, and an estimated 15% of children with impairments are included in this. The violence has had a detrimental effect on the education of affected girls, boys, and teenagers, especially children with disabilities who struggle to receive inclusive, high-quality education in a secure learning environment. (HRP 2023). Additionally, the conflict exposes young people to a number of vulnerabilities that put their lives in danger, such as child labor and GBV. Additionally, when families depart, the number of displaced, separated, or unaccompanied children will rise. The crisis has traumatized students and teachers, damaged learning infrastructure and school spaces, and restricted access to schools, in addition to the pre-existing difficulties connected to a lack of resources. SNFIs– since the conflict happened on April 15, PIN of SNFI assistance i estimated to rise from 3 million to 5.7 million, while the target will see a more modest increase from 1.5 million to 1.9 million due to challenges with access, fuel, market functionality and banking services. (New Revised HRP 2023) Similarly, the FSL people in need are estimated to rise from 11.7 million to 19.9 million, while the target will increase from 8.2 million to 15 million due to the recent conflict in the county according to the FSL cluster report. The highest prevalence of food insecurity is expected in West Darfur, West Kordofan, Blue Nile, Red Sea and North Darfur. About 40 per cent of the population in phase 2 of the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) could slide into phase 3 if the conflict continues. The cost of food and other necessities has increased by 400% since the conflict began due to disruptions in supply lines and markets. Due to the recent conflict's large IDP inflow and rise in hotspot locations, the Child Protection PIN and target both increased accordingly. Child Protection PIN has increased from 3.5 m to 3.9 m while the target is increased to 2.5 m, an increase of 600,000 over the previous target of 1.9 m. The WASH PIN increased from 10.9 mill to 14.1 million people and the target increased from 4.8 mill to 6.05 mill people. Urban areas, which the 2023 HRP initially only partially covered, now have much greater WASH needs. Existing displaced individuals, including the most vulnerable locals, and a surge in displaced individuals in new regions, including across international borders, are serious concerns. Due to increased population density in new IDP concentration points, the already scarce water sources are under tremendous pressure. Additionally, the operation of fuel-dependent water supply systems and water trucking has increased by 300–400% as a result of the skyrocketing cost of fuel.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

TGH projects are designed to consider and minimize their environmental impact. Their imprint is considered in the development phase, and reviewed during the project cycle to allow for project modifications as needed. TGH, in Sudan and globally led from the head quarter, actively integrates appropriate measures to reduce its environmental impact. TGH procedures in Sudan facilitate reduction in paper usage, utilizing electronic signatures and electronic document validation where possible. In addition, as a phase one, TGH has installed solar panels on Um Dukhun base Darfur; with budget and work plan in place for phase two, to ensure all bases within Darfur as well as the country office in Khartoum, are supported by Solar panels. To reduce reliance on long-distance transport options, and thereby reduce fuel consumption, TGH prioritizes localized purchasing procedures. In particular materials for shelter kits, and durable latrines are available locally (sand, wood, stones, mud bricks, etc.). However the focus on these local materials is also not without impact. The use of locally sourced wood, found in nearby forests, can have a considerable impact on deforestation. As such, TGH links WASH with FSL activities to support the planting of trees and regeneration of forests through development of IGA for replanting. Ensuring the quality of the grounds in which we work is essential. TGH WASH procedures use lime when treating fecal sludge; lime being a natural resource itself, it is ideal to minimize the negative impacts of sludge on the community environment. Further, to reduce reliance on access to fuel and reduce pollution, TGH installs solar PV-panel to provide power to water points. These have the added benefit of reducing the fuel consumption rate for the water points, as well as reducing the water fee for community members. TGH installed data logger in the well to monitor the ground water. The data from the logger are used to understand how the ground water resource varies through time which helped to take action before depletion of the resource. Community awareness and engagement is key to the success of responsible environmental practices. TGH conducts community awareness sessions, in parallel with support to solid waste management committees, who can also then carry the messaging forward.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

For mainstreaming Protection, TGH endorses the principles defined by the Global Protection Cluster guidance on Protection Mainstreaming also endorsed by Sudan HCT Protection Strategy 2019 – 2021, namely:1/ Prioritize safety & dignity, and avoid causing harm: Prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the intervention which can increase people's vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks.2/ Meaningful Access: Arrange for people’s access to assistance and services – in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g. discrimination). Maintain focus on individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services.3) Accountability: Set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints.4) Participation and empowerment: Support the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights, including – not exclusively – the rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education. “Do no harm” is a core principle of TGH strategy, interwoven within accountability, inclusion and protection mainstreaming. This ensures all programs are designed to ensure participation and accessibility for communities without discrimination. As such, TGH undertakes a community based approach to all program design and at all stages of the project cycle. All projects commence with community consultation (FGD), with a focus on consultation with women’s groups (facilitated by women) and persons with special needs; along with local authorities. This ensures consideration of specific risks and accessibility for all community members. TGH can then adapt methodology to encompass these needs, to be able to target response to meet the needs of the most vulnerable, and facilitate inclusion without discrimination. An example of this flexibility is seen through TGH distribution methodology. Persons who have been selected as a beneficiary but cannot attend the distribution site, can identify this to TGH team during beneficiary verification process. TGH team then makes arrangements for direct household delivery for that particular person.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Accountability is vital to the way TGH operates. At every stage, beneficiaries are involved. People are put in the center of decision-making through involvement and dialogue as part of the growth of the communities' self-protection capacities. While preparing the implementation of WASH activities, TGH team will involve communities to participate in decision making such as during selection of construction site, HKPs or WUCs selection. In Education and Child Protection, PTAs, teachers and communities will be involved to select the relevant topics they want to receive training on, building on the necessary child protection basic training required. This bottom-up and inclusive approach promoting communities ideas and solutions will be encouraged through cooperation with communities members participating in these structures under the umbrella of Do No Harm TGH principles and strategy. i Age, gender, vulnerability or disability are taken into account while identifying beneficiaries and implementing activities.At the beginning of the project, TGH will present to the communities the different contact points for the Feedback, Complaint and Response Mechanism (FCRM) and identify with them, the most appropriate channel of communication regarding their constraints. Through this consultation, TGH will ensure that each community member has access to at least one channel of communication to report feedback and complaints to the organization. Feedback and complaints are recorded and centralized by the MEAL department or goes directly to the Country Director for most sensitive complaints. The MEAL department then ensure the proper reporting of feedback and complaints to relevant department and team members following TGH Feedback Complaint Response Mechanism procedure. TGH then ensures to close the feedback loop and provide the community member or group (if the feedback is not anonymous) with a clear response and outcome. All feedback and complaints are reviewed and integrated into practices in order to adapt and improve future interventions. PSEA policy is a key component of TGH accountability strategy in Sudan with TGH ensuring regular attendance at National PSEA meetings. Staff training and awareness campaigns are key to ensure teams maintain a ‘do no harm’ approach particularly with highly vulnerable groups such as women, girls, elderly or PWD. As part of International-National sustainability of action approach, TGH has ensured a PSEA referent both at headquarters and mission level, supporting implementation of the policy. All recruited staff participate to PSEA training at the beginning of their contract; along with refresher after 12 months. Any PSEA-related report would be immediately considered by TGH CD, followed by an investigation led by HQ if criminal behavior is potentially involved and donor would be informed of the risk. TGH PSEA policy was validated by UNICEF and UNHCR through their annual accreditation.
