Project Summary

This project overall contributes to Conflict Sensitivity of the list of priorities in the GFFO Strategy. While strictly adhering to humanitarian ‘Do No Harm’ principles, under the Protection Sector, and alongside the other Areas of Responsibilities including Gender-Based Violence, Child Protection and Housing Land and Property, UNMAS will ensure that all interventions are fully integrated within the humanitarian response, to ultimately protect and serve affected populations. Through the proposed action driven from Maiduguri, UNMAS will support the national authorities to reduce the impact of armed violence, contributing to a better understanding of the EO related aspect of conflict dynamics in northeast Nigeria. Essentially, UNMAS’ intervention strategy integrates capacity building of national authorities for an enhanced coordination, in accordance with broader frameworks such as the APMBC.
UNMAS Nigeria had received funding from Germany for the 2020-2021 and 2021-2022 projects, both entitled “Strengthening EO threat-mitigation capacities of Security Service Providers in north-east Nigeria'', which helped enhance the capacity of Nigerian authorities. In addition, “Gender Baseline Assessment in Strengthening EO Threat-Mitigation Capacities of Security Service Providers in North-East Nigeria” and “Assessment of the Impact of EO on Groups of Populations in Borno State, Nigeria” have increased the understanding of the situation, challenges and opportunities as well as provided valuable information to improve the effectiveness and efficiency of future activities. This proposed project will further build on the achievements and lessons learnt, as well as the results of assessments from the two projects to further enhance the capacity of the national authorities to protect civilians from explosive threats, with a focus on support to the establishment of an NMAC and capacity building for the NMAC and NPF personnel.

For this project, UNMAS proposes to support establishment of an NMAC and capacity development for its personnel in line with GFFO strategy’s priority Embedded to National Strategies. The project will, in consultation with relevant stakeholders, support the development of key documents for the NMAC which are essential for a fully functional NMAC, including Terms of Reference (ToR), a National Strategy, a National Workplan, and National Mine Action Standards. It will also focus on enhancing capacity of NMAC staff to conduct IM activities to collect, map and analyze the information and data on EO incidents, coverage by mine action activities and others, as well as conduct QA to ensure the quality of mine action activities which have been and will be carried out by Nigeria Emergency Management Agency (NEMA), Borno State Emergency Management Agency (SEMA), and the NPF trained through the past and this German-funded projects, as well as international and national NGOs and other mine action operators.
In realization of Gender Sensitivity in the GFFO Strategy, UNMAS will train NMAC staff on gender and diversity guidelines. In consideration of the recommendations outlined in the gender baseline assessment, there is a need to create gender parity and inclusion of women in explosive ordnance response. The gender training will be based on the UN gender guidelines for mine action programmes published in 2019.
Furthermore, the project will develop the capacity to establish and manage VA referral mechanisms for NMAC staff and support them in assisting victims of EO incidents in order to enable access to medical, psychological and other assistance for the victims of EO incidents and persons with disabilities.
UNMAS target: 10 direct beneficiaries for the establishment of the National Mine
Action Center and 200 direct beneficiaries during the training of NPF EOD personnel.
Indirect Beneficiaries: Conflict-affected IDPs, host communities, resettling IDPs under Borno State Resettlement Plan, humanitarian and development workers.

1 - Needs

According to the 2022 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) for Nigeria, the conflict stemming from the insurgency of non-state armed groups (NSAGs) in the north-east continues to erode living conditions, safety and security, especially for women and girls. The attacks and insecurity have displaced millions of people, hampers access to agricultural land and essential farming inputs (for example, the military and national counter-terrorism laws regulate and restrict movement of some non-organic fertilizers due to their potential use in manufacturing explosives). This is coupled with structural economic impediments like limited access to credits, inflation and restricted access to markets. The HRP has identified 8.4 million people in need for critical humanitarian assistance in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states of the northeast Nigeria. Approximately 1.2 million individuals including refugees, internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and host communities are exposed to the risk of explosive remnants of war (ERW) most notably improvised explosive devices (IEDs) in BAY states in Nigeria. These risks result in death and severe injuries, acute humanitarian and development challenges while threatening stability in the region and hampering safe resettlement and movements for refugees, IDPs, and returnees. UNMAS recorded 2,048 incidents involving explosive ordnance (EO) from January 2019 to June, 2022 involving approximately 4,000 casualties out of which 1,435 civilians were injured and 800 killed. The Nigerian authorities lack the requisite capacity to safely and efficiently dispose of EO and conduct other humanitarian mine action activities in accordance with International Mine Action Standards (IMAS). Clearance operations are mainly undertaken for military advancement and movement of convoys, but not to release routes and land for civilian resettlement and movement. UNMAS and its partners are not allowed to conduct direct EO and IED Disposal (EOD and IEDD) to remove, deactivate and destroy EO, which is crucial to protect the at-risk communities from the risk of EO incidents. The Nigeria Police Force (NPF) has a designated EOD Unit mandated to protect the civilian population and their properties, and able to deal with explosive ordnance blockages in post-conflict areas ahead of humanitarian aid, providing the requisite expertise and skill levels are achieved. According to GLIDE, FL-2022-000271-NGA, more than 2,000 mm of heavy rainfalls are projected from August onwards with intensification of floods and strong winds, storm and high incidence on displacement. The likelihood exists that during the wet season the local population will move from flood-prone lands to relocate in unknown areas where they could be increasingly exposed to potential EO threat causing multiple incidents. The needs for humanitarian mine action interventions in the northeast Nigeria remain highwith approximately 1.2 million people in need (PiN). According to the Humanitarian ResponsePlan (HRP) 2022, the needs are particularly significant in Borno with 959,083 people in need,followed by Yobe with 147,632 people in need and Adamawa with 66,517 people in need. Bygender and age groups, 19% of people in need are men, 23% are women, 27% are boys and31% are girls. By population groups, 57% are IDPs, 26% are returnees, and 17% are hostcommunity members.In 2021, the Borno State Government announced closure and consolidation of all IDP campswithin Maiduguri by 31 December 2021 along with the departure of IDPs to their areas oforigin and potential secondary displacement, are challenging the humanitarian response, andwill increase protection issues, including EO-related risks. As of 30 June 2022, six of theeleven IDP camps set up in Maiduguri have been shut down. IDPs are expected to eitherrelocate to their areas of origin or live with host communities. Many hundreds of people areon the move, including through areas contaminated by EO.

2 - Activities

According to the HRP and the Protection Strategy, Mine Action intervention is “a protectionactivity that has a palpable outcome in the Nigerian Humanitarian Response” as the explosiveordnance contamination continues to constitute a major protection risk in the country, whereone civilian is killed or injured every day by explosive ordnance incidents.In compliance to the HRP’s two sectoral priorities under Protection,1) to mitigate and respond to the harm caused to persons who have suffered violence,coercion, exploitation, serious neglect or discrimination, and to restore their capacity to livesafe and dignified lives – UNMAS developed a VA approach to facilitate survivors of explosiveincidents referred to a multisectoral assistance;2) To strengthen targeted protection services to address the needs and vulnerability ofpersons at heightened risks of violence, exploitation, serious neglect or discrimination;enhance their capacities and reduce the risk of resorting to negative coping mechanisms –Through a wide range of EORE initiatives, UNMAS ensures to reach IDPs, returnees, hostcommunity members, Government officials, service providers and humanitarian workers tosensitize messaging and promote safe behaviour. NTS is conducted in conjunction withEORE. NTS is a cost- and human resource-efficient way of surveying and releasing lands forsafe and productive use. Project Outcome: Relevant national and local entities manage the explosive threat, based on an accurateunderstanding of the scale and scope of EO contamination.
