Project Summary

This project aims to mitigate and respond to the critical adolescent learning challenges leading to high-risk coping mechanisms as a result from mental health crises among boys and girls, and menstruation health needs among adolescent girls in 20 schools in the West Bank, and East Jerusalem. The primary target group is adolescents, particularly those between 12 and 17 years old. Young people in oPt are exposed to high levels of traumatic experiences and violence due to the recurrent escalations, harassment (GBV), movement restrictions, house demolitions, imprisonment, etc. In emergencies, young people are particularly vulnerable to the risk of mental health and psychosocial consequences and destructive behaviors. At the same time, menstrual hygiene management (MHM) does not receive much attention and only a few interventions address this issue in a humanitarian context.
The continuous exposure to traumatic and violent experiences, leads to progression in high levels of mental, behavioral, and emotional issues among young people, especially adolescents that urgently need to be addressed. There is a humanitarian responsibility, to both protect and address young peoples’ needs
and to engage them in humanitarian preparedness, response, and recovery, where they can play a vital role. In fact, both mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) and MHM remain a cultural taboo in the Palestinian society, particularly in remote areas. In this direction, relevant adolescent life-saving services and information are out of touch and sparse, especially those offering adolescent and sensitive services for the unique needs of youth and adolescents.
Taboos surrounding menstruation have contributed to a lack of knowledge regarding MHM, the topic is barely addressed in the school curricula and misconceptions still exist. Teachers generally do not have adequate knowledge and skills to address menstruation-related issues. Capacities of schools and parents need to be built to ensure critical access to MHPSS and MHM through tackling cultural and societal barriers leading to stigmatization of mental and menstrual health. The project addresses the existing mistrust in confidentiality and stigma that hinder young people from seeking assistance. Moreover, it improves the ability to respond to menstrual health and reduce GBV and early marriage. Additionally, it provides young girls and boys with sound information about MHM, leading the former to be more conscious about their body, and the latter to create a more suitable environment, out of prejudices and misconceptions. Delivering quality MHM resources and information, through different physical and virtual platforms, is important for building the confidence and freedom of girls during adolescence. It is also a gateway for other gender-transformative interventions, such as life skills, sexual and reproductive health and rights, and community-level support.
UNFPA plans to respond to the growing need of MHPSS among adolescents. The organization’s mandate on sexual and reproductive health and gender-based violence gives the organization a unique expertise and a comparative advantage to integrate effective MHM in emergency programming. The project will cover communities in Area C, H2 and EJ, it will complement the ongoing interventions in schools with a focus on providing services through education specialists.
The interventions will cover the following key areas:
-Provision of dignity kits to adolescent girls and boys in 20 schools.
-Provision of physical and virtual adolescent and youth-sensitive mental health and psychosocial support services in schools, to increase availability and accessibility of such services to young people.
-Organize training sessions for educational personnel and students’ parents to increase skills in managing and providing adolescent and youth-sensitive MHPSS and MHM services.

1 - Needs

The ongoing clashes between Palestinians and the settlers in the West Bank, and the overall deteriorating situation in Jerusalem, causes more damage to the already fragile socioeconomic and political conditions. Over the past years, the media reported the destruction of physical assets; buildings, houses, and infrastructure, however, the psychological and mental toll on the people, especially children and youth, are often overlooked. Palestinian Young people typically find themselves forced to live under constant fear, family loss and insecurity. Such experiences can negatively shape their attitudes and in many cases have long-term psychological consequences affecting their learning, which are often underreported, under-treated, and underfunded. Building resilience through targeted life-saving humanitarian responses, when it comes to preparedness and response, is critical in the recovery process of individuals and communities. While relief efforts usually focus on the most immediate lifesaving needs such as food, shelter, and health, little attention is paid to the learning difficulties of vulnerable adolescents, especially young girls and those living with disabilities. As a direct consequence, poor psychological health and lack of girls' access to menstruation health are a solid reality. Adolescents (10-19 years) make up around a fifth (1.5 million) of the Palestinian population, with girls making up approximately half of this segment. Despite the important role that this group will play in shaping the future of the Palestinian society, Palestinian young people face numerous obstacles that limit their potential: a society structure in Palestine that is regularly characterized by patriarchy, gender discrimination, and unequal distribution of political and social power. These limitations are likely to negatively impact Palestinian adolescents and youth mental health, learning and overall well-being. Adolescents living in urban or rural areas or in a crowded refugee camp suffer from the effects of Palestine’s lack of political autonomy, sovereignty, and the consequent destruction of the economy. Tight restrictions on the movement of goods, energy sources, fuel, and people suppress economic growth, expose adolescents to traumatic and violent experiences, with the consequence of mental, behavioural and emotional issues. Moreover, the multidimensional impacts of COVID-19 on young people ranging from health, safety protection, education, and economic stability etc. are especially amplified in humanitarian contexts (COMPACT; IANYD Statement on COVID-19 and Youth). Routine health and education services and information are disrupted, especially those delivered through schools and universities. Particularly vulnerable – and yet often overlooked – are Palestinian adolescent girls. Although rates of high school completion and literacy are high, and Palestinian girls attend university in high numbers, they are frequently subject to a patriarchal culture and myths and misconceptions around their wellbeing, including menstruation. Research also indicates that they are highly vulnerable to child, early and forced marriage, as well as to sexual and gender-based violence. In Palestine, gender inequality, discriminatory social norms, cultural taboos, poverty, and lack of basic services impact girls’ and women’s ability to meet their menstrual health and hygiene needs in a dignified way. This can impact women’s and girls’ lives in many ways, limiting their mobility, freedom, and choices; affecting attendance and participation in school and community life; compromising their safety; and causing stress and anxiety. Emerging research has linked women’s sanitation and menstrual hygiene experiences with increased vulnerability to violence outside and inside the home.Therefore, the provision of critical adolescent school-based interventions focusing on MHPSS and MHM becomes crucial. The way young people are supported when it comes to preparedness and response is critical and has a lasting effect on the recovery process of individuals and communities alike. It is crucial to build resilience through targeted life-saving humanitarian responses. Specific focus on adolescent girls learning and coping with their menstruation health will be integrated in the scope of this project. MHM in emergencies must be considered in its cross-sectoral nature. Different aspects need to be included in the design and implementation of interventions. The provision of MHM materials and supplies should be accompanied by basic menstrual hygiene promotion and menstrual health education for adolescent girls. Programmes should also address harmful cultural or social norms and practices related to menstruation. As such, MHM awareness and sensitization activities should be integral not only to health, protection, but also education in schools.‎In this context, the use of positive coping mechanisms is highly needed to increase the accessibility and acceptability of services and promote a protective and self-helping environment. It is worth mentioning that UNFPA – as a member of the health cluster – is working closely with other organizations on MHPSS, including Save the Children and WHO, to ensure complementarity.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Provide introductory sessions for the targeted school students, parents and educational personnel on the overall project, including an educational session on PSEA and the referral system. Activity 2: Provision of school-based psychosocial support to 60 school personnel affected by education-related violations, COVID-19 pandemic and the recent escalations, through training, PSS service provision and awareness raising campaigns. Activity 3: Provide special training for the 24 schoolteachers to accommodate adolescents, with a focus on those living with disabilities, needs and dispense appropriate referrals in collaboration with the education and protection clusters. Activity 4: The trained school staff to implement PSS sessions targeting 1200 adolescents in the 12 targeted schools. Activity 5: Train 50 health workers in MHPSS to enhance the youth and adolescent health service and referral with the education system. Activity 6: Subcontract specialized organizations (e.g: sports for life, Nafas for music therapy, and Qitaf for Agricultural services and others) to organize MHPSS extracurricular activities to enhance psychosocial well-being and positive coping mechanisms for 250 adolescents. Activity 7: Support the existing help line (Shobak Al-Shabab/Youth Window), in cooperation with SAWA help line, to provide MHPSS counselling services – including MHM – for adolescents, according to their age and sex. Activity 8: Assist parents’ school associations in the organization of meetings and debates on how to cope with their children's needs throughout adolescence. Activity 9: Train 60 school personnel in MHM education techniques, in formal and non-formal programs. It is important to discuss and coordinate this activity with the Wash cluster in order to provide the right tools to deliver the trainings. Activity 10: Provide and disseminate customized hygiene/dignity kits to 1200 adolescent girls and boys in the targeted schools, in consultation with adolescents. Activity 11: Enhance the knowledge and skills of 40 peer educators, tapping into UNFPA’s YPEER network, in MHM practices, and the psychosocial effects of adolescence among female and male students. Activity 12: Y-PEER members to implement 36 outreach sessions and initiatives on MHM practices and wellbeing for students in the 12 targeted schools and in vulnerable communities. Activity 13: Support 50 adolescent-led school-based initiatives (physical and virtual) in partnership with the existing school’s students’ clubs in some schools, to boost their active engagement and participation in the 12 targeted schools. Working in schools means to reach all the schools' stakeholders, not only students. Teachers, parents, and the school's staff will be all engaged. This project will enrich schools' programmes and strengthen the overall education system. In line with this, planning and coordinating the activities with the education cluster is pivotal to better understand the context and address the real needs of young students.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Considering the ever-tens climate in the West Bank, it is crucial that the project integrates protection and prevention measures, such as risk mitigation measures, and adapts interventions accordingly. UNFPA will act upon the gained understanding with the priority to consider and uphold the rights and to not cause any further negative impact and harm. Safety and security will be guaranteed by upholding dignity and protection in the provision of services and information in schools, focusing on and strengthening inclusiveness. All the activities targeting educational personnel, parents, and students, will take place by adopting a disability-sensitive and high-quality approach, to allow full participation and leave no one behind, ensuring confidentiality and privacy. Training sessions and events will take into consideration different needs including for accessibility. At the same time, the specialized personnel need to be supported in delivering services and upholding their right and dignity through being treated equitably and fairly. For young people, it isimportant to establish a physically, emotionally and socially safe and supportive environment. Also, taking into account the existing inequities and divides among them and their communities, and ensure that, when participating in sessions – on MHPSS or MHM – they do not get backlash from community or family. Meaning, culture and customs must be respected throughout the whole cycle in order to ensure that people are not put at risk. To achieve this, beneficiaries will be sensitized on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) to ensure their safety and wellbeing.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

This project is designed to provide equal and impartial accessibility without barriers and discrimination through all planned activities. Impartiality is upheld through striving for a large outreach and accessibility in coordination with other operating actors and continuously conducting needs assessments. Boosting and improving young girls’ and boys' access to mental health and wellbeing, by comprehensive awareness and informational events and initiatives, is vital (e.g. utilizing existing referral systems). The project wants to meet adolescent needs, with a focus on adolescent girls, and taking into consideration possible differences in urgent needs, faced risks and traumatic experiences – which are gender, age, location-related.The project bears in mind the arrangement of peoples’ access and availability to services, including adapting services and information also through virtual platforms. This allows barriers to be tackled that may restrict freedom of movement and/or people from seeking help, for instance, making sure all disabilities are considered (people with reduced mobility and non-mobility-related disabilities).
