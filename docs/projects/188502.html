Project Summary

The project will continue mainstreaming IPC and Health Waste Management (HWM) activities in several HCFs, with a particular attention to the monitoring and follow up of the system, especially in those locations where HWZs where recently constructed and in continuation with previous CDCS funds.
The WASH component aims at consolidating and where needed establishing the safe system of healthcare waste management in several healthcare facilities in the Governorates of Deir ez-Zor, ar-Raqqah, Aleppo and al-Hasakeh. This will allow to strengthen the Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) activities carried out in all targeted health facilities and it is part of the Covid-19 response in NES. This action will also contribute to promote safe and sustainable human settlements for Syrian IDPs and local population in North-East Syria (SDG no. 11).
Regarding the disposal of expired medicines, the project will support the establishment of dedicated infrastructure and clear SoPs, and their application in facilities.
Specific Aim: Consolidating and increasing availability of health waste management and IPC services in healthcare facilities in Deir ez-Zor, ar-Raqqah, Aleppo and al-Hasakeh.

4 - Needs

The needs for HWM remain high in all NES, especially in light of current rise in COVID-19 cases in NES that made IPC activities, even more crucial. In the framework of health interventions, a correct and safe HWM is needed in order to prevent the environmental and health risks related to improper management of medical waste, and so to promote a do no harm approach. During the last two years, attention to HWM in NES has increased, with both local and international actors actively engaged in setting up health waste zones for the disposal of the medical waste. However, important gaps remain both in term of geographical coverage and quality of segregation’s systems. In order to ensure correct segregation and disposal, the construction of health waste infrastructures is in fact not sufficient and a continuous monitoring and follow up of the system is crucial to ensure its functionality and to promote the long-term behavioural change needed. Regular provision of HWM equipment and segregation’s refresher trainings inside HCFs, as well as the prompt implementation of rehabilitation works at HWZs level, are also crucial to avoid disruptions of the system. Disposal of expired pharmaceuticals still remains an important gap since: i) no formal system is in place from local authorities; ii) no ad hoc disposal areas/infrastructures exist; iii) the specific technical skills needed to set up the system are not available locally.

5 - Implementation of activities

Activity 3.1: Support, capacity building and monitoring at HCFs level to ensure correct segregation of medical waste. In continuity with past and ongoing interventions, UPP aims to consolidate and to increase the availability of safe health waste management (HWM) services in targeted healthcare facilities (HCFs) supported by UPP Health projects (differently as 188485 which is supporting HCF that are managed by COH) all across NES, with particular focus on HWM monitoring system and capacity building. Activity 3.2. Supporting and monitoring the systems of transportation and disposal of medical wasteIn order to ensure a comprehensive and functioning system of HWM, it is fundamental to ensure not only a correct segregation inside the HCFs, but also a proper transportation and disposal of the segregated waste. Activity 3.3: Integration of the current HWM system to include the segregation and disposal of expired pharmaceuticalsWith the proposed intervention, UPP will work to integrate the current system of HWM to include also the disposal of expired pharmaceuticals, that remains a strong need since no system is in place yet at local level.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. health risks associated with unsafe disposal of health waste: o health risk for the workers manipulating with waste – Mitigation: UPP with the partner ensures the provision of PPE for the workers transporting the HW and operating the HWZ facility, and provide training and SOPs to the workers for safe waste handling and operation of HWZ. 2. health risk for the population living in proximity of open dumping sites, where the medical waste is disposed off if a HWZ is not available or the waste collection – disposal system is not well established. This poses a risk of injuries and infection, especially for extremely vulnerable groups of waste scavengers (often women and children). The mitigation is the core of the action - increasing the availability of safe HW disposal in various locations by establishing the system of safe HWM from the point of waste generation at HCFs to treatment/disposal of the HW, coordinating with health care providers, local authorities and other actors and by regular monitoring ensuring the sustainability of the mechanism. 3. health risk of environmental pollution by the disposal of the hazardous waste, partners mitigate the risk by conducting feasibility assessment with consultations of local stakeholders, considering the distance from populated areas, water sources and agricultural land as key criteria, together with adaptation of the designs to eliminate the risks that could affect the public health and livelihoods.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Presence of Covid-19 cases in NES, inducing loss of humanitarian access, loss of part of the health workforce, evacuation or quarantine of UPP and impl. partner's non-medical staff; shortages of medical supplies; shortages of pharmaceuticals > priority mitigation measures/needs identified are: Strengthening of all COVID-19 measures already in place with particular focus on IPC, finding additional financial resources to support dedicated COVID-19 facilities; redirect resources from other activities to the preventions and mitigation measures. Improve coordination at camp level to protect the vulnerable. - Financial instability in the region, especially in Lebanon, Iraq and Syria, impacting timely payment, allocation of resources on direct costs, shortages of medical commodities > monitor any change in financial regulations, maintaining regular contacts with financial authorities and banks personnel in order to minimize loss due to additional taxation and/or restriction to the use and transfer of foreign currencies - Turkish forces expand “Peace Spring” operational theater eastward and westward to counter Kurdish presence in NES > maintaining access also unstable areas and negotiate access to accessible western territories with relevant authorities and military councils, revise project action plan and establish alternative operational procedures to restart service delivery as soon as possible in line Humanitarian principles. Move to remote management modalities where possible.
