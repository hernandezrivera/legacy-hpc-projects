Project Summary

The March 2020 Cadre Harmonisé highlights a serious deterioration in the food security and nutrition situation in Mali. Needs have increased by 29 percent compared to the same period in 2019 and by 32% comparing the 2019 to the 2020 lean season, the period of the year when food stocks exhausted and resources the most stretched. Currently, 3.6 million men, women, boys and girls are estimated to be food insecure, including 760,000 of them experiencing severe food insecurity (IPC phase 3 to 5). The situation is expected to deteriorate during the 2020 lean season with nearly 5.0 million food insecure men, women, boys and girls including close to 1.4 million severely food insecure persons (IPC 3 to 5). The most affected regions are Mopti (41% of the needs) and Gao/Ménaka (18% of the needs). The Sahelian belt is also affected, especially Kayes region with 15% of the needs as well as Koulikoro region (12% of the needs).
With a global acute malnutrition prevalence of 10% (SMART results, September 2019), the nutritional situation in the country remains worrying due to, among other issues, poor food practices, food deficit, civil insecurity which limits access to basic social services and the prevalence of diseases linked to poor hygiene conditions.
This alarming food security situation will deteriorate further due to the effects of the COVID-19 epidemic in Mali.
The latest analyzes of the country's food security situation show a deterioration of the food security and household livelihoods .
Considering the deterioration of income, especially in urban areas, and inflation on imported products; WFP (VAM, regional office) estimates that the number of people in severe food insecurity in Mali could raised up to a range of 2.2 million and 2.7 million: meaning an increase between 0.8 to 1.4 million more people than the Cadre Harmonisé forecasts for the lean season period (June-August 2020). As part of the mid-term review of the humanitarian funding call, the Food Security Cluster (which brings together all the Food Security actors) estimated at 2.4 million the number of people that are now in severe food insecurity, that is 1 million more than the last figures of the March 2020 Cadre Harmonisé.
In support to the Government and in coordination with other humanitarian actors, WFP will provide food assistance to the most vulnerable men, women, boys and girls in areas identified by ENSAN and the Harmonized Framework as the most affected by food and nutrition insecurity. This assistance will be provided through cash-based transfer (cash or vouchers) and or food distribution depending on the cost-efficiency analysis and operational feasibility. WFP will promote women's participation in this process and in all other discussions related to this assistance so that their specific vulnerabilities and protection requirements are considered. WFP will also continue to provide technical assistance to the Government institutions (CSA, SAP, MSAH, Jigisemejiri) for food security analysis and monitoring, and food security and nutrition assistance.
WFP seeks to involve affected populations, including men, women, boys and girls, in all steps of the programme design, implementation and evaluation. To do so, WFP focuses on three key areas: first, information provision to affected men, women, boys and girls, in a timely and accessible manner. This includes information provision about their rights to provide feedback and be consulted. Second, consultations to seek and include the views of all segments of the affected men, women, boys and girls, including through focus group discussions. Third, through complaint and feedback mechanisms (CFM), that includes, but is not limited to a toll-free number and complaint committees which include women and men, young and old people. These tools are regularly reassessed in consultations with the affected populations, to ensure they are inclusive and relevant.
