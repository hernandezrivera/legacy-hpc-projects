Project Summary

This project aims to address critical humanitarian needs across three sectors to reach 55,448 IDPs and host communities in Dilling, Kadugli, and Abu-Jubeiha localities of South Kordofan. The project will respond to needs and gaps due to the conflicts between government security forces and the Sudan People's Liberation Movement-North (SPLM-N) erupted in parts of South Kordofan and Blue Nile in 2011, resulting in an additional 354,474 people living in displacement settlements. In addition to recent ethnic clashes between Misseriya and Nuba tribes in October 2022 the displacement of about 450 people to Kadugli and Dilling localities. The project aims to save lives and improve the food and nutrition security, access to safe water and hygiene services, increase protection prevention and response capacities through implementing the following activities:
1) FSL: provision with farming inputs and services to 4000 farming HHs; support the vaccination of 400,000 heads of animals against prioritized diseased, support 2000 agro-pastoral HHs to access emergency livestock treatment services; conditional cash transfer to 1000 HHs for 30 days; and goats restocking to 150 HHs
The FSL interventions target a total of 55,448 individual including: 12,753 men; 13,308 women; 14,416 boys; and 14,971 girls
2) WASH: upgrade of 1 hand pump to solar operated mini water yards, and maintenance of 20 malfunctioning hand pumps to reach 11,500 people with clean and safe water for domestic use, provide hygiene promotion awareness and WASH NFIs to 3,000 HHs
The WASH interventions target a total of 22,300 individual including: 5129 men; 5,352 women; 5,798 boys; and 6,021 girls
3) Protection: support community-based protection networks to facilitate referral of GBV and child protection cases to healthcare, awareness raising on GBV prevention and response to 1500 people, support foster care to unaccompanied and separated children
The protection interventions target a total of 7,200 individual including 1,656 men; 1,728 women; 1,872 boys; and 1,944 girls

1 - Needs

South Kordofan communities rely primarily on farming as their primary source of income and food; nevertheless, inter-communal conflict and tension between pastoralists and farmers over natural persist. Furthermore, rising costs of production inputs have a negative impact on agricultural production, leading to increased risks of food and nutrition insecurity. In the previous farming seasons, South Kordofan was affected by the impacts of climate change including flash flooding and dry spells, resulting in failure of agricultural seasons creating production-consumption gaps, and increasing the levels of food insecurity. On the other hand, access to safe water is limited; functional water sources are often far and contaminated, increasing risks of water-borne diseases. Moreover, in South Kordofan, boys and girls are exposed to violence such as early marriage, female genital mutilation, rape, and child labor. In 2022, tribal conflict broke out in several localities in South Kordofan state, with an estimated 40,000 people reportedly displaced. There has also been growing tension due to increased attacks by armed gangs along the roads. Inter-communal conflict in West Kordofan’s Lagawa locality in October and November 2022 led to the influx of people – mostly from Nuba tribe – into Kadugli and Dilling localities of South Kordofan. About 320,000 people in South Kordofan (16% of the state population) were determined to be in crisis and above levels of food security between October 2022 and February 2023, according to the Sudan IPC analysis. About 950,000 people in South Kordofan state are in need for humanitarian assistance according to Sudan HNO 2023.Fierce clashes erupted between SAF and RSF in multiple cities across Sudan (April 15, 2023) including Khartoum, Marawi, Al-Fashir, Al-Obeid, Nyala, Zalinge, Geneina, etc. This devastating development in Sudan resulted in killing of more than 3000 people, injury of 6,000 people, and displacement of about 2 million people inside and outside the country. The fighting includes frequent airstrikes, shelling, and other explosive incidents targeting military and civilian infrastructure. Looting and destruction of civilian property is widespread, especially in Khartoum and throughout Darfur, as armed groups exploit the outbreak of conflict to steal from banks, shops, offices, and homes. Political bodies, governments, and international entities are calling ceasefire. It remains unclear when the fighting will stop and what will happen next. It is expected that regional and international actors will push for de-escalation and halt of fighting. In the meanwhile, humanitarian actors continue to monitor and report on the situation, and look for opportunities to provide immediate relief services to the most affected people, whenever safe humanitarian passages are created. The security situation has been mostly stable in South Kordofan, but unpredictable with the nationwide political and security upheaval. The current disruption to lives, livelihoods, institutions, systems and programs will definitely have an impact on the fragile humanitarian situation in the target areas, and food insecurity is expected to increase. There are shortages in access to water, fuel, cash, and health services, which will exacerbate the dire situation of people directly affected by conflict and all across Sudan if this crisis continues. The proposed will be especially importnant to mitigate risks and stresses associated with the ongoing conflict in Sudan. In the three target localities VSF G intends to provide live-saving humanitarian support with FSL activities to a total of 55,448 individual including: 12,753 men; 13,308 women; 14,416 boys; and 14,971 girls; WASH activities to a total of 22,300 individual including: 5129 men; 5,352 women; 5,798 boys; and 6,021 girls; and protection activities to a total of 7,200 individual including 1,656 men; 1,728 women; 1,872 boys; and 1,944 girls.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

VSF-G will carry out intensive community mobilization and sensitization campaigns for the target beneficiaries along with the local partners, to effectively inform the target communities on the project and ensure participation and adoption of the project activities. The working methodology will orient the target people on accountability/CFMs and basic protection concepts. As well as the project beneficiaries will be sensitized on gender inclusion (at least 50% women participation in project activities will be ensured). Participation of girls/women and boys/men of all age groups with and without disabilities will be considered through: (a) beneficiary targeting for the different activities (i.e. beneficiary selection criteria which will be developed employing community participation approaches, to reach those who are most vulnerable among affected people including IDPs, returnees, pastoralists and farmers. With focus on families with malnourished children, female-headed households and families with disabled member(s)); (b) selection of water points (sites) to be rehabilitated or upgraded taking into account the safety of women and girls and other vulnerable members of the communities; (c) ensuring women representation in all community committees (at least 30%); (d) CFMs will assist in early detection of problems such as targeting (exclusion/inclusion) and misconduct; (e) the safety and dignity of beneficiaries will be prioritized through assessing and identifying risks associated with different locations/activities, to ensure implementation/distribution sites and roads leading to these sites are safe for all people involved (particularly girls, women and disabled persons), and that beneficiaries will not face problems with theft, intimidation and extortion; (f) and special arrangements will be made for people with special needs, as appropriate. Moreover, VSFG always tries to create equal access and opportunities for all community members to benefit from humanitarian interventions through adequate community sensitization; inclusive participatory beneficiary selection and verification processes; and implementing Accountability to the affected Population (AAP) and Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFMs). Through the abovementioned approaches we expected to be able to reach girls/women and boys/men of all age groups with and without disabilities equally with the planned project activities. VSF-G fully considers “do no harm” in its activities through application of conflict-sensitivity, inclusive targeting of people from different ethnic groups, pastoralists and farmers, selection of distribution sites, and sites for different facilities (water supply infrastructure). The integrated project design addresses needs across multiple sectors including protection mainstreaming in WASH and FSL.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Planning of this project interventions is majorly based on the feedback from the target communities and other key stakeholders in the target locations. VSF-G adopts Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS), Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP) guidelines and Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Task Force on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) in setting standards, building capacity, communication, participation, monitoring and reporting on compliance, addressing complaints and development of strategy to improve the quality of partnership on principles of accountability. VSF-G approach focuses on assessing and addressing the needs of all within the communities, including men, women, boys, girls, elders, persons with disabilities among other vulnerable and marginalized groups. Community participation will be promoted through all the stages of the project (planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation), community consultation meetings and orientation sessions will be held at the beginning of the project to verify needs, gauge acceptance, ensure the project is well understood by target communities, raise the communities’ awareness with regard to their participatory roles and responsibilities, and the responsibilities of the program partners towards them, as well as to identify the most appropriate channels for communication. Community-based committees including representatives from a cross-section (with respect to age, gender and diversity) will be formed to participate in developing an effective communication strategy including appropriate complaint and feedback mechanisms (e.g. hotline, complaints/suggestions boxes), taking into consideration literacy rates, availability of communication devices and accessibility to VSF-G’s ad partners’ offices; to ensure accessibility of all community members (men, women, children, elders and disabled people) and provision of timely response. Community committees will also be involved in developing beneficiary selection process, follow-up of activity implementation and project evaluation
