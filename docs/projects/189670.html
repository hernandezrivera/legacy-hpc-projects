Project Summary

Concern will continue to support preventative and curative nutrition services in Rubkona and Guit county in Unity state, Aweil West and Aweil North in Northern Bahr el Ghazal, in Juba IDP camp and Kajo-Koji in Central Equatoria states following the national Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) and community based management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) guidelines. The treatment package include management of severe acute malnutrition through the Outpatient therapeutic programme and stabilization centre (OTP/SC) and management of moderate acute malnutrition through the targeted supplementary programme (TSFP). Concern will emphasise prevention of acute malnutrition in this response through the focus on behaviour change communication, promotion of home gardening to improve MIYCN practices. In Juba IDP, camp (former PoC) Concern manages two OTP/TSFP sites while SAM with medical complications are referred to stabilisation centre managed by other partners. In Kajo-Keji Concern provides nutrition services in 4 mobile and one static health facilities. In Unity, Concern provides nutrition services in 14 sites, while in Leer county in Unity, Concern supports local partner Nile Hope to provide nutrition services in 5 sites. Similarly, in NBeG Concern provides nutrition services in 50 sites both in Aweil North and Aweil West counties. The Caregivers provide treatment for the majority of children with severe acute malnutrition in the home using nutrition supplies and routine medical care. When necessary, severely malnourished children who have medical complications or lack an appetite are referred to in-patient facilities for more intensive treatment. To ensure community participation and improve access and coverage, Concern will involve community volunteers for community mobilization, screening, follow up cases and dissemination of recommended nutrition behaviours. Concern has developed a mothers and fathers group curriculum to re-inforce the mother and father group sessions in the community.
The level of acute malnutrition often attributed to severe food insecurity, poor access to health and nutrition services, extremely poor diets and limited access to clean water, poor sanitation and hygiene. Therefore, the nutrition service provision will be integrated with provision of WASH, health and food security assistance where possible and appropriate.

1 - Needs

South Sudan remains one of the worst humanitarian crises in the world, the number of people in need (PIN) is growing; with 8.9 million people (70% of population) projected to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2022. The primary needs reported in the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) are food insecurity or lack of food, which weakens people’s health and nutritional status. In 2022, an estimated 2 million people in South Sudan will be at risk of acute malnutrition, including 302,078 children (148,018 boys and 154,060 girls) with SAM, 1,039,214 children (509,215 boys and 529,999 girls) with MAM, and 675,548 pregnant and lactating women with acute malnutrition (Source: HNO 2022, South Sudan). There is slight increase of 100,000 people (5.2 per cent) in 2022 compared to 1.9 million people who needed treatment for acute malnutrition in 2021. According to the FSNMS+ round 27 results, 26 counties in six states have Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence above the emergency threshold of 15 percent (very high acute malnutrition level WHO classification). In the proposed locations, the latest food security analysis indicated that; Guit, Leer and Rubkona Counties are projected to face extreme food insecurity; while Aweil North, Aweil West and Kajo-Keji Counties are projected to face severe food insecurity. The chronic food insecurity, high levels of morbidity, poor diets and feeding practices, and poor sanitation and hygiene behaviours attributed to high levels of malnutrition. The escalated flooding resulted in displacement of people and access constrained to nutrition and other basic services. There is higher need for continuation of integrated nutrition services to sustain and avoid escalated nutrition or food insecurity crisis The admission trends for 2021, reflects continued needs for nutrition services to ensure that malnutrition prevalence will not escalate beyond the normal trends. Despite change in admission criteria and mass screening due to COVID-19, the admissions in all Concern nutrition sites exceeded the initial projected caseload for 2021. In proposed locations, the latest malnutrition analysis, indicate a GAM prevalence of 15.2%, in Rubkona and 14.3% Guit. In Juba, GAM is reported at 10.3%. In NBeG, GAM rates of 9.8% in Aweil North and 9.1% in Aweil West, while Aweil South is 23.1%. In Kajo-Keji, the GAM rate is 4.8%, however as our assessment most of children still staying in Refugee camp in Uganda. Concern will provide both preventing and curative nutrition services in all existing nutrition sites.

2 - Project activities

CO1: equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition: In order to improve the knowledge and practice of MIYCN, Concern will strengthen prevention of acute malnutrition by focusing on behaviour change communication. This will be done through individual counselling and group sessions at nutrition sites and community level. Beneficiaries will receive soap to promote good hygiene practices and mosquito nets to prevent children from malaria. Moreover, mothers and fathers groups will be established for repeated sessions. Gender transformative topics are included in the curriculum for both mothers and fathers groups to improve the knowledge and commitment of men in maternal infant and young child nutrition. Demonstration gardens will be established in nutrition sites for mother to learn on how to produce vegetables. In Aweil North, WFP support Concern on BSFP to prevent acute malnutrition among children under 2 years. Vulnerable HH will be targeted for BSFP and GFDCO2: equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition: Concern will continue to respond to the management of acute malnutrition through the CMAM approach for the treatment of both severe and moderate acute malnutrition, following the National CMAM guidelines. In Unity, Concern will provide nutrition services in 14 sites, while in Leer County, Concern supports local partner Nile Hope to provide nutrition services in 5 sites. In Juba IDP camp, Concern will provide nutrition services in two sites and in Kajo-Keji Concern will provide nutrition services in 5 health facilities. Similarly, in NBeG Concern will provide nutrition services in 50 sites both in Aweil North and Aweil West counties. Severely malnourished children without medical complications will be managed through the weekly OTP while those with medical complications will be referred to the inpatient care until the medical complication is resolved and return to OTP to complete treatment. Children discharged from OTP will be referred to TSFP to prevent relapse. Children and PLW with moderate acute malnutrition will be treated in TSFP. Only in Juba IDP camp elders will be targeted for TSFP based on the WFP plan. To maximize the coverage, CNVs will conduct screening, follow-up of absentees and defaulters. Concern will continue to scale up family MUAC approach.CO3: To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information: Concern will continue to contribute to the national database by providing monthly NIS data to the nutrition cluster: The nutrition cluster analyses the admission trend and exit performance indicators and shares with partners during the bi-weekly coordination meetings. Concern also has a CMAM database to monitor the admission trend and exit performance indicators on a monthly basis. In order to estimate the prevalence of acute malnutrition, to evaluate the impact of nutrition intervention and to contribute to IPC analysis, Concern will conduct SMART surveys in some of the priority project locations. CO4: To increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors: Concern will use all the possible ways to integrate the nutrition with other sectors. The nutrition sites are integrated with health facilities, all SAM cases at admission and SAM and MAM cases with malaria symptoms will be tested and treated for malaria and those who are not vaccinated will be referred to the health facility. To prevent malaria, beneficiaries will receive mosquito nets. Concern provides soap for OTP and TSFP beneficiaries as part of good hygiene practices. Concern promotes home gardening for OTP, TSFP beneficiaries and mother groups. Moreover, cases with protection issues will be referred to the protection partners working in the area.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

During project implementation Concern will closely collaborate with government health department, community leaders and communities. The Camp Management Committee (CMC), sector and block leaders in IDP camps and boma and village chiefs in other locations will support supervising and nominating Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNV) lead mothers and lead fathers based on agreed selection criteria. CNVs and lead mothers will continue to screen for malnutrition, defaulter tracing, early case detection and referral of malnourished children 6-59 months. The regularly monitoring and quarterly reviews will collate the feedback, lessons and will inform about necessary adaptation to project assistance. Similarly, the Complaint and Feedback Mechanism and regular consultation with beneficiaries during monitoring will provide opportunity to beneficiaries to share their feedback which will be considered in adapting the program accordingly. Concern will consult with the beneficiaries about the site selection and distributions location/timing and plan according to the feedback from the community.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Concern understand different vulnerabilities within target communities that may exclude target beneficiaries in their access to feedback and complaint mechanism. Therefore, Concern will consult with the target community, the local chief and state authorities to understand the overall target population profile, access to communication channel and nature of vulnerabilities. While establishing the Feedback and Complaint mechanism, Concern will consider such vulnerabilities and access constrained therefore the target population will be consulted on their preferred mode of complaint/feedback channel. Different mode of feedback such as complaint boxes, phone numbers, email, verbal feedback/complaint during Concern monitoring visits, in-person to Concern office and Help desk at distribution points/beneficiaries registration points will be used to avoid exclusion of access as for as possible considering the operational challenges in target locations. Concern will use both pictorial, written and verbal channels in local languages to communicate the feedback mechanism to target beneficiaries. The individuals living with disabilities, illiterate, women and girls can also share their complaint/feedback through lead mothers and CNVs hired from local community.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Concern has a Code of Conduct and its associated policies including Program Participant Protection Policy (P4), Child Safeguarding, and Anti-Trafficking in-person Policy. Concern staff and any one engaged by Concern including consultants, contractors, partners and visitors are required to understand and comply with Concern safeguarding policies. Concern’s Code of Conduct and Associated Policy is consistent with the six core principles of IASC Task Force on PSEA in Humanitarian Crises. The safeguarding focal points each office ensure implementation and compliance with Concern safeguarding policies. During project inception and regular consultations with the beneficiaries and relevant stake holders, Concern will inform them about right to assistance, entitlements, right to appropriate behavior including Concern’s commitment to zero tolerance on Sexual exploitation and abuse by humanitarian workers. The beneficiaries will be informed about the Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) to raise their voice in case of any issue pertaining to PSEA. The monitoring mechanism will ensure that the beneficiaries are aware about PSEA and how to complaint in case of any exploitation and abuse. In all locations, Concern has a Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) where beneficiaries can register complaints about gender related or protection concerns which are referred to Protection partners. All recruitments of staff, volunteers and trainings will ensure that there is equal chance to women and men to ensure balanced representation. Information will be collected on safety and protection concerns and to improve future programme designs.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Concern’s policies and operational guidelines are in line with IASC’s five Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations. At the start of project implementation, Concern usually introduces the project to community leaders such as block and sector leaders; the Camp High Committee (CHC); boma and village chiefs and payam administrators, County Commissioner and the RRC; the SMoH; and the CHD. CNVs also conduct community mobilization and sensitization to ensure community is aware about the program, target groups, targeting criteria and entitlement. Caregivers will be informed of admission criteria and beneficiary entitlements (size of the ration) during TSFP/OTP days, which will also be displayed at nutrition sites. The banners and IEC materials displayed at nutrition sites will have information about beneficiary entitlements and feedback mechanism. In IDP sites, the Camp Management Committee (CMC) and block/zone leaders will be informed about the feedback mechanism. The staff will regularly orient the caregivers and mothers about the admission criteria, entitlements, feedback mechanism, their right to assistance and complaints during the OTP/TSFP days and in counselling sessions. The mother and father groups will be sensitized about feedback mechanism. In IDP camp, the camp manager implements the CRM but Concern will have a Community Feedback Mechanism (CFM) in all sites where beneficiaries can register suggestions, feedback or complaints. The monitoring and CRM (Dedicate staff) will conduct regular visit to refresh beneficiaries about the right to complaint, suggestion and how to make complaint.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

During assessments, Concern will coordinate with CCCM and Protection partners and identify safety, security and protection risks and findings will be used to adapt the programme. Distribution sites will be located in consultation with communities to ensure they are in safe locations. Prior to distributions, Concern will conduct sensitisations on how beneficiaries can reduce protection risks such as travelling in mixed groups and during daylight hours. Concern ensures gender balance staffing and ensures that all staff aware about protection and gender related risk. The CRM will be used to identify protection cases and make subsequent referrals to Protection partners. The elderly enrolled in nutrition program will be assisted in their access to nutrition program/messaging. Concern safeguarding policies require all staff and any one engaged in its programs to comply with code of conduct and reporting of breaches. The beneficiaries will be sensitised on CRM and their right to share feedback and complaint. Concern will ensure that the staff were trained on GBV, aware about referral pathways and sensitise the beneficiaries on reporting and use of GBV pathways.
