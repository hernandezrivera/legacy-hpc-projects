Project Summary

This project is in line and contributes to HRP Coordination and support services. It seeks to respond to the prolonged humanitarian crisis in the occupied Palestinian territories affecting the Palestinian population under occupation, by contributing to the protection of their rights and dignity in accordance with IHL and IHRL, and with a cross-cutting focus with other clusters such as health and education. The project will support the implementation of the HRP 2023 and is aligned with the strategic objectives of the Protection Cluster as it seeks to increase respect for the IHL and IHRL and accountability for violations as well as support protection response mechanisms to prevent and mitigate the effects of occupation and conflict-related violence.
Child Protection is an Area of Responsibility and Mental Health and Psychosocial Support Services under the Protection Cluster, with UNICEF as the designated lead agency. The CP-AOR/MHPSS is an active member of the Protection Cluster, maintains regular contacts with the Cluster and contributes to overall needs identification, protection analysis, and coordination processes. The CP-AOR/MHPSS’s role is to ensure the timeliness, quality and coverage of coordinated child protection/MHPSS humanitarian responses. Currently the CP-AOR/MHPSS has 30 active members in the West Bank and 30 in Gaza.
This project ensures the effective and efficient coordination of CP/MHPSS humanitarian actors in the West Bank and Gaza, including a common situational awareness and timely, appropriate and effective humanitarian response. It will ensure the regular coordination of CP partners and support a strategic humanitarian response, including the strengthening of needs analysis, contingency planning, promotion of evidence-based programming, and inclusion of cross-cutting issues.
Gender considerations are an integral part of the coordination and response provided by partners in the field. The CP AoR will notably coordinate the interventions of Mental Health and Psychosocial service (MHPSS) providers in Gaza and the West Bank and support the provision of quality services and referrals with specialized services. This project will ensure the required support to CP actors in the integration of CP Minimum Standards, including through training and technical support. This project will ensure Child Protection partners in both Gaza and the West Bank coordinate child protection interventions through a predictable, accountable, harmonized approach and understanding of child protection needs. It also supports and ensures that there are functioning referral pathways for cases affecting children, notably in cases of violence, detention, and injuries, but also the demolitions and settler violence response mechanism. The final aim of this project is to ensure children affected by the conflict and their caregivers are provided with appropriate and quality services including case management, MHPSS support, and legal aid.
The project also supports inter-sectoral collaboration, ensuring the Child Protection sector engages with other clusters so that CP concerns are mainstreamed in other areas of humanitarian intervention. This is notably the case with the Protection, Health, and Education sectors, via the MHPSS Technical Advisory Group (TAG) or comparable arrangement, aimed at ensuring linkages and common standards between psychosocial and mental health actors providing services at different layers of the MHPSS pyramid. Child Protection will also work closely with the Education Cluster to ensure an integrated approach is provided notably for cases requiring a multisectoral response and cases of violence affecting both areas.
UNICEF and the CP-AOR will promote compliance with the AAP, PSEA principles and procedures. AoR members will be oriented on sexual exploitation and abuse and will receive information on interagency channels including SAWA Free toll number, social media platforms, and WhatsApp to report on PSEA

1 - Needs

The protracted humanitarian protection crisis in the State of Palestine, compounded by escalations in conflict-related violence, particularly since April 2021 and continued through 2022 and the COVID-19 pandemic, continues to have a significant impact on the protection and rights of children and their families. Across the State of Palestine (SOP), 90 per cent of children faced a high level of violent discipline at home and 20.1 per cent of children are subjected to severe physical violence from their caregivers. And children with disabilities are more likely to experience all forms of violence and mostly to experience sexual violence (Protection Analysis Update for the West Bank, 2022). While conflict-related violence has an impact on both boys and girls, boys continue to be at a higher risk of being injured, killed or arrested. Violence against children has harmful consequences and long-term negative effects which provoke low self-esteem and affect the mental health and wellbeing of the child (Children in the State of Palestine, UNICEF 2021). In 2021, Child Protection partners documented and verified 2,934 grave violations: 1,216 children were killed/maimed during the context of demonstrations against occupation and settlement activity, 134 attacks on education facilities and interferences with the normal functioning of schools and children’s access to education, hospitals and related protected personnel and 1,583 incidents of denial of humanitarian access, harassment, intimidation and arrest affecting children in Israel and the State of Palestine. Additionally, 637 children were arrested and detained for alleged security-related offences with a high rate of children reporting ill-treatment; and 156 other interferences with education and health as verified (CAAC: Report of the Secretary-General, A/76/871-S/2022/493,23 June 2022 and Protection Analysis Update for the West Bank, 2022). Across the SOP, mental health issues and psychological disorders are one of the most significant public health challenges facing children and women and this is due to exposure to violence and widespread human rights violations as a result of the chronic occupation (Assessment of MHPSS interventions in the West Bank and Gaza Strip, UNFPA August 2021). Persons suffering from mild to moderate and severe mental health problems are increasing and require MHPSS services. These needs are exacerbated by the displacement of families, and dispossession of homes which is the main causes of mental health among adults and children in the West Bank (Protection Analysis Update for the West Bank, 2022). In Gaza, MHPSS needs are highly exacerbated by the ongoing blockade, exposure to trauma and violence from the Israeli hostilities and wars, as well as poverty, unemployment, and dependency on aid resulting in significant effects on mental health issues and contributing to high psychological distress. Violence within Palestinian families including harsh corporal punishment at home and schools, all forms of domestic violence, sexual abuse, early marriages, and child labor were identified as the key drivers of increasing MHPSS needs (Protection Analysis Update for Gaza, 2022). The CP AoR estimates that 780,000 children (395,942 boys, 383,809 girls) across the SOP are in need of protection and MHPSS services, including more than half of all children in Gaza and 25% of children in the West Bank (2023 HNO). During 2022 consultations, AoR members highlighted the increased need for integrated and multi-sector approaches to strengthen children’s access to protection and MHPSS services, due to the inter-related and compounding factors driving the protection risks for the most vulnerable children. This situation requires increased protection interventions including child protection and structured psychosocial and psychological first aid services, case management, legal services and Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) awareness as identified during the humanitarian needs overview assessments. There is furthermore a need to coordinate the interventions of Child Protection sector partners, to: i) ensure a common understanding of child protection needs and gaps, including the specific needs of boys and girls informing needs-based access; ii) promote a harmonized approach to interventions for maximum impact, and avoid overlap, duplication, and inefficiency.iii) provide technical support to integrate common standards, thereby ensuring the provision of quality and sustainable services and reinforcing community-based approaches, resilience, and strengthening of local capacities.

2 - Activities

Gender will be mainstreamed through all activities to ensure boys and girls are considered with their specific vulnerabilities and needs and that the response provided integrates these considerations. The project covers the following activities: 1. The project will strengthen child protection coordination mechanisms and community-based approaches through: 1.1 Ensuring inclusion of key humanitarian Child Protection (notably MHPSS) partners and facilitating coordinated partnerships with civil society organizations to address the identified gaps; 1.2 Support the implementation of improved standards for child protection interventions. 1.3 Refer or support referral process for conflict-related incidents for child protection and MHPSS responses (West Bank and Gaza)2. The child protection AoR’s needs analysis will be strengthened through improved trend analysis and reduced information gaps. This will be ensured through: 2.1 Support CP/MHPSS partners in uploading information about reached beneficiaries through the online 5W matrix (Who does What, Where, When and With Whom) in the West Bank and Gaza resulting in a quarterly updated dashboard articulating child protection and mental health needs and met response. Data included in the 5W is age and sex-disaggregated. 2.2 Complete needs assessment of areas with humanitarian needs and develop inter-cluster emergency contingency plans. These will integrate analysis of gender-specific vulnerabilities and gender-specific impact of ongoing concerns. 3. The CP AoR will contribute to the improvement of the provision of CP services by supporting the capacity building of child protection, MHPSS partners, notably local NGOs: 3.1 Support child protection partner programming and implementation maximizing intra- and intersectoral coordination based on the minimum standards for child protection in humanitarian action and other child protection instruments. 3.2 In partnership with the Protection Cluster Gender Focal Point, provide orientation sessions for CP and MHPSS responders on gender-sensitive approaches to child protection emergency response and mental health in emergencies. 4. The CP AoR will coordinate efforts in the CP field to ensure preparedness measures for humanitarian emergencies are in place: 4.1 Support operationalizing Gaza’s contingency planning and support the updating of West Bank’s contingency planning tools. 5. The CP AoR will support advocacy and resource mobilization efforts to ensure humanitarian needs are addressed and concomitant funding raised for programming: 5.1 Coordinate joint advocacy efforts (including donor briefings) under relevant priorities of the Child Protection AoR Strategy. 5.2 Support preparation and distribution of information products on top Child Protection priorities, needs, and gaps; 5.3 Work with the other clusters and sectors to ensure Child Protection is included in other areas of intervention notably education, health, and other areas of the Protection Cluster. This will be supported notably through intersectoral analysis, provision of guidance and guidelines and trainings to other sectors, and active participation in the West Bank ICCG meetings and processes. Finally, the CP AoR will promote member organizations to adhere to the AAP and PSEA principles, consult with beneficiaries on PSEA and provide information on available channels to report PSEA such as UNICEF emails, SAWA Free toll number, social media platforms and WhatsApp

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Child Protection partners through the CP AoR in both Gaza and the West Bank coordinate child protection interventions and ensure there is a predictable, accountable, harmonized approach and understanding to child protection needs, as well as the integration of common standards in line with the principles of Do No Harm. Additionally, the Do No Harm approach will ensure protection and assistance remain principled, needs-based, and humanitarian in character.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The coordination platform support transparency by engaging partners and holding them accountable for the provision of services for boys and girls of different ages and their caregivers within the HNO/HRP framework. Child Protection severity and needs for the 2022 HNO/HRP are in line with the Joint Indicator Analysis Framework (intersectoral) and complementary Needs Analysis Identification Framework (child protection), globally endorsed common approaches that take into account groups with specific needs. Additionally, the Child Protection AoR consolidates information gaps on existing humanitarian needs based on inputs from protection members. The CP AoR is therefore well positioned to effectively coordinate a humanitarian response to the ongoing protracted humanitarian protection crisis or unexpected escalation into conflict in SOP by ensuring that efforts of national and international humanitarian actors to protect children are well coordinated and achieve maximum quality and impact.
