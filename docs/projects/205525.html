Project Summary

The overall objective of the ARDF’s proposed project is to ensure vulnerable crises-affected people have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable, safe, and dignified access to life-saving assistance to meet their needs. The overall objective is to be achieved through three main cluster objectives namely; provision of food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 4 and 5), enhancement and sustainability of emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock and reduction of dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks & contextual changes.
This will ensure improved food availability and access to vulnerable households in crisis and emergency phases through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support, capacity building and reduction of dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks and shift to resiliency. ARDF will ensure a timely preposition of assorted crop and vegetable seeds as well as tools, complimented by capacity building in the respective livelihoods areas thus providing beneficiaries with the means to reestablish their own production and help them address the threat of hunger. To support the pastoralist households, refresher trainings of Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) and small ruminants restocking will be supported. In addition, the fisher folk will be supported with fishing gear and skills through training on fish hygiene, processing, and preservation.
ARDF will conduct vulnerability mapping during Payam Community Based Participatory Planning (CBPP) consultative meetings at the start of the project, involving key community leaders including women, village headmen, local authorities, key line ministry representatives, religious leaders and sub-chiefs. Households that are extremely food insecure as a result of the severe loss of assets as a result of disasters either natural and man-made including food production inputs and are unable to support themselves will be prioritized for agricultural inputs and livestock support. The projects provided smallholder farmers with both on-farm and off-farm support such as, agricultural value addition, processing and marketing, enterprise development, post-harvest handling, seed multiplication, village saving and loans (VSLAs) as well as cross-cutting issues as gender and GBV, nutrition, HIV/AIDS and group dynamics. In most agro-pastoralists’ communities in South Sudan, women are the main providers of the daily food for their families while men are considered to be providing security and herding livestock. However, women continue to be unequally affected by food insecurity and have less access to land and credit than men, limiting their ability to purchase agricultural tools, seeds, or hire labor that could increase their crop production Reduction in food intake as a coping mechanism has greatly affected the female population. During targeting and implementation, priority will be given to women and people with disabilities and their specific needs will be addressed. The proposed project will be implemented to disaster affected populations in Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Upper Nile States of South Sudan where ARDF has a long record of implementing food security and livelihoods activities. The project targets a total of 44,847 through various FSL interventions. The project teams will work closely with local authorities, protection partners, government and key line ministries.

1. Needs

An increase in sub-national violence reported in many states across the country, including Upper Nile and Northern Bhar El Ghazal states, devastating floods, refugee/Returnee influx due to the ongoing Sudan Crisis, widespread flood-related crop losses and the impact of COVID-19 including 40% hyperinflation and a protracted macro-economic crisis resulting in record high food price spikes has continued to disrupt livelihoods and contribute to an increase in humanitarian needs in 2023 (HNO,2023). According to the Food Security Outlook Update for South Sudan (FEWSNET, October 2023), most households have depleted their minimal harvests in the early season with most current data according to the (IPC June to September 2023) documents stating that the severely food insecure population is expected to increase to above 59% in the targeted counties with Aweil South, Renk and Melut Counties having 63,000 people in emergency; Aweil South having 33,000 people in crisis levels while Renk has 49,000 people in emergency; Melut 10,000 people in famine levels. To compound this dire situation, the (FAO Special report August 2023) forecasts for South Sudan also paints a dire picture for food insecurity which is expected to be severe at the height of the lean season of 2024 due to the impact of conflict, Sudan Crisis, climatic and economic shocks. Local currency depreciation, inflation, high import and fuel costs, and other factors, are sustaining high staple food prices and limiting economic activity, which in turn restricts household purchasing power. For instance, In March 2023, the average exchange rate in the parallel market stood at South Sudanese Pound (SSP) 1000 to the US dollar according to (VAM-food security analysis report April 2023). To verify this information, the (World Poverty Outlook report 2023) projects that given the large economic fallout from Covid-19 and other concurrent shocks, poverty at $1.90 per person per day is projected to increase to 78.2 percent in FY2023/24 from 76.8 per-cent in FY2022/23 in South Sudan with prices expected to continue rising and persist at extremely high levels across 2023. The nutrition assessment FSNMS August 2023 data analysis reports high levels of GAM rates as measured by weight-for-height z-score (WHZ), observed and (GAM (WHZ)>=15%) in Aweil South, Renk and Melut Counties and in some places mortality rates, measured by Crude Death Rate (CDR), exceeded 1/10,000/day within Upper Nile. The NOAA/NCEP Global Ensemble Forecast System report also cites that the production loss due to flood damage is estimated at 26,892 Ha of cultivated land which translates to an estimated loss of 11,916MT of cereals in the flood-affected counties.

2. Project activities

The proposed project will reach 44,847 beneficiaries through the implementation of the following activities; 1. Provide unconditional food assistance to 44,847 population in IPC 4+ in the targeted counties 2. Provide Conditional food assistance/ Asset creation and livelihood to population in IPC 3+ in the targeted counties 3. Provide conditional cash (Cash for asset) to the vulnerable population in the targeted counties 4. Provision of nutrition education and sensitization messages to increase knowledge on balance diet 5. Distribution of quality main season crop, fishing and vegetable kits to 44,847 people which corresponds to 7,475 HH in Renk, Melut and Aweil South6. Distribution of quality dry season fishing and vegetable kits to 44,847 people which corresponds to 7,475 HH in Renk, Melut and Aweil South7. Linking up with FAO to provide vegetable kits 8. Support in the establishment of 38 kitchen garden sites at the nutrition sites in health facilities9. Capacity building training on improved crop, vegetable and fishing practices to TOTs 1,850 individuals 60% women and 40% 10. Capacity building training to 1,850 fisheries 60% women and 40% 11. Training of 1000 youth and women on Income generating activities (IGA) focusing on bee-keeping to improve their livelihood 12. Establishment and training of 3 VSLA groups in each targeted county
