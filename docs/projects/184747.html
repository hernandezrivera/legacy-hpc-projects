Project Summary

The population of Somalia consistently experience some of the worst health outcome indicators in the
world, the average life expectancy is estimated to be 51.5 years. Somalia is among the countries with
the highest rankings in infant and child mortality rates of, respectively, 76.6 and 121.5 per 1,000 live
births.337 The maternal mortality rate (692 deaths per 100,000 live births) and the fertility rate (average of
6.9 children per woman) are also among the highest in the world, 79 per cent of all births are home delivered without skilled assistance.338 Overall, 99 per cent of Somali women have undergone female genital mutilation, 64 per cent of these having suffered the Pharaonic type. Overall, only 11 per cent of children aged 12-23 months are fully vaccinated, around 19 per cent of children in urban areas have received all basic vaccinations, while this is the case for less than 1 per cent of children in nomadic areas. (Somalia HNO 2022)
During quarter three (July-September 2021) in Bay region, the total number of children under one year who received Routine Immunization services in Bay region were 6437 which is less than last quarter of 7965 and more than the project target of 5087 . The pregnant women who received TT Vaccination were 6549 that is less than the last quarter of 7610 and more than the project target of 6360 based on quarterly from the (DHSI2) and DMO district Medical Officer.
Among the IDPs, 72% of the settlement sites assessed in Baidoa have an extreme severity score of ‘4.’( REACH DSA March 2021). Proportion of sites by type of health services reportedly available in the site: Basic primary healthcare 80% ; Vaccinations 52%; Child healthcare 51%. (REACH Baidoa DSA Report)
This project aims at providing quality essential lifesaving and life-sustaining health and nutrition services in Bay and Bakool area, with focus on women, children, and other vulnerable groups and strengthens the local capacity to deliver evidence-based and cost effective services based on the EPHS and Primary Health Care Approach. In addition, this will involve measles case management, immunization campaign, emergency
preparedness, and response capacities, awareness and mobilization campaigns including COVID-19 messaging to prevent the spread of the virus.
READO will support 5 fixed health facilities (1 fixed health facility per district) and 10 mobile teams with a network of CVs (IERT 1 fixed Berdale, 1 fixed Baidoa, 1 fixed in Hudur and 1 fixed in Diinsoor). The fixed facilities will act as referral point for cases requiring observation and further referral for secondary health services. More so READO will engage 50 CVs in the four districts of implementation. Through this project, a total 55,000 individuals in Baidoa, Berdale, Diinsoor and Huddur will have access to a package of Emergency Primary Health Care (EPHC) including 18,000 children under 1 year of age in urgent need of measles vaccination by the end of 2021.
Over the last 5 years, READO has been providing primary health care, immunizations, reproductive and GBV services to vulnerable populations in Baidoa, Hudur and Dinsoor. In regards to complementarity, READO is currently providing reproductive health and GBV services in Baidoa and Hudur with funding from UNFPA. In 2020, READO reached over 10,000 women and girls with reproductive health, GBV and CMR services.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Preventing risks of exclusion: READO will ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable by identifying and prioritizing the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups.READO, will ensure that the lack of documentation does not exclude individuals from accessing services. In addition, READO will identify the power dynamics in the area which included who has access to health care, who manages facilities, who is able to influence decisions on the provision of health services. READO will, in turn, use this information to inform monitoring activities and identify any barriers to access services or discrimination against particular groups. This information will be considered when determining locations for health interventions. In regards to avoiding tensions between the host and displaced communities, READO will provide the local/host communitywith access to camp health facilities in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of basic health services and facilities. In addition, READO will consult both displaced and host communities about health needs and assess whether health interventions could cause tension, harassment or conict between the two communities. Flood or other emergencies: Target communities affected by flooding,AWD, influx or IDPs or forced IDP evictions etc., unforeseen by EWS. To mitigate this, READO will scale-up the beneficiaries targeted with health interventions, to provide emergency assistance to those worst affected. Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age READO will ensure that the location, distance and access routes to the health facilities and services are accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable groups. This will be done through introducing special arrangements for persons unable to access health facilities eg.Mobile health services, facilitate accompaniment arrangements (e.g. youth groups assisting the elderly/persons with disability; community networks). In addition, READO will collect and use datadisaggregated by sex, age and diversity to effectively inform programming.Furthermore, READO will involve and consult all categories andlayers of the affected population in identifying and responding to health needs. READO will consult women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, chronically ill, older persons, pregnant and lactating women and marginalized persons to collect accurate information about their specific needs and preferences for health center location, design and services.Moreover, READO will engage the community and committee representative to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response. READO will also have a proportionate number of female health staff. READO will also establish complaints and response mechanism. READO will use information boards to display Complaint ResponseMechanism phone number during training sessions activities/beneficiary selection and at health centers. Beneficiaries will be able to call or text this number to register their complaint. READO will ensure this information, which could potentially be sensitive, remains internally. All complaints will be documented and reviewed regularly.In turn, the call center will be in charge of calling beneficiaries as part of the monitoring system. Protection of civilians: READO will ensure, that the location and access routes to health facilities and services are safe. This will be done by identifying central locations and avoid areas near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets. READO will not share information on the identity of patients unless informed consent has been given (e.g. names, addresses, or traits)
