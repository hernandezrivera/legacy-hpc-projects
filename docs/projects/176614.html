Project Summary

Over the 12-month implementation period, the project will reach 51,748 beneficiaries (including 2,700 people with disabilities), of whom approximately 51% will be female, in 7 communities and 2 health facilities in Metuge and Chirue districts. The project will work closely with provincial and district health authorities to establish and mobilize clinical teams to provide weekly mobile health services in the most impacted IDP communities. Weekly mobile brigades will offer a comprehensive package of maternal, newborn, child and adolescent health (MNCAH) services to IDPs and other vulnerable populations in accommodation centres, host communities, and resettlement areas. The project will build the capacity of community health committees (CHCs), Community Health Workers (CHWs), and community volunteers to conduct nutritional screening focusing on children and pregnant and lactating women (PLW), and promote disease prevention with a focus on the prevention of COVID-19 and water-borne diseases such as cholera; and identify cases of SGBV to refer to health and social services for treatment, care, support and follow-up. SC will work with the health authorities to train community-based volunteers to identify disease outbreaks and identify the danger signs in young children and pregnant women so that emergency services can be mobilized. The project will restock the health facilities that function as referral centres and distribute COVID-19 PPE to all those at the community and health facility (HF) level who need to visit homes or attend patients. SC will cover transportation-related costs, the government-approved fixed subsidy for basic meals and materials/tools. In addition, we will coordinate and support the existing logistics system to ensure a permanent provision of medicines and essential supplies for mobile teams. The intervention will be guided by the WHO and MOH recommended package for this kind of approach, to be complemented by regular assessments of services and perceptions of needs expressed by the beneficiaries.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The project aims to increase access to quality maternal, newborn, gender responsive child and adolescent health in 7 accommodation camps, host communities, and resettlement sites in Chiure (Chiure – Sede, Marrupa, and Ocua) and Metuge (Ntocota, Ngalane, Nangua, and EPD Namuapala), Cabo Delgado, the selection of which were based on a multi-sectoral needs assessment carried out by SC in January 2021. SC will work with provincial and district health authorities to establish and mobilize the clinical teams to provide weekly mobile health services in these communities. The mobile brigades will offer a comprehensive package of maternal, newborn, child and adolescent health (MNCAH) services to the IDPs and other vulnerable populations in accommodation centres, host communities, and resettlement areas who are in urgent need of medical care. In addition to medical treatment, SC will build the capacity of community health committees (CHCs), CHWs (APEs) and volunteers to visit households to provide nutritional screening with a focus on children >5 and PLW, as well as disease prevention with a focus on the prevention of COVID-19 and water-borne diseases such as cholera. The project will provide essential PPE equipment and non-medical (office equipment and tents for the replacement of essential services) material in need for the 2 district referral health facilities (Marrupa Health Centre in Chiure, and Nanlia Health Centre in Metuge) to substitute items that were damaged or lost during the cyclone or due to the violence; provide medical and non-medical supplies to CHWs (also known as APEs); provide PPE to community volunteers. SC will procure the materials locally. SC will use its pre-positioned PPE stocks to be used as buffer stock in case of problems arising from delays in local procurement.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Save the Children has a robust monitoring system, and installs a functional complaints and reporting mechanism in every community where we work where beneficiaries can anonymously report any concerns they might have regarding project staff, project implementation, and any impact that these might have on vulnerable individuals. All of SC’s staff are fully trained on PSEA and child protection and know how to recognize and report any concerns which might arise during implementation. This project will also have linkages and synergies with SC’s other projects in Cabo Delgado, including an ECHO proposal which implements Child Protection in Emergencies (CPiE) activities across Metuge, Pemba, Montepuez, and Chiure and will incorporate lessons learnt into these new proposed activities. CPiE activities have been supporting girls and boys and host communities affected by conflict, reducing the impact of insecurity caused by armed attacks, as well as increasing resilience. The proposed project will collaborate with district and provincial government sectors to strengthen their capacity to deliver quality services, and with communities to empower them to promote safe environments for children and platforms for meaningful child participation. SC is also currently implementing UNICEF, SIDA, BHA and Irish Aid funded projects in Cabo Delgado for child protection (CP), including case management, with strong referral pathways addressing internally displaced children’s needs, and implementing Family Tracing and Reunification (FTR). Activities from these projects include strengthening community-based CP mechanisms focusing on case management system, FTR, and alternative care arrangements; in addition to the provision of MHPSS for children and families to reduce stress and violence. As needed, this expertise will be brought into the proposed project to evaluate and respond to potential risk situations.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

SC adheres to the Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS) in making sure that the response plan is implemented in a way that ensures participation of affected people in decisions, complaints are welcome and addressed and interventions capture learnings and lessons and improvements on continual basis. To achieve this, an accountability system will be established aiming to fulfil the three key dimensions--information sharing, participation, and feedback mechanisms. Information will be shared with the beneficiaries mainly through community meetings and IEC material with key information. Participation will be ensured through community meetings, operational surveys, FGD with women, men, girls, and boys, as well as child consultation involving children with disabilities, using tailored user-friendly mechanisms. The project will stablish help desks as a complaint and feedback mechanisms (CFM) for the beneficiaries to raise their concerns and feedback about the project. The project will empower people through their work with community leaders, APEs, community health committees (CHCs – or create and train these where they don’t exist) and community health volunteers, who will engage with and provide outreach to the most vulnerable populations. These will be trained, supported and empowered to manage their health concerns, including identifying epidemiological outbreaks; promote disease prevention with a focus on the prevention of COVID-19 and water-borne diseases such as cholera, conduct nutritional screening for CU5 and PLW, and identify the danger signs in young children and pregnant women so that emergency services can be mobilized. The CHCs and health volunteers will also help coordinate and provide support during mobile brigade visits to ensure that the most vulnerable and sick are prioritized and that all COVID-19 protocols are followed.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will be reaching approximately 2,700 people with disabilities. SC will work through community leaders, APE, CHCs and health volunteers to identify children and adults that have disabilities and ensure that they receive the tailored care that they may need, based on their particular disabilities. The project will also ensure that those with disabilities are not marginalized in the engagement of community life and are not discriminated against in any way that might negatively affect their physical or mental wellbeing. Although the construction of facilities (such as latrines) that accommodated disabled people is outside of the scope of this project, the main community structures will be encouraged to work with families with disabled individuals to explore ways that these individuals can be better accommodated. During the mobile health brigades, disabled people will be encouraged to seek services, and if needed and possible, be referred to services that may be able to assist them with their disabilities (for example, provision of wheelchairs; glasses; hearing aids; cataract or other surgeries).

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project does not plan to conduct any environmental assessments. All medical waste created by the mobile brigades will be brought back with the teams and disposed of according to MoH protocols. The CHCs, APEs, and health volunteers will have a focus of promoting environmental hygiene, including creating trash pits, draining stagnating water, and the promotion and proper positioning of latrines to ensure minimal possibility of the contamination of water sources and the community environment during heavy rains. The promotion of the management of environmental resources is outside of the scope of this project.
