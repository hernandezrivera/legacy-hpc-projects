Project Summary

The proposed action is in line with 2019 WASH Cluster Objectives 1 and 3 and Shelter Cluster Objective 2. The action works at community level and HH level according to a comprehensive WASH and Shelter strategy aiming to strengthen the communities capacities through repairing/upgrading of HHs’ main structures facilities (WASH and Shelter), provision of winterization assistance and NFIs to vulnerable HHs at risk of exposure or affected by natural or manmade hazards and improving of service provider’ capacities to mitigate and respond to flooding. At community level, the action will act through life-saving emergency WASH response interventions and preparedness to the most vulnerable communities (improvement of drainage water systems, domestic water accessibility and reducing hazards for private/public hygiene). This will also enhance service providers’ preparedness capacity and support communities’ resilience building against protracted threats and chronic vulnerability, especially in terms of Health and WASH. At HH level, the action will act through rehabilitation and/or upgrading of shelters, distribution of winterization kits, HH wastewater connection and hygiene promotion activities to reduce the exposure of targeted beneficiaries to both man-made and natural disasters, and risk of water borne diseases and health problems, by enabling them to live meeting adequate standards.
Providing adequate shelter will contribute to reduce and mitigate the effects, vulnerabilities and risks associated with living in Access Restricted Area (ARA), especially for women, children, elderly and people with disabilities (PwDs). Shelter rehabilitation and upgrading will contribute to empower communities, in terms of coping capacities and protection response mechanisms, enhancing their capacities to respond to protracted crisis and reinforcing the local capacities as mitigation and preparedness to environmental and man-made hazards.
The project will address the needs of most vulnerable HHs living in substandard overcrowded housing units, improving the accessibility to adequate and safe WASH and shelter conditions, protecting from the effects of extreme climate, and promoting safety, privacy, and hygiene best practice, creating a safer environment to cope with environmental threats.
The proposed intervention will target 18 localities in the North Gaza, Gaza and Middle Area Governorates, specifically Beit Lahia, Beit Hanoun, Jabalia, Jabalia Camp, Umm Al Nasser, Al Mughraqa, Ezahra, Juhor ad Dik, An Nuseirat, An Nuseirat Camp, Az Zawayda, Al Bureij, Al Bureij Camp, Al Maghazi, Al Maghazi Camp, Deir al Balah, Deir al Balah Camp, Al Musaddar and Wadi as Salqa, directly benefiting 41,402 HHs, meaning a total of 264,970 individuals (70,120 girls, 73,242 boys, 54,741 women, 56,682 men, 10,186 elderly including 18808 PWDs) and 662,427 indirect people as catchment area. This action is in line with the PWA Storm water management policy and considered high and urgent priority by CMWU for ensuring access to essential services strengthening the population to cope with protracted threats, and works as an emergency time critical response, which is not currently addressed by CMWU and PWA intervention due to funding constraints. The project includes the following activities based on the protection egg model: Responsive (57.4% of the budget): connection of households to the municipal wastewater system; enhancing of operation of 11 water wells and 17 wastewater pumping stations; Remedial (34.2% of the budget): repairing/upgrading of 150 HHs main structures facilities (WASH and Shelter); hygiene and environmental health awareness campaign and distribution of 711 hygiene kits; Environmental building (8.4% of the budget): Provision of protective clothes and tools; Cleaning and inspection of 5 stormwater collection ponds, 24 wastewater pumping stations, gully traps and wastewater networks.

1 - Needs

WW-GVC conducted a WASH assessment (WW-GVC, 2017), targeting both women and men, in the Gaza Strip, including ARA locations, analyzing the critical WASH needs of 3,977 HHs. It shows that 13%, 18% and 45% of HHs are unconnected to wastewater network in North Gaza, Gaza and Middle Area Governorates respectively, thus forced to use cesspits, generating black water infiltration directly to groundwater. 48%, 38% and 59% of HHs suffers from stormwater accumulation/floods around their houses in in North Gaza, Gaza and Middle Area Governorates respectively.Data analysis of the Ministry of Public Works and Housing (MoPWH) ongoing assessment of substandard housing units showed that 4,013, 3,737 and 5,306 units are in need for rehabilitation and upgrading in North Gaza, Gaza and Middle Area Governorates respectively.In coordination with stakeholders of WASH & Shelter sectors, including PWA, CMWU, municipalities and MoSD, WW-GVC jointly with Rural Women Development Society (RWDS) and Beit Lahia Development Association (BLDA), conducted the following assessments: a)Complementary field survey on WASH and shelter needs related to winterization preparedness, analyzing men’s and women’s roles in North Gaza, Gaza and Middle Area Governorates, Sep 2019; b)Field survey on WASH and Shelter needs in 2,114 HHs in North Gaza and Gaza governorates, Oct-Dec 2018; c)Field survey on WASH and Shelter in 766 HHs in North Gaza, Gaza and Middle Area governorates, Jun-Aug 2018; d)Field survey on WASH and Shelter needs in 201 HHs in Um Al Nnaser, Jan 2018. Main findings are: winter storms and localized flooding continues every year to impact HHs, also caused by poor and deteriorated infrastructures; several HHs and schools suffers from undrained stormwater due to poor drainage systems; wastewater/stormwater facilities are cleaned only in a case of flooding or through specific funded projects; inability of CMWU and municipalities to cover increased operating and maintenance costs; 11 water wells and 17 wastewater pumping stations are in need for critical electromechanical refurbishment and reparation; 711 HHs of 63 locations are connected to overloaded cesspit; 94% of the assessed HHs are highly vulnerable in term of Shelter and WASH minimum standards. This include core shelter items that ensure physical & climate protection, privacy and dignity, safety & security reflecting gender and protection concerns from women, men and PwDs. 75% of the assessed HHs live under roofs made of old sheets of galvanized/asbestos and are in need for protection from natural hazards; 58% of the assessed HHs are connected to overloaded cesspit or illegally connected to the stormwater network with correlated public health risks; 88% of the assessed HHs suffer from bad hygiene conditions, bad hygiene practices and insufficient availability of hygiene materials; 32% of children, 29% of women, and 51% of elderly HHs members rely on poor and unsafe sources of drinking water; 74% of sanitation facilities visited do not guarantee privacy and protection for women and children.The assessment also shows the following issues: presence of shelters with severe electrical damage, persistent dampness, roof leaks; lack of proper insulation; need for installation of doors and windows (especially for women who suffer from lack of privacy in their rooms and for boys and girls to facilitate their homework); unhealthy and unhygienic WASH conditions due to old sewage systems in their shelters and absence of wastewater networks; lack of basic household assets (furniture, equipment, kitchen set, bedding set). The assessments show that the inability of the community to improve structures is due mainly to economic deprivation.Floods affect men, women boys and girls in different ways: delays in reaching work and school, increased workload for taking care of hygiene of children. PwDs are heavily impacted being almost completely prevented to leave the house increasing caregivers’ workload.

2 - Activities

The project works at two levels, strengthen the WASH & Shelter preparedness and response capacity to emergencies for HHs (A.2, A.3 and A.4) and for service providers (A.5). Assessment (A.1) will be conducted to guarantee solid baseline and endline, while awareness activities (A.6) will enhance the sustainability of the action. WW-GVC will sustain coordination and sharing measures with the PWA, CMWU, WASH and Shelter clusters and other organizations working in the target location to join efforts and avoid overlapping. A.1 Pre/post needs assessment. Conduction of a rapid assessment in order to verify and monitor the exact needs including: technical survey for planning and design; HHs field visits and gender separated focus groups to collect feedback and incorporate them in the action A.2 Connection of households to the municipal wastewater system. Preparing BoQ and technical specifications with CMWU for the construction of 8,000 meters of sewer pipeline including all required manholes and fittings. Installing 711 HHs’ wastewater connections and all the associated works benefiting of 4,576 people.A.3 Repairing/upgrading of households’ main structures facilities (WASH and Shelter). Repairing/upgrading of 150 substandard housing units (949 people) for most vulnerable HHs affected by harsh weather and flood risk including at least 20 women headed HHs (modality: self-help/contractor). The repairs/upgrade is based on the shelter cluster minimum standards which include items to ensure physical and climate protection, privacy and dignity; improve access to HH WASH services, safety and security and reflect gender and protection: house roofing, external walls and door, bedrooms, separation of sleeping, living and cooking spaces; access ramps and suitable installations for PwDs, handrails for stairs, LED emergency lighting; smooth and cleanable floor and wall surfaces; proper ventilation. The average cost is estimated at 1,800 USD per HH, based on previous quotations received by WW-GVC. A.4 Households’ winterization preparedness and response. Complementary and based on needs, 150 HH NFIs winterization kit (sealing off kit and bedding set) will be provided (modality: in-kind/voucher/cash). The average cost is estimated at 180 USD per HH, based on previous quotations received by WW-GVC.A.5 Improving of service provider’ capacities. Provision of protective clothes and tools for 100 labors required by CMWU for their operational teams for winterization prevention and responses. Winterization preparedness and stormwater/wastewater facilities cleaning and inspection (5 stormwater collection ponds, 24 wastewater pumping stations, gully trapes and stormwater/wastewater networks for prevention and responses to potential floods). Positive impacts are (i) reducing risk of temporary displacement (ii) reducing the risk of destruction of HHs’ assets. Critical electromechanical refurbishment and reparation for 11 water wells and 14 wastewater pumping stations. Training for 36 municipality’s operators for ensuring and maintaining a high level of disinfection of water supply system.A.6 Hygiene and environmental health awareness activities and distribution of hygiene kits. Conducting 120 public awareness and hygiene training activities and distribution of 711 hygiene kits benefiting 4,576 people. The aim is to reach the shared responsibilities and behavioral practices of the vulnerable women, men, boys and girls living in the targeted areas, promoting women and men’s participation, social mobilization and inclusive behavioral change. In the initial phase of the project, a specific project committee will be established, including the participation of women CBOs, PWA, MoSD, MoPWH and CMWU. It will plan, monitor and follow up the implementation of the activities. RWDS and BLDA, local long experiences NGOs in WASH and Shelter projects, will be responsible for HH field surveys and soft activities such as distribution of NFI, awareness sessions etc.

The direct involvement of the beneficiaries will increase the sense of ownership respecting the no child labour and exploitation. The involvement of women and men of all ages since the beginning of the project implies a real participation in all actions, allowing better identifying and addressing their needs, besides the contribution to the empowerment of women since usually they are not taken into consideration for technical framework projects. WW-GVC will give priority to women headed HHs, girls, boys and men needs in the rehabilitation process. WW-GVC’s will develop M&E plan to track performance against indicators (outputs and outcomes), timeframe and encompass key criteria for baseline/end-lines. WW-GVC’s community-based protection approach and Gender and protection pre/post monitoring surveys are carried out to follow up, assess the impact and incorporate mid-course corrections, if required. Data collected will be disaggregated and gender sensitive using different tools, such as: baseline and end-line surveys to monitor the impact of the project; monitoring visits to follow up the progress of project’s activities and their compliance with the project workplan; monthly technical progress reports, hygiene promotion activities will be specially monitored by pre and post KAP questionnaires; focus groups discussions with different stakeholders, including, but not limited to, children, adults, elders, PwDs, community leaders and partner CBOs; complains and feedback mechanism will be established at preparation phase with well-known access by targeted beneficiaries to ensure WW-GVC accountability and participatory approach.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

WW-GVC stablished of a complaint response mechanism to collect feedback from wide range of project groups, local authorities, local partners, project staff, beneficiaries in relation to any project activity they are taking part in. Context analysis before deciding on a particular feedback mechanism was conducted taking in consideration the literacy and barriers to access different mechanisms especially for women and children.To ensure that the beneficiaries and stakeholders received adequate information and communication, WW-GVC will hold regular bilateral meetings with the target municipalities for the issues related to the intervention at community level, and with HHs representatives. Being the focal point for the WASH Cluster in Middle Area since 2011, has allowed GVC to develop an excellent relationship with municipalities and affected population.Sensitization of target beneficiaries to the purpose and process of Feedback Mechanism (FM) is essential to build confidence and overcome fears about giving feedback. Through complaint boxes WW-GVC field staff support the maintenance and the follow up of communication channel with beneficiaries and stakeholders in order to support real-time adaptation of projects to the needs of their target groups and contexts, and accountability of programmes and service providers. It is important to inform the targeted groups on how the FM works, in terms of availability, template(s) to capture the data, focal points and handling of the information collected. Gender mainstreaming and protection are considered in all the phases of the mechanism. Sufficient scope in the programme design will be ensured in order to make changes and respond to requests to increase or reallocate resources; negotiate with the donor if necessary. Moreover, feedback is intended to be integrated with monitoring systems, given careful consideration to how feedback will be analysed and aggregated and the capacity of staff and systems to do that.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

“Do no harm” principle is reflected and integrated in the project’s objective of ensuring safe and sufficient access to basic services to women, men, girls and boys. Based on complementary needs assessment and coordination with municipalities, CBOs, WASH Cluster and other stakeholders, the project intervention is delivered according targeted communities needs and built on knowledge gained over the past years. The rehabilitation inside HHs will improve the hygienic-sanitation conditions, shelter conditions including protection from climate, and ensure privacy and safety, to contribute to self- recovery reducing the risk of GBV and other protection-related risk. The provision and installation of new water storage units will reinforce the beneficiaries' ability to survive in their current location and seeks to prevent or alleviate the risk of deteriorating their health and dignity. In addition, the distribution of dignified hygiene kits will enhance positive hygiene practicing through encouraging beneficiaries to convert their theoretical knowledge into practical ones and thus protect public health HHs’ members, particularly children and women. Further, the dignified hygiene kit’s content includes specific materials for children, girls and women such as menstrual hygiene management (MHM) items.Besides, the action ensures protection and prioritize women and men, girls’ and boys’ safety through enhancing the winterization emergency preparedness and response through inspection, cleaning and maintenance of stormwater/wastewater facilities to avoid continuous negative impact on family public health, mainly on women and children. This will contribute to increase access to human right and ensure beneficiaries dignity through improving their life conditions and facilitate proper access for stormwater services.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The beneficiaries of this action are selected among the most vulnerable households in terms of socioeconomic and protection related vulnerabilities, which are connected to inadequate WASH and Shelter services. The project will address the needs of acutely vulnerable HHs living in substandard overcrowded housing units, improving the accessibility to adequate WASH facilities through promoting safety, privacy and hygiene.The principle of equity is followed in the distribution mechanism system and construction interventions, ensuring that the most vulnerable and poorer families hold the rights to access their WASH and Shelter needs. Services and assistance are provided in an adequate scale based on a transparency selection criterion, within safe and easy reach, are known by the affected individuals and accessible by all groups to ensure meaningful access for domestic water. In order to strengthen beneficiaries' capacities and assess them to claim their rights a discussion groups with them will be conducted to detect and discuss targeted concerns and ask them to come up with solutions to be followed. Women, men, girls, boys and with and without disabilities will be consulted at all stages of the project intervention. Affected individuals and targeted communities will play an active role in the measurement of the quality of interventions that affect them will be conducted through addressing concerns and complaints to ensure effective accountability. Protection mainstreaming is an integral element of the proposed approach, which aims at incorporating protection principles and promoting meaningful access, safety and dignity in the designed action. Project staff will be trained on the use of WW-GVC standards and indicators for protection. WW-GVC will implement and hold itself accountable to WW-GVC’s Minimum Standards for protection mainstreaming.By ensuring basic access to WASH and Shelter services, the project will apply a protection mainstreaming approach guided by protection principles including safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability as well as participation and empowerment of the beneficiaries.
