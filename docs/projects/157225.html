Project Summary

The overall objective of the project is to provide accurate and up to date information on the available services at the major sites of displacement and/or return in the five states of Darfur, South and West Kordofan, with the purpose of improving the provision of humanitarian assistance by identifying the needs and gaps of the residents through profiling and site assessments covering all basic services sector wise and the production of up to date information that helps in furthering the implementation of humanitarian activities in the context of humanitarian-development nexus.
IOM teams (composed of a representative of the Humanitarian Aid Commission (HAC), INGOs, NNGOs and IOM enumerators)(depending on location and presence in the area) will be trained on information management including data collection, data entry and analysis. Monthly reports will be generated and disseminated to all sector partners and could be used not only for service provision but also as a baseline for all future durable solutions activities such as IDP integration, informing planning and design of longer support on issues of land tenure, available markets and the existence of waste management systems. Also IOM will share site assessments and profiling data through briefings with relevant sectors, as well as ISCG and IMWG work groups through briefings and statistical reports, after each site assessment. The project is also in line with the Multi Year Humanitarian Response Plan Outcome 2: “Displaced populations, refugees, returnees and host communities meet their basic needs and/or access basic services while increasing their self reliance".

Needs

According to OCHA IDPs are living in over 240 IDP displacement sites including formal camps, spontaneous gatherings and host community sites (OCHA, June 2014) in Darfur, utilizing the existing basic services provided in the sites. Some of the basic service facilities are either not functioning or exhausted by the new IDPs' caseloads that were displaced to the main camps in Darfur during the years 2015 and 2016. With the absence of the CCCM cluster in Sudan, an information gap exists, resulting in delays in response, waste of resources, poor management and coordination of these sites. In 2016, IOM carried out camp profiling exercises in 6 IDP camps in North Darfur, and in 2018 a detailed solutions oriented profiling exercise is underway in the camps of Abu Shouk and El Salam, in El Fasher locality, North Darfur on behalf of UN humanitarian partners to inform durable solutions.This project will aim to cover the 10 camps / sites with the most needs in order to identify the gaps and needs and generate accurate, timely and verifiable data for emergency interventions from all other sectors. Camps will be selected based on their size as well as location, focusing primarily on those located away from the main towns and capitals and hence have less access to services. Information collected will include detailed and disaggregated information such as the number of beneficiaries using available services, fees (if any), GPS coordinates, access constraints (e.g. during the rainy season) and number of requests from IDPs made to service delivery providers for immediate assistance. Assessments will provide information on the presence of minors and vulnerable individuals (e.g., pregnant and lactating women, elderly, unaccompanied minors, etc.) to better target assistance to these vulnerable groups. IOM will also conduct intention surveys to ascertain IDPs intentions to return or locally integrate in other areas. The survey exercises will allow IDP’s to share comprehensive information about their experiences and situations.In addition, further information will be collected regarding national and international humanitarian actors working in displacement sites and access roads leading to these sites to improve sector response. Support will also be given in terms of land tenure issues, so information will be collected on land ownership to facilitate mediation between IDPs and host communities. In the event that local integration is considered by the residents additional information such as availability of markets and the existence of waste management systems will also be collected. IOM will encourage IDPs to participate fully in the intention survey and camp profiling exercises while building capacity for HAC to record and disseminate accurate, updated information.

Activities

Activities:1. Train HAC staff, international NGOs, national NGOs and IOM enumerators (depending on the location and presence in the area) on conducting the camp profiling assessments and how to use GPS devices in order to geo reference collected information and improve information sharing. 2. Update the existing database to include all the collected information and train staff in data entry, database management and how to generate information. All the data will be located in IOM HQ in Khartoum and the storing and sharing of the data will follow IOM's data protection policy an internal guidance notes. 3. Assessment teams will collect all relevant information at the displacement sites including GPS coordinates, vulnerabilities, services available, access to markets, potential Identified harmful practices that lead to environmental degradation (e.g. how waste is disposed of). 4. IOM develops questionnaires and the assessment teams collect IDP intention survey and focus group discussion information. 5. Crosscheck and process all the data before processing the information into the database and subsequently analyze all collected data to identify the gaps and IDP intentions and actions required to address these needs. 6. Create a camp profiling info graph / fact sheet for each camp and IDP site. 7. Provide an analysis report that examines the intentions of IDPs in the camps/sites. 8. Recruit community focal points (the number will depend on the camp/site size) to update the collected information as well as to highlight the gaps and needs especially during the arrival of new IDPs to these camps. 9. Recruited staff will visit focal points and basic service facilities once a week to receive updates on the status population movements and facilities. 10. IOM staff will also compile monthly reports to be shared with relevant stakeholders taking into account sensitivity of information and sharing as appropriate in the public domain or bilaterally.
