Project Summary

GOAL’s primary objective for 2019 is to provide urgent and lifesaving assistance through the distribution of emergency livelihood kits, seeds, and tools, support of market functionality interventions, training and capacity building to support resiliency, and diversification and restoration of livelihoods for vulnerable households across IPC 3 and 4 phases of food insecurity.

1 - Needs

The protracted impact of the armed conflict and recent macro-economic crisis have driven poverty rates in South Sudan to unprecedented levels; coupled with high inflation rates, many households, particularly in rural areas, continue to face difficult situations as they are now unable to meet the minimum food basket. Crisis/IPC3 and Emergency/IPC4 food insecurity levels persist throughout the country, with significant food consumption gaps expected during the lean season (January-April 2020) and little chance of improvement later in the year. Kajo-Keji County is currently classified as IPC phase 3 ("Crisis"), with projections predicting emergency or IPC4 in the first quarter of 2020 that requires immediate humanitarian assistance. However, despite extensive countrywide efforts to contain the food security crisis, most recent IPC analyses estimate 3.6 million people (31.4% of the population) are currently facing at least IPC phase 3 or worse levels of food insecurity. Although humanitarian assistance will likely prevent more extreme food security outcomes, aid is expected to reach less than 50% of the estimated need in South Sudan. Risk of IPC 5 "Famine" persists even in the presence of aid; to avoid such scenarios, scaled up food security and nutrition interventions are needed to support most vulnerable households, especially female-headed households, internally displaced persons, and host communities. Programming focus is placed on addressing immediate malnutrition needs (targeting PLWs and children under 5) while protecting immediate access to food and preventing the collapse of livelihoods to help communities withstand environmental and conflict-related challenges. GOAL's country programme will continue implementing integrated food security and nutrition programmes to respond to the needs of vulnerable populations through a more comprehensive package of services. GOAL will also leverage its extensive programmatic experience to provide capacity-building support to national and international partners across the country through cash transfers and resilience programming. To better address these needs, GOAL plans to adapt its current programming to provide a more comprehensive package of interventions designed to increase access to food sources, protect livelihoods opportunities, and improve nutritional status of vulnerable populations in South Sudan.

2 - Activities

- Emergency food production: main season and dry season agriculture seeds & tool distribution; fish, vegetable & crop kits- Reducing pest and crop disease e.g. FAW surveillance, detection, prevention & eradication- Training & capacity building: both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production (demonstration plots;), income generation (business skills)- Promoting climate smart agricultural production techniques including farm managed natural resources targeting community leadership- Diversification & restoration of livelihoods; small grants for petty trade; village saving & loan schemes & micro finance etc.- Promoting use of local biomass briquettes & fuel-efficient stoves

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

GOAL has risk management plans, conduct regular security risk assessments, and established special operation procedures to mitigate identified risks. For example, during episodes of conflict in some operational areas, GOAL was able to continue its projects with little to no interruptions due to risk-mitigating systems in place, impartial reputation, strong community relationships, and national staff capacity. GOAL will maintain existing positive relationship with local and national authorities, while striving to remain impartial when political tensions emerge in operational areas. Decentralizing authority for decision-making, resource allocation and Food Security & Livelihoods service management to county level will allow greater community participation in implementing local food production. Recruitment of a highly competent and experienced management team will ensure timely execution of all project activities.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

GOAL conducts periodic review meetings with stakeholders in its field sites to inform them about programme implementation and provide an opportunity for feedback about its projects. These consultations are used to improve subsequent programming and inform project design. Throughout the life of the project, awareness raising sessions will be conducted regularly within target communities to inform programme participants of GOAL’s Feedback, Complaints and Response Mechanism (FCRM) across all sites, to ensure communities have a strong understanding of their rights as beneficiaries, what to expect from GOAL programming, how adequacy of interventions is evaluated, and provide an opportunity to address concerns and complaints about the project.
