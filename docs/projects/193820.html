Project Summary

The proposed intervention will be focused on achieving the objectives by reinforcing equitable access to mobile primary health care (PHC) services, especially in Area C communities of Jenin Governorate, including quality provision of Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS), Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) and Gender-based Violence (GBV) support. Adopting the humanitarian-development nexus approach, this proposed action also reinforces quality continuum of care in existing PHCCs in nearby Area C communities, ensuring continuous access to PHC services in the absence of a mobile clinic (MC), especially targeting women, men, children, elderly, and persons with disabilities (PWDs).

The proposed intervention will be conducted through MC, in partnership with the Ministry of Health (MoH)- Jenin PHC Directorate, in eight Area C communities of Jenin Governorate, reinforcing the efforts of the MoH as primary duty bearer for PHC service delivery. Moreover, this intervention also aims to strengthen the capacity and quality of the nearby six PHC Centers (PHCCs) in Jenin Governorate as well as to reinforce the referral mechanism between the MC and existing PHCCs. The intervention will also contribute to increasing the capacity of health care providers in providing quality essential health services.
Overall, the intervention (MC and PHCCs strengthening) will benefit a total of 12,012 direct beneficiaries, of which 3756 are men, 3668 are women, 2349 are boys, 2239 are girls, out of them 541 are PWDs. The total catchment population of target areas (73,843) will also indirectly benefit from the proposed action. The proposed intervention is aligned with the Cluster objective 1 related to Strategic Objective 2 (SO2).

1 - Needs

MdM-F has recently conducted an assessment in the north of West Bank (WB) on emergency preparedness of PHCCs and hospitals in ِAugust 2022, including exploratory study on mobile health services. The study used mixed methods of quantitative and qualitative research. Twenty PHCCs and five hospitals in Nablus, Qalqilya, Jenin, Tubas, and Tulkarem were assessed. The findings revealed that public transportation was lacking in some PHCCs as well as human resources, mainly technical, specialized paramedical, MHPSS, and GBV staff. The staff presence of some PHCCs was also irregular. In hospitals, the presence of specialized paramedical and MHPSS staff was also limited. The assessment also showed improper infrastructure of the PHCCs (inadequate space or design), and the lack of essential emergency drugs and equipment. These hinder the provision of essential health services at the existing health facilities (HFs) including PHCCs and hospitals and necessitate the need to rehabilitate them to improve the quality of provided services. The assessment also revealed the need to rehabilitate the HFs when it comes to providing services tailored to vulnerable populations, such as persons with physical disabilities and GBV survivors to ensure accessibility, privacy, and confidentiality. In the MdM assessment, 6 out of 20 PHCCs in the north of the WB are not adapted for persons with physical disabilities. PHC Directors and participants from the community also clearly expressed the need for services tailored to persons with disabilities. The assessment also showed the lack of MHPSS services in PHCCs and hospitals in north WB. Based on FGDs and KIIs conducted by MdM-F, barriers to accessing health services include the following: 1) movement restrictions, such as non-availability of public transportation, high transport costs (taxis) to reach nearest PHCCs or hospitals, lack of road network, especially in Area C; 2) lack of health insurance and negative attitude of PHC staff who refuse to provide services to people without insurance; 3) inadequacy of public health system in terms of limited working hours (mostly open only 3-5 hours per day and doctors coming only once or twice a week, forcing people to seek services from private clinics or use traditional medicines), lack of capacity to handle emergency cases, and strikes held by health staff, leaving clinics with limited staff; 4) poor health-seeking behavior; 5) stigma in accessing MHPSS/GBV services; 6) financial/economic; and 7) occupation-related barriers, such as the presence of checkpoints and complicated permit system. MC or medical outreach services is one of the proposed solutions to address barriers to accessing health services. Community members have proposed the following services to be included in the MC: 1) SRH, including Maternal and Child health, early diagnosis of cancer and vaccination for children); 2) emergency services, including an ambulance with coordination permit and provision of emergency kits 3) MHPSS and GBV services; 4) referral support; 5) essential drugs and equipment; and 6) training health staff on MHPSS, SRH, disability, trauma and mass casualty management and for community members on first aid. According to the Mobile Clinic sub-working Group, only 1/3 of 150,000 Palestinians in Area C, East Jerusalem, Hebron/H2 and the Seam Zone needing MC have access to mobile health services. Based on the latest 4Ws, Jenin Governorate has the highest needs in terms of MC in north WB. Sustainability of funds for MC is also an issue as most MC teams have secured funding until 2nd Quarter of 2023 (source: 4Ws 29 September 2022). This information is confirmed by the assessment conducted by MdM-F in north WB where visited communities affirmed discontinuity of MC services or irregularity of MC visits by MoH and other actors due to lack of logistical resources and lack of funding. The Jenin PHC Director also identified hotspots in Jenin Governorate where clashes and violence often occur and where existing MoH facilities are not adequate to respond. Thus, this proposed intervention does not only aim to provide Level 2 mobile PHC services, but also to strengthen the capacity of select nearby PHCCs surrounding MC areas in Jenin Governorate to provide quality continuum of care for vulnerable Area C communities, addressing the health needs identified in the assessment.

2 - Activities

This proposed action aims to achieve the following expected results: 1) Availability of Level 2 mobile PHC services (including quality provision of MHPSS, SRHR and GBV services) is ensured in eight vulnerable Area C communities of Jenin Governorate; 2) Essential health services are strengthened, and continuum of care is ensured in nearby 10 PHCCs surrounding the MC target areas; 3) Capacity of MC teams and PHCC staff to provide quality MHPSS, SRHR and GBV services among vulnerable groups, such as PWDs, women, and children is strengthened through capacity-building activities. Detailed activities are shown below: 1. Support MoH in conducting mobile Level 2 PHC services in most vulnerable communities in Jenin Governorate, ensuring quality provision of inclusive MHPSS, SRH, and GBV services. 2. Procurement of drugs, equipment and logistics supplies needed for MCs and nearby PHCCs. 3. Rehabilitation of community structures for MCs and ten PHCCs, ensuring accessibility for PWDs and GBV survivors.4. Capacity building of MoH MC and PHCC staff on MHPSS, SRH, GBV, disability, Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), and Community-based Complaint Mechanism (CBCM) topics. 5. Community awareness on medical, MHPSS and GBV topics, including PSEA and CBCM. 6. Supportive supervision on MC teams and PHCC staff, ensuring proper mainstreaming of gender, disability and PSEA in the provision of health services. The strategy to be adopted in conducting MCs includes strengthening the capacity of Jenin PHC Directorate in providing comprehensive, quality health services where SRH, gender, PSEA and disability are mainstreamed in the mobile service delivery. This strategy requires strong collaboration between Jenin PHC Directorate and MdM-F where the former is acknowledged as the duty bearer in providing health services to its populations while the latter shares its expertise and experience in ensuring quality and inclusion of crosscutting themes in service delivery. During the project design of this proposed intervention, the PHC Directorate in Jenin wanted to have an active role in the implementation of MCs, such as provision of human resources (doctor, nurse, lab technician) while MdM-F will support in ensuring quality MHPSS by supporting the mobile team with Social Worker/Psychologist. Additional technical support will be provided by MdM-F in mainstreaming crosscutting topics, such as gender, disability and PSEA, including CBCM as well as building the capacity of MC and PHCC staff on MHPSS, SRHR and GBV. MdM-F will be also in charge of the logistic aspects of the MC, including the provision of an equipped MC vehicle together with its driver and running cost. Moreover, MdM-F will coordinate access to targeted areas with the Israeli Civil Administration. MCs will provide Level 2 PHC services, which include preventive services such as mother and child health care, immunization, and health education as well as curative services such as first aid, general practitioner (GP) consultation, some basic laboratory services, MHPSS consultations, including screening using GHQ-12, management, and referral for specialized services and GBV (screening, medical and MHPSS support, and multi-sectoral referral). Targeted communities will be visited at least once a week and identified community structures where MC will be conducted will be rehabilitated to ensure privacy and confidentiality during consultations. The team includes a doctor who will provide curative general consultations; nurse/midwife for SRH/GBV services, immunization, and health education; social worker for MHPSS services; and other support staff (lab technician, driver). Each community will have identified community volunteers to be involved in the community mobilization, support the nurse in health education, and to serve as focal person, along with the village council focal person to link with the MC activities. Referrals will be done in reference to MoH policy and guidelines for cases needing a higher level of MHPSS and medical care, following an established referral pathway. This can be done through linkage with MdM-F existing projects on MHPSS or through PHCCs and hospitals that are near the MC target communities within Jenin Governorate, as well as with other actors working in specialized topics, not provided in the MCs. This proposed intervention aims to ensure continuum of care for Area C communities; thus, one important component of this intervention includes improving the capacity of nearby PHCCs through rehabilitation of HFs structures to ensure accessibility for PWDs, provision of drugs, supplies, and equipment and training of HFs staff to provide continuous health services to Area C communities, especially when MC is not scheduled to be conducted in these areas. Quality of service provision will be ensured through joint continuous supportive supervision by MoH and MdM team in MCs and target PHCCs. Communities will also be sensitized on various medical, MHPSS and GBV topics, including PSEA. Sensitized population will be an important source of feedback mechanism regarding accountability of service providers in the delivery of needed services to targeted populations. MdM-F staff have been sensitized on PSEA, and the Feedback and Complaint Mechanisms (FCM) of MdM-F, for which brochures are distributed during field activities with corresponding channels of reporting, also includes essential information on PSEA reporting. During MCs, this activity will be done simultaneously, conducted through group sessions, integrated in health education session or through individual sessions done by service providers (doctors, nurses, or social workers), ensuring messaging is done repeatedly to stress the importance of this topic. Information, Education and Communication (IEC) materials will also be distributed and posted in strategic areas to ensure widespread reach of key messages on this topic.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

MdM accountability system is based on sharing information in a timely manner with the different actors involved in the project as well as regular reporting to the Health Cluster. For this proposed project, the presence of MdM teams in the field makes this flow of information continuous and bidirectional, establishing a system that allows the participation of the PHC staff at MoH village councils and targeted community members in the definition and adaptation of MdM activities. In the same way, regular communication is kept with the International Cooperation Department (ICD) and PHC and Mental Health Directorates of the MoH to ensure full information sharing, participation, and feedback on MdM interventions. When feedback or complaints are received through any of the mechanisms in place, an immediate reply will be provided to the user with a reference number so the user can follow-up and track the process. The complaint will be processed first by the focal point who will categorize the feedback into 2 main categories: regular or sensitive feedback/complaint. Regular complaint is a non-sensitive complaint which relates to programmatic issues. A sensitive complaint includes issues related to abuse/inappropriate behavior with beneficiaries involving verbal, sexual abuse (PSEA), physical assault, or discrimination. Based on the details of the complaint, MdM will take up the feedback internally or with the partner, depending on the circumstances of the complaints. The committee will gather information and conduct hearings and visits. Users who provide feedback will be continuously updated about the process through email or phone, and final decisions and actions will be officially communicated through official mechanisms. All complaints will be registered anonymously by the focal point in a protected database and analysis will be done regularly. Feedback will be used to improve service delivery, re-orient approaches.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

MdM has functional Feedback and Complaint Mechanism (FCM) available for beneficiaries, partners, and suppliers through FCM cards which highlight that positive or negative feedback will all be taken into consideration and addressed independently from project team, respecting the principles of confidentiality and privacy, without affecting the possibility to receive assistance in the future. The cards, written in Arabic language, include a unique mobile and WhatsApp (accessible for persons with physical disabilities), project hotline number to receive the feedback calls or text messages, the working days and hours to receive the feedback, and a unique email address to receive electronic feedback. In-person complaints are also available for people who do not have access to electronic means. All these modalities are in place to allow safe access to the FCM by different groups regardless of sex, age, disability status, literacy, etc. MdM teams distribute and explain the FCM cards to the beneficiaries during their presence in the field while MdM logistics department also distributes and explains the card to all suppliers involved in the project. For this specific project, FGDs among groups of men, women, boys, and girls were conducted in a safe, accessible venue within the villages to understand their needs and tailor activities accordingly, and same mechanisms will be used to elicit feedback within the course of the project. A satisfaction survey will also be administered to collect feedback from the beneficiaries of services (trainings, awareness sessions, including PSEA, HF rehabilitation, MCs) to identify the degree of satisfaction and elicit suggestions for improvement by beneficiaries. The survey, FCM, and FGDs will be key elements in the project to take the necessary actions in enhancing the service quality and accessibility via timely analysis of feedback from the target groups who have utilized MdM services.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The form of humanitarian assistance that MdM is providing ensures that it does not further expose people to physical hazards, violence, or abuse. One of the components of the present proposal aims to increase access to quality services taking into consideration age, gender, and safety for healthcare providers and beneficiaries. Within its intervention, MdM is following specific rules for the data collection, gathering, and publishing based on MdM Guidelines, “Ethics in the Field”. For this specific project, FGDs and KIIs were conducted among specific target groups (groups of men, women, boys and girls) where needs of vulnerable communities (women, children, adolescents, PWDs, and elderly) were identified. Based on MdM assessments (2022), some of the barriers identified are related to protection issues, such as transport and access-related and social stigma. Thus, to mitigate these risks, launching MCs and strengthening the PHCCs near Area C communities will be conducted to address identified barriers. In this way, the protection and safety of vulnerable communities are ensured as they do not need to take long travel which will expose them to risks (checkpoints, settlers’ violence on the road, financial expense) to access health services. Moreover, the proposed project also aims to improve accessibility to services at PHCCs near Area C communities (rehabilitating HFs to make it more accessible for PWDs, such as installation of ramps and handrails) as well as promoting dignified access (awareness sessions to fight stigma and promote normalization to access MHPSS and GBV services). HF assessments were also recently conducted where the privacy, confidentiality, and safety of PHCC users were evaluated. Some PHCCs were found not having gender-separated toilets, not having locks on the door, and not having enough lighting in the surroundings. To ensure safety and protection of PHCC users, these elements will be addressed during the PHCC rehabilitation. Trainings will also be conducted among target groups (PHCC staff and MC staff) on inclusive service delivery without discrimination and the principle of Do No Harm approach, ensuring that both genders are represented among training participants. During the implementation of MCs, privacy and confidentiality during consultations, especially on SRH, GBV and MHPSS will be strived for by ensuring minimum requirements (installing curtains to ensure visual privacy and conducting sensitive consultations in a separate space to ensure both visual and auditory privacy). The MC team will also be gender-balanced, ensuring that female staff are available to provide SRHR and GBV services where most of the users are identified to be females. As a rule, all MdM teams signed the MdM confidentiality charter. MdM is also keeping safe storage of the beneficiaries’ data and information by using coding system and only allowing access to relevant people from the project team. When providing feedback to communities and stakeholders, maintaining confidentiality is critical for MdM and its team. The interest of beneficiaries is always protected, and individual names are not revealed in public or group meetings. Human Resources involved in the project, including that of the MoH, will be trained, supervised, and gender-balanced and it will be ensured that they are updated about the cultural and religious contexts. The project will also ensure that the communities are aware of the protection and complaints mechanisms in place. MdM interventions at the community level are provided by experienced teams through outreach programs which reduce the exposure of communities to harm and ensure equal access, including easier access for persons with disabilities.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Impartiality, non-discrimination, gender, and inclusion are adopted and put into practice by MdM within its intervention in Palestine. During MdM’s existing interventions, the capacity and supervision of MdM field staff and partners on gender, GBV, PSEA, and disability were reinforced to promote inclusive access for vulnerable populations, and refresher trainings on these topics will be done regularly. MdM is addressing the needs of the local community based on gender mainstreaming approach (gender-specific health), for instance, developing different IECs for women, men, and children, developing different protocols based on gendered various manifestations, and addressing the barriers preventing PWDs to access services. Through the MC program, MdM can reach vulnerable communities, especially those with mobility problems. Through HF rehabilitation, the principles that will improve access for PWDs (infrastructure and service provision) will be adopted. MdM MEAL processes take into consideration the monitoring and documentation of needs per age, gender, vulnerabilities including that of PWDs and GBV survivors. The established gender policy of MdM guides gender mainstreaming in its intervention. Through MdM recent assessment which was done in consultation with community leaders and different gender and age groups (2022), the needs of vulnerable groups, such as women, elderly, children, and PWDs have been identified and interventions within the proposed action are tailored to target the specific needs: having a gender-balanced team, trained staff on inclusive service delivery, rehabilitation of HFs to ensure access of PWDs and ensuring privacy and confidentiality for sensitive consultations, such as on GBV, MHPSS and SRH, and adoption of community-based emergency responses to promote local empowerment.
