Project Summary

The number of malnourished CU5 and PLW continue to increase due to the drought induced food insecurity and macro level economic challenges depleting community livelihoods and hindering access to adequate diet. The current estimates indicate that in the 12 districts targeted by this project, there is SAM case load of 7274 and MAM case load of 6481 while chronic malnutrition (stunting) has an average prevalence of 27.4% for these districts (national surveys have shown malnutrition to high in boys compared to girls, to be considered in the project). Without significant interventions, this nutrition situation will continue deteriorating as we are in the peak hunger season. In addition to food insecurity and macro-economic challenges, inadequate knowledge among mothers/caregivers on appropriate and healthy diets and poor IYCF practices compounds the situation. Poor hygiene practices prevalent in the communities also contribute to the increasing malnutrition rates. Micronutrient deficiencies for children under five years and PLW (iron) also exist in the target districts. With regard to health systems, there exist capacity gaps within MOHCC staff and community health workers to deliver high impact nutrition services to community members. In addition, there are also reporting and coordination gaps, which lead to duplication, wastage of resources, slow responses.
To address these needs, the project interventions will include strengthening active screening to ensure early identification of malnourished children and referral through community health workers and family led MUAC screening, complemented by increasing availability and access to therapeutic (RUTF) and complementary foods. The project will facilitate micronutrient supplementation to children 6-59 months and pregnant and lactating women; through facilitating distribution of micronutrient supplements for children (MNPs, VAS), pregnant and lactating mothers, (Iron and Folate Supplements). In addition, community nutrition interventions will be revitalised, strengthened through, and equipping of community health workers (Care group lead mothers/VHWs) and facilitating cooking demonstrations to teach mothers preparation of nutritious meals. The community health workers will undertake home visits and counselling sessions for mothers to enhance their knowledge and continued promotion of preparation of nutrition dense meals. The existing capacity gaps necessitate need for strengthen capacity of MOHCC staff and Community Health workers on IYCF-E and IMAM through capacity building trainings, mentorship and provision of job aides. Reporting and quality will be enhanced through supporting monthly review and reporting meetings, support with reporting tools and data analysis.
