Project Summary

Through the proposed intervention, TGH will contribute to achieving objectives 1, 3, 4 and 5 of the 2022 Protection Sector’s Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) for Syria through providing quality, integrated and sustainable protection services through community based approaches to children, young adults and women. Psychosocial support (PSS), including the identification of cases facing protection risks, their access to specialized services and case-management will be proposed. The project will also include awareness raising on a high range of protection concerns such child protection (CP) and risk education (RE) toward explosive hazards. The proposed intervention will be part of an integrated programme linked to Education project in public schools; which will consist in providing support in the communities given that both sectors’ needs are intertwined. TGH is already implementing CP programs under Education sector in schools in Hama, Idleb, Dar’a and Rural Damascus and aims at scaling up its intervention through providing assistance to communities at risk to a total of 4,900 conflict-affected people in Madiq Castle and Easter Bari (Hama), Khan Shaykun and Ma'arrat An Nu'man (Idleb), Dar’a, Iz'ra, Jasim and Kherbet Ghazala (Dar’a), Arbin, Nashabiyeh, Ghizlaniyyeh, Kafr Batna and Hajar Al Aswad (Rural Damascus). The intervention will provide approximatively 4,900 children with CP in public schools and 280 adolescents in safe spaces referrals and case management at home and protection awareness campaigns among the parents and the community. These sub-districts were selected based on their level of accessibility and the severity of needs as per the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) with all the localities being rated over 3 (25% of level 3, 58% of level 4 and 16% of level 5), TGH 2022 field assessments and coordination with municipalities SARC and other partners. The proposed intervention will consist in:
- Providing quality and integrated protection services, with a focus on community based approaches including awareness raising, psychosocial assistance through community centres and outreach mechanisms
- Conducting community-based risk education regarding explosive hazards;
- Building the capacities of facilitators, counsellors and case managers to prevent and respond to specific CP issues;
- Building the capacities of outreach volunteers (ORVs) to conduct data collection, awareness sessions and case identification and management, under the supervision of TGH case workers, in the surrounding communities;
- Providing PSS including risk education and recreational activities to children, including parenting programs
- Establishing and building the capacities of community based volunteers with the objective increasing awareness about child protection risks and ensure sustainability of the project.
TGH will consider and ensure gender-and-age aspects across all sub-sectors of focus – CP, GBV, and general protection. Cultural preferences and sensitivities will be considered through consultations with the staff – the majority of whom will be from local community and the affected populations. Specific needs of children, people with disabilities and other vulnerable groups have been considered.

3 - Needs

TGH’s proposed intervention is built on extensive protection risks identified through TGH field assessment conducted in June and Oct 2022. The conflict had a profound impact on children’s well-being and development, and putting them in a state of psychological distress. Victims are thus more likely to drop out from school than other children because of psychological traumas. It has been reported that around 20% of the children in all targeted areas are either orphans or unaccompanied due to the death or imprisonment of their parents. Child labour is also very common due to the poor economic situation worsened by the economic crisis and COVID-19. It is preventing school attendance and exposing children to a high range of risks, including injuries, work exploitation and harassment. Besides, girls and women continue to be strongly affected reporting to be subject to violence including intimate partner violence, sexual violence, early marriage, forced marriage, deprivation of freedom of movement and forced pregnancy. Lack of education and livelihood opportunities limit their freedom and erode their resilience. Access to specialized service providers including specialized medical care, MHPSS, legal service and civil documentation and alternative care services is very limited due to lack of capacity and resources in the assessed areas.TGH team reported that around 75% of the civilians assessed are still exposed to mine risks as a consequence of the hostilities.

4 - Implementation of activities

Integrated protection services including PSS, CM and referrals, and CP raising awareness will be conducted towards the community through the establishment of community centres and mobile units. Case workers will be able to provide direct support or external referrals such as medical care, MHPSS, legal service and civil documentation and alternative care services, etc. Where decision by mandated government agency is warranted, cases will be referred to the Directorate of Social Affairs and Labour (DoSAL) for appropriate action and legal decisions. TGH will provide services based on community approach and initiatives in order to ensure the coherence, acceptance and the sustainability of the activities. Protection awareness sessions will be conducted among the community with the support of community outreach volunteers. TGH will establish and train community based volunteers in each location who will support TGH team in PSS, identifying the protection risks and organizing the campaign awareness toward parents and community. Specific empowerment activities including PSS, life skills, literacy and numeracy in community centres to early married girls, adolescents male and women. Child protection assistance will be complementary to TGH education programs with a focus on the most marginalized groups such as early married girls and out of school adolescents. TGH will implement the project in coordination with SARC.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Children victim of abuse or violence from TGH staff or from other children (GBV, discrimination, child protection violation). Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: all TGH staff must sign a Code of Conduct, including child protection and PSEA; provision of training on PSEA and on working with children to all TGH staff; set up of TGH PSEA Policy and Child Safeguarding Policy; implementation of a PSEA hotline managed by qualified staff and communication about this hotline on the areas of intervention. In addition to the standard complaint mechanism (for all beneficiaries including children), one focal point is designated in each operational base to collect the potential PSEA complaints and a dedicated hotline was set up to receive reports. The different channels appear in all visuals that are put in TGH office and areas of intervention (i.e. distribution sites, etc.). All PSEA focal points receive specific trainings on how to deal with SEA complaint and their roles and responsibilities are defined in Terms of References; gender-balanced staffing.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The security situation puts TGH staff and beneficiaries at risk. Mitigation: daily follow-upof the security situation in the targeted areas in coordination with SARC and activities onhold until the security situation is stabilized. - Infation rate increases. Mitigation: TGH has already included the infation ratein the budget; in case of continuous infation TGH will adjust the incentives and BoQsaccordingly in coordination with the donor. - Parents/caregivers and community discourage their children/wivesfrom attending TGH activities. Mitigation: door-to-door campaigns and communicationwith support of Community based volunteers towards parents, case management by TGHcase workers. – TGH cannot find available safe space in targeted location Mitigation : TGH will ensure good coordination and acceptance from community, SARC and MOSAL.
