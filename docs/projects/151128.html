Project Summary

Objective: vulnerable and marginalized rural population groups, including women, children, elderly and IDPs of Zaporizhzhia region concern benefit from full and non-discriminatory access to essential services and enjoyment of their rights through Community centers (CC) mobile and static units
The planned activities are aimed at improving the living conditions of people affected by the conflict in eastern Ukraine (Zaporizhia region) in the field of humanitarian and social protection. The support will cover internally displaced persons as well as members of local communities affected by the conflict including women, children and elderly.
The targeted community centers located in Zaporizhzhia are reinforced to reach the most vulnerable people with essential services. Mobile teams will provide PSS activities and support for harder-to-reach beneficiaries.

As part of the response to the diagnosed problems faced by internally displaced people and members of local communities living in Zaporizhia Oblast, the project assumes support in the area of access to adequate protection and means of livelihoods, in particular psychological and legal help and assistance in the field career counseling within static and mobile Community Centers.
In addition, as part of the project, there will also be a Child friendly space for children and youth.
Through an innovative, inclusive approach, children’s and youth vulnerability will be mitigated, their resilience strengthened and sustainable civic engagement practices enhanced. Tolerance building and conflict and crime prevention on the grounds of otherness in society will reduce and prevent stigma and discrimination among children and youth.
Child Smile will work directly with children and their families to identify those most vulnerable and hardest to reach within the community.

Needs

The human toll of the armed conflict in eastern Ukraine is critical, with 4.4 million people affected by the crisis, of which 3.4 million require humanitarian assistance and protection. 800,000 IDPs have found refuge from the armed conflict. The host communities usually are not able to provide even basic services to all people. Recent analysis indicates that people resort to negative, even degrading practices to make ends meet, such as removing children from school, committing crime and survival sex. Continuous insecurity together with inadequate psycho-social support lead to long-term psycho-social problems. In Zaporizhzhia region women with children, beside the elderly, represent the major part of IDPs. The most vulnerable and marginalized people have no access to quality and affordable services, what contributes rights’ violations. Under these circumstance, children and youth often have not sufficient resources and no opportunity for expressing their needs and developing coping mechanisms, for creating trustful relationships and integrating successfully into peer groups, for looking forward to a self-determined life. However, in many communities experience shows that solutions to address these issues are emerging from young people’s own initiative, resilience, creativity, and willingness to engage meaningfully in shaping their own future – and the future of their communities.

Activities & outcomes

Community Centers and Child Friendly Spaces, both static and mobile, are to provide support through semi-structured appropriate activities.Outputs:1. Disadvantaged internally displaced persons and members of local communities receive support in the form of social protection, in the access to social, legal, psychological and professional assistance.2. Project beneficiaries’ material and living conditions improvement by providing minimal social protection.3. Project beneficiaries’ mental and psychosocial health improvement by providing the necessary psychological and psychosocial support.4. Project beneficiaries’ social situation improvement by increasing understanding, scope and access to the good quality legal assistance.5. Increase of soft skills in the area of job seeking, self-employment as well as opportunities for independent living. 6. Increase of full and non-discriminatory access to quality essential services and enjoyment of human rights, with particular attention to the most vulnerable through Advocacy.7. Children are provided with psychosocial services through structured activities at Mobile and Static Units CFS and outreach work. During implementation of the project, Child Smile will work according to approach on Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) (https://www.humanitarianresponse.info/ru/operations/ukraine/document/ukraine-accountability-framework-accountability-affected-population-aap) . Project team will make every effort to include in the project six components as per the IASC Accountability to Affected Population Guideline, which are:1. Leadership/Governance2. Transparency/Information Sharing3. Feedback and Complaints 4. Participation5. Design, Monitoring and Evaluation6. Working with Partners and Other StakeholdersGender-sensitive approach is used at all the stages of the projects. The proposed project takes into account the principle of equal treatment of beneficiaries, regardless of their gender. Child Smile will provide access to the assistance provided to both women and men. Actions proposed under the project are non-discriminatory.In addition, the project is planned in a way that is in line with the overriding humanitarian principle - first, not to harm, and also allows the development and strengthening of the potential of local communities affected by conflict. Child smile activities do not depend on any political will, the fund does not support any party of the conflict.Monitoring and evaluation: The project results will be measured through regular monitoring, Child Smile with the support of the local (municipalities) and regional (regional state departments) partners and stakeholders. Through the final evaluation, lessons learned will be compiled and possible improvements be identified, which can be used for scaling-up, by other stakeholders as well as by Child Smile programs.

Indicators & targets

1.Minimum 75% of the 500 families of internally displaced persons and representatives of the most in need local population state that their needs in the areas of access to quality and affordable services are satisfied to a greater extent.2. Minimum of 75% of persons (male and female) covered by legal support considered that the assistance obtained as part of the consultation contributed to solving their problem.3. Minimum of 75% of people(male and female) who were provided with psychological consultations agreed that the consultation was helpful in improving their well-being affected by the conflict in Eastern Ukraine.4. Minimum of 60% of persons(male and female) supported in the area of seeking a source of income recognized that the support provided contributed to the increase of their soft skills in the area of job seeking, self-employment and ultimately increased their self financing possibilities.6. Minimum of 500 psychological consultations.7. Minimum of 500 legal advices.8. Minimum of 400 professional consultations.9. Minimum of 10 regional advocacy campaigns.10. Minimum of 550 children (boys and girls) attended the CFS.11. At least 80% of children and youth, who participated in information events and SMART Parties, have received reliable information about GBV risks, prevention, referrals, have increased their social skills and have developed leadership merits.
