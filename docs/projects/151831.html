Project Summary

Since the outbreak of conflict in 2011, Libya continues to suffer from the impact of a protracted political crisis and civil conflict, which affected an estimated 1.5 million people in 2018 (Libya 2019 HNO (draft)). In response, DRC proposes to provide a comprehensive package of support, which includes protection, cash assistance and quick impact projects (QIPs) to build a more favourable protection environment for internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees, at both the individual and community level in western Libya (greater Tripoli, Zawiya, Sabratha, and Al Gabal al Gharbi), where DRC has ongoing projects in 2018. The protection activities will have a strengthened protection monitoring and referral mechanism at its core with the aim of a more informed response and advocacy initiatives. This will be complemented with specialized protection service provision targeted at the most vulnerable groups of women, girls and boys. In line with its aim to be a comprehensive programmatic approach, activities have been proposed to reach those affected at each stage of prevention, assessment and response, and therefore will reference objectives 1, 2 and 3 of the HRP protection objectives.
Note: this proposal describes a single programme of three (separately funded) projects.

Needs

Assessments•The needs addressed by this programme have been identified through ongoing protection monitoring programming in the proposed target areas. Additionally, DRC conducted a protection needs assessment from June-July 2018 in four of the target communities (Sabratha, Zawiya, Zintan and Yefren) which indicate multiple protection needs of IDP and returnee groups in these areas as well as a severe gap in availability of services, particularly of protection actors, in those areas. •A previous community-based QIPs needs assessment conducted across all the target areas (also in 2018) identified multiple urgent community-based project interventions in those locations which would serve to promote social cohesion amongst POCs. Needs identified (target areas): •Child protection issues such as children involved in dangerous work, exposure to family and communal violence, unaccompanied children, and child marriage were identified. Meanwhile focus group discussions with IDP youth indicate their vulnerability to recruitment by militia and degraded mental health and esteem after years of protracted displacement.•Women in these remote areas lack access to women's targeted services and safe spaces in which their psychological well-being can be assessed and addressed. However, the Protection Needs Assessment Report recognized that women's protection concerns are under-reported.•Protection monitoring findings continue to demonstrate a lack of registration among IDPs as well as the absence of civil documentation. •Finally, across all areas and target groups, both IDP and returnee households, as well as key informants in those locations (on behalf of their communities) continue to report their inability to meet their basic needs.

Activities

•Conduct protection monitoring to understand protection risks faced by conflict-affected populations, to inform protection interventions and advocacy and referrals•Conduct referrals for PoCs who require access to basic services to existing service providers where available. Referral data will also provide a window into the gap between needs and available services in the targeted Libyan communities.•Increase communication with communities as well as their capacity for self-protection by establishing community based protection mechanisms•Establish a multi-community centre (with a specific focus to ensure access for women and that incorporate's children's recreational activities), which provides access to specialized services such as PSS activities, lifeskills, and counselling..•Provide structured psychosocial support for youth during displacement/return through life skills, PSS and vocational training. DRC will implement this as part of a package, where vocational training will be an entry point for a protection response, while simultaneously increasing PoC’s ability to meet their essential basic needs. DRC's needs assessment and service mapping in the target areas (2018) found that protection needs are high but actors to provide protection services in the remote areas are not available. In addition, key informant's main request was for access to income (due to the liquidity crisis) and vocational training rather than other types of assistance. Thus, the demand for access to income and vocations training is well established in the target communities as well as the need for protection. However, communities in the remote target areas are resistant to engaging on culturally sensitive protection issues. Therefore, to establish community protection mechanisms for youth, DRC will first engage them on a vocational training to build trust and gain access to engage the community in sensitive, humanitarian protection needs and issues.•Comprehensive protection needs assessment in Sirte, in line with Protection objective 1.•Build on an existing foundation of research on civil documentation and the legal framework for IDPs (2018) to provide legal outreach/awareness raising as well as legal counselling through a mobile team (based in centres on a rotational basis)•Build social cohesion in communities through Quick Impact Projects (QIPs)•Support socioeconomically vulnerable households to meet their basic needs through the provision of multi-month cash assistance (MPCA).•Coordinate protection activities through the protection sector coordination unit through integrated/harmonized data collection tools, and strengthened referral pathways •Strengthen the existing referral pathway across health, PSS, child protection, legal etc services to ensure comprehensive service delivery to the PoCs. •Coordinate cash activities with the CMWG. DRC will build on the existing coordination efforts in the CMWG. In 2018, DRC cash working group members coordinated with partners in harmonising the risk and mitigation matrix, MEB and transfer value and M&E tools. DRC also supported the JMMI initiative by collecting and sharing market prices in Tripoli.

Access, safety and dignity of beneficiaries

Access – Project activities such as protection monitoring regularly assess access of beneficiaries to basic services. Also DRC conducts regular follow-up of beneficiaries referred to respective services to ensure PoC receives the service and closes a referral upon confirmation that the PoC received the service. DRC also conducts PDMs to ascertain PoC received the cash assistance and used it as per identified needs. DRC uses the findings to measure the success of assistance. . DRC ensures that all staff are trained on code of conduct once recruited to prevent workplace and protect the PoCs from any form of exploitation or discrimination. The established reporting mechanisms include emails or phone calls to the code of conduct intake committee at the country office, regional or HQ. The committee receives and reviews all reports on misconduct from the staff and beneficiaries, at times investigates and provides recommendation for appropriate action. DRC also maintains a complaint response mechanism in Libya, managed by project staff, through which PoCs call in to register complaints or make inquiry. DRC staff record all cases received through CRM and make follow-ups till the case is closed. The MEAL unit in DRC will gradually take over this CRM. Safety – Similar to the above, using a combination of protection monitoring, and community based protection mechanisms, this project will measure beneficiary's reported feelings of safety and dignity. (Some basic indicator measures on safety include threats of eviction, detention, crime in the area, presence of militia, and risk of UXOs, etc. DRC defers to local focal presence input on whether a visit of staff to the area will raise unwanted attention or safety risks and plans accordingly. Finally, MPCA activities are planned with beneficiary safety and dignity in mind in terms of choice of transfer modality and locations of distributions. PDM responses will also monitor whether beneficiaries feel that the cash assistance exposed them to additional risk within the household or community.
