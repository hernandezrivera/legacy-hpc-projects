Project Summary

The interventions will address acute needs of crisis affected communities and lack of access to basic services, health and protection of services to reach populations in Mudug region. The project will help to address important needs related to health. The IDPs, Refugees and Host Communities will be targeted through provision of health and related services.
The most vulnerable groups identified by Pmwdo internal assessment in Mudug region are the elderly, children, women, pastoral drop outs, disabled and destitute individuals who make up about 65% of the population in need. Of this vulnerable group, 55% are children with a proportion making 20% being children under the age of 5. The immediate needs as assessed by pmwdo include; limited access to clean drinking water, medicine and medical services, hospitals, mosquito nets, high level of illiteracy, among others.
Pmwdo proposed intervention To improve equitable access to quality emergency and essential lifesaving health services for crisis affected aimed at reducing avoidable morbidity and mortality for crisis affected identified beneficiaries in the region. Pmwdo anticipate to support 69,800 people by undertaking the proposed activities targeting men and women, boys and girls, adult and elderly, etc.

Project Activities

The project will support provision of life-saving health service delivery (Immunization, Safe motherhood, Malaria) including treatment and prevention of severe and moderate acute malnutrition in children and pregnant and lactating women through existing fixed facilities in Beyra Health Center, Galdogob Health Center, Harfo Refferal Health center and Bursallah Refferal Health Center. The project will also conduct outreach services and mobile clinics to IDPs, marginalized, nomadic and hard-to-reach communities in Hema, Margaga, Gobsho, Bilcil, Qaxansahley, Riigomane, Hero Jaale, Semede and Buubi in Mudug region.

Mitigation Measures

Strengthening the overall framework for legal protection and service provision for GBV survivors in Somalia is imperative for improving the well-being of women and girls. Some success was recorded in the implementation of legislation against sexual offences in cases of rape, torture and murder of underage and adolescent girls in Puntland and Baidoa. However, the lack of sexual offences legislation at the federal level and in Somaliland are obstacles to access to justice. GBV survivors and their families continue to adopt negative coping strategies of early/forced marriage to leverage social/financial security. Separated and orphaned girls are more vulnerable as they resort to this negative coping mechanism to secure some form of social protection. This increases their economic dependency and vulnerability to sexual exploitation and abuse. In addition, parents in fear of harassment and abuse of their daughters restrict their movements as much as possible to ensure safety. Clan interference and security personnel with limited skills to collect and manage forensic evidence prevent enforcement of sexual offences legislations where they exist.
