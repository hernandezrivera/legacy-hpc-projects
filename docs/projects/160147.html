Project Summary

The conflict, ongoing in eastern Ukraine for more than 5 years, negatively affects the socio-political and economic situation. People living along the demarcation line are the most vulnerable to overcoming the main consequences of the conflict. The situation at NGCA remains unstable. The periods of relative ceasefire alternate with the aggravation of the situation, which leads to further damage to civilian infrastructure. Residents of remote locations live in difficult conditions because they have the most limited access to basic services and limited financial opportunities to purchase warm clothes and fuel in the winter. This entails a reduction in the cost of food, medicine. A significant proportion of people in these settlements (about 30%) are over 60 years old, and the majority are women.
According to the UN Human Rights Monitoring Mission, more than 50 thousand private houses were damaged or destroyed on both sides of the demarcation line. In connection with random shelling of destruction of varying degrees received about 30,000 homes located on the NGCA. Despite a general reduction in hostilities, housing needs remain high. Part of the population, whose housing was partially affected, is experiencing economic difficulties for self-restoration and repair of housing due to loss of work, with the cessation of social payments and pensions. At best, humanitarian organizations were able to provide people in frontline communities with only a limited amount of supplies for emergency housing and basic NFIs for survival. Persons whose housing is completely destroyed are forced to seek shelter from friends and relatives. Those who allow income prefer to use rental housing. But among these victims there are a significant number of people who do not have such an opportunity, and they have been living in collective centers for a long time, mainly near the contact line or in large cities. As a rule, former student dormitories were not allocated for collective centers of residence and temporary accommodation centers, which were not adapted for long-term residence of pensioners and disabled people, as well as families with children. Shared kitchens and sanitary facilities do not comply with basic hygiene standards. Showers are often located in basements, which makes it difficult for people with limited mobility to access them. Gender-psychosocial pressure on IDPs and people affected by conflict is exacerbated by the lack of privacy for families living in the same place. Families with children and senior citizens in collective centers are at risk of eviction. In addition, a systemic problem for IDPs is the interconnected housing-work block: lack of social / suitable and free housing to accommodate so many people; and where there is such housing (for example, abandoned houses in villages), the bulk of IDPs do not want to live because of a lack of work and social infrastructure; there aren’t so many high-paying jobs that people can independently pay for rent and utilities. Therefore, the most vulnerable category of IDPs are able-bodied citizens and families with school-age children, since it is extremely difficult for them to find work and most of the assistance programs do not apply to them. The burden of rental costs is considered one of the main factors forcing IDPs to return to hazardous areas, which creates additional security risks. The aim of the project is to meet the key housing and non-food needs of the most vulnerable IDPs and the conflict-affected population through in-kind assistance, as well as by providing assistance in unforeseen circumstances.

1 - Needs

In accordance with the Humanitarian Response Plan for 2019, 2.2 million people living on the NGCA need various types of humanitarian assistance. All these people to one degree or another felt the consequences of a protracted military conflict. Along with basic needs such as food, access to water and medical services, access to housing and non-food items is a key need for such citizens, especially in preparation for winter. Families in rural areas spend more than 20 percent of their limited income on heating, and food consumption in winter is almost halved. Higher utility bills are forcing families to cut back on drugs, education, and even food. Households that cannot afford the big expenses are forced to collect firewood, often in areas with mine pollution. The conflict in eastern Ukraine has a special impact on older people. Of 2.2 million citizens who need help, 30% are people over 60 years old. These people face serious difficulties in accessing basic services due to their disability and limited mobility, and they are often separated from their families. Due to the lack of a noticeable sustainable world, most of the internal movements have become protracted. IDPs face challenges such as housing affordability and employment. Most IDPs are less resilient and face greater uncertainty in gaining stable employment and access to services, including housing, compared with an undeployed population. Host communities also see rising rents and food prices, a trend towards lower wages and reduced job opportunities. Displacement also causes tension between IDPs and host communities, which jeopardizes social cohesion. Gender and age analysis indicates that the needs for this type of assistance for both gender and age groups are almost the same. However, the intervention will take into account a greater number of women, as one of the goals of the project is to maintain the reproductive health of women and adolescent girls and older people due to their low mobility and special needs for medical care.At all stages of the development and implementation of the project, gender analysis will be an integral part of project activities. For this, age and gender data and vulnerability criteria will be taken into account. All events will adhere to the principles of equal access, as well as appropriate solutions for people with special needs. For a clear understanding of how the program is being implemented and the appropriateness of providing this type of assistance, all project activities will be monitored and evaluated throughout the implementation period. Inclusion of the local population from the very beginning, by explaining the criteria of vulnerability and participation in the targeted impact, will significantly strengthen gender and age equality in supported communities. A survey of beneficiaries after receiving assistance will be used to analyze the relevance of humanitarian assistance. The data obtained will be used for feedback from the beneficiaries.Gender and age characteristics will also be taken into account in the monitoring process to provide equal opportunities for women and men and different age groups to give feedback on the project and improve future intervention.In addition, the Foundation will apply, if necessary, the practice of interaction through social networks. Persons affected by the conflict (men, women, boys and girls) will have equal access to feedback mechanisms to provide feedback on the assistance received and its appropriateness.

2 - Activities & outcomes

As a result of the project activity, it is planned to implement the following activities and achieve the following results: 1. Providing solid household fuel to the most vulnerable categories of the population living in the most remote locations in preparation for winter. Priority - a person older than 75 years. The activity involves verification of beneficiaries, preparation of logistics routes, control of delivery, quantity and quality of coal, post-monitoring. Without the purchase of solid fuel. 2. Providing winter clothing sets in preparation for winter for the most vulnerable categories of the population, such as internally displaced persons 3. Support for centers of temporary compact residence in preparation for the winter, carrying out repair and restoration work, updating equipment. 4. Conducting light and medium repairs of private households that were destroyed as a result of hostilities. Through the implementation of project activities, it is planned to increase the economic, physical and psychological well-being of both individuals and their families, reduce their long-term vulnerability and work out long-term solutions. Targeting the most vulnerable people in hard-to-reach communities affected by the conflict will have a major impact in terms of reducing negative survival mechanisms and supporting beneficiaries. Planned results of the project activity: - The most vulnerable people affected by the conflict will gain access to basic needs, reduce their long-term vulnerability and increase resilience; - IDPs are granted equal access and the right to an adequate standard of living without any discrimination.

3 - Indicators & targets

As a result of project activities: 1. 3000 beneficiaries of age 75 will be provided with solid household fuel for the upcoming heating sea-son 2.200 beneficiaries who were forced to leave their homes will be provided with winter outfits 3. Light and medium repairs will be carried out in 300 households that suffered destruction as a result of hostilities. 4. 10 centers of compact residence and temporary accommodation centers (3,000 persons) will be supported in the form of repair and restoration work, as well as equipment replacement. The number of direct project beneficiaries will be 6500 people.
