1 - Activities

This project seeks to reduce incidences of all forms of Gender Based Violence including sexual exploitation and abuse as well as provision of linkages for separated and unaccompanied children in Mbire, Muzarabani and Rushinga Districts, reaching out to 211,047 women, girls, boys and men. The project will strengthen district referral pathways, facilitate access to essential services by survivors of GBV and build the capacity of communities to respond to GBV, through capacity strengthening of community structures to mitigate their their risk of exposure to protection violations, including GBV, SEA and Child Protection . The following activities will be undertaken. • Dialogue meetings/sessions with community leaders, religious leaders and opinion leaders on drivers of gender-based violence issues and how they can be eliminated• Identification and Training of community based champions or activists to champion GBV actions including awareness raising and reporting to establish community outreach and surveillance• Provision of Psycho-social support will be done through Safe hubs or shelter at St Alberts for continuum of care and support for the physically and emotionally traumatized GBV survivors.• Facilitate birth registration for boys and girls in Mbire, Muzarabani and Rushinga through organising mobile birth and other legal documents registration in remote areas and farms. • Strengthen the referral system to support survivors of GBV through updating the referral directory and updating information on services available in the province and the District• Community monitoring and review sessions on outreach and surveillance action plans • Strengthening strategic platforms for awareness raising in communities on GBV through use of Chid Protection Committees.• Establishment and popularise use of complaints handling and feedback mechanisms at all 25 satellite clinics operating under Alberts’ Mission hospital in Mbire, Muzarabani and Rushinga districts.

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

The project is in response to community calls to expand CAFOD and St Albert’s current gender and child protection programme in the district to additional wards. CAFOD and St Albert’s long standing presence in the district and ongoing programme with regular community consultation and dialogue sessions have informed the choice and development of activities. The community will be involved in quarterly community monitoring and development of the community action plans for outreach and surveillance. CAFOD is certified against the CHS and committed to accountability across our programmes. Staff have been trained on the SADI approach (safety, access, dignity, and inclusion) where accountability is a central principle, and has established comprehensive complaints handling and feedback mechanisms. These will include suggestion boxes, community feedback sessions, child protection committees platfporms and other stakeholder engagements.
