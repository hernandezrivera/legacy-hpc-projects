Project Summary

The proposed project aims at addressing education in emergencies needs in Mingkaman IDP camps following the influx of additional 37,215 IDPs in the area between August and October 2020 due to severe flooding in Jonglei State that started from July 2020. This new caseload added to the existing number of about 71,000 IDPs who have been living in Mingkaman since December 2013. The heavy rainfall that started from July to October 2020 led to the overflow of the Nile, Pibor, Sobat, Lol and other rivers. The floods caused large-scale displacement of people and cattle and damaged/destroyed crops and property, affecting an estimated 1,066,000 people in eight of ten states between July 2020 and January 2021. FCA will use this funding to complement ongoing humanitarian responses in Mingkaman by supporting school reopening activities for IDP populations and out-of-school children. Activities will include establishing new learning spaces in primary schools based on need and rehabilitating learning spaces damaged due to occupation by IDP populations. Alongside the learning spaces, FCA will establish gender-segregated latrines and install hand-washing stands. Cleaning and disinfecting classrooms to ensure safe reopening; Implement back to learning campaigns to reduce the risk of dropout after prolonged closure due to Covid-19, and distribute teaching and learning materials. In addition, the project will print and distribute appropriate IEC materials (i.e. posters and banners) with key messages on Covid-19 prevention and mitigation measures
Outcome: Increased and equitable access to safe quality education for children and adolescents aged 3-18
Output 1: Out-of-school children and youth (IDPs & host family) have access to safe quality learning
• FCA will establish new learning spaces in primary schools based on need and rehabilitate learning spaces damaged due to occupation by IDP populations. Alongside the learning spaces, FCA will establish gender-segregated latrines and install hand-washing stands. Learning environments equipped with latrines & hand-wash facilities allow children to learn in a safe & healthy environment, and have the opportunity to practice appropriate hygiene habits taught in class. In addition, WASH facilities encourage hygienic behaviour, such as using a toilet and washing hands. WASH in schools is essential for keeping children in the classroom and ensuring their safety and well-being, with clean and secure separate toilets for girls and boys and hand-washing services available to students and teachers.
Output 2: Enhanced capacity of teachers, education officials and PTAs to support quality education
FCA will train teachers in education in emergencies pedagogical skills and child-centred methodologies to improve learning for emergency-affected children. In addition, the project will train teachers on psychosocial support and the protection mechanisms of Covid-19. FCA will train PTAs and county education authorities on school management and governance and resource mobilization. The project will also procure and distribute teaching and learning materials to targeted schools.

1 - Needs

The implementation of the revitalized peace agreement signed in 2018 has not reduced the humanitarian needs in South Sudan. Lack of durable peace and limited investment in basic services are holding communities back from stability and sustainable development. In 2020, the multiple shocks caused by intensified conflict and sub-national violence, a second consecutive year of major flooding, and the impacts of COVID-19 hit communities severely. According to the recent Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO January 2021), an estimated 3.4 million (51 per cent girls) school-aged children have lack of or inadequate access to education services in 2021. This is a slight increase from the 3.1 million girls and boys in need in 2020. In addition, there are 98,500 school-aged refugee children, of whom 18,000 children are out of school. The flooding in South Sudan since July 2020 has significantly affected education infrastructure, particularly in Eastern Equatoria, Jonglei, Lakes, Upper Nile, Warrap and Western Equatoria states. Many flood-affected people took shelter in schools. This may affect the first phase reopening of schools as announced by the MoGEI. Since February 2020, about 430 schools in nine (9) states have been damaged/destroyed by floods; displaced populations also occupied most of these schools. Additionally, the prolonged closure of schools due to Covid-19 not only interrupted educational progresses, but also affected livelihoods and hindered children’s access to essential basic needs and services including school feeding, health information on disease prevention, protection and access to clean water and sanitation services.FCA has targeted Mingkaman IDP camps for this response following the influx of additional 37,215 IDPs in the area due to severe flooding in Jonglei State from July 2020. This new caseload adds to the existing number of about 71,000 IDPs who have been living in Mingkaman since December 2013. Following the arrival of this new caseload in September 2020, partners operating in the area conducted an Initial Rapid Needs Assessment to understand the impact and the immediate multi-sectoral needs of displaced populations. According to the assessment report, the new caseload of about 37,215 people (women-13,024, men-9,304, children-14,887) sought shelter in school buildings such as St Paul girls’, Mat Academy, Roordit, Tomrok and Padaar primary school. The prolonged occupation of school compounds by IDPs has already caused damage to classrooms and latrines. Assessment teams that visited schools where IDPs were camping expressed concern over congestion. A condition that made it difficult to keep physical distancing, and thus exposing people to the risk of contracting and spreading Covid-19. According to FCA’s recent monitoring reports, the prolonged closure of schools has forced volunteer teachers in Mingkaman to abandon the teaching profession. Many have sought employment in other sectors, working as community mobilizers and compound guards. There is need to remobilize teachers back to schools and update their teaching skills through refresher training programs.FCA has been supporting education services in Mingkaman IDP camps since 2014, and has gained experience, acceptance & recognition in the area. FCA is currently implementing education projects in Jonglei state (Fangak) and GPAA with funding ECHO and Ministry for Foreign Affairs of Finland (MFA). In both Jonglei and lake state, FCA has built over 65 learning spaces through partnership. In FCA’s previous partnership with UNICEF, the project was supporting 10 primary schools in Mingkaman IDP camps through establishment of learning spaces & rehabilitation of classrooms. According to our recent monitoring reports, of the 10 schools supported, only two schools (St. Paul Girls & Mingkaman P/S) have latrines in good condition. At Roordit & Mat Academy, latrine blocks for girls collapsed, forcing them to share the existing latrines with boys.

2 - Project activities

1. Establish four (4) new learning spaces in primary schools based on need. The project will establish new learning spaces to complement existing learning infrastructures, especially in schools where the number of children has increased following the arrival of additional IDP children. FCA will ensure that the learning spaces meet minimum safety standards (addressing internal, external, environmental and community level threats).2. Rehabilitate five (5) learning spaces damaged due to occupation by IDP populations. Rehabilitation activities will focus in schools where IDP populations sought shelter in school buildings such as St Paul girls’, Mat Academy, Roordit, Tomrok and Padaar primary school. FCA will coordinate with county education office, PTAs and other partners to ensure that IDPs vacate schools and seek shelter elsewhere at the camps to enable preparations for school reopening programs. 3. Establish ten (14) blocks of two stance gender-segregated latrines and install hand-washing stands. The project will establish latrines in schools such as Marik and Pan-Nhial primary school where there is none and at Roordit and Mat Academy where latrines have collapsed.4. Train 70 teachers in education in emergencies pedagogical skills and child-centred methodologies to improve learning for emergency-affected children. The project will also provide monthly incentives to the 70-targeted teachers as a dearness allowance.5. Train 110 PTAs and 20 county education authorities on school management and governance and resource mobilization6. Distribute teaching and learning materials, including IEC materials bearing key messages about Covid-19 prevention and mitigation measures.7. Implement back to learning campaigns to reduce the risk of dropout after prolonged closure of schools due to Covid-19. During these campaigns, FCA will sensitize and encourage parents and the wider community to send children back to school, especially girls and including children with disabilities (CWD).FCA will implement these activities in close coordination with WASH actors and health partners especially during Covid-19 awareness raising activities. FCA is an active member of the Education Cluster and has over 10 years of experience and expertise in Education in Emergencies work in the region. FCA has established good working relations with local actors through ongoing projects, and we are in good position to leverage this experience to implement this project.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

FCA has a focal person for Child Safeguarding and SEA at Juba level and field office. The project will print and distribute materials (flyers, posters) with key messages about Child Safeguarding and SEA. FCA will ensure that these materials are printed in the local language to enable the beneficiaries to understand the key messages. Poster will also be displayed on walls of FCA offices in the field and walls of school buildings and compound of County Education office to act as reminders.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

In order to gather feedback from beneficiaries who are not literate, FCA will encourage individuals to approach project staff directly or through telephone contact of FCA's CRM focal point in Juba and at field level in Mingkaman. This will enable beneficiaries and other stakeholders to express their concern in situations where FCA has failed to meet stated commitments, and where stakeholders have witnessed a misconduct of staff such as bullying or fraud.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

FCA will strengthen its existing Complaints Response Mechanism in Mingkaman. In order to gather feedback from beneficiaries who are not literate, FCA will encourage individuals to approach project staff directly or through telephone contact of FCA's CRM focal point in Juba and at field level in Mingkaman. This will enable beneficiaries and other stakeholders to express their concern in situations where FCA has failed to meet stated commitments, and where stakeholders have witnessed a misconduct of staff such as bullying or fraud. FCA has existing complaint boxes installed at community level and in schools. Through the complaint boxes, FCA's complaints focal person at Mingkaman will open the boxes on regular basis and the complaints committee will have regular meeting aimed at providing feedback to operational complaints instantly and in a timely manner. The lesson learnt from mistakes will help in improving the program.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project will use the feedback from beneficiaries for learning and ensuring that there is improvement in subsequent programs.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Child protection related concerns from this project will be channelled through established protection and referral systems. All dimensions of protection, including physical, cognitive and psycho social protection will be integrated into aspects of education programming, including TLS design & construction, teacher training and community mobilization. Similarly, FCA teacher training/induction will anchor methods that support development of life long behaviours, leadership, resilience, and capacity to create sustainable livelihoods and contribute to the peaceful reconstruction of their communities. FCA will also work closely with Child Protection partners to follow up CAAFAGs and ensure CAAFAGs who are qualified to join main stream primary school at upper level are followed, enrolled and retained in schools.
