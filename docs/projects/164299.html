Project Summary

Within Salamieh District of Hama Governorate, local markets and economies continue to function despite significant disruptions in supply chains, limited financing, and irregular access to productive assets. Furthermore, the misalignment between labour supply and limited demand has resulted in segments of the population experiencing chronic unemployment. Employment opportunities within vocational trades have emerged as an area of opportunity; however, the preparedness of the labour market in terms of skill set has resulted in limited ability to meaningfully engage with these emerging employment sectors. Furthermore, the enabling environment for business creation has been disrupted since the onset of the crisis.
Despite the multiple layers of challenges placing pressure on local markets, a number of businesses have emerged filling gaps in social services (e.g. water trucking, communal power distribution) and other market needs (e.g. food services, clothing, etc.). These micro businesses are able to capitalized on growing market demand and become important actors within the local labour market. E4R seeks to build the current local economic conditions within Salamieh District in order to improve employability of affected people and vulnerable groups and contribute to the creation of local enterprises. The overall objective of this project is to strengthen coping mechanisms of affected people and vulnerable groups, especially women, youth and People with Disabilities (PwD), through vocational training and business development services. During the life of the project 300 affected and vulnerable people will be reached, of which 45% and 4% will be female and PwD, respectively. The project target is reflective of the number of unique project beneficiaries and has been determined based on maximum capacity of vocational training courses as well as the number of new enterprises which can be supported during the life of the project.
Specific Objectives:
• Increase access to market relevant skills/vocational training for 175 affected people and
vulnerable groups, especially for women, youth and PwD. All trainees will be equipped with kits and tools related to their respective vocation.
• Improve business development and management knowledge for 300 emerging and
existing entrepreneurs. In addition to business to business training, all trainees are required to partake in a Life Skills courses which includes modules on communication, leadership, personal financial management, conflict resolution.
• Support socio-economic establishment and recovery of 80 micro- and small-scale enterprises through grants, loans, and asset replacement with a particular focus on vulnerable groups. Upon completion of the entrepreneurship and business management training, individuals will be invited to submit a business plan for funding consideration. In total, 80 business plans will be selected and funded with a blend of grant, loans, and business facilitation services.

4 - Needs

Salamieh District has a population of 223,935 people, over half of which are female (53.7%), 5.3% (or 11,870 individuals) are persons with disabilities, 19.6% (or 43,980 individuals) are internally displaced persons and 2.9% (or 6,498 individuals) are returnees. AKF 2019 updated database of vulnerable HHs in Salamieh District has identified a total of 33,256 households accounting to 145,992 individuals with 50% females as vulnerable. In 2019, AKF conducted the ‘Comprehensive Humanitarian Socio-economic Study in Salamieh” (CHESS), which indicated that approximately 80% of the total population (224,000 people, 54% female) in Salamieh District live below the national poverty line (2 USD per capita per day) by earning less than 300 USD/family-of-five/month, and 94% of households (HH) are assessed as food insecure.The unemployment rate is particularly high among females (75% compared to 27% of males), while the average income of individuals pursuing irregular jobs (e.g. plumbing, carpeting, plastering) is 40 USD/month. The imbalance between household income and expenses is a key driver of vulnerability that contributes to negative coping mechanisms adopted by households, including IDPs. Results from CHESS indicate that 93% of households in Salamieh are employing negative livelihood coping strategies to meet their basic needs. To afford food and medical care, 80% of HHs have minimized expenditure on non-food items; 51% have skipped payments for rent or debts; and 48% took out more loans and fell further into debt to afford food and medical care. In addition, 28% of HHs took-on jobs that are considered dangerous e.g. harvesting in areas known to have mines to support their households. HHs engaged in other coping strategies; for example, 10% of HHs sold productive assets, 7% withdrew their children from school, 7% engaged under 16-years children in child labour, 6% begged for money, 4% moved to less-quality housing, and 3% sent at least one-member to work abroad.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will provide vocational and skills training opportunities to 175 vulnerable young individuals (40% females) who will also be equipped with kits related to their respective vocation through AKF established Continuing Education Center (CEC) which offers a variety of market-relevant courses based on labor market assessment.To promote local entrepreneurship, a 2-week course will be developed on entrepreneurship and business management. This course will equip 300 individuals of both CEC trainees and other aspiring entrepreneurs with the knowledge of how to generate a business idea, start a business, conduct a market analysis, create and accurately price product offerings, maintain basic accounting records. The target group is comprised of 175 CEC trainees and 125 vulnerable individuals including elderly who have either expressed interest in starting a micro-enterprise or are seeking to grow their current business.To support the creation and strengthening of local enterprises, the project will launch a micro seed start-up fund designed at increasing access to financial resources for individuals wishing to start or strengthen an enterprise. Upon completion of entrepreneurship training, individuals will be invited to submit a business plan for funding consideration. In total, 80 business plans will be funded with a blend of grants and loans in partnership with the First Microfinance Institution FMFI. AKF will provide a grant through enterprise-related assets by bulk purchasing common equipment and/or disburse grant financing directly to the entrepreneur. These 80 enterprises will be supported with business facilitation services such as mentorship, and legal advice.Process and outcome monitoring activities will include baseline assessments of target group, rapid labor market assessments, ongoing feedback with project beneficiaries, post-vocational training monitoring, labor market transition monitoring, and FGDs with project stakeholders focusing on youth.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

A: Gender related cultural and traditional norms and roles may limit ability of women to participate in some vocations. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium. Female trainees which are selected will be asked preference of specific vocation in order to ensure their comfort. AKF will maintain regular monitoring of women contribution along with active complaint reporting system, and facilitate gender equality awareness raising or orientation sessions to targeted individuals to facilitate positive interaction and appreciation of female participants.B: Women maybe hesitant to choose non-traditional vocation or may not feel confident enough to manage enterprises themselves due to social pressure and cultural perceptions of women knowledge and capabilities in such knowledge which may expose them to exploitation. Likelihood: high, Impact: high. Regular and confidential monitoring and compliant reporting system will help to identify and investigate any cases of exploitations and take actions against it. Strengthening women decision making through increase their access to technical knowledge and promotion for their participation in such vocation. AKF will facilitate gender equality awareness raising or orientation sessions to targeted individuals to facilitate and promote for female participation and capabilities to be involved in non-traditional vocations.C: Increased risk of inter-community tension where CBOs members might be criticized or accused of bias in selecting the beneficiaries, Likelihood: High, Impact: Medium. AKF will communicate the project objectives and targeting approach and criteria, oversee the selection process and ensure a precise implementation based on the selection criteria.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk1: The deterioration of the security situation limiting movement of staff and beneficiaries Mitigation: AKF regularly monitors the regional and local security situation using a variety of primary and secondary sources. In the event that mobility of staff and beneficiaries in rural areas within Salamieh District is deemed a high risk, AKF will delay the implementation of activities until the situation is able to be reassessed and determined safe for travel and implementation. Risk2: Misalignment between labour supply and demand widening: Mitigation: Conducting a rapid labour market assessment in order to validate the current and future selection of vocations will mitigate any potential labour market gaps widening. Risk 3: Low desire to start-up micro enterprises: Mitigation: Given the context, the decision to start an enterprise takes into account a number of factors. It is possible that individuals who have successfully completed the entrepreneurship and business management training may wish to not start an enterprise.
