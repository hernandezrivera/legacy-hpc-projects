Project Summary

The main objectives of this project are:
1. To ensure the provision of quality, lifesaving nutrition services for acutely malnourished children (boys and girls 6 - 59 months of age) and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) among highly vulnerable communities
2. Improve equitable access to integrated program interventions for the prevention of under nutrition among affected communities
The project targets children aged 6–59 months and Pregnant and Lactating Women and Girls with specialized nutritious foods and social and behaviour change messages aimed at the prevention and treatment of acute malnutrition across selected states and localities in Sudan.
A comprehensive package of food and non-food based interventions are provided taking into account nutritional vulnerabilities.
The proposed response from WFP is in line with the Nutrition Sector Strategy and with the Multi-Year Humanitarian Response Strategy of Sudan. Through this project, WFP will be responding to the nutritional needs of targeted beneficiaries under Outcome 1, 2 and 3.

With a particular focus on Do No Harm and Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) principles, WFP Sudan strives to enhance protection mainstreaming through the following priority areas: 1) Training and capacity building of key WFP and Cooperating Partners (CP); 2) protection risk analysis focused on safety, dignity and integrity of beneficiaries; 3) integration of protection indicators into program tools, notably Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) tools; 4) design, implementation and adjustment of programs in line with identified protection concerns particularly for those disabled and with special needs; and 5) strengthened partnerships and coordination on protection issues.

Needs

Acute and chronic malnutrition are widespread and pervasive and constitute a significant public health problem. Over 2 million children under 5 suffer stunting annually, and 2 million wasting. There are several causes of undernutrition, including limited access to food, marginal and unstable livelihoods, poor quality of health services, including sexual and reproductive health services, unfavourable hygiene and childcare practices, unhealthy dietary habits, poor institutional capacity, gender inequality and poor infrastructure for absorbing and adapting to shocks. Basic health services are available to less than 50 percent of the population, while only 13 percent of the rural population has access to improved sanitation facilities. The national prevalence of acute malnutrition (wasting) is 16.3 percent, placing the Sudan in the “critical” category, with high levels recorded in both conflict-affected and non-conflict affected states. The chronic malnutrition rate is 38 percent (MICS 2014), with 11 of 18 states recording stunting prevalence above 40 percent, considered very high by World Health Organization (WHO) standards. Stunting negatively affects a child’s physical development, cognitive development, ability to complete school and income-earning potential as an adult. At an aggregate level, this translates into a loss of 2–3 percent of GDP per year. Micronutrient deficiencies, particularly in iron, vitamin A and iodine, are rampant among children under 5, school-aged children and women of reproductive age.

Activities

1) Provide preventive and curative nutrition activities to children aged 6–59 months and PLWG: Immediately following a shock and until relevant indicators stabilize, WFP will conduct emergency blanket supplementary feeding for all PLWG and children aged 6-59 months in affected populations, complementing general food distribution. 2) Provide curative and preventive nutrition activities to children aged 6–59 months and PLWG and capacity strengthening to national and state health institutions.In addition to moderate acute malnutrition treatment, emphasis will be placed on making prevention activities the foundation of WFP’s nutrition interventions in targeted areas. 3) Food-based prevention of malnutrition targeting the first 1,000 days of life to prevent stunting and wasting, consisting of nutrition rations 88 for at-risk PLWG and children under zero-23, accompanied by context-specific nutrition-sensitive interventions 4) Home fortification interventions through the distribution of micro nutrient powders free of charge to children 6-59 month 5) Social and Behavior Change Communication (SBCC) as an integral part of all prevention and treatment activities
