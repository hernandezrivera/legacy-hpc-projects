Project Summary

This project aims to support the People in Aleppo and Idleb countrysides through sustainable adequate and stable food availability, accessible source of basic food required to keep people with good balanced nutritional conditions and assure availability and accessibility to all food items and required, utilized and affordable via all people in a fair and decent way saves people dignity via facilitating accessibility for those foods and enhance its continues availability in the market.
Some 6000 households (about 36,000 individuals), consisting mainly of poor IDPs women and households headed by the women, will benefit from the project. Each monthly voucher is worth US$ 50 and can be exchanged for food items in selected local shops.
The implementation plan is based on the distribution of monthly vouchers to beneficiary families so that each family will benefit from eight consecutive rounds of vouchers program, in other words, 6000 families will benefit for seven months. achieving eight rounds of distribution, as two rounds of distribution will be conducted during the last month. Where the last round of distribution will be conducted by the end of the last month to be consumed during the next month.
For local sellers, the vouchers are creating hundreds of new customers, most of whom previously had difficulties affording to buy food in their shops.
Silk Road has developed a strong plan for the implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of this program will start by pre-assessment of the target population aims to collect quantitative data focused on project indicators (measuring all project indicators prior to intervention) and that will take a place in the first month of the implementation.
In the next phase, Silk Road will select the beneficiaries of the program and the participating sellers as well also we will do verification of the selected beneficiaries. We will use clear and logical selection criteria in partnership and cooperation with the community (Through focus group discussion that will be held) in order to ensure that all families understand why some were selected and why some other families were not eligible. We will use a separate team to verify the extent to which beneficiaries meet criteria to ensure transparency and objectivity in selection.
The priority will be given for:
 Newly IDPs families.
 Women-headed households.
 Families with large size above six members and do not have a breadwinner.
 Families with low income. Criteria for selecting Sellers:
 Seller's ability to re-supply goods
 The diversity of goods in the market
 The accessibility of beneficiaries to the market
 Number of days and hours working
 The reputation of the seller in the region
 Fees or commissions payable by the Organization if any
 Cooperation with the organization and the seller's willingness to accept the terms and conditions of the payments
1. Also, within the first month of the project, we will conduct the sealers capacity assessment include the frequency of re-supply of goods focusing on the goods we plan to distribute, the availability of a warehouse close to the shop, notes by the team on the seller's skills in dealing with customers as well as the number of customers who came during the evaluation period, reputation and seller's interest in participating in the program and finally the financial capacity of the seller. Furthermore, we will update the assessment of prices, the current situation of the local market, and the most requested goods by the local population before identifying sellers.
2. In this project, we will use unconditional vouchers (Food for a fixed monetary value (value-based vouchers) in the selected shop) that allow sufficient flexibility for the beneficiaries, where they can buy whatever they need from the list of the items included in the voucher according to the voucher amount.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2021, 14.2 M People in need 51% of them Female and 49% Male, The crisis in Syria has entered into its tenth year, with 2020 having seen the emergence of new and complex socioeconomic difficulties that are further exacerbating the food security and livelihood situation of families across the country. The situation in Syria has drastically deteriorated, with an estimated 14.2 million people in need of some form of food and agriculture assistance, representing 68.6 per cent of the total population. Based on a countrywide assessment of 36,484 households and technical reviews and consultations, at least 12.4 million people are estimated to be food insecure, of which 1.3 million people are considered severely food insecure. Furthermore, all 1.7 million310 people living in camps are deemed to be 100 per cent food insecure. Silk Road had conducted Needs Assessment in 33 communities many people are facing shortages in food items in the assessed locations due to lack of money to buy food and the high price of food when available. The available food in these locations come either from markets or lending food to IDPs from markets or host communities. In case of food access difficulties, IDPs can cope this challenge by reducing the numbers of meals eaten in a day, borrowing food or limiting the portion size at meal time. The most needed food items according to the interviewed HHs are pulses and beans, cereals and bread, oils and fat, sugar, meat, and other items. But the availability with limited quantities and IDPs don't have the financial ability to buy them. The main sources of income for families are Agriculture and livestock breeding, and for the displaced families is daily working and Aid.
