Project Summary

The proposed project intervention aims to increase food availability and access by providing emergency livelihood support kits and adaptive training to vulnerable IDPs, subsistence farmers and fisher-folk households to increase crop, fish and vegetable production, mainly for consumption at the household level and sale of surplus at local markets. The proposed project targets 10,783(Male 5,284 ;Female 5,499) households in Gogrial West (5,600hh’s), Gogrial East (2,033hh’s) and Malakal (3,150hh’s) with emergency crop, fishing and vegetable kits.
These locations have been chosen in accordance with the Food Security Outlook Update for South Sudan (FEWSNET, Jan to Mar 2022 Report) which has the most people affected by the floods in IPC phase 3 & 4 and the long standing presence of SAADO in the proposed locations implementing the SSHF reserve allocation from November 2021 to April 2022;a-3-year CGFD in Gogrial West & Gogrial East; a 7-year SSJR in Malakal County.
The project will target 10,783 households from the three Counties who will be supported with fishing kits, crop kits and vegetable kits which avails immediate benefits by providing white meat for the HHs (fish) as well as providing an alternative source of income through the sale of surplus fish. This income will be used to supplement the HHs food requirements as well as address urgent HHs needs. To ensure maximum realization of benefits as well as ensure sustainability, HHs will be trained on improved fish production practices such as catch hygiene, fish postharvest and preservation as well as utilization and nutrition training. They also will be supported with vegetable kits to address their nutritional requirement in the short-term (early 3-weeks). Income from the sale of surplus produce will be used to purchase alternative food requirements such as (sorghum) as well as supplement the HH dietary needs. Farmers will receive basic training on improved agronomic practices with regards to micro-irrigation of small-scale commercial vegetable production.
All the 10,783 households will be supported with crop and vegetable kits. The emergency livelihood kits will be obtained from the FSLC-recommended/FAO pipeline. SAADO will put in place a mechanism for effective and participatory community vulnerability mapping and use a comprehensive beneficiary selection criterion developed and discussed and agreed upon with the communities to include Men, Women, Boys and Girls. During community consultations at various levels, all men, women boys and girls will be consulted to fully understand and appreciate the impact of the current crisis on men, women and children that would further inform our proposed response. “Do no harm” and AAP principles will be applied throughout the duration of the project, so that any possible negative implications of the project at community level are addressed before they occur through integrating a strong Community Communication and Engagement (CCE) feedback mechanisms. SAADO’s proposed emergency response will also complement the lifesaving, livelihoods and nutrition activities carried out by other FSL partners in the proposed three counties such as WVI in Gogrial West County and Plan in Malakal County of Upper Nile state. The proposed activities are necessary and sufficient to achieve the stated cluster objectives for these interventions based on the relevance of each activity in increasing access to emergency food security and livelihoods support for the most vulnerable households in the targeted communities. The indicators selected are SMART and SAADO has in place a strong monitoring and evaluation system and mechanisms to measure these indicators and to ensure the intended results are achieved. The means of verification, such as monthly reports, distribution lists and regular field visits are strongly credible and are currently used by the organization in its MEAL processes.

1 - Needs

An increase in sub-national violence reported in many states across the country, including Upper Nile and Warrap, devastating floods, widespread flood-related crop losses and the impact of COVID-19 including 40% hyperinflation and a protracted macro-economic crisis resulting in record high food price spikes has continued to disrupt livelihoods and contribute to an increase in humanitarian needs in 2022(HNO,2022). According to the Food Security Outlook Update for South Sudan (FEWSNET, Feb 2022), most households have depleted their minimal harvests in the early season with most current data according to the (IPC January to March 2022) documents stating that the severely food insecure population is expected to increase to above 59% in the three Counties with Gogrial West and Gogrial East having 7,000 people in emergency;14,000 people in crisis levels while Malakal has 5,230 people in emergency;9,000 people in crisis levels. To compound this dire situation, the (FAO Special report Dec 2021) forecasts for South Sudan also paints a dire picture for food insecurity which is expected to be severe at the height of the lean season of 2022 due to the impact of conflict, climatic and economic shocks. Local currency depreciation, inflation, high import and fuel costs, and other factors, are sustaining high staple food prices and limiting economic activity, which in turn restricts household purchasing power. For instance, In July 2021, the average exchange rate in the parallel market stood at South Sudanese Pound (SSP) 383 to the US dollar, equivalent to the rate one year ago, in August 2020. By contrast, the official exchange rate depreciated, as compared to April 2021, by 37 per cent (from SSP 272 to 433) and by 62 per cent compared to June 2020 (dropped from SSP 166 to 433) according to (VAM-food security analysis report Nov 2021). To corroborate this information, the (World Poverty Outlook report 2021) projects that given the large economic fallout from Covid-19 and other concurrent shocks, poverty at $1.90 per person per day is projected to increase to 78.2 percent in FY2021/22 from 76.8 per-cent in FY2020/21 in South Sudan with prices expected to continue rising and persist at extremely high levels across 2022.The nutrition assessment FSNMS February 2022 data analysis reports high levels of GAM rates as measured by weight-for-height z-score (WHZ), observed and (GAM (WHZ)>=15%) in Gogrial West and Malakal Counties and in some places mortality rates, measured by Crude Death Rate (CDR), exceeded 1/10,000/day within the greater Warrap. In Malakal County for example, GAM (WHZ) was 35.9 percent, Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) (WHZ) was 11.4 percent (8.4-15.2), and the non-trauma CDR was 1.29. In addition, according to the (Global weather Hazards Report December 2021), South Sudan received excessive rainfall thereby resulting into floods and hindering the early harvest that used to serve people during the hunger gap. The NOAA/NCEP Global Ensemble Forecast System report also cites that the production loss due to flood damage is estimated at 26,892 Ha of cultivated land which translates to an estimated loss of 11,916MT of cereals in the flood-affected counties.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

A core component of SAADO’s beneficiary accountability and communication in this proposed intervention will be the regular dissemination of project-related information and updates to community members (beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries). Communities will be encouraged to provide regular feedback through monthly community consultation meetings facilitated by established project management committees. Information will be fed back to projectmanagement teams who will be responsible for adapting implementation strategies and workplans accordingly thereby ensuring field realities are reflected in monthly planning hence facilitating flexible and adaptive programming based on lessons learnt and emerging priorities and needs. In addition, information relating to the project’s response to feedback will be fed back to communities by the project management committees through scheduled consultation meetings, thereby ensuring ongoing two-way communication.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

SAADO’s proposed project will ensure tailored transparency and feedback mechanisms such as open meetings, verbal, written and pictorial notifications of decisions and actions; consultations with women and men (separately where necessary to ensure distinct gendered voices are heard), consultations with different age groups and persons with special needs, and confidential complaints mechanisms (such as a complaints box) will be used.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

SAADO’s proposed project will utilize the established beneficiary communication and accountability (BCA) mechanisms and project management committees (PMC’s) to provide information to the beneficiaries as well as address issues around prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse(PSEA). Whenever such issues are raised SAADO’s protection staff will link the survivors with protection implementing partners as part of strengthening the referral pathways. The project management committees will also publicize and manage a complaints and response mechanism which will allow beneficiaries to provide feedback and anonymously report grievances and complaints related to PSEA. The PMCs will use project-specific risk-based protocols to respond to complaints. The project management committees will be trained to follow SAADO’s PSEA policy standards and guidance for reporting and investigation and any other requirements as per national law.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

A core component of SAADO’s beneficiary accountability and communication in this proposed intervention will be the regular dissemination of project-related information and updates to community members (beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries).
