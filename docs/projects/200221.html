Project Summary

The goal of this project is to strengthen protection for women survivors of violence during the complex humanitarian crisis in Lebanon. The project targets underserved Lebanese women, migrant women, and PRL by addressing critical protection gaps in GBV prevention, response and through capacity building. The project will be directly implemented by KAFA, with support from (LUPD), and overseen and monitored by UN Women, providing technical assistance to ensure effective project implementation.
This project will aim to address the following areas:
1.1 GBV Prevention: target women, girls, men and boys who participate in targeted gender equality and empowerment activities in safe spaces or at community level as part of GBV prevention programs.
2.1 Provide specialized GBV services: case management, psychosocial support, legal assistance, and shelter to GBV survivors and those at risk of GBV.
3.1 Assess the satisfaction of GBV survivors who received case management services.
5.1 Increase the capacity of institutions and civil society actors to respond to GBV cases by providing training to improve their knowledge of GBV.
Under prevention, activity 1.1 – the project aims to implement sensitization activities, including education and awareness sessions on GBV, menstrual hygiene management, women's rights, gender existing legal framework related to GBV/gender, and the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). Additionally, the project will provide information sessions, conduct safety audits at the community level, distribute dignity kits to those in need, and encourage participation in community-based committees on GBV to increase community involvement and promote local ownership of the prevention efforts. [KAFA, LUPD]
Under specialized services, activity 2.1, the main outreach mechanism for survivors receiving these services will be through KAFA’s helplines, services are available for women survivors of violence and human trafficking for labor exploitation. Women will receive holistic case management, led by highly experienced and trained case workers and will take place in women safe spaces in Beirut, Bekaa, and Saida, safe housing/shelters, and in case of emergencies, remotely. The case management services, provided inside or outside the safe shelters, include social care services, psychological and psychosocial interventions, health services, legal assistance as well as safe referrals to other multi-sectoral services. For women migrant domestic workers, case management could include assistance with safe voluntary return by ensuring administrative follow up for travel procedures and/or travel tickets to country of origin. In partnership with LUPD, specific staff and interventions will be offered to women with disabilities, including ensuring that physical centers are accessible, and staff are equipped with information and tools to best support their needs. [KAFA, LUPD]
Under satisfaction follow-up, activity 3.1, the project will use various feedback methods to evaluate the satisfaction levels of GBV survivors who received case management services. To ensure that survivors receive adequate case management services, the project will adopt a survivor-centered approach, provide case managers with training and mentorship, create feedback mechanisms for survivors, analyze feedback to improve services, and regularly monitor and evaluate case management services. [UN Women, KAFA]
Under capacity building, activity 5.1, the project will provide training to institutions and civil society actors to increase their knowledge and capacity to effectively respond to gender-based violence (GBV) cases, with a focus on women and girls with disabilities. Target actors will include MOSA and UN Women’s livelihoods partners in order to increase GBV and livelihoods linkages. [UN Women, KAFA, LUPD]
The implementation of the activities will be done by KAFA and LUPD. UNW will oversee the project and support in the Capacity building and M&E.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

This project will use gender sensitive approaches to ensure women’s protection, such as prioritizing the recruitment of female frontline staff, monitoring women’s utilization of protection cash assistance, and adopting new outreach strategies to identify migrant women left behind and not accessing services and assistance available. In addition, UN Women has strong policy and prevention mechanisms in place to prevent SEA in humanitarian response context. Under this project, all partners – KAFA and LUPD- must meet UN Women’s minimum standard requirements for PSEA including having a PSEA focal point, staff mandated orientation/trainings and ensure beneficiaries have access to a safe and confidential referral mechanisms.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Ensuring a feminist approach and women’s empowerment is at the heart of this project, whereby the needs and perspectives of female affected populations will be prioritized. This is an incredibly important issue of all recipients of aid, and particularly critical for projects targeting survivors and victims of GBV. Accountability for affected populations will be integrated into every aspect of UN Women’s project monitoring and evaluation framework. The project will ensure that there is a complaint and feedback mechanism so that all women can voice their concerns and resolve disputes with regards to the delivery of aid. PSEA will be integrated into these mechanisms. In addition, a steering committee made-up of migrant women beneficiaries from the targeted communities will be the main forum for regular community-led feedback. Their needs and suggestions will help guide project implementation to keep agencies accountable to migrant women.
