Project Summary

The present action aims to contribute to addressing the healthcare needs of vulnerable conflict-affected population residing in Eastern Ukraine. Core activities are the provision of essential medical equipment to 20 medical facilities targeting 6,000 individuals, targeted cash assistance for transportation to medical facilities for 1,000 people in vulnerable situations (elderly, people with disabilities, low income households and large families with three and more children) and provision of in-kind support to five exit-entry checkpoints located along the Line of Contact (LoC).

1 - Needs

• The ongoing conflict in the eastern Ukraine has become a big obstacle for the people to access basic and emergency health care services which increasing the number of morbidity and mortality in Ukraine significantly. Non-communicable disease is top of the list causing mortality in Ukraine, which is making 86% of total death. Among them cardiovascular disease is the topmost. In the conflict effected area of Ukraine more than 50% of aged 50 is suffering from hypertension and almost 50,000 of the population is suffering from diabetes in NGCA. Both uncontrolled hypertension and diabetes are causing an increased number of heart damage as target organ. In line with the latest IOM’s NMS report, June 2019, women represented 58% of surveyed household members among which 53% were aged over 60 years old. The surveyed returnee population was significantly older than the IDP population: 59.4 years compared to 37.5 years, based on combined data; • Main population close to contact line are elderly and pensioners. Person with disability and elderly are most vulnerable population within 5 to 15 km of contact line as most of the health care facilities in defined area are compromised to provide specialized health care facility like cardiac care. But this group of the population is suffering from non-communicable disease like cardiac diseases more and not able to go far places where they can get the proper cardiac care facility. So, it is necessary to increase the screening and emergency cardiac care capacity of the existing health care facility close to the contact line; • According to the IOM’s latest NMS report, June 2019, one the most frequently mentioned purpose if visit to the GCA for returnees and other NGCA residence was buying medicines (15%). High need in medicines and essential medical equipment was stressed by the workers of the supported with rehabilitation works and non-food items (NFIs) medical facilities during the focus group discussion conducted with post distribution monitoring; • In line with Right to Protection Report 'Crossing the Line of Contact', January – March 2019, access to the health care at the five Exit-Entry Check Points (EECPs) is also very limited, which is disturbing as the majority of people crossing the ‘contact line’ are older people. Only during the first four months of 2019, 25 persons died at the EECPs due to health complications. Medical assistance at the checkpoints is provided by humanitarian actors and is available during limited hours. According to the Heath Cluster Advocacy Paper on ‘Access to Health Care’, waiting time for the arrival of a public ambulance is up to 30 minutes. Within the current project, IOM will coordinate with the Ministry of Health, the hospitals located close to the contact line and concerned Government unit providing emergency medical responses to support that emergency responders including ambulances are well equipped and readily available at the checkpoints during all hours of operation. That will be achieved through the provision of in-kind support for the ambulances and emergency responders of necessary medication and equipment.

2 - Activities & outcomes

Outcome 1: IDPs, returnees and members of host communities have improved access to healthcare through provision of medical equipment and cash assistance for transport to medical facilities. Output 1.1. Medical facilities in the targeted communities have improved capacity and sustainability to address the healthcare needs of the population. Activities: Select medical facilities in coordination with relevant de facto authorities in Lugansk and Donetsk regions (NGCA); Carry out procurement and distribution of the essential medical equipment among the medical facilities located in NGCA; Conduct post-distribution monitoring (PDM) of distribution of essential medical equipment to assess the effectiveness and efficiency of the assistance provided. To ensure that medical services meet the priority needs of the vulnerable groups (particularly, elderly), health facilities will be chosen to enhance capacity for cardiac disease screening and emergency cardiac care by providing Electrocardiogram, Holter Monitor, Patient vital sign monitor, Infusion Pump, Portable Oxygen concentrator and nebulizers. The list of equipment will be requested from the medical institutions and will be checked by relevant IOM units to ensure its relevance and availability. Output 1.2. Vulnerable households residing in Donetsk and Luhansk regions (GCA) benefited from conditional cash assistance to cover their transport costs to specialized health facilities. Activities: Identify beneficiary HHs in need of cash assistance to access specialized medical institutions defined in coordination with local counterparts and relevant humanitarian actors; Disburse targeted cash assistance to selected HHs based on the visit performed to specialized medical institutions; Operate dedicated hotline for the registration of beneficiaries, consultation and feedback; Conduct PDM of the cash assistance to assess the effectiveness of the assistance provided. Output 1.3. IDPs and conflict affected people crossing the contact line and residing close to the exit-entry checkpoints (EECPs) are able to meet their urgent healthcare needs. IOM will address existing need in medical equipment, mobility aids for people with disabilities, first aid medication and kits for healthcare assistance to people crossing 5 EECPs in Donbas. Activities: Based on consultations with representatives of SBGS of Ukraine and EECPs, IOM will identify the list of the medical equipment and medicines needed for medical kits. The list of equipment and medicines will be selected taking into consideration the diseases that are typical for older people, such as hernias or diabetes; Select medical facilities located close to the EECPs and identify the list of the necessary equipment required for the provision of the first aid and other health care services to the people crossing the contact line; Proceed with procurement of the approved lists of equipment and medicines following the distribution process; Hold trainings for EECPs personnel; Conduct post-distribution monitoring (PDM) to assess the efficiency and effectiveness of the assistance provided. Each activity will be closely monitored to ensure timely and efficient assistance. Implementation challenges and feedback will be analyzed and corrective measures will be taken, wherever necessary. IOM has its own Transparency Hotline and Emergency and Stabilization Informational Hotline that are widely used as feedback mechanism. PDM and focus group discussions provide beneficiaries the platform to share opinions on the assistance received, including quality and quantity, relevance and usefulness to allow IOM to take urgent actions and inform future programming. Lastly, efficiency and cost-effectiveness will be ensured through IOM’s procurement and finance units, which guarantee transparency and the best value for money for the purchase of goods and services carried out by IOM or the IP.
