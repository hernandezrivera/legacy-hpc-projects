Project Summary

Food is the first basic need that a human being needs for survival and lack of it leads to loss of life. According to South Sudan IPC Analysis in November 2024, Aweil East and Aweil North Counties County have been classified as IPC 4 (emergency) and is projected to remain in the same IPC level 4 till July 2025. The acute malnutrition phase classification for the same period also projects that the area will be at critical level with this projected to remain the same till July 2025 indicative of the dire food insecurity and malnutrition levels in the two counties. Some of the factors that have led to loss of household livelihoods contributing to high levels of food insecurity include flooding, household poverty, inflation, poor agricultural practices, lack of agricultural and fishing inputs among others. The counties also play host to IDPs and returnees that also strain the household resources as community members share the little resources with their displaced or settling neighbors. On the other hand, the counties experience a good climatic condition that support agricultural activities and also have rivers and swamps that support fishing activities among the communities living next to them. In order to mitigate the food insecurity and malnutrition situation in the two counties of Aweil East and Aweil North, ACD is proposing to implement an emergency FSL project that will provide agricultural inputs to 6,000 households, train them on good agricultural practices and support them in both vegetable and crop production in order for them to be able to produce enough food for their household consumption and for sale thus improving their livelihood and those of the community members. ACD will additionally target 3,000 fisherforks with fishing activities that will include providing them with fishing kits and training them on various fishing techniques thus enabling them to harvest fish for both household use and for sale and subsequently improving their household nutrition and well being and equally contributing to the reduction of food insecurity in the area.

1. Needs

As per the IPC analysis of October 2023, approximately 7.1 million people out of the country’s total population of 12.4 million were expected to face acute food insecurity at crisis levels or worse during the peak of the lean season from April to July 2024 while as per the IPC analysis of November 2024, 7.69 million people (57 percent of the South Sudan Population) will face acute food insecurity at crisis or worse during the same period in 2025. This represents an increase of over 8 percent of people in need in the year 2025 as compared to the year 2024. This will include 2.53 million people likely to be in Phase 4, and 63,000 people likely to be in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). The nutrition situation has also deteriorated, with an estimated 2.1 million children aged 6-59 months suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025, out of which 650,000 are cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). About 1.11 million pregnant or breastfeeding women (PBW) are also suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition in the same period. An estimated 67 percent of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap. Key drivers to such high levels of food insecurity include frequent climate-related shocks (severe flooding and dry spells), the macro-economic crisis characterized by worsening currency depreciation leading to declined household purchasing power, conflict and insecurity that results into displacements and erosion of household livelihoods, and low agricultural production resulting from lack of proper farm inputs, poor agricultural practices and changing weather patterns. At the peak of the lean season, 44 counties and an estimated 252,000 returnees are likely to face IPC Phase 4 with Aweil East and Aweil North that this project targets also included. The two counties are among the counties that are expected to face IPC Phase 4 from November 2024 to July 2025 including during harvest and postharvest periods indicative of the severe food insecurity needs among the people living in these counties. ACD will therefore initiate livelihood activities in these counties with an aim of improving the food security situation among the people living in these counties thus resulting into improved living standards.

2. Project activities

1. Provision of Inputs through Direct Distribution of Emergency Livelihood Kits (Crop, Vegetable and Fishing).ACD will target eight Payams in Aweil East and four Payams in Aweil North with the distributions of the livelihood kits. In each payam ACD will target 500 households with a total of 6,000 households targeted. All the households targeted will be provided with both crop kits and vegetable kits while fishing kits will be provided other households who practice fishing in the targeted payams with 2,000 fisherfolks to be targeted in Aweil East and 1,000 in Aweil North. 2. Provision of Basic Training and Activities on Good Agriculture Practices. In order to teach the targeted beneficiaries on good agricultural practices on crop production, ACD will implement a two-level approach using demo plots as learning centers and having lead farmers and extension workers supporting the targeted beneficiaries both at the demo plots and at their individual farms. The project will train both the county agriculture extension workers and lead farmers that will be used to transfer good agricultural practices to the farmers since the county extension workers are not adequate to cover all the villages that will be targeted. ACD will engage three people from each targeted village as lead farmers. ACD will also set up demo kitchen gardens at nutrition centers where mothers with children admitted at the nutrition centers will be taught on how to set kitchen gardens and good agricultural activities. Different types of vegetables will be grown at the demo kitchen gardens as example to show how they are properly grown. On a daily basis, there will be either an extension worker or lead farmer at the set up demo kitchen gardens to ensure that the beneficiaries are adequately supported at these demo plots. ACD will set up demo kitchen gardens in two nutrition centers in each payam thus a total of 60 demo kitchen gardens will be set up.3. Initiate dry season vegetable production.ACD will support targeted beneficiaries to set up vegetable gardens along the rivers and near water sources during the dry season. The beneficiaries will be provided with vegetable seeds, watering cans and hoes to support them in vegetable production. The targeted beneficiaries will also be trained on various vegetable production skills, post-harvest handling of vegetables and vegetable utilization and nutrition. The project will target 6,000 households that will be provided with the crop and vegetable kits as stated in activity one. 4. Training of fisherfolks ACD will train the 3,000 fisherfolks that will be provided with the fishing kits. The training will aim at reducing losses in fishing, post-catching handling and conservative fishing practices. The fishefolk will be formed into groups consisting of between 50 members for the purposes of training. The project will also provide the groups with materials such as plastic sheets, empty sacks and salt that will enhance the groups fishing activities. The fisherfolk groups will be trained on making and mending nets, protecting young fish in the river, adopting hygiene and sanitation measures in the handling and storage of fish and preservation techniques such as salting, drying and smoking.
