Project Summary

In 2020, UNRWA will continue to provide essential WASH services to Palestine refugees, with speciﬁc attention to areas and camps affected by the conﬂict. The crisis in Syria has resulted in extensive damage to water supply systems and sewer infrastructure, resulting in restricted access to potable water and affecting domestic and personal hygiene, which in turn has increased the risk of disease outbreaks.
UNRWA will continue its effort to repair, rehabilitate and maintain water supply systems and to ensure that Palestine refugees, particularly in newly accessible camps, have access to potable water. The Agency will also repair, rehabilitate and maintain infrastructure systems including sewerage and surface drainage systems in addition to collecting and managing solid waste rubble removal, and controlling rodents and insects in accessible camps.
Furthermore, hygiene kits will be distributed to Palestine refugees affected by emergencies as well as Palestine refugees spontaneously returning to newly accessible areas and those in hard to reach areas and areas facing movement restrictions, to help safeguard their personal and domestic hygiene.
In the course of 2019, UNRWA was able to extend services to newly accessible areas, requiring extensive work on WASH infrastructure. This trend is expected to increase in 2020, with resumption of access to key areas like Dera'a camp and potentially to Yarmouk camp.

4 - Needs

Of the 438,000 Palestine refugees currently estimated to be in Syria, two-thirds have been displaced at least once since the beginning of the conflict. End of 2019, an estimated 40 per cent continue to be displaced within Syria, with Yarmouk, Dera’a and Ein El Tal camps largely destroyed. The conflict damaged many Agency facilities and resulted in extensive damage to water networks and sewerage infrastructure, resulting in restricted access to potable water and affecting domestic and personal hygiene, which in turn has increased the risk of disease outbreaks. Despite the challenging humanitarian situation in Syria it is expected returns in 2020 will increase. Many of those who have returned are already suffering a secondary displacement. Returnees to newly accessible areas, those facing access constraints in addition to those affected by emergencies often struggle to safely and easily access sanitation and hygiene facilities endangering their health and survival (particularly pregnant women and infants) while increasing risk of violence, abuse and harassment for vulnerable groups, including females, and children. This necessitates furnishing WASH services to beneficiaries, especially for females (adolescents, young adults, and with disability). A socio-economic assessment conducted by UNRWA in December 2017 estimated that 91 per cent of Palestine refugee households in Syria live in poverty. Rising inflation, increasing prices of basic commodities, destruction and damage of houses, depreciation of the local currency and limited livelihood opportunities continue to undermine resilience and coping capacities.

5 - Implementation of activities

In order to address the WASH needs of most vulnerable Palestine refugees, UNRWA plans to: 1. Support the access of Palestine refugees to WASH services (e.g. emergency repair of sewage networks; collection and management of solid waste; and provision of safe drinking water) in areas of high concentration of Palestine Refugees (e.g. camps and gatherings); 2. Resumption of WASH services, including emergency repair (reconstruction/rehabilitation) of water and sewer networks, in newly accessibly camps and camps severely affected by conﬂict, particularly Dera’a camp 3. Provide hygiene kits to all Palestine refugees affected by sudden emergencies, as well as Palestine refugees spontaneously returning to newly accessible areas and those facing access constraints. UNRWA provides WASH items to Palestine refugees in Syria regardless of their residence, therefore both to those living inside and outside the Palestine refugee camps. UNRWA supports access to WASH services (emergency repair of sewage networks; collection and management of solid waste; and provision of safe drinking water; rehabilitation and reconstruction of water and sewer networks) in Palestine refugee camps in Syria. The Palestine refugee camps in Syria are not included in the OCHA locations list, therefore the projects identifies and uses the sub-district level for the locations.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

- Protection Risk: Beneﬁciaries face security/safety risks when travelling to and from WASH services provided by UNRWA. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High.Mitigating measures:- UNRWA prioritizes maintaining and restoring WASH services in camps, including those newly accessible, in order to minimize unnecessary movements of beneﬁciaries.- UNRWA prioritizes awareness raising and training to staff, community members, including children, on reducing exposure to risks related to ERW.- UNRWA conducts protection audits on a regular basis to get feedback from the staff and the community on how to improve its services and how to optimize its compliance to the main protection principles. Protection Risk: Risk of theft of WASH rehabilitation/construction materials such as generators and construction materials (during delivery/storage/ after installation) by groups and used in conﬂict activities. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High.Mitigation measure: UNRWA has safe locations inside all camps where materials and equipment can be stored securely. Protection Risk:- Health risk associated with garbage removal, construction works, exposure to chemical materials, gases, and raw sewage. Likelihood:High. Impact:Medium.Mitigation measure: Staff are trained on dealing with such situations and provided with proper gear to conduct their work safely. Protection Risk: Timing and process of distribution (hygiene kits) put women and girls at higher risk of GBV. Likelihood: High Impact: HighMitigation measures: UNRWA selects distribution points that are safe and secure for staff and beneﬁciaries. DSS staff are present to ensure the process is going smoothly. sites are selected to be spacious and the distribution is done over several days to avoid crowdedness/frustrations. Separate queues for men and women are created. Female staff are present on site to address female beneﬁciaries’ needs. Staff also take into consideration the needs of elderly beneﬁciaries, and persons with disabilities.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

A mix of humanitarian and post-conflict interventions: Whilst Damascus, Rural Damascus and most of the major urban concentrations in Homs, Hama, Latakia, and Dera’a experienced relative calm throughout 2019, hostilities continued in other areas of the country and the overall situation remained tense and unstable. In 2020, the Agency anticipates that a mix of humanitarian and development approaches that are supportive of post-conflict rehabilitation will be needed to respond to the different needs of Palestine refugees across Syria. High levels of humanitarian needs especially amongst the most vulnerable Palestine refugees: A socio-economic assessment conducted by UNRWA in December 2017 estimated that 91 per cent of Palestine refugee households in Syria live in poverty. Depreciation of the Syrian pound, rising inflation, increasing prices of basic commodities, destruction and damage of houses, and limited livelihood opportunities continue to undermine resilience and coping capacities, particularly for those most vulnerable.Increase in returns; a large proportion of Palestine refugees in Syria will remain displaced: the levels of displacement are foreseen to remain high among Palestine refugees, as access to Yarmouk is still restricted and the level of destruction there is significant. Returns to Dera’a and Ein El Tal camps, which have sustained major destruction, remain slow, due to the level of damage to basic infrastructure and the lack of public services. Despite the challenging humanitarian situation in Syria it is expected returns from out of Syria in 2020 will increase, as will HLP and CD needs. Secondary displacements is also anticipated among Palestine refugees due to their legal status. Palestine refugees in Syria will continue to face serious protection challenges: It is anticipated that protection risks will persist and may intensify in newly accessible areas where spontaneous returns, both from within and outside of Syria, are expected to increase.
