Project Summary

Ethiopia is experiencing multiple concurrent shocks, including a prolonged drought and a protracted conflict. The drought, which resulted from three consecutive failed rainy seasons, is already affecting 6.8 million people and could affect more if the March/April 2022 rainy season is also below normal levels. The conflict has been protracting for more than a year, affecting in particular the populations in Northern Ethiopia. Clashes are still ongoing in Tigray, Amhara and Afar, with airstrikes and drone strikes being regularly reported. Humanitarian access remains very limited, with the entirety of Tigray being deemed either “partially accessible” or “hard to reach”. Furthermore, an uptick in clashes in Benishangul-Gumuz and Oromia has led to similarly large swathes of communities that have become hard-to-reach for the humanitarian community.
In this challenging context with overlapping shocks, large pockets of the population are living in or have recently displaced from areas with worsening food insecurity and malnutrition, as well as a lack of water, healthcare, shelter and livestock assistance. The lack of comprehensive and regular information in these dynamic contexts has been a challenge for humanitarian planning and has limited the understanding of needs of these areas – particularly for non-displaced communities who remain in conflict or natural hazard-affected areas, which hinders the efficient provision of humanitarian aid.
REACH Initiative proposes to conduct regular Humanitarian Situation Monitoring (HSM) across conflict- and disaster-affected areas, expanding its current coverage to include all regions of Ethiopia (except for Addis Ababa and potentially South West Region), through direct and partner-led data collection.
A round of the HSM will be conducted every quarter and will be aligned to key humanitarian milestones such as the preparation of the HNO/HRP 2023. The assessment will focus on communities as a whole, aiming to monitor Clusters’ HRP monitoring indicators wherever relevant and community-level multi-sectoral humanitarian needs. As such, this information source is intended to complement existing data sources within the response focused primarily on displaced populations and to also provide a basis for triangulation for partner reports, Cluster assessments, and other information sources across the country.
The HSM will be conducted in close coordination with OCHA and response-wide assessment actors, and will involve national and international partners to make sure the coverage is extended to the entire country. REACH will provide overall coordination and technical support to the activity as well as training to partners and enumerators to make sure the quality of the collected data remains high across all assessed areas.

1. - Projects activities

The Humanitarian Situation Monitoring is a research approach designed to provide multi-sectoral information on community-level humanitarian needs. The approach relies on multiple key informants reporting on conditions in their kebele (or their kebele of origin), which are then analyzed through a consensus-based approach. This allows for information provision across large areas, centred upon what residents are reporting on their own communities.With the context anticipated to remain dynamic across Ethiopia in the next 12 months, the HSM will take on a hybrid approach to accommodate in-person surveys, or surveys with “populations on the move” in areas that remain hard-to-reach. For these hard-to-reach areas, the HSM uses the “area of knowledge” methodology to capture information through new arrivals in key towns or sites to ask questions on the settlement they have recently left. The questionnaires between in-person and hard-to-reach surveys will be harmonized each round to allow for joint analysis regardless of the accessibility of each assessed kebele.This method has been used to capture information in Tigray since mid-2021 and is used in 11 other countries globally. In Ethiopia, REACH now proposes to expand its current HSM coverage to the whole country (except for Addis Ababa) through a phased expansion approach in order to first expand to drought-affected areas prior to expanding towards nationwide coverage. REACH would lead data collection exercises in a third of the regions and covering the remaining areas through national and international humanitarian response partners – thus coordinating a nationwide, multi-sectoral and multi-partner assessment.Under the umbrella of humanitarian response coordination bodies, REACH will be the technical focal point to work with all Clusters prior to each round of data collection to ensure appropriateness of each questionnaire (factoring seasonality of other emerging information gaps) and will support joint decision-making if geographic areas emerge as higher priorities. As much as possible, REACH will seek to harmonize HSM data collection with existing emergency response mechanisms – e.g., SWAN consortium or ERM – in order to leverage existing relationships with local response partners in each region and also to ensure data provision would inform such actors in a practical manner. Working through in-house and Cluster-suggested partners in each region, REACH will lead the training of enumerator teams and will provide overall coordination to this activity to ensure the quality of the collected data is high and constant across regions. As soon as each round of data collection is complete, the cleaned dataset and the aggregated analysis tables will be shared with direct activity partners including those participating to contributing Cluster partners, assessment sub-working groups, and direct data collection partners for joint analysis processes.This nationwide HSM aims to address the critical information gap on communities continuing to live in conflict or crisis-affected areas. These communities often include some of the most vulnerable subsets of the population, including elderly, women and children, and those with limited mobility due to physical, financial, or social factors. The assessment will be conducted at community level and will aim to monitor community dynamics relevant to cluster HRP monitoring indicators.The HSM will be conducted at quarterly intervals. Following a set-up stage and the initial coordination of interested partners, the first round will be implemented during the summer of 2022 and may be a phased approach depending on partner coverage and availability. Overall, the HSM will aim at providing crucial data to inform key milestones of the Humanitarian Programme Cycle (HPC), including HNO/HRP 2023 and the annual PiN calculation. Subsequent rounds will be aligned to other targets, such as the finalisation of the HRP at the beginning of 2023 and the mid-year review.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

While working primarily through the humanitarian coordination system, REACH will involve national NGOs in the project from its early onset. Research design and indicator prioritization will be conducted jointly with Cluster technical partners inclusive of national and local organizations. Key national NGOs envisioned during this phase include Mothers and Children Multisectoral Development Organization (MCMDO), Rehabilitation and Development Organization (RaDO), Action for the Needy Ethiopia, and other similar NGOs with nation-wide expertise. Especially if particular areas are of highest concern leading into any given round of the HSM, additional feedback will be solicited from regional humanitarian organizations, such as Organisation for Rehabilitation and Development in Amhara (ORDA), Relief Society of Tigray (REST), Gambela Children and Community Development Organisation (GCCDO), etc. The participation of national NGOs as direct data collection partners will be encouraged (pending availability of resources to support their engagement). Nonetheless, they would be invited to sub-national trainings and importantly, sub-national joint analyses processes. REACH will provide all participating partners with technical guidance and dedicated trainings to ensure the same technical standard is applied to data collection across the whole country. Through this process, REACH will strengthen the capacity of the involved national actors and will further support national partners in their meaningful participation in the implementation and monitoring of the humanitarian response.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

The HSM will be conducted at community-level, and not at household level – thus there won’t be as detailed disaggregation as REACH would normally include during other assessment approaches. However, many indicators and questions can be formulated in a way that attempts to discern any different trends for potentially marginalized populations within each community, including gender-sensitive, age-sensitive, and ability-sensitive questions. Furthermore, respondent demographics will be disaggregated each round with a view towards more equitable and inclusive respondent profiles. Finally, the assessment will give a unique insight into the needs of non-displaced communities, which often host a high number of elderly, women and other members of the communities that don’t have the means to migrate; and will start bridging the current critical information gap about needs and dynamics inside non-displaced communities. REACH’s data collection is undertaken within the framework of a do-no-harm approach, and in accordance with REACH policy on the collection, storage and dissemination of PII. Sensitive conflict-related information, or information that could potentially pose a protection risk to the respondents will not be collected. Also, any information that could potentially violate respondent anonymity is removed from the dataset before it is disseminated. Respondent contact information is not shared with any other actors unless there is a specific, pre-planned purpose and the respondent household has given informed consent – and even in such cases, only the absolute minimum data is shared. Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) principles will be integrated within the activity approach, both in terms of survey best practices (i.e. informed consent, sharing of information back to communities, inclusion of a grievance / feedback mechanism), as well as questionnaire modules (i.e. asking communities their perspectives on equitable and fair access to assistance, access to information, preferred modalities of assistance, and their experiences interacting with humanitarian actors).
