5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The full impact of the unprecedent rainy season- that led to the worst flooding Sudan faced in more than three decades is yet to be felt. The already fragile food security in the country is likely to be compromised due to this year floods, following the destruction of thousands of hectares of crops just before the harvest. By the end of the year, WFP is planning to conduct emergency food security assessments (EFSA) in flood-affected areas of Kassala, Khartoum, North Darfur, North Kordofan, Red Sea state, Sennar and West Darfur. Similarly to 2020, WFP plans to partially buy food from local markets to provide relief assistance, preposition food in strategic/most potential affected states before the rainy season starts in May 2021 and will continue, as of now to prioritize refugees in its assistance, while mainstreaming environment and climate change as a cross cutting issue through its different interventions (i.e. FFA and IKI), strengthen livelihoods and build resilience over time, emergency preparedness responding to natural multi-hazards such as floods/droughts.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

WFP strives to ensure that beneficiaries’ safety, dignity and integrity are upheld, and that they are not exposed to any harm or protection risks through WFP programmes. WFP also seeks to enhance the integration of protection by identifying and building on opportunities to contribute to protection outcomes where protection risks to beneficiaries may be reduced by WFP assistance. All efforts will be made to ensure that targeted beneficiaries have meaningful access to assistance provided, based onneed and without prejudice. Beneficiary identification is informed by vulnerability assessments that categorize households into 4 main categories varying from those that are severely food insecure to those that are not food insecure. Food assistance is geared towards the most and moderately food insecure. Empowerment is done through engagement of local communities in food management committees, mothers support groups, project management committees and community nutrition volunteers. Efforts are also underway to build beneficiaries’ capacities and assets through assetcreation programmes in FFA programmes. Accountability both to beneficiaries and donors underpins all of WFP’s work. A plan for establishment of standardized CFM in accordance with WFP minimum requirements for functional CFM was endorsed in light of the AAP mapping. The plan is composed of a national call center, community Help Desks and activity specific grievance mechanisms. Thenational call center is planned to go live January 2021.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Accountability is one of core values that helps WFP to provide the best possible service to the people it assists. AAP is defined by WFP as an active commitment to give account to, take account of, and held to account by the people it assists. During the preliminary stages, WFP provides accurate, timely and accessible information to affected people about its assistance. The information provided must be clearlyunderstandable by everyone, irrespective of their age, gender or other characteristics. Upholding the ‘centrality of protection’, the design and planning phase also considers food and nutrition assistance to be provided in a safe and dignified manner to the targeted population. During implementation, regular meetings and beneficiary reference sessions will be held were held with community leaders and representatives of different community groups to ensure that they understand the project objectives and intended outcomes, and to solicit their feedback about any necessary changes or adaptations needed to make the programme a success. During implementation, programme adjustments if needed can be done in consultation with programme beneficiaries) In all monitoring and evaluation exercises, WFP monitors the proportion of assisted people informed about the programme, as well as the proportion of activities for which beneficiary feedback is documented, analyzed and integrated intoprogramme improvement. This also applied for this project, with all complaints and/or feedback received through the Complaints and Feedback Mechanisms (CFMs) being addressed with relevant programme units, and appropriate beneficiaries beingnformed about the action taken or resolution.
