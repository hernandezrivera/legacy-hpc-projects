Project Summary

The nutrition situation in NWSW regions still remains unclear due to inadequate of a comprehensive nutrition anthropometric survey using SMART methodology. Proxy data from rapid emergency food security assessments (EFSA) conducted in October 2019 revealed a precarious situation with a proxy GAM of 5.1% and 7.1% in North West (NW) and South West (SW) regions respectively. Analysis from Mid Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) screening data conducted by nutrition partners between January and September 2020, showed a proxy GAM prevalence of 4.8% and 1.5% in NW and SW regions respectively. Furthermore, poor indicators on Infant and young child feeding practices (IYCF) were reported with 42.5% and 58.3% of exclusive breast-feeding rates for NW and SW region respectively. Proportions of children 6 to 23 months who met Minimum Dietary Diversity (MDD) was reported to be low at 31.8% and 20.7% NW & SW respectively. Access to health care still remains a challenge due to insecurity and persistent attacks on healthcare as this constituted a huge threat to the availability of essential health care to the populations. With the major drivers of malnutrition (food insecurity, morbidity, poor sanitary conditions and repeated internal displacement) likely to remain in the same trend in 2021, the nutrition situation is likely to deteriorate since acute malnutrition in children aged 6-59 months is a direct outcome indicator of recent changes in the nutritional status which could be attributed to changes in food security, health and WASH situations. SHUMAS intervention will be designed to improve the nutritional status of children under 5 years, adolescent girls and boys 5-9 years, pregnant and lactating women affected by the crisis in 6 health districts in NW and SW regions through micronutrient supplementation, BSFP, Iron and Folic acid, home-made fortified food, IYCF sensitization, screening for malnutrition and referrals.
The main objective is contribute towards the reduction of Morbidity and Mortality linked to life-threatening forms of malnutrition of boys and girls under 5, adolescent’s girls and pregnant and lactating women in 6 health districts in the North West (Donga Mantung, Bui, Momo, Mezam Ngo-Ketunjia and Boyo Health Districts Regions of Cameroon for 12 months.
And the specific objectives are;
• To prevent stunting, wasting, micronutrient deficiencies in 1123 Boys and 1169 girls aged under five years in 6 health districts by providing them with BSFP, Vitamin supplementation, deworming and medication in the NW for 12 months.
• To protect 13,447boys and 14, 731 girls 5-9 years from under nutrition, micronutrient deficiency and Anemia in 6 health districts through provision of locally made nutritious food, Vitamin Supplementation, folic acid and deworming medications.
• To protect 1985 Pregnant women and breastfeeding mothers from under nutrition, micronutrient deficiencies and anemia in 6 health districts Donga Mantung, Bui, Momo, Mezam Ngo-Ketunjia and Boyo Health Districts through provision of BSFP, Iron and Folic acid , Micronutrient supplementation for 12months.
• To prevent acute malnutrition in 4000 Boys and 6000 girls aged under five years (including severe acute malnutrition) in 6 health districts Kumbo Donga Mantung, Bui, Momo, Mezam, Ngo-Ketunjia and Boyo Health Districts through screening, referrals of SAM, provision of plumpy nuts and treatment for 12 months.
• To recruit 60 CHW for sensitization of infant and young child feeding practices in emergencies. The beneficiaries will be 2292 children 6-23months, 28,178 children 6-59 months/ 10-19 years, 4112 pregnant women, 1985 PLW and19, 884 men and women.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Food security reports throughout 2020 and 2021, indicate deterioration of food insecurity, disruptions of household livelihoods and increase in prices of staple food. The insecurity levels, temporary closure of point of entries and limited food supply through imported sources are main drivers for price increases in the North West and region that share territorial boundaries with Nigeria. COVID-19 has been identified as a major cause of higher prices nationally in the second quarter. Access to health care still remains a challenge due to insecurity and persistent attacks on healthcare as this constituted a huge threat to the availability of essential health care to the populations. With the major drivers of malnutrition likely to remain in the same trend in 2022, the nutrition situation is likely to deteriorate since acute malnutrition in children aged 6-59 months is a direct outcome indicator of recent changes in the nutritional status which could be attributed to changes in food security, health and WASH situations.
