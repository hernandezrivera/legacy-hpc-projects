Project Summary

This project is targeting conflict and flood-affected host communities, returnees, and IDPs living in Nyirol, Pigi, and Pochalla counties who are at high risk of gender-based violence, death, injury, and life-threatening diseases like HIV/Aids, STIs, and reproductive health-related illnesses aggravated due to violence. The primary purpose of the project is to support vulnerable people to recover from crisis, seek solutions to displacement, and build resilience to acute shocks and chronic stresses brought about by the conflict. The target population is 7,400 individuals broken down as 650 boys and 2,400 girls, 3,700 women 650 men. The project will be implemented in a period of 12 months and is costed at a budget of $ 256,000. Narrative Hub will implement this project directly in the three target locations in collaboration with the target population, the local administration, line ministries, and partners on the ground to achieve maximum project results. Narrative Hub will build upon existing efforts and target vulnerable populations while coordinating closely with the GBV Sub-Cluster and other partners implementing this project and ensure that the standard IASC guidelines for prevention and response services are adhered to. The project will focus on rebuilding broken community protection mechanisms that were compromised by the protracted conflict and caused grave human rights violations of men, women, boys, and girls. Narrative Hub proposes to contribute to the HNO 2021 and sub-cluster cluster objectives 1.2: Ensure the provision of critical protection-related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls, and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas and 2.2: Prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience. The project activities are designed to prevent and respond to GBV, providing support to vulnerable people particularly women and girls. Some of the activities proposed for this project are individual/group PSS; awareness-raising and community outreach (dialogue); capacity building for service providers; risk mitigation (mainstreaming/safety audits/protective accompaniment/fuel energy stoves/lamps, working with other clusters in joint missions and providing a multi-sectoral approach to the intervention; providing women and girls access to life skills training/IGA; establishing or supporting and strengthening community-based structures (CWG/Women groups); and strengthening referral mechanisms, It is expected that by the end of the project period, lives of vulnerable women and girls will be saved, they will have increased access to protection services and equipped with positive life skills and positive coping mechanisms. All activities will be implemented in a way that prevents the spread and transmission of COVID 19.

1 - Needs

The 2021 Humanitarian Overview Report notes that armed conflict, sub-national organized violence, COVID-19 restrictions, and flooding have exacerbated the already extreme levels of GBV. Over 6,000 GBV incidents were recorded between January and September 2020. Nearly all, or 97 percent of the reported GBV incident survivors, were female. A fifth of survivors was less than 18 years old. Intimate partners accounted for more than half of the alleged perpetrators. Displaced women and girls continue to bear the brunt of GBV. According to UNMISS reports, brutal rapes including gang-rapes, have been among the violations committed by armed forces and groups during cattle raids in Jonglei State. According to the IPC report, Canal/Pigi and Nyirol of Jonglei State are classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, while Pochalla is classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3). The most severe acute food insecurity conditions are in counties where chronic vulnerabilities have been exacerbated by a combination of shocks, including floods, insecurity incidents, the macro-economic crisis, and the effects of COVID-19 measures. This means that women and girls will be more vulnerable to GBV in the coming months. REACH Port and Road Monitoring (PRM) in Jonglei tracks the movement of South Sudanese households traveling to and returning from Ethiopia. REACH September 2020 report notes that the number of recorded inbound individuals increased between March and September 2020 in Pochalla, Pigi, and Nyirol that needs protection. The presence of refugee returnees was reported in 51% of assessed settlements across Jonglei in September 2020, up from 34% in March. Reporting remained particularly high in Pigi (100% of assessed settlements), Nyirol (100%), and Pochalla (71%) counties. According to the REACH report of September 2020 in Jonglei, in 37% of assessed settlements, insecurity (which includes killing, rape, fighting, and looting), was reported as the main push factor for IDPs. This is likely a consequence of a continued intensification of sub-national violence. The pressure that the inbound population movement has seemingly placed on access to food appears to have been compounded by the reported inability of many households to collect wild food, hunt, cut poles, and collect firewood due to insecurity in rural areas. Women and girls reportedly faced protection concerns in 49% and 39% of assessed settlements, respectively, in September 2020. Early marriage was reported as the main protection concern for girls in 18% of assessed settlements in September, possibly due to recent livelihood collapse in these areas, which might have in turn increased the need to access livestock through dowry. Findings suggest that women continued to face protection concerns both inside and outside the home. In 21% of assessed settlements across Jonglei State, domestic violence was reported as the main protection concern for women in September 2020. Existing inequalities, such as the unequal and gendered distribution of chores and responsibilities, have been compounded by insecurity throughout the reporting period. Typically, women across Jonglei are responsible for collecting water and wild foods, which many food-insecure households rely upon, especially during the lean season, to fill increasingly large food consumption gaps. According to FGD participants, wild food collection has put women in rural forest areas at high risk of abduction. There are few opportunities for women and girls to gather together and support one another. The opportunity to generate income has reduced as the economic situation has been more difficult. The creation of friendly spaces for women and girls has been a key strategy for NH in the protection and empowerment of women. However, these opportunities still lack in many locations in Pigi, Nyirol, and Pochalla and therefore present a need to be addressed by Narrative Hub.

2 - Project activities

Activity 1: Conduct comprehensive case management ( individual, group, specialized psychosocial support that includes recreational activities livelihood services, and safe referrals) targeting both adult and child survivors. Narrative Hub will engage competent and qualified caseworkers to provide case management services in line with the IASC guidelines for GBV. Referrals will be channeled through referral pathways in each location. Caseworkers will follow up with survivors and support the M&E officer to establish levels of client satisfaction in regards to access to services in order to fully meet their needs. Activity 2: Data review/analysis and programming. Sexual Violence continuous data review will inform community-level Sexual Violence Prevention Programming and advocacy efforts. Activity 3; Train Frontline workers on GBV minimum standards (case management, PSS, guiding principles, and safe referrals). Activity 4: Provide lifesaving information including information on available services. This activity will be coordinated with other sectoral partners to ensure agencies deliver accurate and harmonized messaging. For example, this will be done through the establishment of referral pathways. Messages will be translated into local languages and disseminated with respect to age and cultural sensitivities of the targeted beneficiaries Activity 5: Conduct assessment for livelihood support and income-generating activities including alternative fuel sources for girls and women such as energy-efficient stoves. Activity 6: Establish and support/strengthen women & girls friendly spaces programming, including strengthening integration with other protection partners. In this activity, Narrative Hub will build on the current work at the existing WGFS to replicate in other proposed locations and ensure continuity and sustainability of the existing WGFS. Apart from life skills, and PSS services, the WGFS will provide a platform for women and girls’ peer engagement and information sharing on critical aspects like trauma, shock, and stress. In coordination with child protection partners. Narrative Hub will design appropriate approaches to deal with GBV survivors. Activity 7: Establish and strengthen community-based structures like CBPN teams, women support groups, and state female paralegal associations. Provide Legal awareness, assistance, and counseling for survivors. Narrative Hub will build on its pro bono legal services and scale up the provision of legal services to GBV survivors. At the same time, police and justice authorities including traditional justice mechanisms will be trained on the South Sudanese legal framework. Activity 8: Conduct Community awareness raising and sensitization on women’s rights: Narrative Hub intends to establish a multi-sector task force to be tasked with community awareness. The task force will deliver life-saving information and messaging on key protection threats affecting women, girls, men, and boys. Activity 9: Train women and girls on life skills/IGA Activity 10: Conduct awareness-raising and community outreaches (dialogue) on available services and use of referral pathways. Activity 11: Provision of dignity kits to women and adolescent girls as part of GBV risk mitigation measures. Activity 12: Train community mobilizers on protection risk mitigation, preparedness, and early warning.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Narrative Hub will ensure that training is conducted to all the staff members both at Juba office and field staff on PSEA before the beginning of the project implementation. During signing of contracts, all staff will sign a PSEA code of conduct. The training will then be extended to beneficiaries during project inception. This training will to beneficiaries will be done by Juba based staff members and not by field teams to avoid biases, victimization or any conflict of interest. The beneficiaries will be trained on communication channels available to them to report PSEA and contact persons.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

A complains and feedback committee will be selected by beneficiaries to engage every beneficiaries (PWDs, individuals who are not literate, women and girls), listen to their stories and give upward feedback. Where necessary, focused group discussions of each constituency (PWDs, individuals who are not literate, women and girls), will be held at project site with them to provide feedback. Where distribution is involved, a PDM (which will involve individual interviews, FGDs and observations) will be conducted to harvest beneficiary feedback.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Narrative Hub has presence in the locations and hence there is already community engagement and trust with beneficiaries as well as the local authorities, who already had given some of the needs to help design this project. Narrative Hub will spend some time with beneficiaries at the inception of the project creating an awareness of the project and its goal including how to report complains and feedback and how they will be addressed. Narrative Hub will then set up an implementation committee composed of the same beneficiaries. Beneficiaries and other agencies on ground will be involved in monitoring to continuously review and and gaps will be identified and discussed jointly and addressed in order to meet the main goal of the project. Key community members including individuals living with disabilities, women and girls will be involved during joint monitoring of the project and from the different constituency groups. A complains and feedback committee will be selected by beneficiaries to engage beneficiaries, listen to their stories and give upward feedback. For those who can write, a complains box will be put around distribution locations and WGFCs upward complains and feedback. Where necessary, focused group discussions will be held at project site with beneficiaries and community leaders to provide feedback. Sampling for FGDs will be representative for all affected groups of people. Where distribution is involved, a PDM will be conducted to harvest beneficiary feedback.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

While Narrative Hub sets up a feedback and complains mechanism at the project location among the beneficiaries, at Juba level, a committee composed of Juba staff and led by the M&E will be in-charge of receiving those feedback directly from the field locations and conducting the feedback analysis. Each feedback will be collated and grouped accordingly into areas that will ensure they are better addressed. Those that are related to programming will be addressed from the perspective of the beneficiaries advise and lenses and they are at the centre of the project. Whether it is changing the meeting or distribution location or time, that will be changed, and beneficiaries will be informed of the changes through downward feedback from Juba office. The M&E staff will travel to the field locations to communicate directly with beneficiaries and also notify the field staff of the changes that has been made.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

This project is conceptualized based on protection risk analysis and will mean-stream protection at every level. NH is mainstreaming protection in all its programming by ensuring that the most vulnerable groups such as women and girls and others such as PWDs, elderly, receive protection when they are providing information at any stage of the project cycle and that the whole process is confidential. NH is a very active national NGO on GBV in Jonglei State and will employ a protection advisor for this project to work closely with established committees to guide the process with a serious protection lens. Information provided by survivors, victims of sexual exploitation and abuse, or beneficiaries will be safeguarded not to be used to expose them to harm and referred to a level that they can seek help. NH with other agencies will develop referral pathways in the targeted locations and ensure that beneficiaries are made aware of the pathways where they can get services or help. NH will ensure that the meeting sites or locations are safe and closer to beneficiaries by conducting safety audits to all meeting sites and distribution points. Barriers to access particularly the meeting and distribution locations will be discussed with all the beneficiaries in separate groups (constituencies) before it will be agreed upon on where everyone is comfortable to access. Monitoring tools NH will use have a section of monitoring the safety of beneficiaries, and other barriers to access as protection is mean-streamed in the tools and also have a section that asks questions as regards the use of the items support and whether the beneficiaries feels comfortable using them. The project activities will address inequalities and protection threats by analyzing power dynamics in the community. Local leaders and other external stakeholders will not be in charge of determining who will benefit but beneficiaries with support from our staff based on our objective assessment of needs. NH staff will sign PSEA before engagement in the project and that will deliberately seek to create awareness to the beneficiaries on SEA and the channels of reporting SEA during implementation. NH will come up with feedback and complaint mechanisms for beneficiaries by use of a complaint and feedback desk during distributions. An implementation committee will also be set up composed of beneficiaries that will also help in feedback. FGDs with different constituencies of beneficiaries will also be used by the project team to involve and include all beneficiaries in the deision-making during consultations and also to get feedback and complaints. Our actions will address both basic needs that improve the protection of those at risk of violations. NH is also a very active member of the Protection Cluster.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

All projects activities will be implemented with a protection lens, ensuring that safety of women and girls, boys and men and taken into consideration through safety audits without compromising on quality and time. NH will through through a multi-sectoral approach and joint missions with other agencies and clusters to negate negative livelihood coping strategies, which expose beneficiaries to additional risks, and providing them with safer ways to meet their basic needs. As a usual practice,Narrative Hub, as a protection partners, will work in conjunction with protection teams on the ground, ensuring meaningful information on protection risks and concerns in a specific areaare properly identified and conflict sensitivities are taken into consideration through a do-no-harm approach.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

Narrative Hub will emphasize on skills training to beneficiaries and the need for durable solutions within the communities we serve. The beneficiaries will be taught basic life skills/IGAs so that they have skills and knowledge to general some income for themselves including farming. Narrative Hub will establish local implementation committees, and equip them with leadership skills which will also act as local development catalysts which the project comes to an end as part of resilience building. Women and girls will continue to uplift each other through WGFS and the other skills such as developing energy efficient cooking stoves. Narrative Hub will give ensure that local communities change their attitudes through community awareness campaigns such that the narrative is changed from 'we cannot do it on our own' to 'we have the capacity to do it'.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

Narrative Hub will strive to bring other clusters and agencies dealing with other interventions/services on joint missions so that the vulnerable population (including children, women, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons) are served with various services at the same time in same location. This will reduce pressure and demand on their side of walking long distances every time to access services by different agencies. They will also be able to save on time so that they can use it attend to other pressing needs in the community.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

While implementing this project, Narrative Hub will develop a clear messaging mechanism to beneficiaries. The messages will also include nutrition to women and girls so that they are able to understand its importance to the health of a child under the age of 5 years. Narrative Hub will also deliberately relay information on nutritional values of vegetables and other foods, infant and young child feeding program during awareness and outreaches, in WGFS, and during distributions.
