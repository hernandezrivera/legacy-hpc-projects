Project Summary

The project aims are providing Emergency and Essential and critical life-saving health care services to IDPs,returnees and vulnerable host communities to reduce morbidity and mortality among vulnerable crisis- affected women , men and children in Kismayo.The project targets the most vulnerable 3016 Children under 5 years, 9511 children under 1 years of age, 8236 WCBA and 1005 Mothers and 3200 men through the following activities: Provision of refresher training for the health staff, Provision of Emergency and essential Health Care Services (PHC), Mobile medical services providing PHC and referral to IDPs and hard to reach vulnerable communities, integration of mental health and Psychological Support services to PHC and community Care services, Emergency Reproductive Health Services, immunization services to the remote villages/hard to reach, community engagement in health education to community members, scale up outbreak early warning response (EWAR) and case management, provision of essential medications and supplies, establishing referral pathways in remote areas/hard to reach areas in Kismayo The project is informed by the fact that Somalia particularly in Kismayo, Lower Juba Region has limited access to preventative and curative health services (in terms of quality, quantity and distribution), resulting in high levels of morbidity and mortality. The core problem can be attributed to two root causes, which are: shortage of essential resources (technical, financial, material and human) and inadequate governance at both country and regional level. These two problems are synergistic and aggravate each other which increase their impact. In addition these causes are further exacerbated by the scattered population, long distances to health facilities, outdated socio-cultural practices which are not conducive with good health, poor environmental conditions, insecurity, high levels of poverty experienced in the region and limited interventions undertaken by humanitarian agencies in the area owing to insecurity and corresponding access challenges. The situation is exacerbated by acute food insecurity and sustained malnutrition rates amongst children aged under 5 years and pregnant lactating women. Furthermore, diseases outbreaks re-occur due to poor health infrastructure and a lack of clean and safe water, limited sanitation facilities and poor hygiene conditions and practices.
