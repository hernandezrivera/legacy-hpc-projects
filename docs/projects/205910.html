Project Summary

The Emergency WASH assistance to IDPs, returnees and host communities in Aweil South and Aweil East Counties of Northern Bahr El Ghazal State in South Sudan project, implemented by the Impact Health Organization (IHO), aims to address the escalating Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) crisis in the Republic of South Sudan, particularly in the counties of Aweil East and Aweil South. The purpose of the intervention is to improve the WASH conditions in these areas, which have been severely affected by conflict, flooding, and displacement.
The project targets a total of 28,889 beneficiaries, comprising 68% (19,645 individuals) from Aweil East (Children Male 5536, Children Female 5968, Adult Male 3026, Adult 3694, Elederly Male 721, Elederly Female 701) and 32% (9,244 individuals) from Aweil South. These beneficiaries include children, adults, and the elderly, with a specific focus on those most vulnerable.
The intervention involves a range of activities, including the provision of standard kits to children suffering from Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and their caretakers, the construction and rehabilitation of latrines and boreholes in nutrition facilities, and the promotion of hygiene practices. The project also aims to improve access to water and sanitation for women and girls, construct and rehabilitate flood-resilient WASH facilities, and design disability-inclusive sanitation facilities.
By addressing these critical needs, the project aims to reduce the prevalence of malnutrition and WASH-related morbidity and mortality, mitigate the risks of gender-based violence, and promote the inclusion of people with disabilities. This will contribute to improving the health, well-being, and livelihoods of the affected communities and setting the foundation for sustainable development in the two counties.

1. Needs

The Republic of South Sudan has been grappling with significant humanitarian challenges since the outbreak of conflict in December 2013. These challenges have escalated considerably, particularly in the counties of Aweil East and Aweil South, which are among the most affected areas, especially in the Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) sector. The situation has been exacerbated by a combination of factors, including flooding, the influx of returnees from Sudan, and internal displacements. According to the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) Acute Food Insecurity and Acute Malnutrition Analysis for April - July 2023, released on 18th August 2023, Aweil East County and Aweil South County were classified as Emergency (IPC Phase 4), indicating a severe crisis. A multi-sector rapid assessment conducted by Impact Health Organization (IHO), Anglican Relief and Development Fund (ARDF), Water and Rural Transformation (WART), and Humanitarian Emergency Logistics & Preparedness (HELP) in Northern Barh Ghazal State revealed a dire WASH situation. The assessment found that 89% of the total population practices open defecation, and over 33% use water from open sources for household use due to a lack of boreholes. Furthermore, the assessment discovered that individuals use less than 10L of water per day and travel 5km to reach the nearest water point. Poor handwashing practices, limited access to soap, Jari canisters, water purification supplies, and high morbidity from diarrhea and vomiting are among the significant challenges related to poor WASH conditions. Additionally, Aweil East and South are experiencing floods due to increased rains, leading to the displacement of masses who settle in other nearby areas in poor sanitation and hygiene conditions, exposing them to diseases. In response to these indicators, IHO plans to undertake WASH interventions to meet the needs of 28,889 beneficiaries in the two locations. These interventions are crucial in addressing the escalating humanitarian crisis in South Sudan, particularly in improving the WASH conditions in the affected areas. The proposed interventions align with the South Sudan Humanitarian Response Plan 2024, which highlights the worsening humanitarian situation in the country and the rising needs driven by years of conflict, sub-national violence, food insecurity, climate crisis, and public health challenges. The project targets a total of 28,889 individuals, with 68% (19,645 individuals) from Aweil East and 32% (9,244 individuals) from Aweil South. The beneficiaries include children, adults, and the elderly, with a specific focus on those most vulnerable. The project aims to provide these individuals with improved access to water, sanitation, and hygiene facilities, and to promote better hygiene practices. This will contribute to reducing the prevalence of malnutrition and WASH-related morbidity and mortality, and to mitigating the risks of gender-based violence and the exclusion of people with disabilities.

2. Project activities

The Impact Health Organization (IHO) plans to undertake the following key activities based on the indicators: 1. Providing Standard Kits to SAM Children/Caretakers: IHO will provide standard kits paired with hygiene promotion to 4000 children suffering from Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and their caretakers.2. Constructing/Rehabilitating Latrines in Nutrition Facilities: IHO will construct or rehabilitate 10 latrines in nutrition facilities.3. Constructing/Rehabilitating Boreholes in Nutrition Facilities: IHO will construct or rehabilitate 10 boreholes in nutrition facilities.4. Improving Access to Water: IHO will work towards increasing the number of people having access to an improved water source, with a target of reaching 5000 individuals.5. Enhancing Sanitation Facilities: IHO will focus on increasing the number of people with access to sanitation facilities, with a target of reaching 5000 individuals.6. Promoting Hygiene Practices: IHO will reach out to 5000 individuals with hygiene promotion and soap.7. Improving Access to Water and Sanitation for Women and Girls: Following a safety audit, IHO will work towards improving the access of 9889 women and girls to water and/or sanitation.8. Constructing/Rehabilitating Flood-Resilient WASH Facilities: IHO will construct or rehabilitate 10 water points and 20 latrine stances with a flood-resilient design.9. Designing Disability Inclusive Sanitation Facilities: IHO will design 5 disability inclusive sanitation facilities.IHO will integrate with nutrition at both the community and facility level to contribute to reducing the prevalence of malnutrition. IHO will also integrate with health at both community and facility level (technical support) to contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality. IHO will mainstream protection with a focus on GBV mitigation (safety audits) and disability inclusion. Mitigating GBV is recommended in all WASH programs, with a minimum package that includes assessment, analysis and planning, resource mobilization, programming, and communication and information sharing. The project will also focus on disability inclusion, which requires WASH partners to consult people with disabilities and collect disability-disaggregated data. This will allow the identification of specific needs/vulnerabilities, risks, and barriers which prevent people with disabilities from accessing WASH services on an equal basis with other people.
