Project Summary

In 2025, UNESCO will expand and scale up the Accelerated Adult Learning (AAL) initiative in Cox’s Bazar, building on the foundation set in 2023 and 2024. The AAL program equips Rohingya refugees, especially youth aged 18-25, with essential literacy and numeracy skills to transition into vocational training and livelihood opportunities. By 2026, the program aims to reach 3,000 learners, with 1,000 targeted for 2025. This expansion underscores UNESCO’s commitment to resilience, self-reliance, and sustainable livelihoods within the refugee community.
To broaden accessibility, UNESCO will introduce a digital AAL E-book in 2025, complementing the in-print literacy materials to support diverse teaching modalities. This digital resource enhances flexibility for both classroom and self-study environments, addressing the varied educational needs and access levels within the camps, and making learning more adaptable and scalable.
Additionally, UNESCO seeks to enhance technical support across the Livelihoods and Skills Development Sector (LSDS) by promoting high standards in training and a robust implementation framework. UNESCO will assist LSDS partners in elevating training approaches and materials, including developing detailed course competency frameworks and trainer competency frameworks to ensure consistency and quality. Strengthening trainer professionalism will also be key, with mentoring, coaching, action research initiatives and professional networks to help trainers continually refine their skills and meet learners' needs. This will enable trainers to explore effective, contextual teaching methods, building a network of well-equipped educators across the sector.
In line with sector needs, UNESCO will support partners in delivering essential digital literacy and financial literacy training which can be added to existing course. Basic digital skills will prepare learners for future livelihood opportunities and help them navigate online environments responsibly, reducing vulnerability to misinformation and digital threats. Financial literacy, meanwhile, will enable learners to manage income, debt, savings, and expenses, which is crucial for economic resilience.
Cultural programs will also be integrated within UNESCO’s programming, emphasising the role of cultural identity in fostering social cohesion. Preserving cultural heritage and strengthening a sense of belonging are vital to community resilience, especially in displacement contexts. These initiatives support a more cohesive and stable community by providing a positive sense of identity and purpose for young people, crucial for their mental well-being and community stability.
UNESCO plans to explore certification options in Cox's Bazar, drawing on its experience with programs for vulnerable and displaced populations. Building on partnerships with organizations like UNHCR and initiatives such as the Passport to Qualify and refugee teacher certification in Kenya, UNESCO aims to develop pathways that recognize the skills of Rohingya youth and adults. This approach would support learners in Cox's Bazar in gaining credentials that enhance their access to vocational opportunities and strengthen their self-reliance, aligning with global standards for education in emergencies.
In 2025, UNESCO’s expanded role in LSDS will not only strengthen literacy and numeracy education but also contribute to sector-wide quality, adaptability, and sustainable implementation.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

"Youth, and young adults aged 18-25, lack access to formal education and stable livelihoods. Nearly 90% of youth aged 15-24 in the camps are not enrolled in any education, leaving significant gaps in foundational literacy and numeracy skills. Limited literacy particularly affects women, who start with lower literacy baselines and require targeted support to enable their participation in vocational training.The demand for quality training materials that are culturally relevant and gender-sensitive has been highlighted by beneficiaries. High-quality resources and competency-based frameworks are essential for adult learners and trainers. Partners with technical expertise often lack educational design skills, underscoring the need for UNESCO’s support in developing materials that support certification, equivalency, and career progression. Certification is also crucial for skill recognition in a context lacking formal employment structures; with nearly one-third of camp households reporting no income sources, recognized qualifications could support entry into livelihood programs and third-country resettlement.Gender inclusivity remains a critical challenge. Women, while making up a large share of beneficiaries, earn on average 60% of male earnings. Achieving gender equity in training and outcomes requires gender-sensitive approaches, flexible learning options to accommodate caregiving responsibilities, and adaptable self-study or digital formats. Safe and gender-friendly learning centers with amenities such as washrooms and seating are necessary to improve engagement and allow women to participate comfortably. Addressing structural barriers that restrict women’s movement is also essential, as limited mobility impacts their access to both training and employment.To ensure sustainable and high-quality program delivery, UNESCO will expand technical support across the sector. By building trainer professionalism through mentoring, coaching, professional networks, and action research, UNESCO will strengthen instructional quality. Trainers require continuous support to adapt to mixed-skill classrooms and effectively engage learners. This includes developing robust course and trainer competency frameworks that set clear standards and integrating financial literacy to enhance household resilience—key for communities where over 57% of households earn less than 5,000 taka per month.ICT and digital skills are essential for navigating the 21st-century landscape. Improved digital literacy would enable youth to access future livelihoods, participate in social networks, and mitigate risks such as misinformation. These skills are especially pertinent for youth in fragile settings, equipping them for safer online interactions and increasing resilience to potential influences from harmful online content.Cultural programs are also needed to strengthen social cohesion and reinforce cultural identity among youth, fostering a sense of belonging within the camp. Engagement in these activities provides opportunities for young adults to connect positively with their heritage, which supports community unity and resilience. Finally, there is a need for education, beyond school education, which may fall outside the scope of the LSDS. This includes non-livelihood related learning, such as more advanced literacy and numeracy, and higher education. There is a need to address the current gap in provision, and also to address which sector adult learning (without earning) falls into."

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

"The Accelerated Adult Learning (AAL) project will integrate protection mainstreaming principles to ensure that assistance is accessible, safe, and dignified for all learners. In addition, the AAL project will contribute to the participants’ access to protection in other aspects of their lives by capacitating them with the skill to read and utilize information made available to them.1. Safety and Dignity: The project will minimize harm and safeguard participants from physical and psychosocial risks by providing safe, culturally sensitive learning environments that respect learners' dignity.2. Meaningful Access: To remove barriers, AAL will ensure equitable access to learning, especially for vulnerable groups such as women, persons with disabilities, and older youth. 3. Accountability: AAL will establish feedback and complaints mechanisms so participants can voice concerns safely, promoting transparency and trust. Regular assessments will ensure the program remains responsive to community needs.4. Participation and Empowerment: AAL will encourage active community involvement, enabling youth and adults to shape the project’s direction, strengthening ownership and fostering self-protection capacities.5. Community Engagement and Information Sharing: The project will regularly consult with participants, providing information on rights, services, and program goals to promote informed decision-making.Furthermore, by incorporating digital skills and cultural programs, the project looks to capacitate young people with the skills to navigate the contested identities they live with in a constructive way. By implementing these principles, the AAL project will foster a safe, inclusive, and responsive learning environment that respects participants' rights and addresses their unique protection needs, and at the same time support key pillars of social cohesion for a peaceful community."

2.3 - Age & Disability

"The Accelerated Adult Learning (AAL) project will integrate age and disability inclusion to address significant challenges faced by youth aged 18-25 in the Rohingya refugee camps, especially those with disabilities. The AAL project will ensure at least 3% of participants are persons with disabilities, aiming to increase this percentage where possible. This will be achieved by actively seeking participation of people with disabilities through coordination with CiCs and specialist outreach. In addition, given the low percentage of the camp population who have been identified as having a disability, UNESCO believes there are many participants who have a disability and are already participating in AAL activities, but have not had their disability recognised (and therefore they may not receive the necessary support). The project will screen for participants with disabilities using the Washington Group Questions (WGQ)-Enhanced Set, allowing for tailored support as needed. Staff and volunteers will receive training on inclusive programming to ensure accessible communication and a supportive learning environment, including accessible access toFemale youth, often facing intimidation and harassment, are at risk of early marriage due to limited educational and livelihood options. Extrapolating from data in the education sector, the safety threats cited by learners are fear of abduction (32%) and fear of armed groups (21%). Therefore, implementing partners will coordinate participants to ensure at risk women are traveling to and from training centres in groups and at suitable and safe times of day. The project will encourage female participation by creating safe, inclusive learning spaces, empowering them with skills that foster independence and challenge restrictive gender norms. "

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

"The Accelerated Adult Learning (AAL) program upholds Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) by ensuring transparency, inclusivity, and responsiveness. During technical support in 2023 and 2024, materials and training programs were developed through an iterative process of community engagement, allowing for continuous refinement based on community needs and insights. As part of the 2024 implementation, the UNESCO Institute of Lifelong Learning (UIL) conducted an evaluation, gathering extensive qualitative feedback from participants, trainers, and community stakeholders. The findings will inform future planning and implementation, ensuring the program remains aligned with the priorities identified by affected populations.The program will also use ongoing feedback mechanisms, such as key informant interviews and focus group discussions at each level of the cascade model (participants, trainers, master trainers, and project managers), to refine implementation and resources. Transparent beneficiary selection and regular communication on selection criteria ensure fair and informed participation, particularly for marginalized groups like women, persons with disabilities, and undocumented new arrivals.Community dialogues will precede any adjustments to program activities, with updates shared through accessible channels. Coordination with donors allows for adaptive responses based on community feedback, keeping the program responsive to evolving local needs. By prioritizing age, gender, and disability inclusion at every stage, AAL addresses the specific needs of women, youth, older persons, and persons with disabilities, fostering an accountable, transparent, and inclusive program."

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

"The Accelerated Adult Learning (AAL) program strictly adheres to Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) standards, ensuring a safe and respectful environment for all participants. As a mandatory member of the PSEA Network, the program complies with all requirements to minimize Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) risks and respond effectively to any incidents.UNESCO maintains a zero-tolerance policy toward SEA, with mandatory reporting of any allegations to the UN Resident Coordinator and to UNESCO’s HQ, in line with standing policies. This is regardless of whether they involve the organization directly. The program will conduct regular SEA risk assessments, addressing identified risks with support from the PSEA Network. All staff and partners are required to undergo PSEA training, including refreshers, ensuring they are equipped to respond to SEA disclosures safely, respectfully, and confidentially.PSEA awareness materials with key messages and reporting mechanisms will be visibly accessible to beneficiaries, ensuring that all participants are aware of their rights and available protections. Community input, particularly from vulnerable groups, will guide the development of safe, accessible, and culturally sensitive reporting channels.The organization’s code of conduct embeds PSEA principles, and all contractors, vendors, and third parties engaged in the project are required to commit to these standards. A designated PSEA focal point will oversee PSEA integration throughout the project cycle, ensuring alignment with PSEA Network priorities. To reinforce accountability, PSEA will be a standing agenda item in all project coordination meetings, fostering a proactive and protective environment across all program activities."

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

"The Accelerated Adult Learning (AAL) program supports climate change adaptation by providing literacy and numeracy skills that empower learners to better understand climate risks and resilience strategies, fostering informed decision-making. With foundational literacy and digital skills, youth gain the skills to access critical information on climate risks and adaptation measures, laying the groundwork for future learning and enabling greater engagement with sustainability initiatives.The program also aligns with the Energy and Environment Technical Working Group’s (EETWG) Climate Action Strategy by preparing learners to transition into livelihood and vocational training that emphasizes low-impact, climate-sensitive skills. AAL participants will be directed toward skills training that minimizes environmental impact and prioritizes sustainable practices, supporting a climate-resilient future for the refugee community. Through this alignment, AAL contributes to building community awareness and resilience against climate challenges without contributing to environmentally harmful practices.Further, AAL’s flexible approach, which includes self-study and digital resources, allows learners to continue their education on climate and sustainability independently. By enhancing literacy and encouraging climate-conscious livelihoods, AAL plays an essential role in supporting the community’s long-term adaptation and resilience."

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

"The Accelerated Adult Learning (AAL) program will enhance emergency preparedness and response (EPR) by equipping learners with essential literacy and numeracy skills that enable them to actively participate in community emergency planning and implement vital safety measures. Through improved literacy, participants will be better able to understand and act upon materials that disseminate information on risks, adaptation strategies, and mitigation measures for natural hazards, including floods and cyclones.AAL aligns with the Inter-Sector Coordination Group’s (ISCG) Multi-Hazard Response Plan and the National Plan for Disaster Management (2021-2025) by supporting disaster risk-informed programming and community-based preparedness. The program will integrate information on emergency planning and response into its curriculum to raise awareness among learners on standard procedures (e.g., Cyclone and Fire SOPs). This approach ensures that participants are not only recipients of information but can also contribute effectively within camp disaster management committees (CDMC) and other local initiatives.Additionally, gender inclusion in EPR is strengthened. Literacy and digital training promotes the participation of both men and women in preparedness activities by fostering an informed and resilient community. AAL aims to build local capacity for effective emergency response, thereby reducing loss and damage during disasters."

By incorporating participatory approaches, AAL enables youth to take an active role in shaping their The Accelerated Adult Learning (AAL) program directly addresses the challenges Rohingya youth face in the camps, including limited educational access, economic insecurity, and gender-specific vulnerabilities. 4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the Accelerated Adult Learning (AAL) program promotes self-reliance and resilience for refugees by equipping youth with foundational literacy and numeracy skills that facilitate their transition to livelihood and vocational training. This skill set is essential for refugees to access sustainable livelihoods, both within the camps and in any future contexts, such as relocation or repatriation. By building a strong educational foundation, AAL enables learners to engage more effectively in vocational programs, enhancing their ability to support themselves and contribute productively to their communities. This preparation not only fosters economic stability but also reinforces the resilience and adaptability needed to navigate uncertain futures, ensuring that refugees are better equipped to rebuild their lives wherever they may be.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

"In 2024, UNESCO launched pilot livelihood training programs within the host community of Cox’s Bazar. Building on this foundation, we aim to expand these initiatives in 2025, contingent upon available funding. Future programs will integrate digital and financial literacy development, equipping participants with essential skills for economic resilience and growth. By incorporating financial literacy, participants will gain a stronger understanding of managing personal and household finances, budgeting, and savings strategies, fostering financial resilience amid economic uncertainty. Digital literacy will further enhance resilience by expanding access to online income-generating opportunities and digital marketplaces, which are increasingly critical in today’s economy. Additionally, the livelihood training will support income generation directly, empowering participants with skills that translate into practical, sustainable earning opportunities."
