Project Summary

The proposed project profile will complement ADO's Food security and live saving intervention for Cyclone Sagar and drought affected populations in Awdal and Sanaag region and will enable ADO to provide a complete assistance package to the cyclone Sagar and drought affected communities in Awdal, W.Galbeed and Sanaag regions in terms of Live saving interventions and Livelihood asset restoration activities. The proposed project will support through unconditional cash/food relief transfer for 1500 HH vulnerable households for the period of 4 months through voucher system. People in Emergency live saving situation will be provided unconditional food/cash relief and particular attention will paid to women, children and most vulnerable HH’s. Currently 36,000 people are in Emergency situation in Awdal while 17,000 people are in Emergency in W.Galbeed (FSNAU 18). Likewise the project will support restoration and sustaining of social livelihood assets in project target areas. 800 pastoral households will be provided restocking of small in age goats and each household will provide 10 herds, therefore the project will distribute 8000 herds. 1,200 smallholder farmers both irrigated and rainfed farms will be supported through provision of certified seeds, land preparations, tools, irrigation water pumps, EP pipes, and farmer training workshops. ADO will provide the most vulnerable households with conditional cash transfer through construction of 70,000 linear meters of contour bunds in agriculture areas to harvest rain water in cultivable lands, rehabilitation of 3 Dams. Therefore, the project will create 317 jobs for 90 days that will enhance household’s resilience and income opportunity. additionally the project will provide livestock water trucking 910 pastoral households in target areas. This will be done in close collaboration with the local authorities, Ministry of Agriculture in the respective areas. A total of 4,727 HHs ( 28,362 individuals of which 7,374 are women, 6,523 men, 7,658 girls and 6,807 boys) are expected to benefit from this project action.

Needs

Despite the humanitarian situation showing significant improvements in the food security and nutrition, an estimated 1.5 million people in Somalia are expected to be again under Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse through December according to findings from the post-Gu seasonal assessment conducted in June and July 2018 by the FSNAU. However, 4.6 million population in Somalia need humanitarian assistance and livelihood support including more than 1.5 million people who will face Crisis or worse (IPC Phase 3) or higher) and 3.1 million people that are classified as Stressed (IPC Phase 2). With these great figures and number of (4.6) million people, they require continued assistance to become resilient and recover from the residual drought impact of 2016/17. Primarily, livelihood support should be sustained to bring long lasting improvements to the Somali food security situation while a greater likelihood of normal to above normal 2018 Deyr (October-December) rains across Somalia as forecast issued by the Greater Horn of Africa Climate Outlook Forum (GHACOF50) shows. Rapid assessment conducted by ADO in early October 2018 shows food consumption gabs in Awdal, W.Galbeed and Sanaag regions. This will affect a large number of youth and women due to the decreased crop productivity and livestock heads. In addition, the “Sagar” cyclone affected communities will remain stressed and under crisis (IPC Phase 3). This project will focus the vulnerable community members in Awdal, W.Galbeed and Sanaag regions that are under stressed (IPC Phase 2) 218,000, 140,000 and 149,000. In addition, under crisis (IPC Phase 3) of 49,000, 86000 and 121,000 respectively. ADO will provide immediate lifesaving and livelihood support while targeting vulnerable and marginalized youth, women and men in these regions. This will be done through provision of unconditional food access to ensure lifesaving to most vulnerable HH’s; livestock support while providing life animal

Rehabilitation of 3 earth dams by removing deposited sands in the dams through cash for work approach engaged 65 casual labors for the period of 2 months. 21 workers will be female while 44 will be male workers. 7. Conduct 6 farmer training workshops attending by 65 farmer trainees in Baki, Cadaad and Marawade localities.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Exclusion and discrimination of socially marginalized groups are contributing to high levels of acute humanitarian needs and lack of protection among some of the most vulnerable. In addition, drought and conflict have triggered massive displacement and further exposed civilians to serious protection threats and the emergence of negative coping mechanisms. This has led to further erosion of the resilience of communities and social fragmentation. Women, children, persons with disabilities, and marginalized communities are impacted differently by the crisis and displacement, having distinct humanitarian needs and facing specific protection concerns.However, according to ADO expertise in implementing humanitarian projects, maximum preventive measure will be exercised and there will be no feasible risks that target and vulnerable communities will meet whereby nothing will prevent them from inclusion of the project implementation and resource accessibility. ADO will make sure that all categories of the population in respective target villages will be consulted selected and be part of the people to supported. Host community or leaders off target villages will not be allowed to decide who get what solely. ADO will apply all measures and techniques to minimize social risks of this project using by the protection risks analysis and guidelines. Trained staff for risk analysis will be engaged to assess on monthly bases and create where people to access to project management units without any restrictions.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

ADO acknowledges and reaffirms that the complete realization of gender equality is an inalienable and indivisible feature of all human rights and fundamental freedoms. The systematic promotion of this principle in measurable results is essential to ensuring protection and durable solutions for women and men of all ages and backgrounds served by the Organisation.However, ADO is deeply committed to ensuring that vulnerable community members, refugees, stateless and displaced people have equal access to their rights, protection, services and resources, and are able to participate as active partners in the decisions that affect them. To this end, ADO has committed to mainstreaming an Age, Gender and Diversity (AGD) approach. AGD is a human rights and community-based approach. Mainstreaming AGD means to plan, programme, implement, monitor and evaluate operations, keeping in mind equality and full participation as guiding principles. Working in partnership with people of concern and other stakeholders, ADO is committed to ensuring that safeguards are in place to prevent any action from inadvertently increasing marginalization, vulnerability, exclusion and stigmatization that may put some people/groups at further risk.Therefore, ADO has clear set of gender and age equality policies that are applied in any project, to maintain and make sure those risks of gender and age based exclusions are prevented with strict supervision and staff training to make sure all types of gender were taken into account to maintain gender equality and avoid exclusion of gender.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The armed conflict, drought, and internal displacement have led to a highly complex crisis of protection in Somalia. Protection violations are both a root cause of the crisis and a grave consequence, resulting in a myriad of acute and evolving protection concerns affecting the vulnerable communities. Of particular concern is the situation of conflict-affected populations, including those in hard-to-reach areas and displaced and marginalized populations who face an elevated risk of protection violations. However, the proposed project is targeting Northern Somalia of Galbeed and Sanaag regions whereby armed conflicts and violence are rare. Nevertheless, ADO will take into account the possibility of having armed conflict and violence by the target communities, and will come with conflict/violence mitigation approaches that mainly lower the impact of the armed conflict and violence. This will be main through provision of life saving, increased access to food security and livelihood support to soften the impact and will coordinate with concerned security agencies, movement agencies for further support.
