Project Summary

This project is an integrated project (30% Nutrition and 70% Health)
The goal of this project is the reduction of morbidity and mortality among the Syrian population in Idleb and Aleppo, and this will be accomplished by increasing the coverage of the existing health and nutrition services through community services to improve the awareness of best nutritional practices according to the IYCF guidelines and to identify and refer cases of malnutrition to specialized services.
The project will provide outreach preventive nutrition services focusing on the appropriate infant and young child feeding practices through household visits according to the standard guidelines developed by the Community Health Working Group (CHWG) and the Nutrition Cluster in Gaziantep. The services will be part of an integrated primary health and nutrition project supported by a network of Community Health Workers (CHWs), to provide integrated health and nutrition services with a focus on IYCF counseling and messaging to 30,000 caregivers, in addition to the screening for malnutrition of 25,000 boys and girls under the age of 5 years and 15,000 pregnant and lactating women. The CH team will identify and refer cases of malnutrition from the community to specilized care providers in Atareb, Aldana and Armanaz subdistricts and will conduct the necessary follow-up for the population in the targeted area with a focus on IDP concentrations and remote, rural communities. The CHWs will be receive refresher training on child healthcare and nutritional screening and referral. They will also be provided with sufficient education to help raising the awareness of the related communities.
The outreach team will be linked to the existing primary healthcare system to maximize the coverage and impact of facility-based nutritional services at the outreach level, with support from the health directorates which will support the implementation of the project with MTI.
This project will continue to employ 120 community health workers (CHWs) supervised by twelve (12) team leaders and two (2) district supervisors and linked to Atareb, Sarmada, Termanin and Armanaz Primary Healthcare Centres (PHCs), which are currently supported by MTI.
Of subdistricts in Idleb and western Aleppo, Atareb, Aldana and Armanaz subdistricts have received the highest number of IDPs during the recent waves of displacement in Idleb and from rural Damascus, northern Homs, eastern Ghouta and Dara’a. These newly affected IDPs comprise the main community groups who will benefit from this project. Through the community referrals, beneficiaries will have access to MTI’s referral pathway from the health centers to specialized nutritional services within and outside Atareb Armanaz and Aldana.
For the implementation of this project, MTI partnered with Relief Experts Association (UDER) which is a local NGO registered in Turkey. While MTI will be the overall accountable for the implementation of this project, and will lead on the technical components and the quality of activities, REA will be responsible for the administrative component in Turkey including hiring staff and conducting local procurement.

Needs

Aldana and Atareb subdistricts host 134 IDP camps with 40,382 children under five. As of November 2018; Sarmada has a population of 54,053 (including 32,801 IDPs), Termanin has 31,948 (15,268 IDPs), Armanaz has 18,679 (6,549 IDPs), and Atareb has 46,819 (16,577 IDPs). The health status of this population was impacted by the lack of access to PHC and nutrition services. Health centers are overwhelmed by the mass influx of IDPs. Between February and June 2018, the MTI-supported health facilities witnessed an increase of 30-70% in the number of consultations and this was not matched by an increase in the capacity of facilities. Combined with a shortage in health personnel, especially of female workers this results in overworked practitioners and reduced quality of services. In a survey conducted by REACH in March 2018, 173 out of 215 communities assessed in Aleppo and 97 of 193 communities in Idleb reported barriers to accessing healthcare services, including citing insufficient numbers of health facilities, high transportation cost and lack of transportation means. A quarter of respondents in Aleppo and 16% in Idleb reported access barriers for particular vulnerable groups, such as the elderly or people with disabilities/injuries/illness. Patient exit interviews collected at the MTI-supported health facilities show that transportation presents a significant challenge to accessing fixed facilities for vulnerable communities especially for women and young girls. They have to rely on relatives or ask strangers to transport them. In many cases, women, girls, young children, and elderly people must be escorted by a male relative in order to access healthcare which limits these populations from accessing the available PHCs outside their communities. Cultural misconceptions and lack of trust with the current health system push people in NW Syria to fail to adhere to optimal nutritional practices. The project aims at eliminating misconceptions and encourage the community to adhere to standard nutritional practices and prescribed treatments to ensure the best utilization of nutritional services. A well-targeted education plan is needed to raise the awareness of communities of malnutrition and best nutritional practices according to IYCF guidlines for preventing malnutrition and improving child and mother health. Community-based nutritional interventions will empower households for identification of cases of malnutrition and support for adherence to treatment plans. Support for pregnant and lactating women at the community level is needed to improve healthcare seeking practices and stress on the importance of breast feeding. As the crisis in Syria protracted, the importance of CH was acknowledged, and it was included in the essential package of services for NW Syria. Existing CH projects provide limited coverage within their targeted communities and subdistricts, only reaching 10% of the targeted population at times; additional coverage must be established to reach vulnerable communities in underserved locations.Without an effective CH program to support the current PHC and Nutrition services, the coverage of services will remain limited, access for vulnerable groups will be restricted, fixed facilities will remain overwhelmed, and quality of services will decline. As formal education in these communities deteriorates due to ongoing conflict or displacement, household nutritional practices and health behaviors are likely to deteriorate as well, leading to an increased incidence of common diseases and a potential increase in the overall mortality rates.

Implementation of activities

** Output 1: Targeted and appropriate nutritional education and behavioural change messages are delivered to households of IDP and vulnerable residents in Atareb, Sarmada, and Termanin. * Activity 1.1: Conduct household community health visits. MTI recruited and trained 120 CHWs from the communities of Atareb, Armanaz, Sarmada, and Termanin. Each 10 CHWs will be managed by one Team Leader. CHWs work in pairs so that one can carry out the educational session while the other collects data and fills the household visit record. Each CHW is assigned to 200 households (400 households covered by each pair) and responsible for maintaining records and following up on their health needs according to the standard SoP developed by the CHWG. The team leaders are responsible for managing the CHWs, facilitating field visits, and communicating with community leaders and the medical staff. Team leaders handle security incidents and collect the daily records generated by the CHWs. They meet weekly with the medical staff at the health facilities to review the types of cases referred by the CHWs and suggest improvements to CHWs’ performance. Leaders and Supervisors analyse the data collected at the district level, and report to the health directorates and to MTI. They are responsible for conducting refresher training to the CH team every four months during the project, and they are regularly improving educational messaging and the related IEC material according to the project requirements and the changing needs of the targeted communities. * Activity 1.2: Deliver key behavioral change messages.Key behavioral change messages will be prioritized each quarter by MTI, the health directorates, and community leaders. The suggested messages are related to optimal nutritional practices and the importance of breast feeding. Each message is delivered through household visits and meetings with community leaders, medical staff, and mothers and caregivers visiting the health facilities. The CH team conducted a family mapping between November and December 2018 and an end project analysis will be conducted using the family health records to capture any changes in key morbidity and mortality indicators and assess if the messages were delivered correctly. The CHWs are trained on family mapping and the survey tools as part of their basic training before the launch of this project, and they will receive a refresher one-day training before they conduct the end project survey. ** Output 2: IDP and vulnerable resident communities are linked to the existing nutritional services. * Activity 2.1: Refer IDP and host communities to the existing health facilities in Atareb, Sarmada, and Termanin. CHWs conducted a family mapping to collect demographic, nutritonal and health-related information, to identify the vulnerable communities in need for the improved community health referral system.

The surveys include question about respecting privacy and PSEA. -Conduct training for staff on PSEA, code of conduct and ethical data collection. -All staff sign and commit to MTI’s PSEA, child protection and code of conduct policies -Contract include a strict term of dismissal towards any misconduct.-Enhance Complaint and feedback mechanism. -Record all beneficiary data through an online database that prevents the storage of data at the field level. MTI will monitor for this risk through: - Monitoring and Evaluation surveys conducted by MEAL department including staff behaviour and including SEA questions. - Announce PSEA focal point’s contact details and ensure that the beneficiaries understand the sensitivity and confidentiality of this process. Electronic database for safe storage of data.The above-mentioned risks are unacceptable however with mitigation actions and proper monitoring the risk level becomes acceptable.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions include:Permission for MTI to operate in Idleb and Aleppo continues.Current conflict doesn't affect program activities.Donor funds continue to be provided for NW Syria.Community acceptance for this project continues. Risks and mitigation measures:There is a potential escalation of violence in Idleb through a military offensive on southern Idleb, or fights among Organized Armed Groups as the Government of Turkey (GoT) leads an effort to eliminate sanctioned armed groups. MTI’s chosen locations for this project (Atareb and Aldana subdistricts) are located near the Turkish border and away from potentially targeted locations. Massive IDP movement to northern Idleb and Aleppo might put increased pressure on the health system in northern Syria. This requires maximizing the coverage of primary and secondary healthcare systems. MTI will ensure that the network of CHWs continues to be in place to be ready to respond to potential waves of IDPs. Close communication will be maintained with local councils and community leaders to ensure that CHWs are covering the communities with the highest concentration of IDPs. Given that a functional mobile phone network does not exist in Idleb and Aleppo, MTI will equip the CH team with communication devices (walkie talkies or local mobile phone subscriptions) to ensure that they can communicate to the team leaders, provide information about their location, and receive immediate guidance from Team Leaders and facility managers as needed. Team Leaders will be primarily responsible for handling and reporting security incidents related to their teams, and they will receive support from IHD and the civil defence on safety and security measures, including proper walkie talkie communication practices. MTI will work directly with IHD and IHD will contract the Idleb CH Supervisor, Team Leader and CHWs directly. This is based on the sufficient capacity of IHD, proven through previous capacity-building projects supported by GIZ. Aleppo Health Directorate does not have the same level of capacity; therefore, MTI will contract the Aleppo CH team with Atareb PHC, and establish an indirect reporting line with AHD through which MTI will be able to involve AHD in this project, slowly build the knowledge of AHD staff of the project, and share information about the management model implemented by IHD and MTI in Idleb without creating a risk to the activities in Aleppo.
