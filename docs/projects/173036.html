Project Summary

During 2021, and especially after the Peace Agreement, it is anticipated that more IDPs will return to their rural areas which are relatively peacefulAlshoorq Organization AOSCD with this funding proposes to provide humanitarian food and livelihood support to returnees and vulnerable host communities in the nine target villages including Shangi, Abuadam, Jogana, Fasha, Tagla, Kukuja in Bilel Locality, Sani Deleiba in Alsalam Locality and Omusabk in Mershing Locality.
On the 7th of August and during the flood, a need assessment was conducted by the AOSCD team in South Darfur ( Beliel, As-Salam - SD, and Mershing ), which linked to HRP and IPC which highlight the need of venerable groups targeting by the project group.
The project will provide basic agricultural inputs including main crop seeds and vegetables for 600 vulnerable farmer households to support staple crop and vegetable production of returnees and vulnerable groups in these rural areas. The project will train 600 vulnerable farmers in improved and sustainable agricultural practices. The project will also promote rural livelihoods diversification and income generation activities including restocking of 600 small ruminants (sheep) for 200 households, distribution of 200 irrigation kits for small scale irrigation, and distribution of 1,000 poultry for 200 households as means for their early settlement in their area of return. Two Livestock and poultry vaccination and treatment campaigns will be conducted to protect animals and poultry from common diseases. Environment conservation activities will be implemented including training of 100 farmers on tree nursery management and tree planting, distribution of 2,000 fruit tree seedlings, and training of 100 farmers inefficient energy-saving stoves. Capacity building will be conducted for partner NGOs, staff, and CBOs. The training courses will include managing projects in emergencies, gender, and environment protection, food security and livelihood issues, and disaster risk reduction.
AOSCD will ensure equality in targeting and will involve women and men in program design and targeting. The project will ensure equal participation of women, girls, boys, and men in decision-making and capacity building. The project will ensure that the distribution arrangements (time, place, schedule, size and weight, etc.) do not discriminate against vulnerable or marginalized groups.AOSCD will ensure that all people of concern are equally and fully informed about the seed distribution, for example, the size and composition of the ration, beneficiary selection criteria, place and time of distribution, no service required in exchange for receiving the ration, and proper channels for reporting abuse cases. In consultation with women, the project will anticipate and address any negative impacts that the distribution of seed to women and the issuance of seed entitlements in women’s names may have on the community and intra-household relationships.AOSCD will ensure monitoring and evaluation take gender issues into consideration and will ensure equal participation of women and men in monitoring, evaluation, and review of progress and results. During project reviews, the project will assess the impact of the seed distribution on women and men and will consult women, girls, boys, and men in the identification of remaining gaps and areas of improvement.

1 - Needs

According to the United Nations Office for Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) Humanitarian Update an estimated 200,000 IDPs and refugees have returned to their home villages in different parts of South Darfur in recent years. In South Darfur State Bilel, Alsalam and Mershing Localities have continued to receive returnees from Deriege, Sakaly, Elserif, Kalma and Alsalam IDP camps initially displaced as a result of the Darfur conflict. The FEWS Net report reveals that both permanent and seasonal returnees of IDPs have increased desire to cultivate and produce crops this year. The report attributes this to the relative improvements in security in some areas, the reduction in humanitarian aid, the sharp increase in food prices over the past two years and the relative good rains this year. According to Datat available form The Assement conducted on food security and livelihoods using participatory methodologies to establish the priority needs of the returnees and the host communities ,it confirmed that returnees are keen to engage in farming and other livelihoods enhancing activities provided that they get access to the necessary support. The assessment covered 9 returnee target villages include Shangi, Abuadam, Jogana, Fasha, Tagla, Kukuja in Bilel Locality, Sani Deleiba in Alsalam Locality and Omusabk in Mershing Locality with a total of 4,152 households having 22,876 individuals (10,980 males and 11,806 females). According to data from the assessment, 83% of the households are comprised of returnees and only 17% households are host communities. During the assessment - community members including men and women, leaders, youth groups (girls and boys), elderly (women/men) and the disabled expressed their critical needs in increasing their food security especially for returnee families and vulnerable host community households. The assessment revealed that these areas suffer from food insecurity during the months of May to October and after harvest the food usually lasts for only 2 months as a result of low agricultural production and productivity. The longer periods without sufficient food have had negative impact, particularly on women and children leading to malnutrition as both eat last after men. Usually in good times families have 2-3 meals a day while in critical times the families survive on 1-2 meals a day. According to the WFP food security monitoring report, in South Darfur, cereal prices have followed an upward trend at the start of the harvest season and sorghum prices were 90% higher than last year. The assessment revealed that 94% of both the returnee and host community households have access to land either through ownership (51%) or renting (49%). In the three localities, agriculture is the major source of livelihood in addition to firewood collection, charcoal burning, handicraft making and keeping of small animals like goats and sheep. The main crops grown are staple crops including millet, sorghum and groundnuts in addition to okra. All family members including women, men and old children participate jointly in the farming activities including land clearing, land opening, planting, weeding and harvesting. Both men and women have equal access to productive resources such as land and tools but it is the men who make major decisions particularly related to any sale of farm produce. Approximately, 10% of the households are female headed and 20% of the children are children under 5 years. All groups consulted claimed urgent needs in reducing food insecurity, and decreasing aid dependency by improving their livelihoods and self-reliance. Most farmers face a number of constraints including lack of agricultural inputs such as, improved high yielding and early maturing staple and vegetable seeds and tools. Farmers also are faced with pests and diseases in addition to a lack of improved knowledge and skills in agricultural production.
