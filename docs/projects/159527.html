Project Summary

The proposed six-month project will provide innovative and traditional cost-effective durable solutions to support the most vulnerable individuals and HH (HHs) affected by the conflict to withstand in critical times of crises, prevent them from falling deeper into vicious circle of poverty and insecurity. The project envisages provision of critical life-saving emergency assistance for the most vulnerable population affected by the conflict on both sides of the contact line under Food Security and Livelihoods and WASH clusters. Protection of beneficiaries will be mainstreamed throughout the project through provision of tailored assistance, home-based care for elderly and persons with specific needs (PSN) under Cash for Work modality (CfW), home deliveries by TGH partner shops to physically impaired individuals, and strengthened community links and enhanced accountability to affected populations (AAP).
Outcome 1. Access to critical food assistance and income generation for vulnerable conflict-affected people including IDPs increased
To improve access to food and food security level of 1 400 most vulnerable individuals along the contact line in both Luhansk and Donetsk Oblasts in GCA and in Donetsk oblast in NGCA, the project envisages provision of critical life-saving food assistance during 3 consecutive winter and spring months. This will be achieved through multiple modalities such as e-vouchers, CfW, vouchers for agricultural inputs (GCA) and food kits (NGCA). Provision of agricultural vouchers will ensure more efficient, sustainable, and durable solutions aiming at building resilience of beneficiaries and subsequent decrease of beneficiaries’ dependence on humanitarian aid. The conflict has grave consequences on social fragmentation, which worsens the situation of isolated population who cannot benefit from traditional solidarity mechanisms. The disruption of the traditional solidarity network mostly affects vulnerable and isolated individuals, such as the elderly or people suffering from disabilities, and in strong lack of mobility. CfW modality will not only provide access to employment to 300 vulnerable individuals for 3 subsequent months and contribute to implementation of community rehabilitation projects but will also ensure provision of home-based care for 300 vulnerable elderly and persons with disabilities, and will strengthen community links. In NGCA assistance will be provided in-kind by Variant, TGH local partner.
Outcome 2. Access to hygiene for the most vulnerable conflict-affected population including IDPs both in GCA and NGCA is ensured
5,000 most vulnerable conflict-affected individuals in GCA and NGCA including men and women, boys and girls including children under 2 years old will benefit from improved access to hygiene during three subsequent months to help them withstand critical times and keep from resorting to negative coping strategies. In GCA the project will employ the e-voucher modality TGH successfully employs in Ukraine since 2016 to provide fast and easy access to essential hygiene in partnership with local shops along the contact line. The project indirectly will contribute to enhancing capacity of local shops, supporting local economy and strengthening solidarity mechanisms within and between communities. In NGCA in-kind assistance is envisaged and will be provided in partnership with Variant.
Access in NGCA: In NGCA both critical food and hygiene assistance will be provided in-kind in partnership with Variant, TGH local partner, based in Donetsk and registered with de facto authorities. The project has been designed jointly with Variant, is built on existing TGH-Variant partnership and a previous multi-sector pilot project successfully implemented by TGH and Variant in Donetsk oblast, NGCA, in 2018 with food and hygiene components being an integral part of the project.

1 - Needs

With the sixth year into the conflict, humanitarian needs along the contact line in eastern Ukraine still remain high. The effects of the protracted conflict continue to have negative consequences on the socio-economic status of the population. On both sides of the contact line and most dramatically in NGCA, the crisis has severe impact on the population and the economy. The nationwide unemployment rate is the highest for five consecutive years. Lack of income generation opportunities, financial resources and absence of prospects affect the ability of people especially in NGCA to access essential products and cover their basic needs. High prices of food items have an important impact on HHs’ capacity to access a satisfying level of food security. In areas closer to the line of contact and in isolated settlements, access to essential goods is also a challenge due to active hostilities (REACH 2019). The results of PDM conducted by TGH in June 2019 demonstrated that the average income per beneficiary per month was 1,403 UAH which represented 72% of the official minimum subsistence level (1,936 UAH/person/month) and only 39% of the actual minimum subsistence level (3,586 UAH/person/month, Ministry for Social policy, March 2019). The study further demonstrated that 10% of HHs headed by unemployed women and men aged 40 to 60 and elderly had poor or borderline food consumption scores with food security levels dropping during winter and spring. The ongoing conflict had a serious impact on the agriculture sector, traditionally one of the major sources of food and livelihoods in rural Ukraine. Available evidence suggests that 55% of rural HHs are exposed to monetary poverty, with one third living in poverty on incomes of less than USD 100 per month (FAO). Around 21,000 rural HH residing within 20-km from the contact line in GCA rely on self-production of food (HNO). According to a Food security and livelihoods study conducted by TGH in 2019, lack of agricultural inputs is perceived by rural HHs as the most important aspect limiting their potential to increase HH’s income generation and food production activities. Among HHs surveyed by TGH during 2018-2019 comprising HHs with women of reproductive age, children under five, elderly and single headed HHs, 21% spend more than 50% of their income for food, leaving a tight scope for HHs to face unforeseen expenses. Ranking as the first negative coping mechanism, 74% of older persons decrease their food intakes hence severely decreasing their food security levels . Further, TGH HH identification demonstrated that hygiene represented only 11% of HH’ expenses far behind food (42%), health (25%) and shelter (22%) leaving few disposable cash for hygiene. HHs along the contact line notably HHs with low income, unemployed or pre-pension men and women, HHs with multiple children under 5 and isolated elderly are deprived of access to essential basic hygiene. The low incomes levels explain the narrow share allocated to hygiene products. Such distribution of costs represents a sanitation risk especially for families with small children, elderly or persons with special needs who cannot afford buying certain types of hygiene products such as diapers which are extremely expensive. According to a recent assessment of the WASH cluster 14% of HHs in GCA and 13% in NGCA have unsatisfactory hygiene practices including the use of unhygienic menstrual hygiene materials or lack of access to basic hygiene products. This clearly demonstrates the severe consequences of the conflict on the population contributing to growing divides and poverty. With significant basic humanitarian needs remaining unmet the vulnerable populations in GCA and NGCA risk falling deeper into vicious circle of poverty and insecurity.

2 - Activities & outcomes

Outcome 1: Activity 1. Selection of beneficiaries of food e-vouchers, food kits, agricultural vouchers and CfW. Selection of beneficiaries will be done in coordination with local authorities and other actors based on the FSL cluster criteria. In NGCA selection will be done in partnership with Variant. Selection of CfW beneficiaries will additionally look at employment status. Important selection criterion for beneficiaries of agricultural vouchers is availability of backyards used as a source of food. Short-listed beneficiaries will be visited to assess feasibility of successful use of funds before final selection. Activity 2 Distribution of e-vouchers and food kitsIn GCA distribution of e-vouchers will be done through TGH e-voucher modality through one of the 101 local partner shops along the contact line. Beneficiaries will receive 3 rounds of e-vouchers with value of 825 UAH per FSL cluster recommendations. Prior to distributions, prices will be fixed for priority food items. Shops will ensure home deliveries to beneficiaries with low mobility. In NGCA food kits will be distributed to target areas in partnership with Variant. Activity 3: Distribution of agricultural vouchersThe value of the voucher is set at 2000 UAH to ensure sufficient funds to purchase quality agricultural inputs such as seeds, fodder, tools, etc. Beneficiaries will have a choice in procuring inputs based on their needs. Vouchers will be distributed through local banks or Ukrpochta based on beneficiaries’ preferences. Monitoring of utilisation of vouchers will be conducted by TGH in cooperation with local authorities/community members. Activity 4: Workshops for CfW beneficiariesWorkshops will be tailored to 2 types of activities: community rehabilitation projects and provision of home-based care to persons with special needs. Training will cover programmatic, administrative and Do no harm and conflict sensitivity aspects. Activity 5: Implementation of CfW activities Projects and recipients of home-based care will be identified in cooperation with local authorities and communities for 3 rounds of CfW. Each CfW beneficiary will provide 60 hours of assistance per month for UAH 825 and an e-voucher kit based on individual or group plans developed. TGH in partnership with local authorities will monitor the implementation of activities. Outcome 2:Activity 1: Selection of beneficiaries of hygiene kitsSelection will be done based on WASH cluster criteria in coordination with local authorities and actors working in the same areas. In NGCA selection will be done in partnership with Variant. Final selection will be made based on a scoring system and cumulative vulnerabilities. Preferences will be given to persons with disabilities, single-headed households, families with multiple children, unemployed or households with low income, IDPs. Activity 2: Distribution of e-vouchers for hygiene and hygiene in-kind kitsIn GCA distributions will be done through e-voucher modality. 3 rounds for different types of needs are envisaged including family kits which include women dignity kits for women of reproductive age; PSN kits and baby kits for infants under 2 y.o. Prices will be fixed prior to distributions. Shops will ensure home deliveries to beneficiaries with low mobility. In NGCA food kits will be procured and distributed in partnership with Variant. Monitoring is a cross-cutting activity and will be implemented based on TGH Results-based Monitoring (RBM) framework. Baseline data collected at the project start will be used as a reference to measure outcomes. Results of the PDM will be shared with clusters and other stakeholders. AAP is an integral part of the project. Beneficiaries will be included in all stages of the project cycle. A project Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) will be established as part of the overall TGH CRM and will have multiple feedback channels to ensure improved access and inclusion.
