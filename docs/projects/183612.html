Project Summary

The proposed Action Against Hunger (AAH) response to be implemented in 2022 across the Gaza Strip are aligned with HRP 2022 Strategic Objectives 2 and 3 as well as the WASH Cluster Objectives 2 and 3.
AAH interventions aim to support vulnerable families (including women, men, girls and boys, elderly and people with disability and specific needs) to have safe access to WASH facilities in public facilities (health care facilities (HCF) and schools) as well as the unconnected communities depending on cesspits to discharge their wastewater, and families prone to stormwater floods in three communities of the North Gaza.
Through connecting households of the Maan, Alstar and Jawrat Alluwt communities to an adequate wastewater drainage system, and other three communities of North Gaza (Jabalia and Biet Lahia) through stormwater drainage systems, all women, men, girls and boys will be protected from being in contact with flooded stormwater or wastewater during the winter season due to the accumulation of stormwater in the cesspits flooding in the streets, in hospitals and schools. This will further help prevent the risk of infections and diseases from flooded wastewater and other health-related issues.
By improving access to sanitation facilities in public places and communities alongside the hygiene awareness campaign developed with the communities, AAH hopes to improve the communities’ hygiene practices, provide a safe environment and reduce public health risks for all of the community members, women and girls in particular.
The public awareness campaign will target the vulnerable communities and spread messages on the proper use of the WASH facilities at households, schools, hospitals and HCF, in addition to safe water handling and improving the capacity of families in targeted areas to address hygiene practice issues at household level. This awareness campaign will be developed using the communication with communities methodology and will include information on COVID-19 mitigation measures, to contribute to the efforts in limiting the spread of the pandemic in the Gaza Strip. Orientation sessions, initiatives and IEC materials will be conducted by AAH staff to the targeted women, men, boys, girls, elderly and people with disabilities.
A communication plan with communities will be developed at the beginning of the project to ensure proper community engagement at all stages of project implementation. The plan will take into account different groups within the targeted communities, women, men, boys and girls and PWDs. AAH aims to enhance community ownership and active participation from the community, in addition to improving communities’ access and trust to AAH’s Feedback and Complaint Mechanism channels.
The proposed intervention is guided by the AAH Gender and Protection policy and AAH staff endeavor to streamline safety and dignity in all humanitarian interventions. Accountability and participation will be ensured through interviews and focus group discussions and through applying AAH’s thorough Feedback and Complaint Mechanism.

1 - Needs

WASH vulnerabilities in the Gaza Strip have been driven by the longstanding Israeli restrictions and closure in the Gaza Strip, the internal Palestinian divide, the COVID-19 outbreak, and the May 2021 escalation (that affected more than 290 WASH infrastructures) along with the fiscal and socioeconomic consequences of these factors.According to the WASH Cluster partners’ assessments during July-Sep 2021 for vulnerable communities, health centres and schools, four main key issues were identified as the key needs for WASH assistance: insufficient water supply to households, poor sanitation and solid waste services, limited WASH services to public facilities, and risk of flooding. Due to the limited capacities of the local authorities and the Israeli restrictions on the entry of materials, the WASH service providers are still struggling to fully restore and maintain the functionality of WASH services in the Gaza Strip. As a result, several communities still do not have wastewater networks and rely totally on poorly managed cesspits, and others are located in the depression zone that are vulnerable to stormwater and/or wastewater flooding.The WASH cluster members conducted a vulnerability needs assessment for 125 Health Care Facilities (HCFs) during Aug-Sep 2021, and for 265 schools in Sep 2021 in Gaza Strip. The assessments concluded that 145 schools and 17 HCFs (according to HNO 2022) are suffering from insufficient WASH facilities (primarily toilets, drinking and handwashing stations) to meet the standards set up by the Ministries of Education and Health. This results in overcrowding the existing facilities and undermining hygiene practices, all of which raise particular concerns during the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic. Additionally, AAH conducted 7 FGDs with technical and medical staff working in 6 hospitals in 2021 with 117 medical staff members, the study has shown that the absence/shortage of hot water supplies has further stressed the health system, leaving staff and patients to find individual level solutions to cover the gap in hot water, such as relying on 2 liter kettles and most critically, patients are sometimes forced to use cold water due to this shortage, even during winters. In addition to the insufficient domestic water supply due to deteriorated water supply networks that are affecting both quantity and quality access to water, and finally the poor status of the sanitation facilities, is adding an additional layer of stress on both medical staff and patients to cope with the weak adaptations of these facilities and limited number of them compared to the high needs.AAH’s WASH in health qualitative assessment in March 2021 using the WASH FIT tool to assess 6 public hospitals, showed that the hospitals are lacking adequate toilets that meet the privacy, safety and dignity needs for both genders, particularly children, women, elderlies and people with disabilities, while the poor status of the sanitation facilities is adding additional layer of stress on both medical staff and patients to cope. There are also a limited number, insufficient domestic water supplies, and a shortage of hot water supplies has further stressed the health system, and its staff. AAH’s gender sensitive assessment in January 2021, covering 97 governmental schools, showed a lack of sufficient quantities and poor quality of domestic water. 27% of the school principals reported issues in either the access to sufficient quantities of water, or the quality of domestic water, in addition to the lack of privacy and safety in the schools’ sanitation facilities (100% of the female teachers do not feel safe while using the schools’ sanitation facilities mainly the toilets, and neither do the female students, a female teacher from East Gaza Al Nasera school said, “during all my work period I never used the toilets, the only time I needed to use them I had to go home to use the bathroom then came back to the school”. The poor status of these facilities is impacting the students’ hygiene and their ability to maintain adequate hygiene practices inside the schools as reported by 82% of the school principals, forcing both male and female students and teachers to adopt negative coping mechanisms, mainly in light of the COVID-19 pandemic, lack of hygiene and cleaning materials, insufficient number of sanitation units for students or teachers and 27% of the targeted schools are still using cesspits for sewage discharge.It was concluded that the deprivation of access to WASH and health services, in particular the lack of sufficient water, poor and deteriorated domestic and drinking water systems, lack of wastewater and stormwater drainage systems, and poor hygiene situation inside hospital and schools is adding to the poor human practices and community attitudes towards the WASH facility.This situation will affect people on different levels of vulnerability, particularly children, women, the elderly and people with disabilities (the users of public facilities (hospitals, schools, HFCs), who have poor access to domestic water and being unprotected from wastewater/stormwater floods due to the poor or deteriorated WASH infrastructure and services, at households and community levels.The constructed/rehabilitated wastewater and stormwater drainage systems, based on the existing storm water master plan developed by AAH in 2018 and that will be updated in 2022, takes into consideration the existing status of war’s affected networks, that will serve the vulnerable communities and support public WASH facilities (schools and HCFs) and shall improve the domestic water systems, hot water systems, drinking water, sanitary and wastewater networks and accessibility in schools, hospitals and level #4 HCFs (the highest MoH’s priority) and will overcome the serious public health hazards and negatively impacts on the environment.

2 - Activities

During the interventions planning phase, AAH will develop the communication with communities plan to ensure proper community participation within the targeted communities through relevant and accessible channels and various community engagement activities such as introductory/information meetings to present objectives, activities and the Feedback and Complaint Mechanism to the beneficiaries, community representatives, municipalities, and stakeholders, the Focus group discussions (FGDs) to address the needs of men, women, boys, girls, elderly and PWDs which will allow the team to collect dimensions, gaps and needs that are relevant for different groups. Follow-up interviews especially with schools, HHs and community representatives during the activities will be undertaken to ensure that the beneficiaries will receive adequate information and communication. Field visits to schools, HHs and community activities to allow the MEAL/project team to collect timely feedback, and finally the active feedback and complaint mechanism with various communication channels such as hotline and field visits. As a result, AAH interventions will its objectives through the following activities that will target in total of 295,490 individuals representing 75,635 boys, 70,544 girls, 72,788 women and 76,523 men: Activity 1: Access to WASH services in institutionsActivity 1.1: The construction/ rehabilitation of WASH facilities in HCFs (handwashing, latrines, sewer system) targeting 2 hospitals (in North Gaza and Gaza) and 3 public HCFs of level 4 in Gaza. The number of beneficiaries is 195,625 individuals representing (47,369 boys, 46,647 girls, 49,482 women and 52,127 men).Activity 1.2: The construction/ rehabilitation of WASH facilities in schools (handwashing, latrines, sewer system) to conduct maintenance works for water and sanitation facilities in 10 schools (3 in Gaza, 4 in Khan Yunis and 3 in Rafah) and construction of 2 sanitation toilets blocks in 2 schools (in Gaza and Dier al Balah) to overcome crowdedness, respect gender needs and privacy in both boys and girls schools. The number of beneficiaries is 8,888 of individuals representing (6,431 boys and 2,457 girls) Activity 2: Access to sanitation and hygieneActivity 2.1: The installation/extension/rehabilitation of sewer collection network and HH connectionsTo connect households in three localities (Maan, Jawrat alluwt and Al-Star) of Khan Yunis city and to close of 1500 cesspits used by families in the targeted localities to discharge their wastewater. The estimated length of wastewater networks is about 11.7 km. The number of beneficiaries is 38,004 individuals representing (9,113 boys, 9,002 girls, 9,729 women and 10,160 men). Activity 3: Floods mitigation and preventionActivity 3.1: The construction/ rehabilitation of storm water network/ rainwater drainage systems in Gaza Strip targeting three localities in North Gaza (2 in Jabalia and 1 in Biet Lahia). The estimated length of stormwater networks is about 3.25 km. The number of beneficiaries is 52,973 individuals representing (12,722 boys,12,438 girls, 13,577 women and 14,236 men). In parallel, AAH in 2022, will update the stormwater master developed with PWA during 2013-2018, in cooperation with WASH stakeholders to identify and prioritize the flooded areas. This will enable the stakeholders to better plan their responds and interventions. Activity 4: Hygiene Promotion Awareness and community initiativesA needs assessment will be carried out in schools, hospitals and HFCs in coordination with MoH and MoEHE. Based on the findings and with the participation of communities’ representatives, the contents of the awareness sessions in schools and HCFs and the IEC material will be agreed upon. AAH, in coordination with HP working group, MoH, MoEHE and the solid waste council will review and validate the contents of the messages that will cover solid waste and medical solid waste in HCFs, COVID-19 mitigation and protection measures, improve hygiene knowledge and practices of students in schools and at HH levels. These messages will be disseminated in the IEC material, sessions and home visits. Also, AAH will ensure the communities participation by conducting 30 initiatives within targeted communities (considering distribution of dignity kits in female schools) to enhance the hygiene good knowledge and practices of women, men, boys and girls. 18,000 individuals representing (7,093 boys,4,710 girls, 3,080 women and 3,117 men).

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries alike will be able to reach out to the project with complaints, suggestions, and any other feedback through the mechanism managed by AAH MEAL Unit. AAH actively promote the existence of its feedback mechanisms through information sessions and information material (e.g., flyer) handed over during projects’ activities (e.g. assessments, distribution, training session etc.). Our programs maintain fluid communication with all rights holders regularly and extensively throughout the project management cycle. Our team consults and meets with rights holders (including different groups women, men, boys, girls and PWDs) when carrying out needs assessments in relevant beneficiary areas and employs a series of methods to ensure that their perspectives and priorities are addressed in the project design. These methods used to receive feedback include interviews with key informants, home visits, group discussions (taking into account gender, conflict, and age sensitive tools), questionnaires and regular monitoring sessions to ensure that the project is appropriate and relevant to their needs. The information collected through complaints and feedback mechanism or directly in the field will be regularly fed into our programming to ensure no harm is done and to guarantee continuous improvements of assistance delivered respecting internal humanitarian standards, including informing the budget. Beneficiary feedback will be analysed and presented in a systematic way, including to project partners that we work with. Learnings following this feedback can often be used across projects and we make sure this is shared among relevant stakeholders so that complaints are taken seriously and The AAH MEAL unit will be in continuous contact with the Program teams to analyse the data, address concerns, respond to complaints and adjust the project. AAH MEAL unit produces a report with an anonymous and gender sensitive analysis regularly for internal learning.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

AAH pays particular attention to the way vulnerable groups are included in our work. Information on the feedback and complaints mechanism is given to every beneficiary individually when distributing assistance at household level and through community sessions. To ensure that our beneficiary feedback mechanisms are accessible to all, AAH have a variety of different methods so that people can choose which they prefer (e.g. email, phone, field, office) but AAH promotes mainly the use of a hotline to give feedback and complaints to avoid having vulnerable population spending time and money in coming to AAH offices. Particularly for vulnerable groups, we make allowances to gender, age, disability, and those that would prefer to stay anonymous. To accommodate and cater to some of these different groups, home visits or private spaces can be provided, to investigate and discuss a complaint received. AAH team composed of gender balanced team to maintain BNFs’ privacy and feeling of safety while sharing feedback.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Through the whole project cycle AAH ensures protection mainstreaming and Do No Harm. AAH considers safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability as well as participation and empowerment of paramount importance. AAH has mechanisms in place to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse by all staff. AAH has in place focal points at base and mission levels, as well as safe and confidential ways to report abuse. All AAH staff have the obligation to adhere to the organisation code of conduct and our held accountable if they do not respect it. AAH has made use of cluster specific Protection and Gender Mainstreaming Tip Sheets, along with learnings from various interventions and external evaluations. Overall AAH’s aim is to take decisive humanitarian action with attention to risk mitigation thereby avoiding doing harm to the greatest extent possible. Risks assessments and analysis of the protection context are undertaken for all the projects, and even more so when implemented with at risk groups (e.g. minors) or in areas where risk of doing harm is exacerbated (e.g. Area C in the West Bank or inside health facilities). In order to inform its assessments and implementation AAH also continuously works on understanding gender roles and how its interventions might impact them without aggravating existing power relations between genders. By understanding gender roles and household dynamics, AAH believes that the organization mitigates the risks of doing harm. In 2019, AAH conducted a masculinity study in both Gaza and the West Bank, which focused on identities/roles of men in order to fully understand men, women, girls and boys and gender dynamics. As part of AAH accountability to affected populations, a feedback mechanism emphasizing fluid and transparent communication will be in place. COVID-19 protective measures and equipment will be applied throughout all interventions to further prevent harm being done to beneficiaries, communities and staff members.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

AAH upholds the humanitarian principles of Humanity, Impartiality, Neutrality, and Independence. During the course of the project, AAH will ensure all projects are easily accessible to those most at need, avoiding risks to the beneficiaries. AAH is committed to operate in an ethical manner and on a programmatic risk mitigation strategy for risk of fraud, waste, and abuse within the proposed activities. At a global level, AAH relies on the organizational Policy on Anti-Bribery, Corruption and Abuse of Power and Code of Conduct. Organizational whistleblowing mechanisms and procedure are in place, `ZERO TOLERANCE ́ towards fraud, bribery or corruption is applied. Reporting mechanisms have also been implemented to report these behaviours to headquarters. Ethical Risks related to any kind of abuse (sexual, power, etc...) are mitigated by providing compulsory briefing to each new staff member on ethical policies. AAH Code of Conduct is aligned with IASC principles. AAH has a Policy for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and in 2019, AAH conducted in its Middle East country offices a Protection Mainstreaming Internal Evaluation for which the consultant spent March in oPt. Particular attention was paid to the way AAH includes vulnerable groups in its work, for instance through inclusive participatory assessments. As one of AAH’s core values, AAH thrives to prevent discrimination and to ensure equal and impartial access though transparency and accountability to affected populations. AAH aims for all community members to be involved and consulted as much as possible in project design and implementation, as well as evaluation, including through household and community consultations and focus group discussions, accessible to all. In order for their participation to be meaningful, AAH focuses on ‘qualitative inclusion’ meaning safe spaces for beneficiaries to participate. AAH is reporting under the HRP disaggregated data by sex and age; though pending the reporting format, AAH usually includes further disaggregation on vulnerability such as data on people with disabilities. Random checks will be carried out by the MEAL team members to ensure transparency, neutrality and objectivity and spot potential bias or incongruency.
