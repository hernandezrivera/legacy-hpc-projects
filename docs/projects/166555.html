Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in Zimbabwe is deteriorating, with the economic crisis (rising food prices, under-employment, high unemployment among the economically active group) compounding the impacts of erratic rainfall which has been characterised by recurrent El Nino-induced droughts and the recent Cyclone Idai. The 2018/19 agricultural season was severely affected by these weather phenomena resulting in delayed start of the season which was followed by prolonged dry spell. The Eastern and parts of Southern parts of Zimbabwe was affected by Cyclone Idai. About 45% of the rural population is in Crisis or Emergency (IPC Phase 3 and 4) while 29% is Stressed (IPC Phase 2). This is a deterioration from the 2019 IPC analysis, when 38% of the total population was in IPC Phase 3 and higher. The increase in the number of acutely food insecure population is primarily due to the lean season expected to extend until June. More households are likely to face food security challenges. These challenges are likely to be worsened by the current COVID19 pandemic that has forced the nation to be in lockdown hence reducing people’s movements and their activities in their various livelihood activities. Zimbabwe recorded its first case of COVID-19 on 20 March and had 48 confirmed cases by 20 May, including four deaths (all with co-morbidities). Of the 10 provinces in Zimbabwe, seven (Bulawayo, Harare, Masvingo, Matabeleland North, Mashonaland East, Mashonaland West and Midlands) have confirmed COVID-19 transmission as of 20 May 2020. Some of the new cases being recorded are coming from returning residents coming from neighbouring countries like South Africa and Botswana. Over 8,000 Zimbabwean returning migrants are expected from neighbouring countries, mainly South Africa, Botswana, Zambia and Mozambique. This number is expected to increase in the coming months due to the socio-economic impact of COVID-19, creating additional pressure on already vulnerable communities.
The prospect of significant deterioration in the health and nutritional status of the communities cannot be ruled out unless support to these communities is delivered. The results of the 2019 rapid ZimVAC rural assessment showed that malnutrition rates are still high with 8 rural districts having a GAM rate above the emergency threshold. The nutritional outcomes are expected to deteriorate as households continue to adopt negative coping strategies affecting dietary diversity. Women and children particularly girls are disproportionately affected by as they are the first to drop from school and assist bin seeking food, women carry double burden of feeding the family and caring for the children at home especially in time of crisis like the current COVID19 pandemic, drought and the recent cyclone idai disasters.

In response to this emergency, Save the Children proposes to implement high impact nutrition interventions in 12 Districts across the 7 provinces thus Binga, Kariba, Gokwe South, Wedza, Chimanimani, Chipinge, Bulawayo, Shamva, Mt Darwin, Mazowe, Rushinga and Hurungwe districts. The project will target the most vulnerable population thus the children under the age of five, pregnant and lactating women.
The project aims to save the lives of under-five children with acute malnutrition through improved multi-sectoral coordination and integrated emergency response. This will be realized through provision of leadership for a coordinated nutrition and COVID19 response, capacity building of health workers and community volunteers on COVID19 prevention and management, mass screening for early case identification of moderate and severe acute malnutrition, IYCF, strengthening of mother to mother support groups and supportive mentorship of health workers within the 12 selected districts for a period of 12 months. Systems will be developed in child safeguarding and PSEA and the programme will be connected to referral pathways.
