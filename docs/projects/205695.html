Project Summary

To safeguard continued access to quality and protective education across crisis-affected communities of Nagero (Western Equatoria), Raja and Jur River (Western Bahr El Ghazal), Pochalla (Jonglei) and Abyei (Abyei Administrative Area). Nagero experienced insurgencies which came as a result of the inter-community crisis and the impact of the conflict led to the displacements of men, women and children to the neighbouring counties however the miscalculation affected learning in the areas. Abyei has been experiencing antagonism between Dinka Ngok and the Masariya in the North and the Twic to the South, these have been a threat. February 2023 attacks from both sides led to displacement, schools destroyed and scholastic materials In addition flood also affected the education sector in Abyei
This project will support 8,578 children (4,031 boys and 4,527 girls) school-going, and 180 adults (149 females and 31 males) inclusive of teachers and school management committee members. The 12-month project will support all the 3 cluster-specific objectives. By providing learning spaces and gender-segregated latrines, the project will contribute to increasing access to education opportunities for out-of-school and at-risk children, support teacher training, ensure learning educational materials, facilitate recreation activities among other participatory initiatives for children dedicated to relieving psychological distress, boost school governance systems as well as county education coordination and information flow. Hold the Child will work in collaboration with other education players as well as other sectors to ensure key message inter-linkages and collectively contribute to making communities safer for the children.

1. Needs

The ongoing conflict in Sudan has forced more than 40,000 school children to flee into South Sudan, a United Nations report said (July 26.2023) according to the report, south Sudanese returnees in unity are predominantly vulnerable groups of women and children who arrived with physical injuries and reported they were harassed and looted by armed groups during their journey,South Sudan has the highest proportion of out-of-school children in the world. High levels of poverty, years of conflict, the impact of COVID-19, inter –communal and recurrent climate-related crises have left 2.8 million girls and boys in South Sudan without access to education (an increase from 2.2 million in 2018). The under-supported Tumbura displacements into Nagero are going without scholastic activities. In Pochalla due to flooding schools have been submerged and learning has been halted in Obudi, Pochalla, Ajwara, Ojangbai Payams (Sept IRNA report). Following a series of clashes in Abyei only 17 out of 48 schools re-opened in April, a third of school age going children did not return to school; and enrolment data indicates an average of 1,200 leaners per school (the highest value record at the education cluster). Across these locations school latrines have collapsed, open defecation is visible, and more drop-outs especially girls on the top of the record highs learners are challenged with a lack of scholastic materials and limited numbers of trained and volunteer teachers Abyei is a case in point. The project is therefore designed to address needs including • Limited or lack of learning spaces and WASH facilities due to collapses under floods and vandalization during clashes/occupation of IDPs • High drop-out rates, and low enrolments in some locations example Nagero due to distances, and local ignorance among other social economic barriers • High learner-teacher ratios, a trend characterized by a lack of trained teachers, constrained remunerations • Distress among learners (girls and boys) due to displacements and associated life experiences including neglect, abuse etc. • Weak or no school governance structures such as the SMC to ensure effective handling of existing school resources and direct engagement of communities in school business among measures to ensure higher enrolment and retention rates • Last of scholastic materials and the need for inclusive lifesaving key messages in collaboration with other sectors to ensure protective learning environments for boys and girls; and strengthened education sector information flow from project counties

2. Project activities

The project will majorly focus on formal education as per the following outlined activities♣ Establish temporary learning spaces/classrooms in areas with high IDP populations ♣ Rehabilitate schools (earning spaces and classrooms) formerly destroyed by floods ♣ Establish gender segregated latrines and hand washing points in IDP hosting communities, and renovate flood-affected units ♣ Distribute or provide sanitation and hygiene materials (hand washing soap, sanitisers, masks, etc.) and PPE for students and teachers ♣ Conduct teacher pieces of training on EiE pedagogical skills and child-centred methodologies to improve teaching and learning process for emergency-affected children. ♣ Conduct teacher training on crosscutting themes including PSS (MHPSS), CP, and referral mechanisms including CP/GBV, health and nutrition ♣ Conduct stakeholder inclusive ‘back to school campaign’ to ensure that school-age going boys and girls are enrolled children in Pre-primary, Primary and Secondary schools/learning centres. ♣ Provide teaching and learning materials for ECD and primary schools ♣ Provide recreational materials and promote learner participatory (peer-peer) PSS approaches ♣ Facilitate school-based key message dissemination through clubs and co-curricular activities; thematic message areas to include PSS (MHPSS), CP, and referral mechanisms including CP/GBV, health and nutrition ♣ Provide life-skills and basic vocational training to youth and adolescents ♣ Participate in the national education cluster coordination activities, ensure 5Ws reporting, and participate in County/Payam level needs assessments (for education cluster). ♣ Provide technical assistance to county education authorities, facilitate local coordination, and improve information flow with state and national stakeholders ♣ Conduct training for SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management, governance, psychosocial support (PSS) and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR)/ climate change Support prevention and mitigation of infectious diseases through training school communities by providing appropriate Information and Education and Communication (IEC) materials (i.e. posters, leaflets)
