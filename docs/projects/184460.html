Project Summary


Conflict and environmental shocks have been the primary drivers of humanitarian needs in Somalia, leaving girls and boys out of school. According to the Protection Return Monitoring Network (PRMN), 593,000 people were displaced in Somalia between January and September 2021, of which 420,000 were displaced due to conflict and 98,000 due to drought1. Of the people displaced this year, an estimated 237,200 (40%) are school-aged children. In total, there is about 2.9 million displaced people in Somalia and an estimated 1 million of these are school-aged children.
FCA rapid needs assessment (10/21) highlighted the challenges faced by the assessed schools in baidoa and Dinsoor which include lack of accessible and gender friendly structures such as chairs, desks, classrooms, latrines and handwashing facilities, accompanied by shortage of learning and teaching materials such as books, pens and chalks. Lack of qualified and inadequate number of teachers, especially female teachers as well as teachers who are trained in special education needs to handle children with CWDs and negative cultural norms have contributed to the low enrolment, attendance and retention of girls and CwD in schools. The barriers faced by CwD are not limited to those faced by girls, but their enrolment, retention and movement to education are hindered by additional stigma, discrimination and misconceptions. Lack of adequate fencing of the school compound to promote protection and safety and long distances to school cause safety concerns particularly for girls. EiE actors require capacity to provide quality education to CwD. Caregivers also faced with challenges in covering school fees, uniforms and scholastic materials. Strengthening the involvement of community in school management activities and mainstreaming child protection and child safeguarding mechanisms into education will improve access to education for girls and CwD.
Aligning with the 2022 Somalia Humanitarian Response Plan, the proposed initiative aims to restore and maintain access to and retention of crisis-affected school children to safe, protective, inclusive and continuous learning opportunities in Bay region of South West State of Somalia. The project will put a strong emphasis on an integrated approach to education focusing on access, quality of education, protection, child safeguarding, gender equality, conflict sensitivity, and disaster risk reduction (DRR) in the design, approach and activities.
The proposed project will benefit 7100, crisis-affected children (4,400 children in Baidoa and 2,700 children in Dinsor) with at least 45% of girls. The vulnerable poor host community children (40%) will also be targeted since they are also affected by the crisis and have limited access to quality education. Protection and psychosocial well-being of the affected boys and girls as well as teachers is central to the project. FCA will closely work with Somalia Institute for Special Education Needs and Disability (SISEND), the only disability NGO in Somalia that specializes in special education needs, to provide capacity building to teachers, MoE and other education personnel.
FCA has excellent capacity to scale up, applying best practices and lessons learned from its EiE experiences in other districts of South West State (Baidoa, El-berde and Hudur) to Dinsoor.. Utilizing FCA integrated and innovative approach based on well-known Finnish education system, FCA will take into account the potential impact of Covid-19 pandemic and develop innovative solutions to ensure uninterrupted access to education by the target children.
The proposed project will be implemented in Baidoa and Dinsoor where there are high number of IDP and are in IPC 3. The implementation will be done in close partnership with GREDO; a local NGO that is currently partnering with FCA on the ECHO funded EiE Projects. The project will also be implemented in close collaboration with MoE of Southwest State.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) provided to target population affected by crises and emergency has become the most relevant, practical and innovative solutions that could timely address the acute needs of the affected population since such a response could reach the affected groups faster and in the most efficient way. In addition, rather than substituting markets through provision of free goods, integrating mobile cash assistance in EiE response will also support local markets to recover from crisis through the injection of the capital hence improving the local economy. For the proposed intervention, FCA will use conditional mobile cash assistance to transfer the money for the caregivers of all enrolled children in the target schools to ease their financial burden by covering costs for school uniforms and scholastic materialsThe one-off mobile cash transfer on school uniforms and scholastic materials will enable the caregivers to enrol their children especially girls and CWD in schools. The caregivers will have the opportunity to select their own suppliers in the local market and timely purchase the materials for their children, enabling fast and smooth enrolment and attendance in schools. This process is more efficient than in-kind distribution and voucher distribution, since the procurement process may take time to respond to immediate shocks and crisis. Vulnerability criteria and selection procedure will be thoroughly developed at the beginning of the project, together with MoE, school administration, camp managers and other actors, based on the agreed vulnerability criteria. The actual selection will be done through a thorough process, which will involve a comprehensive assessment of the target beneficiaries with relevant actors. CECs will also be engaged in the formulation of criteria, selection and monitoring processes to ensure ownership, transparency and accountability. Following the initial assessment, FCA’s Senior Project Manager and Education Officer, and GREDO field officers in each district will verify results and individually assess HHs selected for the transfer. The beneficiary list with detailed information on HHs including marital status, age groups, sexes, disability/malnutrition condition and previous history in receiving humanitarian assistance will be compiled and shared with other relevant stakeholders before operation. Tracking forms indicating status of enrolment and attendance of their children in schools on monthly basis will also be developed and closely monitored. The cash assistance for uniforms and scholastic material will be done through a one-off transfer. The post distribution monitoring will be done by FCA MEAL team in Baidoa and Mogadishu as well as GREDO field team. The project will ensure alignment with cash transfer tools and protocols implemented by the Cash Working Group in Baidoa. Complaints Response Mechanisms will be set up at different levels to manage potential complaints or reports on misuse of the money and corruptions. The independent investigation will be carried out for any reported case. Necessary actions and measures will be taken to address the issues and the issues will be reported to the donor.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

Selection of mobile cash transfer is based on FCA ongoing Cash and Voucher Assistance in EiE projects funded by MFA Finland in Baidoa and ECHO HIP 2020 and 2021 in Baidoa, Elberde and Hudur of SWS. Following years of implementation in CVA in EiE, FCA has acquired solid expertise and experience in implementing CVA with thorough monitoring and accountability mechanism in provision of effective cash support. Mobile cash transfer is the selected modality in CVA response in this project. The selection was informed by the outcomes of the market assessment carried out by FCA in Baidoa Hudur, Dinsoor, Wajid and Elberde of SWS in September and October 2021. The assessment aimed to assess market, its functionality, and feasibility of mobile cash transfer in relation to EiE response in the areas. During the assessment, Hormud mobile phone operator in Somalia was consulted about the feasibility of mobile cash transfer in the districts. FCA also met with other actors such as NRC and Save the Children who have implemented cash based interventions to learn about their experiences. FCA is also part of the Education Cluster's Joint Monitoring Market Initiative on CVA in education sector in Hudur, El-berde and Dinsoor. The JMMI is carried out every 4 months and provides insightful information on the functionality of the markets and price list of education materials in each target location.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

The project anticipates two highest risks during the implementation of the proposed activities. Mitigation measures are carefully designed and included in project cycle to address the risks and effectively deliver the project1. Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance- FCA will actively engage with camp managers, local leaders, CECs and local communities to assess the potential risks of sexual violence in the target area.- FCA will conduct advocacy and awareness raising on sexual violence through active involvement of the traditional leaders from different clans/community groups on the right of women and girls, especially girls with CWD to promote prevention of sexual abuse - ‘FCA will promote Do No Harm’ approach ‘in all of its project activities by taking all measures necessary to avoid exposing people to further harm as a result of our actions by continuously training staff and partner involved in the response, this helps the implementing team timely assess and identify potential risks- FCA will further promote gender equality in all its project activities - FCA will work with others in the area to promotes the non-discriminatory inclusion of PoC especially women and girls in other sectors services such as health, livelihood education, and wash , and where necessary- FCA will work with the local authorities by advocating and providing technical guidance and support for the inclusion and non-discriminatory access for PoC especially women and girls within the state in accordance with international standards.- FCA will work with local authority esp. the police to enforce law for the perpetrators of sexual violence so that they face the rule of law. 2. Risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure and/or contamination by explosive hazards in targeted project area- FCA will engage with the camp managers and local communities to timely access information on evictions. - FCA will also build capacity of CEC members and school administration on how to manage education facilities during evictions.- FCA will work with other actors such DDG and UN mine action to support on issues of explosive hazards in the target area for possible dangers - The project will work with local authorities and landowners to negotiate for appropriate notice periods for evictions to enable children to complete learning during the academic year.- FCA will closely work with MoE of SWS and respective local authorities to find most suitable mitigation measures for any potential eviction risks
