Project Summary

Mercy Corps (MC) will respond to priority needs with life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI assistance across the governorates of Al-Hasakeh, Aleppo, Ar-Raqqa, Deir-ez-Zor and Idleb. Mercy Corps is a cash first agency and estimates 25% of the programme will be delivered through cash and vouchers.
MC will improve access to dignified shelter through rehabilitation of unsafe shelters. MC will take into account home ownership along with vulnerability criteria to ensure HLP rights are considered, and vulnerable HHs are not further displaced or taken advantage of after receiving assistance. Shelter interventions will focus on small scale rehabilitation upgrades, including repairs and measures to enhance security and privacy, reaching 15,840. MC will further provide NFI and/or winterisation kits when needs arise to 88,913 participants. This may include: Winter kits, Clothing Kits, Hygiene Kits, Dignity Kits, New Arrival Kits, Infant Kits and Basic Shelter Kits. Through these interventions Mercy Corps will reach 216,005 participants.

4 - Needs

Over the course of the crisis, shelter and infrastructure damage has occurred on a massive scale, with the scale of rebuilding and reconstruction needed which goes beyond the capacity and remit of the Shelter & NFI sector and the humanitarian response in general. HNO 2019 highlighted that shelter situation in Syria continues to worsen, in terms of the available shelter capacity, the adequacy of shelter conditions. Some of the displaced people are hosted by family and friends, some pay rent, other resort to illegal occupation of abandoned buildings. Around 4.7 million people are in need of shelter due to inadequate conditions in existing accommodation, and the overall lack of available, adequate and affordable shelters. The rent cost has a great impact in the displaced families’ depleted financial resources. Lack of income opportunities aggravate the livelihood and housing situation. The uncertainty around the housing situation exacerbate the fragility of the families’ structure already challenged by years of conflict. Displacement and impoverishment put families in need of support of critical seasonal items (winter clothing, fuel and blankets) especially for the out of camp populations where access to humanitarian assistance is discontinued. As a result of the recent hostilities in In North West Syria, the OCHA February situation report, covering the period from December 2019 to 2 February 2020, stated that over half a million people were displaced. Many of them have been displaced many times throughout the Syria crisis, eroding the resilience of families and communities. The high levels of displacement has immensely increase the number of people seeking shelter in IDP sites, where overcrowding has been reported as a growing concern, with some sites accommodating more than 400 per cent above their intended capacities. Displaced families have reported lack of access to electricity and overcrowding as their main concerns, exposing them to potential protection risks.

5 - Implementation of activities

1.1 Provision of core and essential NFIs (e.g. mattress/mat, plastic sheet, blankets, jerry can, kitchen set, solar lamps, fuel; including cash/voucher for these items): MC will provide one-off, in-kind assistance to 200 newly displaced HHs (1,200 individuals) within 72 hours of displacement (access and availability permitting). In NWS, all the selected HHs will receive a kit comprised of: 1) new arrival kit; 2) hygiene kit; and 3) kitchen set. The composition of the kits is in line with SNFI cluster guidance and take into consideration age and gender considerations. 1.2 Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs and shelter assistance (e.g. winter clothing, fuel, winter-specific shelter upgrades; including cash/voucher for these items): MC will provide winter kits comprising of mattresses, blankets, floor mats, tarpaulin and a solar lamp, in line SNFI cluster guidance. MC will also provide winter clothing., taking into consideration age and gender. 1.3 Provision of life-saving and life-sustaining shelter: MC will provide emergency shelter kits to recently displaced HHs and/or protracted displaced HHs living in informal settlements. The emergency shelter kit will answer acute shelter needs - either first phase response 1.4 Rehabilitation/upgrading of Life-saving and life-sustaining shelter spaces in collective centres, unfinished buildings, spontaneous settlements, and/or other emergency spaces (including: in-kind, cash, voucher, physical repair, etc.) and 1.5 Support to sustainably repair/rehabilitate housing and related community/public infrastructure and facilities to owners/tenants/host families (can include: materials, cash, voucher, cash-for-work, labour, etc.): MC will assist HHs with small scale rehabilitation upgrades for individual shelters, informal settlements and collective shelters with proper HLP.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Risk of inter or intra-community tension due to service delivery locations or types of services offered if one community is served and a neighboring community not served. Protection risk faced by girls and boys may persist if community members do not play an active role in the delivery of protection services. Risk of existing traditional and religious practices increasing due to limited community engagement or community acknowledgement of certain traditional and religious practices as a protection concern. Risk of girls and boys exposure to protection risk due to the limited initiatives to enhance children's participation in their own protection. Mitigation Strategy: To mitigate these risks, Mercy Corps will integrate findings from conflict analysis conducted in project communities to have a full understanding of how to approach service delivery and targeting. Mercy Corps will carry out a stakeholder’s analysis as well as service mapping and will take steps to informed communities about available services in their communities while ensuring adequate consultation and engagement with the communities. Transparent targeting criteria in consultation with community stakeholders will be developed.Risk: Girls, boys, women and men’s physical security is put at risk as a result of the violence associated with civilian populations/spaces being targeted by armed groups; a lack of consideration for the safety and security to access/receive services; lack of consideration of contextual information (e.g. aerial bombardment) and targeting of civilian infrastructure enroute to, during and directly after service provision.Mitigation Strategy: Mercy Corps will ensure to conduct a comprehensive community risks mapping involving all stakeholders most especially children and adolescents. Mapping of risky zones in the communities will better inform Mercy Corps and provide the opportunity to conduct activities in areas considered safe by the communities.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks and Mitigating Measures: Community tension over aid distributions: Provide clear participation targeting criteria, and original selection is done with the Local CouncilAir strikes or shelling: Mercy Corps has a Security Management Plan in place that is regularly updated. Operational Indicators are also established to guide decision-making that facilitates prepared contingencies.Fraud and theft of cash transfers: Mercy Corps revised its Cash Programing SOPs in December 2019 to ensure financial controls and oversight of the full cash transfer activity remains adapted and relevant to the North Syria management contextDelay in staff deployment for response: Mercy Corps already has staff on the ground ready to scale up the response for the newly-displaced families Failure of UN Security Council cross-border resolution to be re-approved: Cash programing does not rely on the operation of the cross-border mechanism.Interference from designated group: Mercy Corps has a dedicated Humanitarian Access team on the ground, who are able to coordinate with the Salvation Government directly. They also receive any direct requests from armed factions in order to prevent the Program or Monitoring and Evaluation teams facing direct exposure to armed groups or individuals. Assumptions:1. Average Household Size in the North Syria is six;2. An enabling political and security situation in North Syria will allow program staff to reach beneficiaries;3. Financial institutions and cash liquidity will remain functional and available;4. No further restrictions will be applied to the movement of cash into North Syria;5. Needed essential needs remain readily available in local markets and prices do not increase significantly during the project timeframe
