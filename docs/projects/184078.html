Project Summary

In line with the prominent sectoral needs described in the section below, ACTED intends to extend and scale-up the humanitarian response for the vulnerable resident families living in Al Fao and Al Mafaza areas of Gedaref State. On this purpose, the Action envisages a comprehensive intervention in WASH sector which aims at improving the access to water and sanitation infrastructures, as well as to hygiene services, while building local capacities through the existing social bodies and WASH Committees and promoting hygiene practices. The Action priorities a conflict-sensitive and a community-based participatory approach in all its phases.
The proposed project is aligned with the three Sudan WASH Cluster objectives in terms of access to water, sanitation hygiene and environmental health by the rehabilitation of water pumps and water yards, and the rehabilitation of emergency shelter latrines. While both activities will be complemented with hygiene promotion and hygiene kits distribution for 2000 HHs
ACTED will embed anti-COVID-19 measures in all the proposal activities. ACTED teams will receive trainings on COVID-19 preventive measures and a full set of PPE stock to be used during project activities. During activities, ACTED will ensure the beneficiaries and ACTED staff follow social distancing measures. Moreover, ACTED will install handwashing stations and provide soaps at training and distributions sites for beneficiaries.

1 - Needs

The proposal takes into account the major WASH gaps detected in Al Fao and al Mafaza areas of Gedaref State and aims at coordinating with the other sectoral partners to integrate the humanitarian response and provide an improved access to WASH services to the most vulnerable communities. ACTED intends to extend the geographical coverage and the population target of the ongoing WASH Programs, and fill in the existing gaps. In line with this approach, the proposed Action acknowledges that 26,612 vulnerable residents in Al Mafaza (22% of the total population) and 87,156 vulnerable residents in Al Fao (24.9% of the total population), are currently in need of WASH assistance, as per WASH HNO 2022 shared by the Cluster. On the basis of 2019 population projection for the target areas, less than 10% of Al Mafaza and Al Fao communities relies on improved water sources, let alone the collection time for a round-trip, while 14,7% people in Al Mafaza and 12,8% in Al Fao are expected to consume surface water, thus facing a catastrophic level of need; and, if only the vulnerable residents are taken into account, the percentages increase to 59 and 51% respectively. Population status is above the critical level also under the Sanitation component, considering that 18,9% of individuals in Al Fao are usually practicing open defecation; the percentage reaches the 75,8% if only the vulnerable HHs are included. In Al Mafaza, almost one fourth of people have no other option than using unimproved sanitation facilities or practising open defecation. Overall, the number of vulnerable individuals having access to improved and not shared sanitation facilities is below the 10% in Al Mafaza and barely above the 5% in Al Fao. Furthermore, HHs in Al Mafaza are facing a Critical to Extreme Phase 4 in terms of hygiene needs, whereas in Al Fao the 24% of the population fall into an extremely Critical/ Catastrophic Phase 5.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The Action will consider the project’s environmental footprint and possible adaptations or mitigations to limit a negative impact. Local resources and expertise as well as a greening approach in procurements of all the Items will be favoured. Overall, the proposal prioritises the rehabilitation of existing WASH infrastructures to the construction of new ones, whenever feasible and without affecting the quality of the intervention. Solarisation will be favoured, when applicable, for water sources and sanitation facilities (i.e., solar lamps). The response will also include sensitisation sessions on WASH-related DDR subjects, to be conducted both at community/local committee level and at HH level.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The Program envisages the specific needs faced by different socio-demographic groups direct and encourages gender-balanced participation of target communities– with a zero-tolerance policy towards discrimination– throughout the Action, which is embedding the “Do No Harm Principle” and follows a conflict-sensitive approach. Preparation Phase:- Before each intervention, ACTED conducts an assessment of the security context and protection risks, followed by a regular monitoring of the situation throughout the project’s lifespan. - The community will be sensitised on the scope and the activities of the project and fully informed on the selection criteria, which have been designed to address the protection risks and cover the sectoral needs of the most vulnerable (single women, PLWS, children, elders, PwDs, HHs with no source of income, HHs with PwDs, HHs with more children under 5 years old). Program Implementation Phase:- ACTED mainstreams gender-adapted and key protection messages during its community sensitization and trainings . - ACTED will ensure easy-to-access and gender-segregated latrines, with lockable doors and solar lamps. Sanitation facilities will be suitable for elders and PwDs. - MHM component included in the proposal. - Distances to fetch water, waiting time, safety during traveling will be addressed through rehabilitations and ad hoc protection measures. - ACTED will set-up a gender-balanced Program team to ensure a safer environment, improve protection mainstreaming and favour inclusionary mechanisms. In all activities, ACTED will promote an equal gender participation. - First aid kits and protective wear are also provided to ACTED’s staff, beneficiaries and daily workers, in line with the type of activities. Accountability:- ACTED has an independent Appraisal, Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU) with experience in setting up Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRMs). A dedicated staff will be responsible to manage the CRM at Area office and Field office levels, where beneficiaries can report complaints/feedback in a safe and confidential way. Beside its staff permanently deployed in the field, ACTED will also conduct regular spot checks. ACTED will evaluate if the complaint was triggered due to flaws, shortcomings or gaps in procedures or implementation strategy. In addition to case-by-case handling, anonymized complaints reports will be shared internally to possibly reshape the ongoing Action and inform the future projects’ design. A more detailed description of the CRM is provided under the AAP section of this proposalHuman Resources - ACTED has a Code of Conduct that is consistent with the U.N. Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Task Force on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse in Humanitarian Crises. ACTED also complies with The Code of Conduct for The International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement and the UNHCR Code of Conduct.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

As part of its accountability strategy, ACTED will mainstream protection and PSEA elements during all activities, particularly trough the safety audit tools, the CRM and the inclusion of PSEA training modules into capacity development action plans for community governance structures. ACTED has appropriate and proportionate procedures and controls in place to prevent, report, detect, respond and report on safeguarding issues & allegations. Recruitment- ACTED prevents employment/deployment of unsuitable individuals through rigorous screening processes. All people employed by ACTED already have the obligation to read and sign the code of conduct and internal policies. - Administration and employee cycle:- Presentation and signature of the Code of Conduct for staff; - Monitoring of trial periods for cases detected,- Disciplinary measures follow-up on cases detected in conjunction with country coordination. Training program related to safeguarding issues. - ACTED has an in-house training program related to safeguarding issues: i) Annual training on the Code of Conduct and Policies in all countries and at headquarters for 7000 staff; ii) Pre-departure training for international employees. Iii) Specific ad-hoc training on SEA & Child Protection in countries of intervention. ACTED Complaints and response mechanisms. - As part of its on-going efforts to improve accountability to beneficiaries, ACTED will establish a beneficiary Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) for beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback in a secure way, and receive a response in a timely manner. Complaint’s investigation & disciplinary measures- ACTED has the capacity to investigate safeguarding issues in an independent and confidential way, while protecting both the alleged victim and the alleged perpetrator (ACTED SEA Policy, Articles 19 to 21; ACTED Child protection policy, Articles 20 to 22). In any case, ACTED must always ensure that the author of a report does not suffer from any harm as a result. ACTED also endeavours to protect victims of prohibited acts (ACTED SEA policy, article 23). ACTED has psychologists available to support individuals who would have been impacted as a result of a failure to comply with ACTED's safeguarding policies. The HQ HR department will assess all allegations to design an investigation plan and select an investigation team. Proactive approach- ACTED aims at improving its gender, age and disability representativeness in its team, to the extent possible. Because sexual exploitation and abuse cases disproportionally affect women and girls, ACTED intends on implementing protection and gender mainstreaming throughout the project implementation.
