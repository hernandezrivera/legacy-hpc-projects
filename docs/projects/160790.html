Project Summary

DRC plans to implement Sexual and Gender Based Violence (SGBV) prevention and response activities in hard-to-reach conflict areas in South Sudan, through static and mobile responses. DRC strives to contribute to the two Protection cluster strategic objectives:
- Provide immediate lifesaving assistance to address priority protection needs for the most vulnerable populations in hard-to-reach areas
- Prevent, mitigate and respond to protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience
DRC will provide SGBV response activities for identified cases, including case management and psychosocial support (PSS). The SGBV response element will follow a survivor-centered approach, following the Inter Agency Standing Committee (IASC) GBV guidelines, and ensuring DRC staff are well trained and qualified to handle sensitive and confidential cases. The prevention activities will focus on promoting community engagement and ownership in addressing sexual and gender-based violence, while ensuring communities are aware of GBV services available. Awareness raising sessions, community activists’ trainings, mass awareness campaigns and information campaigns will be organised to ensure GBV messaging is consistent and effective, as well as community-led. DRC uses the SASA! methodology, developed by Raising Voices, which aims to address one of the root causes of GBV and HIV, the power imbalance between men and women, through community mobilisation and activism. DRC will also engage in advocacy with the GBV sub-cluster at the state-levels, and with the relevant authorities, such as the minister of gender, to ensure buy-in and support from the main and relevant stakeholders. These activities will be linked with the food security and livelihood activities, which will focus on women empowerment and resilience, while engaging with men on improving gender equality.
DRC has static presence in Unity State, operating from Bentiu, and in Upper Nile, operating both in Maban and in Malakal, Aburoc and several locations on the West bank of the Nile. In addition, DRC operates three mobile protection teams, which conduct integrated protection analyses, identifying SGBV risks and issues in hard-to-reach areas and among populations affected by conflict and displacement (targeted locations identified by GBV sub-cluster). In Upper Nile and Unity States, the GBV program will focus on increasing access to services for survivors and setting up safe spaces for women, where they can discuss their concerns, and alleviate stress and trauma through PSS activities. In all target locations, beneficiaries engaging in awareness raising and activism will be selected based on their motivation to support to behavioural change in the power relations between men and women, while survivors will not be sought out but supported based on need and referrals. Through the intervention, DRC intends to ensure women and men are equally sensitised on the issues concerning gender based violence and gender inequality, while providing life-saving assistance to survivors, encouraging help-seeking behaviour, and support to vulnerable men and women. Overall, DRC aim to reach approximately 40,000 vulnerable individuals affected by displacement and conflict, including approximately 6,400 men, 19,200 women, 4,400 boys, and 10,000 girls.

1 - Needs

In Western Bahr El Gazal (WEG), Eastern Equatoria (EE), Upper Nile (UPN), and Unity States (US), both reports and assessments have found that women in these locations are directly targeted for killing, rape, and sexual assault during active conflict, leading to displacement and suffering populations. Traditional and often discriminatory practices, such as bride price and forced marriage, as well as violence within the family, have been exacerbated by years of conflict and instability. For instance, rapid assessments completed by DRC in Koch County have found that although the level of active conflict is reducing, there continues to be a high prevalence of GBV with incidents of early marriage and domestic violence commonplace. Similarly in WEG, the resolved ethnic tensions around Wau town have correlated with incidents of GBV. The gender related impact of the war and displacement is intrinsically linked to traditional gender practices. For example, women tend to suffer more from food insecurity than men; as women are more likely to pass on their food ration to the children, when there is not enough food to feed the entire household. In general, women are also at a high risk of gender-based violence (GBV) due to the significant cultural power imbalance between men and women, which is intensified during conflict Women are often harassed and raped by men, often armed men, while collecting firewood, fetching water, and receiving or harvesting food, which are mainly female-assigned chores. Such instances were observed during the sharp increase in GBV incidents in Unity State in 2019, with women and girls assaulted and raped while they travelled to receive their food assistance. In a September 2019 study, protection actors emphasize that women are disproportionately affected by the humanitarian crisis and are often forced to move, whether return or relocate, as a coping mechanism to survive. Displaced women in South Sudan are particularly vulnerable to GBV, due to the interlinkage of increased exposure to threats while traveling on the road, the widespread presence of armed actors, and reduced coping strategies or mechanisms to protect themselves due to displacement. The compounded vulnerabilities linked to displacement and conflict have also led to women and girls using survival sex as a coping mechanism, and sexual exploitation is a widespread concern. While the signing of the peace agreement by some of the warring parties has overall reduced armed conflict, it has not reduced the presence of armed actors, who in many locations have increased movements, due to reintegration of forces driven by the peace agreement, and who are often unpaid and unsupported. A UN Human Rights report from February 2019 indicates that the large number of fighters on “standby mode” awaiting the R-ARCSS implementation has increased the risk of sexual violence for women and girls traveling on the roads in Northern Unity, particularly in the areas of Guit, Rubkona, and Mayom. The report also states that “impunity has contributed to the normalization of violence against women and girls”, which has become all the more apparent when armed actors sexually assaulted, raped, and gang raped women traveling on the road between Guit and Bentiu between September and December 2019. The types of GBV that occur and affect women and girls predominantly , include unlawful killings, rape, sexual assault, and beating. Emotional abuse, denial of resources and forced marriage are also among types of GBV that occur across the country, according to the GBVIMS reports. Furthermore, while GBV services/actors are present in some areas such as Fashoda, Malakal, Koch, and Rubkona Counties, there is an continued need to expand and scale up service provisions and GBV continues to be widespread and human rights abuses continue to be perpetrated by all parties to the conflict with near impunity.

2 - Activities

DRC proposes to implement a GBV project that aligns with the Project Cluster Strategic Objective (PO) 1 ‘Ensure the provision of critical protection related assistance and specialised services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, boys, and girls in hard to reach areas and priority geographic areas’. The proposed intervention also aligns with PO2 ‘Prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience’. Considering these objectives, DRC proposes to implement the following: SGBV response activities for identified cases, will include case management, PSS, individual and group counselling, and will be combined with FSL activities when possible. The SGBV response component will follow a survivor-centred approach in line with the Inter Agency Standing Committee (IASC) GBV guidelines. The prevention activities will focus on SASA! training, advocacy, and information campaigns promoting community engagement and ownership in addressing SGBV, while ensuring communities are aware of SGBV services available. In all target locations, beneficiaries will be selected based on their motivation to support behavioural change in the power relations between men and women, while survivors will not be sought out but supported based on need and referrals. Through the intervention, DRC intends to ensure women and men are equally sensitised on the issues concerning SGBV and gender inequality, while providing life-saving assistance to survivors, encouraging help-seeking behaviour, and providing support to vulnerable men and women. DRC uses the SASA! methodology, which aims to address one of the root causes of SGBV; the power imbalance between men and women, through community mobilisation and activism. Through SASA!, DRC SGBV teams will train male and female community activists in strategies for de-normalizing cycles of violence and abuse and in community awareness raising. Community activists will be selected based on their willingness to engage in the program and their capacity to conduct outreach activities. DRC’s SGBV prevention officers together with the identified activists will further engage the community to increase their awareness of gender power imbalances, shared power and non-violence. The activities will be community based and focus on empowerment of existing and new community structures, such as women’s networks, community safety committees, and women’s groups. DRC will ensure community-based awareness raising focuses on available services, legal awareness, and GBV prevention messages. Trainings will be provided for humanitarian actors and community structures on GBV mainstreaming and prevention of GBV. In static locations, the GBV program will focus on increasing access to services for survivors and will link with FSL to set up safe spaces for women where they can discuss their concerns and alleviate stress and trauma through PSS activities. Case management will be provided to survivors and individuals at risk of SGBV; some may be referred through referral pathways, while others might seek assistance directly with DRC caseworkers. Case management for survivors will include linking survivors who request legal aid with the available justice mechanisms, as well as providing legal counselling upon need and with consent from the survivor. As part of the case plan, identified women at risk may require livelihood support in order to mitigate the protection risks linked to their vulnerabilities. These activities of skills training, tools and material for income generating activities, and fuel efficient stoves trainings. These activities will be linked with the FSL activities, which will focus on women’s empowerment and resilience, while engaging with men on improving gender equality. DRC will engage in advocacy with the GBV state-level and national-level sub-cluster, and with the relevant authorities, such as the Ministry of Gender.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

8.1. Members of marginalised community’s/minority groups are not included in consultation and/or excluded from humanitarian services/assistance. DRC will carry out sensitisation among its own team members to raise their awareness about the need to engage members from all segments in the consultations/project implementation. DRC will also carry out home to home visits to ensure the inclusion of community members who may be unable to leave their household due to physical or cultural/social restrictions. 8.10. Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance. As was observed in Bentiu, Unity State, late last year, women and girls face an increased risk of sexual violence when accessing humanitarian assistance. Considering this, DRC will ensure protection mainstreaming across its programming and seek to reduce/mitigate protection risks that may be incurred during the course of the project. Examples, of steps that will be taken by DRC include engagement of beneficiary members around selection of the location for the WGFS. 8.4. A lack of access to/awareness of referral services for survivors of protection incidents (GBV, child protection for example): Under this project, DRC proposes to carry out case management for survivors of GBV. DRC will carry out service mapping in locations to map existing service providers such as Healthcare providers. In the event that there are service providers absent, DRC will advocate with other humanitarian stakeholders to increase service provision to ensure that survivors receive a holistic response.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Accountability to the affected population is one of DRCs organisational principles. As part of DRCs adherence to this principle, it actively shares project information with beneficiaries, this ensures that communities do not develop unrealistic expectations about the programme. In addition, DRC also shares information with the target community about protection risk, human rights, and the prevention of sexualexploitation and abuse (PSEA). DRC has an established community feedback and complaint mechanism in place across all it’s programming. To ensure that there are no barriers to access the complaint and feedback mechanisms, DRC operates several mechanisms across one project. For example, DRC currently have a feedback hotline number that beneficiaries can call to register complaints or feedback. DRC also operates complaint and feedback desks at its project locations to enable beneficiaries and wider community members to report issues in person. DRC also measures beneficiary satisfaction with service provision through satisfaction surveys and focus group discussions.
