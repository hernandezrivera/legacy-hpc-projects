Project Summary

It is estimated that over 6.56 million Somali people require lifesaving essential healthcare in 2022. Excess mortality in Somalia is driven primary by malnutrition, disease outbreaks (e.g., COVID-19, AWD/cholera, measles, malaria) and violence; as well as non-communicable disease including complications of pregnancy and chronic disease.
In 2022, overall health needs are likely to remain insufficiently addressed. Health services have eroded to bare minimum levels in many locations as health partners face severe funding gaps. Health service delivery in Somalia remains dependent on humanitarian partners who finance and deliver a large proportion of health care to the population. In the absence of sufficient funding there is a danger of erosion of minimum standards of care of primary health care services. Cholera is endemic and seasonal spikes have been identified during rainy seasons, along riverbanks and in locations with high number of IDPs and where access to clean water and sanitation are limited. Oral cholera vaccination (OCV) in hot spot districts to protect vulnerable population against cholera has not been implemented in 2020 and 2021 as public health response efforts focused on COVID-19. As expected with the low measles vaccination coverage rates measles outbreaks continue to occur. There is an urgent need to increase routine vaccination and implement campaigns to ensure vaccination coverage of those children never vaccinated.
CESVI will deliver lifesaving emergency health services including maternal, neonatal and child health through 7 static Health facilities (Elgula, Galbarwaaqo, Abudwaaq, Elasha, Jazeera, Wadajir, Halane) and 4 mobile health clinics (Hobyo, Beletwayne, Elasha and Jazeera). CESVI has a strong presence in all the target locations implementing long term project in different sectors. This action will address endemic diseases like pneumonia, anemia, AWD and ARI which are the leading causes of infant and child mortality in the target villages. The Action will provide primary and referral health care, with special attention to maternal and child health, including basic emergency obstetric care (BEmONC) and EPI Emphasis will be laid on application of standard treatment guidelines as per EPHS at PHC. Health mobile clinics will serve IDP camps and villages in the far villages to deliver quality primary health care services to populations without a static MCH in close proximity. Moreover, Health facilities will be equipped (after minor rehabilitations) with essential drugs, vaccines and post- rape treatment drugs including medical equipment. Further, this intervention will enhance a referral system from primary to secondary health services and TB services and from nutrition to health services and vice versa. Community Health Workers (CHW) will be engaged to actively act as first responders for health and nutrition issues playing a key role in the referral system ensuring better access to health care services for the population to prevent AWD/cholera epidemic outbreak, hygiene promotion campaigns will be organized regularly throughout the action within the target villages. Cesvi will also integrate surveillance and emergency preparedness ensuring adequate stocks for epidemic outbreaks of AWD/Cholera and measles are prepositioned. Moreover, Personal Protective Equipment (PPEs) will be purchased for frontline Health workers as a measure for infection prevention and control (IPC) at service provision points. The project will also undertake confidential identification of GBV survivors for provision of comprehensive care, CMR and psychosocial support in-line with IASC MHPSS standard. The project will provide trainings targeting thematic areas such ANC and BEmONC, drugs consumption and GBV/Protection mainstreaming among others. Joint supportive supervision will also be undertaken with MoH as per of efforts to build their capacity. Health service is integrated with the nutrition being in the same target locations

Rehabilitation of 7 Health facilities will be undertaken in Hobyo, Wadajiir, Abudwaaq, Banadir and Afgoye health centers. The ﬁxed health facilities will be fully equipped with essential health supplies and trained staff to ensure that the quality of services provided as per the EPHS standards. The mobile clinics will equally be equipped with qualiﬁed personnel (1 qualiﬁed nurse, 1 qualified midwife, 1 auxiliary nurse, 1 vaccinator and drug dispenser). The Health facilities and mobile clinics will provide OPD consultations and treatment of endemic diseases like pneumonia, anemia, acute watery diarrhea and ARI which according to IMCI guidelines, these four diseases are the leading causes of infant and child mortality in the 5 districts. /Objective 4: To Improve case management and referral services survivors of sexual or gender based violence in Hobyo, Beletweyne, Abudwaaq, Wadajiir, Afgoye, Garowe and Bossaso districts of South Central and Puntland, Somalia. Health awareness session targeting GBV related topics will reach 3,840 beneﬁciaries, all female. This action will also provide CMR services in the supported facilities to 108 beneﬁciaries (all female) in the supported facilities. Treatment for STI and HIV prevention services will also be provided to 650 beneﬁciaries (50 males and 600 females in the supported health facilities). This project will also offer referral services for 108 GBV survivors including psychosocial support and appropriate service provision. The GBV activities will be implemented in the following facilities: Garowe hospital, Bosaso hospital, Elgula health center, Galbarwaaqo health center, Wadajiir HC, Helane HC, Abudwaaq health center, Elasha HC and Jazeera HC.

In order to mitigate exposure of beneficiaries to violence during the activities as a result of the distance or location of humanitarian assistance and services, this action as a mitigation measure will mainly deploy mobile clinics to the villages without static health facilities within their vicinity. Services is provided in a safe and protective environment and beneficiaries will be able to access the humanitarian assistance without being exposed to the risk of sexual violence. These mobile clinics will be equipped with trained staff and nutrition supplies to deliver both quality curative and preventive nutrition services to the affected populations. This is to be sure that location and access routes to health facilities and services are safe and both risk sexual violence and attacks from armed sector are minimized.
