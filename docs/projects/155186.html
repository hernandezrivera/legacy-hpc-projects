Project Summary

This project aims to enhance the food security and livelihood opportunities of Rohingya refugees and host community. In addition, this project will also include an environmental component such as reforestation and vetiver plantation. The overall target of this project is 51,968 beneficiaries who will be reached through the following activities;
- skills development linked to market
- Cash for work (assist in plantation of trees and vetiver slips)
- cash support for livelihood and food security
- homestead vegetable gardening training on plantation techniques
with inputs packages
- poultry rearing training with inputs packages
- ultra-poor graduation programme
- establish nursery

Needs

The food and nutrition security of both refugee and host community is a growing concern. Under the existing conditions, the refugee population remains 100 % reliance on food distribution to sustain the minimum required kilocalories per individual. The hard-hit host community is also struggling to ensure basic needs of their family due to the increase of the commodity price and to cheaper refugee labor market, making clear that the response actors will also need to increase their focus on host community food security. Sustaining the food assistance needs is threatened by the funding gap and is limiting expansion of livelihood activities that could foster self-reliance. The least vulnerable households will be gradually phased out from unconditional food assistance, toward self-reliance activities within the camps. As, there are plans to shift from in-kind food rations to food voucher programme, livelihood/self-reliance programming is needed to provide Rohingyas access to local markets, and provide self-reliance support to enhance their physical access to main commodities. For the host communities there are differences of needs and preferences of work for men and women. For men, they opted for small business opportunities, mobile repairing and masonry training, interest in building/constructing different facilities in camp settings and cash for work opportunities, technical training needs and input supply/subsidy facilities for agriculture and provide opportunities to local businesses and to people who are underserved by other mainstream financial opportunities. The women of the host community have differences in their needs and preferences of livelihoods strategies. These include, receiving training on tailoring, embroidery, beauty parlor, making bamboo basket and stitching nakshikatha. School dropped out girls and boys are interested to receive skills training at market place on tailoring, mobile phone servicing, carpentry, computer training etc. There is a specific needs to increase the support to income -generating activities among the host communities, especially in the areas of agriculture and host communities. The needs of Rohingya communities. ● Opportunity to learn new skills that can lead to income generating activities ● Interest in livelihood/self-reliance support and activities can serve as an entry point for other services where demand is latent e.g. psychosocial counselling, nutritional support, child friendly space, etc. ● Desire to transition from relief recipients to active earners ● Increased local market access to create more income-generating activities ● Increased access to relevant supplies of materials/capital/assets and financial support for active involvement in the self-reliance activities
