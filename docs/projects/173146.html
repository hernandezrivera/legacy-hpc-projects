Project Summary

The project will participate in improving WaSH services in camps and host communities in the area of intervention of SI. It will be a combined approach of operation and maintenance of existing services such as water supply and sanitation (including solid waste management) and implentation of additional activities such as creation of Women Hygiene Spaces, WaSH in schools, WaSH in health. SI will continue to follow the WASH sector strategy and get involved in long term solutions such as water network extension, or construction of new feacal sludge management sites. Additionnally SI envisages to work on a research program regarding water demand and water analysis (ground water quality and quantity) in Teknaf Upazila in order to assess the feasibility of sustainable water supply infrastructure. The research would benefit the whole WASH Sector and help guiding the longer term supply systems to be developed. Finally, some contigency activities are kept in 2021 for emergency response due to AWD, Covid-19 or cyclone.

1 - Identified Needs

In the refugee camps, 54% of the households now accessing chlorinated water through piped distribution networks according to the first findings of J-MSNA, which means 46% are still needed to be covered. In the Teknaf camps, access to water is of particular concern, with major use of surface water in the southern camps that is insufficient during dry season. Regarding sanitation, one of the major concerns is the quality of the fecal sludge treatment as facilities require upgrades and improvements. With 500 cubic meters of Solid Waste produced daily in the targeted area, SMW remains one of the main challenges as only 51% of the refugee households are disposing waste in designated area and 27% reporting the presence of garbage near their houses.Appropriated hygiene practices remains low despite the fact that progresses have been observed partly due to fear from Covid-19 and focus on hand-washing promotion. As a result, 98% of the refugees increased handwashing practices since the COVID-19 outbreak and 95% reported having access to soap. Those improvements need to be confirmed on longer-term through both practice and knowledge as only 60% of the refugees can mention three critical time to wash their hands, according to SI KAP survey. In respect of Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM), only 1.3 MHM kits were provided to women and girls on average between January to September, instead of one every trimester. In Host communities, the large majority of households (88%) access water from tubewells/handpumps, but only 74% reported having enough water for their domestic need in Teknaf where groundwater is particularly scarce. Over the past year, this figure has significantly increased (from 63% in 2019) thanks to the drilling of 893 boreholes in Ukhiya and Teknaf, but there is a major challenge in terms of equity of the water access over the area. Only 63% of households have at least basic access to sanitation leading to 14% of the HH practicing sometimes open defecation. Host’s communities have been less supported by hygiene items and campaign, but findings show encouraging data as 96% of the HH reported having access to soap. Hygiene knowledge seems also better than in camps as 81% of HC respondents being able to mention 3 critical times for handwashing.
