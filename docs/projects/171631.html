Project Summary

The goal of the proposed intervention is to improve GBV response through increased access to immediate, quality, timely, confidential and comprehensive lifesaving services to survivors, and GBV risk mitigation for vulnerable women and girls through the following interventions: Clinical Management of Rape (CMR), establish and operationalize integrated referral pathways, case management (CM); psychosocial support services (PSS) including psychosocial counselling to deliver one on one and group psychosocial support and counselling for traumatized women and girls, psychosocial ﬁrst aid (PFA), creating social networking within women and girls to have access to information on available GBV services through establishment of GBV stop Centre in all hotspot areas in Lower Jubba, Middle Shabelle, Sool and Banadir. The ARC will establish and strengthen women and girls’ safe spaces; in the expansion of easy and comfortable access to comprehensive GBV services including legal, PSS, medical and livelihood services where ARC supports transport for referral services for GBV survivors in the target regions. ARC also plans to further provide emergency material support including re-usable dignity kits to survivors, most vulnerable and displaced female headed households and people living with disability and other special needs including IDPs and vulnerable host communities. ARC will also strengthen GBV risk reduction through strengthening and engaging community protection groups, distribution of dignity kits, whistles and solar lanterns to vulnerable women, girls and mobility reaching Aids and Grabbers for PLWD, provide capacity building and trainings to community volunteers/workers and GBV service providers to enhance service provision and risk reduction for women and girls, and conduct safety audits for GBV risk assessments to inform the project implementation and decisions, among other interventions. The proposed plan will therefore increase confidentiality, quality service delivery, accessibility and coordination of GBV services for conﬂict and drought affected communities in Somalia and Somaliland regions. The ARC will work with various stakeholders to ensure women and girls are protected from GBV and receive support when they experience harm. Some of the stakeholders include line ministries at state government level, the local administration, the police, and women leadership at community level, community and religious leaders and will establish protection networks for women and girls. Community action plans and protection mechanisms will be developed and implemented to reduce protection risks for women and girls including those in IDP settings

Project Activities

Activity 1) Support Safe space activities: Support the established women and girls’ friendly spaces and provide support and resources to deliver a platform for information sharing, awareness raising, peer-to-peer networking and support, training, PSS provision, referrals, and linking the community protection structures. Therefore, will support women and girls learn different skills at the centre as well benefit multisectoral GBV services.Activity 2) Training of duty bearers, medical personnel, and humanitarian staff (GBV): ARC will provide Clinical Care for Sexual Assault Survivors (CCSAS) capacity building opportunities as per WHO guidelines to health and GBVWG staff and provide Health facilities' supplies, guidelines, protocols, and regulations in support of timely and quality GBV service delivery. Activity 3) GBV and Disability Awareness raising advocacy and public outreach campaigns on GBV prevention, response and mitigation (GBV): ARC will engage communities in discussing GBV, child protection and disability risks including the identified risks and ways of addressing them. Awareness activities will be intensified during the marking of international days like the international women’s day, sexual awareness month, HIV/AIDS day, disability day in relation to GBV and the 16 days of activism. Activity 4) Strengthen the protection mainstreaming within the humanitarian actors in Somalia: ARC will conduct a series of protection mainstreaming trainings for international NGOs, Local NGOs and CBOs staff from their various sectors. The primary objective of this activity is to ensure that all humanitarian interventions are provided in a safe, dignified and in accountable way. Activity 5) Identify and train GBV community activists: In line with the recommendations of the safety audits and protection assessments, ARC will identify and train GBV activists at IDP site level who will act as link between humanitarians and the target communities. The community activists will disseminate the GBV validated messages and refer survivors in need for services from community settlements to service providers.Activity 6) Create and train women vigilant groups: ARC will identify and train women vigilant groups in Kismayo, Banadir, Sool and Afmadow IDP and returnees’ camps. The established groups will be trained for GBV protection, reporting mechanisms and will be the first point of contact for GBV survivors at the regions. The activist groups will monitor GBV incidents and support the survivors to report and seek services. Activity 7) ARC will conduct Safety Audit Assessment for 120 IDP camps to identify risks, gaps and most pressing protection concerns for IDPs and displaced populations in the target locations. Activity 8) Provide Case Management capacity strengthening to local actors and GBVWG to deliver effective and efficient services to survivors.Activity 9) Provide Unconditional Cash Transfer (UCT) to 300 survivors or vulnerable female headed households to provide resilience to cope from the harmful experience and gain recovery. Activity 10) Installation of solar lights/lanterns at communal latrines and female headed households at IDPs for risk reduction at night. Activity 11 Distribute 200 Mobility Aid Kit to people with limited mobility in the target areas, provide them GBV and protection messages for risk mitigation. Activity 12) Procurement and distribution of dignity kits to GBV survivors, adolescent girls at school and vulnerable women and girls at reproductive age.Activity 13) Psychosocial support sessions for survivors and crisis effected communities - ARC will adopt the Psychosocial first Aid (PFA) guideline from WHO and IASC prevention and MHPSS guideline for Psychosocial support to foster PSS activity implementations, the guideline is designed to orient helpers to offer psychological first aid (PFA) to people following a serious crisis event including GBV and other disaster effected women and girls.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

ARC will implement cash transfer programming for vulnerable female headed households at risks of sexual exploitation and other forms of GBV and oversee the registration of and distribution to all beneficiaries. ARC will use Hormuud or Dahabshiil, an ARC-preapproved money transfer company, to disburse the funds. ARC proposes to conduct 3 UCT distributions to 150 survivors and female headed households from the drought and conflict region of Banadir, Middle Shabelle, Lower Jubba and Sool with 60% will be from the IDPs. Each household will receive 70 USD monthly for a period of 3 months. With the proposed distribution schedule, the beneficiaries will be cushioned during the lean months as well as months when beneficiaries can restart their livelihood activities. ARC proposes to provide (70 USD) of the MEB under the proposed Action, as it is assumed that the beneficiary households may receive remittances from their families abroad or may have some other means. By providing only a share of the Minimum Expenditure Basket, ARC is able to reach more beneficiaries. ARC field teams in collaboration with key community representatives and the relevant line administration will develop the selection criteria. By ensuring a community-agreed approach to identifying the beneficiaries and with community leader buy-in, the security of the beneficiaries will be ensured. Upon finalization of the selection criteria, the community representatives and ARC staff will sign the beneficiary lists, and share with the community and the ARC Somalia head office based in Mogadishu for auditing purposes. Once registered, the selected beneficiaries will receive UCT distribution identity cards with their picture and serial number, which will be used as identification during the cash distributions. To measure the impact of the UCT, ARC will conduct a series of M&E activities, including a baseline, routine monitoring of field sites, post-distribution monitoring (PDM) and final end line assessment of results. ARC will train enumerators on how to use the data collection tools and travel to field sites

Please provide further explanation

ARC will select beneficiaries based on their needs and will consider disability to all activities and 15% of the targeted population will be people with limited mobility, a defined selection criterion with disability inclusion will be developed in consultation with community-based targeting committees and leaders. While need influences the decision to select beneficiaries, ARC will give consideration to groups, who remain highly vulnerable in crisis situations, such as female headed households, who have no extended social support, child-headed households, persons with disabilities, elderly and marginalized and minority groups who have no major income source or at risk to violations. Disability beneficiary involvement will be enhanced through meaningful disability inclusion and engagement process that allows participation of the different disable groups and the committees will be included formal and informal local authorities who are responsible of the people with limited mobility in the selection of beneficiaries. The process of the target beneficiary’s selection will give priority to communities to identify their most vulnerable households through locally established community-based targeting committees who work hand in hand with well-respected clan elders and influential leaders to enhance local ownership and accountability practices at local level. Due to the increased concern of inclusion and exclusion errors, the ARC will carry out joint verification with Community based committees to affirm the beneficiaries selected indeed meet the agreed criteria and the agenda of disability inclusion. In the event that problems emerge the ARC will use locally appropriate negotiation channels to handle such problems including consultations with traditional elders, influential and religious leaders to promote the rights of people with limited mobility during community participation and need identification. Therefore, the project will address the need of people living with disability and they will be fully considered through out the project. Under this action, ARC will train community-based protection networks, leaders, opinion and community leaders to pass GBV information to sustain awareness and information dissemination. The trained community members will be tasked to continue supporting the larger community in addressing the GBV root causes, consequences and work with other communities in ceasing violence against women and girls in the target regions. The operational safe spaces will be handed over to the trained women and girls to continue and self-managing with the support of relevant line ministries to oversee the sustainability aspects of women empowerment and GBV services at the centre. ARC through the funding will establish safe space community Safe space resilience Centres for women and girls’ meetings and engagement. Safe space Centres on allowing Disable people, women and vulnerable girls to better cope with cyclical shock, droughts, displacements, evictions while also becoming better prepared for those emergencies in the future. One way the ARC supports this within humanitarian programming is by including emergency response to anticipated shocks, local capacity building, and sustainability components within programming wherever possible. Another important way is by layering or leveraging programmes between different funders to provide greater community level resilience within fragile areas. This can mean combining programmes of different sectors to address more complex needs or by modifying awarded funding during a major shock to immediately fund an intervention while greater emergency funding is secured. The local committees will also allow the ARC to monitor and respond to localized or wide-scale emergencies more decisively should they occur.
