Project Summary

Situation Overview:
Sudan has a huge burden of acute malnutrition, or wasting, and the S3M showed that over half a million3 children will suffer from life-threatening severe acute malnutrition during one year. Prevalence of GAM in many areas across the country is classified as critical, which is above 15% as per the WHO threshold for assessing severity of malnutrition (S3MII, 2018). Acute malnutrition is a wide-spread public health problem affecting every state, with a concentration in the East (particularly Red Sea), Blue Nile, Central and North Darfur. Red Sea state recorded the highest prevalence’s of malnutrition with all but one locality well above the 15% threshold for a critical situation. The deteriorating macroeconomic situation in Sudan is worsening household food and nutrition insecurity for all Sudanese, particularly for the most vulnerable families and children. The 2020 HNO identifies 6.2 million people in need of food and livelihoods assistance, an increase from 5.7 million people in 2019. This means that food insecurity remains alarmingly high in Sudan with increased and protracted displacement, economic decline and inflation, and high food price hikes exacerbated by the impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic. The Sudan’s food and nutritional security are eroding, deepening a crisis already affecting 14 per cent of the population. It is estimated that over 570,871 children require treatment for severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and the operational costs of reaching these children are rising. An additional 2.4 million children need moderate acute malnutrition treatment. The persistent food insecurity is a key driver of malnutrition among children under 5 years and PLWs. Pregnant and breastfeeding/lactating women (PLWs) are the demographic group most at risk of malnutrition in Sudan after children, making up some 12 percent of all those on supplementary feeding programmes. Such women’s vulnerability can be largely attributed to the higher nutritional needs associated with supporting a foetus or nursing an infant. The persistent food insecurity is a key driver of malnutrition among children under 5 years and PLWs. Maternal nutritional status also affects the healthy development of the fetus and a healthy birth weight, as well as a mother’s ability to sustain breastfeeding, and care for her children as they develop. There has been substantial attention to the provision of treatment services in conflict-affected states over the years, however, most Sudan’s acutely malnourished children (52 per cent) live in nine non-conflict affected states. Although the national Infant and Young Child Feeding Strategy (2015-2024) of Sudan has been developed as part of National Nutrition Program Strategy (2014-2018), very many children are not exclusively fed due to food shortage and poverty in the house. Preventing malnutrition in Sudan is complicated, but the improved understanding and acceptance of the scale of the issue, combined with ongoing lifesaving and longer-term support and services, are critical steps forward in attempts to break the cycle of malnutrition. In this project, we target to reach to at least 70% of the 14% malnourished individuals, majority of whom are children (6-59months) and PLWs with expanded coverage for OPTs and TSFPs including addressing MIYCF challenges at households and community levels. In addition, the sector will target 430,000 children (aged 6-23 months) who have nutritional deficiencies with micro-nutrient powders supplementation. Notwithstanding resource limitation, we plan to admit and treat all accessible SAM and MAM cases in IPT/OTPs and TSFP respectively. We shall strengthen local capacity and coordination with the Federal Ministry of Health (FMoH) and other partners.

1 - Needs

Prevalence of GAM in many areas across the country is classified as critical, which is above 15% as per the WHO threshold for assessing severity of malnutrition (S3MII, 2018). Acute malnutrition is a wide-spread public health problem affecting every state, with a concentration in the East (particularly Red Sea), Blue Nile, Central and North Darfur. Red Sea state recorded the highest prevalence’s of malnutrition with all but one locality well above the 15% threshold for a critical situation. Malnutrition has negative consequences for people, families, communities, and countries. The national prevalence rate of global acute malnutrition (GAM) is 14.1 percent, which is just 0.9% away from the WHO threshold. Over 1.65 million children under five years of age are in dire need of nutritional support to avert mortalities and morbidities related to acute malnutrition ( Severe and Moderate). The continued suffering of Sudanese children from protracted conflict exacerbated by current economic-stress resulting from COVID-19 pandemic causing livelihood disruption, poverty, and destitution. The key drivers of malnutrition remains poverty, inadequate feeding practices, and social-economic insecurity. To address the above critical situation of malnutrition amongst the North Darfur vulnerable Communities, the nutrition response will be guided by two main objectives: provide affected people with the highest levels of undernutrition with curative nutrition services, and strengthen and provide preventative nutrition services to the most vulnerable groups. HDPO has planned to deliver promotional nutrition services through existing Health Facilities in the region. The overall goal is to increase equitable access to nutrition services and provide timely nutrition assistance for acutely malnourished children (boys and girls 6-59 months of age) and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) in emergency-affected and highly vulnerable communities. More specifically, the project will provide affected people with the highest undernutrition with curative nutrition services and strengthen and provide preventative nutrition services to the most vulnerable groups. Through these objectives, the Nutrition Sector aims to target around 1.58 million people with multi-sectoral nutrition-sensitive intervention. The nutrition services will include nutritional assessment of children under 5 years, pregnant and Lactating Women, infant and young child feeding (IYCF) and other nutrition-related counseling, early years stimulation services for 0-36 month children, diet management and lifestyle modification for pregnant, lactating women, older people and patient with chronic diseases, distribution of IFA, Vitamin-A, deworming tablets. The quality of and access to integrated nutrition services for at-risk groups will be improved by strengthening local nutrition services and supporting community-based nutrition services. The project areas' outcome will be more equitable, accessible, and higher-quality nutrition services for at-risk groups such as poor mothers and children. Community capacity to carry out nutrition hygiene interventions will be strengthened through community nutrition packages and mobilizing community facilitators. The outcome will be a greater community capacity to implement nutrition, hygiene, and sanitation interventions to prevent malnutrition in young children—enhanced project management capacity, including planning, monitoring, and evaluation to support nutrition programs.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The Project is designed to deliver three activities/outputs which includes to: improve quality and integrated nutrition services to the most vulnerable groups in the project areas; to enhance community capability for and implementation of nutrition related interventions; and to enhances the capacity for project management including planning, monitoring and evaluation of nutrition programs.Activity 1: To improve quality and integrated nutrition services to the most vulnerable groups in the project areas The Project will explore alternative approaches for nutrition service delivery in poor urban and rural communities. It will focus on two demand-side approaches increased access and quality of facility- and community-based nutrition services, improved case management and its referral of severely malnourished children, and upgrading nutrition service infrastructure and nutrition related equipment. Better referral of severely malnourished children and people, expanded outreach, and high-quality counseling will strengthen nutrition service delivery by county and State hospitals and health centers. The Project will introduce multi micronutrient fortificants (also called “sprinkles”) to combat anemia in children under five years of age in the project areas. Training of medical doctors, nurses, and nutritionists will focus on discouraging the use of infant formula and educating staff and caretakers about breast feeding. Better equipment and the provision of micronutrient supplements at the hospital will improve the response to severe malnourished children. Also the activity will involve community-Based Nutrition Services. This subcomponent will strengthen community institutions to ensure a sustainable improvement in nutrition status, hygiene, and sanitation and related behaviors. Through community vulnerable groups, mothers and caretakers will be actively involved in growth monitoring and nutrition education to change behavior with regard to child feeding & caring practices and their personal feeding and will be trained on how to prepare nutritious diet. Activity 2: To enhance community capability for and implementation of nutrition related interventionsThis will be implemented by mobilizing community facilitators. The intervention will finance about 900 community facilitators to help build community awareness of nutrition, hygiene, and sanitation, and to train members of the community to identify locally appropriate interventions with regard to nutrition and hygiene, preparation of community plans, and monitoring of activities. The community facilitators will be trained by the organization on various topics of nutrition. Community nutrition package (CNP) which is defined as assistance to improve nutrition and hygiene of nutritionally at-risk women and children at the community level, will be provided to different localities of North Darfur and Red Sea for a period of 1 years. The project committee will select localities and poor urban areas. Participating communities will establish a community nutrition committee (CNC), an elected committee, which can include representatives of the community cadre. The core of the project design is that communities will be empowered to assess their own requirements for improved nutrition. With the assistance of community facilitators, communities will identify CNP activities. Activity 3: To enhance the capacity for project management including planning, monitoring and evaluation of nutrition programsThis component will provide project management support in term of M&E reporting and financial accountability expected to be achieved by the project.
