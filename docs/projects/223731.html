Project Summary

DRC will provide unconditional food assistance through cash transfers to 39,900 conflict-affected people in Borno state in Northeast Nigeria. The Project aims to provide unconditional 4-month of cash assistance during the non-lean season from February to May in anticipating the lean season and scaling up the response during the lean season from June to September to the most vulnerable IDPs affected by the protracted crisis in Northeast Nigeria. During Project implementation, regular monitoring activities will be conducted during cash distribution and monthly data collected against performance indicators for reporting. DRC has an obligation to ensure accountability to affected populations which means using power responsibly, ensuring the rights and voices of affected people are valued, heard and that they have an opportunity to participate in and influence relevant decisions affecting whether and how we work with them. The targeted vulnerable IDPs and local populations will be closely involved in various stages of the project including the targeting process. The affected people will be regularly informed of the project activities and plans. Through the complaints and feedback mechanism, they will freely express their views and concerns on the project implementation. In line with DRC's Code of Conduct policy and accountability to affected populations, beneficiaries’ complaints are treated confidentially, and their views are considered for project adjustment.
DRC will conduct assessments to identify and update the list of the most vulnerable people among IDPs and the local populations to be considered in the project based on vulnerability criteria based on food security and socio-economic criteria.

1 - Needs

The continued deterioration in the food security situation is linked to protracted insecurity as evident in renewed attacks by non-state armed groups which have led to a new wave of displacements, limited access to farming and grazing land essential for livelihood opportunities, and further exacerbated by the economic shocks in 2023 which caused a high food prices in 2024. In recent months, the closure of camps in some LGAs in Borno state has added to the number of people in dire need and this has placed increased pressure on humanitarian actors to meet their most basic food, nutrition, and other needs. Notwithstanding, the continued assistance by the Government of Nigeria and the humanitarian community to deliver food and nutrition assistance as well as livelihood support, access to farming/grazing land, labor market, productive assets, and livelihood activities remains limited. The food insecurity and malnutrition analysis, Cadre Harmonisé, led by the Government of Nigeria and supported by partners, alerts on the deterioration of food security in Nigeria with 33.1 million people expected to face high levels of acute food insecurity during the coming lean season (June-August 2025). This represents an alarming rise of seven million people from the same period last year, driven by economic hardship, coupled with record high inflation, the effects of climate change and persistent violence in the northeastern states of the country. Nationally, the number of people experiencing Emergency levels (Phase 4) of food insecurity is projected to increase from 1 million people in the peak of the 2024 lean season to 1.8 million people at the same period in 2025, representing a worrying 80 percent rise. Approximately 5.4 million children and nearly 800,000 pregnant and breastfeeding women are at risk of acute malnutrition or wasting from six of the most affected states of Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe in the northeast, and Sokoto, Katsina and Zamfara in the northwest. Of these, an alarming 1.8 million children could face Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and require critical nutrition treatment. According to CH latest analysis; staple food prices rose to averages above 75%, impacting negatively on households’ capacity to maintain acceptable dietary intake and FC outcomes. In all the states analyzed, at least 70% of the HHs have Stress (Phase 2) to Crisis (Phase 3) Food Consumption Score (FCS), Household Dietary Diversity Score (HDDS) and Household Hunger Scale (HHS) despite the adoption of Crisis to Emergency Food-Related Reduced Coping Strategy (rCSI). Risk areas with Critical (Phase 3) thresholds included; Borno, Katsina, Yobe, Sokoto and Zamfara States where the interplay of insecurity and natural disaster induced displacement were most evident. n the current period (October to December, 2024) about 25,089,957 (12.1%) persons across the analyzed states are under Crisis to Worse (Phase 3 to 5) severity phase of FNI. These populations are comprised of 422,686 (1.68%) Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), resident in Borno (377,322), Sokoto (25,586) and Zamfara (19,778) States, respectively. The populations in Emergency (Phase 4) is estimated as 919,170 made up of 191,839 (20.9%), 414,033 (45%), 90,442 (9.84%) and 70,995 (7.72) residents in the BAY, Kano and Katsina States including the FCT.

2 - Activities

1. To ensure, DRC addresses beneficiary needs comprehensively, DRC technical teams, with the support of MEAL team, will conduct multi-sectoral beneficiary selection assessment, including screening for food security indicators. When assessing beneficiary needs for food security, DRC will focus on assessing the IPC level of targeted HH combined with protection vulnerability criteria to ensure that the assistance reached the most vulnerable HH. Only HH with IPC/CH Phase 3 or higher will be targeted with this action. HH with compounding protection risks/ needs will be prioritized just as HH with severe needs across other sectors. DRC will also accept beneficiaries that fulfil the targeting criteria referred by other DRC sectors and other partners. 2. DRC will provide Unconditional Food Assistance through cash to 39,900 vulnerable and food-insecure individuals in Adamawa and Borno States. Cash for food assistance will be provided during the non-lean from February to May 2025 and scaling up in the peak hunger season (June to September 2025) when needs are expected to be highest as recommended by the Nigeria Food Security Sector. DRC will employ a community-based targeting approach to identify those eligible for both Cash for Food Assistance. Inception meetings will be held with community stakeholders representing all different groups within the targeted communities to discuss with them the primary selection criteria and targeting process. DRC will use household interview questionnaires which will incorporate food security indicator tools (rCSI, FCS, HHS), other vulnerability indicators (such as sources of food, income). The questionnaire will be administered to each household that meets the primary selection criteria. To ensure transparency and accountability, priority will be given to households with the following: a) poor Food Consumption Score (FCS); b) severe to moderate Household Hunger Scale (HSS); c) “High Coping” on the reduced Coping Strategy Index (rCSI). Other vulnerability criteria will also be applied such as labour-constrained households headed by elderly, chronically ill, PwDs, or widowed people supporting large numbers of young dependents, some of whom may be orphaned. DRC will deliver cash for food assistance via a Financial Service Provider (FSP). DRC will continue to assess the performance of these FSP and research other available options to ensure rapid and reliable delivery, and value for money and for this purpose, DRC will encourage the beneficiaries of food assistance to use their bank account or open a bank account if a bank is available in their locations. DRC harmonizes its food assistance with the Food Security Sector (FSS) based on their recommendations on the cash transfer value Minimum Food Basket for Northeast Nigeria (BAY States). 3. Market monitoring and analysis monthly, the availability and prices of staple food items that constitute the food basket in its locations of interventions and analyse the changes in the market dynamics and access by beneficiaries. 4. Post distribution monitoring (PDM) will be conducted 30 days after each distribution to measure the progress of achievements of outputs and outcomes and ensure the distributions are safe. 5. Targeting, profiling and selection of participants in the livelihoods activities according to their preferences and motivations and skills.
