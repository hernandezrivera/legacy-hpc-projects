Project Summary

The project activities will support the provision of appropriate and efficient child protection and GBV services as well as raising awareness on prevention of child protection, GBV, and harmful practices in the two regions including hard-to-reach areas. Services including Medical, Psychosocial support and case management will be offered. Material supports such as dignity kits and solar lights will be provided to both adults and children. The project will also work on recovery and resilience of survivors and their families-This will be done through capacity building, involvement of communities in protection activities, and through provision of Cash-Based Intervention (CBI) to enhance self-reliance and resilience of survivors and at-risk groups. SEDHURO plan to conduct awareness raising through community meetings, dialogue sessions and out-reach sensitisation campaigns.
The project will provide CMR training to HW. The content of the CMR training will cover legal, psychological, medical and involvement of communities for better collaboration for the help of survivors. Health providers will also be trained to be compassionate and provide emotional support to survivors. The training also intends to roll out the CMR protocol, endorsed by the ministry of health in December 2015.SEDHURO will assess the capacities of health clinics before they receive the kits with respect to their capacity in the provision of reproductive health services including family planning, antenatal care, normal delivery care, or management of STIs.
The project will establish community-based mechanisms for prevention and response of separation of children and Gender Based Violence against children and women to enhance grassroots identification, prevention of and response to child protection concerns.
Case management process will be initiated for all unaccompanied children, including provision of interim family–based care, family tracing and reunification. The vulnerabilities of UASC will be assessed based on the individual circumstances of each child including the type of separation, the duration of separation, contact arrangements, the suitability of care arrangements, child’s age, gender, children with disabilities and the child’s best interests. Following this assessment an appropriate care plan will be developed. CP officers will work with CPC and coordinate with other agencies involved in way-stations management, IDP camp management, food distribution points, madarasa/school teachers, medical facilities to identify UASC. Identification of UASC will also take place in outlying villages to ensure maximum coverage and response. This will be done with the informed consent of the children.
CFS will ensure children have a safe place to gather for play, for self-expression and learning. To enhance ownership and resilience and the likelihood of sustaining the longer-term impacts of psychosocial interventions, CFS will be established and operated in a way that engages or strengthens support systems within families and communities.
The project will provide specialized social service and/or mental health care for parents with MNS disorders and support for the wellbeing and coping ability of parents, raising awareness of distress reactions of children in emergencies, according to age and developmental stage and further promoting positive parenting knowledge and skills among caregivers. They will be introduced through discussion and dialogue sessions parenting messages that improve relationship with children under their care with Focused care for distressed parents/caregivers, including PFA to be provided.
Public broadcasts of key protection messages will be conducted twice per week for four weeks during the first quarter of the programme and twice per month for the rest of the programme. These broadcasts will cover 200,000 people within the IDP camps and host communities and ensure broad based knowledge and awareness.

Project Activities

1.1 The project will Facilitate age, gender and culturally appropriate recreational activities for 7000 children and adolescents in communities and learning spaces through support to club/group activities, play/tournaments, games, peer to peer support, basic counseling in CFS. 1.2 The project will provide guidance and support to 300 caregivers to improve care for their children and deal with their own distress, and to link them to supports and services. This will be done through involving caregivers in meaningful activities including craftsmanship i.e making of baskets, mats among other things. 1.3 The project will provide Comprehensive CM systems for UASC and other vulnerable children and adolescents including children/adolescents with disabilities and those from minority groups. Through CBCPM, the project will identify and document 800 UASC and provide them with CM and IDTR services inline with the Inter-agency Guiding Principles on UASC1.4 The identified UASC will be provided with reunification kits such as clothing, beddings, food, dignity kits, shoe ware, (200 cases), transport costs for reunification (500 cases). SEHURO will facilitate alternative care arrangements for 100 children by working with 40 foster mothers who received training in the two regions.1.5 The Project will carry out inter-agency service mapping to determine child protection agencies and service providers in the two regions. The project will build on the service mapping to establish referral pathways among child protection agencies.2.1 Center-based psychosocial support activities: SEDHURO will use existing entry points such as community centers, social development centers and other community based platforms for conducting psychosocial activities and safe space for 1200 women and girls including 120 children with disabilities. Activities will include cultural and artistic performances or networks, creating opportunities for youth to engage with younger boys and girls, and encouraging peer-to-peer support. 2.3 SEDHURO will train 100 CP actors (social workers, community volunteers, government officials and field coordinators and non- child protection actors in CP training to acquire skills and knowledge in providing age-and gender-sensitive child protection services. 2.4 SEDHURO will identify and train 300 community child protection committees (200 in Gedo and 100 in Lower Juba region) and will be facilitated to form 30 committees in the two regions. This will be an upscale from the existing 30 CPCs to 60 CPCPC in the two regions.2.5 Facilitate child protection awareness raising and outreach activities on prevention of family separation, GBV, FGM and promoting non-violence in schools and communities. This will be provided through community dialogues, school club activities and CPCs targeting 30,000 community members (20,000 in Gedo and 10,000 in L.Juba)3.1.1 Provision of clinical care to 600 sexual violence survivors through health centres, and/or one stop centers in in Gedo and Lower Juba regions- SEDHURO will distribute purchased/received PEP kits to health clinics that received adequate training in CMR. 3.1.2 Train 100 health care providers on clinical management of rape. SEDHURO has a qualified trainer of Clinical Management of Rape (CMR). The trainer will strengthen the capacities of other CMR service providers in 10 districts including those from outlying villages and hard-to-reach areas. 3.2 Provide PSS to 500 GBV survivors to be provided by professional PSS workers. 3.3. Provide legal aid services for 150 GBV survivors. The project will coordinate with Jubaland ministry of GFHR, JRA and other relevant community stakeholders in providing representatives and legal aid for GBV survivors to ensure access to justices.3.4 The project will provide 700 age-and-culture appropriate dignity kits to survivors of GBV and at-risk groups in in Gedo and Lower Juba regions. 300 IDPs and returnee households will be provided with solar lanterns.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

1. GBV survivors, UASC and vulnerable returnees will be empowered through VT and conditional cash transfer to be self-reliant and resilient.2. SEDHURO wants to employ cash transfer to affect positive change, notably where a condition can be tied to a behavior change such as stopping child labor or child marriage.3. A main driver for SEDHURO use of cash transfer programming is that it isrooted in a more dignified approach to humanitarian response. 4. cash transfer programming provides an opportunity for childprotection practitioners and researchers across humanitarian anddevelopment contexts to leverage and share research and learning. Given theevidence gap from humanitarian settings and the challenges such contextspose to undertaking quality research, the opportunity exists to determine thetransferability of evidence from development to humanitarian settings and tofoster stronger linkages between the sectors. 5. Economic empowerment programms can help uproot negative gender and social norms that confine women to the domestic sphere and can help to build women’s agency.Provision of Solar Lantern to 500 Vulnerable IDPs/Returnees

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

The project will equip 100 children GBV survivors & other vulnerable groups including minority groups, PLWLD and returnees with marketable skills and market driven VT skills identified through market/demand assessment. SEDHURO will provide 6-months comprehensive VT & basic literacy & numeracy sessions for 100 girls from vulnerable IDPs & host communities. They will receive cash to jump-start business. The Kismayo and Beledhawo is operational and will be able to accommodate the supply of capital kits to the graduates. The project is designed in away that the materials required for survivors to jump-start business is locally available and the size of beneficiaries is reduced to hundred to ensure the natural operation of the market is not tilted.

Mitigation Measures

1. Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services: - Utmost confidentiality will be exercised when incidents of violation are documented. - Information on survivors will only be shared with service provided on "need-to-know" basis. - Consent will be sought even for children under 5 (from guardian) - Survivors facing further risk will be accommodated in Safe Houses. 2. Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance: SEDHURO will strengthen child safeguarding measures including PSEA and code of conduct to ensure that all interventions are safe for children. SEDHURO has at least three staff trained as a PSEA and child safeguarding focal point.In accordance with SEDHURO strategy to Prevent/Respond to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and Sexual Harassment, SEDHURO commits to:a. Organize training for its staff on its internal code of conduct on PSEAs with basic concepts (definition of SEA (that is aligned with the UN's definition); i) a prohibition of SEA; and ii) actions that personnel are required to take (i.e. prompt reporting of allegations and referral of survivors).b. Have in place a referral system for the systematic care of survivors by qualified services. 3. Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents. The project will carry out aggressive information sharing by way of forums and through radio broadcasting which has large audience in the implementation areas. 4. Risk that marginalised groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities: All communities systematically represented in governance structures of projects conducted - Project volunteers will systematically include members of marginalised communities - Phone number for feedback and complaints disseminated among all communities in areas targeted - Permanent contact with marginalised communities maintained by teams on the ground, for protection monitoring, advocacy, and inclusion • People with disabilities and/or their representative organisations will be consulted during the design and implementation of the project. • The project will identify and address the heightened risks faced by people with disabilities in humanitarian contexts. • The project will identify and address barriers to participation for people with disabilities. Exclusion and marginalization: Women, girls and boys are not included in consultations and decision-making processes. Mitigation Measures: - Women and girls will make up 50% of project committees, Child Friendly Space Facilitators and members of children clubs. - Women and girls will also make up 50% of training participants in child protection and GBV. - Phone number for feedback and complaints disseminated among all communities in areas targeted - Data on survivors and assistance provided will be disaggregated. 5. Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources: SEDHURO enjoys cordial relationship with Jubbaland administration and doesn't expect external interference in the beneficiary selection. SEDHURO will involve the administration in the launch of the project to enable them understand the nature of the project and its target population. 6. Risk of the transfer of cash to children and women:- The project will provide cash transfer through mobile to reduce attention survivors may get during distribution. The project will also make follow-up to ensure survivors don't face violation.

Durable solutionThe project builds the capacity of community members to enable them prevent and respond to child protection and GBV. Health workers and providers of health services will be trained in Clinical Management of Rape (CMR). The training will coordinate with UNFPA, Jubaland health ministries to ensure the utilization of systematic, standardized training manuals which will lead to a well-trained pool of individuals who, besides providing quality CMR services, will be able to do further training in the future. SEDHURO will conduct follow-up to determine the impact of the training on health provision.The project will also work on recovery and resilience of survivors and their families-This will be done through capacity building, involvement of communities in protection activities, and through provision of Cash-Based Intervention (CBI) to enhance self-reliance and resilience of survivors and at-risk groups. Community EngagementThe project will build on 6 existing child friendly spaces (3 in Gedo and 3 in Lower Juba) that was constructed thanks to SEDHURO-UNICEF and SEDHURO-SHF Partnership. The project will further establish three more child friendly spaces in Garbaharey, Beledhawo and Dhobley and six mobile recreational facilities in the two regions. 45 facilitators who were trained in childcare will run the CFS. The project will mobilize child and youth groups and networks can be a key strategy for promoting peer-support. Collective community activities such as cleaning up schools and after-school groups, parent/teacher committees and parent-child groups will be established to promote a sense of togetherness. SEDHURO will involve community volunteers in psychological first aid in these activities. Project will also establish community-led IDTR procedures for separated and unaccompanied children from both IDPs and host communities in 7 districts in Gedo and three in Lower Juba. CPC structures are expected to identify, report/refer child protection concerns or children at risk as well as raise awareness on Child Protection, hygiene promotion (including awareness and prevention against CONVID-19), and prevention of family separation among others. Regular monthly meetings will be organized to enhance coordination and information sharing. It’s envisaged that at least 200 cases will be referred by the CBCPMs put in place. SEHURO will facilitate alternative care arrangements for 100 children by working with 40 foster mothers who received training in the two regions through SEDHURO/UNICEF and SHF-SEDHURO partnerships to provide knowledge-based interim care to children awaiting reunification. Affected Community The project puts the affected community at the heart of the programming with almost all the activities aligned to the NDP9. The project will provide specialized social service and/or mental health care for parents with MNS disorders and support for the wellbeing and coping ability of parents, raising awareness of distress reactions of children in emergencies, according to age and developmental stage and further promoting positive parenting knowledge and skills among caregivers. The project will equip children who are GBV survivors & other vulnerable groups including minority groups, PLWLD and returnees with marketable skills and market driven VT skills identified through market/demand assessment. SEDHURO will provide 6-months comprehensive VT & basic literacy & numeracy sessions for 100 girls from vulnerable IDPs & host communities. They will receive cash to jump-start business.The project will also provide legal support to survivors with the support of Jubaland ministry of Gender, Family Affairs and Human Rights to enable GBV survivors access justice.
