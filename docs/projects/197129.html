Project Summary

700,000 children have been affected by school closures in Cameroon’s Northwest (NW) and Southwest (SW) regions (referred to from now on as the NWSW regions), as a result of the Anglophone crisis that began in 2016 (OCHA 31/05/2019). Education is a key issue of the Anglophone crisis. In 2017, the west Region is amongst one of the regions of Cameroon that is host to a significant number of the internally displaced children. A recent baseline survey conducted by WESAD with Global GLOW Club girls in Cameroon shows that; 15% of children have access to smart phones which they own or they are free to use in their home, 95% have no basic skills on the use of the computer, 83% can use a smart mobile phone to make and receive calls only, 65% can use the mobile phone on social media platforms like Facebook and WhatsApp and 98% can neither use the computer nor smart phone to browse for study programs/opportunities, attend zoom or team meetings as needed by the “new normal” brought about by the effects of the Anglophone crises and COVID-19.
Project Need
The start of the crises and coming of the COVID-19 pandemic has brought a “new normal” with most study, work and meetings being done online using the internet, social media and other meeting apps like zoom, team and many more. Education remains an essential element of all society – with SDG 4 focusing on inclusive and equitable education for all-the sector became an immediate priority (Mindel, 2020). Although there are progressive changes within the school environment in the NWSW, the security situation in these regions remains uncertain and volatile. Some school days are progressively taken off and termed as “Ghost Town”.
Project objective
This initiative therefore will create Youth E-Resilience Hub centres in the North West Region (Mezam Division-Tubah, Santa, Bafut, Bamenda I, Bamenda II, Bamenda III), and South West Region (Buea, Muea, Tiko, Tole) and to equip young boys and girls between the ages of 9 and 18 in with necessary basic computer and ICT skills to meet with the new normal situation created by the effects of the Anglophone crises and COVID- 19.
Project Target
The project targets 2000 youths from 9 - 18years of age and indirectly will reach out to 15,000 other beneficiaries. The 2000 direct beneficiaries to be reached by the proposed program will include youths from different schools, colleges and target communities (direct beneficiaries) and 15,000 others, like their family members, school mates with whom they interact in their various communities.(indirect beneficiaries)
Project Activities
1. Create awareness in 10 localities reaching out to 10,000 persons to communicate project goals to selected beneficiaries on “Youth E-Resilience Hub” for young boys and girls
2. Prepare and produce “Youth E-Resilience Hub” learning content
3. Identify and recruit 20 community based volunteers (10 males, 10 Females) and give them orientation on the “Youth E-Resilience Hub” and how to use it to empower the young persons to achieve learning even from home.
4. Set up “Youth E-Resilience Hub” centers in the 10 project localities for young boys and girls to improve access to the use of computer, smart mobile phones and the internet for online study and meeting purposes
5. Accompany the community based volunteers to Build the capacities of 3000 young boys and girls on basic computer skills, the use of mobile phones for study and meetings apps like zoom and teams.
6. Hold Coordination Meeting with identified partner organizations focused on communication project goals, expected outcomes and impacts.
Project Output:
1) 10,000 persons sensitised on the project in 10 localities
2) A Well Developed ICT training manual for Youth E-Resilience learning.
3) 20 ICT Community based volunteers trained
4) 10 Youth E-resilience Hub Centres set up
