Project Summary

The project aims at continuing & strengthening Site Management & Site Development interventions within the Kutuplaong Balukhali Extension (KBE) site & Teknaf area through direct implementation & in partnership with SMSD actors. Through this project Rohingya refugees & the host community will benefit from improved service provision, Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) measures, site improvements & site planning interventions which contribute to ensuring equitable access to assistance, protection, & services for refugees living in the sites, improving their quality of life & dignity during displacement. The project will combine several of IOM’s programmes—Needs Population Monitoring (NPM), Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), Site Maintenance & Engineering Project (SMEP).
It is expected that the specified programme activities will result in reinforcing the overall capacity of unions in Ukhiya Teknaf & surrounding host communities to cope with natural disasters like floods & cyclones & respond better through strengthening structures to manage natural disasters & emergency situations. Approaches used to reach the overall goal of the project will mainly focus on community participation, age, gender, diversity inclusion, social interaction & peaceful co-existence.

Objectives

1. Continue to support the Government of Bangladesh’s (GoB) Camp in Charge (CiCs) officials in managing the camps & strengthening their capacity while ensuring equitable & safe access for refugees, including women & girls. Assistance & protection will be standardized & monitored against agreed standards & multi-hazard emergency preparedness. 2. Strengthen field-level coordination by facilitating joint initiatives on macro settlement & establishing community consultation & dialogue mechanisms. Assessing social cohesion dynamics with host communities in close vicinity of the camps. 3. Holistically upgrade & improve the settlement areas & their immediate surroundings, through community-participatory & mid-term macro planning, linked to construction & maintenance works that enable safe, dignified & resilient living condition—ensuring access for women, girls & children. Community-led decision making is at the forefront of all SMSD initiatives. 4. Continue to support the GoB in host communities to enhance the capacity of Disaster Risk Management institutions & local authorities on preparedness & emergency response management. 5. Rehabilitate cyclone shelter & evacuation places in host communities for any natural calamity like cyclones.

Needs

1. SMSD Though conditions have improved refugees continue living in congested areas exposed to hazards. 47% of respondents to the 2019 J-MSNA reported structural improvements; 37% reported improved sanitation & 26% reported stronger shelters. There has been an increase in access to services but many gaps remain unaddressed. Camp coordination has improved through regular meetings across sites. However, the number of actors remains high & needs stronger organization. The camp environment has improved since 2018 yet terrain remains vulnerable to natural hazards. 22% of HHs highlighted insufficient infrastructure as a challenge. In addition to road/pathway & drainage network extensions, repairs to existing infrastructure will become more important as many of the materials previously used were not durable. Refugees do not have access to cyclone shelters nor fall under the national Cyclone Preparedness Program. SMSD & CwC teams will continue to play a key role during emergencies for preparedness & response by strengthening coordination mechanisms. 2019 saw the development of a democratic community representation system to replace the Majhi system which is unrepresentative & unaccountable. The Community Representation Task Force developed guidelines via community consultations. However, the system has not been approved by the GoB. AGD & women empowerment remain key for quality programming. In 2019 IOM started the WPP with UN Women & aims to expand it across camps. The initiative fosters mechanisms for balancing the power of Majhis. SD will further engage PwD & women ensuring vulnerable groups have equal access to CfW. 2. SMEP | Teams operate, maintain & upgrade >60km of vehicle access roads, ~100km of large stormwater drains & an inventory of slope stabilisation. Many brick paved roads are not sufficiently durable to resist the impact of trucks & rains. Large infrastructure repair, maintenance & upgrades are needed to ensure access for humanitarian, refugee & HC. The construction & maintenance of drainage/slope stabilization are required to mitigate risks to beneficiaries & accessibility. The ADB & WB are planning road installations, drainage & slope stabilisation in the camps. SMEP will ensure success for these initiatives via connectivity & sustainability of planned infrastructure. The LGED will not accept responsibility for maintenance, stating it is the RRRC’s remit. In 2018, RRRC requested SMEP to operate & maintain vehicle access roads—which continues to stand. Operational teams will seek to include >30% female participation/safe inclusion of 5—10 PwD in each of the yards. 3. DRR | Despite DRR programming in HCs, school safety & preparedness remain an evolving need. All rehabilitated cyclone shelters are used as schools 9am–3pm. IOM is considering a school safety component for cyclone shelters to create awareness among schoo children on disaster preparedness & evacuation plans. IOM will build the capacity of school committees to respond to disasters. To encourage the inclusion of women in disaster risk planning, Disaster Management Committees & other bodies will be guided with gender sensitive programming & mainstreaming. 4. NPM |As the crisis persists, so does the need for evidence-based interventions, supported by a broad IM framework for comprehensive information on the needs & vulnerabilities of affected populations. While population movements have stabilized to some extent, facilities & services are changing in each settlement. Furthermore, challenges presented by the monsoon & cyclone season require a robust framework for effective monitoring of the impact on affected populations. Analysis on needs & access to services requires consistent updating to understand key gaps in service provision. Protection indicators are incorporated in ways that are compatible with NPM methodologies, but that also acknowledge the sensitivities associated with protection data. All NPM staff & enumerators are trained on protection principles.

Activities or outputs

1. SMSD will strengthen community participation by forming additional/strengthening existing AGD groups; support them in project design/implementation. Extend & ensure Women Participation Projects (WPP) are rolled out based on UN Women's EPL curriculum.Harmonize approach to community engagement, feedback & messaging in IOM’s AoR. Ensure Rohingya input in agendas through community listening program & Interagency Common Feedback & Complaints Mechanism.Strengthen representation committees in IOM’s AoR; create representation structures where possible.Contribute to the Sector’s Capacity Sharing Initiative in training up to 180 GoB staff to support CiC camp management activities.Strengthen coordination of service providers; prioritize facilities/services; avoid duplication/advocate for solutions. Strengthen emergency preparedness/response at camp & catchment levels. Improve living conditions; reduce risks by improving infrastructure; build capacity of Rohingya volunteers to maintain camps. Implement DRR activities through CfW (includes women & PwD).Install additional solar streetlights based on community input.Coordinate major infrastructure interventions across camps in line with Macro Settlement Development Plan; guide sectors in rationalizing facilities/planning for decongestion.Support HC adjacent camps to promote social cohesion through CfW; WPP; linkage with TRD initiatives.2. SMEP will use its machines/operating teams for the development/maintenance of vehicle access roads & major drainage; small roads & drainage installations will accommodate changes in infrastructure in 2020. Activities will be operationalised via 17 crews within the camps, resourced with light machinery/technical personnel to provide practical guidance.3 casting yards will be maintained across KBE with equipment/operatives, tools & materials to fabricate reinforced concrete cross-drain slabs, slope stabilisation panels & bamboo fencing. SMEP will provide technical support to the LGED. 3. DRR | DMCs are mandated by GoB at different administrative levels. These structures need training/capacity development. Interventions will facilitate community risk assessments. Risk reduction action plans will be formulated in 16 unions.Cyclone preparedness volunteers will be trained in response management. Fire safety volunteers will be trained on fire safety/early rescue. Committees will be trained on evacuation/contingency plans & briefed on their responsibilities as first responders.Cyclone shelters are used as schools & will be rehabilitated. All entrance roads will be repaired/nearby ponds cleaned. There will be no duplication. A mechanism mapping out shelters was formulated by REACH & includes BDRCS/CPP efforts. All district DRM institutions will be provided with emergency kits, first-aid/early warning equipment for emergency use. Shelters to be provided with emergency kits for use in an evacuation & supplied with assistive devices for PwD. A safety plan will be implemented in cyclone shelters. Evacuation plans/drills will be developed to instill preparedness culture among children. School committees will be activated/oriented on responsibilities. 4. NPM will provide site imagery via UAV footage; elevation modelling for risk analysis/planning; mapping; IM/GIS; rapid needs assessments for emergencies; facility rationalization, mapping. Continue the Daily Incident Assessment, which captures key information on the impact of weather-related incidents. Support the Sector through a facilities monitoring tool, allowing actors to 1)monitor infrastructure changes periodically 2)provide overview of service coverage across a geographic area 3)provide a planning tool for installation of roads/infrastructure & its potential impact. Thematic research determined via consultation with key stakeholders/WGs to inform operations. NPM alongside UNHCR, ISCG & the Sector will develop IM training modules for RRRC staff in 2020.
