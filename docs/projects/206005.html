Project Summary

TADOs intent for E/SNFI intervention on the highlighted context below is based on Organizational Assessments, IPC reports on Food Security and UNOCHA Humanitarian and Situational Reports that led to calls on Humanitarian Organizations to conduct Inter-Related Needs Assessments on the affected areas and populations affected. Below is the situational analysis on areas TADO is proposing to intervene and the circumstances.
In the various counties and their payams on this proposal, the main causal of the displacements and the dire situation in the counties of Ulang, Maiwut, and Nyirol is attributed to;
- In Ulang and Maiwut County of Upper Nile State, the suspension of food assistance in Ethiopian refugee camps of Gambella region and inter-communal conflicts (between the Anyuak and the Nuer) that led to high influx of refugees/returnees from Kule 1, 2 & 3, Jewi and Pinyidu refugee camps in Ethiopia and Rabak camp from Sudan to seek refuge in South Sudan. The IRNA conducted by Humanitarian partners (on 16-21 Aug 2023) and The DTM estimated figures at 134HH with 800 individuals from Ethiopia and 987HH 1,413 individuals from Sudan. In Maiwut County that was faced by the same influx, an IRNA conducted by ADRA and RRC reported on 15th August 2023 reflected 4070 individuals recorded.
-On Nyirol County, reported an alarming rise of returnee’s influx as repeatedly reported by the RRC Coordinator. The increasing number of the returnees from Ethiopia was due to shortage of Humanitarian Food Assistance at the Gambella Refugee Camps and the Ethnic fighting between Anuak and Nuer tribes at the camps. A joint Inter-Agency and multisectoral Assessment and verification was conducted from 21st -22nd August to establish the actual figures and measure the general situation of the returnees. The actual number verified was 17, 707 individuals.
In Nyirol County, the outbreak of Sudan conflict resulted to massive displacement with people moving to South Sudan as returnees and other nationalities asylum seekers. Nyirol RRC Directors office has reported more than 3,543 returnees and 230 asylum seekers. An IRNA assessment was carried out from 5th – 12th July 2023.
On the above, the level of vulnerability among the residents, the IDPs and Returnees is very high with few actors on the ground overwhelmed. The assessment findings among the communities affected included; shortage of food as food is shared with relatives from the residents, cases of acute and severely acute malnourished were observed with nutrition mobile outreach sites are overwhelmed, the returnees have reported cases of Malaria, fever, skin disease and cold among the children and elderly along the way. Lack of NFIs/shelter materials as majority of returnees especially those residing at Commissioner’s compound seen sleeping in an open place without blankets, mosquito nets, and sleeping nets. In addition, majority of the returnees have reported to be sharing shelters with friends and relatives from residents. There were cases of unaccompanied and separated children from their parents, food security, documentation by the Asylum seekers reported to have lost, Health and WASH/SNFIs.
TADO intends to carry out E/SNFI activities in Upper Nile and Jonglei State County Payams of 26,000 individuals. In Upper Nile, Ulang County (7,000 individuals) and Maiwut County (9,000 Individuals) while in Jonglei, Nyirol County 10,000 Individuals.
TADO will have a monitoring plan that will ensure smooth implementation of the project including post distribution monitoring that will help assess the impact of the interventions by TADO in collaboration with other actors ad the cluster.

1. Needs

Ulang County: Ongoing Sudan conflict Crisis ,suspension of food ratios in Ethiopia camps which lead to inter-communal fighting in Ethiopian refugee camps happened to be of the primary reasons for high influx of refugee and returnees. IRNA was carried out by partners from 16-21 August 2023 to assess the needs and conditions of the returnees. DTM report confirmed the figures as 134HH with 800 individuals from Ethiopia and 987HH 1,413 individuals from Sudan. The main challenges/needs included; limited shelter and Non-Food Items like kitchen sets, blankets, sleeping mats, jerricans, and mosquito nets. Others included limited Health/Wash facilities, Nutrition,protection and food security.TADO intends to intervene in ESNFI distribution to 8000 individuals in Ulang County in ( Doma, Kuormok, Yomding and Ulang Centre Payams ).Maiwut County: The ethnic fighting and food crisis in refugee camps in Gambella with fighting from Sudan contributed to the influx of the returnees to South Sudan. An IRNA report dated 15 August 2023 by ADRA and RRC protection Team indicated 4070 individuals were recorded. The findings recorded that most of the IDPs and Returnees lacked SNFI materials including plastic sheets, Kitchen sets, mats,mosquito nets, solar lamps and sleeping mats. Other essential services were limited including Limited Health and WASH facilities, Nutrition and Food Security and livelihood. Elderly persons, women and children are the most vulnerable at risk of mosquito infestation and extremely cold weather. TADO therefore intends to intervene to the mostly affected vulnerable Residents, Returnees and IDPs in Maiwut County ( Jekow and Maiwut Payams ) targeting 10000 individuals. In Nyirol the outbreak of Sudan conflict resulted to massive displacement with people moving to South Sudan as returnees and other nationalities asylum seekers. Nyirol RRC Directors office has reported more than 3,543 returnees and 230 asylum seekers. An IRNA assessment was carried out from 5th – 12th July 2023.The main needs assessed included; there were cases of unaccompanied and separated children from their parents, food security, documentation by the Asylum seekers reported to have lost, Health and WASH, SNFIs. TADO intends to carry out in the 2 locations of Pultruk and Thol targeting 8000 individuals.

2. Project activities

This project will be implemented in line with the following cluster strategies/activities. These activities and priorities will;a) Provide access to emergency shelter (in-kind) with a mix of cash e.g. Emergency shelter materials will be provided as well as cash and the beneficiaries will use a combination of provided materials, cash to buy S/NFIs where there is market, local materials and labor to setup new shelter. b) Provide access to life-saving NFIs (in-kind) NFI personnel will access and distribute life-saving NFIs such as blankets, mosquito nets, kitchen sets and mosquito nets to beneficiaries to enable them to mitigate the harsh conditions facing them. c) Shelter upgrades (in kind) IDPs, returnees and host community whose shelter is dilapidated will be supported to repair/rehabilitate them using both local materials and plastic sheets provided d) Replenish essential NFI and emergency shelter. The project intervention will build as a way of obtaining feedback resilience by ensuring that life-saving NFIs and emergency shelter supplies are integrated with Livelihood project of vegetable growing for resilience and are adequate for the beneficiaries so that they do not get exposed to life-threatening conditions. e) Engage the community in a way that stimulates active participation Collaborative meetings and consultations will be held throughout the intervention. Training and appropriate context-specific messaging to create awareness and build resilience will be shared f) Mainstream HLP and conflict sensitive approaches throughout all stages of S/NFI response Beneficiaries will be exposed to HLP messaging to reduce harm and build resilience by building on existing systems and community dynamics. g) Mainstream GBV throughout all stages of S/NFI response This will ensure that gender and protection risks are addressed directly, mitigating chances of exacerbated vulnerabilities h) Mainstream HLP issues throughout the stages of S/NFI response as land is the most single factor of production and livelihood for sustainability, i) Community engagement and consultation in the implementation of S/NFI response will be critical as a way of feedback mechanism.
