Project Summary

VSO in partnership with Shushilan is implementing Education in Emergency (EiE) programme in Cox’s Bazar to provide equitable, vital education and safe space for learning to help crisis affected Rohingya and Host community children. The programme aims to provide community based accessible Early Childhood Care and Education (ECCE) female caregiver (Big Sister/Mother) for Rohingya and Host community children and transitional learning opportunities for Rohingya children (aged 6-14) in a safe environment. VSO is also responding to COVID-19 situation within the project area by distributing hygiene kits and awareness activities. VSO’s intervention is designed to contribute significantly to gender equality. The different needs of girls and boys have been analyzed and integrated well in the activities and objectives. Sex and Age disaggregated data was collected to understand the different needs of the groups.
The project will promote inclusive teaching methods, ensuring participation of girls and boys by building the capacity of the teachers through using gender-sensitive methodologies. The project targets to increase coordination and collaboration among stakeholders to improve the quality of education provision for Rohingya community children that is aligned with education sector standards. Children living with disabilities are also enrolled in Community Based Centers. The project will continue identify the children with the disabilities(impairment) and other special needs who can access the ECCE in community-based centers. VSO highlights gender responsive programme through enrolment, recruitment material development, center management and teaching learning process. Social inclusion and gender’ are one of VSO’s core approaches, along with resilience and social accountability.
VSO integrate technology-based education solution with ECCE intervention using VSO school app following the curriculum of MeshGuides, and VSO family booklet in partnership with Ustad Mobile. The app is to support learning and provides an innovative and portable way of accessing interactive content for facilitators with no professional experience of teaching.

1 - Identified Needs

A number of national and international NGOs and the government are working to secure the education for Rohingya children in such fragile context, but still more efforts need to be made to make education for these excluded children include girls, and children with disabilities. With Covid-19 pandemic from March 2020, education activities are still to be resume and the enrolled children are out of learning for long time. Therefore, not only for the new children, but organizations need to come forward with different strategies and commitment for the already enrolled children. VSO will continue work with families and volunteers to develop community-based early childhood education spaces, whilst training them to become community educators. MSNA Factsheets suggest that only 60% of boys and 57% girls, aged, 3-5 in Rohingya camps reported to have attended a TLC even before Covid-19. VSO will target this aged child to bring them in to the education. Realizing the scarcity of available spaces in the camp, VSO will mostly work with community based ECCE programme as an expansion; as for the including more students, aged 6-4 for the learning centers, VSO already have 12 Learning Centers in camp 15, and for the newly targeted camp 11, VSO will engage with existing partners and Camp in Charge (CiC) of camp 11 to explore the existing learning centers or will set up the new ones. No doubt that the host communities are very much affected due to this influx and several children in the host communities have not received any form of education. J-MSNA Report (October 2020) suggests that 40% of individuals, aged, 4-24 were reported to not have attended any form of learning before Covid-19. VSO has been supporting children within the Rohingya refugee community at the camp 15 (Jamtoli) through community based ECCE programme, and we aim to initiate this successful intervention for the children in the host communities given the extremely low number of school going children there. MSNA factsheets (host community) suggest that only 17% young girls and 12% young boys, aged, 4, have attended any form of formal learning even before Covid-19 outbreaks. J-MSNA reports mention that several children indicated availability of learning materials as one of the most significant challenges. By developing and adopting novel technologies, VSO can support educators and caregivers to access quality learning materials, wherever they are. We support volunteers to create low-cost, sustainable resources, so a lack of supplies doesn’t have to be a barrier to learning. Alongside developing alternative and accelerated education programme to make sure young learners don’t miss out at this crucial time, we provide psychosocial support to help children cope with the stresses of their experience and environment. Creating safe spaces for learning and play helps bring a sense of normality, allowing those who’ve suffered trauma to regain their childhood. The project considers the child protection issues as high importance as well as cross cutting components towards all interventions Due to lack of awareness and some Traditional Harmful Practices, the Rohingya children particularly girls are supported to continue their education. Sexual Reproductive Health and Rights (SRHR) are sensitive issue and have many stigmas and myths attached to it. Girls are not confident to share their concerns. In addition, educators may not feel comfortable discussing SRHR due to lack of knowledge or cultural reasons. Institutions may also not be prepared to include SRHR in their program due to the negative attitude attached to it. VSO has developed a sensitive approach based on involvement of relevant stakeholders to address misconceptions and stigma attached to SRHR.
