Project Summary

The Whole of Syria Inter-Agency Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) program, hosted by IOM, works to support the entire Syrian humanitarian response in providing one common voice and joint, coherent, implementation of international commitments on PSEA.
Since its beginning in 2017, the program has established a robust inter-agency PSEA Network with hubs in Gaziantep, Damascus and Northeast Syria. At the end of 2019 the Network had 88 member organizations and 192 PSEA focal points. With great growth in membership over the past months, enhanced coordination and the implementation of PSEA interventions specifically designed for the remote context, the program aims at reaching a total of 300 member organizations in 2020. The PSEA Strategic Plan for 2020 has been developed in close collaboration with member organizations and working groups. In Northeast, it will involve the NES Forum, in Northwest it will involve the clusters and in Damascus the PSEA Coordinator and Gen Cap Advisor will be engaged in Network activities.
The program contributes to the protection of civilians, the reduction of suffering and to building the resilience of affected populations throughout the Syria Response by providing technical support to humanitarian actors participating in the inter-agency PSEA Networks in all hubs. Building upon the accomplishments in 2019, which included the provision of training to more than 370 humanitarian workers from 68 organizations, 3 specialized workshops on PSEA investigations for more than 100 network participants, the creation of a pilot interagency call center based on the results of extensive community consultations inside Syria, and the development of specially designed PSEA awareness posters and materials distributed to for more than 600,000 humanitarian staff and beneficiaries, in 2020 the program will focus on harmonizing activities across the three hubs of Gaziantep, Northeast Syria and Damascus. Reflective of the interests of Network Members in all three hubs, special emphasis will be given to expanding investigatory capacities, the integration and expansion of Community Based Complaints Mechanisms and enhanced referral mechanisms, consistent with AAP principles.
Importantly, working in conjunction with AAP and related initiatives, the program will reach out to and include additional humanitarian stakeholders, support organizations in ensuring effective community mobilization and response mechanisms, work directly with clusters and working groups to systematize protection and mitigation measures as part of a comprehensive PSEA response and continue to develop local leadership to build sustainability of the Network.
Activities in 2020 will be guided by the provisions of the 2020 PSEA Program Strategy:
Pillar 1: Accountability, Monitoring and Evaluation
Pillar 2: PSEA Mainstreaming and Capacity Building
Pillar 3: Outreach and Engagement
Pillar 4: Community Mobilization and Response
Pillar 5: Sustainability and Knowledge Management

4 - Needs

Despite the significant progress made in instituting international commitments on PSEA in the Syrian response thus far, incidents of sexual exploitation and abuse continue to occur, causing damage and distress to populations in need. The inter-agency program conducted community consultations in northwest Syria in 2018 in the governorates of Aleppo, Idleb and Hama. The consultation found that one in six persons reported having been asked for something in exchange for aid. An additional 7.5 per cent preferred not to answer the question, indicating that the 1-in-6 figure may be an underestimate. It also noted that misconduct remains chronically under-reported because of fear of retaliation, the low level of trust the affected people have in the existing complaints mechanisms, and the perception that reporting misconduct would not result into a positive outcome. These findings underscore the imperative that a comprehensive PSEA response architecture, including safe and accessible reporting channels, should continue to be strengthened. One of the main challenges related to PSEA is the lack of knowledge concerning the prohibition against SEA and the mechanisms and responses available to members of affected communities. Reporting is also hindered by the fact that SEA remains a culturally sensitive issue. Affected populations are reluctant to report incidents for fear of consequences to both the perpetrators and the survivors. When a case is reported, a lack of investigatory capacity and the challenges associated with investigating in the remote context in which many organizations work, make already complex and sensitive investigations that much more difficult. The failure to investigate and to act on SEA cases potentially demotivates members of affected populations to report and the integrity of the PSEA program and commitments are impacted.

5 - Implementation of activities

The program is coordinated by IOM, under the responsibility of the UN Syria leadership (Regional Humanitarian Coordinator, Deputy Regional Humanitarian Coordinator and Humanitarian Coordinator in Damascus). It will make available funding for PSEA coordinator positions and/or implementation of activities inside Syria based on priorities from various hubs (Damascus, northwest and northeast). The activities listed below will contribute to the overall objective of decreasing the risk of crisis-affected communities experiencing incidents of sexual exploitation and abuse. • Awareness-raising and Training on PSEA commitments through in-person sensitization and online sessions for humanitarian workers and senior management of UN entities, INGOs, and NGOs.• Increasing Engagement of humanitarian actors in quantity and quality through outreach campaigns with various stakeholders.• PSEA mainstreaming through systematic integration of PSEA into sector specific assessments and activities with a focus on mainstreaming, prevention and mitigation measures.• Building investigative capacity through trainings, expert coaching and mentorship of relevant organizations.• Conducting information-sharing sessions on beneficiary rights and safe and confidential SEA reporting, promoting the use of inter-agency procedures for PSEA cooperation and reporting SEA incidents.• Maintaining database of SEA cases with utmost compliance with data protection standards and analysis of trends. Throughout Network activities gender and age are systematically addressed. Organizations are asked to be represented by one female and one male PSEA focal point for gender balance. Gender and age sensitivities, including child safeguarding principles and the principles of Do no Harm are integrated into all Network activities and methodologies, as are specific gender and age safety considerations. Additionally, in line with the Gender and Age Marker, targeted actions are driven by needs assessments and data.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks: 1. Physical security risk due to conflict activity and barriers to access. 2. Beneficiaries unwilling to report PSEA through reporting mechanism 3. Resistance or backlash from the community on PSEA messaging 4. Lack of timely and accurate information sharing and referral of beneficiaries’ complaints may expose survivors to further harm. Mitigation Measures: 1. Through inter-agency collaboration, monitor security risks and barriers to access to ensure safety of staff and beneficiaries 2. Through community consultations and outreach, engage in awareness raising with the community on confidentiality provisions of PSEA reporting mechanisms. 3. Ensure that PSEA communication materials are properly contextualized. 4. Ensure that all staff with direct engagement with beneficiaries receive training on PSEA and have capacity to provide guidance on how to report SEA and the process. Throughout Network activities gender and age are systematically addressed. To ensure gender balance, member organizations are asked to be represented by one female and one male PSEA focal point. Gender and age sensitivities, including child safeguarding principles and the principles of Do no Harm are integrated into all Network activities and methodologies. Specific safety considerations relating to different genders and age groups are considered when assessing activity risk, and relevant mitigation measures are taken. Additionally, in line with the Gender and Age Marker, targeted actions are driven by needs assessments and data.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: • Deteriorating political/security situation with repercussions for access • Insufficient funding Assumptions: • Safe and secure access to communities to allow for awareness raising activities amongst the field staff and beneficiaries • That if secure channels and referral pathways are established to solicit complaints from affected population, that they will have enough trust and willingness to report SEA • Inter-agency differences do not hamper communication and planning • Sufficient funding is available • Inter-agency coordination will include PSEA concerns and efforts consistently • The PSEA Networks in all hubs will enjoy wide participation of agencies to allow for scaling up of PSEA mainstreaming in activities within their respective programming. Mitigation measures: • Continued advocacy on the importance of PSEA among all relevant stakeholders including donors, the Government of Syria, and humanitarian leadership and sectors/clusters. • Open and transparent dialogue on PSEA amongst the humanitarian community and with key stakeholders • Implementation using a consensus-based, and inter-agency (PSEA Network-centered) approach.
