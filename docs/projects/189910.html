Project Summary

The project will target vulnerable young children, pregnant and breastfeeding women—both displaced and in host communities, addressing immediate nutrition related deprivations by providing nutrition services and promoting optimal feeding practices to meet nutrition requirements as well as adoption of best practices on healthcare, WASH and integrated childcare.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

UNICEF aim to prevent further deterioration of the nutrition situation among women and children under five through the scaling up of proven set of high impact nutrition intervention in a two-pronged approach of prevention and treatment of malnutrition. This will be supported through outreach activities, e.g. integrated mobile brigades, for nutrition screenings, micronutrients supplementation & deworming and community management of acute malnutrition. The Nutrition Rehabilitation Programme at the hospital, health centers and community level will serve as main entry point for timely provision of quality care for SAM children, while adopting simplified approaches for improved program coverage, increasing HR capacity, technical assistance and strengthen supply chain.UNICEF will support Ministry of Health and partners in strengthening linkages between TDC and PIN to expand coverage and quality of community-based services to support IYCF-E, including cooking demonstrations and setting IYCF corners in resettlement sites, early identification of malnutrition and adoption of health, nutrition and WASH practices to prevent malnutrition among IDPs and host communities. Synergies with health, food security and WASH sectors will be strengthened, while mainstreaming PSEA and child disability into the nutrition response. UNICEF will support prepositioning of lifesaving nutrition commodities for SAM and conduct nutrition assessments to inform program.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Protection issues for nutrition mainly concern the security while accessing and utilizing nutrition services delivered at health facility and mobile brigades which are normally located and built in such a way that limits protection risks. Nutrition humanitarian partners will be trained on specific protection considerations and referrals pathways, in close collaboration with child protection services to support protection mechanisms for vulnerable children.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

UNICEF uses mechanism such as Linha Verde and promote community dialogues to assess specific health and nutrition needs of different population groups and their access to quality nutrition services. Furthermore, feedback mechanisms are part of the community-based platforms and will be enforced by the district level health teams. Accessibility and follow-up issues will be discussed on a bi-weekly basis in the subnational cluster coordination meetings.Social behaviour change and community engagement is a key component of these activities. Communities, both host communities and new resettlement sites for displaced persons, will be engaged and mobilized to adopt key preventive and protective practices to improve the well-being of children zero through five and their caregivers. Communication and active community engagement platforms will stimulate demand for services and enhance beneficiary’s knowledge to practice home-based and community behaviours that positively impact children’s survival, growth and early childhood development. Key behaviours that will be promoted will include breastfeeding, young child feeding, early childhood development, handwashing and hygiene, training caregivers on positive caregiving practices, signs and how to address malnutrition, and how to recognize disabilities in young children.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Malnutrition and disability are closely interrelated and have a number of points of convergence. Commonly this relationship is framed as a lack of appropriate nutrition in both pregnant and breastfeeding mothers and young children leading to impairment. Therefore, UNICEF efforts focus on improving maternal and child nutrition to prevent or mitigate disability, while strengthening the early identification of developmental delays among children with SAM.The inclusion of children with disabilities will be achieved through a) capacity building of UNICEF’s implementing partner; b) training of health workers on early identification of developmental delays; c) data collection/disaggregation for medical mobile brigades and d) mainstreaming disability and identification of development delays among SAM children.Case management of acute malnutrition services will be adapted to include children with disabilities or caregivers of children with disabilities as 5 per cent of the total target.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Climate change exacerbate undernutrition through three main causal pathways: 1) impacts on household access to sufficient, safe and adequate food; 2) impacts on care and feeding practices; and 3) impacts on environmental health and access to health services.The Global Climate Risk Index scores Mozambique fifth among the countries most affected by natural disasters over the period 2000-2019, and first in 2019, having experienced cyclones of unprecedented severity that affected nearly 2.5 million people, many of them from Northern provinces. Aligned with the UNICEF Nutrition Strategy 2020-2030, UNICEF will: a) mainstream assessments on impact of climate change related effects on maternal and child diets in the planned nutrition assessments, b) increase knowledge and awareness raising for action through SBCC programs for use of locally adapted foods who are more resilient to climate change and c) support implementation of climate resilient emergency programming and adaptations.
