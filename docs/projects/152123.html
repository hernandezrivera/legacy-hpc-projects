Project Summary

UNICEF through its partners aims to contribute to scaling up equitable access to quality formal and non-formal education for 39,000 most vulnerable school age children on the move (boys and girls aged 6 to 17) in greater geographic coverage particularly in the conflicted-affected/underserved areas including Azzawiya, Benghazi, Misrata, Murzuq, Tripoli and Zwara through the provision of non-formal education opportunities, teacher training, education supplies, and improvement of learning spaces.
Also, UNICEF will strengthen the capacity of 200 teaching personnel in Education in Emergency (EiE) and conflict resolution and tolerance to ensure needs of the children and adolescents are timely addressed in the protracted crisis/in sudden onset of emergencies.

Needs

- There are 28,583 migrant children and 24,098 refugee children that have little access to education opportunities in Libya (DTM 2018). Data shows that while Tripoli, Misrata, and Ejdabia are the top three mantikas hosting migrants, for refugees Tripoli, Ejdabia, Alkufra are the top three mantikas where refugees are present.- In Libya, Arabic speaking children from these specific countries Syria, Palestine, Sudan, Tunisia, Morocco, Algeria, Egypt and Mauritania, including refugees and asylum seekers, have access to Libyan public schools. However, even among children who are able to enroll, lack of official documents remains a challenge for children to receive certification. This means that while children have access to formal education, not all of them will get the certification needed at the end.- Children on the move and children with disabilities, are systematically discriminated against and have difficulty accessing education. Children that are non-Arabic speakers face the main difficulty as the main barriers include legal status, language and cultural difference, discrimination and lack of documentation etc. - There is little data about the existence of community schools that cater to non Arabic speaking refugee and migrant children. The curriculum that is being taught, is not standardized, data around the number of these schools in the country is not available and the qualifications of teacher vary. All of this, severely impacts the quality of education services provided to a vulnerable population. - Access by humanitarian/education partners to this population has been constrained, thus, little support has been provided to children in need. Data collection and analysis has been extremely limited and challenged, in addition to the little presence of national/local partners that can respond to humanitarian/education needs.

Activities

- Provision of language appropriate essential teaching and learning materials to school age children - Provision of non-formal education along with psychosocial support in safe and protective environment. Activities will be tailored based on the needs of age group, gender, and status. Particularly, coordination with UNICEF’s Children Protection program and other key actors, IOM and UNHCR is essential for interventions for children on the move. - Improving learning environment through the rehabilitation of community schools or alternative learning spaces including inclusive and gender-sensitive WASH facilities in coordination with UNICEF’s WASH program/partners. - Professional development of teaching personnel in Education in Emergencies (EiE) including psychosocial support, and conflict-resolution & tolerance.
