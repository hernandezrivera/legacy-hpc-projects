Project Summary

The intervention intends to enhance the protection and learning outcomes of 10,000 individuals, including children, through the provision of integrated child protection and education services and strengthening community-level mechanisms. In this frame, 6,000 individuals (5,000 children and 1,000 caregivers) will benefit from child protection services which will improve children, adolescents & caregivers’ subjective wellbeing. In addition, 960 conflict-affected out-of-school children will have access to safe, inclusive, and protective learning opportunities. Following the completion of the non-formal education curriculum, the learners will be transitioned into formal education. Through the community-based mechanism, community members will benefit from awareness-raising sessions on child protection issues. Furthermore, Tdh will coordinate with other humanitarian actors to ensure effective service delivery and referral of children who require specialized services. As such, 50 humanitarian actors (CP and non-CP actors) will benefit from protection mainstreaming, PSEA, safeguarding, safe identification, and referral of at-risk and vulnerable children. To ensure effectiveness and sustainability, the program will be delivered in close collaboration with community and government stakeholders such Bulamas, Lawan, camp leaders, SAME, MWASD, LGEA, and SUBEB. The intervention in Rann will be delivered remotely through community-based mechanism (volunteers and community members) under the guidance of staff who will conduct regular technical field support visits (minimum twice a week) based on UNHAS availability. In Pulka, activities will be facilitated and led by locally recruited staff and community volunteers at the existing spaces for children to learn and play.

1 - Needs

Rann (Kala-Balge Local Government Area), composed of 37,842 individuals, is a landlocked location suffering from massive humanitarian needs since 2016. In Nigeria (ranked at 8.5 in the Children’s Climate Risk Index), Rann is one of the most isolated locations prone to flooding resulting from the rainy season, punctual water release from Cameroonian dam which results in cutting the road access and regular harassment by insurgents (latest attack on October 28th, 2021). The attack led by a Non-State Armed Group took control of the entire city for a few hours on August 30th, 2021 (resulted in displacement of 1,769 individuals to Cameroon). All these factors make of Rann one of the most challenging areas operationally in humanitarian contexts in Borno State. Heightened access constrains and annual flooding that destroys hectares of cultivated fields, hinders road access from Ngala to Rann during the rainy season (road access only from December to June in best case scenario and from February to June at worst) and put the population at very high-risk WASH-related diseases prevalence. As for Pulka, which is one of the localities of Gwoza LGA, the town comprises of 5 formal IDP camps namely Camp A, B, C, D, E and one Transit camp. In April 2020, it was estimated that the camp population stood at 5,048 households (26,978 individuals) across the 5 formal IDP camps, while the transit camp hosted 2,710 individuals. This report also highlighted that among Pulka population, the majority is leaving in the host community while less than 40% was living in camps settings. Outside Pulka town, the security remains tenuous, and people are at high risk of protection concerns.Both localities are characterized by their high percentage of Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and the lack of humanitarian access. Displacement, increased poverty, the danger of severe injury or death, reduced opportunities for socialization, play and education, recruitment risks, coupled with uncertainty, all undermine the protective environment for children and young people.TLSs and schools can serve as a protective environment for children to prevent physical dangers around them (including abuse, exploitation, and recruitment into armed groups) and can create space for them to receive psychosocial support and the lifesaving messages they need to cope with the situation. However, many of them are unable to access education due to displacement, while others are out of school due to the impact of the conflict on their communities or live-in host communities where education facilities are non-existent or overstretched.In addition, children are at the forefront of current crises, often families engage children in income-generating activities, this increases the likelihood of exposure to protection risks i.e. abduction, sexual violence, forced recruitment, child labor, early marriages etc. Food insecurity in the target location leads to school drop-out and child labor.In emergency settings, violence against children increases due to the erosion of social protection mechanisms, insecurity, and unmet basic needs. Many children idle and disengaged young people are at risk of developing bad coping mechanisms (such as drugs, alcohol).

2 - Activities

• Provision of case management services for at-risk or at risks children through Tdh trained and experienced case work officers. The case work officers will work with caregivers and children to assess, plan and manage the cases. Beneficiaries will be identified through vulnerability and priority criteria. • Through the referral pathway, Tdh will collaborate with other humanitarian actors to respond to children who need specialized services. Tdh will refer and follow up on the response from the agencies that received the cases. In the case where the agency could not fully respond to the case but only to limited service, Tdh will provide financial and/or material support as recommended by the specialist agency. • Age appropriate structured psychosocial, recreational and creative activities for children will be provided by trained and experienced PSS volunteers under the guidance of PSS officers. These activities intend to improve emotional wellbeing of conflict affected children and adolescents. • Psychosocial services and positive parenting sessions for caregivers of vulnerable children will be provided through Emotional Support Groups delivered by trained and experienced PSS volunteers under the guidance of PSS officers. The eight-week structured PSS sessions will cover topics such as psychosocial well-being and parenting skills. The caregivers will have access to basic life skills and referral services and support their psychosocial well-being. • Life skills education for adolescent girls and boys will be provided. The life skills sessions with adolescents will cover topics such as self-awareness, empathy, interpersonal and relationship skills, communication, critical thinking, creative thinking, decision making, problem solving, coping with stress and emotions. Tdh will work with the children and adolescents to use varied methodologies such as You Create, Move On and Engage, RIDE ON, football for protection, sports for protection tools. • Support community-based mechanisms through engagement and awareness raising to address community practices and attitudes that affect the rights of children. The intervention will set up or strengthen child protection focal points to monitor, document, respond and refer child protection issues. The child protection focal points will undertake community awareness sessions and events on thematic issues of child protection. Tdh staff and volunteers will provide regular follow-up support in the implementation of the community action plan. • Capacity building for community-based child protection committees, child protection staff, and CP and non-CP actors on safeguarding, PSEA, child protection mainstreaming, issues and standards. • Accountability of affected population mechanism will be set up or strengthen in the target locations. The mechanism will include training sessions for CFRM group members, and provision of equipment and materials for community sensitization. The group will be supported to raise awareness in the community. Regular review and feedback meeting representatives from community stakeholders including local authorities and beneficiaries will be held. Education• Existing temporary learning spaces (TLS) will be rehabilitated or equipped. • Conflict-affected out-of-school boys and girls (5-17 years) will access non-formal education in the Temporary Learning Spaces (TLS). The learners will be certified and transitioned into formal education by LGEA, SAME and SUBEB. • Teaching and learning materials including SIB kits, ECD kits and recreational kits will be provided for education volunteers, learners at the TLS, learners transitioned and formal school where they are transitioned. • Education volunteers and teachers will benefit from training on improved teaching and learning approaches. • School-based management committee (SBMCs) members will be trained on school management, leadership, resource mobilization and management.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Diversity and disability inclusion assessment will be conducted in target locations to understand the situation of marginalized groups in access to services. The outcome will help develop modality in ensuring unhindered access and friendly IEC materials, and activities. A deliberate effort will be put in place to ensure all community-based structures provide a quota for marginalized groups to be represented. Community members including girls, youths, and women will be involved in consultations and decision-making including beneficiary selection criteria. Community members will participate in project design, assessment, and implementation. Tdh will organize community-led joint monitoring to assess the progress, emerging changes and suggest areas for improvement. All staff and volunteers working with the affected population will be trained in safeguarding and PSEA and community members will be trained and participate in awareness sessions on PSEA and safeguarding. In addition, community-led complaint feedback and response mechanism will be set up. Lastly, a protection mainstreaming survey targeting humanitarian actors will be delivered to understand how protection is being mainstreamed into other sectors. The outcome of the survey will inform the training of humanitarian actors in protection mainstreaming. Joint protection monitoring field visits and reflection sessions will be conducted by humanitarian to assess mainstreaming of protection in the implementation of project activities.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will provide timely, quality information and concise communication with community stakeholders and beneficiaries to ensure accountability for all activities implemented. The project intends to establish and/or strengthen the accountability of the affected population mechanism to ensure regular feedback and adaptation. Tdh staff and volunteers working for the project will communicate with beneficiaries in local languages that are easily understood, respectful, and culturally appropriate for different members of the community, especially vulnerable and marginalized groups. The intervention will ensure feedback on beneficiaries’ level of satisfaction and efficacy of the aid received. The feedback will be delivered at all stages of the program through community meetings, satisfaction surveys, focus group discussions, key informant interviews, CFRM groups, community dialogues, and community leaders. Affected populations will be involved in the program assessments as key informants and/or actors who collect data. Tdh will ensure that the results of the assessment will be presented to the affected population for more discussion. All Tdh staff, volunteers including vendors will sign Tdh code of conduct and child safeguarding policy to protect children from violence, abuse, exploitation, and violence. Furthermore, the address concern@tdh.ch will be diffused among affected population and communities to receive complaints on staff misconduct, any discriminatory practices, corruption or abuse of power cases, lack of quality of an activity or product delivered, lack of communication and information, and any potential negative effect or harm caused by the project.
