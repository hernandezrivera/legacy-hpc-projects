Project Summary

Recurrent Conflicts, floods and macro-economic challenges in South Sudan continue to worsen nutrition emergency. According to the SS 2023 FSNMS and SMART survey findings, the national GAM level remain above the WHO threshold of 15% with 1.65 M U5 children and 870,000 PLW at risk of acute malnutrition in 2024. Fangak and Nyirol Counties are classified as IPC 3 & 4 respectively with the latter having 3000 people classified in IPC Phase 5 (IPC Nov 2023), Similarly, they are among the 18 NC 2024 priority counties with multiple aggravating factors namely nutrition wasting, food insecurity, Poor WASH, Morbidity & GBV-in Emergency concern. This calls for a concerted effort for urgent treatment and prevention interventions and this is the background upon which this project has been designed and proposed.
In order to optimize results, an Integrated Package of interventions using nutrition sites as entry point will be adopted which involves: 1) Working with other sectors for multiple services vulnerable families 2) Collaboration with key sectors such as FSL, WASH, Nutrition, Health and GBV Interventions. In order to strengthen sustainable nutrition outcomes, the project shall adopt preventative actions that reduce the rate of malnutrition and improve the sustainability of treatments; enhance community engagement by taking services closer to households, through the roll out of the revised CNV guidelines and Family MUAC screening; as well as facility-based data analysis and feedback information to improve overall performance of service delivery and timely coordination of response decision making.
In the context conflict and flooding, services will be delivered from targeted static OTP-TSFP sites and mobile outreach services to reach the displaced with nutrition survival kits to ensure timely emergency response. The response activities will cover (1) case-finding screening using the Family-MUAC in all sites including IDP locations; (2) admission to OTP services for SAM U5 children and TSFP services using expanded criteria where needed including treatment of malaria, diarrhea and other high prevalence diseases (3) provide SC services for SAM children with medical complications; (4) deliver expanded MIYCN services to PLW and care-givers of children under 23 months through counselling PLW, care-providers and MtoM support groups, including vitamin A supplements, de-worming and kitchen gardens. To address these multiple drivers of chronic malnutrition, this project will scale up multi-sector solutions by working with co-located partners.
This project will strengthen AAP, by establishing QAAP committees and community complain and feedback mechanism in the nutrition facilities plus initiating community FGDs with community members. Similarly, programme staff will be trained on how to serve best the beneficiaries using the QAAP approach, while sensitization will focus on raising community awareness of systems in place for representation of their different interests and needs. Further there will be focus on raising awareness and providing information to beneficiaries on their rights and entitlement in all the nutrition sites and ensuring that communities are involved in the design, implementation, M and E on the planed nutrition project.
The drivers of GBV remain unchanged from past years. Therefore, this project will mainstream protection and disability inclusion and emphasize gender and age considerations and also conduct safety audits and collaborate with the targeted communities to implement protection practices to reduce risk of SGBV. To improve sustainability, this project will be integrated fully with health and use the BPHNS to guide targeted capacity building of both health and nutrition staff. Strengthening local capacity will target SMoH CHDs, project staff and CNVs as well as other common interest groups such as MtoMSG and local authorities with a focus on job-relevant training including refresher courses.

1. Needs

Conflict, flooding and macro-economic challenges continue to worsen the nutrition emergency in Fangak and Nyirol counties of Jonglei State. According to the 2023 FSNMS and SMART survey’s findings, the national GAM rates remain above the WHO emergency threshold of 15%, with 1.65 M U5 children and 870,000 PLW at risk of acute malnutrition in 2024 and will need urgent treatment (IPC Nov 2023). NC reports indicates that U5 SAM and MAM admission rates for the January to July 2023 period are significantly higher compared to the same period in 2022, and Fangak and Nyirol are among the counties with the highest increases (NC Program Updates, July 2023, slides 2- 4). Although PIN for the two counties stands at 78,730 and spread across them in varying proportions, the Cluster target is at 57,250 (Fangak 34,733 and Nyirol 22,517,) which explains the burden of malnutrition in the two targeted counties (NC PIN & CT 2024). All the two (2) counties targeted in this project are rated at the severity of need classification 4 for AMN. Malnutrition prevalence for PLW is 35.8% in Fangak and 65.4 % in Nyirol Counties. The underlying drivers of the crisis are inter-communal conflict and the economic crisis. Over the past 3 years, shocks from seasonal flooding and the ongoing Sudan conflicts have contributed to worsening the crisis. Flooding has caused displacement, loss of food-crops and damage to facilities causing service disruptions and access barrier for all those seeking nutrition services. Fangak and Nyirol, are among the worst food affected counties. Areas such as Juaibor, Keew and Nyadin in Fangak, Pultruk,Chuil & Pading in Nyirol have experienced severe flooding for 3 consecutive years. It is universally recognized that the sustained improvement in nutrition status can only be achieved when food security improves, when people have access to improved water and sanitation infrastructure, and adopt improved hygiene practices, and when people have access to prevention and treatments of malaria, diarrhea and other high prevalence diseases. Whereas attempts have been made by some IPs in addressing these multiple drivers of chronic malnutrition, not much deliberate efforts and investment to scale up multi-sector solutions have been registered. The fact that the NC is emphatic on the adoption of Integrated Package of interventions using nutrition sites as entry point exposes the need for strategic partnership and collaboration where each partner is expected to know the key Health, WASH, FSL & Nutrition partners and what they are doing & link together. This package clearly spells out the interplay between these sectors and the favourable nutrition outcomes. For instance, under FSL, it advocates for the establishment and maintenance of kitchen gardens at nutrition sites supporting nutrition facilities with demo site for vegetable as well as inclusion of CNVs in agronomic & fish preservation techniques training. Under WASH, the package advocates for the distribution of WASH kits to SAM/caretakers at nutrition sites, WASH facilities and Water points in nutrition sites and hygiene promotion. Whereas tangible success has been registered in integration with health, more needs to be done to ensure that critical services are offered e.g integration of SC in Health delivery, Prevention, testing and treatment malaria, Prevention and management of diarrhea including provision of Zinc & ORS as well as EPI (Immunization). Lastly the package is emphatic on mainstreaming child protection and GBV risk mitigation at nutrition sites. It is, however important to note that this package can only be complete if the Nutrition program scales up the provision of MIYCN counselling, micronutrients, blanket supplementary foods, detection and treatment of wasting as well as integration of child stimulation and ECD in nutrition sites.

2. Project activities

This project will focus on the implementation of a minimum package of actions to improve diets, services and practices for better nutrition outcomes. The project will be implemented in close collaboration with the national Ministry of Health-Nutrition Department and other clusters (WASH, Health, FSL & GBV). The project will provide for a gender-balanced team of frontline workers where 42% are female. Field personnel have been trained on CMA’s PSEA policy and all are expected to sign a PSEA code of conduct. The activities to achieve output targets are defined below: Objective 1: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality nutrition services to prevent malnutrition and build resilience among children, adolescents, and women in prioritized locations by the end of 2024. To achieve the above objective, the respective activities will include the following: 1.1 Prevention of malnutrition through behavior change communication, 1.2 Prevention of micronutrient deficiencies through micronutrient supplementation using campaign approach Objective 2: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of wasting for girls and boys under five years of age and PLW in prioritized locations by the end of 2024. For objective 2, the following activities will be prioritized: 2.1 Early detection and treatment of girls and boys under 5 years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition, 2.2 Conducting Monitoring & Supportive supervision visits, 2.3 Advocating for integration of SC in the health facility service delivery 2.4 Scaling up capacity strengthening efforts through on job training (OJT) & mentoring 2.5 Promoting community participation for a responsive nutrition service delivery 2.6 Supporting CNVs to undertake home visits, 2.7 Conducting spot exit interviews to assess the level of satisfaction of nutrition service delivery 2.8 Strengthening AAP Objective 3: To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis, and utilization of nutrition information by the end of 2024. Corresponding activities for objective 3 will include the following: 3.1 Supporting FSNMS survey to integrate nutrition indicators, 3.2 Supporting planning and roll- out of population-based surveys, 3.3 Conducting barrier analysis for MIYCN services 3.4 Improving facility-based data collection and management 3.5 Supporting roll-out of the Data Quality Assurance tool, 3.6 Improving nutrition indictors in DHS2. Objective 4: To strengthen coordination & integrated programming with health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors for equitable access to services that address key drivers of malnutrition among vulnerable children and women in prioritized locations by the end of 2024. Under objective 4 these activities will be undertaken: 4.1 Prevention, testing and treatment for malaria among PLW and children under five years with acute malnutrition, in nutrition sites, 4.2 Provision WASH kits to children with SAM in nutrition sites, 4.3 Sensitize households on the correct use of aquatabs at by the CNVs, 4.4 Sensitization of communities in the priority counties with hygiene and sanitation messages, 4.5 Establishment or maintenance of demonstration kitchen garden at nutrition sites or in the vicinity, 4.6 Integration of child stimulation and ECD in the provision of care for children with SAM and MAM, 4.7 Mainstreaming of GBV risk mitigation at nutrition sites, 4.8 Collaborate with health in the management of diarrhea, 4.9 Provide Zinc and ORS to children with diarrhea at health and nutrition sites.
