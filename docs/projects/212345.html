Project Summary

A. Needs:
The ongoing conflict in Syria, soon entering its fourteenth year, has caused immense destruction and unimaginable losses to the Syrian people and all aspects of their lives. Residential buildings have been severely damaged due to the war and the February 2023 earthquake, leading to a significant percentage of homes being partially or completely destroyed, forcing most residents to seek shelter elsewhere. The combination of low income, high construction material prices, and rehabilitation necessities hampers residents' ability to restore their homes.
Most families in the cities of Ma'arrat An Nu'man and Abul Thohur have lost their homes and possessions due to the war and the February 2023 earthquake, resulting in significant destruction that affected over 150 houses and families. Residents are gradually returning to these areas amid urgent need for home rehabilitation, repair, and provision of essential amenities. The crisis intensifies due to poverty among residents and their struggle to meet basic needs. There is a severe lack of support for building restoration efforts in these areas. Currently, most families find themselves in dire need of assistance to cover their shelter needs. Residents are forced to live in damaged homes that do not provide privacy or security, threatening their dignity and safety, especially during aftershocks. The urgent need for home rehabilitation and improvement to meet basic living requirements is vital for safe return of residents and early recovery across various sectors in these areas, contributing to restoring normal life, social stability, economic recovery, enhancing security, and fostering a sense of belonging.
B. Intervention, Implementation of Activities:
The regions of Ma'arrat An Nu'man and Abul Thohur were chosen due to a relatively large return of residents and urgent need compared to other areas. The rehabilitation of damaged homes will involve implementing minor repairs for residences affected by sanitary installations (Sanitary and electrical installations, Watertank, 6 doors, 2 windows), providing families with solar energy systems (battery, solar panel, charger), and essential living supplies (dignity kit, relief kit for newly returned families).
C. Protection Risk Analysis:
- Variation in aid distribution among targeted categories has a high impact and requires mechanisms for beneficiary acceptance, distribution, objections, and complaints.
- Medium impact challenges in accessing the main center will be mitigated by establishing mobile teams for distribution in rural areas.
- High impact from beneficiaries' negative reactions to aid disparity necessitates mechanisms for beneficiary acceptance, distribution, objections, and complaints.
- Exploitation by staff for personal gain is a high-impact risk that will be addressed by disseminating complaint contact information among beneficiaries and subjecting staff to protection policy training with strict penalties for exploitation.
D. Protection from Exploitation and Sexual Abuse Strategy:
Al-Birr and Social Services Association in Idleb adopts a code of conduct and protection policy that all staff and volunteers must adhere to, prohibiting any actions or behaviors indicating exploitation, harassment, or sexual abuse. The Society maintains assistance and protection offices that receive complaints following confidential procedures ensuring the safety and security of complainants while providing training on protection policies and procedures to individuals and beneficiaries as needed.
E. Risks, Assumptions, and Mitigation Measures:
- Theft is a moderate impact risk mitigated by hiring guards and installing surveillance cameras.
- Fire disasters are of moderate impact mitigated by having fire extinguishers in warehouses and alarm systems.
- Increased return of beneficiaries is a moderate impact risk mitigated by sending new lists to supporting organizations to meet needs within available resources.
