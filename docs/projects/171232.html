Project Summary

Somalia remains in a state of chronic catastrophe characterized by a complex political environment, extreme poverty, food insecurity, conflict and instability. This has led to poor access to basic health services, recurrent disease outbreaks, food insecurity and high levels of malnutrition, lack of safe water supplies, and significant rates of population displacement. Furthermore, the triple threat of COVID-19, floods and desert locusts has aggravated Somalia’s already complex and protracted humanitarian crisis. The crisis is largely driven by climatic shocks, decades of armed conflict, widespread poverty and long-term vulnerability. The project will focus on provision of quality essential lifesaving and life sustaining health services in Middle Shabelle, Bay and Bakool Regions with focus on women, children, neonates and other vulnerable groups and to strengthen the local capacity to deliver evidence-based and cost effective services based on the Integrated Primary Health Care Approach (IPHCA) with Basic Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (BEmONC) services and an enhanced referral system for severe medical conditions and obstetric complications in the targeted areas of intervention.
The health sector in Somalia, especially in the targeted districts, is in critical condition with very poor Health indicators. The high-risk groups of the direct beneficiaries are children U5 (67,404), children under 1 year (13,481) and pregnant women (16,851). This project aims at reducing rates of morbidity and mortality among undeserved communities of Bay, Bakool and Middle Shabelle Regions (Baidoa and Dinsoor districts in Bay, Wajid and Hudur districts in Bakool and Jowhar, Mahaday and Balcad districts in Middle Shabelle) with focus on children under 5 years and pregnant and lactating women through the provision of life saving emergency health services by supporting 10 static health facilities' and 10 mobile clinics supported by a network of Community Volunteers (CVs). The 10 ﬁxed facilities will provide PHC integrated BEmONC and further referral for secondary health services. More so, INTERSOS will engage 20 CVs in every district of operation. The Primary Health Care Package will include curative consultations, Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH), Integrated Management of Childhood Illnesses, Immunization, screening of under ﬁve children and PLWs, immunization (routine and supplementary), provision of medical supplies and health education messaging for 168,510 beneficiaries in the targeted locations. Each of the 10 fixed facilities will be run by 1 Medical Doctor, 4 Nurses, 2 Midwives and 3 Auxiliary Nurses while each Mobile Clinic will be managed by 2 Nurses and 2 Auxiliary Nurses. Each facility (static and mobile) will therefore serve an estimated 720 beneficiaries per month, translating to a total of 172,800 beneficiaries (720 monthly beneficiaries x 12 months x 20 facilities) for the entire project period, with estimated 51,840 outpatient consultations (30% of the total target).
The project will deploy Basic Life Support (BLS) ambulances equipped with basic medical supplies to all targeted locations. The BLS ambulances will provide referral services from outreach to ﬁxed facilities and those requiring secondary health care will be referred to secondary healthcare provider, ensuring rapid transportation of critical cases (priority will be given to under 5 children and pregnant women with obstetric complications). The project will establish an eﬃcient outreach and referral mechanism from community level to primary health services and from primary to secondary health services through a network of CVs and BLS capacity for the transport/stabilization of critically patients. Community Volunteers will do screening, assessing and identifying the sick patients and severely ill persons to refer to the hospital for further diagnosis and treatment.

Mitigation Measures

INTERSOS strongly believes in the importance of gender equality in humanitarian assistance, and recognizes that men, boys, girls and women have specific needs and constraints. In Somalia, women and girls have been particularly exposed to violence. The needs of women and young girls’ remain unmet and INTERSOS will continue involving the vulnerable groups at all stages of the project cycle. INTERSOS will put into consideration recruitment, training and deployment both male and female staff with varying age groups, to create a free interaction between a health care provider and the clients. Participatory sessions will be used to access and incorporate gender equality and age perspectives in all aspects of the project cycle, with a specific focus on gender during all implementation phases, with community discussion and assessments mandatorily conducted with groups of men and groups of women. As well, protection mainstreaming among the partners will be ensured throughout the intervention. Implementation of this project is aligned with cluster objectives of saving lives and the desire to help mitigate and respond to public health shocks through provision of quality health services in emergencies. INTERSOS, using the mobile teams, will reach out to the IDPs and the marginalized communities to provide basic health care and refer cases whenever necessary.
