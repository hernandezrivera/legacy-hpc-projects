Project Summary

Ensure children and families have access to WASH services and adopt good hygiene practices and essential WASH services with COVID-19 related activities integrated. Specific activities will include distribution of individual hygiene kits, family WASH kits including water purification supplies (aqua-tabs) and jerry cans for water storage. Distribution of hygiene kits will be accompanied with hygiene promotion, including guidance on the correct use of WASH infrastructure that has been set-up for the emergency (if the affected population usually practice open defecation). There will be a focus on the rehabilitation of Community Water points/small water supply systems. Water management Committees (WMC) will be revitalized/ trained while respecting COVID19 barriers, to ensure water infrastructure is maintained. The programme will involve monitoring of Free Residual Chlorine (FRC) at household level after Aqua-tab distribution. The programme will involve the construction of family/household latrines through the provision of latrine digging kits and squatting slabs. The affected population will be encouraged to use locally available materials to construct family pit latrines. Only families without latrines will be selected. WASH Committees will be formed in each of the target areas, and as community volunteers, their main role will be to guide people on the operation and management of their latrines. Each family will be responsible for cleaning, maintenance and decommissioning of its latrine. The affected population will also be trained on how to treat and reuse squatting slabs once the pits are full. Displaced and vulnerable individuals will be provided with adequate knowledge and means to ensure basic personal hygiene practices, and to maintain existing WASH infrastructure. Hygiene promotion and C4D activities will be implemented in line with the recommended WHO infection prevention and control measures. For effective and efficient implementation of the C4D/Hygiene promotion activities under the rapid response modality, hygiene promotion and C4D key messages will disseminate. One of the main objectives of the programme will be to ensure access to safe drinking water for vulnerable people (mainly IDPs and host communities). This will mean prioritizing communities hosting IDPs who do not have access to adequate safe water, rehabilitating non-functioning water points serving communities and in the vicinity of health centers, markets or schools and targeting non-functioning water points in returnee areas, including in key institutions such as health facilities and child-friendly spaces. The project location is in the NW region, in Bui, Donga Mantung, and Mezam Division). The project is expected to reach 9000 people before the end of 2022.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The Anglophone crisis that started since 2016 in the two English speaking regions of Cameroon escalated into an armed conflict and insecurity in 2017 and has rendered many people displaced from their localities. The increasing tension and hostilities has promoted insecurity and significant internal displacements. While humanitarian needs, joblessness, widowhood, crime and scarcity of food are on the increase, lack of access to basic WASH needs, most farmers have fled from the villages into bushes, forest and a large number made it to nearby cities. A need assessment conducted at the end of March 2021, identified that priority needs were: food security, shelter and non-food items (NFI), health, and access to safe drinking water and sanitation (WASH). WASH related needs are at the top of the list; this is evident by the recent outbreak of cholera in the SW region. According to a need assessment conducted by SUDAHSER Foundation the common water related problem identified ranges from: Non-functional and unprotected water points / systems in villages / (seri-)urban areas hosting IDPs, or affected by conflict with particular focus on key institutions and public spaces (e.g. health center, school, market) within targeted areas; Open defecation and/or unmanage wastes amongst IDPs in the bushes and villages; No designated areas for solid waste disposal (burial / burning) to minimise vectors / hazards; In areas of concentration of IDPs in villages / (semi-)urban areas, they is lack of gender-segregated communal emergency latrines, and the existing once are not regular clean and maintained; Damaged sanitation infrastructure at key public institutions (e.g. Health Facilities, Schools, CFSs, etc) in locations hosting IDPs or affected by conflict; No solid waste collection services and / or designated areas for solid waste disposal (burial / burning) to minimize vectors / hazards; Lack of adequate waste disposal within Health facilities and other public institutions to be promoted; capacities of existing water point or system governance and maintenance need to be reinforce; Need for hygiene practices promotion amongst displaced peoples, affected communities, and public institutions, particularly with regards hand washing, safe water treatment, handling and storage, food handling and storage, menstrual hygiene, waste management and the elimination of open defecation. This project is therefore design to address these identified problems
