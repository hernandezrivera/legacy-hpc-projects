Project Summary

As a result of the prolonged and intense conflict, and despite the significant increase of protection responses and services throughout the country in 2018, the protection situation for those inside Syria remains dire. While each location has its own specificity, the overarching picture is that everyone in Syria is possibly touched by at least one - if not multiple - protection concerns. The high occurrence of almost all issues in almost all locations shows that most protection concerns are (logically) inter-linked. for example, between dependency on assistance sexual exploitation, early marriage, and child recruitment and labor. Proximity to armed hostilities, displacement, increased poverty, and family separation, have been identified as critical factors that increase the protection risks and vulnerability of persons living in Syria.
The psychological impact of conflict-related violence coupled with the ongoing stressors related to the ongoing conflict and internal displacement can have a significant impact on the mental health and psychosocial well-being of Syrian adults and children. Some of these ongoing challenges include lack of resources, risks of exploitation, violence, discrimination, disruption of social networks, and lack of livelihood options. These challenges can result in a sense of hopelessness, which in turn contribute to psychological distress, increased family violence, a diminished sense of dignity and control, and reliance on negative coping skills.
Pre-existing mental health conditions may be exacerbated, or psychosocial distress may be brought on by daily stressors. As the conflict shifts into a protracted crisis, it is important to gain an understanding of Syrian culture-specific expressions of distress, healing practices, and coping strategies. Understanding the way in which Syrians may experience and cope with psychological distress and mental health problems can offer practitioners insight into how to be the most helpful. This can be used to develop programming that is most effective for the Syrian children, shaping community initiatives to promote resilience and increase the use of positive coping mechanisms.
Proposed Activities:
Rahma Foundation will provide Syrian children in Azaz District, Aleppo, and Jisr-Ash-Shugur District, Idleb with quality child-protection services, including psychological first aid PFA, psychosocial support PSS, and cases management, through two main approaches, fixed and outreach. The project interventions can be explained through the following four components:
1. Awareness raising: This activity is targeting the Young adults of men and women and middle-aged men and women in the community
2. Provision of group psychosocial support: This activity is targeting children and Adolescents
3. Conduct of outreach protection visits: This activity is targeting children, adolescents, young adults of men and women and middle-aged men and women that their children are part of the PSS support
4. Case Management: This activity is targeting children and Adolescents

Needs

While the crisis in Syria has left no one untouched, it has impacted upon people in different ways, with some parts of the country and segments of the population more acutely affected than others. Humanitarian access continues to be heavily constrained in several areas across the country, especially in Aleppo and Idlib governorates.children continue to endure multiple protection risks and serious rights violations as an impact of the crisis. Continuous displacement, exposure to violence, increasing poverty and the persistent lack of access to services and even the most basic necessities are heavily affecting the wellbeing of children. Grave child rights violations remain a critical concern. This environment leaves the psychosocial needs of many girls and boys unmet, leading to experiences of profound distress (HRP 2018)Based on OCHA situation reports in North-western Syria (Apr 2018), there is a significant need to mitigate protection risks, including for child protection (CP) and gender-based violence (GBV) cases in Euphrates Shield areas and Idleb suburbs which received the large concentrations of IDPs coming from Eastern Ghouta in 2018.People arriving are very distressed and/or traumatized from the experience of living under siege and their subsequent displacement. They are exhausted and express fear for their future, as well as for family members, friends, and property left behind. They are in urgent need of psychological first aid (PFA) and psychosocial support (PSS). Many children were separated from family members during the movement and other children arrived unaccompanied.Based on the multi-sector needs assessment conducted by Takaful Alsham, Aug 2018 in Azaz district, the parents were asked about the importance of providing services to adolescents and children, the first place was the activities in social and psychological support due to the psychological disorders suffered by adolescents and children during the current crisis. 73% of interviewed families were IDPs in this assessment. Moreover, the Situation Overview "Idleb Governorate and Surrounding Areas North-west Syria, REACH, August 2018" reported that the most commonly reported child protection issues in assessed communities (including Badama) were: Children working (reported in (54%) assessed communities), Psychological trauma (reported in (23%) assessed communities), Child marriage (reported in (22%) assessed communities), and Violence against children (reported in (10%) assessed communities).The latest dashboard shared by the protection cluster (Jan 2019) indicated that the severity scale was estimated as catastrophic (six points on the severity scale) in both targeted locations.Rahma Relief Foundation’s field team collected data in January 2019 in Azaz city, Aleppo and Badama town, Idleb in coordination with the local councils taking into consideration needs assessment conducted by other implementing partners in the area. in addition, Rahma Relief Foundation cross-checked the numbers with the HNO 2019 draft and concluded to the following PIN numbers as follows:1) Azaz:# of People in need of protection: 202,121 (35% Locals and 65% IDPs)# of Children in need for protection 121,272 (Ages < 17 y/o)2) Badama# of People in need for protection: 38,276 (70%Locals and 30% IDPs)# of Children in need for protection 22,956 (Ages < 17 y/o)Before initiating the implementation process, Rahma Relief Foundation will conduct a comprehensive Protection assessment to recognize other implementing partners that are working in the same field. Furthermore, the foundation will establish a bilateral communication with the cluster to fill any potential service gaps and prevent any duplications.

Implementation of activities

The project will target girls and boys who are at risk of or have experienced violence, abuse and exploitation (separated and unaccompanied children, and child labor etc.).The project will be achieved by implementing the following three activities:1. Provision of group psychosocial support: Rahma Relief Foundation’s protection team will conduct structured PSS to a group of Syrian children and adolescents in Azaz and Jisr Al Shoghour. This PSS will be implemented through designing and implementing weekly PSS sessions in two community centers and will directly target children aged 7 to 17 years old, with a focus on the most vulnerable (children dropped off school, orphans, children affected by moderate disability, psychologically traumatized children …). Two trained PSS workers and two animators will be the facilitators of these sessions, and they will work under the close supervision of a psychologist on a daily base.2. Case Management: The Case Management will be handled by the caseworkers who will focus on the needs of an individual child and their family, ensuring that concerns are addressed systematically in consideration of the best interests of the child and building upon the child and family’s natural resilience. The services will be provided in accordance with the established case management process, with each case through a series of steps involving children’s meaningful participation and family empowerment throughout. In addition to involving the coordination of services and supports within an interlinked or referral system. A psychologist will be hired as a general supervisor to the overall projects’ activities, and his/her main role will be:- Provide training to the PSS workers, and caseworkers on child protection minimum standards and child protection in emergencies.- Provide technical support to the teams.- Monitor the progress of children who are referred to caseworkers and measure the development of the case.3. Awareness raising: The project aims at sensitizing the community about the concept of child protection. This activity will target the local community and IDPs, by conducting three main activities:• Conducting awareness sessions, lectures, and workshops, for both the host community and IDPs, with a focus on the relevant stakeholders. Rahma plans to do this activity in the center and aims to conduct one session per week in nine months period.• Conducting field awareness raising sessions through the house visits to the families. The sessions’ topics will be child protection and mental health best practices, positive coping mechanisms, and ways of self-care.• Distributing IEC materials.Activities Monitoring: • Rahma Relief Foundation will include measures of the change resulting from these activities in terms of children’s knowledge, attitudes and practices (KAP) to their own protection and psychosocial wellbeing, e.g. pre/post tests.• Undertake a systematic/AOR-wide measurement of quality and impact of PSS interventions. Consider peer-to-peer approaches and child-feedback mechanisms.During the project implementation, Rahma Relief Foundations' staff will ensure compliance on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). Under the proposed project, RRF will remain accountable to affected populations through open dialogue and feedback/complaint mechanisms provided to these populations; including:- The provision of complaint boxes that can be seen and accessed easily,- Boxes will be checked periodically, and the response will be generated within a week (except urgent complaint)- Using social media and mobile applications to receive complaints from beneficiaries.- collecting gender and age-disaggregated data from project implementation and impact, which will be analyzed and routinely reported on while project activities are monitored for improvements in self-reliance as well as beneficiary satisfaction and resilience of both women and men
