Project Summary

The proposed project aims to improve access to integrated and lifesaving services through static (PHCCs, PHCUs) and mobile health and nutrition facilities amongst vulnerable populations of Maiwut, Nasir, Ulang and Longechuk in Upper Nile.
The intervention will focus on screening, prevention and treatment of communicable and non-communicable diseases through outpatient consultation and mobile outreach, preventive health promotion campaigns, injury management, SRH and MNCH services (incl. ANC and PNC), safe delivery in facilities and community through skilled birth attendants/mid-wives, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services, clinical management of rape (SGBV services), regular and routine disease surveillance, reporting and prompt response to outbreaks, routine and outreach immunization services and strengthened referral pathways. Protection will be mainstreamed across interventions. Health services will be provided through close collaboration with the CHD and in coherence with BHI. More female staff and volunteers will be recruited to ensure that women’s health needs are addressed
The project will integrate WASH and Nutrition into health programming in order to ensure positive and sustainable health outcomes. Community mobilization and sensitization will be strengthened on health-related messages, through integrated messaging that includes WASH and nutrition messages. The proposed project will ensure early detection and treatment of Tuberculosis and HIV cases in the community and management of SAM with medical complications. Communities in the targeted counties will be engaged throughout project life cycle to ensure programme ownership and sustainability. Health worker capacities will be enhanced in immunization, disease surveillance, BEmoNC, MPHSS, SGBV/CMR, IMCI and case management. This project will complement RI World Bank project in Longechuk and Maiwut in management of communicable diseases, immunization, save motherhood (PNC, ANC and deliveries), disease surveillance, management of SGBV, and Mental health problems among others which are part of WB project activities.
Approx. 92,444 vulnerable hosts, IDPs and returnees are living in Upper Nile and Jonglei states. (Maiwut- 6778 people - 3321 male and 3457 females; Nasir County - 17202 people 8429 male and 8773 females; Ulang County 63,048 people – 30894 male and 32155 females; and Longechuk county 5416 People – 2654 male and 2762 females
In this project, RI will engage communities during the selection of Mobile medical (MMUs) locations, identifying storage spaces for essential drugs and MMU equipment such as Metallic trunk for keeping drugs and registers, table, chairs, mats and contributing with space and locally available material for establishing the MMUs. MMUs and health facilities (PHCCs and PHCUs) will be equipped with medical equipment such as sphygmomanometer, Stethoscope, Respiratory beat timers, weighing scale, Fete scope, thermometers and data collection and reporting tools. In addition, MMU will store essential drugs and test kits, such as MRDT, rapid test for syphilis among others, and medical equipment for quality services provision. MMUs will be staffed with local health workers and build their capacities. Staff in each MMU will have a work plan for weekly visits to all reachable villages and weekly reporting. The community mobilizer will mobilize the village ahead of the MMU team visiting the village for providing consultations and other services responding to community needs. Integrated service provision, that includes WASH and nutrition services required for positive health outcomes, will specifically address protection concerns of the most vulnerable groups in the community. Staff will be trained in humanitarian protection principles of “DO NO HARM” and beneficiary feedback will be encouraged and captured. Monitoring visits by RI and community representatives/ local authorities will ensure needs and rights-based service delivery.

1 - Needs

Protracted conflict and displacement in South Sudan have drastically reduced the availability of and access to health services, safe water, food and livelihoods opportunities, aggravating already high morbidity and mortality amongst the most vulnerable and remote communities and resulting in loss of control over, and access to vital resources, and especially impacting life, safety and dignity of women and children. Conflict has affected the existence and functionality of health facilities in providing lifesaving healthcare to vulnerable IDPs and host communities. Women and children are disproportionally affected by the crisis, representing 85% of the IDP population, facing heightened risk of SGBV with mental health and SRH consequences. South Sudan’s MMR is one of the highest in the world estimated at 789 per 100,000 live births, whereas neonatal and under-five mortality rates are 39.3 and 99.2 per 1,000 live births respectively. Existing services are extremely limited and challenging to access. Only 22% of health facilities are fully functional, and especially lacking basic maternal, newborn and child health (MNCH), obstetric and SRH services, often resulting in maternal and infant mortality. In Nasir, Maiwut, Ulang and Longechuk counties of Upper Nile state, 42% of the population has limited access to health services due to vast tracts of swamp land, non-functional and lack of health care services, placing people at risk of disease burden. According to REACH report, Malaria was prevalent amongst 42% of the population in Upper Nile, followed by AWD and malnutrition. Despite health services provided in some Payams, many villages lack access to health services located within the same Payams due to vast swampy tracts and distance. Health facilities are 6 -12 hours on foot from many villages making it impossible to provide emergency care or for sick people to reach the facilities (some examples are Bimbim, parieng, Duk and Wunbut in Ulang; Dichin, Wecdeng and Kechfour in Nasir; Panamdetek, Wuor in Maiwut; and Pamach, Belwang, Palgup in Longechuk). Some health facilities in the above Bomas/villages have few competent health personnel and often lack essential medicines and and medical supplies.According to recent IPC report (May-June IPC 2019 report), people in Maiwut and Nasir of former Upper Nile state are in emergency (IPC3) and Ulang, Longechuk and Maiwut are likely to deteriorate further due to poor harvest, lack of access to food, livestock and market, affecting health, nutrition and WASH conditions of the population, resulting in increased incidence of communicable diseases in the community. Immunization coverage remains low in the above counties. In addition, in the Upper Nile counties, the lack of preventive and curative services for SRH, MNCH, counselling for MHPSS especially amongst survivors of SGBV, and communicable diseases like HIV/AIDS, TB, malaria, acute respiratory infections and AWD, among others. Lack of local capacity in terms of medical skills, knowledge and equipped facilities to address emergency health needs, especially SRH and MNCH, exacerbates the risk of increased mortality and morbidity. RI currently implements health programs in the above Upper Nile counties through OFDA, UNICEF/World Bank, and SSHF second allocation 2019.

2 - Activities

RI’s proposed response plan addresses above needs, aiming to improve and expand access to integrated emergency lifesaving health care through static (PHCCs , PHCUs) facilities and mobile clinic services amongst the most vulnerable and remote populations of Maiwut, Nasir, Ulang and Longechuk counties in Upper Nile, targeting 92,444 persons. Interventions will complement RI world bank in Upper Nile State focus on screening, prevention and treatment of communicable and non-communicable diseases through outpatient consultation, mobile outreach, preventive health campaigns, injury management, SRH and MNCH services (incl. ANC and PNC), safe delivery through skilled birth attendants/mid-wives, MHPSS services, clinical management of rape, regular and routine disease surveillance, reporting and prompt response to outbreaks, routine and outreach immunization services. Currently RI is implementing WB project in Longechuk and Maiwut. Protection will be mainstreamed across interventions, with special attention to the needs and rights of persons with disabilities, survivors of SGBV, MHPSS needs, case management and strengthened referral systems. Health services will be provided through close collaboration with CHDs and in coherence with BHI. Local capacities will be built to ensure sustainable and continued service provision. Female staff and volunteers will be recruited to ensure women’s health needs are addressed. Community engagement will be ensured through life of project and include regular consultations, information provision with community elders, women, youth groups, religious leaders and beneficiary representatives reaching to the grassroots community leaders such as women representatives, youth representatives, local chiefs and local authorities and CHDs, and SMOH.In addition, the project will integrate WASH and Nutrition into health programming in order to ensure positive and sustainable health outcomes. Community mobilization and sensitization will be strengthened on health-related messages, through integrated messaging that includes WASH and nutrition messages. The proposed project will ensure early detection and treatment of Tuberculosis and HIV cases in the community and management of SAM with medical complications. Communities in the targeted counties will be engaged throughout project life cycle to ensure programme ownership and sustainability. Health worker capacities will be enhanced in immunization, disease surveillance, BEmoNC, MPHSS, SGBV/CMR, IMCI and case management. RI will engage communities during selection of MMU locations, identifying storage spaces for essential drugs and MMU equipment such as Metallic trunk for keeping drugs and registers, table, chairs, mats and contributing with space and locally available material for establishing the MMUs. Selected MMUs and health facilities will be equipped with medical equipment, essential drugs and test kits, such as MRDT, rapid tests for quality service provision. All selected MMUs will be staffed with health workers (Nurses, Community Health workers, midwife, vaccinators and community mobilizers). All staff will be recruited locally and their capacities built. Staff in each MMU will have a work plan integrating community mobilization, consultations, vaccinations, home visits for specific cases, weekly visits to all reachable villages and weekly reporting. The community mobilizer will mobilize the village ahead of the MMU team visiting the village for providing consultations and other services responding to community needs. Integrated service provision, that includes WASH and nutrition services required for positive health outcomes, will specifically address protection concerns of the most vulnerable groups in the community. Beneficiary feedback will be encouraged and captured. Monitoring visits will be conducted by RI and community representatives/ local authorities to ensure needs and rights-based service delivery.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Beneficiary rights are recognized through the rights-based programming approach and protection mainstreaming whereby the safety, rights and dignity of beneficiaries is paramount and determines project design, service provision and implementation methodology. Beneficiary needs, rights and voices have informed project activities and will be key to informing advocacy with government and non-government actors. Further, interventions will factor-in the rights of host communities, IDPs and returnees, bringing the target communities on board the finalization of selection criteria, beneficiary selection and site selection for infrastructure activities, messaging modalities and needs and right-based information dissemination.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project includes perspectives of displacement-affected people through baseline assessments, FGDs, key informant interviews and KAP surveys, conducted amongst target host communities, returnees and IDPs, with community elders, women and local authorities. These assessments aimed to understand needs and challenges, priorities and protection concerns amongst displaced communities.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project is linked to government/community systems since engagement with community is central to all interventions through project life, including design, implementation, monitoring and quality evaluation. The community is engaged during site selection for MMUs, in providing storage space for MMU supplies and medical drugs. Community volunteers are involved closely in community mobilization and awareness building activities. Skills development of community based TBAs and awareness building amongst Lead Mothers is core to SRH and MNCH activities. In addition, project activities are in coherence with the BHI and in close coordination with the County health Department. RI holds regular meetings and joint monitoring visits with community elders/representatives, ROSS, CHD director and local Commissioner. Regular meetings will be organized with informal groups of women for timely assessment and response to needs.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is aligned with the national objectives through its coherence with the health cluster objectives, contributing to (i) reduction of mortality and morbidity, especially through MMU services amongst remotely located and vulnerable groups; (ii) improving and scaling up response to integrated health, nutrition and WASH services with specific health outcomes; (iii) mainstreaming protection, especially targeting women, girls, children and persons with disability through MHPSS, CMR, ASRH and MNCH activities, amongst IDPs, returnees and host communities, ensuring safety and dignity of affected populations

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The protection environment is not sufficiently safe or stable, impacting lives and dignity of the most vulnerable groups. Safety concerns are heightened in the target counties due to a volatile local environment of inter-communal conflict, cattle raiding and child abduction. Protection concerns are high, especially amongst women, girls, children and persons with disabilities, whose vulnerability exposes them to GBV and life-threatening medical conditions and insecurity related to WASH facilities and services. SRH, MNCH and MHPSS services are lacking in the target counties, especially in the remote locations, largely due to lack of local skilled/competent staff, non-functional facilities and remote locations. Referral pathways and CMR is weak and disease surveillance requires strengthening. There is often vandalisation of health care facilities accompanied by looting equipment, essential medicine and supplies.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

In order to ensure sustainability and handover, the response will focus on local capacity building for health service provision, close and strong collaboration with the local authorities, CHD and ROSS as well as adherence with the Boma Health Initiative. The project will ensure gradual handover of facilities and services to CHD while building capacity and mentoring health staff. Community engagement, consultation and dialogue will be central to all interventions through life of project to ensure local ownership and sustainable solutions. Through preventive health promotion and information dissemination as well as capacity building on safe practices in Health, nutrition and WASH, communities are expected to build resilience and invest in long term solutions

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Gender sensitive awareness building sessions will be conducted with male community members to build trust and confidence on potential activities and related benefits for women and families. The key drivers of female, especially maternal and child, morbidity and mortality will be discussed in order to identify life-saving interventions in SRH and MNCH. Potential gender-responsive interventions and direct impact on lives, safety and dignity of women will be discussed before interacting directly with women of the community. Female staff will be assigned to implement community mobilization, health awareness building and provide SRH, MHPSS and MNCH consultations, counselling and service provision.Lack of access to/awareness of referral services for survivors of protection incidents (GBV, child protection, for example). Project activities include establishment and strengthening of referral pathways for survivors of protection incidents. RI will work with other local actors, including protection, health, WASH and nutrition actors as well as local authorities to design and ensure the functionality of the referral pathways.RI will follow up on the ground and advocate with OCHA, donor community, local authorities, clusters and the NGO forum. Humanitarian assistance and service provision will be brought closer to target communities (for ex. Mobile Medical Units and weekly visits to each target boma/village for vaccinations, medicine provision, supporting referrals, ensuring safe distribution points) and sound feedback mechanisms will be put in place to ensure that accessibility constraints and distance from service delivery points do not expose women and girls to risk of sexual exploitation and abuse. Awareness will be raised amongst community members on the value, importance and benefit of the feedback mechanisms.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Feedback mechanisms will be embedded into project activities as part of ongoing monitoring visits and post distribution monitoring. Satisfaction surveys will be conducted for those visiting the static and integrated iSatisfaction surveys As part of accountability to vulnerable communities and ensuring feedback from them, RI will employ two types of complainant and feedback mechanisms: (i) responsive and proactive, permitting direct and indirect beneficiaries to provide feedback and/or lodge complaints, even anonymously to organizational staff (and senior managers in case of complaints against staff). The system will respect the local culture but will not be impeded by local power holders. The feedback system will be developed in a transparent manner after sessions of community awareness building on the benefits and need for such feedbacks Special care will be taken to ensure that excluded and/or the marginalized groups can access the mechanism and that their voices are heard. (ii) post distribution monitoring of service provision at the facility level and through home visits will allow beneficiaries to provide their feedback on satisfaction with quality and quantity of services which will subsequently inform ongoing and future health related interventions.
