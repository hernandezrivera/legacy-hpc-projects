Project Summary

In the Far North region, the Boko Haram insurgency in Nigeria has displaced 100,504 Nigerian refugees in Cameroon as of October 2018, and 98,590 of them live in the Far North region (UNHCR, October 2018). Boko Haram attacks have also resulted in 227,581 internally displaced persons (IDPs) (UNHCR, October 2018), who are struggling to survive in strenuous conditions in villages or small towns in the area. The region is predominantly arid and regularly hit by drought and seasonal floods. Access to basic services is also more limited than in other regions of the country. The arrival of IDPs in food-insecure areas has severely affected host communities as well. This has put enormous pressure on already scarce community resources and the local economy by reducing farm productivity and access to land and food markets. Boko Haram attacks have disrupted the economic, agricultural, and pastoral activities in the Far North region, causing a depletion of livelihoods and household assets. As a consequence, the existing chronic vulnerability and structural food insecurity have been aggravated. To support the chronically food-insecure and shock-prone areas during the lean seasons in the Far North region, WFP plans to target the most vulnerable 30,000 local population through seasonal food assistance with half ration from May to October in 2019.

Needs

The overall insecurity, the influx of Nigerian refugees, and the internal displacement have negatively affected the local communities and population since the outbreak of the Boko Haram insurgencies. In addition, local population have suffered from chronic poverty and natural disasters. The results of the Comprehensive Food Security and Vulnerability Analysis (CFSVA), jointly conducted by the Government (Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development, or MINADER) and WFP in May 2017, revealed that 3.9 million Cameroonians are food insecure, and among them 211,000 are severely food insecure. The Far North region shows the highest figures, with 33.7% food insecure households (around 1,460,000 people), of which 3% are severely food insecure. Mayo-Tsanaga division has an alarming food insecurity rate (65.6 %), followed by Mayo-Sava (37.8%) and Logone-et-Chari with 13%. These high prevalence rates are the consequence of poverty (65%), high food expenditure (54.2%), and inadequate food consumption (36.2%).The results of the Food Security and Monitoring Survey (FSMS) indicate that, in the Far North region, the prevalence of food insecurity decreased from 20% in October 2017 to 11% in February 2018. This relative improvement is partly because the period of the survey coincided with the second cycle harvests, coupled with the provision of humanitarian food assistance. However, levels of severe food insecurity remain high and stable constant levels between the last two rounds of FSMS. For 24% of households in the Far North, at least 65% of expenditure is spent on food. In addition, among those 24%, 13% of households are extremely vulnerable economically so at least 75% of expenditure is spent on food). In addition, 7.6% of households sold animals in the Far North to address their food needs and 8% of households reduced their health and education expenses. In addition to the Boko Haram Attacks, the Far North region has also been impacted by natural disasters and climate change. WFP aims to address the urgent food needs of the most vulnerable local population specifically during the lean season through the seasonal food assistance.
