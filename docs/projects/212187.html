Project Summary

As per the HNO, more than 14.9 million people are in need for life-saving primary & secondary care health assistance in 2024.The February earthquake damaged 67 health facilities in Northwest Syria, of which 6 were destroyed. In contrast, 6 health centers in Latakia and Tartous suffered significant damage and went out of service. In addition, 133 primary health care centers and 18 hospitals in Aleppo, Hama, Latakia, and Tartous were partially damaged. Emerging and re-emerging infectious, vaccine-preventable, and neglected tropical diseases, such as influenza-like illnesses, measles, meningitis, and waterborne diseases including cholera, hepatitis A, and leishmaniasis; continued to pose a significant threat to affected communities across Syria accounting for more than 16% of the total consultations reported from the health system through the surveillance system
The cholera outbreak declared in September 2022 is expected to persist in 2024, with seasonal variations in caseloads. As of 21 October 2023 (epi-week 42), 217,512 suspected acute watery diarrhea/cholera cases reported from all 14 governorates of Syria, with 106 associated deaths at a case fatality rate of 0.05 percent. While the outbreak initially overstretched the already-weak health system, and had direct consequences on vulnerable communities and patients, increasing morbidities and mortalities, the latest reports showed a gradual decline and partners have started considering integrating response operations into existing primary health services.
Access to and functionality of basic health services remain a huge challenge; almost 40 percent of primary and secondary care health facilities - serving over 4.8 million people in need of life-saving health services - are either partially functioning or not functional. In addition, non-functioning primary health care and specialized facilities increased from 16 percent and 18 percent in 2022 to 24 percent and 31 percent in 2023 respectively.
Children and women are the most vulnerable groups especially in NES and IDP camps, coverage with vaccines is still below 80% which is the minimum level to achieve the heard immunity, and it is more critical in camps due to hardship living conditions. Women in childbearing age needs more support for antenatal and postnatal care in addition to safe deliveries in camps or in high-risk areas.
Primary and secondary health care are constrained due to the high percentage of health centers who are damaged due to the war or the earthquake, and referral to hospitals in NES is restricted to very few private hospitals due to the unfunctional public hospitals or very limited specialized services in those hospitals and shortage of specialized doctors.
Delivery of supplies to the NES governorates is also constrained due to security issues and long leading time to reach the destination after many liars of approvals.
This proposal aims to support the provision of basic lifesaving curative, preventive and promotional health services (including vaccination services) and health seeking and care practices, while enhancing early recovery and resilience of the sector by contributing to: strengthening service delivery to vulnerable population who cannot afford paying for health services, specifically children (through providing free-of-charge medical services by NGOs, rehabilitating damaged public health facilities, and installing prefab clinics in areas with no health centers), supporting community health delivery platforms through capacity building for those NGO, with major focus on community engagement through volunteers to include and integrate them within the health facilities and be part of the community health system to ensure sustainability of those voluntary services.
To overcome the problem of delays in getting approvals for transporting health supplies to NES, UNICEF is engaging the state planning commission in approving a list of planned supplies and getting a blank approval for the planned list.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The project will provide health services to more than 5 million children and women including 517,580 boys, girls, and women with disabilities on equal basis. UNICEF will work through local NGOs who run 86 fixed health centers and 62 mobile teams across Syria and will support the vaccination program for all children under the age of 1 year, and Big catch-up campaigns for 3.2 million children under 5 years.UNICEF will Provide support to 1000 HFs with supplies, cold chain, and capacity building, and will support SRH services including 250, 000 antenatal care visits for more than 50,000 pregnant women. UNICEF will support MHPSS through capacity building for nurses in the nursing schools.The project is also conceived with a view to promoting complementarity with other sections and multi-sectoral integration within UNICEF & other sector partners.UNICEF will focus on highest severity districts as defined by the Health Sector. Program effectiveness will be enhanced by applying a risk informed lens to planned activities. The accountability towards affected population is integrated throughout the program cycle, UNICEF started to collect information from the affected population on the quality of services through hotlines, SMS, and complaint boxes in fixed clinics of NGOs. UNICEF will work in 233 sub districts with a severity scale of 3 or higher and will monitor the project through prog monitoring visits & financial spot checks by UNICEF staff and by third-party monitors.Protection Risk Analysis: GBV: women & girls at acute risk of sexual and GBV, health facilities are an entry point for survivors. Mitigation measures: affected population should have access to RH services of MISP. Health workers to be trained on providing survivor with first-line support, clinical management of rape and safe referrals. Training health workers should be in synergy with CP section & UNFPA. Ensure confidentiality of all patients. Guard against fraud: UNICEF implement programmatic visits & financial spot checks on regular bases to all IPs. All partners have signed on UNICEF rules to keep their records & financial documents for 5 years after project completion. All partners are assessed and classified for their "risk", and all high-risk IPs are asked to take corrective action & checked more regularly and reassessed later.Sexual Exploitation & Abuse: risk of SEA persists. Mitigation measures: enforce PSEA policy for all IPs with zero tolerance, with a documented commitment from all IPs. Establish community-based complaints mechanisms, awareness-raising on SEA with training, monitoring, especially for mobile medical teams, and encourage female workers. Duty of care: it is obligatory to provide physical, mental safety, security & wellbeing to all staff. Health care for Vulnerable Groups & Equitable Access to Health Services: work in high-severity districts. Safe Closure of Facilities and Protecting Data: HW trained on safe withdrawal Community Engagement & Participation.
