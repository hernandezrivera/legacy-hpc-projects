Project Summary

Boys and girls including adolescents, children with disabilities in IDPs and host communities, facing life-threatening risks of abuse, neglect, violence, exploitation, and severe distress have access to well-coordinated and disability, gender-sensitive quality child protection services. This will be achieved through strengthening community-based child protection structures to raise awareness, prevent, respond and refer cases of violence against children, providing case management support and referral services to children with protection concerns including SGBV, providing FTR services to UASC and alternative care arrangements. In addition, families under stress will receive activities that ensure the mental health and psycho-social wellbeing of affected children, through intensive child protection activities within child friendly spaces including structured child resilience building sessions and other psychosocial support for children; parenting sessions with care providers; and training to increase capacity of staff and key government stakeholders. Trainings on child protection, SGBV and MHPSS will be delivered to sector staff (Health, Education, Nutrition, WASH and Livelihoods) to ensure mainstreaming of CP and improved coordination of services. Efforts will be made to ensure equal participation of minority groups like excluded clans, women, girls and as well as people and children with disabilities. According to Somalia Humanitarian Needs (HNO) 2020 Overview, an estimated 480,000 people in need live with disability; physical, mental, intellectual or sensory impairments, and as a result, face serious risks of exploitation, ill-treatment, neglect and stigmatization. The 2021 HNO states that families of children disabilities regularly chain their children and in the assessment 28% of caregivers stated that the practice is necessary to protect the children from harm such as car accidents, falling into pits/trenches, discrimination, physical and sexual abuse, hurting other people or being hurt.
Explosive hazard accidents continue to affect the civilian population across the regions in South and Central Somalia. Although the Federal government of Somalia recently ratified the treaty on Convention on the Rights of People with Disabilities, persons and children with disabilities continue to be largely absent from the political and development agenda and their needs are not properly addressed by the authorities. Due to prevailing misconceptions about disability, both children and adults with disabilities are stigmatized, segregated and referred to with derogatory names. Survivors of landmines/explosive remnants of war (ERW) and other persons with disabilities continue to suffer the absence of adequate services including emergency medical care, rehabilitation and reintegration.
Children with disabilities face many barriers to inclusion including inaccessible physical environment, lack of awareness in the communities, insufficient teaching skills, negative attitude and stigma, poverty, and a severe shortage of assistive devices and mobility aids. Girls with disabilities are particularly vulnerable, as boys with disabilities tend to get the few opportunities available.
The project will ensure that children with disabilities are actively targeted during the selection of most vulnerable children. It will also ensure community awareness raising is done on issues of protecting children with disabilities against sexual abuse. Laws adversely affecting children with disabilities will be part of the advocacy on legal reforms plan.
SC will comply with the relevant sector standards and promote good practice by including PSEA and child safeguarding measures at all phase and programming activity. SC shall promptly and confidentially record and report allegations of sexual exploitation and abuse, or any reasonable suspicion or allegations of child safeguarding violations of which it would have been informed of or become aware of to the donor.

Project Activities

1.1 Strengthen existing CFS to provide psychosocial support services for children affected by drought and conflict (17 CFS/area) to provide psychosocial support (including Psychological First Aid), especially age appropriate CFS activities that support children psychosocial wellbeing, as per international guidelines. 1.2 Support to caregivers and families (MHPSS, parenting without violence and other kind of support including child resilience programme) of children facing protection risks in order to enhance their capacity to care for and protect their children. 1.3 Increasing the capacity of community-based child protection structures and mechanisms (CBCPM) to prevent and mitigate CP risks through multi-layered, community based psychosocial support interventions to reduce vulnerabilities and risks to children by building a protective environment at family and community levels. Peer to peer awareness raising will be led by CBCPMs, youth and community volunteers’ networks to prevent child protection risks, identify vulnerable children in need of support such as case management and emergency child protection care (i.e. alternative care or basic PSS activities. (Target 550 persons: 1.4 Provide protection and support to children at risk and child survivors including unaccompanied and separated through case management services that meets their unique needs, which include family tracing and reunification services, alternative care. The capacity of community volunteers will be strengthened to support the case management process, namely through identification of children at risk, making home monitoring visits and offering peer support to caregivers1.5 Conduct awareness raising activities targeting community wide on prevention messages and awareness sessions including on recruitment/CAAFAG, SGBV, harmful practice, family separation, and Children’s rights. 1.6 Provision of gender sensitive dignity kits (underwear, sanitary towel and soap) for vulnerable girls and boys. 1.7 In collaboration with SC health teams, train health service providers (female and male) on the clinical management of GBV including provision of Post Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP) and Emergency Contraception (EC) and on PFA for GBV survivors. 1.8 Provide support to children and adolescents’ survivors of sexual and gender -based violence in coordination with the Gender Based Violence service providers through referral and direct support. The capacity of case workers will continue to be built through learning strategies including case management training, mentoring and coaching. Mitigation Measures

In order to mitigate the above identified risks Save the Children will undertake the following measures (most of which address more than one risk); 1. Empowering girls and boys - Children play a key role in protecting themselves and each other, therefore Save the Children will empower girls and boys to protect themselves according to their specific and unique needs. This will be achieved through participatory methods to facilitate discuss protection issues and plan their own actions to improve their situation and influence adults around them. 2. Community mobilization: Like families, communities are often the first line of protection for children, but also a source of harm, so we always engage and mobilize community actors in our child protection programs. Community mobilization is a capacity building process through which local individuals, groups or organizations identify needs, plan, carry out and evaluate activities on a participatory and sustained basis, so as to improve the protection of children. In community mobilization, we support women’s groups, establish community child protection focal points or groups or committees (CWCs), facilitate community dialogues and conversations around child protection issues, support child rights clubs, youth groups and facilitate mass media campaigns such as radio programs and messaging. 3. Save the Children will develop a beneficiary selection criterion in close collaboration with local leaders and community representatives to ensure transparency and selection of beneficiaries will also be supervised by SC staff. 4. Save the Children will apply effective data protection measures to ensure that beneficiary data is kept confidential and safe from unintended use 5. Survivors of gender based violence will be facilitated to seek assistance in a confidential and dignified manner that does not expose them to further trauma 6. All SC staff are trained in Child Safeguarding and Sexual harassment in addition to signing a legally binding code of conduct as part of their employment contract. In addition, all SC service providers are screened and sensitized on the same.
