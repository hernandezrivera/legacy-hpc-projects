Project Summary

The project meets strategic and cluster objectives:
Strategic objective 02: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL.
HEALTH AND NUTRITION CLUSTER OBJECTIVE 1: Ensure the availability, accessibility, acceptability and quality of essential lifesaving health services to the most vulnerable communities in Gaza and West Bank/EJ, including through health system strengthening, preparedness and community resilience building.
The project’s main two objectives are:
1- Ensuring the availability of life saving items and essential infection control materials at the neonatal units. MAP aims to achieve the project’s objectives in coordination with the Ministry of Health, UNFPA, UNICEF, WHO, QRCS, UNRWA
2- Improving the skills and competencies of staff at the neonatal units on neonatal life support, neonatal safe transport, and infection control.
In general this project will contribute to addressing the life-saving needs for high risk babies at neonatal units in the Gaza Strip and improving neonatal care management in order to contribute to reducing mortality, morbidity and long term disability among newborns.

Needs

According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview HNO (2018), there are 1.9 million people of vulnerable groups across Gaza Strip. A study conducted by UNRWA (2015) in Gaza Strip and validated by WHO in 2016 revealed that infant mortality rate of 21.3 per 1000 live births, with a neonatal mortality of 14 per 1000 live births. These findings indicate similar levels of infant and neonatal mortality in 2014, with slower or absent improvement, especially related to neonatal mortality. However, the same data also showed no decline in neonatal mortality. The WHO validation study identified infections, congenital anomalies and birth asphyxia as the main causes of infant and neonatal deaths, and prematurity and low birth weight as main contributory factors. “Infant mortality is one of the best indicators for the health of the community,” said Dr. Akihiro Seita, Director of the UNRWA health programme. “It reflects on the mother and child’s health and in the UN Millennium Development Goals, it is one of the key indicators.” (UNRWA, 2015). This problem is further exacerbated by the everlasting and critical shortages of medicines and supplies in Gaza’s hospitals. MoH’s 2018 reports indicates that up till now the monthly average shortages of drugs related to maternal and child health are 48% while shortages in medical consumables related to the intensive care services are 18%. On other hand and according to WHO, three quarters of all newborn deaths occur in the first week of life, moreover, based on the monthly Sepsis surveillance in the Neonatal Intensive Care Units (NICUs) conducted during 2018 by MAP and MoH’s Quality Improvement and Infection Control Unit, it was found that 22.7% of the newborn deaths are due to neonatal sepsis. Neonatal morbidities and mortalities are considered as serious problems that mostly result in dramatic complications with heavy medical, social and economic burden on individuals, families, communities and government. MAP has been intervening in this area since 2008 and coordinated its interventions with MCHN group, however and based on the assessment conducted by MAP in 2015, the neonatal health services are still facing significant challenges including (A) shortage of life saving drug items and infection control materials which are correlated to the prolonged shortage of drugs and consumables at MoH (B) understaffing and the lack of qualified staff in neonatal ICU and care management. To address the lack of adequate policies and practices related to infection control, MAP has supported the MoH to produce the infection control booklet on policies and procedures, provided a training to the neonatal staff including cleaners an d supplied infection control materials, however, based on MAP’s recent regular surveillance in 2018, 22.7% of the newborn deaths are due to neonatal sepsis /infection which suggests there is a need to continue the provision of supplies of infection control materials and training. MAP has provided before NLS/NST training to health staff and provided lifesaving drugs, supplies and disposables to respond to newborn’s needs who were critically ill especially when such items were at zero stock. It is worth mentioning that MAP’s response has contributed to saving the lives of several cases of sick newborns such as the ones who needed surfactants (medication necessary for pre-mature babies). The need to continue and expand this support is critical. It should also be highlighted that special attention should be given to newborns as a component within the frame of continuum of care.

Activities

In coordination with MoH, UNFPA, WHO and all related parties, procurement of life saving neonatal drugs and consumables to be used for 10,000 at high risk new born babies/year including female and male babies admitted to neonatal ICU to benefit from the neonatal care project’s interventions. In coordination with MoH, UNFPA, WHO and UNICEF, procurement of infection control supplies and educational materials to be used for 10,000 high risk new born babies/year including female and male babies admitted to NICU and who will benefit from the neonatal care project’s interventions. In coordination with MoH and NGOs, procure necessary equipment that is urgently needed to address the over crowding in NIUCs. In coordination with MoH and NGOs, conduct three NLS and three Infection Control training courses for neonatal and obstetric department for 200 health professionals. 114 female and 86 male health professionals working at the neonatal and obstetric units of MoH will be targeted. In coordination with the aforementioned parities, conduct three on job NLS training courses, and procure the needed equipment for the courses. Plus all the emergency services that we will provide will contribute to the reduction of disabilities in neonats as these babies are endangered and subject to death or develop long-term disabilities. With regards to gender, the project will target male and female neonatas but for the training, more emphasis will be given to female health practitioners as they form the majority of the workers at NICUs.

Indicators

To ensure that the work is implemented according to the plan and to reach the goal of this project; MAP will include a rigorous monitoring and evaluation plan. It is worth mentioning that MAP has recently finished an evaluation for its intervention on neonatal units and this project serves to target some of the gaps that were identified in the report. To add, MAP supervises a monthly surveillance for sepsis cases admitted at NICUs, and this will continue with this project. At the start of the project, MAP will collect data for monitoring and reporting through a variety of project specific and outside sources to mark progress against each of the indicators and activities in the M&E Plan, including but not limited to: training reports, pre and post tests, on the job supervisor’s checklists, and training evaluations; procurement tracking lists; project activity reports and assessments/evaluations; and management checklists and reports. The above data will help setup a baseline, and continuous M&E will insure that progress is measured against that baseline.
