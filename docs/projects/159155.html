Project Summary

Somalia Based on the WASH Cluster Emergency Response Preparedness (ERP) plan, 0.4M (0.15M new
IDPs) additional people will be in need of emergency life-saving WASH assistance for a duration
of minimum 4 months in case of above normal Deyr 2019 and/or Gu 2020 rainy season. Priority
regions would include Lower Juba, that is being targeted in the project.
If dryness and/or drought conditions are maintained throughout the end of 2019 and the first
half of 2020, an additional 0.9M (0.3M new IDPs) people will face serious restrictions to access
to water and will require emergency water supply assistance in all Somalia. Epidemic of acute diarrheal diseases with an increase of the related mortality and morbidity could break out in AWD/Cholera hotspots following contamination of water points after heavy rainfalls or increased pressure on limited water sources, resulting in 2M additional people in need of emergency WASH assistance.
WRRS will deliver the programs in line with the national strategic plan with a scope of reducing the prevalence of illnesses that are related to unsafe drinking water, sanitation as well as hygiene. We will Provide access to safe Water, Sanitation and Hygiene for women, men, boys, girls, marginalized, minority and the physically challenged in emergency need in our target areas. We will ensure that the program delivers developing sustainable, gender sensitive and disaster responsive WASH infrastructures and resilience. These interventions will include provision of safe water and for both domestic and animal use, Rehabilitation and protection of existing water source will be enhanced. Just but to include: provision of solar water pumps to sampled boreholes, rehabilitation of shallow wells, provision of water storage containers/bladders within IDPs in Dohbley and Kismayu, distribution of water through water tracking/vouchers and chlorination of the same at facility level and HH. Conflict and flood affected areas will have construction latrines at the facility level and IDP settlements. Gender sensitive communal latrines will be constructed with lighting especially within the IDP settlement. Some of the flood affected latrines shall be desludging and repaired in our target areas. Hygiene promotion in communities and schools, distribution of hygiene kits and sanitation tools. Through our nutrition and health centres we shall teach beneficiaries on best hygiene and sanitation practices as an act of behaviour change, community workers will be equipped with emergency response preparedness and plan as a mitigation measure.
WRRS through the interventions will reach 6,000(2,000 women, 500 men, 2500 girls and 1500 boys) through equitable WASH interventions in Lower Juba (kismayu, Afmadow, Badhadhe, and Dhobley) region of south central Somalia. Monitoring and supervision for all programme activities will be done through post distribution monitoring. In order for the Community Led Sanitation to be successful and sustainable, community sensitization forums will be held led by community leaders and the outreach team. Coordination with the led clusters, ministries, local authorities beneficiaries and agencies will be adhered to for quality project implementation.

Mitigation Measures

A number of potential risk areas are anticipated by the organisation in implementing this project which range from medium to high. These include:• An anticipated new influx of IDPs during the project period,• A sudden onset of other emergencies such disease outbreaks or natural disasters• Bureaucratic impediments by local authorities• Conflicts between beneficiaries• Diversion of aid resources, fraud and corruption• Poor infrastructure such as roads becoming impassable• Deterioration of security• High inflation/ escalation of construction material pricesAccountability to affected population is a key aspect in the trans-formative agenda This will be enhanced through proper community engagement and ownership to avoid causing any risks to the affected or targeted beneficiaries. . The project intends to involve the community in all stages of the program cycle, so that they understand the aim and the outcome of the entire project and embrace its as it rolls, they will also understand the guiding principle behind the decisions made.The project intends to use already existing community governance structures and use them as health committees. These will be involved in recruitment of staff, community mobilization and monitoring of the project.Their presence will represent the interest and role of the community in successful completion of the project. The project will also have a feedback mechanism , from which complaints and comments from the community will be collected, and fed back to the same. This will be done in a highly confidential manner with very few staff in the management allowed to interact with this process. WRRS will address the complains as they come and embrace the comments for the sustainability and acceptance of the services within the community since these are live saving interventions.The organization will apply several mitigating measures to manage the above risk. Action plans will be reviewed regularly and synergies with other projects will be expanded. Further, coordination between IDP programmes and with other coordinators will be strengthened. There will be a constant monitoring in case of other emergencies.Active engagement of relevant authorities during implementation will be enhanced and advocacy through all levels of organization governance will be ensured. Beneficiary expectations will be managed through transparent communication with community and authority. The organization will also ensure transparent procurement and recruitment processes and build strong networks among all parties involved.To combat poor infrastructure, careful planning of implementation and logistical activities will be done in order to take into account seasonal changes. The organization will utilize local staff to gain access to and conduct mobilization activities at the community level to ensure acceptability and buy-in of the activities. There will be a continuous risk assessment and monitoring as well. Timely signing of the grant agreement will be done to avoid inflation and price escalation of equipment.

WRRS will factor in people living with different disabilities to ensure that they are met during the service delivery depending on their capability. They will not be segregated but factored in. WRRS has been working in areas where security and conflict has been a major factor affecting the beneficiaries. Accountability for violations is limited, with traditional or formal mediation and justice mechanisms having been disrupted, or having become disrespected or inaccessible. As a result of general insecurity, inter-clan conflict escalations, indiscriminate attacks as well as military offensives and withdrawals in the context of the conflict between AMISOM and AS, and supporting actors. This is further aggravated regarding violations for marginalized groups where societal inequalities result in reduced protection. WRRS will work closely in collaboration in providing education on Disaster Risk Reduction, and how to go about in areas with explosive remnants of wars.Gender disparities are on the rise in the operational communities, where women usually take a back seat in issues of community development. WRRS through the project, through community mobilization and awareness, will ensure women are empowered and represented to influence decisions and outcomes. A Do No Harm approach will be adopted in all our interventions. Contextual analysis is important in the implementation of this project considering the various potential sources of conflicts in the target locations both at leadership and community level. Regular assessments and community engagements will be done to learn if the project activities, approaches and methodologies are sensitive to the local context. Vulnerable community members generally refer to those disadvantaged with compromised access to resources to meet their daily basic needs. The project staff will ensure that during community mobilization and sensitization, all community members are given equal opportunity to participate.
