Project Summary

Search for Common Ground (Search), in partnership with the Centre for Sustainable Peace and Democratic Development (SeeD), proposes a 6-month project that will encompass 1) rigorous and innovative research compiled into policy briefs to inform decisions on program design for increasing access to education in 3 conflict-affected regions of Mali (namely , Mopti, Timbuktu, and Gao) and 2) capacity building on Conflict and Disaster Risk Reduction to strengthen the Malian education sector’s ability to prevent and mitigate risks in targeted areas.
This project will also contribute to COVID-19 response, trough:
- Inclusion of COVID-19 prevention sensitization and information in all focus group discussions, Key Informant interviews and all field researches
- Inclusion of COVID-19 prevention key information and messages in teacher training in line with existing Covid-19 school protocols
- Taking into account the risks of COVID-19 and other epidemics in the training of teachers, risk mapping and risk mitigation school plan
The first project pillar will combine two complimentary conflict assessment, social cohesion and violence and disaster risks prevention instruments that will provide data-driven analyses enhancing evidence-based policy making and program design for increasing access to education. These two instruments are a SCORE Index assessment and conflict scans. The backbone of this pillar is a quantitative survey that will utilize the SCORE (Social Cohesion and Reconciliation) Index methodology, calibrated to specific local needs, and designed to provide granular policy insights. The results will inform UNICEF, international and national partners to design evidence-based programs and policies in support of improving access to education in conflict-affected regions of Mali.
Under the second project pillar, Search will contribute to strengthening the Malian education sector in targeted areas by conducting trainings in Conflict and Disaster Risk Reduction, including COVID-19. This intervention will build the skills of education actors (teachers) to analyze risks and develop a local response which will contribute to prevent, mitigate and reduce conflict and disaster risk within and around schools. In collaboration with the Malian Ministry of Education, decentralized services of the national education system (Académies d’Enseignement, AE, and Centre d’Animation Pedagogique, CAP) and UNICEF, Search will develop a training curriculum and strengthen the capacities of 240 teachers from 80 schools in target communes on conflict transformation, conflict and risk analysis, implementation of school mitigation and response plans. Furthermore, Search will equip education actors with confidence and skills to lead participatory risk mappings in each school on the basis of which a local / school based risk mitigation and response plan will be developed, as well as a monitoring system to track progress and strengthen accountability.
Despite the current COVID-19 pandemic situation, Search is confident that all of the above activities can still be implemented, in strict observance of and compliance with existing COVID-19 protocols (hygiene and barrier measures, social distancing, etc.) . Search has designed a specific protocol to guide program implementation in line with the Protection cluster recommendations as well as the regulations enacted by the Government of Mali.

1 - Contexte

Security and sanitary crisis due to Covid-19 Mali has been at the epicenter of the instability plaguing the Sahel for the past few years. Armed groups, driven by intertwined dynamics of banditry, violent extremism, and community-level conflicts, which were initially contained to Northern Mali, have established a strong foothold in the Mopti region, taking advantage of weak state presence, porous borders, and local grievances. Since the beginning of 2019, intercommunal conflicts between Bambara, Dogon, and Fulani communities triggered a cycle of communal violence in the Central regions of Segou and Mopti, which caused massive human rights violations and forced displacements, which further restricted access to education in the region. As of October 2020, 1,230 schools were closed compared to 920 in June 2019. The Mopti region is the most-affected, with 741 schools closed which represent 60% of the total (Education Cluster). However, teachers and schools continue to be targeted by non-state armed groups . The COVID-19 pandemic has aggravated the multidimensional crisis that Mali has been experiencing since 2012, with very painful social, health and economic consequences for the population. According to Mali's Covid-19 situation report of 29 November 2020, the country recorded a total of 4,688 positive cases of Covid-19, of which 152 (3.2%) death and 3,187 people healed (67.38%). Geographically, 9 regions and 39 health districts affected. Regions include Kayes (431); Koulikoro (445); Sikasso (126); Ségou (28); Mopti (260); Timbuktu (581); Gao (61); Kidal(50) and Bamako District (2,706). Since the month of October, it is observed an increase in the notification of positive cases from a week to another. For example, 433 new cases were reported in the 29 November report compared to the previous week. In the field of education, the pandemic led to the closure of all schools between mid-March and mid-September 2020 (after the reopening of examination classes in early June). However, despite the official reopening of schools on September 14, the long period of interruption has increased the number of dropouts, some children have not returned for various reasons and many schools are still closed for security reasons in the targeted regions.
