Project Summary

Bonyan Organization designed its 2020 ERL intervention based on the analysis of humanitarian needs (MSNA data) of affected population in NW Syrian, need severity for ERL assistance, and key messages of HNO 2020, as well as, in line with Strategic Objectives of HRP and ERL Cluster. The project aims to strengthen access to livelihoods, improve access to basic services and infrastructure, and support efforts of local economy-recovery in addition to support social cohesion for the affected-population, targeting 24,330 INDV (11,435 M/ 12,895 F, 10,608 Children (<18), 12,530 Adults) in Aleppo and Idleb governorates. The project will empower youth participation through establishing Youth Groups, whereas the project activities will fall into two main categories as following:
***** Improving access to income-generating opportunities, and to production and economic infrastructure as well as to basic services targeting 18,330 individuals (9,715 F/ 8,615 M), of which 75% are children and youth
[1] BO will rehabilitate 3 local economic infrastructures (i.e. markets, key road, wheat silos or bakery) benefiting 9,000 INDV (~4,200 M, ~4,800 F), also, will rehabilitate 15 schools (240 classrooms and 60 latrines) benefiting 6,750 children, 6-17 years old (3,175 Boys/ 5,575 Girls). The rehabilitation of schools will include repairing of wall, doors and windows; whiteboard installation; painting; and simple decoration. Cash for Work modality will be used in the rehabilitation to create 80 short-term income-generating opportunities for the most vulnerable people. The workers will be selected based on clear eligibility and vulnerability criteria.
[2] BO will provide 100 hours training on vocational skills, and 20 hours on management and finance skills to 150 youth (18-35 YO) on the most critical vocational/ skill gaps, that will be identified based on market need assessment, also, will deliver in-kind support to the youth (i.e. maintenance kit, barbershop chair, sewing machines, or solar system installation kit), so that the youth accessibility to livelihood opportunities will improved.
[3] BO will support 100 entrepreneurship and 100 rural enterprises by grants and productive assets, in addition to provide training on basic marketing, management principles, and cost/ price estimation techniques; that will allow the entrepreneurs to initiate, rehabilitate or expand their enterprises such as sewing, barber, small market, carpentry, water and sanitation workshop, electrical workshop, or mini-grocery market.
***** Improving resilience and cohesion of affected-community targeting 6,000 INDV (3,180 F, 2,820 M)
[4] BO team observations and reports shows that absence of banking or credit services is pointed as a major problem for many people created a significant gap in their ability to address their basic needs. Therefore, BO will establish 3 “village micro-credit associations” to serve as a small window for micro-credit. Bonyan will constitute management committees, formulate rules of operation, establish the capital window of USD10,000, and provide advices in managing the associations.
Throughout the project, BO will select the target communities based on scoring and prioritizing system in coordination with ERL Cluster, then will engage the selected community in all phases of the project, especially youth. Also, BO will ensure that the support will be provided to the most vulnerable people through transparent process based on clear vulnerability criteria (HLP verification will be included in case of providing enterprise support). Principles of “Do No Harm” and “Accountability to Affected People” are incorporated in the project design and will be ensured during the project timeline. Also, MEAL team of BO will conduct ongoing monitoring of project activities to verify achievement of indicators and to correct any deviation.

4 - Needs

HNO2020 indicates that:• 60% of the working-age population are without access to sustained employment (55% of M and 75% of F), as well as, without access to vocational and skills training (65% of M and 75% of F). This may lead populations, especially for youth, to resort to harmful coping strategies including but not limited to joining extremist groups, crime, and trafficking.• 75% of PwD do not have access to or income, which strains their physical and mental well-being and increases dependency on other HH members. • 25% of basic and social infrastructure is destroyed, putting stress on communities’ access to basic services, which erodes HHs’ living standards and weakens their capacity to cope with internal and external shocks.A recent report on HLP issues showed ‘unaffordable housing’ as a concern in 100% of sub-districts surveyed in NW Syria, and many families cannot afford rent costs due to lack of livelihoods. As for 2015, more than 83% of Syrians lived below the poverty line, and more recent indications suggest that it has worsened. HHs spend savings, running up debts buying on credit, depleting HH assets and reducing food consumption as the most common negative coping strategies to compensate for the lack of income. These coping mechanisms are ultimately unsustainable and may continue to lead people to resort to increasingly exploitative coping mechanisms when their financial and material resources are exhausted. Such mechanisms disproportionately affect the most vulnerable groups, such as female-headed HHs, adolescents, PwD, IDPs, youth, etc. Adolescents and youth are amongst the most economically and socially marginalized groups with few available opportunities to participate in economic activities and decision-making that can contribute towards the realization of their rights. Also, high number of displaced people within the communities in NW Syria overburdens social services and economic situation, leading to potential rupture in the social fabric

5 - Implementation of activities

BO will coordinate with relevant stakeholders to conduct need assessment survey in order to collect up to date data about ERL needs of IDPs, Host Community and Returnees (with keeping in mind the specific needs of different age and gender groups), in addition to collect any other necessary data (i.e. market assessment or security assessment). Then, BO will select beneficiaries following transparent prosses based on clear vulnerability/ eligibility criteria to ensure provision of support to the most vulnerable affected-population. Along with the implementation, MEAL team will carry out ongoing M&E activities to verify project indicators.
