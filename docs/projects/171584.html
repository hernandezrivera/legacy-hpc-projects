Project Summary

The overall objective of the project will contribute to education cluster objective 2 of the HRP strategic objective, by supporting and coordinating a coherent and effective Education humanitarian response. This includes the coordination of needs assessments; development of a response strategy, long-term planning, cross-sector collaboration and advocacy.
Specifically, the project will ensure uninterrupted implementation of the core cluster coordination functions including:
1. Supporting service delivery and coordination
2. Informing strategic decision-making of the HC/HCT for the humanitarian response
3. Planning and strategy development
4. Advocacy
5. Monitoring and reporting
6. Capacity building in contingency/preparedness planning
In the context of Palestine and especially with the recent COVID 19 pandemic, authorities lack national coordination mechanisms for addressing the enormous challenges facing the education system; each education system in East Jerusalem, Area C and Gaza has different duty bearers and face different significant protection challenges. The Education Cluster will support the relevant duty bearers to ensure that children (age 8-12) 50% girls, youth (13-18) and teachers in the most vulnerable communities can safely access schools that meet minimum humanitarian standards and provide a protective environment in the context of protracted conflict. All interventions planned through the 2021 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) will be closely coordinated with local communities and meet the requirement of the strong protection mainstreaming and community engagement especially in the needs assessment as well as the implementation of activities.
The Cluster is also planning to strengthen the partners level engagement on gender through joint cluster needs assessments on gender specific needs in the field, joint HRP mid review and related gender analysis, strengthening partners capacity on data collection and reporting of disaggregated data related to special needs of women, girls, boys or men. Partners will jointly identify the cluster priority interventions and responses based on the gender disaggregated needs assessments, reports and testimonies.

1 - Needs

Across the oPt, an estimated half a million Palestinian boys and girls are in need of education humanitarian assistance, around three quarters of whom live in Gaza and 51% are girls. Against the unique contextual backdrop of the oPt and the recent outbreak of COVID-19 there is a continued need for coordinated humanitarian response and advocacy. There is a high demand within the local humanitarian community, as well as in the broader international community, for support to develop a robust, detailed, evidence-based common situational analysis; for the coordination of advocacy and operational humanitarian responses; for the management and monitoring of country-based pooled funds; and for the development and monitoring of strategic needs analyses and response plans. With over 40 implementing partners having participated in the 2020 HRP, and financial requirements reaching over USD 15 million targeting 0.4 million people in 2019, there is a clear strategic need for continued coordination of humanitarian action in oPt to maximize achievements, while reducing gaps and duplication in the provision of humanitarian assistance. Education Cluster unique role in facilitating and coordinating these streams of humanitarian work continues therefore to be of critical importance, especially with the COVID-19 draining impacts on the education sector. Humanitarian access to education continues to be compromised due to protracted conflict and the occupation. For half a million child (51% girls) in the West Bank, East Jerusalem and Gaza access to quality education in a safe, child friendly environment is impeded by the presence of checkpoints, barrier, military and armed group activities and incursions, settler related incidents, as well as the lack of infrastructure. The disruption of schooling affects education quality and brings about an array of psychosocial effects, such as excessive stress, fear and distress. Through service delivery, advocacy and capacity development, the cluster will seek to mitigate the effects of, ensure preparedness and an immediate response to, emergencies such as localized natural disasters, COVID-19, a resurgence of conflict, forced displacement and attacks on schools. The response will focus on advocacy efforts to address the increasing trend of education related violations. 9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The education cluster aims to provide a relevant and holistic education response by promoting and integrating all fundamental cross-cutting issues and principles. This will help ensure all interventions planned by partners are protective, inclusive and taking into consideration gender equity. The emergency education strategy in oPt is driven by protection-sensitive activities, which underpin the Sector Response. In addition to school-based psychosocial support, providing protective presence for students and teachers, legal aid and advocacy to help protect schools from attacks, the Education Cluster will work with the Child Protection AoR to link schools with child protection service providers to address protection needs in the most vulnerable schools.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Gender equality and gender-responsive programming is promoted through conducting gender analysis with due care to the different needs and protection concerns in health, protection and education of females and males. All data collected by the education cluster partners should be age- and gender-disaggregated. Projects will address gender discrimination in terms of access to education, as well as the diﬀerent risks that boys and girls face on their way to, from and inside, school. Partners will ensure equal participation of boys and girls in student clubs and ensure that school WASH facilities are gender segregated.Community engagement will be enhanced by the establishment of school-based safety committees, through existing community structures, in some targeted schools; to identify priorities, map existing resources within the community, prepare school-based contingency plans, and coordinate activities in the school. Parent-teacher associations will also be involved in key education advocacy, in addition to collecting feedback from community members to enhance accountability to aﬀected populations with due consideration of gender equity. Student groups will also ensure that students’ voices are integral to the design and implementation of emergency education activities. The Education Cluster will encourage partners to establish feedback and complaint mechanisms, where students and beneficiaries can raise their concerns and participate in projects’ design and share recommendations.
