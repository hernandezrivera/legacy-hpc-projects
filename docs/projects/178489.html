Project Summary

Shafak's health program aims to contribute to the provision of access to life-saving and life-sustaining humanitarian health services for people in need and conflict-affected populations in northern Syria. The proposed intervention will prioritize internally displaced people and other vulnerable populations especially (women, children, and people with disabilities) in the targeted areas. The intervention will be implemented through:
1. Continue supporting a comprehensive package of health services including Primary Health Care, and Mental Health in six of the existing health facilities in northwest Syria. The motivation of this project is to continue providing health services in the already established and equipped centers to the population in western and southern rural Aleppo and rural Idlib with outreach and referral activities to the higher level of health services. The project will comprise two convergent components: primary health and mental health care, and their activities will be provided through the following health centers:

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2021, Syria is considered one of the most complex humanitarian emergencies characterized by ongoing hostilities which have killed hundreds of thousands of people, triggered one of the worst displacement crises of our time, and led to the widespread destruction of civilian infrastructure, including schools, health facilities ..etc. Today, 13.4 million people in Syria are in need of humanitarian assistance, with increasing needs being exacerbated by economic decline.The same source reported 7.78 million people are living below three critical emergency health standards, and a localized household survey in NWS found crude and under-five mortality rates were below international standards. With just 58% of hospitals and 53% PHC fully functional, the COVID-19 pandemic has weakened the health system. According to Sitrep No. 26, there were 21,282 confirmed cases of COVID-19 in NWS as of March 26, and 19,353 have recovered. In addition, Sitrep No. 25 reported that the greatest challenge for 2021 will be to sustain an appropriate response and manage the spread of the virus in northwest Syria.Shafak has conducted a multi-sectoral (Education and Health) rapid needs assessment in April 2021 (attached) in Aghtrin, Suran, Dana, Salqin, and Qourqeena sub-districts, where the influx of the IDPs are highest. The key findings concluded the following: - the assessed areas have a huge need for health services where the total PiN of medical in the targeted communities is 37,200, (56% IDPs), and 37,171 (100% IDPs) in the 11 assessed camps. - 60% of the affected population visit the supported health facilities to get health services. - 80% do not do the usual routine visits to health facilities.

5 - Implementation of activities

In accordance with Shafak’s cross-border strategy, we will be responsible for direct implementation in the target locations coordinating with and gaining approval from health authorities in NW Syria, building on their extensive network, and facilitating access. Local communities will be involved in Shafak’s capacity development efforts and suitable training. Shafak will be responsible for ensuring that quality and accountability standards are met, will provide required training to staff, and ensure that contractual requirements are adhered to and met. Further, we will be responsible for all program and financial reporting to the donor. Shafak will also continue to coordinate with relevant clusters and other coordination bodies in Turkey. This joint program will be conducted through three main phases as the following: Phase I: - Procurement of needed equipment, devices, drugs, consumables, tools for training, and tools for implementation. Phase II: Launch of - Outpatient services, comprehensive reproductive health services, and chronic disease treatment in all facilities. - Emergency surgery, emergency obstetric, and newborn services in the hospital. - PHC services. - Coordinate with health partners to identify locations of mobile clinics. - Providing specialist care for renal failure patients. Phase III: - Reporting, data analysis, monitoring, and evaluation. At the start of the project, Shafak will conduct a project opening meeting with Shafak staff. The project activities and timeline will be discussed and all data gathering tools, including monitoring links on specific tools, will be shared and agreed upon.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project's overall objective aims at obtaining full respect for the rights of the individual where they need them the most. Throughout the project life cycle, the principles of protection mainstreaming are incorporated. All activities are designed to avoiding causing harm and prioritize the safety and dignity of targeted populations, ensuring meaningful access, accountability, and participation through several, action such as • The project is designed to meet the needs of the community especially women, boys, and girls considering the context, tradition, and norms in the community • Location of facilities will be accessible and easy to reach for all people., also safe and away from any military location. • Community will be clearly and widely informed about the date, location, and timing of the service provision, and their opinion will be highly considered. • Team staff will be trained to be skilled in delivering PFA, identifying CP&GBV cases, and refer them to adequate services. • Ensure that the location is suitable for women, girls, people with disabilities, children-headed households, old people, and other affected categories in the community--- outreach. • Distribution and providing services for specific categories mentioned above will be to the door. • Teams will be trained on PESA, Child safeguarding, and will sign the code of conduct related. • project team will set up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can receive the information they need, provide feedback on the activities. accountability officer will be responsible for information provision, collecting and responding to feedback, and will report directly to HQ to ensure transparency •

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

All staff members sign the Shafak PSEA code of conduct upon recruitment, where a protection specialist provides orientations and regular PSEA training sessions on child protection policy and the PSEA code of conduct.The areas targeted have high numbers of IDPs. Host communities are the front-line responders to the needs of IDPs. Due to limited local resources and limited aid hosting communities can see IDPs as a burden to the community. This can result in tensions over sharing limited resources. This can be exacerbated by projects that use the status of IDP as a single proxy criterion for “in need”. To avoid this, and to promote a no do harm approach the project seeks to meet the needs of IDPs and host communities based on need, working toward equity between these groups.Moreover, Shafak would provide activities that include prevention and response services, identification of GBV and CP cases, referral to specialized (GBV and CP) and non-specialized services in accordance with the guidelines shared by the protection cluster, and provide direct support to high priority cases. Shafak protection outreach teams will help the education project team as well by identifying cases in schools and health facilities supported and refer them to the proper specialized service needed.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Airstrikes and Shelling over NWS Shafak would make sure to follow the below measures in response to this expected risk: • security assessment of the operational areas to avoid sites that have been targeted before. • Prevent and decrease crowds in front of the center. • Equipping the staff with VHFs. • Training the staff on how to deal in cases of shelling and conduct practical training. • Equipping the center with fire extinguishers • An evacuation plan for center ready and communicated to all staff • An emergency contact list is available at the center. • Staff to be aware of communication protocols Risk 2: closing of Bab al-Hawa BCP that can result in a shortage of consumables, equipment, and kits. Shafak would make sure to follow the below measures in response to this expected risk: • Monitoring the general security situation in the border area. • provide each center with consumables and medical kits that are enough for each center to work for 3 months at least. • using a different route for securing the supply chain process instead of Bab al-Hawa BCP by using Bab al-Slama BCP near Azaz in the northern-countryside of Aleppo. Risk 3: Deviation of aid. Shafak would make sure to follow the below measures in response to this expected risk: • Informing field team not to comply with any request from a person or armed faction to give them vouchers. • Stopping the work and withdrawal from the center, relocation also can be an option according to the situation, if there is any threat from the military factions, and then report the subject case to our donor. • An ATC Check to be done for all suppliers\ vendors before signing the contract with them.
