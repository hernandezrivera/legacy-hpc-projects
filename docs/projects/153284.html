Project Summary

The project links to Sector Objective 2 which is to “Protect, stabilize and safeguard rural & urban livelihoods to improve food availability and reduce the food gap”, it also links to Cluster Objective 2: to “Increase vulnerable household food production to protect the erosion of livelihoods through y agricultural seeds and fishing inputs”.
The project aims to “contribute to improved food security and livelihoods of 11,667 most vulnerable households in Twic East County of Jonglei State, South Sudan”. CASI will support 11,667 vulnerable households with emergency food and livelihood kits comprising fishing kits and rapid response kits (made up of cow peas and three varieties of fast maturing vegetable seeds). The CASI fisheries field assistant will train the fishers on improved fish handling, processing and preservation techniques and provide them with fishing kits that will enable them to produce good quality products for own consumption and for sales Similarly the agriculture field officer will train crop growers on improved husbandry methods for production of vegetables and field crops. The trained individuals will be provided with cow peas and vegetables seeds to produce own high nutritious vegetables and cow peas for household consumption and for the market. The technical skills and productive inputs provided by CASI will enable them to produce own food and thereby achieve some degree of food and nutrition security for their households.
Twic East faces chronic food insecurity and by January-March 2019, it is projected that about 81,000 individuals (65% of the population) will fall in the Crisis and Emergency phase (IPC phase 3 and 4). CASI will target 11,667 most vulnerable households (70,000 individuals) in Twic East County with fishing kits, cow peas and vegetables for own food production as well as for sales. The livelihood kits will be accessed from the Food Security and Livelihoods Cluster pipeline and distributed to vulnerable households who are negatively impacted by the conflict related to inter-communal and clashes, cattle rustling and child abduction. The productive inputs combined with the skills acquired through training will enable the most vulnerable households to produce own food, increase their food consumption and avert the need to resort to negative coping strategies that will result in depletion of livelihood assets and food insecurity.
CASI will involve the community in formulating criteria for the selection of beneficiaries. The community will elect a Project Committee which in collaboration with CASI staff will assist to identify and select vulnerable households to benefit from the interventions. The committees’ members will be trained on participatory planning, leadership and group dynamics. CASI will aware the beneficiaries on nutrition, hygiene and primary health care. This will be in addition to training on protection related issues (rights to life with dignity, right to receive humanitarian assistance and rights to protection and security)
The proposed activities are necessary and sufficient to achieve the project outputs. The output targets are reasonable and achievable considering CASI capacity and experience in successfully implementing food security and livelihoods interventions in Twic East County. The selected indicators are SMART and represent key inputs, processes, outputs and outcomes, in addition to impact. CASI Monitoring and Evaluation staff will track how the project is progressing in terms of expenditures, resources use, and implementation of activities and delivery of results. The major methods/sources of data for monitoring and evaluation of the project will include registration and inputs distributions record, Internal Activity report, minutes of stakeholder meetings, feedback from focus group discussions and key informants, post distribution monitoring report, and monitoring and evaluation report. The monitoring tools to be used include periodic reports-weekly, monthly, quarterly, half yearly and annual, regular staff meeting, field visits and observation.

Needs

The deteriorating and precarious levels of food and nutrition security in South Sudan during the peak of the June-July 2018 lean season have been attributed to multiple underlying factors which included persistent conflict, disruption of livelihoods, poor economic condition and challenges delivering humanitarian assistance to vulnerable populations According to the August 2, 2018 joint report of the UN Children’s Fund (UNICEF), the UN Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO), and WFP timely humanitarian assistance prevented food security conditions from worsening to Famine—IPC 5—levels of acute food insecurity. The report indicated that vulnerable populations in Jonglei, Unity, Upper Nile, and Western Bahr el Ghazal remain heavily dependent on food assistance to meet basic food needs. According to the report. FAO, UNICEF, and WFP emphasized that conflict remains the primary driver of worsening food security and acute malnutrition levels, particularly due to loss of livelihoods and humanitarian access constraints. Timely humanitarian assistance remains critical to preventing further deterioration of food security and livelihoods.According to the September 2018 IPC, in Jonglei state about 1,093,000 persons (63% of the population) fell in the IPC Crisis, Emergency and Catastrophe phase and the projection for October-December 2018 is that 945,000 persons (54% of the population) will be food insecure. The food security situation is expected to worsen during the January-March 2019 lean season whereby about 1,182,000 individuals (65% of the population) will be severely food insecure. In Twic East County by October-December 2018 about 54,000 individuals (about 45 % of the population) is expected to be in Crisis and Emergency phases. The food insecurity situation is projected to drastically deteriorate in January-March 2018 onset of the lean period where about 81,000 persons (about 65% of the population) will fall in the Crisis, Emergency and Famine/Catastrophe phases. This large proportion of TWIC East population will be unable to meet their food and other essential needs and therefore require urgent humanitarian assistance to limit the expected deteriorating food security and livelihoods conditions. The UNICEF South Sudan Report, May 2018 expressed concern about the nutrition condition in Twic East County, with critical global acute malnutrition (GAM) levels of 24.1% above the WHO emergency threshold of 15 percent In Jonglei state conflict continues to be a major driver of food insecurity, inter-communal clashes resulting in population displacements, loss of crops in the fields and food stocks left behind by fleeing IDPs. The systematic looting of domestic animals allegedly by youth from neighboring Pibor resulting in depletion of livestock assets and disruption of livelihoods in Twic County. These factors do contribute to inadequate access to food for the affected households and thus lead to food and nutrition security. A combination of recurrent dry spells and heavy rains and flooding do annually impact negatively on vegetables, cereals and leguminous crops production in Twic East County. Fish production is constrained by limited access to fishing gears, and high post harvest losses.. CASI has prioritized interventions in vegetable, crops and fisheries and will distribute the fishing kits/emergency response kits to 70,000 individuals (11,667 households) in Twic East County during the 2019 agriculture season. Increased agriculture and fisheries production is expected to contribute to improved food security and livelihoods for about 86 percent of the 81,000 persons (65% ) in Twic East County who are projected to fall in IPC phase 3 and 4 by January to March 2019.

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

The consultations were in the form of community meetings, focus groups discussion and key informant interviews to find out the felt needs of the communities. The meetings and consultation involved men, women, boys and girls so as to find divergent needs and priorities of the community. and how the organization can help address them. The consultations also involved meetings with County and Payam authorities and the local authorities to validate the information obtained from other sources. The organization also had a series of meetings with the Country Agriculture Department on the the problems and issue related to agriculture, livestock and fisheries in the localities. Key informant interviews were conducted with the technical experts in agriculture, livestock and fisheries and in addition we had a series of meetings with CASI farmers groups on the problems they face and their priorities for resolving the constraints in a sustainable manner.
