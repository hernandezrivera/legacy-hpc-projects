Project Summary

The project aims at improving equitable, affordable and sustainable access to safe and sufficient WASH services during emergencies and shocks in accordance with dignity of the underserved and most vulnerable communities of Wadi Al Salqa in the Gaza Strip. The project targets the most vulnerable communities of Wadi Al Salqa Area with particular focus on hardship cases, female headed households, GBV survivors, and people with Disabilities. The objective of this action is consistent with the assigned objectives of the WASH Cluster 2019 HRP, objective 1: “Ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable access to safe and sufficient WASH services in accordance with dignity of the. unserved, underserved and most vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank"; and objective 2: “Strengthen WASH preparedness and response capacity to the unserved, underserved and most vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank, to reduce threats and to cope with emergencies and shocks”. The main activities include (1) installing renewable energy / solar systems for public desalination plants, (2) provide spare parts for public desalination plants, (3) maintenance of in-house WASH facilities (age, gender, and culturally sensitive WASH facilities (including toilets, kitchen and bathing facilities), (4) Maintenance/replacement of cesspits, (5) distribution of storage tanks for domestic and drinking water, (6) distribution of jerrycans, (7) Building the Emergency Planning and Response capacity of Wadi Al Salqa Municipality to be responsive for the flooding emergency situations, and (8) Hygiene promotion activities and distribution of sanitary supplies and hygiene materials
In addition, the project responses to detected GBV concerns in the conflict affected area of Wadi Al Salqa. The project targets WASH beneficiaries who showed GBV related concerns. This includes mainly the cases of GBV survivors who already had been identified with poor access to WASH services through the conducted assessment, besides other cases who will be supported under WASH component and show GBV concerns. This action is consistent with the assigned objectives of the Protection Cluster 2019 HRP, objective 2:” Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence”. The main activities include (1) provide case management and referral services; (2) distribution of dignity kits; and (3) awareness raising on GBV. The intervention will include to study more in-depth the relation between GBV and poor access to equitable and adequate WASH services.

Needs

Following a humanitarian appeal launched by Wadi Al Salqa Municipality in early 2018, MA’AN had carried out an in-depth assessment addressing people access to equitable, safe and sufficient WASH services and examined the related risks of GBV. In coordination with the Ministry of Social Development, 136 of the most underserved vulnerable households in Wadi Al Salqa had been visited, besides 16 cases of GBV survivors and 5 cases of people with disabilities. Teams of social workers and civil engineers, including 9 women and 4 men, were responsible for the data collection. GBV specialist was consulted throughout the planning, design, analysis and interpretation of the assessment. Consultations with the Municipality, CMWU, women organizations, and protection services providers were conducted. Among the assessed population 94.3% are abject poor and other 5.7% found poor. 25.5% of households reported no income and a completed dependency on aid. The assessed population included 16.6% female-headed households, 14.6% elderly headed households, 2.1% people with disabilities, and 0.6% pregnant/lactating women.According to the assessment results 45.2% of the assessed households have poor and unsafe storage tanks for domestic water. 43.9% of the households do not have potable water storage tanks. 86.6% of the households have toilets in poor conditions that are neither healthy nor safe. 74.6% of the households have kitchens in unhealthy/unsafe conditions. The results showed that 98.1% households use cesspits where 62.3% of the cesspits are in poor and unsafe conditions. 80.0% of the visited cases of people with disabilities reported the inadequacy of the WASH facilities to their particular needs. The poor access to adequate and WASH services have reflections on the social relations and affects adversely the psychological well-being of women and increases the risks women face in the form of violence. The visited cases of GBV survivors showed high concerns related to the toilets and kitchen facilities which are unsafe and unhealthy with poor privacy. The majority of interviewed women reported that their poor access to adequate and safe WASH services has further intensified their responsibility for unpaid care and domestic work. Poor hygiene conditions of in-house WASH facilities combined with shortage of hygienic material inside homes was recognized. Children in particular are negatively affected, where the unsafe water and insufficient hygiene are the main causes for recurring diarrhoea. 60% of the surveyed families reported poor access to sanitary supplies and hygiene materials Four public drinking water desalination plants are the main source for drinking water in Wadi Al Salqa. The desalination units are in need for minor maintenance activities, spare parts, and sustainable source of energy.As resulted from the assessment, women and girls are majorly involved in decisions of drinking water sources, and the choices of sanitary supplies and hygiene materials. The majority of women are responsible for bringing drinking water for their households. Several women take on the role of emptying cesspits and discharging sewage outside their homes. Women reported safety and security concerns related to their responsibilities. Women lack of influence and participation concerning WASH services at the community level was figured out.

Activities

The elaboration of the project activities had considered the conducted needs assessment, the coordination with the clusters and PWA, a market assessment, and further consultations with the target beneficiaries regarding their priorities and the proper response mechanisms with particular focus on women and children. The project activities prioritized female headed households, individuals with physical disabilities, the elderly, GBV survivors, and other highly vulnerable individuals and groups who are more likely to resort to negative coping strategies. The project activities include: - With equal participation of men and women, establishment of community based WASH committees to ensure accountable, gender sensitive, and environmentally friendly policies, practices, and decisions of duty bearers that are related to WASH issues.- Rehabilitate sanitation facilities for unconnected 400 households to meet SPHERE standards (age, gender, and culturally sensitive WASH facilities, and adequate to the needs of people with disabilities). This includes 50 cases of GBV survivors with poor access to equitable, safe and adequate WASH services. The cases had been already identified through the conducted assessment.- Maintenance/replacement of cesspits, 300 units. This includes emptying cesspits and discharging sewage to safe places in coordination with CMWU. This will be considered as a temporary solution till the public sewage system installed. Safe and efficient cesspit design has been developed and coordinated with the CMWU to be installed.- Installing renewable energy/solar systems for four public desalination plants (a total of 30 KW).- Providing spare parts and operational supplies for the four public desalination plants. This includes the Eastern Well Station, Al Ma’sara Well Station, Kusofeem Well Station, and Al Quds Street Well Station.- Distribution of 200 functional and safe Roof Storage Tanks (1000 liters’ capacity), and 50 functional and safe Potable water storage tanks (200 liters’ capacity)- Distribution of 800 jerrycans. As the majority of families in the targeted area have no access to water transporting services and they rely on using jerrycans for handling the water transporting. 800 families will be supported to have safe jerrycans. The families will be identified in coordination with the community based WASH committees. - Distribution of 800 hygiene kits (60% of the total population of Wadi Al Salqa). Sanitary supplies and hygiene materials will be appropriate to distribute to women and girls, especially related to menstruation. This will be accompanied with hygiene awareness activities.- In partnership with Women Affairs Center, the 50 cases of GBV survivors, who benefited from the WASH component, will receive set of GBV related services including: (1) case management and referral services; (2) distribution of dignity kits; and (3) awareness raising on GBV reaching women, men, and boys. Other cases of WASH beneficiaries who reported GBV concerns will be supported (estimated at 10 cases).- Conduct an in-depth study addressing the relation between GBV and the poor access to equitable, safe, and adequate WASH services in Gaza.

Indicators

A monitoring and evaluation mechanism will be established to follow up the progress of the process, to ensure the matching of the identified indicators and the expected outcomes of the project. The M&E plan identifies tasks, quality of outputs, means of verification and persons in charge in respect to the project timeframe and budget. It will compile also:a. Full coordination and involvement of relevant stakeholders during all project phasesb. Ensuring the gender mainstreaming in all project’s phases and outputs Accountability to involved stakeholders is encouraged and assured through a complaint mechanism which involves a direct communication flow among beneficiaries, the Municipality, and MA’AN. MA’AN’s M&E Officer will be responsible to supervise and monitor the project activities throughout the development of the action. MA'AN team of site engineers will stay in the working fields observing onsite the construction and maintenance activities. Biweekly monitoring visits will be conducted by MA'AN M&E officer to ensure the quality of data and to measure the beneficiaries' satisfaction. The main tools MA'AN M&E department will employ are questionnaires, baseline and evaluation surveys to measure outputs and progress on indicators, and beneficiary satisfaction assessments. All monitoring data collected will be fully disaggregated by age, sex, disability status and governorate. Data collection and monitoring process will be done with the engagement of beneficiaries in realization with the cultural and social challenges specific to the targeted communities with the emphasis of doing no harm approach. Specific focus will be on providing appropriate opportunities to amplify the voices of groups with protection needs including female-headed households and disabled persons, not only to articulate their own needs but to give them space to contribute to project decision-making and evaluation. - # of people with improved access to safe drinking water (Target: 8000 individuals including: 2085 Women (18-59); 2026 men (18-59), 1718 girls (under 18), 1821 boys (under 18), 350 elderly (above 59))- # of people with improved access to sanitation (Target: 1789 individuals including: 466 Women (18-59); 453 men (18-59), 384 girls (under 18), 407 boys (under 18), 78 elderly (above 59))- # households that have a handwashing facility with soap and water available on premises (Target: 2385 individuals including: 622 Women (18-59); 604 men (18-59), 512 girls (under 18), 543 boys (under 18), 104 elderly (above 59))- # of people received hygiene materials/kits (Target: 4,769 individuals including: 1,243 women (18-59); 1,208 men (18-59), 1,024 girls (under 18), 1,085 boys (under 18), 209 elderly (above 59))- % of women (18-59) and girls (under 18) reported less concerns about hygiene and accessibility to water (Target:100%)- # of GBV survivors (women, girls, men and boys) receiving dignity kits (Target: 45 women (18-59), 15 girls (under 18))- # of cases externally referred (Target: 30)- # of persons (women, girls, men and boys) participating in GBV awareness raising sessions (Target: 45 Women (18-59); 60 men (18-59), 15 girls (under 18), 60 boys (under 18))
