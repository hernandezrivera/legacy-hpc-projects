Project Summary

The objective of this project is to enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19 for households classified in Crisis (IPC 5, 4 and some 3) in Panyijiar and Mayendit counties (Unity state), Aweil South (NBeG state) of South Sudan. The IRC proposes to provide a sequence of emergency food production to support the most severe food-insecure households in Panyijiar, Aweil South, and Mayendit counties from the 2021 lean season. To augment household resilience, the program will mainstream gender, conflict sensitivity, disaster risks reduction, and COVID-19 risk mitigation measures to support at-risk households focused on improving crop, livestock, and fisheries production and bolstering resilience towards recurrent shocks and stresses in communities. In pursuit of the program objective, the following activities will be implemented:
(a) Engagement and consultation of community and local leaders to confirm needs and agree on most vulnerable household’s selection criteria and register beneficiaries.
(b) Preposition and distribute emergency (short-term maturing) agricultural inputs (seeds and tools) and fishing kits including through input trade fairs (ITF) to farmers where the market is functioning and inputs available. The IRC anticipates the Food and Agricultural Organization (FAO) will provide complimentary in-kind inputs to support the project. ITFs will be utilized to promote restocking of mainly small ruminants (sheep and goats’ assets) and backyard poultry targeting households with malnourished mothers, Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLWs), and most vulnerable households including flood and conflict-affected female-headed households, widows, and widowers, and the elderly. During distribution, IRC will engage community health volunteers to disseminate COVID-19 messages, provide personal protective kits including facemasks, and limit crowd at distribution points following COVID-19 protocols.
(c) The IRC in collaboration with the County Agriculture Department (CAD), State Ministries of Agriculture, and FAO, will contribute to livestock disease surveillance and monitoring, and facilitate vaccination campaigns, mass vaccination exercises, and treatments to livestock in the three targeted counties.
(d) Facilitate capacity building to county-level extension agents (CAHWs, AEA’s), local crop producers, pastoralists, and fisherfolks on sustainable production skills and provision of extension services around the package of production inputs and post-harvest handling to be provided through the climate-smart vegetable and crop demonstration gardens established. Also, fisherfolks receive basic training on fish preservation and kit repairs to enhance household’s nutrition and income.
The IRC will mainstream gender equity and promote gender inclusiveness, allowing the most vulnerable and disadvantaged women, girls, boys, and men to have equal participation in the program and to realize benefits for their own wellbeing. A participatory beneficiary identification and selection including agreeing on affirmative action to register at least 70% female beneficiaries. Community-led project feedback and complaint mechanism to established to foster equal chances for female and male to voice their complaints and feedback. Complementary programming will be promoted through vigorous coordination with health and nutrition, protection.
Overall, the IRC will reach 23% of the most vulnerable households’ classified in IPC Phase 5 and 4 in Panyijiar (22,080), Mayendit (29,353) and Aweil South (45,033). This is equivalent to a total of 96,367 (36,936 Women, 23,917Men, 19,258Girls, 16,255Boys) beneficiaries. By program type: 96,367 through input trade fairs, 48,183 through production skills training (50%), 12,833through small ruminants and poultry restocking and livestock vaccinations, 35,350through fisheries.

1 - Needs

South Sudan is experiencing severe food and nutrition insecurity, recurring inter-communal conflicts, flooding, and stresses from the global COVID-19 pandemic. Some 8.3 million people are estimated to need humanitarian assistance in 2021 (HNO, 2021). Food and nutrition insecurity is estimated to worsen by March 2021, with over 1.79 million people are likely to face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity; with an estimated 11,000 people in catastrophe. While the food insecurity is likely to increase in the 2021 lean season, 7.24 million people (60% of the population) are likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, 2.47 million to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4), and some 31,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). During the April to July 2021 lean period, an estimated 201,000 people are likely to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, and 7,000 people are likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) including in Aweil South county. While in Unity state, an estimated 205,000 people are likely to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity in Mayendit and Panyijiar counties (December 2020 IPC Report) . The 2020 seasonal flooding has exacerbated needs, OCHA estimated 1,042,000 people in eight out of ten states were affected by floods between July and December 202; of which some 480,000 were displaced. Among the worst affected in Unity (126,000) is Mayendit and Panyijiar with over 83,000 people displaced while in NBeG some 15,000 were affected (OCHA Flooding Update- December 2020). Rising food insecurity is attributed to poor seasonal harvests of 2021 due to flooding, the impact of COVID-19 on livelihoods and incomes, economic crisis and currency devaluation eroding purchasing power, decreased livestock productivity and milk production. Protracted ethnic conflicts have caused displacements and limiting access to crop farms and subsequent harvests, productive assets such as livestock assets and other livelihoods have been depleted. Slightly stable counties like Aweil South and those in mild violence like Panyijiar and Mayendit have remained extremely vulnerable to the worsening economic crisis and disruption of normal trade including livestock and influx of IDPs. Hyper-inflation (now at 76%) driving high food prices and cost of living - a situation contributing to erratic and cross-border movements in search of income and food. The severely affected population are the poorest and vulnerable displaced persons without kinship support including elderly, disabled, children under five years, widows, and female-headed households. Across these locations, youth remain a concern and are coerced into crime and inter/intra fighting, lack skills ad incentives, face limited employment opportunities and are most cases excluded from a stake in the country’s future. Notably, the recent peace process has improved access to more vulnerable persons in hard-to-reach areas with climatic shocks exacerbating situations in form of flooding. These factors have rendered vulnerable households and communities of Aweil South, Mayendit, and Panyijiar in dire need of emergency food security and livelihood support. The transitional government offers cautious hope for stability, inter-communal conflict, and displacement in and from neighboring states and counties, and returnees from bordering countries such as Sudan have created more demand for assistance and livelihoods. Pockets of conflicts are still anticipated from dissatisfied factions vying for political positions, and persistent localized Intra and inter-communal level conflict. This will further disrupt livelihoods and propel poor economic conditions and food insecurity. The current volatile economy will further contract with 82% population's officially poor. An influx of returnees may lead to increased numbers of vulnerable persons facing multiple shocks (climate, pests, public health, and risk of COVID-19).

2 - Project activities

Building on IRCs program integration experience with multisector responses in Unity and NBeG, The IRC proposes the following activities to be implemented during the seasonal calendar of 2021, across Aweil South, Panyijiar, and Mayendit counties.a) Form and train Project Management Committee (PMC)/AAP: The IRC will establish and train Project Management Committee (PMC)/APP on roles and responsibilities, using guidelines from OCHA and WFP’s/FA. The PCM/APP will ensure gender inclusivity and consist of beneficiary representatives, co-opt local leaders and authority including County Agriculture and Livestock Directors, Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC). PMC will validate beneficiary selection criteria and process ensuring inclusion of vulnerable groups and conduct routine monitoring of project activities. b) Identification, Selection, and registration of beneficiaries: Using standard participatory identification and registration processes, vulnerable households will be registered for ITFs activities. PMCs will be given capacity-building training on complaints handling, do no harm to ensure participatory monitoring by committees.c) Provide agricultural inputs (tools; fishing, vegetable & crop kits): The IRC will facilitate agricultural input trade fairs (ITFs) to ensure beneficiaries access seeds, tools, and restocking support and including pipelines from FAO such as fishing gear. Local seed dealers will be identified for Input Trade Fairs, payment to seed dealers, and post-distribution monitoring exercises conducted. Throughout, IRC will engage community health volunteers to disseminate COVID-19 messages, provide personal protective kits including facemasks, and limit crowd at distribution points following COVID-19 protocols.d) Support livestock mass vaccinations, treatment, and surveillance through CAHWs: To contribute to livestock disease surveillance and monitoring and facilitate vaccination campaigns. The IRC will coordinate with Ministry of Livestock and Fisheries (MLF), partners, and FAO to support the vaccination and treatment services in Mayendit, Panyijiar, and Aweil South, and farmers will be mobilized pastoralists herds to a central vaccination point and receive services during campaigns.e) Provide agricultural extension services to enhance food production: Through County Agriculture Department (CAD), IRC will identify and train community-based extension agents (Community Animal Health Workers-CAHW and Agricultural Extension Agents-AEA), who in turn train target beneficiaries and continue to provide extensive support. Demonstrations to farmers led by AEA/CAHWs and staff on climate-smart agriculture practices including manure management, pest management, flood- and drought-tolerant seeds. IRC will promote biorational pest and disease control methods to mitigate the impact of pests and diseases on crops that are environmentally friendly for a balanced ecosystem.f) Monitoring, Evaluation, and Learning: IRC technical staff will directly implement and monitor the project activities at field. Beneficiaries and local authorities will be engaged in participatory monitoring and evaluation of the project with bias on Gender Inclusive Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms (GICFM) approach. Payam PMC/AAPs review meetings and routine joint field monitoring exercises will be organized by the project manager. IRC will conduct periodic data collection and tracking through standard tools, Post distribution Monitoring (PDM), project baselines evaluations and end-line evaluations. The IRC will engage stakeholders and beneficiaries and AEA’s/CAHW to document project lessons learned for replication.IRC will integrate with Nutrition and WASH sectors including beneficiary selection through referrals, implementation, reporting, and follow-up of beneficiaries for support- e.g households with children and mothers under treatment for malnutrition -referred from the Nutrition projects or protection cluster within the counties.

3 - Project indicators

The IRCs' principal goal is to enhance and sustain emergency food production through the complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing, and livestock support in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19. To determine the success of the project towards its set goal above, the IRC will setup an M&E system that systematically tracks sets of prioritized indicators enlisted below. Upon establishing a baseline, realistic targets will set and measured during and at end of the project to determine success. The IRC has prioritized the following indicators subject to review during actual implementation. • Number of HHs (female, child & male-headed) receiving agricultural inputs/ vegetable & fishing kits• Number of HHs receiving cash/ vouchers (ITF/ nutrition) – SADD• Quantity & variety of seeds distributed.• Quantity & variety of crops produced (outcome)• Number of animals vaccinated & treated.• Number of CAHWs trained/ supported through cost recovery.• Number FDP with functioning CRM/ CFM;• Beneficiary satisfaction with information, participation, and CFM; • % women receiving livelihood kits (crop/ veg/ fish);The IRC will facilitate review and lesson learning meetings to be held with project staff and clients to document best practices for replication.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Project staff and volunteers including CAHWs/AEA and community beneficiary selection committees will be sensitized on the do nor harm principles and prevention of PSEA. By working with community leaders, community representatives, and elders, IRC undertakes through sensitization and awareness-raising of stakeholders on the rights and responsibilities of all project parties. Beneficiaries are made aware of their entitlements, freedom of choice, and how support is provided. Stakeholders and project staff are sensitized to exercise and abide by the core humanitarian principles of impartiality, neutrality, humanity, and independence while providing and accessing support.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

During project baselines, IRC will conduct focus group discussions with men, women, and youth to ascertain their unique safety needs. IRC institutes measures to mitigate and address safety gaps that exist such as the provision of shelters for both male and females at input distribution points, setting up temporary shades for mothers and children to rest and breastfeed during training sessions, and provision of sanitary materials for women and girls through integration with the protection program. Beneficiaries (men and women) are also consulted to decide on the suitable time and locations for project distribution exercises and activities. This allows for women to be involved in activities and distributions while having ample time to attend to their household chores. A relative measure of beneficiary safety is conducted through tracking numbers of all beneficiary gender, complaints addressed through the feedback mechanism, and the number of safety cases recorded and addressed under the project.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

IRC Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms (CFM) will embed an inclusive Gender-Sensitive Feedback and Complaint Committees, and through Payam level Project Management Committees (PMCs) doubling as AAPs. Gender-Sensitive Complaint desks and boxes will be provided during distribution and at demonstration plots. Monthly project joint monitoring exercises and review meetings with feedback committees will be held to assess the progress of project activities and course correct.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The Complaint and feedback mechanism committee will be established together with the project management committee to be responsible for receiving and addressing all complaints and feedback on the project. The IRC will conduct post-distribution monitoring exercises to determine the level of beneficiary satisfaction and utilization of inputs, findings from the PDM will inform improvement in subsequent distribution.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

8.1 Mitigation Measures The project will ensure the safety and protection of beneficiaries during registration/distribution. The project team and community stakeholders (CFM committee, PMC, Payam chiefs etc) will map out safe locations within communities where the registration/distribution will take place. In locations that are not fenced/protected, local ropes will be used to demarcate the registration/distribution points to ensure the safety and protection against any physical violence includes beating, kicking, punching, or the use of objects or weapons. The CFM and PCM committees will be engaged in crowd control during registration/distribution. 8.2 Mitigation MeasuresIRC will ensure distribution points are close to beneficiaries’ homes to mitigate exposing beneficiaries especially women and girls to sexual violence including rape. The CFM committee will sensitize beneficiaries especially women and girls before and after distribution to report or submit their complaints using the suggestion box that will be placed at distribution points. 8.3 Mitigation MeasuresThe IRC has an existing child safeguarding policy, the project will adopt the policy and ensure all protocols are followed. The IRC will train AEA’s /CAHW’s and CFM committees who will be directly supporting the implementation to ensure adherence to child safeguarding and PSEA 8.4 Mitigation MeasuresThe IRC will exercise program integration with the protection sector to sensitize and provide protection assistance to survivors and ensure referral pathways including during beneficiary selection through referrals, data triangulation and sharing, reporting and follow-up of beneficiaries receiving integrated support.8.5 Mitigation MeasuresThe project will be inclusive in targeting the most vulnerable groups. During project lunch, IRC will ensure the representation of marginalized groups in reviewing the selection criteria. At distribution points, IRC will designate lines and provide chairs especially for persons with disabilities. 8.6 Mitigation MeasuresThe IRC will conduct FGD’s to increase access to beneficiaries who may not have access to the complaint feedback mechanism committee. The sessions will provide a feedback platform for beneficiaries to complain or provide feedback on IRC services. 8.7 Mitigation MeasuresIRC will use FAO mobile application or CommCare/Kobo for registration. The beneficiary’s data will be stored and saved electronically in a centralized database by the IRC M&E unit. IRC will ensure non-disclosure and protection of beneficiary’s personal data.8.8 Mitigation MeasuresDuring the project lunch, the IRC will be engaged, Commissioners, County Agricultural Inspectors, Paramount chiefs, Executive Directors and Payam Administrators, Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC), the ministry of agriculture, women and youth representatives. To explain the project target, services, and expectations from community leaders and beneficiaries. Also, the beneficiary’s selection criteria will be shared and agreed upon registration this will mitigate the risk of impartiality or inequality in beneficiary's selection. 8.9 Mitigation MeasuresAll land to be used in the project for demonstration plots to enhance farmers' knowledge on production will be jointly identified and agreed by the community leaders, County Agriculture Department, and beneficiaries of the project. The IRC will sign a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) with the landowners/community leaders to use the land within the project duration. 8.10 Mitigation MeasuresThe IRC Safety and Access Unit together with community stakeholders will carry out assessment and map out project locations with potential explosive and hazard. Analyzes will be carried out and advisory provided for safe locations to be used as demonstration plots and registration/distribution points. Also, beneficiaries will be sensitized on explosive hazard following the outcome of the assessment to ensure their safety.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

During beneficiary registration, the project will use referral pathways to collaborate with the IRC protection unit and other partners to identify vulnerable GBV survivors who include women, girls, men, and boys to access livelihood support.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

The IRC will reach 23% (96,267 households) to decrease the proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5 in Panyijiar (22,080), Mayendit (29,253), and Aweil South (45,033). The vulnerable food insecure in the three counties will have improved access to productive inputs (Stable & vegetable seeds, tools, and fishing kits), skills, and knowledge on sustainable agriculture - this will transform into an applied enhanced capacity to increase acreage, production, and productivity for sufficient and diversified food for both home and markets. Vulnerable households will thereby become more resilient and food secure. Consumption of nutrient-rich foods will contribute to the reduction of nutrient-deficit-related malnutrition cases, leading to a healthier community.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

The project will ensure the safety and protection of beneficiaries by holding sensitization meetings with beneficiaries (Male and female) before, during, and after agriculture inputs distribution to reduce all forms of abuse and intimate partner violence. The project will facilitate linkages with the IRC Nutrition unit to provide group and individual counseling on optimal IYCF practice focused on complementary feeding, also during the counseling sessions, WASH key messages mainly hygiene and sanitation promotion will be incorporated. To ensure environmental sustainability, climate-smart farming best practices will be provided to households on crop production and animal husbandry.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The project will focus on increasing the production of food rich in micronutrients and proteins for a diversified diet. Production materials for vegetable crops and fish kits will be provided to households with acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4. The use of nutritious foods will be enhanced through the engagement of community women groups (MtMSG) through linkages with the IRC Nutrition unit and other partners to disseminate information including the use of IEC materials on best nutrition habits at home. Women groups will deliver cooking demonstrations and testing of nutritious food diets to encourage adoption and local consumption of foods produced.
