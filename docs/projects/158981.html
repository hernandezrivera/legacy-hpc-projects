Project Summary

Tamer will work closely with two partners covering both North of Gaza and South area of Gaza strip (Jabalia for rehabilitation and National society for rehabilitation) to contribute in reducing the number of children with disability who are exposed to the danger of drop out the schools due to the inability of their families to afford the other basic needs as one of the reasons. in addition to the lack of the rehabilitation society to continue their regular services.
Based on Tamer’s thirty years of experience in employing children literature tools and techniques in education through focusing on the storytelling, theater, music and drama as tools to increase their engagement and belonging to the education process along with the value and ability of these tools to alleviating children stress and fears and increasing their self esteem.
Tamer has developed and adapted the above mentioned tools to implement remedial classes for the children focusing on literacy boost and numeracy boost, Tamer will also use its publications including the caregivers manuals that has been developed previously in partnership with UNICEF to get the parents and the community involved in the intervention and to give them the needed skills to deal with their children who are in need to PSS or need to be referred, considering the holistic intervention of Tamer in its different projects; Tamer has been providing case management service to the vulnerable children suffering from child protection concerns including those who dropped out from schools due to different reasons whether because of the acute economic situation or the vulnerability of their parents or for being injured in the great march of return which illustrates almost 20% of the children. Tamer will expand its intervention in protection to reach affected CWDs through its family centers and referral pathways in particularly CWD’s who dropped out from Jablia for rehabilitation and NSR societies.

1 - Needs

The decades-long occupation of Palestine and resultant conflict and blockade exacerbated since Palestine came under the political division in 2007 which impacted on all aspects of political, economic, environmental and social life, causing unending hardships and suffering for children and families in Gaza.Especially since the beginning of the GMR demonstrations and along with implications of political division which exhausted children and families’ resilience capacities as well as overwhelming already overburdened institutional and non-institutional service providers preventing them from meeting the increasing needs of children and families on different aspects of life in which resulted negatively on developing coping mechanisms particularly for children with disabilities including GMR injured children. (HNO education report). Another significant factor, the parents of the students who negatively affect their children’s wellbeing, due to difficult financial situation that puts them under pressure for not being able to fulfill their children’s educational needs, According to findings of Education in Emergencies Rapid Assessment by NRC, in 2018 nearly half of the interviewed children suffer from low wellbeing and 12% of them showed no interest in pursuing higher education. 52% of the students reported facing frequent nightmares, while the remaining had troubles in sleeping. Deep poverty and the need to earn an income or external shocks such as health crises or deaths in family constitute the primary reason for their dropout. According to OOSCI report of UNICEF, a very few children of primary school age are excluded from education, but nearly 5% of 10-15-year-old children and 1out of 3children 6-9-year-olds with disabilities are out of school, about 1 out of 3 children with disabilities and about 1 out of 2 children with multiple disabilities do not attend school.In October 2019, Tamer conducted 2 FGDs with injured children, their caregivers and another one with following rehabilitation societies (Jabalia for Rehabilitation Society, National Society for Rehabilitation NSR, the Atfaluna for Deaf Society and Palestine Avenir for childhood foundation PACF) to understand the roots and impact of children’s drop out, particularly the CWD’s, as a result, FGDs indicated that humanitarian – economic situation has severely affected PWDs institutions’ ability to meet the increasing needs of their beneficiaries, as all societies confirmed that two reasons stand by beyond CWDs access to education, the first; indicated by shortage of fund in which limited societies’ ability to provide the service for many of their targeted children whether in affording teachers’ salaries or covering operational expenses and the second; was in the vulnerability of children’s caregivers to afford the tuition and transportation fees which threaten to provide the service with the high quality; at least 40 - 50 % of the children dropped out or at risk to drop out from those societies’ rehabilitation schools. According to the Atfaluna Society for Deaf children, 120 out of 300 children are no longer enrolled in their school due to the fund’s shortage. As well in Jabalia for Rehabilitation society at 50 children are in danger and at risk to drop out as their families cannot afford to cover their transportation expenses.According to the FGD’s findings and based on the actual necessity of supporting the local institutions to continue their services to the community, Tamer will work closely with two partners covering both North of Gaza and South area of Gaza strip (Jabalia for rehabilitation and National society for rehabilitation).

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Tamer considers the safety and dignity of its targeted groups as one of its top priorities through designing, implementation of its activities to ensure avoiding unintended harm to its targeted groups, through the following: - Tamer work with experienced staff to implement its activities and provide child protection services to maximize the benefits and avoid negative impacts on children. - Tamer considers children's needs during the designing process for any project by implementing FGDs to ensure a better understanding of their needs and to engage them within the designing stage. - Tamer does mapping for possible partner CBOs and sets selection criteria that ensure different measures; safety, accessibility and equal participation for girls and boys before choosing its partners. - Tamer considers using proper monitoring and evaluation tools to ensure sufficient implementation that meets children's needs.-Tamer orients its staff and volunteers on child protection and child safeguarding to avoid harm towards children. - when planning for capacity building programs, tamer considers equal gender participation, also engage experienced staff members in designing, for example when designing an expressive arts activity to pass child protection Tamer's counsellors review the plans to avoid unintended harm to children. - Tamer does not discriminate its children when referred to receiving child protection services. - Tamer drafted up safeguarding policy and feedback and complaint mechanism to ensure the safety of children and implementing its activities and providing its child protection services properly.
