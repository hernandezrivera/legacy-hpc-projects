Project Summary

The main objective of this project is to Provide Quality Life-Saving education to conflict affected children and youth in Upper Nile State, to increase access to inclusive and protective lifesaving education both formal and non-formal to 2,985 ( 1,620 girls and 1,365 boys) children affected by emergencies through Rehabilitation of existing learning spaces and/or construction of TLSs where there is influx of new IDPs , Rehabilitation and/or construction of school fences, roofs, doors and/or windows, locks, shelter and salary for guards, to improve the security and safety of students, Establishment or rehabilitation of wash facilities, including latrines (gender segregated), water points and provision of soap for handwashing, provision school meals in areas most affected by food insecurity Early childhood development activities targeting mother groups and pre-school children combining nutritional supplementation and socio-emotional stimulation Construction of school stores and basic kitchens for meal preparation.
• The project is in line with the Cluster objective 1,2,3; To contribute to saving lives by ensuring access to safe learning spaces where children can benefit from key life-saving and life-sustaining activities such as psycho social support, water, sanitation and hygiene education and school meals Specifically, SSUDA intends to put emphasis on timely lifesaving education services that will include rehabilitation of emergency Rehabilitation and/or construction of school fences, roofs, doors and/or windows locks, shelter and salary for guards, to improve the security and safety of students
• . The project will target two counties in Upper Nile, (Panyikang and Manyo) in Upper Nile where SSUDA is currently present and will strengthen and complement ongoing projects including Child Protection, Education, C4D, NFI-ES, FAO Seed and tools distribution, EU – funded PRO-ACT. The project that will cost USD. 200,000 will be implemented by well-coordinated and skilled staff. SSUDA has well-established coordination structure with well-equipped office in Malakal and other locations in Upper Nile. SSUDA is an active member of WASH Nutrition, health, food security/Livelihood and protection Clusters and will continue to maintain close working relationship with all of them at state levels to maintain participation and coordination of Education programming.

Needs

In Upper Nile where SSUDA is present and relative calm is witnessed, Displaced residents continue to return with very limited infrastructure for children to attend schooling. The capacity of National and State Governments remain very low to support education system in paying of teacher’s incentives, providing school supplies and infrastructural development. According to (Inter-Cluster Response mission report, 17th March to 27th March 2018 and SSUDA's internal assessment) indicates that, there are over 20,000 (11,900 boys and 8,100 girls) children of school going age in Panyikang and Manyo who are not attending school. The report indicates that the second wave of fighting in 2017 and 2016 ensured all schools in the two counties in Upper Nile are closed. (Inter-agency assessment report 30th to 31st January 2018). The younger boys of school going age are most likely to be lured into armed groups, while girls forced into early marriage if schools will remain closed. SSUDA aims to roll out back to school program with an objective of targeting 2,985 children (1,620girls and 1,365 boys) , building teachers capacity, establishing learning structures and distributing of school supplies in the two counties (Panyikang and Manyo). The large number of people returning to their original homes are women and girls and are likely to be exposed to sexual gender-based violence. A visit to most areas of Panyikang, Manyo and parts of Malakal, reveals that, existing schools are completely in a dilapidated state which SSUDA aims to renovate to the learning standards. SSUDA supports, several humanitarian response projects and displacement has remained a key characteristic with heavy impact on education. Majority of schools in the region were destroyed, damaged and looted or are occupied by armed groups/forces or IDPs. Reports from SSUDA field team that conducted general humanitarian assessment in Manyo on 10th October 2018, shows that no single school is functional in Panyikang and Manyo Counties of Upper Nile State. Since 2015, the two counties, remained relatively calm and hosted thousands of IDPs who fled from parts of Lire in Northern Sudan and Ethiopia. The two Counties has experience on and off violent conflicts causing multiple displacement in the Western Bank of the River. An education facility plays a critical role as a protected space and it has become difficult for us implementing child protection without any referral to a school. The worrying signs of the crisis on education have been manifested by the increased presence of the number of street children and children begging in social places in recent months. SSUDA believes that education is both a human right and an indispensable means of realizing other human rights as enshrined in major documents such as Transitional Constitution of South Sudan as well as UDHR, ICESSR, CRC, EFA, CEDAW, etc. In April 2017 SSUDA received funding from UNICEF to provide education in emergency for 7,000 children in Kaldak in Upper Nile. Just before the project kicked off, violent conflict broke out in the area scattering and displacing children further to Aburoc and other areas including Manyo. SSUDA went ahead and intervened in Aburoc and later Malakal town. However, the needs in Manyo and Panyikang counties require more support and attention. SSUDA has over the last 7 years partnered with UNICEF and SSHF (formerly CHF) to provide education services to emergency affected children and youth in Upper Nile.

Activities

The main purpose of this project is provide quality lifesaving education to conflict affected children, adolescents and youth in Upper Nile State.The project will implement the following key activities: - Output 1: Crisis affected girls and boys (3-18 years) have access to safe, healing and inclusive learning spaces:• Conduct community mobilization and awareness sessions to increase participation of community members in the provision of education in target area include establishment of school structures. • Establish new safe, healing and inclusive learning spaces for boys and girls using locally available materials and provide desks and blackboards to the learners.• Establish gender segregated latrines for boys and girls and female and male teachers with hand washing facilities;• Preposition and distribute UNICEF/Cluster provided Education supplies to children and youth • Conduct teachers and volunteers’ recruitment and provide incentives.• Rehabilitate damaged or occupied education facilities and reopened them for use by children and youth. • Deliver WFP Food-For-Education (School feeding Food Programme) in target schools that meets the criteria and Construction of school stores and basic kitchens for meal preparation. Output 2: Capacity of teachers /volunteers and education stakeholders enhanced to provide quality education and improve school management and sustainability. • Conduct training for teachers, PTA/SMC and volunteers (formal and volunteer) in the concepts and delivery of EiE, PTA tool kits and code of conduct, basic pedagogy and learner-centered methodologies. • Mobilize and conduct capacity building training for PTA/SMC members on the PTA tool kits and code of conduct;• Deliver training for teachers and PTAs on PSS, Life Skills, GBV, and MRE• Organize training of teachers in the following components, ECD , ALP and Pedagogy • Mobilize stakeholders to participate in the Monthly Education sub-cluster meetings convened at the county level Output 3: Resilience and well-being of the child and youth is strengthened to cope with the conflict situation • Mobilize PTAs and SMCs to support the establishment of recreational facilities (include football pitches) for girls and boys receive and use recreational materials • Organize training session for education personnel, service providers and selected community members on child rights and referral pathways on protection/GBV, and health;• Conduct awareness sessions in schools/TLS on peace education, children and teachers, targeting schools and learning spaces • Organize Inter-class/school competitions conducted using; poems, dramas and debates• Organize Reproductive Health, environmental and Hygiene promotion and lifesaving awareness sessions in schools using trained teachers and volunteersSupport inter-community life-skills activities (out of school youth) competitions through football, netball and cultural activities.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes, The project has identified and clearly suggest solution to the barriers that different population could face in relations to project. One of the main barrier is the appropriateness of the latrines to be constructed for both men and women. the project puts in place mechanism to address this issue by ensuring women and girls are consulted and involved in the selection of locations/sites as well as design of the latrines. The project will also ensure that women are provided with hygiene kits and that latrines will be equipped with handwashing facilities.
