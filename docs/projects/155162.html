Project Summary

Secured space is one of the crucial needs for the most vulnerable population in terms of protection including women,adolescent girls and children. Lack of meaningful access to services and infrastructures affects children and vulnerable women and girls. Girls and children who do not live with their immediate family are particularly vulnerable to abuse, violence, sexual exploitation and neglect. Not only are these women and girls dealing with the emotional distress of being separated from their closest caregivers, but in this vulnerable position abuse and exploitation.
One of the most pressing issues is psychosocial distress, as girls and boys have witnessed horrific violence in Myanmar, including seeing family members killed and their homes burnt down. Women and girls explain suffering from stress and feelings insecurity. Overcrowding and unaddressed psychosocial distress can in turn lead to negative coping mechanisms, increased vulnerability to physical and sexual abuse and isolation.
Adolescent girls also face the risk of early or forced marriage, particularly as families are engaging these negative coping strategies in order to access additional food rations.
Safe spaces including psychosocial support activities will help to mitigate the risks and provide a positive environment to women and girls. Also community based awareness activities within the catchment area in regard of GBV concept,available services and benefits of the services for the survivor will be helpful to mitigate the risk of GBV.
ACF will improve the women and girls’ wellbeing by continuing the running of existing safe spaces to promote their sense of security as well as provide a place which they can own including psychosocial and case management support.
ACF will improve the awareness of the community for improving the GBV situation including GBV concept,available services and benefits of seeking service who are survivors of GBV. Staff will be trained on survivor centered approach according to IASC GBV Guideline including GBV Guiding Principles .Regular representation,active participation and timely reporting will be maintained in the GBV SS. To prevent harm,Staff are following ACF's own code of conduct and active on feedback and complaint mechanism including SEA.Furthermore ACF will ensure protection of data according to WHO recommendations. ACF will continue to refer the beneficieries within ACF's internal services as well as other stakeholders based on Beneficiarie's need maintaining referral slips for the betterment of the beneficiary. In addision,ACF is piloting a project to mainstream GBV into different sectors like nutrition,wash and food securitry where GBV referral pathway will also be established.Volunteers from refugee community will also be trained on basic skills on responding to a GBV disclosure and minimum referral information. Dignity Kit materials will be stored for emergency preparedness. ACF will train the Staff on emergency preparedness response plan where GBV issues will be considered.The organization identifies the GBV survivor through observation in nutrition centers by observing sign of vulnerabily such asa bruses,sadness etc. Trained worker gently approaches and encourages to share any tensions by ensuring confidenciality. ACF's community mobilization activities of wash and nutrition services also include discussion about violences and share information about possible support services available within ACF.Adolescents are also targeted for psychosocial wellbeing session where anger management and GBV both are included.We will be awaring the community leaders on harmful consequence of GBV and share referral service information.ACF maintains communication with CICs,site management team and other GBV service providers.ACF will provide service to any beneficiary irrespectively without discrimination.
