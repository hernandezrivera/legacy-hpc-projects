Project Summary

The project provides for the protection and safety of vulnerable families who have poor access to adequate shelter. It includes supporting 145 families living in substandard shelters through rehabilitation and upgrade activities to meet shelter basic needs and minimum standards, focusing on meeting the needs of people with disabilities. The project targets 145 families of people with disabilities who have serious protection concerns due to shelter conditions. This project aligns with the HRP 2023 objective 2 “Vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns are supported to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacities”. The main activity is to provide cash grants for shelter rehabilitation to address shelter-related vulnerabilities. A cash-based self-help approach for shelter rehabilitation will be implemented according to families' needs. This will maximize the impact on beneficiaries while fostering ownership and resilience of the targeted community. The project complements ongoing activities of MA’AN that support addressing the protection concerns of people with disabilities. The identification of the targeted families will be coordinated with the shelter cluster and the Ministry of social development. MA’AN capitalizes on its community-based emergency response structure to support the implementation process to ensure accountability to the affected population.

1 - Needs

n the last two years, MA’AN has been working with the Humanity and Inclusion organization to address the needs of people with disabilities. A total of 989 have been reached to address their immediate basic needs and to enhance their access to protection services. Out of this pool of beneficiaries, in 2022, a need assessment for 205 families who are living in the Middle Area and Khan Younis was conducted to address their socioeconomic vulnerabilities and protection concerns. The surveyed families included 494 men, 471 women, 335 boys, and 381 girls. Teams of social workers, including 8 women, were responsible for the data collection. GBV and inclusion specialist was consulted throughout the planning, design, analysis, and interpretation of the assessment. In addition, a team of civil engineers conducted field visits to a random sample of 50 families to observe the shelter conditions against the shelter minimum standards. Among the assessed families, 100% are abject poor. The assessed population included 13.2% women-headed households, 16.6% elderly-headed households, 18.3% people with disabilities, and 2.3% pregnant/lactating women. 27.8% of the surveyed families have no access to any source of income and are totally dependent on aid. 100.0% of the surveyed families reported that in the last six months they were forced to have detrimental coping strategies due to the lack of access to resources to meet their basic needs. 82% of the PWDs reported that their shelters are not adequate to address their special needs. The onsite observations of the technical team revealed significant shelter vulnerabilities, in reference to the shelter minimum standards, with poor access to adequate and safe WASH services. As reported by the surveyed women, the poor living conditions have reflections on the social relations and affects adversely the psychological well-being of women, and increase the risks women face in the form of violence. The surveyed families reported the shelter upgrade as one of their main priority needs. The 205 HHs were ranked based on their vulnerability and technical scores for the purpose of prioritization. In September 2022, MA’AN received fund from OCHA to support only 60 families out of the assessed 205 through rehabilitation and upgrade activities to meet shelter basic needs and minimum standards. Significant gaps are still in place for the remaining 145 families. For the proposed action, MA’AN will provide for the protection and safety for the 145 families in the waiting list through rehabilitation and upgrade activities to meet shelter basic needs with a particular focus on meeting the needs of people with disabilities. A comprehensive technical assessment will be conducted to update the needs of the 145 families and to address any new emerging needs since the regional need assessment.

2 - Activities

The project addresses the protection concerns of 145 vulnerable families (1189 individuals including 349 men, 333 women, 269 girls, and 238 boys; 218 are PWDs) who are living in poor shelter conditions that impose grave risks on people’s safety and health, particularly PWDs. It focuses on improving equitable and adequate access of vulnerable families to adequate shelter in accordance with protection principles and the shelter minimum standards. Families of people with disabilities will be supported with restricted cash for shelter rehabilitation (an average amount of 2200 USD per family) to meet the minimum standards in Gaza while addressing the special needs of PWDs. This action complements MA’AN ongoing activities working with the Humanity and Inclusion organization supporting the people with disabilities to meet their basic needs and promote their wellbeing. A self-help approach will be applied to ensure efficiency and responsiveness. This includes training, coaching, and provision of cash assistance for self-repairs. MA'AN capitalizes on its extensive experience applying the proposed modality. The main activities are as the following: - Update the needs of the 145 families through conducting assessment addressing the shelter vulnerabilities and the related protection concerns. The assessment includes home visits that will be conducted by a team of civil engineers who are trained on the protection principles and the shelter minimum standards. A structured questionnaire will be used for data collection- Share the final list of beneficiaries with the shelter cluster for cross-checking - Sign MoUs with the target beneficiaries. This includes providing beneficiaries with detailed information about their rights, the working methodology, and the applied complaint mechanism. According to the self-help approach, a team of qualified engineers will conduct the first visit which includes signing the MoU, orienting the family about the implementation methodology, and providing technical guidance on how to manage the repair activities.- Activate the project complaint system. The complaint mechanism involves a direct communication flow among beneficiaries, emergency community-based structures, and MA’AN. Specific focus will be on providing appropriate opportunities to amplify the voices of groups with protection needs not only to articulate their own needs but to give them space to contribute to project decision-making and evaluation.- Conduct shelter upgrading by applying a self-help approach. This includes training, coaching, and provision of cash assistance for self-repairs. A team of qualified civil engineers should conduct three visits to each family to provide technical training and coaching and to monitor the work progress according to the endorsed maintenance plan. The disbursement of the cash transfers will be conducted according to the work progress that will be informed by the visits. Three installments will be disbursed to the targeted families based on progress tracking and validation.- Conduct cash assistance post-distribution monitoring after each installment in order to ensure well utilization of the cash to address the shelter-related vulnerabilities. Three field monitors will be appointed to conduct random field visits to the families who received the cash installments. The validation of the work progress and the entitlement of the cash installments will be according to the field monitoring records.- Print out communication material on GBV, Child Protection, and PSEA to be distributed to the targeted 145families. These materials had been developed by MA'AN to enhance the families' knowledge about the protection threats and the available protection services (GBV, and Child Protection).- Conduct awareness-raising sessions on GBV, Child Protection, and PSEA. At least one woman from each family should participate in one session. Five sessions should be implemented targeting 62 women of the beneficiary families.

3 - Indicators

The project implementation will be supported by a comprehensive monitoring system to gather all relevant data to steer the action, maximize its impact, measure its effects, anticipate and mitigate any challenge. MA’AN, monitoring and evaluation unit is independent from the Program Department and will be in charge of the monitoring and evaluation of the project. A monitoring and evaluation mechanism will be established to follow up on the progress of the project activities and to ensure the match between the identified indicators and the expected outcomes of the project.The M&E plan will identify tasks, quality of outputs, means of verification, and persons in charge with respect to the project timeframe and budget. It will also include full coordination and involvement of relevant stakeholders during all project phases, supported by MA’AN M&E teams and ensuring gender mainstreaming in all project’s phases and outputs. MA’AN’s M&E Officer will be responsible to monitor the project activities throughout the development of the action. Full documentation of the achievements of the project activities will be ensured according to the pre-set outputs' indicators of achievementsThe main M&E tools that will be deployed are field visits (field monitors, project field coordinator, M&E officer), baseline/endline surveys, focus group discussions, and beneficiary satisfaction survey. A cash assistance post-distribution monitoring will be undertaken after each installment. The monitoring will focus on the respect of shelter protection principles and minimum standards, as well as accountability through the already established and functioning Complaint Response Mechanism. All monitoring data will be fully disaggregated by age, sex, disability status, and governorate. Full documentation for the achievements of the project activities will be ensured according to the set outputs and indicators of achievements. Based on the aforementioned, the following indicators will be committed and monitored through the proposed action: - # of individuals protected and have improved access to adequate shelter.- # of accessible shelters adapted to persons with disabilities- # of females, elderly, people with disabilities who report improved access to safe and dignified shelter after the intervention.- # of assisted HH living in upgraded unit in accordance with minimum standards. - % of households who report satisfaction with the shelter intervention.- % of females, elderly, people with disabilities who report improved safety and dignity after the intervention- # of community members who received information and awareness raising on PSEA

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

The project will facilitate shelter repair and/or adaptation works through a self-help approach by providing conditional cash assistance accompanied with technical assistance to ensure compliance with the Shelter Cluster minimum standards. The self-help is an owner-driven approach that foster participation, provides beneficiaries with greater choice and flexibility to meet their household needs, empower them, builds their technical capacities and skills and enhance their ownership regarding the intervention. MA’AN has accumulated an intensive experience in shelter-based interventions adapting the self-help approach over the last 6 years. A methodological approach to cash assistance distribution was developed and applied in many projects. The cash will be distributed in three installments. A post-distribution monitoring will be undertaken after each installment and recommendations will be provided to the program and technical team after the provision of each installment in order to enhance an optimized value for money before the second installment.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

MA’AN has been managing an accountable, accessible, and gender-sensitive complaint system. The system offers different easily accessible means to address complaints or feedback in writing, verbally, via phone (free hotline) or SMS, directly to the responsible person from MA'AN, and verbally and directly to MA’AN offices. Complain boxes will be placed in partner CBOs close to the targeted families and communities. The system includes particular procedures to address gender-sensitive complaints with clear procedures to guarantee the responsiveness and confidentiality of the complaints handling process. The system has been managed through an online portal facilitating efficient and more accountable complaints and feedback management involving different layers of MA’AN governance structure. This project will include introducing the system targeted communities and families ensuring well-orientation about the system. The communities’ feedback on the system will be considered for further adaptation.During the project implementation, a feedback mechanism will be applied to ensure the involvement of the targeted communities in the project management and decision-making. Orientation workshops will be conducted at the beginning of the project to discuss the project activities and get further feedback from the targeted families about the implementation methodology. A set of focus group discussions will be conducted during implementation to get regular feedback from the targeted communities about the implementation process and the applied measures of accountability.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

In all its projects, MA’AN ensures respect and promotion of the rights of all targeted individuals and communities. In order to take account of affected populations, the project will provide strong consideration for the needs and interests of beneficiary communities, particularly through the design and implementation of the activities. The design of the project was informed by the affected communities through a need assessment that involved women men, and PWD. The assessment addressed the protection and safety risks to be considered while planning the project activities. At the start of the project, a comprehensive technical assessment for the selected families will be conducted taking into consideration the safety, dignity, and confidentiality of the families. The technical assessment will also consider all the gaps in place considering the shelter minimum standards which will contribute to enhancing the privacy and protection of household members compromised by such deficiencies. The technical team in MA’AN will provide coaching and guidance to the targeted beneficiaries to ensure that the shelter adaptation and rehabilitation works will be carried out safely, carefully considering the time and the safety factors.A project-specific protection and gender risk and mitigation analysis has been elaborated and will be further refined and triangulated through direct feedback from the target communities and families along the project implementation timeline. Through the proposed intervention, MA’AN will undertake regular visits to the targeted communities and families to ensure that protection concerns are addressed throughout the intervention, thus enhancing safety, security, and privacy for women, children, and PWD. The proposed implementation methodology ensures to enhance the affected families' knowledge about the protection threats and the available protection services (GBV, PSEA, and Child Protection) through awareness raising activities.A plan will be put in place to raise awareness about complaints mechanism and PSEA among the targeted families and communities. The plan will include awareness raising messages during community orientation sessions, via SMS’s, and through broachers and booklets and other awareness tools. The established community structures and the project staff will be requested to attend PSEA training in order to ensure that they are aware about PSEA and the related procedures and measures. The project includes printing out communication material illustrating the available services to be distributed to the targeted families. MA'AN will offer safe and confidential communications channels to provide for the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). PSEA messages will be placed on the main communication platforms.MA'AN team of field monitors and civil engineers will receive orientation about MA'AN policies of child protection, discrimination and harassment, gender equity, and PSEA. MA'AN program director in cooperation with the human resources team and the M&E department will ensure compliance with MA'AN policies. The field staff will receive training (utilizing MA'AN in-house expertise) on the detection and referral of children encountering protection threats and cases of GBV. MA'AN access to the child protection and GBV referral network should support effective management of the needs of the detected cases.
