Project Summary

Maan organization aims to provide food assistance to the most vulnerable IDPs in NW Syria in many components:
1-Emergency Food baskets to 2000 HH as one-off
2-Supplemntary food assistance in the form of cooked meals during Ramadan to 1000 HH i.e. 30,000 meals in total
3- Supplementary food assistance in the form of meat share to 400 HH
4- Regular food baskets to the most vulnerable to 100HH for 8 months to provide at least 1550 Kcal per person per day as per FSL guidance
Total Number of beneficiaries:
3500 HH, 17,500 individuals (2100 Men, 3,150 Women, 5,950 Boys, 6,300 Girls)
Proposed Targeted locations: Dana, Maaret Tamsrin, Salqin, Darkosh, Harim and Daret Azza sub-districts
The selection of the targeted locations are flexible and will be finalized in coordination with FSL cluster and where the gap in the ground.
The fund for this project is not secured yet

4 - Needs

Syria remains a complex humanitarian emergency characterized by over 10 years of ongoing hostilities and their long-term consequences including widespread destruction, explosive ordnance contamination and the largest number of internally displaced people in the world . In addition to protection threats, the accelerating economic deterioration and impacts of climate change have become additional key drivers of needs, compounding vulnerabilities even further. In 2022, 14.6 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 1.2 million from 2021. Syria remains one of the largest humanitarian responses in the world, with assistance delivered to 6.8 million people per month in 2021. Across Syria, households are now reverting to negative coping mechanisms more frequently than before. This includes child labor and child marriage and the sale of productive assets – all of which increase protection needs and/or reduce households’ capacity for self-sustenance in the future. Across the country food insecurity has grown – with an estimated 12.8 million severely food insecure people, Syria ranked amongst the ten most food-insecure countries globally by mid-2021. (2022 Syria HNO Draft). 5 - Implementation of activities

This project will be implemented based on the following steps:1-Need Assessment 2-Selection of the targeted camps 3-Registration and verification of the beneficiaries 4-Procurement and service contracting process 5-Coordination with all stakeholders including FSL cluster through Skyp groups and 5Ws to avoid any possible overlap6-Coordination with the camp management for distribution time and dates7- Distribution process: *Each Household will receive one food basket as one-off, total of 2000 HH *1000 HH will receive supplementary cooked meals during the holy month of Ramadan (30 days) i.e. total 30,000 cooked meals *Distribute supplementary meal shares for 400 HH *Distribute 100 food basket for 100 HH on monthly basis for 8 months i.e. total 800 food baskets. 8-Monitoring and Evaluation across all stages

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Sexual Exploitation and Abuse Risks: 1- Overcrowding in the distribution sites 2-Mixed distribution lines between males and females3-No CRM mechanism in place 4-No distribution monitoring in place which might cause discrimination or abuse.5-No qualified staff on the humanitarian principlesMitigation Measure:1-Allocate specific time for some beneficiaries to avoid overcrowding 2-Dedicate one distribution staff to organize the distribution process3-Separate distribution lines for males and for females4-Dissemtinate Complaints response mechanism information including complaints number to be accessible to all beneficiaries and take action immediately especially for the sensitive complaints related to SEA and refer them directly to the specialized NGO in the area.5-Enough monitoring staff during the distribution process6-Conduct special training about the humanitarian principles to all projects staff in the beginning of the project


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks:1-Low quality of purchased items2-Increase prices of items due to inflation3-Shortage of required items such as sunflower oil4-Delivery delay of the purchased items from vendors 5-Delay in the fund dispersal from the private donors Mitigation measures:1- All items will be aligned with the FSL cluster guidance in terms of quantity, quality and packaging, and this will be highlighted in the conditions book during the tender process and the technical analysis will be given accordingly.2-Where possible, all items will be purchase and contracts will be signed with USD to avoid any price increase. 3-Maan may use local procurement in Syria or regional procurement in turkey to avoid shortage of items.4-Maan will allocate enough time to the items delivery, so the procurement will be done before the response time to ensure timely response.5-Maan may use the internal fund until the committed fund arrives from the donors to avoid any delay in the winter response
