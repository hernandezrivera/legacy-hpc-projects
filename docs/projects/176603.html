Project Summary

The UNHCR ES/NFI project for 2021 is focused on responding to the humanitarian needs of internally displaced persons (IDPs) and ensure humanitarian standards are maintained. IDPs continue to be exposed dire living conditions with limited livelihood options, the lack of access to markets and often more than one displacement. The need for provision of basic and domestic items remains a priority. Families are often displaced without substantial household items, fleeing with little more than they can carry. Inadequate shelter may also lead to increased protection risks and susceptibility to diseases. In addition, populations in situations of protracted displacement require replenishment of assistance to ensure basic humanitarian needs are met. With COVID-19 preventive measures mainstreamed, considerations for decongestion of sites and number of persons per shelter shall be taken into account in consultation with the CCCM cluster and other stakeholders. Emergency stocks need to be replenished and prepositioned on a regular basis in order to allow rapid response to the identified protection and assistance needs. UNHCR’s material assistance will continue to be targeted and protection focused, to ensure that the most vulnerable displaced households are not left without basic material support, including the specific needs of women and girls are catered for. In 2021, UNHCR will continue to identify the most vulnerable IDPs (up to 30% of the priority target) through protection monitoring, community-based mechanisms, and outreach efforts, to implement targeted interventions aimed at reducing the burden on communities. UNHCR both directly and through partners, will leverage their expanded operational coverage to ensure protection monitoring and participation of populations of concern in RPAs, IRNA and RRM. These assessments, ES/NFI analysis tools, and inter-cluster discussions will reinforce the centrality of affected persons to decision-making, and help determine the most appropriate methodology of distribution, taking each unique context into consideration. The project will focus on delivery of timely life-saving activities, streamlining prevention-oriented initiatives including COVID-19 preventive measures, evidence-based planning, and strengthening community-based protection for capacity development of displaced communities to cope, recover, contribute to and achieve solutions. In efforts to minimize risks including SGBV, prevention/response activities will continue with a focus on community engagement, strengthening referrals and prevention with awareness raising. At the same time, there is an increasing trend and willingness of IDPs to return to their homes. It is anticipated that more development-oriented programming will be implemented in South Sudan considering the R-ARCSS, 2019-2020 UN Cooperation Framework, and the 2018-2022 National Development Strategy for South Sudan. In order to increase the absorption capacity of receiving communities, UNHCR will focus support at the community level, maintaining targeted assistance at the individual level to persons with specific needs (PSNs) and the most vulnerable. UNHCR will use its age, gender and diversity policy and ES/NFI analysis tools, to ensure the entire population is reached, the voices of marginalised members of the community are taken into consideration and the response does not place any person at heightened risk. In 2021, UNHCR aims to reach 50% of the projected IDP population equal to 800,000 persons with targeted assistance to 200,000 persons. IDP and refugee returnees often face additional challenges of finding their houses damaged, destroyed and/or occupied. The need for decent shelter in areas of return is critical to realizing transitional and durable solutions. UNHCR will work with the Protection Cluster and HLP working group to address HLP rights issues through the provision of legal assistance, issuance of property and identity documents, and mediation mechanisms.

1 - Needs

In 2020, the COVID-19 pandemic compounded an already challenging context in South Sudan, including severe flooding across the country, as well as an unprecedented increase in sub-national violence resulting in new displacement and and highlighting the need for a bolstered ES/NFI response. UNHCR will remain an active member of the ES/NFI Cluster, particularly at the field level, and will maintain its cooperation with the Cluster actors to advocate for protection mainstreaming and enhanced vulnerability assessment and optimize the available resources and focus and timely respond to the most critical needs of vulnerable IDPs among other affected populations and the risks remain high among women, girls, boysand persons with specific protection needs. In 2021, ensuring access to required assistance is critical in light of the global COVID-19 pandemic, with shelter and material assistance to help reduce exposure to cross-cutting protection risks, including exposure to diseases. UNHCR’s intends to further bolster its outreach activities to address the needs of the majority of IDPs outside Protection of Civilians (PoC) sites/IDP camps. About 88% of IDP population continue to live in host communities and spontaneous settlements, many in remote areas with limitedinfrastructure and accessibility. Through an expanded operational coverage and strengthened outreach response, in 2021, UNHCR aims to reach 50% of the projected IDP population equal to 800,000 persons with targeted assistance to 80,000 persons. Expansion of community support activities will further be focused and prioritized based on the anticipated impact, despite growing needs for the creation and strengthening of community structures due to the high mobility of displaced communities, COVID-19 preventive measures, and in view of the anticipated returns. While large scale, nation-wide return movements in 2021 would most likely depend onimplementation of the peace agreement and presence of humanitarian and development actors in those areas, a larger number of sporadic and area specific spontaneousreturn/relocation movements are expected. Support for solutions, however, will remain a challenge considering that IDPs are deciding to return to areas where there are no available services with a limited presence of humanitarian actors.

3 - Project indicators

UNHCR will collect and analyse data against the set protection indicators for the response plan regularly. This will include disaggregated population data and profile, sector data and routine collection of project indicators to inform protection analysis and success of the project. Protection monitoring and participatory assessments will inform a robust situation analysis and ensure targeted population, including those with specific needs and the most vulnerable, are consulted during the design, implementation and follow-up of the activity. Rights-based and the Age, Gender, and Diversity Approach principles are applied throughout the programme cycle to ensure that the voice, needs and feedback of persons of concern, particularly those with chronic medical issues, older persons, separated and unaccompanied children, persons with disabilities, women and children are taken into account, and that gaps, challenges and solutions are identified. Various community outreach channels and communication modalities will be used to ensure accountability to affected communities in the response. This includes bolstered feedback and complaint desks, hotlines, focus discussion groups, community-based networks, and community coordination meetings.

Market functionality is considered a key component in project design particularly to inform on socio- economic conditions in a given area, determine potentiality for sustainable returns and enable designing of solutions-oriented projects. This is captured during the multi sector needs assessments, or through the community-based networks that provides information on viability upon which the context and design is adopted. The project design and CVA considerations will be adapted accordingly to the current context and feasibility.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

When people are forced to flee their homes, they leave with little more than they can carry. They also lose their ability to earn and spend in the process. Cash or vouchers seek to protect displaced persons by reducing the risks they face and to maintain their capacity to spend. CVA can be used in a variety of settings, as long as there is a stable market and a safe way to provide displaced persons with cash or vouchers. The flexibility that CVAs offer makes them a more dignified form of assistance, giving displaced persons the ability to immediately prioritise and choose what they need. CVA is contingent on a market assessments and protection assessments. Therefore, the CVA response will be based on the operational context and feasibility assessments.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

UNHCR has a zero tolerance policy with regards sexual exploitation and abuse and has clear communication and messaging strategy to inform persons of concern that will be absorbed in to the project. UNHCR has systematically integrated the issue of PSEA in its information campaigns, trainings and meetings with PoC’s and applied through the AGD lens. A PSEA focal person is usually appointed and discreet information sharing lines made available.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

UNHCR's age, gender and diversity (AGD) policy is at the forefront of each intervention as it ensures that all persons of concern fully participate in decisions that affect them and enjoy their rights on an equal footing with others. Coupled with a community-based approach, through consultation and participation, communities engage meaningfully and substantively in all programmes that affect them and play a leading role in change. Through these networks, two way communication will be engaged to ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

UNHCR generally undertakes a community-based approach in all its projects relating to affected population. Through consultation and participation, communities engage meaningfully and substantively in all programmes that affect them and play a leading role in change. UNHCR recognizes that, without the engagement of persons of concern, our intervention cannot be impactful on the lives of persons of concern. UNHCR incorporates the knowledge and resources of communities to achieve a holistic intervention which ultimately strengthen their capacities. If communities affected by crises are empowered, they are in a stronger position to protect and support their families, promote social cohesion and peaceful coexistence with host communities, respond to the aspirations of young people, and rebuild their lives. Feedback and complaint mechanisms, community-based networks, coordination meetings, and regular meetings with IDP leadership and communities have been rendered crucial. Setting up via protection or complaints desks provides the needed feedback and complaint mechanisms in order to ensure a two-way channel which maintains the integrity of persons of concern likewise providing feedback for appropriate action. There will be hotlines in addition to complaint boxes depending on location, which feedback on the modality and items disbursed can be given.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

An annual participatory assessments exercise is undertaken using the Age, Gender-Diversity Mainstreaming Methodology in all project locations or implementation areas. The PA outcome sets out the agenda for program planning and design. It is a feedback session engaging the communities which involves representatives from all target populations including youth groups, community leaders, women groups and persons with disabilities for feedback on implemented projects and provided UNHCR resource for advocacy and review if any of its project implementation based on the needs of the persons of concern.
