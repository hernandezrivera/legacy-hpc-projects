Project Summary

Almost ten years of crisis in Syria have continued to erode the socio-economic foundations of the Syrian people, in parallel to the fallout from the Lebanese financial crisis, the rapid depreciation of the Syrian currency, dramatic inflation, and the entry into effect of new trade restrictions in June 2020.
Moreover, the global COVID-19 pandemic has led to additional stress on social and basic services while also depriving Syrian people of their jobs and livelihoods.
Furthermore, ERL needs remain considerable in scale, severity, and complexity across the country, with an estimated 11.6 million people needing dignifying early recovery and livelihood support.
In the context of the efforts that been made by Al-Birr Association to improve the economic situation of the population, and help them obtain income-generating sources.
The proposed project aims to support the ability of the population in Homs city and its countryside to withstand and live under the prevailing conditions through improve the economic situation of the population, and help them obtain income-generating sources through providing productive assets grants, and vocational training.
AL-Birr studied the needs of the region through in-depth interviews with key people in those areas such as the village head and the mayor as well as the information obtained from the association’s branches and its medical and relief points deployed in the area.
We will provide the livelihoods grants to 400 beneficiaries that suit the nature of the region and the population to people who lost their productive assets, and are able to work and develop their small projects.
In addition, to provide vocational training for 2000 beneficiaries on some occupations such as blacksmithing, wood, and aluminum carpentry, sanitary installations, shaving and decorating for men, sewing and embroidery, hairdressing, and decorating for women.
- Vocational training will be provided by professional trainers with high qualifications in their fields, each course will consist of 20 beneficiaries for 192 hours of training hours over a period of 6 months.
- The plan of reaching the beneficiaries:
A training center will be rented and equipped in each of the three regions in the countryside, with a capacity of 4 training rooms for each center, and training courses will be conducted simultaneously in all halls, where 20 beneficiaries will be able to attend each session, at a rate of two sessions per week for a period of 4 hours.
This way, 960 rural beneficiaries will be reached.
As for the city, two training centers will be rented and equipped, as each training center will consist of 4 large halls, in which the training courses will take place simultaneously in all the halls, where 20 beneficiaries will be able to attend each session, at a rate of two sessions per week for a period of 4 hours.
As the training courses will last for a period of 6 months, to start with others for another 6 months.
This way, 1040 beneficiaries of vocational training will be reached in the city.

4 - Needs

Almost ten years of crisis in Syria have continued to erode the socio-economic foundations of the Syrian people, in parallel to the fallout from the Lebanese financial crisis, the rapid depreciation of the Syrian currency, dramatic inflation, and the entry into effect of new trade restrictions in June 2020.Moreover, the global COVID-19 pandemic has led to additional stress on social and basic services while also depriving Syrian people of their jobs and livelihoods. The combined effects of these concurrent crises contributed to a significant contraction of the overall economic activity, affecting people’s ability to sustain adequate living standards, seek decent livelihood opportunities, access social services and essential infrastructure, and further undermine social cohesion. ERL needs remain considerable in scale, severity, and complexity across the country, with an estimated 11.6 million people needing dignifying early recovery and livelihood support.While precautionary measures against the spread of the virus were crucial to contain transmission, they exacerbated pre-existing and underlying socio-economic vulnerabilities by reducing availability and access to basic services and employment opportunities. COVID-19 restrictions further and heavily impacted employment opportunities across the country, with unemployment estimated closer to 50 percent of the working-age population at the end of 2020, pushing up prices and further increasing dependence on negative coping mechanisms. (Humanitarian Need Overview 2021)

5 - Implementation of activities

1- Vocational training: We will prepare and equip a center with some equipment and supplies to be suitable to provide vocational training for beneficiaries, as we will provide the vocational training of professions that are needed in their areas according to the nature of each region and they will be able to integrate into the labor market and become productive individuals in their societies.- will be opening the registration to the needy and affected population within the targeted areas, and determine beneficiaries who deserve to receive the training and who are able to work.Accepted persons will receive 6 months of training, and a small grant of tools to help them start their work.- Vocational trainers who have good qualifications will be contracted to train the beneficiaries. 2- Productive assets grants: This will be done by opening the registration to the needy and affected population within the targeted areas, and then a socio-economy study will be conducted to determine beneficiaries who deserve to receive the grant and who are able to work and develop it. - The beneficiaries will be chosen from those who lost their productive assets through the crises, and are not able to restore them.- We will conduct periodic visits for the beneficiaries to ensure that they are using the grants and developing them to generate income. - Moreover, the 2 monitoring and evaluation officers will be recruited and responsible for monitoring and evaluation of the project implementation under the supervision of the M&E Unit of the Project Management Office, as a periodic visit will be conducted according to the M&E plan.
