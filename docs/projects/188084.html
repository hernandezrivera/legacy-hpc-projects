Project Summary

The project aims at supporting war affected households by rehabilitating partially damaged houses and distributing NFI kits to displaced people in Idleb and in Al Bab in Aleppo. In total, 28,000 people will benefit from the activities of this project (14,140 males – 13,860 females) 18,000 people out of them in 2022. The rehabilitation will target 200 houses that are partially damaged houses that people are living in or have returned to. NFI kits will be distributed to 2800 HHs that have been displaced from different areas into Idleb and Aleppo and 1200 HHs will receive winterization assistance. In camps, roads of five camps will be levelled, and rain drainage established.

4 - Needs

As the war enters its twelfth year in Syria, the humanitarian situation is becoming worse. 2021 witnessed several forced displacements from south of Idleb into Idleb and Aleppo. According to CCCM ISIMM, as of December 2021, the CCCM database includes 1,398 IDP sites that host 1,694,712 IDPs or 321,085 families. Of these, 80% are women and children, and 45,997 IDPs are reported to be persons with specific needs. Snowstorm incidents occurred in January 2022 in various IDP sites in Aleppo (22 sites) and Idleb (9 sites) governorates, mainly in the districts of Afrin, Azaz and Harim, 362 tents were affected of 2,142 individuals and 429 families, including the IDP’s belongings. According to the reports received, there is a crucial need to open snow-closed roads, moving the affected beneficiaries to safe places, securing means for heating, and replacing destroyed tents.(CCCM).HNAP Shelter situation in September 2021 reported that 27% of IDPs in NWS live in camps, 12% live in damaged houses, while 43 of IDP households have been unable to afford shelter repairs, the bad shelter conditions of camps, mainly the road conditions that need gravelling and compacting in addition rain drainage are badly affecting the movement of IDPs and and the movement of water trucks and other services making some facilities in the camps not accessible.

5 - Implementation of activities

This project will compromise Rehabilitation of 200 partially damaged houses (Activity2.1.1.1), distributing 1500 core NFI kits ( Activity 1.1.1.1) distributing 650 vouchers for winterization (1.1.1.2), in addition to improving the infrastructure in camps (road gravelling and water culverts) (1.2.3.2). The activities of the project will benefit 16,500beneficiaries (3,795 boys under 18 , 3,630 girls under 18, 4035 men, 4620 women).Upgrading of 200 unfinished houses for 200 HHs. HIHFAD receives applications from beneficiaries in need of house upgrade, the application will include the ID of the applicants in addition to property ownership documents that will be verified. Two members of the staff (a male and female) who are trained on HLP will be responsible for any HLP issues confronted during implementation of the project. A technical needs assessment of houses will be conducted before starting the implementation of the rehabilitation works. The house should be unfinished, and the cost of all rehabilitation works will not exceed $900 for each house. The selection of beneficiaries for the Shelter rehabilitation activities will be based on the following criteria: Families who have returned to their damaged houses. Female headed households; Families with chronically ill members or with disabilities (both host and IDPs) - PWD will be taken into consideration in all activities: door to door distribution for PWD, during upgrade,a ramp, etc,.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment ) Mitigation:1 - Establish a confidential complaint mechanism 2 - Train staff on code of conduct – 3 -Include code of conduct in staff contracts about SEA complaints. Risk: Some beneficiaries might be at risks such as harassment during the distribution of NFI kits. Mitigation: 1 Separate between men and women during the distribution 2 - Give priority to vulnerable people in addition to door to door distribution to PWD. Risk: Eviction from shelter due to lack of approval from property owner. Mitigation: 1. Abide by the HLP guidance document by the SNFI Cluster 2. Do not proceed with the Shelter rehabilitation unless with the consensus of the owner, proof of registered HLP ownership, verified ownership claims. If this won’t be available, feasible, refer to the HLP DD. 3. Include HLP caveat into the MoUs signed Risk: Tension between host communities, and IDP. mitigation: 1. Ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs only. 2. Selection criteria are transparent and groups are fully informed. Distribution partners engage with the communities to sensitize the population. -Risk of physical harm at distribution points (security, safety)Distribution points will be organized to deal withCovid19 risk, clear marks with physical distance of 1.5 m, hand sanitizers will be available, staff wear masks, crowd will be organized through timing of distribution, maximum of 10 people at distribution point.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Persons with disabilities are at higher risks of neglect and exclusion from the families and the service providers. Women and girls with disabilities are two times at higher risk of neglect and exclusion from the families and the service providers, to compare with their peers without disabilities all of this makes the persons with disabilities in higher risks of SEA . Females with disabilities remain most invisible groups in the Syrian society, they may not leave their houses, and not participating social events in the communities hugely isolated. Thus, the project will ensure outreaching house-bounded females by ensuring appropriate gender balance within the team, so ensure our access to this group. HIHFAD provides all the hired staff an induction training on the protection principles, PSEA and GBV so they become oriented enough to provide the services in a dignified and safe way for the beneficiaries. A focal point for PSEA and GBV would be appointed per area. HIHFAD has a pre-established feedback system and safeguarding reporting channel that supports the ability of the targeted populations to submit their complaints, comments, and suggestions in a beneficiary-friendly and easy-to-access way.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Access restriction due to local fi ghting between different arm source groups in thetargeted location which may restrict the access. The local fi ghting might delay theimplementation of the activities. Mitigation: HIHFAD Security team will monitor thesituation on the ground and send reports when necessary. The works will be suspendedduring the fi ghting and resumed after assessment of the situation. Assumption: thesecurity situation remains relatively stable which allows people to continue living in the area and the activities of the project to take place. Risk: Closure of the border for commercial shipments or changing some regulation related to allowed items to be exported to Syria may have a very heavy negative impact on the cross-border activities. Mitigation: 1. Explore alternative supply lines through local procurement 2. Communicateregularly with logistic cluster to have valid information about new or changing regulations. Assumption: The crossing borders are open for humanitarian and commercial shipments. Risk: Risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment of women and girls) Mitigation: 1 - Establish a confidential complaint mechanism 2 -Train staff on code of conduct – 3 - Include code of conduct in all staff contracts that address any SEA complaints.
