Project Summary

UNHCR response planned for 2019 is aligned with the CCCM Cluster Strategic Objectives 1, 2, 3 and 4. The project aims to ensure the provision of timely, well-coordinated and integrated humanitarian interventions for improving the well-being of displaced populations, their resilience and participation in decision-making processes. In this regard, UNHCR aims to strengthen coordination, advocacy and capacity building of key actors to ensure quality services are delivered in an equal and needs-based manner, considering the views of the community (SO1 &4 ). Further, the project covers continuous and prioritized support to new arrivals, returnees ,existing IDPs in displaced sites with specific needs and the most vulnerable, ensuring response and prevention takes into account peculiarities relevant to each group, integrating protection mainstreaming in all CCCM activities (SO 2 and 4). A key component of the project is also focused on strengthening community mobilization, community ownership and self-management structures, with support to community-based structures to enhance participation in planning, decisions and response, with further empowerment of communities, particularly vulnerable groups to share their views in improving service delivery, management structures, site planning and communication channels vis a vis their needs, identified risks and priorities (SO 3).
To facilitate sustained response and need-based intervention, UNHCR also intends to rehabilitate, renovate and install communication equipment at the Bentiu Humanitarian Hub in order to ease partners operations related to Beyond Bentiu Response plan as it is anticipated that some of the humanitarian actors may opt to live outside the UNMISS Bentiu compound to be closer to the affected population for improved service delivery.
The project will also aim to provide timely assistance and necessary support to returnees from the neighbouring countries following the signed revitalized peace agreement in Khartoum on the 12th September 2018 and the subsequent peace celebration on the 31st Oct, 2018 in Juba by all the warring parties and other key actors. UNCHR intends to establish way stations in strategic locations along the border of South Sudan and its East African neighbours so as receive and facilitate the return of South Sudanese to their homeland and areas of their choice.

Needs

Since the outbreak of the conflict in South Sudan in Dec, 2013 exacerbated by the June 2016 crisis after the relative peace that followed the signing of the Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in the Republic of South Sudan (ARCRSS) in Juba multiple displacement of the an estimated population of about 1.9M by October 2018. New displacement had been reported in parts of Equatorial, Bahr-le-Ghazal, Unity and parts of Upper Nile in the 3rd quarter of 2018 as a result of continued intermittent fights among the adversaries with the intention of expanding their perceived territories.Also displacement has been influenced by unprecedented levels of food insecurity, resulting from climate effects but aggravated by the conflict. In such context, the needs remain dire as IDPs continue to face serious risks across the country such as lack of privacy, inadequate humanitarian support in the different sites of displacement more so in the hard to reach areas and inadequate livelihood activities particularly among the youth which has made them to resort to criminal activities or exposed them to forceful conscription by armed elements.As of 30th Sep, 2018, 197882 IDPs are hosted under UNMISS Protection of Civilians (POC) sites, while 80054 are hosted at the collective sites and 27438 IDPs in the informal settlements across the country. With food insecurity in some of regions expected towards the end of the year and the beginning of 2019, new displacements towards the POC sites will likely persist since IDPs perceive that better services and consistent food distribution is always done on monthly in POC basis unlike other areas. While opportunity for durable solutions may take longer for a good part of the POC population, the signing of the revitalized peace agreement in Khartoum on the 12th Sept, 2018 may allow movements towards safe areas of return or intended relocation from areas of displacement both within the country and from the neighbouring countries. Serious gaps in registration and profiling pose a significant challenge to ensure accurate planning and response; as well as in advocacy for assistance and planning for durable or transitional solutions aggravated by the rampant cattle raids and revenge killings. Technical expertise is required for data collection, analysis and sharing; as well as to addressing practical challenges and the increasing frustration of POC population; while coordination to address gaps in basic services and information sharing needs to be improved across the POC sites. To improve population profiling UNHCR will engage JIPs profiling agency in early 2019 for a collaborative profiling exercise to informed HCT strategies for durable solutions. About 88% of IDP population continue to live in host communities and spontaneous settlements, many in hard-to-reach areas with limited infrastructure and accessibility. Major efforts will be required to ensure potential for integration is fully explored, and peaceful co-existence initiatives; enhancing thereby community participation. To this effect, further engagement in integrated needs assessment, profiling of IDP intending to return to their home villages, organizing for go and see visits and improving the support of the host communities remains a priority; with targeted assistance to be provided for the most vulnerable. Therefore this project will mainly focus in Mayendit, Leer and Bentiu informal Settlement, collective sites in Juba, Rubkona &Bentiu and other camps-like setting in Unity State beside the overall coordination of CCCM at the national level.

Activities

Output 1: Coordination, advocacy and capacity building strengthened. (SO1 &4 ) Activities: • Support overall CCCM coordination at the national level and provide coordination forum in Juba, Bentiu & Rubkona collective sites and camp-like settings in Unity State(Kock, Guit, Mayom, Mayendit & Leer)• Ensure flow monitoring in selected sites• Provide capacity building for community-based structures and other stakeholders in the different sites.• Conduct needs assessments, population profiling and other data collection exercises in the hard-to-reach areas of camp - like settings to inform advocacy and humanitarian interventions.• Participate in joint initiatives for assessments and planning response • Conduct service mapping, data analysis, information products (including factsheets) and sharing with humanitarians • Organize and facilitate monthly and other ad-hoc meetings in the hard-to-reach areas, Bentiu, Leer, Mayendit, Juba & Rubkona collective sites.• Mapping of potential areas of return across the sites to ease planning and support to the population.• Establish and/or strengthen the existing complaints desks in different sites, updating SOPs for the operation of a two-way community feedback mechanisms.• Establish/strengthened PSEA complaint mechanisms with appropriate SOPs agreed among relevant partners Output 2: Support to returnees and persons with specific-needs provided (SO2) Activities: • Ensure site maintenance activities address PSN needs • Ensure women, youth and PSN participation in site community-based committees• Ensure age, gender, diversity (AGD) mainstreaming across coordination mechanisms and services provided in different sites• Train CCCM staff and community structures in protection and AGD mainstreaming.• Conduct assessments which promote data collection on needs of PSNs and most vulnerable, using the outcome for strengthening advocacy and integrated response• Support PSN identification mechanisms in registration during return flow.• Identify returnees in coordination with RRC and other actors. Output 3: Community mobilization and self- management strengthened (SO3) Activities: • Establish community structures in new sites and support the existing ones through capacity-building initiatives.• Support community ownership in all the collective and informal settlements by improving participation in planning and decision-making processes.
