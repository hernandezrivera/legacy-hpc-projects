Project Summary


The project will support livelihoods of most affected population by providing food security in the most in need areas in Albab district. the intervention will be through the distribution of wheat seeds and sheep to the most affected and needy farmers to ensure their food security and support their livelihoods in the long term. The project will also support the local market by purchasing all project inputs from the target areas.
Selection priorities:
1. most affected farmers from host communities and have more than 10 dunums. As we cannot target IDPs because of their lack of adequate land ownership
2. Agriculture is the main source of income for beneficiary households
3. Farmers directly or indirectly affected by the ongoing crisis
4 - family affected and headed by a woman
the project will enable most affected population to resist poverty and gain resilience to fight the severe economic conditions, to overcome challenges caused by conflict, to become independent from humanitarian aid and move towards self-reliance.
this project will provide an opportunity to female-headed households and engaging them to create sustainable income and lives in dignity.

Needs

According to HNO 2019Now in its eighth year, the humanitarian crisis in Syria has led to the internal displacement of at least 5.9 million people, and left an estimated 13 million in need of humanitarian assistance.According to the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) Food Security Sector 2018 Mid-Year Review, food security further deteriorated during the first six months of 2018, with the number of food insecure people increasing from 6.5 million to 6.7 million. The number of people at risk of food insecurity has also grown, from 4 million to 4.5 million, bringing the total up to 11.2 million Syrians who need of urgent life-saving and life-sustaining food and livelihoods assistance.Insecurity and conflict are among the major drivers of food insecurity in Syria, causing widespread, often multiple displacement and constraining physical access to food and markets.Vulnerable populations such as people with disabilities, single headed households, and the elderly often face particular challenges physically accessing food, for reasons ranging from a lack of mobility to threats to their physical safety. In many communities, an influx of internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees contribute to socio-economic pressures that also create vulnerabilities for host communities.Food insecurity in Syria is also driven by a lack of financial access, as high food prices, low purchasing power, and the collapse of income earning opportunities have hampered mostSyrians’ ability to acquire food. These challenges are reflected in a heavy reliance on negative coping strategies, a lack of dietary diversity, and significant gaps in food consumption. Among conflict-affected households, inflation, market disruptions, and the extreme loss or depletion ofproductive assets all contribute to the economic challenges to either purchasing or producing food. The agricultural sector, which normally serves as a major engine of the economy and asource of cheaper, more abundant food, is also threatened by factors such as land contamination, lack of availability of and/or financial means to access essential inputs, and damaged infrastructure. Also by referring to FSL Cluster Key messages, eight years into the crisis, the main drivers to food insecurity are prolonged displacements, loss and lack of livelihoods and reduced production capacity which in turn resulted in limited physical and financial access to food, high prices and inflation contributing to reduced purchasing power and continuous depletion of livelihoods assets of the most vulnerable populations.Also Unprecedented livestock asset losses estimated to about half of the pre-crisis situation affecting livelihoods leading to irreversible coping strategies. In conclusion, the humanitarian needs in Syria are expected to remain high in 2019 and multiple actions are needed to ensure that all four pillars of food security related to access, availability, utilization and stability of food are adequately addressed through lifesaving, life sustaining, appropriate resilience, and early recovery interventions. As a basic reference, Maram field team conducted a field visit to AlBab countryside to verify from the needs and take a close look about livestook and agriculture situation where Maram field staff visited (Borj, Hadath, Hazwan, Maqri, Noman, Olan, Qdeiran, Qemmet Daghelbash, Saflaniyeh, Sheikh Elwan, Sheikh Jarrah, Sosyan, Su Sinbat, Tal Jerji and Waqqah) More than 3000 HHs in needed to food security and they practicing coping strategies.Maram’s programme will target vulnerable populations affected by conflict and displacement living in the most sub-standard living-conditions in Al Bab district with through wheat and sheep distribution. As defined by the FSL cluster, the most vulnerable populations include female headed household the project will target female3 headed household with sheep distribution.

Implementation of activities

The direct beneficiaries of the project will be (250 female-headed households and 2000 Farmers), Maram will support farmers and female-headed households by:Small scale farmers’ activity will be implemented through the following steps:  Community mobilization and sensitization for the further understanding of the project. Training on the planning, implementation, monitoring and management of especially agricultural project. Registration of targeted beneficiaries. Purchase and distribution of viable seeds and sheeps.  Supervision, monitoring, reporting and evaluation of project activities.The results of this activity will contribute as inputs for supporting beneficiaries in cultivation of wheat and livestock, while during the planting phase these products will be procured from the local market and being given to the beneficiaries so they can start the activity in sync with farming activities. 1. Purchasing of wheat, sheep, pesticides and veterinary medicines 2. Transport and storage of wheat 3. Distribution of wheat, sheep, pesticides and veterinary medicines Through this project vulnerable populations and IDPs will be supported by: • Creating job opportunities• Enhancing food security levels• Build family and society resilience • And fight the war economy.

Protection Risk Analysis

Girls, boys, women, and men's physical security is put at risk as a resultof the violence associated with civilian populations/spaces being targeted by armedparties to the conflict; a lack of consideration for the safety and security to access/receiveservices; lack of consideration of contextual information (e.g. aerial bombardment) andtargeting of civilian infrastructure in route to, during and directly after service provision. -Likelihood: High - Impact: High - Mitigation: Consider conflict patterns and contextualanalysis as part of decisions for establishing a fixed site or mobile service provision. -Ensure safety audit is conducted to ensure safe access (route and location) by boys, girls,women and men, elderly and populations with decreased mobility (due to physical,visual/hearing deficits, psychological issues). - Engage with local authorities on safetyand security in the community.
