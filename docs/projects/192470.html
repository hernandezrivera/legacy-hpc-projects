Project Summary

As part of the intervention, RI aims to improve nutrition outcomes and practices, including attitudes towards breastfeeding, Infant and young Child Feeding (IYCF), hygiene practices, nutritionally balanced diets for pregnant and lactating women and children under two, and adolescents. These activities will be implemented across Bekaa, Beirut, Mt. Lebanon, and North for vulnerable Lebanese and migrant communities.
Prolonged, economic deterioration, social and political tensions, and major food shortages continue to undermine food security and access to basic needs and services, limiting access to nutritional diets and knowledge of nutrition practices. Households (HHs) containing Lebanese and migrants across the country have been pushed into severe economic difficulty. With limited financial capabilities, HHs are unable to seek or obtain the critical nutrition services required, have limited access to necessary nourishable foods, are limited in movement due to major fuel shortages, and have limited employment prospects. Such circumstances mean HHs prioritize other needs and children and pregnant and lactating women are placed at increased risk. According to recent data collected, poor nutritional practices are becoming more widespread and common against the backdrop of the unfolding humanitarian situation. As illustrated in the Needs and Vulnerability Assessment of Migrants in Lebanon report by IOM (May 2021), poor nutritional health among migrants is common with 60% of 674 respondents recording low dietary diversity. Likewise, Lebanese are struggling, 80% of Lebanese are now classified as poor, directly impacting nutrition outcomes, impacting expenditure on diets and being forced to purchase inferior foodstuffs and limiting daily food intake. According to the World Bank, GDP declined by 20.3% alone in 2020, and economists at Bloomberg reported a 400% increase in food prices.
RI determined targeting rural areas such as Tripoli Akkar, Bekaa, Baalback and El Hermel, due to the fact that these areas host a large population of vulnerable Lebanese with limited access to health and nutrition services, aggravated with the overall devastating economic situation. In addition to Baabda, an area known of its vulnerability and crowdedness with vulnerable Lebanese and migrants. RI will continue to coordinate with IOCC to ensure a strong referral mechanism for malnutrition cases. This will be done across all areas of intervention and will include referral and follow up on malnourished cases identified through screening at the community of PHCs to the malnutrition treatment centers supported by IOCC.
Aligned to the nutrition cluster strategic objectives, RI will contribute to addressing the growing humanitarian needs, by targeting 6,500 individuals, made up of children and pregnant and lactating women.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

RI project activities will include the following:- Support vulnerable children aged 6-59 months and PLWs with nutrition inputs through high energy biscuits provided by UNICEF to reduce malnutrition and prevent anemia in Bekaa, Baalback-Hermel, BML, North, and Akkar.- Establish mother support groups to encourage and improve hygiene (food and personal) and help establish a balanced diet for the most vulnerable. This activity will be closely coordinated with UNFPA to avoid overlapping and harmonized approach.- Lactation specialist and PHCC staff, provide IYCF counselling to improve knowledge and nutrition outcomes.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Main protection risks of this project for Lebanese and migrants are related to barriers (physical, cultural, language) in accessing health and nutrition services. RI will build on existing networks and community engagement across all areas to reach the affected population. As the deteriorating security situation poses high protection risks (conflicts, robbery, roadblocks) for staff and beneficiaries, RI will coordinate movement and activity through its Security Focal Point, LAF and municipalities to minimize harm. For COVID-19, RI will follow WHO standards to reduce risk of transmission. All staff members trained on PSEA policies and beneficiaries will receive awareness of the strong referral and feedback mechanisms displayed to ensure feedback and complaints are received and followed-up. RI is part of the PSEA network and signed the PSEA SOPs. RI’s staff care policies will ensure concerns/challenges are addressed as possible and access to specialized MH services is available.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

RI will ensure AAP is implemented across all project activities through:- Consulting project participants throughout the project lifecycle to enhance and reinforce leadership and ownership of the project. This will take place through one-on-one consultations, FGDs, surveys;- Sharing information with project participants about RI’s and other services available to them based on articulated needs. RI will ensure that this is achieved during all field visits;- Ensuring gender and protection mainstreaming through close consultation with women, girls, boys and men at all project levels and project evaluation to assess whether their needs were met;- Ensuring RI’s FCM is accessible to all project participants by establishing at least two channels of reporting (hotline, in-person, complaint boxes). In addition, RI will continue to use the RIMS for referrals to external service providers and report referrals’ status on Activity Info.
