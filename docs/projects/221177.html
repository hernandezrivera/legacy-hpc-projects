Project Summary

Data from the Protection Monitoring Working Group indicates
that the situation in South Sudan constitutes a crisis primarily
focused on child protection. The extended conflict, coupled
with recurring climate-related challenges, has significantly
affected 2.5 million boys and girls, exposing them to various
forms of abuse, including recruitment, displacement,
separation from caregivers, and exploitation, with child
neglect and sexual violence among the concerning issues.
While the needs across population groups remain similar, the
CP AoR will focus on areas significantly affected by different
types of child abuse, particularly areas hosting children
fleeing the Sudan crisis.

1. Needs

According to the assessment that was done last September in Mayom County by Inter-Agency partners. 1. High Caseload of Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC): There is a pressing need for holistic support services aimed at Family Tracing and Reunifications (FTRs). Efforts must be intensified to locate family members and provide a safe environment for UASC in Mayom County. 2. Psychosocial Support Services (PSS): The population affected by floods is experiencing significant psychosocial distress. There is an urgent requirement for expanded PSS services for both children and adults, as current resources are insufficient to meet the demand. 3. Reactivation and Strengthening of Community-Based Child Protection Systems: The capacity of existing community-based child protection structures is weak. There is an identified need to reactivate or establish these systems to create a supportive and protective environment for children. 4. Child Protection Service Gaps: Specific gaps in child protection services have been identified in the greater Mayom County due to limited resources, resulting in the downsizing or withdrawal of many national partners. A strategic plan to fill these gaps and bolster existing services is critical. 5. Community-Based Child Protection Mechanisms: Despite the weaknesses caused by the emergencies, some child protection structures exist within the community (e.g., child protection committees, awareness-raising activities). These mechanisms should be leveraged and strengthened to improve overall child protection in the area. In conclusion, addressing these needs requires a coordinated response involving community engagement, resource allocation, and collaboration with existing organizations and stakeholders.

2. Project activities

Activity 1: Provide comprehensive case management services for children in Mayom County. These services aimed to address the diverse needs of each child, ensuring their safety, well-being, and access to essential resources. Activity 2: Provide cash-based services for children (boys and girls) to receive essential case management services. Activity 3: Conduct mapping for Child Protection Services and referral pathways at the county level. Activity 4: Provided specialized and structured community-based mental health and psycho-social support activities for boys, girls, men, and women in the County. Activity 5: Create awareness-raising sessions for children and caregivers, focusing on critical Child Protection issues in the county. Activity 6: Conduct training program for community leaders, humanitarian workers, child protection partners, and government social welfare staff on Child Protection in Emergencies (CPiE) in the County.
