Project Summary

Shafak Organization aims to contribute to the provision of access to life-saving and life-sustaining humanitarian health services for 233,065 people in need and conflict-affected populations in northern Syria in 2022. The proposed intervention will prioritize internally displaced people and other vulnerable populations especially (women, children, and people with disabilities) in the targeted areas. The intervention will be implemented through:
1. Continue supporting a comprehensive package of health services including Primary Health Care, and Mental Health in eight of the existing health facilities in northwest Syria. The motivation of this project is to continue providing health services in the already established and equipped centers to the population in western and southern rural Aleppo and rural Idlib with outreach and referral activities to the higher level of health services. The project will comprise two convergent components: primary health and mental health care, and their activities will be provided through the following eight health centers:
1)- Al-Jeineh Comprehensive PHC Center with basic obstetric and neonatal care (HF-Code: B0200004). Aleppo, Jebel Saman, Atareb, Jienieh.
2)- Harbanoush PHC center (HF-Code: G0700005): Idleb, Maaret Tamsrin, Harbanush.
3)- Akhtarin PHC Center (HF-Code :B0204077): Aleppo, A'zaz, Aghtrin.
4)- Ziadiyah PHC Center (HF-Code :B0204066): Aleppo, A'zaz, Aghtrin, Ziadiyah.
5)- Arshaf PHC Center (HF-Code :B0204027): Aleppo, A'zaz, Aghtrin, Arshaf.
6)- Kafr Ghan PHC Center (HF-Code: B0204071). Aleppo, A'zaz, Suran, Kafr Ghan,
7)- Ayidoun Kadimoon PHC (HF-Code: G0703006). Idleb-Harim-Salqin-Salqin
8)- Darkoush PHC (HF-Code: G0704016). Idleb-Jisr-Ash-shugur-Darkoush-Darkoush
In average the expected beneficiaries for all proposed PHC centers is: 168,000 for the whole year (33,000 Men, 45,000 women, 50,000 girls, 40,000 boys).

The provision of Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (EmONC) services and management of SRH-related diseases in addition to the pediatric health care through supporting the specialized hospital for women and children which provides maternal and child health services to most vulnerable people in the rural of Idlib, Marret Tamsrin and through outreach teams:
a) Shafak Specialized Hopsital for Women and Children (HF-Code: G0700073). Idleb, Maaret Tamsrin.
In average the expected beneficiaries for the proposed hospital : 30,000 for the whole year (20,000 women, 6000 girls, 4000 boys).
3. Continue Supporting 2 dialysis centers in Idlib (35 patients per month) and Atareb (30 patients per month) with running costs and dialysis kits. In addition to conduct capacity building and training for the medical staff of the two centers:
a)- Al-Atareb Dialysis center (HF-Code: B0200181): Aleppo, Jebel Saman, Atareb.
b)- Shafak Dialysis center-Idleb: (HF-Code: G0700074): Idleb, Idleb, Idleb.

a) Ariha Surgical Unit (HF-Code: G0705061): Idleb, Ariha, Ariha.
In average the expected beneficiaries for surgical unit : 15,000 for the whole year (5500 Men, 4000 women 3500 girls, 2000 boys).

In average the expected beneficiaries for all BeMONC PHC centers: 20,000 for the whole year (15,000 women 3000 girls, 2000 boys).
By achieving the above components, Shafak will ensure the continuation of essential and life-saving health interventions in addition to maintaining essential health services to contribute in decreasing mortality and morbidity percentage in NWS.
In 2023, according to Shafak strategy and budget availability, it is expected to reach the same target through supporting the same health services and facilities.

4 - Needs

According to 2022 SYRIA HNO final draft, 12.23 million people are in need of health services in 2022,including 4.4 million displaced persons, 1.33 million children under 5 years – including an estimated 503,000 live births expected16, and 3.38 million women of reproductive age (15-49 years). Just over 500,000 older people will require inclusive health services, as well as those with early onset non-communicable diseases (NCDs) which are estimated to account for 45 per cent of all mortality in Syria. Youth and young persons, which comprise 1.98 million of the health sector PiN, are also reporting increasing stress and mental health concerns. Based on the 2021 MSNA, within the health sector PiN, 1.3 million persons are estimated to have a disability which places them at greater risk for exclusion from health services. Households affected by disability were most likely to report living more than 1 hour from a health facility, paying for health care, non-availability of needed services, fear of COVID-19 at the health facility, and lack of access at health facility for persons with disability or mobility challenges. Ongoing COVID-19 response operations must continue, including case management capacity for acuteinfection and long-COVID30 care. COVID-19 vaccination coverage must be expanded with sufficientresources to increase not only the overall vaccine supply, but also delivery capacity, including strengthenedcommunity mobilization and awareness to promote vaccine acceptance

5 - Implementation of activities

In accordance with Shafak’s cross-border strategy, we will be responsible for direct implementation in the target locations coordinating with health authorities in NW Syria, building on their extensive network, and facilitating access. Local communities will be involved in Shafak’s capacity development efforts and suitable training. Shafak will be responsible for ensuring that quality and accountability standards are met, will provide required training to staff, and ensure that contractual requirements are adhered to and met. Further, we will be responsible for all program and financial reporting to the donor. Shafak will also continue to coordinate with relevant clusters and other coordination bodies in Turkey. This joint program will be conducted through three main phases as the following: Phase I: - Procurement of needed equipment, devices, drugs, consumables, tools for training, and tools for implementation. Phase II: Launch of - Outpatient services, comprehensive reproductive health services, and chronic disease treatment in all facilities. - Emergency surgery, emergency obstetric, and newborn services in the hospital. - PHC services using the health cluster and TWG guidelines especially EHSP-NCD and MHPSS. Phase III: -Reporting, data analysis, monitoring, and evaluation regularly 1 time per week .The Indicators Performance Tracking Table (IPTT), M&E work plan, and disaggregated database will be developed. The database tools, using Access and Excel Microsoft software.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project's overall objective aims at obtaining full respect for the rights of the individual where they need them the most. Throughout the project life cycle, the principles of protection mainstreaming are incorporated. All activities are designed to avoiding causing harm and prioritize the safety and dignity of targeted populations, ensuring meaningful access, accountability, and participation through several, action such as • The project is designed to meet the needs of the community especially women, boys, and girls considering the context, tradition, and norms in the community • Location of facilities will be accessible and easy to reach for all people., also safe and away from any military location. • Community will be clearly and widely informed about the date, location, and timing of the service provision, and their opinion will be highly considered. • Team staff will be trained to be skilled in delivering PFA, identifying CP&GBV cases, and refer them to adequate services. • Ensure that the location is suitable for women, girls, people with disabilities, children-headed households, old people, and other affected categories in the community--- outreach. • COVID-19 Mitigation measures will be applied for staff and Patients using the last WHO guidelines• Teams will be trained on PESA, Child safeguarding, and will sign the code of conduct related. • project team will set up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can receive the information they need,


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

All staff members sign the Shafak PSEA code of conduct upon recruitment, where a protection specialist provides orientations and regular PSEA training sessions on child protection policy and the PSEA code of conduct. The areas targeted have high numbers of IDPs. Host communities are the front-line responders to the needs of IDPs. Due to limited local resources and limited aid hosting communities can see IDPs as a burden to the community. This can result in tensions over sharing limited resources. This can be exacerbated by projects that use the status of IDP as a single proxy criterion for “in need”. To avoid this, and to promote a no do harm approach the project seeks to meet the needs of IDPs and host communities based on need, working toward equity between these groups. Moreover, Shafak would provide activities that include prevention and response services, identification of GBV and CP cases, referral to specialized (GBV and CP) and non-specialized services in accordance with the guidelines shared by the protection cluster, and provide direct support to high priority cases. Shafak protection outreach teams will help the education project team as well by identifying cases in schools and health facilities supported and refer them to the proper specialized service needed.
