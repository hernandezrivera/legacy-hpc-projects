Project Summary



Mezam which is identified by official statistics as trailing behind the central, western and eastern regions in terms of poverty reduction, has experienced multiple and severe shocks such as the Anglophone Crisis which has lasted for over 3 years. Many people have lost their sources of livelihoods such as poultry farming since their structures have been burnt down or they have moved to other locations due to insecurity in their neighborhoods. The inability of the government to call for a ceasefire has aggravated the situation of poultry farmers to rehabilitate their farms and improve on their livelihoods. It is in this context that this project on broiling and fattening of table birds is designed to help ameliorate the living and livelihood conditions of some IDP’s who have moved into Mezam as their host community. The ASWEDO poultry farming project for IDPs is the result of a three months’ process of field consultation and analysis on food security and livelihoods with national and international NGOs, UN agencies, and civil societies. In terms of scope of interventions, the ASWEDO poultry farming project for IDPs aims to create and promote the conditions for addressing root causes of livelihood erosion by linking short term/immediate actions with longer term measures and considerations. It also aims to improve self-reliance of IDPs and host communities through food assistance to affected persons in Zha-Bya village, Mejung, Alahkuma and Nkwen.
Thus the Plan proposes a set of balanced responses that aims to protect, rehabilitate and diversify the livelihoods of IDPs in Bamenda. In that respect, the document is meant to complement long-term development strategies and focuses on the range of emergency, recovery and rehabilitation interventions needed for the IDPs of Alakuma, Mankon and Nkwen. The implementation of the poultry project will be through donors and self funds. The selected option is based on a pro-poor and community self-reliance approach as the most sustainable way to achieve productivity growth and improve use and access of natural capital. In areas with higher IDP concentration poultry farming will pursue high-value livelihood opportunities. The use of improved technologies will be supported to raise productivity growth. The goal of the ASWEDO poultry project for the IDPs is to improve the livelihood security of IDP households in Mezam. The anticipated outcome sets the medium-term scenario on which the programme is expected to make an impact and is to support and stabilize food security of IDPs’ households in Mezam. The outputs of the Project is designed to have a rapid impact, building on existing and successfully implemented approaches and systems.
Output 1 – Increased food availability
Output 2 – Reduced vulnerability to food security risk
Output 3 – Better access to food through safety nets
The outputs of the poultry project will be achieved through three activities.
1 - Broiling of 4800 day chicks
2 – Distribution of 3week old chicks feed, vaccines, feeders and drinkers to 96 IDPs
3 - Helping the IDPs to market the fattened chicken.

Besoins / Needs

The assessed needs of this poultry project through its activities will address; Employment which is the second most reported priority need across the North West Region of Cameroon, reported by 72% of households. Noticeably, employment was reported on average twice by many households in the Alakuma, Mankon and Nkwen as compared to previous Cluster reports. Given that many IDPs will not be able to safely return to their area of origin so soon, lack of access to livelihoods creates severe challenges for sustainable capacity to cop with displacement which often worsens social cohesion and tensions in the area of displacement. The majority of IDPs do not have access to a stable form of income, with many complaining of no livelihood at all in the North West. 47% of IDP households reported not generating any form of income in the month preceding assessment, and 20% were relying on seasonal forms of labour, including agricultural, skilled wage and low skilled wage labour to generate an income. But due to the crisis they had to abandon all these livelihood operations due to lack of security and the rampant killings, burning, lootings and destruction. Consequently, 27% of households reported that they cannot afford all of their basic needs –amongst this subset of households, food was the need most reported as unaffordable .The proportion of economically inactive households was highest in Mankon (56%), Nkwen (44%), all predominantly IDPs where the availability of jobs is scarce. 75% of the households in the affected communities have at least 1 meal a day, 25% of the households can afford 3 meals but not balanced. Thus a critical need for food assistance. Employment rates for males aged 18-59 is 54%. 46% of females aged 15-17 years were reportedly employed. Multi-Cluster Needs Assessment for Internally Displaced Persons in Mankon and Nkwen –October 2019, Total employment rates in the month before assessment of IDPs access to jobs appears to be particularly difficult for the IDP community as a direct result of displacement and their status as IDPs. In fact, 47% of households across all assessed host communities reported that they have unemployed members who are looking for work. The main obstacle cited for not being able to be employed was increased competition, (79%) as a result of swelling populations looking for livelihoods in areas hosting IDPs. 21% also reported that other IDPs work for less remunerations. Looking at average incomes, IDPs residing in more urban environments were found to be economically unstable. Needs Assessment for Internally Displaced Persons in Bamenda–October 2019 with limited access to livelihoods over the course of the survey. Majority relied primarily on charitable donations (51%) and a sizeable proportion also resorted to debt (49%). Particularly high rates of debts were identified in the mankon and Nkwen neighborhood. Rent was reported by the majority of households in debt as one of main reasons for borrowing money, especially Mankon (60%) and Nkwen (40%). The large majority of households are in debt to friends and relatives either in the host or IDP communities. This is an indication that many have exhausted borrowing money as a coping strategy and are forced to move to lower quality shelters. Overall the high rate of coping strategies across Bamenda raises concerns because facing continuous displacement many households will soon exhaust all coping strategies available to them once they have crossed this threshold, their vulnerability and inability to meet basic needs is likely to increases sharply. Thus empowering them through poultry farming to improve on their livelihood and nutrition is of great importance to ASWEDO.
