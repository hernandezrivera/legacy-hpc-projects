Project Summary

The project aims to increase access to integrated multi-sectoral GBV/SGBV services for women and girls, including harmonized evidence Based Protection Monitoring in MMC, Bama (Banki) & Gwoza LGAs of Borno state. In 12 months’ time and with a budget of $400,000, COWACDI will be carrying out this project targeting 21,452 direct beneficiaries (male, female, boys and girls) prioritizing Persons with Disability (PoD) with an objective to enhance a robust reduction on the risks of Sexual and Gender Based Violence and Protection Right Restoration through strengthening community structures for GBV & SGBV prevention and response, provision and distribution of 250 dignity kits through the utilization of GBV Sub-Sector established referral pathway, support integration of 3 GBV toll free lines together with GBV Case management and referral, supporting the coordination of the GBV Sub-Sector, capacity building for GBV actors and evidence community base monitoring and reporting in camps and host communities, basically targeting vulnerable populations in IDP camps and host communities in Maiduguri (MMC), Gwoza and Banki(Bama) with the aim of reducing suffering and vulnerability of persons affected by crisis and addressing Protection risks/concerns for women, girls, men, boys and persons living with disability. COWACDI will construct and furnish 2 Safe spaces in Gwoza and Banki to provide a secure environment for women and girls to access integrated daily GBV case management services. The project will also collaborate with Borno State Government through the building the capacity of 30 staff from relevant Ministries such as Women Affairs & Social Development & Humanitarian, 20 frontline GBV actors on GBV/SGBV core concepts to enable them respond adequately. The project will provide Skills acquisition and empowerment for 80 vulnerable/physically challenged women & girls in Banki and Gwoza, it will also commemorate 16 days of activism against GBV in MMC. 1 - Needs

The major humanitarian crisis in Nigeria’s North-East states (Borno, Adamawa and Yobe) has persisted in 2021: the continuing conflict will still severely affect millions of people in 2021, subjecting them to displacement (new or continued), impoverishment and threat of violence. What started as a localized insurgency more than eleven years ago has turned into a regional armed conflict with no clear end in sight. Some 1.9 million people in the Lake Chad Basin areas of the neighboring countries of Cameroon, Chad and Niger need humanitarian aid. However, the insurgency’s effects in Nigeria are the greatest, with 8.7 million people in need. The humanitarian crisis in north-east Nigeria remains one of the largest and most severe in the world today (OCHA, March 2021). Borno State has the highest number of Internally Displaced Person’s in the North East due to the multiple security hotspots which has led to the displacement of over 2.5 million people predominantly who are women and girls. This widespread insurgency and displacement has been accompanied by a large number of SGBV cases due to the rising number and influx of internally displaced persons (IDPs) from liberated areas following the military operations in Borno states (UNHCR report 2018). UNHCR SGBV report for 2020, shows IDP returnees from the recently liberated areas continue to suffer due to fresh attacks in their LGAs particularly in Borno State. The situation continues to worsen for new arrivals in many camps across the north-east region particularly in the hard to reach LGAs in Borno-majority of whom are women and children; they are confronted with inadequate food and non-food Items, shelter, insufficient psychosocial support, medical supplies and lack of critical protection materials, exposing them to risks of survival sex and sexual exploitation. The study further shows denial of resources is being more reported as the most widespread form of SGBV in Borno state, and represents 32% of identified SGBV incidents in 2019. Women, especially heads of households and girls, are at particular risk within the current environment due to inadequate access to livelihood and socio-economic support as part of the multi-sectoral response. Thus, they are more prone to survival sex as a coping mechanism usually in exchange for food, money and other necessities. The population has been exposed to increasing incidences of sexually transmitted infections including HIV, unwanted pregnancies, and obstetric fistula caused by sexual violence. Overall, this has led to poor sexual and reproductive health outcomes. Sexual abuse and other violations against women and children are widespread inside and outside of IDP camps cannot be overemphasized, including a culture of impunity by perpetrators contributes to the continued violations. CARE Rapid Gender Analysis presented in September, 2020 indicates that due to the pervasive patriarchal Context of Borno State, men’s incomes have been adversely affected as they were primary bread winners. Additionally, because of women needing permission from men to go out, those with businesses have also taken a hit as fears of contracting the virus (COVID-19) coupled with https://projects.hpc.tools/project/187178/view 2/9 12/12/22, 8:33 AM HPC - Project Module: View Project increased domestic work has negatively affected women’s income generating activities. Hence there is need for skills acquisitions programmes to support women and girls source of income. The Camp Management bi-weekly tracker report by the CCCM/Shelter/NFI sector for 01 – 15 September 2021 witnessed torrential rains and windstorms that damaged IDP dwellings, thereby increasing shelter and non-food items (NFI) needs. 14,456 are completely damaged and 198,852HH in managed camps need complete NFI kits. Furthermore, 1,231 showers and 4,595 latrines are damaged while 22% (6,035) of latrines need dislodgement in 17 LGAs these include Bama as part of the affected LGAs.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

COWACDI will employ the following system to prioritize the dignity of project beneficiaries. I. Conscientiously carryout activities using an approach that aims at spiritedly involving individuals (male & female) and communities to realize respect for rights in safety and dignity at all times. II. Empower communities and individuals (male & female) to identify and strengthen risks prevention and response strategies to increase access, safety, respect with dignity. III. Develop local community structure capacity, engaging leaders (male and female) and local networking to strengthen community-based protection mechanisms, to ensure equal and impartial access to services. An open and transparent selection process will be implemented in collaboration with the GBV and Protection referral pathways. COWACDI will also monitor all activities using the field officers & volunteers and conduct PDM. For other participants COWACDI will collaborate with community-based structures and the state government to identify the most vulnerable such as the physically challenged persons in a bid to ensure transparency, protection mainstreaming. The protection team will also put in place self-protection capacities such as empowerment programs for survivors of SGBV, peer and family support groups, community awareness and legal support; survivors of SGBV are also provided with legal counselling, legal rights advice as well as access to justice. All these are complementary in building resilience and self-protection capacities, supporting participants in claiming their rights in safety and dignified manner.

9.1a - Specify (and give URL link if possible to) the relevant needs assessment document.

An assessment was conducted by COWACDI from June – September 2021, in Gwoza, Banki & Monguno, with the objective of analysing different GBV risk, needs, capacities and coping strategies of PoCs experience and adopt during crisis. Results indicate a high level of SGBV and Protection cases prevalent, affecting mostly women and girls. Women especially widows, single or divorced and physically challenged are either sexually exploited, harassed or abused as they seek means of livelihood, haven adopted certain Survival or negative coping mechanism such as exchange of sex for money, food, shelter, benefits and other survival needs and often experience forced marriages. Also, the reintegration of surrendered armed groups, released captives and Borno State Government resumption of IDP Camp closure and facilitated relocation and returns of IDPs has further increased the level of cases witnessed. The assessment also revealed certain GBV and Protection responses carried out by other Humanitarian actors (GISCO, DRC, FHI 360) and host government, but the gaps and needs identified calls for timely intervention. In response, COWACDI organized a public information and awareness intervention to enlighten the community members, community leaders and local authorities reaching 7500 women and 5500 men. Similarly, COWACDI has implemented GBV projects in MMC and Jere LGAs of Borno State with 3800 women & girls reached through interventions such as NFIs (dignity kits) distribution; sensitization and awareness (5000 women and girls reached), 300 economic empowerments, 250 with capacity building. Also, in collaboration with Government and other GBV actors, COWACDI participated in addressing the gaps of poor access to case management services for GBV survivors. In the past two years we have produced and disseminated 885 mass communication materials and messages and reached about 35000 women, girls, men and boys with GBV behavior change messages.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Participants and affected population have always been involved from the project design phase through the following: (i) community engagement and (ii) assessments. Information feeding this proposal is being derived from several interviews, focus group discussions (FGDs), and one-on-one interactions with some sampled community members/stakeholders where feedbacks were documented, analysed on project implementation gaps discovered the needs assessment as well. Secondary data of needs assessment and gaps from participants were derived from reviews of publications of GBV and General Protection Technical Working Group Sub-Sectors as well as other partners working on GBV and General Protection programs in the field. Different consultations will be done during the project implementation with representatives and leaders and other relevant stakeholders to make inputs, recommendations, and proffer solutions on implementation strategies at the community level on activities that will support the impact and sustainability. Periodic assessments will be conducted as well as post distribution monitoring (PDM) for feedback from the participants to determine the level of participant’s satisfaction of the intervention as well as provide feedback for program improvement. Evaluation of information will be used by COWACDI management to revise implementation plans and to monitor improvement. Information sharing with the project participants is vital in the implementation of these projects as monitoring and data collection will be led by project volunteer’s resident within the target communities. This project having mainstreamed Protection & GBV is built on the principle of DO-NO-HARM. Project staff and volunteers will be trained on GBV, and Protection principles which will include ethics in intervention programming.
