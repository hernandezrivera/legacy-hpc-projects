Project Summary

In line with the results of the Multisector Initial Rapid Assessment (MIRA), Emergency Food and Security Assessment, and the Cadre Harmonise. To save lives and protect the food security and nutrition status these vulnerable people, WFP will provide immediate humanitarian food assistance to the targeted 107,000 (67,000 CBT and 40,000 in-kind) IDPs and local vulnerable host populations (63,200 in the NW and 43,800 in the SW) in their various locations in urban, semi urban and bushes. The modality for transferring food aid to beneficiaries will be mostly cash (CBT) through unconditional monthly cash distributions to support vulnerable households' access to adequate and nutritious food, thus preventing them from adopting negative coping practices. The monthly cash transfer ration is 9,500 FCFA per person to beneficiaries who are entirely dependent on humanitarian food aid. A further 30% of beneficiaries will be assisted in-kind via monthly food distributions. The full ration of the in-kind distributions will provide 2,100 kcal energy per day per person for the beneficiaries who entirely rely on humanitarian food assistance The daily food rations will comprise 370g cereals, 120g pulses 40g vegetable, and 5g iodized salt. Mechanisms will be put in place to assess the evolution of household needs to ensure a gradual approach from one form of assistance to another.
During project implementation, regular monitoring activities will be carried out during food distribution, and monthly data will be collected against performance indicators for reporting through WFP's COMET system. In line with WFP's responsibility, affected populations, targeted vulnerable IDPs and local populations will be closely involved in the various stages of the project, including the targeting process. The people concerned will be regularly informed of project activities and plans. Through complaints and feedback mechanisms, they will freely express their opinions and concerns about project implementation. In line with WFP's responsibility to the affected populations, beneficiaries' complaints are treated with respect, and their opinions are considered in project adjustments. The WFP, in collaboration with its partners, will carry out assessments to identify and update the list of the most vulnerable IDPs and local populations to be considered in projects, based on vulnerability criteria.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Since 2016, the North-West and South-West regions of Cameroon have been in the grips of a crisis. The presence of State Security Forces (SSF) is concentrated along the main roads and cities, leaving many rural areas under the control of Non-State Armed Groups (NSAGs). Civilians are thus exposed to risks arising from regular clashes between SSF and NSAGs as well as armed violence among rival armed groups. High levels of criminality and impunity lead to a climate of insecurity with civilians and humanitarian workers facing risks of abduction/ kidnapping for ransom, assault, unlawful detention, carjacking, and threats of violence. The conflict and clashes between SSF and NSAGs and NSAG attacks were responsible for 90 per cent of the displacement before 2021. The weekly Monday’s lockdowns enforced by NSAGs particularly affect the civilian population, limiting movement and hence economic and agricultural activities. Lockdown days also cause substantial interruptions in humanitarian operations and are not limited to one day, as evidenced by a three-week lockdown imposed by NSAGs in August and September 2022 which affected humanitarian operations. While some humanitarian partners were able to continue the provision of limited assistance, including through phone services, most activities and services requiring physical presence or movement of population were put on hold. This led to almost 200,000 people not receiving timely in-kind and voucher/cash food assistance, over 125,000 persons being prevented from accessing primary health services, and the suspension of community mobilization activities, among others. Some schools were attacked for having started the school year as per the Government calendar. The continuous high levels of insecurity in the North-West and South-West regions, the rise in conflicts over natural resources (water, pasture, etc.), and floods continue to have a severe impact on food security, leading to population displacements. The Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM), as well as WFP assessment, reported an increase in food insecurity and the deterioration of livelihoods as a result of multiple population displacements, but also the continued deterioration of livelihoods that the economic sector in the crisis regions suffers year after year. According to March 2024 Cadre Harmonisé realized by Government and partners in all the 10 regions, the NWSW regions have about 927,395 persons in phase 3 to 5 of the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC). 03 of the 07 divisions in NW and 05 of the 06 divisions in SW are under food insecurity crisis phase.
