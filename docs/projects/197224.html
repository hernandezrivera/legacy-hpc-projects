Project Summary

The proposed action aims to improve access to quality health services to girls, boys, adolescents and Youngs affected by lake chad crisis in the far north region specially IDP, Refugees and host communities. The objectives of this intervention is to ensure increased access to information about SRHR for adolescents through strengthening the community protection mechanism, provide support to adolescents and young who are disproportionately affected by emergencies and crises due to their development, dependence on adults, limited participation in decision making, lack access to SRHR information to achieve their sexual and reproductive health and rights; organise the capacity of service providers and health structures about adequate attitudes and skills on adolescent- and gender-responsive SRH service provision; support parents and caregivers as well as members of the project's target communities to be able to access quality care, information and services for their children in accordance with gender standards; ensure that children, adolescents and young people receive comprehensive, high-quality sexuality education in safe and non-violent learning environments. By Considering the socio-cultural factors and gender inequalities in the Far North region, the project plans to increase advocacy efforts to promote the rights of children, especially those of young girls who are exposed to forms of discrimination and gender inequality.
The project targets both younger adolescent girls (10-14 years) older adolescent girls (15-17 and 15-24 years) affected by the conflict.

1 - Besoins / Needs

In 2021 the Far North region remains the target of attacks and incursions by non-state armed groups. Ongoing hostilities have resulted in the forced displacement of 107,180 households of 582,944 people 117,099 refugees, 341,535 internally displaced persons, and 124,310 returnees, resulting in the abandonment of some villages by their residents. Children are the category most affected by forced displacement, representing 64% of the total displaced population. The number of internally displaced persons has increased from 32,186 at the end of 2020 to 3,415,35 in October 2021.The affected population is regularly subjected to armed attacks, kidnappings, looting and destruction of property and infrastructure. Protection risks are often different, depending on gender, age and diversity. Girls and women remain particularly vulnerable to violence including sexual and gender-based violence due to the combined effect of pre-existing cultural and traditional discriminatory norms and practices. In this case, girls learn about sexual and reproductive health from their husbands after marriage, and husbands remain the primary source of information about contraception. Early or unwanted pregnancies are also linked to the practice of early and forced child marriage. Fathers marry off their daughters early as a protective mechanism out of fear of early pregnancy and the stigma of pregnancy outside of marriage. Adolescent face many barriers to accessing information and services relating to their sexual and reproductive health, include limited availability of adolescent-friendly services, lack of support from their families and communities, stigma, as well as lack of privacy and confidentiality of services. In addition, adolescents and young people are often not aware of existing SRH services, have limited knowledge about their sexual and reproductive health and rights. Mistrust of SRH services and a lack of confidentiality causes many to seek help only when they are in desperate need of care. Adolescent girls (10-19 years) are particularly affected by SGBV in intervention areas due to the insecurity and displacement and engaging in transactional sex to meet their basic needs. They need access to life-saving information and services, and should be educated about the possible consequences of SGBV and how these can be averted earler. Youth (15-24) are at elevate risk of rape, sexual exploitation, early or forced marriage, sexual abuse, unwanted pregnancies, SRHR as well joining insurgent BH group. The youth and especially girls often completely excluded from decision making, they are not consulted and involved in issues that impact their life and their communities thereby creating a sense of exclusion and lack of belonging. Youth need information about, and access to, decision-making spaces, the skills and capacities to navigate the socio-cultural barriers that limit their participation. Parent and care givers need information to accompany and support their children referral of sexual health problems. To better address these different needs, ensure quality health services to girls, boys, adolescents and Youngs in the different target divisions and reached our main results, we will carry out specific activities based on the indicators identified below. The project shall target the rights holder in their diversity, to increase their knowledge on SRHR, gender equality and inclusion, and empower them in making informed decisions on their SRHR. We will also empower them in risks identification, self-protection, as well as equipping them with skills for group mobilization for change to promote demand for and access to SRHR services from relevant service providers and institutions. These capacities include how to anticipate, absorb, adapt to, and transform in the face of different shocks and stresses. We will work with community/traditional/religious leaders to improve their understanding of gender and inclusion dynamics in the realization of SRH rights.
