Project Summary

Gonoshasthaya Kendra (GK), a national NGO specializing in primary, secondary, and tertiary health services, has played an active role in responding to the Rohingya crisis since 2017 with substantial financial and technical support from the German Federal Foreign Office (GFFO) via Malteser International. The proposed project is aiming to improve the health and well-being of vulnerable communities in Rohingya camps and the host community in Cox's Bazar. Emphasizing equitable access to essential primary and secondary healthcare services, the project will sustain its two basic health posts in Camps 1E (UID- 007) and Camp 11 (UID-112), aligning with Joint Response Plan (JRP) objective 1. Furthermore, the project will contribute to JRP objectives 3 by promoting health and well-being at the individual and community levels through outreach activities. In line with JRP objective 2, the project incorporates emergency preparedness and response measures to provide immediate support to affected communities. Beneficiary Overview: The project will directly impact 49538 individuals, including 39630 refugees. Specifically, 9908 people will benefit from the sustained operation of the 2 health posts in Ukhiya. Project will support strengthening 5 community clinics in Rajapalong and Palongkhali Unions.
The project outcomes aligned with JRP objectives:
1.Preventive Health Measures: Focuses on reducing transmittable and non-transmittable diseases, 2. Curative Health Measures: Aims to enhance well-being and address transmittable and non-transmittable diseases through comprehensive healthcare services, 3.Timely Emergency Response: This outcome strives for promptness and the enhancement of capacity in responding to any emergency that may impact the community.
Outcome 1: Activities
1.SRH Services (ANC & PNC): Qualified midwives and paramedics will continue providing Sexual and Reproductive Health services in two health facilities, adhering to essential health service packages. 2.Nutrition Counseling for Infants and Children under 2: Trained counselors, supervised by a nutritionist, will conduct IYCF education sessions in two health facilities to prevent malnutrition.3.Health Education and Hygiene Promotion: Over 75 Community Health Workers/Outreach Workers will promote health, hygiene, and nutrition through awareness and education sessions. Home visits and group sessions targeting various demographic groups, including pregnant and lactating women, adolescent girls, elderly individuals, people with disabilities, and local leaders will be conducted following health sector guidelines.
Outcome 2: Activities: 1. Continued Operation of 2 Health Posts: The medical teams will persist in delivering integrated primary healthcare services at the two health posts six days a week, adhering to the Minimum Package of Essential Health Services. Patients will receive medical consultations from qualified doctors and medical assistants, undergo necessary medical tests, and obtain prescribed medicines free of charge from the health posts. 2.Nutritional Screening: Biometric screening for nutritional deficiencies, specifically MAM and SAM, will be conducted in two health posts, with identified cases referred to specialized centers. 3. MHPSS Services: The provision of MHPSS services will be available in all two health facilties. Qualified psychologists will provide psychosocial counseling through individual and group sessions, with severe cases referred to secondary healthcare facilities.
Outcome 3: activities: 1. Contingency plan and Stockpiling: The project, recognizing its susceptibility to various disasters, ensures a timely and flexible humanitarian emergency response. Collaborating closely with other humanitarian actors, the project maintains a stock of essential relief items for an immediate response. 2.Staff capacity building: Staff undergoes training in emergency procedures, participates in regular simulation exercises, and refresher training.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The Rohingya people have suffered targeted persecution in Myanmar's Rakhine State for decades, leading to the consistent forced migration of Rohingya across the border into Bangladesh. Large-scale displacement events occurred in 1978, 1992, 2012, and 2016 due to violence in Rakhine State. The most significant wave of displacement took place in August 2017. As of July 31, 2023, approximately 962,416 Rohingya refugees are officially registered through the Government-UNHCR joint exercise, residing in thirty-three overcrowded camps designated by the Government of Bangladesh in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazilas, part of the Cox’s region. Hosting such a large number of refugees significantly impacts the lives and livelihoods of the host community, resulting in a demographic imbalance.Rohingya refugees in Bangladesh rely entirely on humanitarian assistance for food, education, health, and other life-saving essential services. It is crucial to ensure that essential health care services within and near the Rohingya camps remain accessible to host community members in Ukhiya and Teknaf. These health services must be available with equitable access to a range of preventative and curative services, including maternity care, sexual and reproductive health (SRH), MHPSS and gender-based violence (GBV) services. Emphasizing efficient primary healthcare services, covering communicable and noncommunicable diseases, emergency care, and robust referral systems, is crucial for reducing suffering, morbidity, and mortality.Maternal and child mortality is a concern in both the Rohingya and host communities. Efforts should be directed towards increasing facility-based deliveries, currently at only 48% according to MSNA 2023. The situation is almost similar in the host community, with only a 2% higher than the Rohingya community. Strengthening SRH services in health facilities through trained midwives and employing innovative community engagement approaches is essential. Antenatal care/postnatal care visits to health facilities for the early detection of complicated and high-risk pregnancies are low in the Rohingya community, necessitating measures to prepare mothers for facility-based delivery. Emphasis should also be placed on raising awareness in the community about potential post-delivery risks and complications to reduce prenatal maternal and infant mortality.Ensuring access to care for medical emergencies and life-saving secondary care services through medical referral systems remains a constant priority. Commitment and capacity are needed to address and mitigate the impact of health threats, encompassing both communicable and non-communicable diseases such as COVID-19, Diphtheria, Measles, Varicella, Cholera, and Dengue.There are still concerns raised by the community about the access and quality of services, as reflected in MSNA 2023. Continuity and strengthening access to life-saving interventions and the implementation of multi-sectoral integrated health approaches are paramount. This includes reinforcing routine health services in health facilities, supporting vaccination campaigns, and bolstering epidemiological surveillance to prevent outbreaks of communicable diseases.Access to health facilities for persons with disabilities is reported as a challenge, with higher reported difficulties among the host community compared to refugees.Mental health is a significant issue in both the Rohingya and host communities, with 38% of households in the Rohingya community and 33% in the host community reporting signs of psychosocial distress or trauma (MSNA).To ensure efficient and quality service functionality, health service providers need support for staffing needs, capacity building through training, and the provision of essential resources

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The proposed project is fully aligned with the Rationalization approach aims to ensure that all Rohingya refugees in Bangladesh have equitable access to all basic services in a predictable, efficient, and timely manner, and that the humanitarian community is more transparent and accountable. The project will work in close collaboration with the Government, UN, NGOs and donor community together with the relevant Sectors and Working Groups to avoid gaps and overlaps. Services will be provided following the relevant standard package of services to all refugees approved and agreed by the relevant Sector. GK is committed to the health sectors integrated approach aiming to improve (a) inter-sectoral camp-based coordination, (b) service delivery to refugees and host communities, and (c) maintaining relations with the Government agencies in particular, with CiCs in the respective camps. GK as one of the key health sector partners fully aware of avoiding working in the same block undertaking the same activities with other active partners in same blocks. As specialized organization in health sector, the project approach incorporated protection, MHPSS, nutrition within the proposed health care services. GK has strategies partnership with the other NGO specialized in providing family planning services from the same facilties. GK has been implementing 3 health posts in camp 1E, camp 11 and camp 22 since 2018, has been maintaining strong relationship with the refugee and host community and the government. Current funding will be partially covering the 2024 cost with a similar scale and has a possibility of extending the funding for another phase from GFFO through the Malteser International, Germany. GK is also a key partner of UNHCR since beginning of the influx and the trusted partner of the health, sector and nutrition sector, SHR working groups, adolescent working groups, PSEA network and outreach working group CHWG), MHPSS working Group etc.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

GK adheres to protection mainstreaming as the process of promoting meaningful access, safety and dignity of people at risk through its humanitarian interventions. In designing and implementing programs protection risks and potential violations are taken into consideration. To mainstream protection, GK try to explore who is at risk, from what or whom as well as why, and the consequences its actions may have. This includes knowing how and where to refer people in need for specialist support to prevent or recover from exploitation, as well as understanding when, how, and to whom to refer specialized protection issues. GK considers the elements of protection principles in all its humanitarian response program: 1. Prioritize Safety and Dignity, and Avoid Causing Harm: Prevent and minimise as much as possible any unintended negative effects of its intervention, which can increase people’s vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. 2. Meaningful Access: Arrange for people’s access to assistance and services in proportion to need and without barriers. Pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services. 3. Accountability: Set-up appropriate mechanisms, through which affected populations can address concerns and complaints. 4. Participation and Empowerment: Support the development of communities’ and individual capacities and assist people to claim their rights, including health and awareness. Through the incorporation of protection principles into program, GK ensures that its activities target the most vulnerable, enhance safety, dignity, and promote and protect the human rights of the beneficiaries without contributing to or perpetuating discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect and exploitation.

3.3 - Age & Disability

Age and disability are systematically considered throughout the project cycle, from survey to evaluation, and are also reflected in the project's MEAL plan. To support the processes, Standard Operating Procedures (SOP) and checklists are prepared to identify barriers and enabling factors for accessing the various services. Services are designed to support target groups regardless of ethnicity, status, disability, age and gender. Equal treatment for all is the main motive of health services. Privacy during medical examinations is maintained in accordance with cultural norms. Where necessary to reach populations with limited mobility, health interventions include mobile clinics. Emergency referrals are supported by a liaison person who provides administrative support to facilitate access. The community outreach component is designed to promote equity and access through home visits. The staff are sensitized to ensure that people with disabilities and the elderly are identified and can access services when needed. Home-based care for persons with disabilities is provided by a mobile medical team. If there is no caregiver in the family who can collect the medicines from the health facility, they will be delivered by volunteers.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

GK will incorporate the feedback into its strategies as well as in the collective response to the crisis. It will put in place innovative approaches to joint feedback mechanisms that will reinforce transparency, mutual accountability and have a positive impact. As part of giving account, GK will share information in an effective and transparent way across communities. This will include information about the organization and project, GK’s roles and responsibilities, about entitlements and targeting criteria, about how to provide feedback or how to raise complaints. Communities view on the appropriateness and quality of the service or response will be consistently sought. GK will be involved in consulting communities on what they think about the quality of the response. GK has been working in the FDMN camps in Cox’s Bazar since the end of 2017 and the main activities are, therefore ongoing activities that are continued and known by the population. GK's health services have a very good reputation in the camps and are prioritized. In order to incorporate the expectations and needs of the target group into the project planning, the experiences from the previous project will be used, e.g. through feedback, review of the services offered, discussions with the communities and the local leadership structures (Majhis). Community outreach is an integral part of the project to ensure feedback and complaints from the target group and host communities during project implementation. It is also planned to use the quarterly meetings with people in the community as a channel for complaints and feedback. Each health centre also has an information board in the entrance area, which includes the services offered as well as the telephone number of the clinic manager, who can be called in case of emergencies or complaints.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Gonoshasthaya Kendra (GK) is one of the members of the PSEA network and has a zero tolerance for sexual exploitation and abuse. GK does not tolerate its staff, associates, partners or any other representatives associated with the delivery of its work to engage in any form of sexual exploitation or abuse. GK is committed to protecting all communities that the organization works with from sexual exploitation and abuse at all times. The policy ensures that all GK staff, associates, and partners are aware of their role and responsibilities in keeping communities and stakeholders safe from any form of sexual exploitation or abuse. The policy clarifies definitions and responsibilities regarding prohibited behaviour and the associated PSEA procedures outline the reporting and investigation processes.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The project locations are regularly affected by (natural) disasters such as cyclones, floods, fire, epidemics or violent conflicts. GK will therefore work closely with other humanitarian actors through clusters and adhoc working groups to support emergency coordination and ensure complementarity between organisations. GK will respond flexibly to meet needs through the most appropriate modalities, i.e. cash, in-kind contributions or increased provision of services, depending on the context and availability of the required commodities in the local market. Emergency goods are procured and stored in advance, and emergency aid is provided with the modality possible in the contextIn order to be able to respond as quickly as possible in the event of a disaster, a stock of essential relief items (e.g. non-food items (NFI), hygiene kits) will be maintained throughout the life of the project to ensure an immediate response capability. In addition to contingency stocks, a financial reserve will be available for emergency response activities tailored to specific needs, either in the form of cash, or in-kind contributions.
