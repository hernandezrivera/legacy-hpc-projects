Project Summary

As the conflict remains protracted in Eastern Ukraine, it is vital to invest in the resilience of populations affected by the conflict and at risk of humanitarian disaster. With 3.4 million people in need of humanitarian aid and noting the potential negative impact from a major prolonged disruption to local infrastructures and systems, compounded by the risk of COVID-19 infection which requires access to water and hygiene for effective prevention and control, the risk of humanitarian disaster in conflict-affected areas is high.
Currently, millions of Donbas residents who are dependent on central water systems are at risk if/when critical infrastructure fails. Since 2014, critical water and sanitation facilities close to the line of contact (LoC) have been under constant attack, disrupting access to water for millions of people on both sides of the LoC, with 65 incidents affecting the utility supply in 2019 and 57 incidents in 2020 in the first 7 months of the calendar year. Notably in July 2020, 3 pipelines from the Siverskyi Donets Donbas channel, which supply water to 3.2 million people, were severely damaged as of a result of shellings (WASH, 2020). Water safety risks stemming from damage to infrastructure due to conflict are compounded by the consequences of the global pandemic. COVID-19 has heightened the sensitivity and urgency of securing clean and reliable water.
In addition to problems related to large networks, which require regional and/or national action, municipal/communal water and wastewater systems are also in disrepair. ACTED found that several regions in the area, incl. settlements in the Bakhmutski and Popasna raions, are at high risk of experiencing dysfunctions in their sewage systems, and water treatment and pumping station buildings continue to need support to operate at satisfactory capacity in most raions in the area (ACTED, WRA, 2020). Considering difficulties in undertaking adequate repairs of water supply systems or damaged power lines, local authorities and the humanitarian community have had to fill the gap in water provision through water trucking, while communities consume water from non-potable sources. The impact of water-related risks is also compounded by state responders’ limited capacities and planning, partly due to insufficient resources.
In response, ACTED will implement a water safety project to a) assess water risks in targeted communities close to the line of contact; b) develop – jointly with local authorities (LA), communities, and public utility companies (PUCs)– water safety plans; and c) provide targeted material support towards the implementation of the plans, and/or community-led projects which aim to increase the quantity or quality of water. Through the water safety planning approach, ACTED aims to ensure its humanitarian response leads to a strengthening of regional and local response capacity by continuously working in tandem with LA/other local actors to enable them to then take up responsibility for the response and continue to provide clean and safe drinking water to their constituents.
Beyond water, COVID-19 transmission mitigation measures also require soap and other hygiene items. Therefore, to complement its water safety interventions, ACTED will distribute hygiene kits to individuals and cleaning kits to institutions to support them to implement key infection prevention and control measures. Additionally, ACTED will operate and maintain hand washing stations in selected locations (or cover them through sanitizer distribution when capacities to maintain the station during and beyond project end are insufficient).
Leveraging in-country and global capacity, and based on needs and gaps identified, ACTED aims to – through a community-based approach – mitigate some of the WASH hazards that conflict-affected communities are vulnerable to and which could trigger or compound a cascading disaster, including the spread of communicable disease.

1 - Needs

Most of the municipal/communal water and wastewater systems in conflict-affected areas are dilapidated or broken, noting limited access for repairs and a systematic lack of investments and maintenance. Access to and quality of water in target areas has decreased since pre-conflict and in 2020, 18% of households within 5km of the LoC and 12% within 5-20 km of LoC reported at least one water shortage per week (REACH 2020). COVID-19 has made the need for reliable water systems even more urgent. Frequent handwashing is one of the best personal preventative measures to take against COVID-19 and the need for reliable water in health facilities treating COVID-19 afflicted patients is even more crucial. 29% of health facilities in conflict affected areas reported problems with their main source of water for cleaning, incl. for 2 of 18 COVID-19 designated hospitals (KAPA, REACH, 2020).Generally, populations in settlements closest to the LoC tend to rely on decentralized water sources given distance from centralized systems, and/or dilapidation of pipelines. In rural areas close the LoC, almost half of the population relies on dug wells (49%), 15% rely on piped water and 15% rely on tube wells/boreholes, 5% rely on water trucks and 4% rely on bottled water for their drinking water (REACH 2020). A little over one-third of households in small urban areas (39%) rely on piped water, 12% on dug wells, 10% on tube wells/boreholes, and 3% rely on bottled water. For non-drinking purposes, households living in areas close to the LoC rely mostly on piped water (63% within 5km, 71% within 20km), noting recurring water shortages given damage incurred by pipelines and limited access for repairs due to the conflict. Insufficient access to water has been a challenge for years in conflict-affected areas and COVID-19 has compounded the situation. Prior to the outbreak, 31% of residents in GCA already had problems with water supply, noting that need increases with proximity to the LoC (WASH Cluster Assessment, 2019). Clean water is of course vital to carrying out infection prevention and control (ICP) measures such as hand washing and cleaning surfaces. According to REACH KAPA 2020, 77% of people in GCA report they are protecting themselves from COVID-19 by washing their hands. However, 29% report some difficulty with hygiene in their household. Additionally, before the outbreak, 26% of GCA residents reported issues with sanitation (WASH, 2019) Issues of water safety/access impact men and women differently. Women are disproportionately heading households in GCA (71% female vs 29% male) meaning women bear the brunt of the effects of increased HH expenditures (up from 14 to 17 % in 2020) and daily water cuts (6%; REACH, 2020). Noting also low access to basic hygiene items, water cuts can affect women’s ability to address menstrual health needs, with women reporting unhygienic menstrual hygiene practices in 5% of HH (WASH, 2019). 71% of heads of household within 20km of the LoC are women (REACH, 2020). COVD-19 and the related socio-economic shock has further threatened women’s access to menstrual hygiene items, as they are often among the first household expense to be cut when resources are scarce.Impact of water/safety access also varies across age groups. The elderly may have more severe needs, noting that they tend to be less mobile and are more likely to have chronic illnesses or disability. Advanced age and chronic illness are high risk factors for COVID-19 related complications: 42% of elderly individuals report feeling either "extremely fearful" or "very fearful" about COVID-19 (KAPA, REACH, 2020).In addition to being consulted through the various needs assessments that informed the design of the project, particularly vulnerable groups (of diverse age, gender, socioeconomic status, incl. IDPs and people with unique needs) will be consulted during project implementation to ensure appropriateness and feasibility of the intervention.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

The project will increase water safety, incl. increase infection prevention and control as pertains to COVID-19 and contribute to the resilience of conflict-affected peoples. Water Risk Assessment (WRA):Water risks will be assessed, incl: availability, safety, and sustainability quantity/quality of water; need/feasibility of water treatment, whether bulk or at household level; social, political or legal factors affecting water sources. ACTED will use sensitive qualitative methods and enumerators will be trained so women of all ages/other vulnerable groups are comfortable sharing their perceptions, needs and priorities. Teams will be composed of men and women, FGDs with women will be moderated by women, and questionnaires will be tested for gender mainstreaming before data collection. Water Safety Plans (WSP):Working with LA/PUC, ACTED will develop evidence based WSP to ensure safety of drinking-water supplies. A community centered approach will be fostered, with local administration supporting coordination with PUC representatives and the public. ACTED will communicate to local authorities and PUCs involved that engagement with diverse age, gender, socioeconomic groups is a priority. ACTED’s approach will encourage ownership of the process and mitigation measures, esp. by responsible stakeholders who have the capacity to implement WSP. Once finalized, the WSP will be handed over to the local communities and ACTED will support their selected measures.Material support:ACTED will support WSP implementation and community-led projects, whose work plans and budgets have been evaluated and are reflected in the proposed budget, that aim to increase access to water. Location selection will be based on WRA and done jointly with stakeholders. Protection issues and inputs from affected groups will be considered. Material support could incl: partial rehabilitation of water treatment/source stations; provision of equipment for water access works. Joint implementation of these projects with existing water management structures will ensure that immediate local needs are meet and community involvement increases. ACTED will ensure that men and women from different age and socioeconomic groups, including IDPs and people with unique needs, are consulted during the process. ACTED will ensure that water points are well-lit and in safe areas to mitigate protection concerns. Water testing will be conducted at repair sites to evaluate the impact of the intervention.Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM):ACTED has established CRM across all projects to enable beneficiaries to report any abuse by staff and others. Staff are trained and beneficiaries are aware of the CRM through community meetings, visibility materials, etc. The CRM is available to people with disabilities through SMS, Viber and email. ACTED also facilitates exchanges through social workers and community leaders.COVID-19:As frequent handwashing and cleaning surfaces are among the most effective ways to reduce the risk of COVID-19 transmission, ACTED will provide blanket coverage of hygiene kits to selected settlements and will distribute cleaning kits to approx. 70 institutions, incl. schools and hospitals in the region. Additionally, information, education, and communication (IEC) materials with be delivered to individuals and institutions across the targeted area. The IEC will consist of printed materials to be displayed in public spaces as well as support public hotlines and other LA-owned response mechanisms. The aim is to hand them over to LA at the end of the project to transition responsibility to regional and LA enabling humanitarian exit from GCA. ACTED will operate and maintain hand washing stations in selected locations or distribute sanitizer when capacities to maintain the station are insufficient. As the project will provide blanket coverage to areas close to the LoC, it is expected that women (59%) and the elderly (34%) will most benefit from this intervention.
