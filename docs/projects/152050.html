Project Summary

In light of the dynamic context in Libya, maintaining the flow of updated evidence is essential to develop efficient and adequate programming to address the needs and vulnerabilities of affected populations. As various populations groups are typically assessed in silos in the Libyan context, REACH proposes to conduct an area-based city profiling providing a detailed analysis at community and city level of interactions between various population groups (including IDPs, returnees, hosts and migrants) and access to services. This approach appears particularly adapted and useful to allow for in-depth analysis of areas, of communities who define these areas including migrants, who are often assessed separately as well as provide more contextual analysis on the financial crisis in each area and how areas deal differently to the liquidity crisis.

Needs

As Libya is characterised by a liquidity and migration crisis, there is a need for a detailed area-based needs assessment to inform evidence-based programming. This can be achieved by providing humanitarian and stakeholders with a better understanding of vulnerabilities and methods of re-establishing basic service delivery across key hubs in Libya. Access to basic services and commodities in Libya is dictated to a significant extent by the availability and functionality of markets and services as well as the nationality and status of the people of concern. Little so far is understood of the impact of the crises on the Libyan society, at the community and city level in terms of service delivery and access, and the interactions between various populations groups including IDPs, returnees, hosts and migrants.

Activities

Interviews with Libyans, migrants, key informants in a city identified as high in need, based on consultations with local authorities, HCT, clusters and other stakeholders at national and sub-national levels, targeting will include settlements and locations of high priority relating to 1)severity of humanitarian needs, 2) Presence of particular vulnerable groups which access to services is particularly impeded such as migrants and refugees, 3) Level of disruption of access to basic services
