Project Summary

Somalia continues to experience the world’s most complex and longstanding humanitarian crises in which 2.6 million people are displaced and more than 42% of the population in urgent need of humanitarian assistance. Up to 2.1 million people are projected to face acute food insecurity (IPC3) or worse outcomes between October and December and around 850,000 children are projected to face acute malnutrition between July 2020 and June 2021 due to multiple risk factors including COVID-19 and its associated socio-economic impact, floods, Desert Locust upsurge and residual impacts of previous droughts. The 2020 Deyr (October-December) rains are forecasted to below average associated with La Niña conditions; if the forecast stands, Somalia is likely to face drought-like conditions and higher than normal temperatures in early 2021 based on past experience, exacerbating food insecurity in the country.
In light of the deteriorating food security situation, the IRC proposes food security and livelihood interventions to vulnerable multi-shock affected households in Puntland, Galmudug, Hirshabelle, Southwest and Banadir. The aim of the project is to improve vulnerable households’ access to food and other essential basic needs through appropriate modalities as dictated by markets, and to protect and restore livelihoods related to food and incomes through distribution of seasonally appropriate inputs and services.
The project will specifically target vulnerable groups such IDPs, urban poor and non-displaced rural households in crisis or worse outcomes who need immediate life-saving assistance to meet their basic food and non-food needs and avoid negative coping strategies. The project will be achieved through the following objectives:
Objective 1: Improved access to basic food and non-food needs for 2,800 vulnerable households in IPC 3 and 4 through provision of unconditional and conditional cash transfers. Under this objective, The IRC will provide multipurpose cash assistance to 2,300 vulnerable households facing severe food consumption gaps (IPC level 3 and 4) for 3 months to meet their immediate basic food and non-food needs and avoid negative coping strategies. Unconditional multipurpose cash assistance provides households with flexibility and the dignity of choice when exercising life-saving decisions that are best-aligned with their households. Multipurpose cash transfers will also have a direct bearing on strengthening local market capacities due to the increased purchase of basic needs following enhanced purchasing power of target beneficiaries. In addition, 500 households will be engaged in gainful temporary employment through cash for work involving rehabilitation of prioritized community infrastructures. CfW activities are intended to provide households with much needed cash injections to meet their basic foods while rehabilitating community assets and infrastructure that are vital for sustaining and restoring the livelihoods of the communities.
Objective 2: Productive livelihoods and livelihoods assets for 1,800 households are protected and/or restored through provision of seasonally appropriate inputs/services, skill training and income generation. Under this objective, the project will support 250 smallholder farmers with appropriate agri inputs (seeds, fertilizers, tractor ploughing hours) and farming tools needed to increase production both for domestic consumption and for commercial to increase household income. Appropriate engagement will be made with smallholder farmers accompanied by market assessments to understand inputs needed, availability and market prices to best design appropriate input distribution modalities. In addition, 1,550 poor pastoral households through animal treatment/vaccination campaigns to treat livestock against infectious and endemic diseases to protect livestock and improve animal health and productivity.

Project Activities

1.0 Provision of multi-purpose cash transfers to 2,300 (at least 50% female) vulnerable shock affected households in Bari, Nugaal, Mudug, Galgaduud, Hiiraan, Lower Shabelle and Banadir for a period of 3 months, with priority given to female-headed, disabled, elderly and child-headed households, as well as IDP households to enable them meet basic needs and avoid negative coping strategy. Transfer values will be based on recommended transfer values by the cash working group and will be coordinated with Cash working group to ensure interventions are coordinated with FSC and Cash working Group partners. Markets remain functional and are able to provide need basic commodities, but people lack the cash to purchase needed. The intervention will therefore provide unconditional cash transfers to provide beneficiaries with flexible options to purchase their most pressing needs. IRC will monitor markets and conduct post-distribution monitoring to understand market changes and beneficiary perception to provide timely corrective actions.1.1 Identification and engagement of 500 households in crisis and stressed situation through cash for work initiatives to create income opportunities during crisis, enhance their purchasing power and improve access to basic needs. The CfW interventions will not only improve household income but will also support target communities in rehabilitation of key communal infrastructures such water catchment sources, market access roads and public facilities-schools, clinics, community centers that are critical for early recovery and resilience. 2.0 Provision of agri inputs and farm tools to 250 smallholder farmers with appropriate agri inputs (seeds, fertilizers, tractor ploughing hours) and farming tools needed to increase production both for domestic consumption and for commercial to increase household income. Appropriate engagement will be made with small holder farmers accompanied by market assessments to understand inputs needed, availability and market prices to best design appropriate input distribution modalities. 2.1 Conduct 1 organized animal health campaigns to treat/vaccinate 120,000 livestock belonging to 1,550 poor pastoral households in pastoralist target areas of Bari, Nugaal, Mudug and Galgaduud to protect livestock and improve animal health and productivity. Treatment/vaccination campaigns will be coordinated with FAO and relevant ministries of livestock in Puntland and Galmudug to avoid overlap and duplication in treatment.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

A recent assessments carried out by the IRC in September 2020 market functionality and physical accessibility was overwhelmingly good, with 73% agreeing that they have functioning markets, easily accessible and restocked with all the basic commodities they require. The remain 27%, mainly in rural small villages, rely on main markets and incur significant transport expenses. Moreover, basic food commodities were reportedly readily available in the market, however, a steadily spike in the price of common staple foods were reported when compared with the previous year. The ease of restocking food supplies was found to take some time and on average was reported about 5-7 days. In addition, 85% of households surveyed have stated preference to unconditional cash over other modalities, 10% on food voucher and the rest 5% on livestock and agricultural support. More importantly, cash and vouchers are used in the country by different humanitarian actors and the cash and voucher modality is familiar to both humanitarian actors and service beneficiaries. This among other factors justifies the use of cash and over in-kind.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

Market functionality is a key determinant of any cash transfer programs. The project design will ensure that appropriate market assessments are done to inform the feasibility of cash before any cash intervention. The project will also routinely monitor market prices and will conduct post-distribution monitoring to understand market changes and beneficiary perception to provide timely corrective actions
