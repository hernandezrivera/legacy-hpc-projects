Project Summary

Activities: Acted will provide unconditional cash transfer to an estimated 3,067 households (approximately 18,400 individuals) in Idleb and Aleppo governorates following a sudden shock, delivered immediately following displacement caused by conflict, climate-related shock, or other types of natural or man-made disasters such as the eartquake that took place in february 2023. ACTED will provide emergency multi-purpose cash assistance for up to three rounds, with a first one-off 150 $ distribution. The 2nd and 3rd would be 150 $ each as per the latest guidance from the CWG. While Acted will aim at providing three rounds of MPCA to all targeted households, it should be noted that displacement movements following the earthquake are highly fluctuant. Cash will be provided to targeted households through a contracted Money Transfert Agency (MTA). The provided MPCA will facilitate households’ access to critical services, including healthcare, transportation and telecommunications, which are critical needs especially for vulnerable individuals with health and mobility issues.
The process followed to trigger a cash intervention will be as follows: - Step 1: Acted team deploys into a new area of displacement or underserved protracted displacement and conducts a rapid needs assessment including a rapid market assessment to assess the feasibility of a cash-based intervention (i.e. access and functionality); The RNA also includes a cash risk assessment. - Step 2: Based on the results, ACTED consults and coordinates with the FSL cluster, per the CWG guidance, to target the most vulnerable households, who are not covered with food assistance. - Step 3: ACTED carries out a rapid Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDM) within ten days to two weeks after each distribution.
ACTED will coordinate with the local authorities, humanitarian actors and clusters to discuss the best approach to adopt to respond to the needs and coordinate the action to bring all capacities together, ensure a timely response and avoid duplication. Considering the extreme vulnerability of the beneficiaries, ACTED will ensure that the distribution is organized in a timely manner after the shock. The distribution will be organized during the day in a safe and central location to avoid the beneficiaries carrying money with them on long distance. The distribution will be disaggregated between women and men as needed
Harmonised PDM tools : Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) databases are analyzed by an MEAL officer/manager, not involved in field operations. The MEALU performs quality and consistency checks on PDM reports in collaboration with AME HQ. PDM data is crosschecked with an analysis of the types of claims received through the complaints’ mechanisms on the field (complaints box, Acted accountability officer, complaint desks) or recorded through our free hotline and WhatsApp number, handled by Acted capital office and collected directly by accountability staff, and senior staff of MEAL.
Needs : While large-scale hostilities have subsided following the March 2020 Idleb ceasefire agreement, localized hostilities have continued throughout 2022. Households across the country face a gap between incomes and expenditures on essential needs, and this has worsened in 2023: at the end of the third quarter of 2022, the national average price of a WFP standard reference food basket reached a new record at SYP 329,526, up 40 % since the onset of the crisis in Ukraine and fourfold since two years. High inflation, currency depreciation, and increases in commodity prices continued to drive humanitarian needs in Syria (according to HNO 2024). This resulted in an increase in poverty and reliance on humanitarian assistance. PLEASE REFER TO SECTION 2.1 FOR THE REST OF THE NEEDS, RISKS AND MONITORING TOOLS

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Since 2023, the Syrian pound (SYP) has lost about half of its value against the US dollar (US$), averaging around SYP 14,200/US$ in the parallel foreign exchange market in November 2023. Affected populations cope with the purchasing of lower quality items, increasingly borrowing money, cutting down on non-food expenses, and skipping meals. The lack of ability to meet basic needs is evident despite 94% of households with an employed head of household. Most often, households cite the combined impact of high prices, lack of income as the main reasons for their inability to meet basic needs. 8% of HH are completely unable to meet, while 77% are insufficiently able to meet basic needs. According to REACH, in Greater Idleb, 94% of assessed communities reported insufficient income of households, citing rent, fuel, water trucking, food and health services as unaffordable. ACTED will target locations with severe food insecurity populations and conduct high-level coordination with the FSL cluster and other partners to get the green light before the intervention initiation based on the results of the Gap Analysis, need assessments and feedback from the community. Furthermore, ACTED will retain a continuous high level of coordination with the FSL cluster/CWG and acting agencies to ensure an efficient and harmonized regular food assistance response and no duplication of assistance in the areas of intervention. In this process, Acted takes into account the markets accessibility and functionality RiskCVA : Extortion of beneficiaries after distribution : All claims are reviewed by the MEAL department. In case of suspected fraud, the Investigation Unit at Acted HQ level is informed and supervises the investigation if one is warranted. Acted undertakes post-distribution monitoring including questions about possible extortion and taxation. Conflict escalation: movements are suspended in specific areas; relocation of staff to other offices to continue activities CRM inside Syria: stakeholders can safely provide feedback and report any project issues or concerns. Non-inclusion of highly vulnerable groups and minorities needs during project design/implementation Likelihood. The proposed intervention will be implemented in accordance with ACTED’s gender approach, which follows the Gender Handbook for Humanitarian Action reviewed in 2017 by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC). ACTED incorporates gender analysis and considerations into the full project management cycle, from project design to implementation and monitoring. Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse Likelihood: all ACTED staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct which directly refers to PSEA and GBV. As part of ongoing efforts to improve accountability, ACTED is committed to implement a Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its projects to allow beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on ACTED work, as that of the contractors.
