Project Summary

DRC proposes a comprehensive and targeted multi-sectoral response in government-controlled areas of Syria to save and sustain lives and alleviate suffering among the most vulnerable, in both the acute phase of a crisis and in protracted settings where needs are found to be equally severe. Communities will be targeted under multiple Results, where complex needs are identified across sectors, to ensure a holistic response. Through the inclusion of a Crisis Modifier, DRC will maintain the capacity to agilely respond to new or escalating crises that require immediate humanitarian action, informed by a sound risk analysis and contingency planning with pre-defined triggers in place. A total of 133 Partially Damaged Apartments (PDAs) will be rehabilitated and brought to Sphere standards in Hama (80 Karnaz), Rural Damascus (13 Maliha), and Dar'a (40 Dar'a) to ensure households are protected and have access to safe and dignified shelter solutions. Damaged WASH infrastructure that are essential to the survival and recovery of communities will also be rehabilitated to reduce morbidity and mortality associated with a lack of water suitable for human consumption and environmental and public health risks. This will include the rehabilitation of 1 water network in Aleppo (Maskana), 1 water network in Dar'a (Dar'a Al Balad), 1 water pumping station in Aleppo (Rasm Haram El-Imam), 1 borehole in Hama (Karnaz), 1 borehole in Dar'a (As-Sanamayn), and 1 sewage network in Rural Damascus (Harasta). DRC will also deliver Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) in communities acutely endangered by EO contamination, while training Community Focal Points (CFPs) to conduct further outreach as a sustainable and localized approach to risk education. DRC will directly deliver EORE for 2,200 individuals in Aleppo (600 Jebel Saman), Dar'a (1,000 Dar'a), and Hama (600 Karnaz). Meanwhile, the CFP component will take place in the same locations (2 CFPs per sub-district) to maximize reach and enhance sustainability. Finally, the collective response and operational space will be strengthened through continued support to the Damascus INGO (DINGO) Forum and joint protection advocacy initiatives.
The Action is informed by the findings of DRC’s Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) as well as secondary evidence, placing priority on underserved communities and areas categorized in severe or extreme inter-sectoral severity (HNO 2024). As part of DRC’s commitment to a protection-centric approach, Protection teams funded under complementary projects will conduct periodic FGDs and KIIs in the response locations to monitor protection risks and trends to inform a risk-sensitive design. Gender-segregated FGDs will provide qualitative insights into the perceptions, attitudes, preferences, and priorities of participants on various protection-related topics. Meanwhile, KIIs will allow DRC to capture more in-depth information of protection risks and needs in the target areas, without the social pressure or power dynamics of a group setting. DRC will monitor and evaluate activities regularly, using gender and age-disaggregated indicators and feedback mechanisms, to ensure that they are responsive and adaptive to the changing needs and preferences of women, men, girls, and boys.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

DRC conducted a MSNA in November and December 2023, employing a mixed-methods approach to capture diverse perspectives and insights on community needs. The MSNA draws on a combination of direct qualitative information gathered through 52 Key Informant Interviews (KIIs) and 19 Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) covering 38 communities in 18 sub-districts across Aleppo, Hama, Homs, Idleb, Dar’a, and Rural Damascus governorates, as well as direct observations and learning from DRC’s ongoing programming and long-term presence in the areas. This is complemented by qualitative and quantitative information from secondary sources, including population and Sector-specific data from UNOCHA. In January 2024, DRC also conducted preliminary environmental assessments across 7 sub-districts in Aleppo, Dar’a, Hama, and Rural Damascus utilizing a shortened version of the Nexus Environmental Assessment Tool (NEAT+) to identify environmental risks and mitigation opportunities associated with the planned Shelter and WASH interventions. Risk Mitigation: DRC will conduct security monitoring and analysis to anticipate access challenges. Mitigation measures will be taken in accordance with DRC’s Safety and Security Plan. DRC will remain abreast of the political situation at national and local levels, and will conduct Access Workshops between safety, programme, and support staff to identify key constraints and actions to be taken to overcome barriers. DRC’s global Humanitarian Access trainings will be conducted for frontline staff to navigate access risks. DRC will maintain minimum conditions that must be respected by SARC and authorities, including independent targeting and participant selection based on pre-defined criteria and a stringent verification process. Clear and transparent sensitization of selection criteria will be central to all engagement. DRC will adhere to best practices and internal control procedures to ensure all recruitment and procurement processes are strictly independent. BoQs for infrastructure include will include an amortization for inflation; and DRC will routinely monitor markets and exchange rates. A comprehensive procurement plan will be developed at project onset, and Supply Chain will maintain a pool of qualified suppliers throughout the Action. Contractor performance will be routinely monitored to inform corrective measures. Safeguarding measures are embedded in DRC policies (CoC, PSEA, Child Protection), procedures for recruitment of staff, CFM mechanism and reporting & investigation routines, as well as in the roles/responsibilities of senior management through to field staff. Staff are aware of their obligation to report suspected misconduct and to direct all SEA-related cases to the HQ-based CoC team for investigation and management. DRC will the provision of refreshers and trainings to all staff and contractors and will have signposting on reporting channels at project sites.
