Project Summary

The overarching objective is to scale up Sexual Reproductive Health integrated services in accessible Local Government Areas (LGAs) rebuilding and strengthening health systems, build capacity, provide sustainable SRH Services and Medical Care to Survivors of Sexual Violence in Line with the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) for Reproductive Health Services and the Nigeria Health Policy.

1 - Needs

The 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview identified 5,216,907 people in dire need of life saving essential health services. These include 1,304,227 women of reproductive age and 1,043, 382 sexually active men. Up to 156,510 women are currently pregnant and the same number expected to get pregnant within a period of one year; this will result in 208,677 live births with 23,477 facing complications and requiring emergency obstetric care services. Up to 31,302 of the pregnancies estimated to end in abortion and would require post abortion care (PAC) services; furthermore, 26,085 women of reproductive age are at risk of sexual violence and would require CMR services.There is an estimated 3,926,353 young people including adolescents in need of sexual and reproductive health services. According to the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) 2019, less than one primary health care (PHC) facility per 10,000 inhabitants is functional. 70% of 751 health facilities in Borno State, 55% of 1,120 health facilities in Adamawa State, and 31% of 593 health facilities in Yobe states are partially or not-functioning, especially in remote/rural areas. There are insufficient numbers of skilled and appropriately trained health care staff; health workers are also reluctant to work in remote and newly liberated local government areas (LGAs). Poor referral services further limits access to life-saving emergency maternal and newborn healthcare services. Most of the IDP camps are located in urban areas and all clients are expected to pay-out-of pocket in order to access healthcare. As a result, pregnant women would not have access to antenatal care, delivery care, emergency obstetric care, and postnatal care. The project will be implemented in 44 of the 65 local government areas of Adamawa, Borno and Yobe States. The project would primarily target women of reproductive age, sexually active men and young adolescents. The main strategic approach will be the minimum initial service package for reproductive health in emergencies (MISP) services. The key components include prevention and managing consequences of sexual violence, prevention and treatment of HIV and other sexually transmitted infections, preventing maternal and newborn death and illness, and strengthening of the health systems capacity for comprehensive sexual and reproductive health care. The Project activities would include: 1. training of health workers on provision of MISP 7 CMR services; 2. procurement and distribution of life saving RH kits; 3. procurement of essential maternal health medicines and equipment; 4. training of health workers on provision of quality family planning, antenatal care, obstetric care, postnatal including emergency care to mothers and newborn; 5. deployment of health workers on medical outreaches in remote/hard-to-reach communities and where existing health facilities are not functioning; 6. establish and strengthen referral networks for maternal and newborn emergencies; 7. provision of adolescent sexual reproductive health services through safe spaces; and 8. Mobilization and sensitization of communities for uptake of SRH services

2 - Activities

• Procure emergency RH kits for health facilities and essential maternal health medicines, other reproductive health medicines and supplies (haematinics, antihypertensive, multivitamins, antibiotics, anti-malarial, HIV test kits), consumables (gloves, syringes,gauzes, cotton wool, needles, disinfectants) and equipment (stethoscopes, weighing scales, BP apparatus) to support SRH service delivery outreaches• Procure essential maternal health medicines (hematinic, anti-hypertensives, multivitamins, antibiotics, anti malarials, HIV test kits), consumables (gloves, syringes, gauzes, cotton wool, needles, disinfectants) and equipment (stethoscopes, weighing scales, BP apparatus) to for health facilities and outreach service delivery points. • Distribute RH kits and supplies to health facilities and outreach teams Activity• Support the establishment/upgrading of comprehensive obstetric emergency care centers in general hospitals in Bama, Gwoza, Mobbar and Munguno local government areas of Borno State, Madagali and Mubi in Adamawa and Gujba and Gulani in Yobe• Conduct training and mentoring of health workers on minimum initial service package for RH in emergencies, clinical management of rape, family planning service provision and emergency obstetric care• Procure 4 ambulances for emergency obstetric referral transport Activity• Provide support for referrals from communities to primary health centers to comprehensive obstetric emergency centers• Conduct medical SRH outreaches (ANC, skilled delivery services including referrals for emergency obstetric care, treatment of STIs, family planning, PMTCT, treatment of maternal malnutrition, clinical management of rape, post-abortion care)• conduct CMR mapping to health facilities providing CMR Activity• Conduct community mobilization for uptake of SRH services • Establish and support appropriate community mechanisms for referrals to primary health and maternity centers.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

At inception, the community leaders, beneficiaries will be sensitized of the intervention through the existing UNFPA supported programmes in the area, as such they will have information on the scope of the project and anticipated benefits. This will support the mobilization of communities to utilize the project services. Government structures at State and LGA levels will be fully involved, having been briefed of this at the SRH working group meeting. After final approval, an inception meeting will be held to share the best approaches to implementation. IEC materials regarding the project will be developed in a community friendly nature and shared for wider dissemination of the project activities and expected benefits and outcomesThe SRH interventions shall address the needs identified by the IDPs during the needs assessment. The mobile medical outreach teams shall undertake community entry exercises that shall include the engagement of the IDP populations by the community mobilization officer. A critical element of the monitoring exercise to be undertaken by the state counterparts from the SMOH, SPHCDA and the SEMA shall be to assess the performance of the SRH interventions through key informant interviews with the IDPs and host communities and thereafter ensure that all concerns are addressed on relevant platforms and ensure compliance at relevant levels of the program management cycle.
