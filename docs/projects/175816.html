Project Summary

The Water, Sanitation and Hygiene needs for 2021 will majorly be driven by events happening in 2020. In 2020, many counties in South Sudan experienced unprecedented Flooding. Many parts of Koch and Fangak counties were affected. The flooding had a devastating effect on WASH infrastructure including water points and Latrines. Secondly was the outbreak and spread of the Covid-19 pandemic. This pandemic was first reported in South Sudan in April 2020 and has continued to spread to many parts of the country. This disease outbreak happened at a backdrop of an already weak health system. Even before Covid-19, water borne diseases including various forms of diarrhea were already on the rise. The health system cannot cope with treatment of the disease and therefore prevention remains the only viable option. With a weak economic state, vaccination against COVID-19 may not be feasible in the country for the rest of 2021T. All these events in 2020 occurred in a context of poor access to WASH services and a socio-cultural environment that is not very responsive to adoption of WASH interventions.
The WASH intervention provided by WRSS will be implemented as across cutting service provided alongside other services. The main purpose of this WASH intervention is to ensure increased access to WASH services for beneficiaries within the counties of Koch and Fangak. This Project will address improved access to water services through drilling of bore-holes using village drill. Cognizant of the fact that water provided through drilling may not be sufficient for all the needs, the project will continue to promote safe water treatment and storage through training and provision of WASH NFIs like Aqua tabs, buckets, jerricans and filter clothes. The project will also promote hand washing through provision of handwashing enablers like handwashing points and soap within the health and nutrition facilities and encourage beneficiaries to establish the same in their homes.
The project will also respond to sanitation needs through construction of latrines within the Health and Nutrition centers. At community level, the project will work with 'Good Examples' to promote construction and use of latrines for human waste disposal. The project will be implemented as an integration with Nutrition. At community level, the project will work with care groups who are care givers of children as well as Pregnant and lactating women admitted in the nutrition centers. Messaging on proper hygiene as well as Covid-19 Infection Prevention and Control measures will be communicated through these care groups. The WASH team will continue to follow up on the care groups to coach and mentor beneficiaries in adopting the hygiene and sanitation measures.
Throughout implementation, the project will address cross cutting themes including gender based violence and general protection concerns. The project will ensure all constructions for water points, latrines and other infrastructure take into consideration the needs of the most vulnerable in society including women, girls, elderly, disabled and children under five years. Community structures including water management committees will be established and trained to manage these resources in a sustainable manner but also ensure no harm caused by these interventions. Resources, particularly water points will be managed in a way that everyone will have access without causing any conflicts among the intended beneficiaries.

1 - Needs

The HNO for 2021 estimates that 5.6 million people will be negatively impacted by lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices in 2021. Furthermore, an additional 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers will lack access to sufficient and quality WASH services (HNO,2021). The main drivers of WASH needs in Koch and Fangak counties include limited physical infrastructure, sub-national violence and recurring flooding in many parts of these counties. Increased displacement also places an extra burden on existing WASH infrastructure in displacement sites and creates barriers for WASH partners when implementing activities. At national level, it is estimated that only 36 per cent of households reported having access to an improved water source in under 30 minutes without facing any protection concerns. The remaining 64 per cent of people across the country either rely on unimproved or surface water sources (41 per cent), take more than 30 minutes to reach an improved water source (19 per cent), or are able to reach an improved source in less than 30 minutes but face protection concerns while accessing the water (4 per cent). According to a KAP survey conducted in Fangak, a large majority (94.1%) indicated use of surface water, e.g. from rivers, streams, ponds, puddles, or unprotected springs. Whereas there was better access to borehole water which is safe, only a small percentage of 30.5% indicated use of safe storage and treatment of water in Koch(WRSS KAP Survey, May 2020). Access to clean and safe drinking water therefore remains a challenge that needs to be addressed. Access to sanitation remained low, with only 17 per cent of households reporting owning a latrine in their compound, 4 per cent have access to a shared latrine, and 2 per cent have access to a communal/institutional latrine. This means that 77 per cent of people do not have access to a latrine. In Jonglei, only 10 per cent of households reported using latrines (HNO 2021). Whereas there has been an improvement in knowledge on handwashing, the practice has remained low with only 38.5% and 59.6% of respondents in Fangak and Koch respectively practicing handwashing at 3 out the 5 critical moments of handwashing (WRSS, Evaluation report, May 2020). Some of the factors limiting handwashing practice is access to handwashing enablers, including soap and water as well as having a handwashing station at home. The COVID-19 Pandemic first case was reported in South Sudan in April 2020 and Health report shows increase in cases as at January 2021. Vaccination campaigns are not expected to cover sufficient number of people any soon and therefore hygiene promotion remains critical in managing the spread of the pandemic in the country. Handwashing, body hygiene and Infection Prevention and Control messaging will be a focus of this intervention to manage the spread of COVID-19. South Sudan has continued to experience flooding which in 2020 reached levels that have never been experienced in the country for a long time. This affected the physical infrastructure and worsened the already poor state of these physical infrastructure including latrines. Due to the flooding, Malaria and other water borne diseases have been on the rise and are expected to persist in 2021(IRNA Fangak, October 2020). It is reported that over one million people in 43 counties along the Nile River have been affected and this situation is expected to persist in the first quarter of 2021 (HNO, 2021). By October of 2020, 593,000 cases of water and bloody diarrhea were reported nationally. A KAP survey conducted in Fangak shows watery and bloody diarrhea are still a common morbidity coming third and fourth respectively after Malaria and ARI(WRSS, KAP Survey, May 2020). Safe human waste disposal therefore remains a challenge in many both Koch and Fangak counties.

2 - Project activities

Programming in WASH activities will be organised around three key outputs: Increased availability of safe water, sanitation and hygiene facilities at nutrition sites. 1) Owing to the devastating effects of the flooding, most of the latrines within nutrition centres have been destroyed. WR will therefore construct 11 sex-segregated latrines one in each nutrition centre. All latrines will be lockable to mitigate protection risks to children and caregivers visiting nutrition sites. Latrines will be constructed in strict adherence to sphere stands. Similarly, a few selected ‘community examples’ will be supported to construct latrines at their homes so as to provide example to motivate the community to establish their own latrines.2) WR will construct 8 water points located at the static nutrition sites in both Koch and Fangak Counties. For each rehabilitated/constructed water point, a Water Management Committee (WMC) will be formed of 4 women and 3 men. WMC members will be trained on their roles and operation. Furthermore, WRSS will train 2 pump mechanics per borehole to avail repair services.3) In addition to new latrine construction, the nutrition facilities will receive handwashing facilities, constructed at appropriate heights for children. Each nutrition site will have two handwashing stations, one to be placed just outside the entrance to the site, and one to be placed by the latrine. Nutrition site staff will ensure the constant availability of soap.Second objective will be to ensure Improved hygiene and sanitation practices among malnutrition-affected households (households with Children Under 5 with SAM/MAM), activities under this will include:1) Capacity building of nutrition workers and CHW/CNV on hygiene and nutrition messages within the 19 supported static Nutrition sites2) While caregivers/PLWs wait at nutrition sites for their children Under 5 or for themselves to receive treatment, they will be invited to attend hygiene education sessions alongside regular sessions on MICYN in a shaded, guarded waiting area. Furthermore, WR will organize community into care groups for hygiene promotion at the community level through care group promoters and volunteers in villages identified with high SAM and MAM caseloads through nutrition site registries. CHPs and volunteers will receive refresher training on handwashing, safe water chain, safe excreta disposal, food hygiene and household waste management, hygiene promotion skills and messaging. WRSS has already developed materials on hygiene promotion and will print more for this year. School Hygiene Clubs (SHCs) will be established in schools in Koch and New Fangak and will be trained using elements of Child Hygiene and Sanitation Training (CHAST) with focus on what students can do within their schools to improve hygiene and educate their peers. 3) As part of ensuring a famine prevention minimum package for children U5 admitted to CMAM programs, during the peak of rainy season, WR will distribute water treatment materials (e.g. PuR, Aquatabs, filter cloths, and clean water storage containers) to households with children admitted to OTP who lack access to safe sources of water at the community level4) Post-distribution monitoring: WR will conduct a PDM survey among households who received a hygiene kit to assess the satisfaction with and ultimate use of kit contents. WRSS will also conduct a KAP survey to assess the impact of WASH interventions on the target populations Thirdly, WRSS will Mitigate WaSH related gender-based violence, especially for acutely malnourished children and women caregivers.1) Caregivers and PLWs admitted to TSFP will receive MHM kits upon admission of themselves and/or their child to CMAM treatment programs. MHM kits will be requested from the WaSH Cluster Core Pipeline. Kit distribution will be accompanied with instructions by nutrition and WaSH staff (female whenever possible) on the proper use of the kits contents.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

World Relief takes Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) seriously at all levels. All staff are trained on PSEA and must sign on to the organization’s established policies and code of conduct. WR will also build the capacity of the nutrition sites to treat women and girls who are survivors of rape and other incidents of sexual or gender-based violence. For the community to report any incident of PSEA, WR will establish community-based beneficiary feedback mechanisms and will do consultation with community members and community leaders on how reporting should be done, to whom it should be reported and how the compliant is handled. The community will be clearly informed that any reported case will be kept confidential and will be dealt by responsible individuals at WR. During project implementation, all staffs and are expected to adhere to PSEA policy but also to sensitize beneficiaries on this policy. The protection team within WRSS is charged with responsibility to ensure continuous sensitization about PSEA. Sensitization will be provided at all stages of project implementation and any public gathering. Such public events will include registration of beneficiaries, during distribution of inputs and trainings. The Project Implementation Committee (PIC) will be involved in identification and selection of distribution and training sites. Distribution and training venues will be in central places for ease of accessibility to all persons, but also in safe locations. This is to ensure that women and girls are not exposed to sexual exploitation and abuse because of their involvement in any project activity, including training or inputs distribution. Distributions will also have to be conducted and completed on time to allow women and girls time to reach home before dark. All beneficiaries will be sensitized on the availability of this feedback mechanism for their use. Confidentiality on any information provided through the feedback mechanism will be ensured to build the confidence of the community to use this feedback mechanism. Complains registered will include sexual exploitation, abuse or any other form of protection concern/violence. Inputs will be provided in small quantities. This is to ensure that beneficiaries will not have another financial burden to transport the inputs. Such financial burdens can expose these women and girls to sexual exploitation and abuse.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The most vulnerable members of community will be included in every aspect of project implementation. The project will target to include 60% of beneficiaries as women and girls. This is so because women bear the burden of ensuring that the water is available for home use including cleaning and cooking. Providing women and girls with an opportunity to have better access to water will ensure that hygiene and sanitation at home is improved. Similarly, women and girls will be consulted to ensure that activities planned are in line with the socio-cultural needs of women and avoid over-burdening them through this intervention. For instance, distribution of inputs as well as training will take into consideration feedback from women to be in line with other roles assigned to women like fetching water, firewood and cooking at home. Persons living with disabilities will also be given special consideration. For instance, during distributions, persons with disabilities, breastfeeding women, elderly will be accorded an opportunity to be served first. Trainings will also take into consideration the needs of vulnerable people. Training schedule and timing will consider the needs of such groups.Similarly, training will also take into consideration needs of the illiterate population. Most of the project beneficiaries are illiterate. The project will employ most of its staff from the local community who can be able to communicate and conduct training in the local language. Training will also involve mostly demonstrations and practical sessions which emphasize more on learning by doing than theory. Information Education and Communication (IEC) Materials will include more pictorial information for ease of understanding.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Community engagement will be ensured at two levels. Right from inception, beneficiaries will be engaged at various levels. Project objectives will be discussed and agreed upon by community leaders at County, Payam and Boma levels. Number of beneficiaries targeted, and geographical location will be discussed at project inception. Feedback provided from those meetings will be used to adjust subsequent plans to ensure project is aligned to the context and needs of the beneficiaries. Secondly, there will be a project Implementation committee (PIC) at every Payam. This PIC will be involved in beneficiary identification, selection and registration. The PIC will include representatives from the vulnerable groups including women, disabled, youth and elderly. The PIC will also be involved in project monitoring and evaluation. Feedback provided by PIC at every stage of project implementation will be used to improve on project execution and achievements. At project level, the project directly engages Hygiene promoters on voluntary basis. Hygiene Promoters will be representatives from the respective communities. They will be the contact between the project and beneficiaries. Communication on project objectives and expectations will be done primarily through the Hygiene promoters and meetings. These Hygiene Promoters will also provide feedback on project implementation. The Monitoring and Evaluation team working with the protection team will institute a complaint registration and resolution mechanism. Complaints will be registered at every stage of project implementation and solutions sought for immediately where possible or referred to other levels for resolution. During beneficiary registration and input distribution, beneficiaries who feel unfairly left out will be free to register the complains and have their grievances addressed either by the project team or referred to higher management for resolution. Same mechanism will be instituted during training and any other project implementation stage. Water Use Committees will also provide feedback on project progress and how the intended targets are being met. Water User Committees will meet regularly to deliberate on how water is being used and correct any deviations that may arise. Complaints on amount of water, conflicts on human and livestock use will be registered and addressed in the best way possible to avoid any escalation of conflicts.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries feedback will be the gist of project development. Given that WRSS has been operational in these project locations for a long time, WRSS has had a chance to engage the beneficiaries at various stages and times. In the past, WRSS has been able to conduct post distribution monitoring surveys. The project has also been able to conduct Knowledge, Attitude and Practice Surveys. The baselines being used to develop this project are from these past surveys. This will continue even during this project. Beneficiaries will be engaged during mobilization and sensitization. Project objectives and key activities will be discussed. Feedback provided from these sensitization meetings will be used to tailor project interventions to meet the needs of the beneficiaries. The Project Implementation Committee (PIC) will also be very instrumental in providing feedback on the project. Selection criteria and targeting of beneficiaries will be discussed with the PIC. Feedback from PIC will be used to adjust the selection criteria and targeting. PIC will also be consulted to ensure training and distribution locations are easily accessible and are safe for all beneficiaries. The PIC will also be involved in monitoring project progress. During those monitoring sessions, views from the PIC as well as views of the direct beneficiaries will be used to adjust subsequent schedule and type of activities to address the needs expressed by the beneficiaries. For instance, if certain trainings were not well understood, they could be offered again. Feedback on timing, quantity and type of inputs will be received through post distribution monitoring (PDM) reports. This feedback will be used to adjust subsequent distributions. Based on the feedback, the project will adjust on duration of time spent in trainings or distribution centers. Frequency of training will also be adjusted based on feedback received from beneficiaries. Water user committees (WUC) will also provide feedback on water use. Timing of water use and any conflicts that may arise from water use will be documented and addressed to ensure that everyone has a safe and unhindered access to water.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Women and young children particularly are at risk because of implementation of project activities. WRSS will therefore undertake several measures to ensure protection of the intended beneficiaries. These measures will include:Prioritizing safety & dignity of the beneficiaries and avoid causing harm. WASH facilities and water sources will only be established after consultation with the intended beneficiaries to ensure they are sited in safe locations and easily accessible to women and girls. Since they are ones that mostly fetch water for home use. In principle, water points will be located not more than 500 meters from population settlements. Distribution centers for WASH NFIs will also be in consultation with the community members. Such distributions will also be conducted in daylight hours to allow beneficiaries enough time to travel home before nightfall. Other WASH NFIs including menstrual hygiene management (MHM) kits and water purification tabs will be done on quarterly basis to avoid providing too many kits at the same time which may require transportation, and which puts beneficiaries at risk of attacks and pillage. Size of kits will be appropriate for the beneficiaries to be able to carry home with no additional physical or financial burden and without being dependent on others to carry it for them.The Design and construction of WASH facilities will also take into consideration the safety and dignity of its users. The project will construct separate latrines for males and females, clearly marked in pictorial form for illiterate users and work with community to ensure they are used by the indicated sex. For privacy, locks will be provided on the doors to latrines on the inside. The safety risk to children will be ensured by making size of drop hole small enough to avoid any accidents or deaths. WUCs will be trained to manage water points. Where overcrowding at water points will be experienced, WUCs will be encouraged to consider scheduling time shifts for water collection in consultation with the beneficiaries. Hygiene promotion will be conducted through Care groups. This will be done in a respectful manner and inclusive of cultural and religious practice of the intended beneficiaries. Care group volunteers will be mostly women of child bearing age. All WASH facilities, including boreholes will be placed in central locations and managed by the WUCs in such a way that there will be no community tensions. Any disputes arising from use of WASH facilities will be resolved in coordination with protection actors to put in place measures to prevent and respond to physical/sexual violence arising from use of or lack of WASH facilities.WASH intervention will also be designed to Promote equitable and impartial access for all. This will include collection and use data disaggregated by sex, age and diversity to effectively inform programming. The project will also Identify and prioritize the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups.The project will as well endeavor to make infrastructure adaptions to WASH structures to make them accessible to persons with reduced mobility (eg. persons with physical disability, older persons).During WASH NFI distribution, Priority will be accorded to those who cannot stand in line for long periods, such as older persons, persons with disability, unaccompanied children, pregnant and lactating women, so that they wait for less time. Distribution teams and WUCs will also consider the gender balance to ensure that there are enough women available as a contact point for women in the community. Before distribution days, there will be enough mobilization and information dissemination to ensure that beneficiaries know about WASH services, how to obtain hygiene kits, where and when the distributions will take place, what they will get, how long it should last and how to use the items.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

WRSS has an integrated approach in service delivery. This includes provision of protection and Health services. WRSS provides primary health care services in both Koch County in Unity State and Fangak County in Jonglei state. In these two counties, WRSS runs both PHCU and PHCC services.WRSS also ensures protection to children, women at risk and GBV survivors. WRSS will also able to work with protection partners to establish referral pathways. Through these referral pathways, children, women at risk and GBV survivors will be able to access information on prevention and management of GBV. WRSS is also able to handle clinical management of rape within the PHCC and where such cases would be beyond WRSS capacity, WRSS is able to refer such cases to other facilities with better capacity.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

There is a direct relationship between poor WASH conditions and high rates of malnutrition. By improving on the WASH conditions, the project targets to reduce the proportion of population facing IPC 3,4 and 5. Working with Nutrition programe, WASH interventions will provide handwashing and sanitation facilities to children and care givers admitted within the nutrition programme. This will improve the WASH conditions within these health and nutrition centers. At community level, this project will work with care groups who are caregivers of children admitted in nutrition programme. The WASH team will sensitize care groups to establish hand washing facilities at home using locally available materials. Care groups will also be encouraged to set up sanitation facilities to improve on human waste disposal. The WASH team will also work with farmers and farmer groups to sensitize them on proper hygiene during food preparation as well as proper water treatment and storage. This will reduce incidences of diarrhea and water borne diseases, which impacts positively on health and nutrition status of the targeted beneficiaries and therefore reducing proportion of population in IPC 3,4 and 5.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

WRSS integrates interventions to ensure equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to the vulnerable populations. FSL interventions will be conducted alongside WASH interventions. During FSL distributions, the project will continue to sensitize the community on use of appropriate water treatment techniques, the importance of proper hygiene and sanitation. Messaging on COVID-19 Infection Prevention and Control will also continue to be disseminated during these events. Same messaging will also be disseminated during trainings. High standard of hygiene will be emphasized especially during training on food utilization to ensure that food is prepared in a safe and hygienic environment. Beneficiaries will also be encouraged to ensure they have adequate sanitation and hygiene facilities at home. Working with lead farmers, the project will promote construction of gender sensitive latrines with designated use for both males and females cleared labeled. To ensure safety of use, the latrines will have a lock that can be controlled from inside. Similarly, every Lead farmer will be encouraged to have a handwashing facility, made from basic and locally available materials but sensitive to the needs of the vulnerable populations. The handwashing facility will have to be constructed so as to be at a height that can be used for children and any disabled members of the household.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

This intervention will aim at reducing prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children, Pregnant and lactating women. WRSS will organize intervention activities through integration centered around nutrition services. WRSS will establish care givers of malnourished children as well as PLWs through care groups which are groups of 12-15 families with malnourished children as well as PLWs. These Care groups will meet regularly to share experiences on managing malnutrition at community level facilitated by care group supervisors, promoters and volunteers. The Care group supervisors, promoters and volunteers will continue to impart knowledge to these beneficiaries through trainings and mentorship. The same Care groups will be targeted for WASH Interventions. Messaging on WASH will be conducted though these care groups. There is a strong linkage between WASH and malnutrition. Evidence has shown that communities with high malnutrition rates also exhibit very poor WASH conditions. Through those care groups, this intervention will engage care givers of malnourished children as well as PLWs to ensure that their hygiene and sanitation is improved so as to have reduce prevalence of global acute malnutrition. The caregivers will also be the primary beneficiaries of WASH Non-food items required to ensure improved WASH conditions. They will be provided with materials for water treatment and storage including jerrycan, bucket, filter cloth and Aqua tabs. Similar, Care groups will be targeted for improved sanitation facilities. members of a care group will be sensitized on proper waste disposal and use of latrines at home. They will also be educated on importance of hygiene and encouraged to set up handwashing facilities at home using the basic available resources.WASH intervention will also target Nutrition centers. within the Nutrition centers, hygiene and sanitation faculties will be provided to ensure under five children visiting these centers are served under improved hygiene conditions. Each nutrition center will have a latrine and handwashing facility.
