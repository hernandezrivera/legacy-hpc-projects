Project Summary

Purpose: Provide live saving & integrated basic service assistance of WASH, Health, Nutrition & Livelihoods to meet critical needs of 213,113 people (177,594 refugees including new arrivals, needy existence caseloads & 35,519 host communities). The project will seek to provide live saving interventions & sustain services of WASH, Health & Nutrition & livelihood through safe, clean water & adequate sanitation facilities, quality primary health care, preventive & curative nutritional interventions & opportunities to build self-reliant. The services will be provided in a holistic & collective manner to enhance peaceful coexistence between refuges & host community & improving the peoples’ well-being.
Sudan host about 1.1 Million Refugees, the projection figures by year end total 2019 is 86,3849 South Sudanese refuges, 127,958 Eritreans & 14,569 Ethiopian. New arrivals & returns trends are expected during 2020 with end projection figure of 1.2 M including 884,771 SSR, 144,259 ERT & 14,034 ETH. Reports coming from South Sudan indicates ongoing violence, restriction of movements in some counties & food insecurity which may lead to an increase in numbers of new arrivals. CARE project targets 177,594 refugees including SSR living in camps & out of camps settlement in South Kodofan, Khartoum & East Darfur & 80,895 Ethiopians & Eritreans’ refugees living in Kassala State in Shagarab camps & 35,519 vulnerable host community members living in all target areas. The majority of the affected & target population are women & children. (82%) of the SSR are women & children who arrived in poor health after traveling many days to reach Sudan, often by foot, & who are in urgent need of protection, WASH, nutrition, shelter & health support. The project will work in an integrated manner to address WASH; nutrition & livelihood needs to save lives & reduce unnecessary human suffering through reduced morbidity & mortality & life free from violence.
The aim of integrated sectors is to contribute to the immediate life-saving needs of women, men, boys & girls through provision of adequate & safe services to reduce morbidity & mortality among the SSR, Ethiopians & Eritreans. This project will be done through improving water & sanitation services, linking to health & nutrition facilities for synergistic impact through mobilizing communities for individual & common efforts. WASH activities will focus to ensure adequate & safe water supply, provision of acceptable sanitation services through rehabilitation/construction of facilities as decided by the community & sector partners & hygiene promotion activities. The WASH activities will be integrated to nutritional prevention & curative actions & community based health response mechanisms. WASH voucher & tariff system will be injected in Kassala response whenever possible. Health activities will include delivery of basic health packages of PHC services, medical supplies, expand and maintain disease surveillance, reproductive health; emergency obstetrics & support measles vaccination. Nutritional interventions will include package of CMAM & IYCF services to support provision of CMAM & SAM services and admission to TFP & SFP, community training & conduct SENS surveys. Children under 5 & PLWs in needs will be targeted. Livelihood interventions will support targeted families that experience malnutrition, food insecurity & have special needs & capability to engage in income earning opportunities to promote self-reliant. Productive assets, conduct of vocational training & promote self-employment are key activities. Gender, protection & accountability will be mainstreamed throughout the implementation. CIS with national partners will work in coordinated manner to provide the needed support, staff will participate & attend coordination meetings at different levels to provide assistance in coordinated & collaborative manner.

1 - Needs

Sudan host over 1 Million refugees & asylums, this includes about 810,000 SSR & 137,000 ERT & ETH. CARE target areas host big numbers of (ED- 74,441, SK-36,644, Khartoum-283,895 & Kassala-109,816). Despite some progress made in 2019, significant gaps remain & exacerbated by funding gaps, economic crises including fuel, cash & medicines shortages. Rising tensions with host communities has introduced new protection concerns, with increased risks of violence, including SGBV targeting refuges communities. New arrivals continued to arrive through different crossing points along the borders of South Sudan, Eritrea, Ethiopia, CAR etc. In 2019, by 31st Oct 17,221 SSR arrived to Sudan. There is a need to provide live saving interventions to new arrivals & sustaining services provision in the camps, big gathering settlements & out-of-camp settings where refuges are integrated with the host communities in remote & underdeveloped areas sharing their limited resources. In the seven years of the response, there is a need to move beyond emergency assistance to focus on longer term solutions, resilience and self-reliant. The expansion of services will help to enhance peaceful co-existence between refuges & host communities in addition to meeting lifesaving needs of both groups. There has been a dramatic deterioration in the economic situation in Sudan through- out 2019 which has compounded the humanitarian needs of the already vulnerable population. Rate of inflation is very high making the price of basic household necessitates such as bread & medicines un affordable for a significant proportion of the population forcing them into further vulnerability. This has been compounded by a fuel crisis across the country which has further increased food prices, hindered agricultural activities for communities as well as humanitarian operations across the country. The impacts of the economic situation on refugee communities is compounded by limited access to livelihoods & high poverty. According to RRP May 2019 despite the ongoing response by different partners, many areas are still below emergency standards, significant gaps identified, remains unmet & need immediate assistance. E.g. 41% of school-aged children are enrolled in schools & retention rates are low with poor school’s environment, water supply quantities average 10.2 l/p/d across the response, with just 5 l/p/d in some areas. Latrines coverage in 2018 worsened as heavy rains caused latrines to collapse. & overcrowding contributed to an average of 91 persons per latrine in some SSR camps. Open defecation is a serious issue & the rate of hand washing is low due to gap in soap supplies. Poor WASH situation is driven factor for water borne diseases, spread of diarrhea among households & poor personal hygiene. Needs in Shagarb camps of Kassala is critical; water supply systems & pipeline networks are old & need rehabilitation. Water shortage are common in camps due to broken systems, increased demand of refuges & host community & poor maintenance work. The need is high to maintain existing water system & introduce tariff & voucher systems for improving service provision & sustainability of interventions. Also, there is high needs to provide menstrual hygiene support for women & girls to improve personnel hygiene. The health & nutrition status of many refuges is still poor associated with a challenge of sustainable screening services upon arrivals. There are critical GAM rates (over 15%) & SAM (over 3%) in many areas of the response as well as high rates (over 40%) of anemia. Food insecurity is driven factor of poor nutrition status exacerbated by pipeline breaks & ration cuts. Refuges are dependent on fire wood for cooking, housing & lighting & only 5% of households are able to purchase firewood, mainly by selling food ration. Depend on fire wood has also led to forest degradation, increasing host community tensions & increase protection risks especially for women & girls.

2 - Activities / Outputs

WASH: Operation/Maintenance of water supply facilities; construction/rehabilitation of water sources, borehole drilling/construction; upgrading of water sources including water pipeline networks, upgrading water treatment system; chlorination of water sources; water quality testing; water user committee’s formation/revival & training; Households latrines construction/rehabilitation & decommissioning; Construction of latrines at facilities; CLTS triggering & latrines construction by beneficiaries; Shower construction, rehabilitation & maintenance; distribute water voucher/cash; recruitment & training of hygiene promoters; hygiene promotion awareness campaigns; Garbage collection & safe disposal facilities construction; Distribution of hygiene items & supplies (soap, child potties, Jerry cans, menstrual hygiene kits; etc.). Health &Nutrition: construction, rehabilitation & equipping of health facilities; Provide general clinical services including outpatient consultation services for diagnosis & treatment for common illnesses, basic laboratory investigations, short stay admission & emergency referral to secondary & tertiary for selected cases; reproductive health services to provide maternal & new borne health care services, antenatal & postnatal care, referral services & pregnant women vaccination & skilled care during child birth; Provision of essential drugs & consumable supplies through the PHC facilities; Provide EPI services; pay monthly incentive for seconded staff from SMOH; vaccination for children under 1; conduct health awareness campaigns; training of health care staff & community health works on PHC services provision; train midwives & birth attendants on safe motherhood infection control & EmOC; full package of CMAM and IYCF services will be provided, admission to TFP (OTP + SC) feeding program and to SFP, train community volunteers, conduct SENS survey. Livelihood: provision of productive assets; conduct of vocational training & business support activities. Activities will cover 80% of refuges & 20% of host community from the target to meet the ongoing and unmet needs. The implementation of the activities will be done in close coordination and collaboration with Government of Sudan's Commission of Refuges (COR), Humanitarian Aid Commission (HAC), UNHCR, related ministries and other national partners in each state. This will help to avoid duplication of efforts & resource misuse. CARE will attend coordination meetings through Refugees Consultation Forums & refugees working groups at Federal & States levels and strategies and plans will be shared so that responses are needs based & timely. CIS will support response monitoring & analyze progress through regular reporting & share information on targets & activities on regular bases & as required.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

CARE recognizes that water and sanitation, health and nutrition response programs andlivelihoods diversification interventions do have impact on the health of the public,environment and natural resources. Interventions to preserve and protect the environmentwill be included from the analysis, design phase, implementation and monitoring of each programintervention. The mitigation measures could include but not limited to: analysis of the potential impact of key interventions and discussion with community leaders and sector experts; regular joint water and sanitation facilities monitoring and supervision; emphasis on environmental education and capacity building for increased environmental awareness among beneficiaries and implementing partners; solid and liquid waste management; actions to avoid water pollution due to excavation, back filling and concreting, hauling and damping of earth materials; controlling formation of stagnant water pool to avoid soil erosion and breeding mosquitoes. Health and nutrition facilities measures include cleaning campaigns, quality and monitored incinerators, and refresher training to health professionals, WASH experts and community volunteers. Youth, women, men and others will be organized into water user’s committees, hygiene promoters, management groups, health workers, committees & volunteers, nutrition volunteers, Livelihood committees and groups where appropriate and tailored training will be provided to contribute positively to the management of natural resources and peaceful co-existence. Environmental impact assessment will be carried out if interventions require to do so and will be decided with sector ministries.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

CARE will take extra caution in analyzing the protection of at risk groups, including children and girls. Efforts will include: no creation of new risks, address discrimination, analysis of the most vulnerable, safety and dignity in service delivery, orientation and familiarization on humanitarian principles and rights, and promoting empowerment. Furthermore, CARE and partners will exercise principles of protection mainstreaming: meaningful access through consultation and involvement of the communities about the best way to deliver the services and considerations for vulnerable groups, accountability mechanisms and Field visits. CARE and partners will empower communities by training & capacity building & awareness raising about key protection concerns and risks, consulting them in decision making process, identifying volunteers and establishing and training community committees. Strengthening community protection mechanisms and improving access to services through information dissemination on available services, good selection criteria and identification of the most vulnerable and referrals will be prioritized. Do no harm analysis will be carried for each sector and the activities will be screened not to enhance risks for all especially women, girls and children. Facilities such as latrines and water points, income generating activities and trainings will be analyzed and discussed with at risk groups to ensure that the beneficiaries are protected and that the services are easily accessible and safely usable by all. Protection risks faced by women and girls, and in particular S/GBV risks, will be the specific focus of capacity building for staff and partners to ensure that they are able to detect cases of abuse. Staff will also be oriented in order to make sure that programming does no harm and that they are able to respond safely and appropriately to protection cases when they are reported, using a confidential and survivor centered approach to disclosures of violence. An internal mechanism for reporting complaints and ensuring that required measure are in place will be promoted throughout the implementation period.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

CARE and its partners will take into consideration information sharing of the project and will get feedback of beneficiaries in the design of the activities and make deliberate efforts to incorporate their views and concerns throughout the programming cycle. To ensure accountability within the project, the staff and local government experts working under each proposed sector will be trained on accountability to program participants and to community leaders. CARE’s recent efforts to employ different accountability mechanisms such as a toll free number, rapid community review and reflection, and community score card represent important steps towards the project’s success as the beneficiaries will be able to provide direct feedback to CARE and partners staff. The communities will receive orientation and familiarization sessions on CFRM in general, and on community meetings and forums such as women’s groups, men’s groups, Water User Committees, other project structures, Community Committees, nutrition and health workers’ meetings. Local communities and beneficiaries will be consulted on the design, implementation and monitoring of complaints-handling processes. Local committees in each project location will have two CFRM focal points (a woman and a man) to lead the initiative. This will enable beneficiaries in the targeted areas to safely report any concerns or provide feedback on the project. The participation of affected populations throughout the project period is fundamental in the programmatic approach. CARE has worked closely with its partners on more than four similar projects on APP issue, which has provided the opportunity for partners to learn from each other’s experiences, as well as to strengthen systems and policies enhancing accountability to the communities. Focus Group Discussions will be conducted and results will be used to monitor the participation of women, men, boys and girls and to gather feedback of all on the overall project implementation. In order to prevent sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse of those seeking access to services, all staff, partner, contractors and volunteers will be required to complete a dedicated training on PSEA as a commitment to core values and Core Humanitarian Standards. Communities will also receive information on PSEA policies, as well as on their rights and on available reporting mechanisms. This will be done during projects launch events and via regular reminders thereafter. Complaint response and reporting mechanisms shall have adequate scope to address the protection concerns of all.
