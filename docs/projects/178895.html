4 - Needs

Syria remains one of the world’s most complex humanitarian emergencies characterized by ongoing hostilities which have killed hundreds of thousands of people, triggered one of the worst displacement crises of our time, and led to the widespread destruction of civilian and agricultural infrastructure, including homes, schools, health facilities, water supply, and irrigation systems. Today, 13.4 million people in Syria are in need of humanitarian assistance – a 21 percent increase compared to 2020 – with needs increasingly being exacerbated by economic decline. Continued civilian casualties and forced displacement due to ongoing hostilities, in addition to reduced access to already degraded basic services, limited and inadequate housing and shelter options, and a wide array of specific protection risks and concerns continue to cause and perpetuate humanitarian needs among the population. While hostility induced displacement in early 2020 generated additional needs amongst the population in Syria for internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees and host communities, particularly in north-west Syria (NWS), the ripple effects of the economic downturn - including the loss of income and livelihoods, sharply reduced purchasing power and resulting financial unaffordability of f basic goods - have exacerbated living conditions for people who were already in humanitarian need, and have tipped previously less affected segments of the population into humanitarian need, including food insecurity, across the country.

Impact: HighMitigation: Violet will ensure the supplier is aware of the CP policy through the mandatory CP awareness session to all suppliers, an associated provision will be added in the supplier contract to ensure the suppliers are aware and accountable3- the risk of COVID-19 spread in the targeted community especially on those at risk.Likelihood: High impact: High Mitigation measures: Violet will ensure all teh preventive measures are applied during the implementation, elderly and people at risk can stay at home and delegate someone on their behalf to collect their assistance, if the People at risk doesn't have anyone to delegate, violet filed staff may deliver the assistance to his door

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

1- sexual exploitation and abuse through humanitarian workers. As the staff will interact with the most vulnerable groups of the community, the power dynamics between staff and BNFs can be exploitedLikelihood: Medium. Impact: HighMitigation measures: Violet trains all staff on PSEA, raises awareness of BNFs, and informs on available complaints mechanism. The M&E team will include post-monitoring and PSEA topics in supervision sessions to identify any misconduct. Any complaints, allegations, or rumors will be elevated on the country director level and investigated immediately2. GBV can be compounded by overcrowding during the distribution activities:Likelihood: Medium. Impact: HighMitigation measures: ensure the ques of both men and women are separated during the distribution, ensure the female staff is presented in the distribution site to address the women-specific needs, consultation with women, girls, boys, and men should be organized.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1- Logistical operations are affected by the security situation like transportation, storing the assistance- Likelihoods: medium- Impact: High- Mitigation: The safety and Security team will study all possible scenarios for the storing and transportation and the distribution will be HHs to HHs not in one place 2- inability to respond to the IDPs within the first phase of their displacement- Likelihoods: Medium- Impact: High- Mitigation: Violet has an IDPs tracking initiative by setting communication channels with all LCs in the whole community which usually hosts IDPs, then once an emergency occurs, the LC will inform Violet focal points to respond immediately 3. host HHs might ask to include their families in the project- Likelihoods: High- Impact High- Mitigation: Selection criteria will be clear with no chance for personal judgment taking into consideration the participation of the local communities as references and the IDs information. MoUs will be signed with all local councils to clarify the project's scope of work which will facilitate the processes in their communities and apply the selection criteria transparently Risk 4: COVID-19 transmission as a result of the action- Likelihoods: Medium- Impact: High- Mitigation: adjusting the activities and applying the preventive measures including sterilizing the Distribution sites, wearing the masks during the distribution, screening the BNFs temperature before getting into the distribution site and supplying the distribution point with adequate hygiene solutions, minimizing the number of BNF at the distribution site and setting different distribution sites at once to avoid the overcrowding.
