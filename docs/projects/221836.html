Project Summary

WFP’s School Feeding Program is a contribution to developing the Human Capital potential of the most vulnerable children in south while also providing food assistance to foster access to education services. The program targets young children of vulnerable households by increasing their access to education, reducing gaps in caloric and nutritional intake, and protection from risks, especially amongst girls from early pregnancies and marriage.
The school feeding programme includes the provision of on-site freshly prepared meals and dry take-home rations. WFP either provides food commodities at the school level directly or facilitates food provision through a monthly value voucher (home grown school feeding - HGSF). With meals as incentives, the programme aims at contributing to improved education performance (enrolment, attendance, and retention), social cohesion, while also ending short term hunger. The home-grown approach relies on cash-based transfers to facilitate the implementation of school feeding at the school — using locally-produced food, especially from smallholder farmers and increasing national and community ownership in school feeding management as well as linking up with the private sector (retailers/wholesalers) in local markets.
WFP’s support to the school feeding programme is informed by a collaboration agreement with the Ministry of General Education and Instruction (MoGEI). The collaboration takes place at central and state levels through joint assessments, coordination meetings, monitoring missions, and institutional capacity building. The importance of school feeding for human capital development is further recognized by the government of South Sudan by ensuring its inclusion in the forthcoming Education Strategic plan as well as the Social Protection Policy Framework.
Non-governmental organization partners (national and international) play a key role in implementing school feeding activities from food delivery, information collection and verification, monitoring, and training for schools and communities with WFP and MoGEI. WFP has field level agreements with a number of cooperating partner NGOs to coordinate and facilitate implementation of the school feeding programme.
Food insecurity is the key criterion for deciding on targeted counties. Government and community-owned primary schools with poor education indicators are selected. Accessibility for food delivery, teaching, basic infrastructure, and community participation are other prerequisites for program implementation.
In 2025 WFP plans to provide schools meals to 366,305 children aged 6-18 years in 776 schools across south sudan.

1. Needs

The dire situation in South Sudan is driven by unresolved conflicts at national and subnational level, recurrent natural disasters, compounded by climate change and poor disaster management capacity. In 2024, the protracted crisis in South Sudan was exacerbated by spillover of the conflict in Sudan, a fifth consecutive year of unprecedented flooding and an ensuing macroeconomic economic crisis. In 2024, according to the Integrated Food Security Phase classification, with 7.72 million in need of food assistance and 63,000 projected to be facing 'Catastrophe Likely' conditions. By October, an estimated 1 million people were affected by severe flooding particularly in Unity State, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Jongolei and Warrap. Violence continues to be one of the main drivers of severe food insecurity in South Sudan. Despite the signature of the Revitalized Agreement for the Resolution of Conflict in South Sudan (R-ARCSS) in September 2018 which ended the unrestricted war, the political situation and conflict dynamics are fragile. Protection issues are particularly significant. Vulnerable people, especially older persons, women, boys and girls and persons with disability are especially at risk. Girls and women are at risk of abduction, injury and killings in addition to all forms of gender-based violence (GBV) which remains common, including sexual violence in homes and public spaces. Conflict and violence continue to contribute to a rise in GBV incidents through rape, forced prostitution and other forms of sexual violence. Cultural and social perceptions lead to the social stigmatization of women and reinforce patriarchal practices within the household and community. Economic shocks, high food, firewood and charcoal prices, and local currency depreciation continue to impact food availability and household access to food, thereby increasing the need for humanitarian food assistance. In South Sudan, around 48% of schools remain non-functional, and over 2.8 million children are out of school, with approximately 1.3 million schoolchildren at risk of dropping out (with more girls dropping out after completing their primary education), and school enrolment rates among the lowest in the world. School feeding acts as a pull factor for increased enrolment enhancing children’s attendance, and retention in schools with subsequent improvement to their education and learning outcomes. The enrolment rate remains more than four times higher at the primary than the secondary level of education (62.9 percent and 13.4 percent, respectively) as most children do not transition to secondary school. There has been a decline in back to learning enrolment due to a sharp increase in protection risks, particularly for girls, as well as economic pressure on families since Covid19. School closures have also led to a significant loss of learning. Economic pressure in the wake of the Covid-19 pandemic triggered food insecurity across South Sudan, which is continues to drive irregular attendance, increase drop-out rates, and impact learning as more than 50% of children arrive at school hungry.

2. Project activities

As per its mandate, WFP concentrates its efforts and resources on addressing critical food and nutrition insecurity and provides nutrition-sensitive food assistance to crisis-affected populations comprising of refugees, IDPs, returnees, vulnerable host communities and vulnerable school going children in hard-to-reach areas. The school feeding program will provide in-kind (food) to vulnerable children (boys and girls) in counties facing high levels of food insecurity. Under the regular school feeding modality, in-kind assistance in the form of food rations will be delivered to schools intermittently which will be received by school management committees so that the school management committees may organize school meals at the school level. WFP will expand its support in 2025 to the government by furthering capacity strengthening initiatives such as enhancing their monitoring and oversight capacity of the school meals program in the counties and sub county levels. WFP will also advocate for greater government ownership, promote fundraising from government sources or government-influenced funds, along with enhanced data management capacity of the Ministry. These initiatives will build upon the framework laid out in the MoU signed between WFP and the MoGEI in 2023 and the subsequent workplan which was agreed upon in 2024.
