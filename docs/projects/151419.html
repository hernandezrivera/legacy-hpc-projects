Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims to ensure that vulnerable disaster-affected populations (affected by emergencies such as famine, drought, floods and conflict) in Kismayo and Afmadhow districts of Lower Juba region are able to access immediate WASH services. The action proposes to support the improvement of water supply utilities, improve environmental sanitation and hygiene by ensuring functional public sanitation facilities and promoting community led total sanitation (CLTS) to eradicate open defecation, and strengthening/improving quality of effective WASH service delivery. A strong focus will be placed on strengthening systems to ensure sustainability and enhancing WASH capacity by particularly strengthening local authority capacity to effectively oversee and enforce policy applications in service delivery. Further, this action will endeavor to engage the youth in opportunities for the provision of basic O&M services required to effectually sustain the operations of existing functional systems. Further capacity building in emergencies preparedness and response planning of the WASH sector in Jubbaland will be enhanced. The intervention has been designed to meet the needs of vulnerable girls, women, boys and men, IDPs and host communities in targeted districts.

Needs

The two districts of Kismayo and Afmadow are perennial victims of alternating natural and anthropogenic disasters. Plagued by over two decades of conflict and natural disasters, the local communities have been left with socio-economic vulnerabilities that demand more resources. With a significant IDP and returnee population, vulnerable fisher-, pastoralist and urban-poor, majority of whom live in abject poverty, coping mechanisms have been eroded and livelihoods adversely affected. Water scarcity is common and waterborne diseases are rampant; Cholera is endemic, with Kismayo being a hotspot. According to the latest FEWS NET forecast, there is an elevated likelihood of an El Niño event in the Deyr season 2018. These rainfall episodes are usually accompanied by spiraling cases of Cholera caseloads.ADRA’s assessment on WASH situation conducted in June 2018 at 10 schools, 5 health facilities and 20 water points in the 2 districts drew the following findings:1. Access to clean safe water remains the main challenge facing IDPs and host community. Severe water shortage and suffering (per capita of ca. 5 lpd). 2. Many water supply facilities are not well managed worsening the crisis. Poor accountability at operational water supply schemes often occasion long downtimes during breakdowns, thus the need for capacity development.3. From KII interviews, out of 35 wells only 15 were effectively functional in Afmadow causing flocking, over-abstraction and depletion. Reported salt-water intrusion and salinity problems force buying of drinking water at exorbitant prices.4. Open defecation is common at many settlements. Sanitation facilities at public places like hospitals, schools and markets are not-functional or in pathetic state -poorly cared for and unhygienic-. Many require rehabilitation. Soms were also destroyed -forcing people to opt for open defecation. Unhygienic practices are observed. 5. Hygiene practices are largely poor and vulnerability to diarrhoea is high.

Activities

Based on ADRA own rapid assessment in Afmadow and in newly liberated areas in Kismayo district where a total of 15 villages were visited, the following activities are proposed: Output1: 8,500 beneficiaries (4500 female, 4000 male) have sustained water supply through 10 improved sources 1.1. Rehabilitate /upgrade 4 strategic wells/ boreholes into community piped water supply schemes 1.2. Support rehabilitation of 6 wells installed with hand pumps 1.3. Provide water supply and storage facilities to 6 schools and health care facilities 1.4. Conduct water treatment at waterpoint and household level in disease hotspot areas 1.5. Provide disaster-affected households with emergency water supply through vouchers Output 2: Improved sanitation and hygiene at institutions (10 schools & 3 health care facilities) and at vulnerable settlements for 10,000 people (5,500 females and 4,500 males) 2.1. Implement a WINS program in 10 schools to ensure 3-Star certification. 2.2. Implement CLTS in 10 remote villages to ensure ODF settlements 2.4. Rehabilitate/construct 25 public toilets / latrines fitted with HWFs in schools and MCHs 2.5. Desludge 30 filled-up latrines in Cholera hotspot areas 2.6. Conduct hygiene promotion and sanitation campaigns in 15 main villages including in urban areasOutput 3: Capacity of 10 partner stakeholder groups (210 people) strengthened to support operation and maintenance of water supply and sanitation facilities and ensure sustainable delivery and management of WASH services 3.1. Train 10 water committees on management, conflict mitigation and DRR 3.2. Train youth and engage youth groups in operation and maintenance service provision 3.3. Provide refresher training for hygiene promoters and outreach workers on hygiene promotion 3.4. Train community workers, religious/community leaders on CLTS 3.5. Train teachers/ CEC members on WINS and MHM in schools

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

This action will engage local authorities, water user management committees and local youth in te quest to develop the most ideal and sustainable solutions to maintain the WASH service delivery in the IDP camps and host communities. Only sustainable solutions will be chosen and implemented. Capacity building will not only emphasize technical operations but simple business management procedures. If properly executed, these WASH services will be operated as be income-generating enterprises and therefore self-sustaining. Local governance structures will be closely involved to ensure support and ownership. Working in collaboration with relevant local and state authorities, youths provided with tailor-made short-term vocational training courses which will make them useful in the society and crucial in essential WASH services provision, which is the missing link in sustainability equation. Linkage with suitable supply chain with be supported to ensure that established enterprises remain relevant in the market and are able to address emerging need in good time.Besides life-saving interventions, other water supply utilities that will be developed and supported will be strategic ones that offer durable solutions for the local communities. The project will also support social and economic inclusion and engagement that are important for consolidating state-building efforts while supporting sustainable peace and development. If fully supported, a great number of adolescents and youth are poised to benefit if these initiatives that are not only cost-effective but also relevant to basic local livelihood needs of the community. Efforts will also be made to the state authorities to grout the achievements in policy and ensure proper enforcement within the legal frameworks to ensure sustainability of such programs.The project will be registered with MOPIC with whom all the details including budget, activities and outputs will be shared. Activities will have to be aligned to the Jubbaland State Strategic Development Plan 2017-2020. ADRA will work closely with Ministry of Water, Education and Health and involve them in different aspects of the project where relevant to ensure ADRA accountability. MOPIC will also be involved wherever a survey is to be conducted. Working with respective ADSRA will alert health practitioners where heavily contaminated water is encountered and the organization will work closely with the health department where cholera cases are confirmed.ADRA will also present the projects progress periodically at the cluster meetings of the different relevant ministries. Further, ADRA will be open to peer-monitoring and monitoring by OCHA. At these monitoring meetings, the relevant authorities can always be invited to provide their opinion also about ADRA work which will enhance their involvement and accountability on ADRA side.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Drought and conflict continue to be the main drivers causing displacement in Somalia. Combined, these two drivers have forced more than 2.1 million to be displaced. Over 80,000 children are also out of school due to drought induced displacement. The youth/young adolescents make a significant proportion of this displaced population. Given their vulnerable circumstances, this population chapter is often faced with serious violations including forced recruitment into armed groups, engagement in petty theft and organized crimes or sexual exploitation that end up ruining their lives and future. The Somali youth are among the most talented, industrious and motivated young people who, if engaged in meaningful productive work, can contribute in significantly enhancing quality and innovative service delivery to their communities. This action provides an opportunity for meaningful engagement of youth groups that would lead to productive contribution to their communities while in displacement. One of the areas where the youth in IDP camps can be meaningfully involved is in the provision of WASH services.Another driver for conflict over resource and resource distribution (e.g. water points and usage, rangelands and economic opportunities like employment). Efforts will be made to ensure the opinions and viewpoints of minority clans, disadvantaged, less educated, and marginalized groups are taken into consideration in a participatory and inclusive manner, and that the implementation process and benefits of the action and related service delivery are realized in an equitable manner that does not result in any exclusion or deprivation of any of these groups or persons. Careful selection and prioritization of targets will be done to enhance equity. This action will ensure equal participation of youths of both gender in all aspects of the project. Youths from marginalized and socially excluded groups and youths living with disability will be given priority.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The proposed action will ensure equal participation of people of both gender in all aspects of the project. Gender segregated data will be collected for planning and reporting. ADRA will ensure that service delivery considers and addresses the distinct needs of female and male youth, men and women and the elderly in a systematic manner. Of essence in this project will be to equally include, train and engage females in operations and activities of this action, as they express interest, and in management roles of the youth entities created. ADRA will ensure WASH-related services remain robust and that service delivery sensitive to gender related needs is maintained throughout this action’s implementation. Considering the micro-entrepreneur training which is vital for the youth in Somalia in general, ADRA will ensure that both male and female are given equal opportunity to gain knowledge on entrepreneurship for future investment in income generating activities that will contribute building their resilience to the recurrent emergencies’ shocks in their localities and in the country.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Tested conflict-sensitive approaches effective at addressing risks related to the alienation and exclusion of adolescents and youth will be employed. This action will address Pillars 5 and 6 in the Jubaland State Strategic Plan 2018-2020. If correctly executed, the action presents an opportunity for longer-term options for youth employment besides the programming that can help strengthen and improve WASH service delivery. Despite the opportunity, there is gaping lack of capacity and knowhow requisite for the provision of such WASH services. To help realize this objective, this action will collaborate with relevant state authorities to support youths for short term vocational training courses at centers in Kismayo and offer tailor-made courses in; (1) hand-pump repair and maintenance, electromechanical training including solar energy, plumbing, and (2) sanitation -construction of new or desludging/ emptying of latrines, (3) book keeping and entrepreneurship etc.Developing capacity of youth in the IDP and host community settings will not only guarantee a medium to long-term engagement but empower young people by offering employment and meaningful roles aligned with their skills, experiences, enthusiasm and interests. It will also create a platform for interaction and communication among their peers (IDPs and host) and local authorities. The approach will support the social and economic inclusion through alternative education/vocational training programmes and engagements that are important for consolidating state-building efforts while supporting sustainable peace and development. If maximum benefits are to be realized, the governments will need to be more intimately involved in developing, financing and delivering systematic certified vocational skills and training opportunities. Greater number of adolescents and youth are poised to benefit if these initiatives are not only cost-effective but also relevant to local livelihood needs and employment opportunities.
