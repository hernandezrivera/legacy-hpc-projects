Project Summary

The project will reach 3000 women and youth (especially young adolescents) affected by the conﬂict, and are in need as direct beneficiaries among the most vulnerable population in affected host communities and internally displace (IDP) camps) in Borno and Yobe States to acquire contemporary innovative and context demand skills that enhance their economic recovery and facilitate their leadership and participation in peace processes. It will provide access to acquisition of vocational skills, rice and vegetable farming, including the processing of products for value chain; as well as other income-generating activities (IGAs) and livelihoods options. Similarly, the project will support young adolescent girls (especially former adoptees) and young women who have never attended any formal education to access functional literacy/basic education and market-relevant technical skills to increase employment, livelihood and entrepreneurial opportunities and self-reliance. It will further strengthen community structures to restore social cohesion and improve social inclusion including to promote the empowerment of women and girls as well as increasing their access to basic services in order to address social-economic, and psychological damage caused by the conflict.
The project will also inﬂuence socio-cultural transformation to reduce barriers women and girls face in accessing basic services, support improvement of policies, institutional capacity and ﬁnancing framework to facilitate access to market-relevant vocational skills for conﬂict-affected young women and girls. A signiﬁcant part of the project will build on existing mechanisms to ensure long-term continuity and sustainability. Existing vocational training centers will be identiﬁed and strengthened to provide training to adolescent girls and young women. With regards to engagement in peace processes, the project will train beneﬁciaries on conﬂict resolution, reconciliation, and peacebuilding, as well as engage communities (including men and boys as gender champions) to create an enabling environment for the active participation of women and youth in decision making structures and peacebuilding and recovery processes.
The proposed interventions will create an enabling environment for the delivery of humanitarian relief, reduce reliance on aid, and enhance the resilience of affected communities, especially women and young women to withstand and recover from the hardship caused by the conflict.

1 - Needs

North-East Nigeria has experienced violence and armed conflict since 2009 due to the Boko Haram insurgency and operations by the security forces in Nigeria. The multi-year Humanitarian Response Strategy (HRS) (2019-2021), stated that the violent conflict has resulted in deaths, displacement of about 1.8 million people (440,000 women, 364,000 men, 614,000 girls, and 516,000 boys) loss, and destruction of property, deterioration of living conditions, disruption of livelihood, accumulated stress, weakened resilience, and heightened vulnerabilities. The conflict has had disproportionate impacts along gender lines as in other armed conflicts, casualties in NE Nigeria are predominantly young men. Similarly, the separation of families and the systematic migration of men toward larger and safer cities to be able to provide for their families back home has translated into a significant increase in female-headed households particularly adolescent girls as main breadwinners. Women have also become targets of violence during the conflict, being victims of sexual and gender-based violence and abduction and have been disproportionately affected by forced displacement. According to the UNHCR Vulnerability Screening (2017), 63% of vulnerable households within the most conﬂict-affected areas in the North-East are female-headed. Female-headed households are forced into new roles as breadwinners with limited skills and less likely to access services and assistance. The situation is worsened by pre-existing gender inequalities due to unequal access to opportunities that have contributed to limited resources and skills among women and girls. With limited skills for employability and carrying the burden of child and sometime family-care often single-handedly, many women are at high risk of adopting negative coping mechanisms including survival sex, to gain ﬁnancial resources to support themselves and their families. Also, many women who were previously engaged in the agriculture sector have lost sources of livelihoods as a result of displacement and limited movement associated with insecurity. As the security situation relatively improves, some Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) are now returning to their communities, whilst others are still staying in host communities with the hope of returning later to their communities of origin or integrating into host communities. In 2018, about one million IDPs remain in host communities while 1.6 million have returned to their LGAs of origin in Borno (Damboa, Konduga, Monguno) Adamawa (Madagali, Mubi North, Hong) and Yobe (Gujba, Potiskum, Fika) States. Even though these communities are not experiencing active ﬁghting and therefore considered safe for returns, there are limited access to basic services, limited income and livelihood opportunities, and increased resource demands which prevent voluntary returns and also cause societal tensions among IDPs, returnees and host communities (Source: Multi-sector needs assessments and Livelihoods and Economic Recovery Assessments). The current transitioning of the conﬂict from a purely humanitarian to that of a recovery phase has seen an expansion of development actors in the operational context, as the humanitarian-development nexus approach takes hold. Conﬂict-affected women and youth need to be provided with vocational skills training, livelihoods and economic empowerment options to curtail negative coping mechanisms and reduce their vulnerability. Empowering them will also restore their self-conﬁdence and dignity and build their resilience for recovery, reintegration, and rehabilitation which correspond to humanitarian-development nexus requirements. Also, the participation of women and youth is key in conﬂict resolution, reconciliation, and peacebuilding efforts, as communities have been socially divided and there is high mistrust, especially towards those suspected of association with non-state armed groups. 2 - Activities

The project activities will focus on two-sector objectives, the first component will address objective two, support livelihood stabilization and income recovery through the following activities: Skills training and Income-Generation Activities/Livelihoods through Providing vocational skills training for women and youth; creating access to IGAs to boost their socio-economic capital and self-reliance. Assessments will be undertaken to determine preferred and innovative context-demand-driven vocational and income-generating activities for women and youth, including non-traditional skills normally available at vocational centers. Upon completion of training, beneﬁciaries will be supported to form cooperatives; provided with start-up kits/small grants for the establishment ofmicro/small-scale enterprises. Also, drip irrigation technology will be provided to women to support vegetable production during the dry season for increased proﬁt. Women and young mothers will be supported with alternative childcare options to provide them time for their training/businesses. Activities include: • Undertake an assessment on preferred vocational skills and IGAs/livelihood options by women and youth and existing vocational skills centers as well as potential employers • Equip 400 female youth and 200 male youth with vocational skills • Strengthen existing vocational training program delivery mechanisms • Train 400 women/female youth and 200 boys/young men on literacy and numeracy skills • Train 1000 women on marketable income generation skills of their choice • Train 1,000 women on entrepreneurship, value chain and access to market strategies • Organize women into 50 groups/cooperatives (30 members per group) and provide them with start-up funds/kits • Train 300 women on improved parboiling techniques (IPT) • Organize women trained on parboiling skills into 13 cooperatives (30 per group) and provide them with basic parboiling equipment/start-up funds • Provide 6 rice milling destoning machines to the cooperatives of women trained on IPT (for value-added processing) • Support 300 women farmers with climate-smart and labor-saving technology, including Drip irrigation for production of high-value vegetables • Support young mothers with context-speciﬁc alternative childcare facilities (Target to be determined by the number of beneﬁciaries with young children) • Establish a mentoring and coaching system involving young women mentors. The second component of the project will focus on objective three, promoting inclusiveness and peaceful co-existence among IDPs in host communities, returnees, and host communities. it will emphasis, engagement and participation of women and youth in peace processes and will support the implementation of National and State Action Plans on Resolution 1325 developed in Borno and Yobe States. Peace huts will be established to provide a space for community women and youth to discuss issues of peace, and the prevention of gender-based violence (GBV). The project will support women to work in partnership with the police to strengthen community policing and provide an alternative justice system for rural women, through mediation that adheres to national and international human rights standards. Activities include: • Train 400 women, 200 female youth and 100 male youth between the age of 18-25 on mediation and peacebuilding techniques/recovery processes • Organize advocacy meetings and facilitate the representation/ participation of women and youth in entrepreneurship and vocational learning, community/state peace, and recovery structures• Link trained women and youth with media organizations, and support their participation in economic recovery discussions and the dissemination of peace and reconciliation messages • Establish 4 ‘Peace Huts’ in communities and support their operationalization • Sensitized 300 community leaders on gender equality and women’s rights.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

UN Women will work with community members including women, men, girls, and boys, particularly adolescent female-headed households, and people with disabilities to establish gender-sensitive and appropriate compliant mechanisms in project target communities. The established complaint channels will provide for women and girls, particularly young adolescent female-headed households and people with disabilities that may be directly affected by the project a means to raise complaints about the quality of program and or staff behavior including volunteers. UN Women and partners shall work with the project team, beneficiaries, partners, Protection Early recovery Sectors and local actors like Volunteers and community support groups to develop a combination of feedback mechanism, starting with the simple ones such as simple forms for staff and volunteers to use daily to record key feedback and program complaints heard from community members during the early stage of a response. Stationed desk or designated place with focal persons at set times by a staff member or volunteer, allowing community members to approach with program queries, feedback, and complaints as needed. The project will set an inclusive community committee trained on gender equality, designated to record complaints and feedback in target LGAs. The committee and community need to be briefed on the committee’s role, and a logbook supplied for recording all complaints and feedback. Regular meeting times will be agreed for staff and the committee to review the logbook together, and deciding action needed.
