Project Summary

WFP School Feeding Programme aims to improve children’s learning by addressing the root causes of hunger and micronutrient deficiencies that can affect their cognitive growth.
In the host community, WFP will provide a packet of 75gms of micronutrient-fortified biscuits to pre-primary and primary school children in Government schools, and community-based schools every school day.
In camps, children are benefitted from different programmes of food and nutrition assistance which are absent in the host communities. Considering these food and nutrition supports in the camps 50-gram packets are provided to children enrolled in learning centers every school day.
These locally produced biscuits are fortified with 16 vitamins and minerals and provide approximately half of the daily micronutrient requirements for a child between 5 and 11 years.
WFP also implements health, WASH, and nutrition activities and will conduct awareness-raising activities with parents, School Management Committees, teachers, and children on health, hygiene, nutrition, and other social issues as supplementary activities.
Through implementing partner WFP provides literacy-based activity supports in 78 government primary schools in Ukhiya. The literacy programme aims to develop early-grade students’ reading competencies and reading habits through the provision of teacher’s skill development training, learning materials support, providing a print-rich learning environment, classroom-based libraries, competency-based story books, community mobilization, etc. This programme specifically supports grade one and two students developing reading competencies and covers all students in developing reading habits in government primary schools.
The school feeding programme both in the Rohingya refugee context in camps and the host community of Bangladesh has the potential to bring transformative benefits to gender roles and relations.
By encouraging girl’s regular attendance in schools, the programme challenges traditional gender norms that often prioritize boys' education, empowering girls to pursue their education.
Through the equal provision of nutritious meals to all students, regardless of gender, the programme promotes a more equitable environment, breaking down gender-based disparities in access to education and nutrition.
By showcasing the importance of nutrition and education for all children, the programme challenges stereotypes and fosters a more inclusive understanding of gender roles within the community.
The programme's success relies on community participation, involving parents and caregivers of both genders, which can lead to more equitable decision-making and involvement in education.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Teknaf and Ukhiya subdistricts of Cox’s Bazar are particularly vulnerable to disasters due to a combination of geographical and environmental factors. Located in the southeastern region of Bangladesh, this area is prone to tropical cyclones, storm surges, and heavy rainfall, primarily because of its proximity to the Bay of Bengal. The flat topography, low-lying coastal areas, and dense population contribute to increased susceptibility. Moreover, climate change has intensified the frequency and severity of natural disasters in this region. Rising sea levels and warmer sea surface temperatures lead to stronger cyclones and heightened storm surges. These geographical locations & these disasters result in the loss of livelihoods, agricultural productivity, and infrastructure, pushing the population deeper into poverty. Additionally, the influx of Rohingya refugees in 2017 has strained local resources & increased competition for jobs, making it even more difficult for the existing population to escape poverty.In the Rohingya refugee camps, the situation is also deteriorating with the initiation of ration cuts due to funding shortage, which pulled down the per-person per-month cash assistance transfer value from USD 12 to 8. This ration cut intensifies the nutrition deficiencies for all, but the most vulnerable are the children. It has led to increased child involvement in wage-earning activities (20% adolescent boys & 19% adolescent girls), causing a decline in learning center attendance. Additionally, early marriages have surged following the ration cut (8%), according to the 2023 Joint-Multi Sector Needs Assessment (J-MSNA). 79% of school-aged girls & 49% of boys (aged 5-18) reportedly could not enroll & attend education during the school year 2022-2023 while 28% of school-aged girls and boys (aged 5-18) in the host communities also suffered the same. In this refugee context, education is not a priority for 23% of adolescent boys and 21% of adolescent girls aged 12 to 18, the same study says. Both camp & host community students, residing in poverty-prone areas, tend to attend schools on empty stomachs, impacting cognitive function, and impeding the learning curve. Here, fortified biscuits of the school feeding programme, enriched with 16 micronutrients, serve as a crucial incentive, addressing short-term hunger and providing 71% of the daily micronutrients needed which contribute to optimal cognitive development. 45,000 school-going children of age range 5 to 14 of host communities along with 260,000 children of age range 3 to 18 in the camps are expected to receive 75 gram-packet and 50 gram-packet of fortified biscuit respectively in every school day throughout the year. In camps, children are benefitted from different programmes of food and nutrition assistance which are absent in the host communities. Considering these food and nutrition supports in the camps 50 gram-packets are provided there. In the Rohingya refugee context, gender inequality is another prevalent issue. Women & girls are disproportionately disadvantaged due to several factors.Firstly, cultural norms & conservative practices within the community often restrict women's mobility & participation in public life limiting access to education, healthcare, and economic opportunities.Secondly, crowded living conditions in the camps expose women to increased risks of gender-based violence, including domestic abuse & sexual exploitation.Additionally, lack of proper sanitation facilities and privacy can further compromise women's safety & dignity.Furthermore, women often bear the primary responsibility for household and caregiving duties, leaving them with limited time & energy for personal development or income-generating activities.In summary, gender inequality in the Rohingya refugee context predominantly affects women and girls due to cultural norms, increased vulnerability to violence, inadequate living conditions, & disproportionate caregiving burden.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The School Feeding Programme of the World Food Programme (WFP) conducts its operations in both camps and host communities by collaborating with partners. In the 33 camps in Cox's Bazar, one cooperating partner assumes responsibility for overall operations and coordination, covering all learning centers. This partner engages in coordination with CiC offices and government stakeholders through various meetings and reporting mechanisms, attending camp coordination meetings, education partner gatherings, and other cross-sectoral integration activities to prevent service overlap and implement the programme efficiently in geographically contiguous areas.In Ukhiya and Teknaf host communities, a similar approach is employed, with one cooperating partner assigned to each geographical area. In host communities, partners coordinate with local government officials, including the Upazila Nirbahi Officer, Upazila Education Officer, and Upazila Health & Family Planning Officer.Cooperating partners of the School Feeding Programme implement the programme in the same region and sector for multiple years to ensure predictability and continuity, despite the rigorous annual selection process. Securing funding for multiple years poses a significant challenge. For the phase-out strategy, the handover of relevant facilities, case files, and other elements to new partners will be conducted responsibly, respecting data protection protocols during a reasonable transition period. The performance, code of conduct, and adherence to the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) principles remain central to the cooperating partners' implementation.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

School Feeding will not be directly involved in protection but the program will ensure no harm to the program participants. The program staff and the partner staff are trained on protection issues, especially PSEA and regular monitoring will be in place to ensure the protection issues are addressed.

3.3 - Age & Disability

School Feeding Programme targets the age group of 3-17 years in the camps and 4-14 years in the host community. WFP ensures that all children attending the learning centers and in the schools who have disabilities are included in the programme.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

In the complex context of the Rohingya refugee crisis, ensuring Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) within the School Feeding Programme is pivotal. Active community engagement and participation stand as foundational principles, necessitating regular consultations and feedback sessions to involve the Rohingya community in decision-making processes. Clear and accessible information dissemination, considering linguistic and educational diversity, is imperative to keep the affected population informed about programme objectives, distribution schedules, and nutritional details. A community feedback mechanism, a two-way communication channel for the Rohingya to express concerns and suggestions is in place both by WFP and its cooperating partner organizations. Cultural sensitivity and gender considerations play a paramount role in AAP. Rigorous monitoring and evaluation, conducted with active community participation, offer insights into programme impact and effectiveness, enabling timely adjustments. Capacity-building initiatives empower the Rohingya community to advocate for their needs, fostering a sense of ownership, specifically the engagement of Rohingya volunteers in the distribution processes. In essence, by adhering to these principles, the School Feeding Programme aims to ensure AAP in the Rohingya refugee context, aligning humanitarian assistance with the specific needs and preferences of the community while upholding dignity and promoting a collaborative approach.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

WFP, in collaboration with its partner NGOs, offers comprehensive training and orientation to its personnel regarding the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). Additionally, the NGO partners involved in the School Feeding Programme equip schools and learning centers with a dedicated PSEA reporting hotline number to address any related concerns. Both WFP and its partner organizations maintain a strict policy of zero tolerance for sexual exploitation and abuse.
