Project Summary

It is estimated that 80 per cent of the 278,938 students (male 143,973 and female 134,965) registered at UNRWA schools in the 2018/2019 school year in Gaza require assistance in overcoming a variety of challenges, including poverty, additional learning needs, access restrictions and electricity shortages at home. Recurrent hostilities and the blockade continue to have a deleterious impact on the learning environment for Palestine refugee children in Gaza, who are accommodated in 274 schools, 70 per cent of which operate on a double-shift basis. In response, the Agency-wide Education in Emergencies (EiE) approach provides inclusive, equitable, quality and safe education to Palestine refugee children and youth in times of emergencies. In addition to implementing the EiE approach across all Agency schools in Gaza, UNRWA will: (i) through the recruitment and training of additional support teachers and the provision of remedial materials, assist selected students in grades 3 and 4 who are struggling to meet minimum academic standards in Arabic and mathematics; and (ii) ensure that 80 percent of children have access to basic school and learning materials through the distribution of back-to-school and recreational kits and stationery. In line with cluster logical frame work and plan, this intervention will target 15,558 students for remedial education assisted by 230 support teachers.

Needs

In the school year 2018/19, 278,938 students (143,973 male and 134,965 female) attend 274 UNRWA schools across Gaza. This represents an increase of more than 7,000 students compared to last year. To accommodate such growth, 70% of UNRWA schools are operating on double shifts basis. The increasing number of students further stretches the capacity of UNRWA schools, leaving little time for support or extracurricular activities although these are vital to foster a child’s psycho-social well-being.According to the country report on out-of-school children (OOSC , 2018), barriers to accessing and completing education include : - low academic achievement and lack of interest in education among children which is a main reason for dropping out ; - Well-being of the child and the family e.g. external shocks such as health crisis or death in the family- The economic barriers arising from the cost of education e.g uniforms, textbooks ,school stationary, transportation - Exclusionary administrative policies and practices. In-light of the above, UNRWA estimates that, 80% students are in need of humanitarian education assistance. Such students are poor students, students with special needs (estimated as 10,000), low achievers (approximately 75,000 ), new entrants or first graders (34,160), repeater (2,974 -1,954 boys and 1,020 girls), drop outs (1,430 - 1,100 boys and 333 girls), over age students, students living in Access Restricted Areas who are all in need of support and empowerment. The unprecedented deterioration of the economic situation compiled with the energy crisis seriously impact the educating environment of children. This situation does not facilitate at-risk-of-failure students , who are already in need for extra support in several topics, including Arabic and Mathematics. Moreover, there is a critical need in terms of providing all low-achieving students, repeaters and at-risk-of-failure students with learning materials not only in Arabic and Mathematics but also in the other curriculum's core subjects. However, line with cluster logical frame work and plan , this intervention will target 15,558 students for remedial education assisted by 230 support teachers.The blockade, as well as the dire socio-economic situation, have had serious repercussions on the psychosocial well-being of Palestine refugees. As a result of the current crisis in Gaza, the WHO estimated that approximately 10,400 people (5% of the population) will have severe mental health problems, and 41,700 (20% of the population) will have mild to moderate problems requiring MHPSS, including 26,000 children. UNRWA conducted a study in May 2017 in Gaza assessing the psychosocial well-being of 2,262 adult refugees attending 15 health centres and 3,142 refugee students attending 45 UNRWA elementary and preparatory schools. The study indicated a high level of psychosocial stress among both students and adults, with almost one half of adults (48.9%) experiencing poor well-being (63% of these warranting further screening for depression) and almost 30% of children experiencing serious difficulties. Both children and parents reported a prevalence of emotional problems (students spoke of a continuous sense of fear and anxiety, as well as learning difficulties and lack of future career opportunities; adults had difficulties identifying their strengths and coping resources, focusing instead only on problems and hardships). therefore , psycho social support to student is an integral component of building resilience for students during emergencies.

Activities

In response to the described needs to enhance the learning environment for refugee children, UNRWA aims to ensure access to protective, inclusive and child friendly quality education for all boys and girls attending UNRWA schools. UNRWA focuses on two key interventions to ensure refugee children are able to access education by supporting students struggling to meet minimum academic standards and ensuring that all children have access to basic school stationary and appropriate learning materials. These will include students from grades 3 and 4 who fail at the end of the 1st semester in Arabic and Mathematics as well as students at risk of failing the 2nd semester in those topics. Focusing on the basic concepts and skills that the students need in order to deal with the 2nd semester curricula in addition to increasing the drills of evaluation activities and exercises related to individual differences between students, the materials will include the formative assessment tools that guide the teachers on the mechanism of the student’s academic improvement. Support materials will be used by regular teachers, support teachers and parents and will be prepared by the Professional Development and Curricula Unit coordinators in cooperation with the education specialists at UNRWA Education Programme. To ensure the success of educational support, all support teachers will assist low achieving students in during the scholastic year. Support teachers will check students’ work, monitor and follow up low achieving students’ performance, communicate with parents and school management, encourage and motivate students, prepare regular quizzes to follow students’ progress, follow up students’ homework and assignments and liaise with regular teachers on catch up plans and student levels. Regular-class teachers and school principals will monitor and evaluate the performance of the support teachers to make sure that they support students in the best way. To ensure that children are prepared to go back to school, UNRWA will provide them with basic stationary to participate in class and complete study at home at the start of each semester. UNRWA will provide 231,192 (80%) of all students with a range of subject copybooks, pens, pencils and erasers thereby ensuring the provision of teaching and learning requirements according to minimum humanitarian standards. In line with cluster logical frame work and plan, this intervention will target 15,558 students for remedial education assisted by 230 support teachers. On the other hand , the psycho social activities and interventions at 230 UNRWA schools will include the structured life skills and school guidance program, which is complemented by parent education sessions to promote a holistic and integrated approach to child and family well being. This is not indicative of a decline for individual and group counselling, but reflects a need to ensure that all children have essential life skills (peer relations, tolerance, and conflict resolution skills) to succeed in life. Individual and group counselling remain important interventions for children experiencing more serious psychosocial difficulties.
