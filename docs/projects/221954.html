Project Summary

In this project, Save A Life International will target 6952 (3476 Girls) school aged among internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees and host communities in Aweil South, Northern Bahr El Ghazal State. Activities in the project include and not limited to back to learning campaigns to raise awareness on importance of education, increase enrollment and retention of boys and girls in schools, establishment of learning spaces, latrines and corresponding wash facilities in locations with no schools or where learning is being conducted under trees, rehabilitation of damaged learning spaces, hygiene promotion and provision of Sanitation and Hygiene materials (hand washing soap) in schools, provision of age appropriate recreation materials to primary and Early Childhood learners, training of teachers in education in emergency pedagogical skills and child centered methodologies to improve teaching and learning processes, training teachers and learners on psycho-social support.
To integrate the education response, referrals of child protection concerns will be done to protection partners while cases of malnutrition will be referred to nutrition partners for specialized care. Health and WASH partners will be engaged in life saving messages in schools and at the community level. School Management Committees (SMCs) and Parent Teachers Associations (PTAs) will be trained on school management and governance, psycho-social support (PSS), Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and methods they can use to mobilize resources to support schools and learning in their communities. Save a Life International will work closely with the Ministry of Education . the education cluster at state and national levels to coordinate the response and for quality assurance.

1. Needs

As of 25th September 2024, an inter-agency rapid needs assessment (IRNA) in Aweil East, West, North and South counties in Northern Bahr El Ghazal found 154,960 people severely impacted by floods, with Aweil East and Aweil South being the most affected. According to the IRNA, health, education, nutrition, water and sanitation services have been disrupted, with 29 health facilities, 103 water points and 127 schools damaged. The negative impact of flooding in the state is being witnessed at a time when the state is experiencing increased returnees from Sudan. As of 15th September 2024, the number of returnees in the state were more than 56,986. In Aweil South, more than 37,500 people are displaced by floods and the number is projected to increase in the last quarter of 2024. Destruction of schools by floods has disrupted learning and teaching and is expected to increase the number of out of school boys and girls in the county. Already, an estimated 1,060,000 girls and 940,000 boys between 6 and 17 years are at risk of missing education in South Sudan including 1 million children between 3 and 5 years of age. This is attributed to significant obstacles in accessing early childhood development education, early marriage, gender-based violence, lack of learning spaces, displacements induced by floods, conflict and food insecurity, lack of awareness on importance of education, lack of trained teachers and little investment in the education sector. Whereas the primary focus for 2024 and 2025 humanitarian and education cluster response plan is to enhance access to safe and conducive learning environments for vulnerable groups, particularly IDPs and returnees, and catering for the educational needs of over 311,000 IDPs, 1,535,000 resident communities and 189,000 returnees, addressing existing challenges require a combination of interventions that address scarcity of teachers,teacher’s training, ensuring acceptable pupil- teacher ratios, provision of teaching materials and life-saving incentives to ensure quality education. Moreover, efforts will be made in ensuring learning materials for students, including those in early childhood education. The current textbook ratios at 8:1 for primary students s needs to be addressed and comply with the recommended 1:1 guideline. Further, considerable efforts should be made in providing a secure and enabling learning environment and facilities with a particular focus on gender-sensitive learning spaces and segregated WASH facilities. Special focus is needed to address education needs of children with disabilities and residing in crisis-affected areas to ensure they continue to receive adequate support for quality education.

2. Project activities

Outcome 1: 6952 school aged children comprising of 1952 Host Community,3000 Internally Displaced and 2000 Returnees are having access to adequate, safe and quality early childhood and primary education in Aweil South County. Output 1.1: Increased awareness on importance of enrollment and retention of children in schools.Activity 1.1.1: Save a Life International will conduct education workshop in Aweil South with local authorities, Ministry of education officials and stakeholders, representatives of teachers, school management committees, parent teachers associations, chiefs, IDPs, host community and returnees representatives to provide information on the project, clarify the role of the community and Save a Life International in the implementation, obtain community’s views and support of the project and advocate for increased and meaningful participation of women, youth, persons with disability and marginalized communities in the project. Activity 1.1.2: Conduct back to learning campaigns at Payam, Boma and school levels to increase enrollment and retention of children in schools. In 12 Months, save a Life International in close collaboration with the Ministry of Education, Project Implementation Committee, Local authorities, teachers, PTAs and SMCs will conduct Back to Learning Campaigns targeting parents and pupils. The aim of the BTL Campaigns will be to create awareness on importance of education, mobilize parents to enroll and retain children in school. Output 1.2: Children are having increased access to temporary learning spaces and corresponding WASH facilities in hard to reach areas and in locations where learning is being conducted under trees.Activity 1.2.1: Conduct a rapid mapping of education facilities and needs in Aweil South: Save a Life International, Project Implementation Committee, SMCs, PTAs, MoEST Officials will conduct a rapid needs assessment in Aweil South prior to commencing education activities. The assessment will focus on enrollment, out of school children, state of learning spaces, wash facilities and safety in schools. Thereafter, each target school will be assisted to develop realistic action plan to address or mobilize needed resources to establish or rehabilitate learning and wash facilities. In this project, SLI will establish 5 learning spaces with corresponding latrines and hand washing facilities in locations with no schools or where there is overcrowding. The facilities to be established will be as per the design developed by UNICEF and MoEST for TLSs in South Sudan. Output 1.3: Children and teachers have access to learning and teaching materials in target schools and communities.Activity 1.3.1: To improve on quality of learning, recreational and teaching, Save A Life International will collaborate with the Education cluster focal points, MoEST Officials, UNICEF, SMCs, PTAs to map the number and quality of learning and teaching supplies in each of the targeted schools. Thereafter, SLI will raise a request of missing supplies from core-pipeline, transport and distribute them on respective schools. In this project, SLI will distribute learning and recreational supplies to 8,600 pupils and teaching supplies to 250 teachers. Activity 1.3.2: Training and support of teachers: To improve on quality of learning, Save A life International will conduct inception and refresher training to existing and new teachers. The training will focus on new curriculum, ensuring protection in schools, peace building, psycho social support, education monitoring, Pedagogy and prevention of physical exploitation and abuse in schools. Activity 1.3.3: Training of PTAs and SMCs:To enhance education management and resource mobilization, 160 PTAs and 90 SMCs will be trained in targeted schools on how to support teachers, manage schools, ensure safety and protection of learners and teachers.
