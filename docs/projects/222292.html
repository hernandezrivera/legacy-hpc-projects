Project Summary

This project will be implemented by Environmental Action for Sustainable Development (EASD) to provide life-saving WASH services aimed at meeting the critical needs of returnees, host communities, and IDPs in 16 payams of Uror and Canal/Pigi County, Jonglei State, who are affected by climate shock, conflict, and GBV threats when accessing WASH facilities. The project is aiming at providing safe, efficient, and equitable access to sufficient water sanitation and hygiene services to mitigate WASH-related diseases and gender-based violence among 25,165 returnees, IDPs, and host communities, and 2% of persons with disabilities in Uror and Canal/Pigi County. Targeted sites are communal water collection points, nutrition facilities, and public places agreed upon after a GBV risk analysis through focus group discussions and meetings with women and girls and people with disabilities. The budget to achieve this project is estimated at $ 500,000 for two counties.
Activities that will be implemented in Uror and Pigi County for 25,165: 10,713 in Canal/Pigi and 14,452 in Uror are 2,675 men, 3,505 women, 2,186 boys, and 2,347 girls; and 3,547 men, 4,371women, 3,092 boys, and 3,442 girls; and 2% people with disabilities, for 12 months, except for hygiene promotion activity for 6 months, in close coordination with government and, local authorities, youths and women leaders, beneficiaries, and humanitarian partners within Uror and Canala/Pigi County and the WASH Cluster, Nutrition, Health, and Protection Cluster at the national and sub-national level. The activities prioritised in this intervention are based on facts recorded from the targeted project areas during the recent information from the government authority in Uror and Canal/Pigi County. The project will ensure that WASH services are provided in safe and secure locations and that facilities are gender-sensitive and accessible to people with special needs. The project will begin by jointly reviewing the needs of beneficiary members of each activity site and the technical assessment of target WASH facilities suggested by beneficiary members for repairs. 8 water points will be rehabilitated by flashing, pump testing, water quality analysis, platform construction, and the replacement of worn-out spare parts in all selected locations. 3 water points will be drilled and constructed in nutrition, IDPS site and Host community and with flood resilience design, 6 blocks of sanitation facilities will be rehabilitated in nutrition, IDPs sites and host community considering stances for persons with disability, construction & rehabilitation of 13 blocks of permanent and semi-permanent latrine in nutrition sites, IDPS and host community, including persons with disability, hand washing facilities will be constructed/ installed for the all constructed latrines, safety audit will be conducted for WASH facilities, distribution of WASH NFIs to children and caretaker in the nutrition site and IDPs, returnees and host community, provision of hygiene promotion activities, training of 11 community water user committees for the rehabilitated and drilled water points in Uror and Canal/Pigi county, 99 members where 60% of the members will be female, and training of community pump mechanics as a sustainability and exit strategy of the project, there will be 7 members per water point, 77 pump mechanics, and 30% trainees will be female.
EASD will build the capacity of female WASH staff deployed on the ground to lead in observing and reporting on AAP indicators, coordinate with protection partners on the ground, and support beneficiaries with feedback and complaints. EASD will coordinate the project activities with the Department of Rural Water and Sanitation in the county, and the RRC Secretariat already has strong experience working with the communities and authorities in South Sudan counties and will prioritise reporting as communication gaps will be improved by the field staff from the project locations.

1. Needs

Uror and Canal/Pigi are the hard-to-reach counties in Jonglei state due to road access and insecurity. No recent IRNA was conducted in the two counties. The population of Uror and Canal/Pigi County is estimated to be 245,386 and 136,742, respectively, according to the South Sudan National Bureau of Statistics 2017, of which about 36% are still living as IDPs and in refugee camps. Uror IRNA was conducted in 2019, and Canal/Pigi in 2023, respectively, and as of now, no IRNA has been conducted in all the counties, including 16 Payams such as Alam, Atar, Belewach, Kaldak, Korwach, Mareng, Nyainthokmalual, and Wunlem in Canal Pigi and Karam, Motot, Pathai, Payai, Pieri, Puolchuol, Tiam, and Uror in Uror County. According to the previous IRNA report and RRC coordinators, the WASH situation in the two counties is alarming; in Uror, Tear Fund was the only WASH partner doing hardware, but their project ended in Sept 2024. As of now, Uror has no WASH partner. Canal/Pigi IRNA was conducted in 2023 and organised by the Protection Cluster, showing an alarming lack of clean drinking water, poor sanitation, and hygiene practices that increase WASH-related diseases in the counties. On the recent phone call to Pigi RRC, there are PAH and ALASKA doing hardware in the health facility; hence, the WASH need is in an alarming situation. This project is targeting 25165: 10,713 in Canal/Pigi: 2,675 men, 3,505 women, 2186 boys, and 2347 girls; and 14,450 in Uror: 3,547 men, 4,371 women, 3,092 boys, and 3,442 girls. People with disabilities are estimated at 2% of the total population, and with more returnees arriving from IDP and refugee camps, the majority of the population continues to share existing houses as a result of flooding and insecurity in Uror and Canal/Pigi. Facing a huge and alarming need for WASH services since latrines are lacking and have collapsed due to floods, and most boreholes are nonfunctional due to difficulties in maintenance. Kits for constructing sanitation facilities are lacking. Most handpumps, broken and functional boreholes in areas affected by floods, were submerged in water, which resulted in the contamination of boreholes in Uror and Canal/Pigi in the targeted 16 Payams. Achieving water quantity and quality in Uror and Canal/Pigi is a key priority for the affected population due to the turbidity of the water from the boreholes caused by solid particles, worms, and rusted pipes inside the water. According to RRC coordinators and County WASH focal persons in Uror and Canal/Pigi County, diarrhoeal disease is highly recorded, as witnessed on facility records, showing that mostly children and the elderly are affected rather than other age groups; women and girls have no access to appropriate sanitary protection materials. Soap is not easily available in the local markets. High public health risk realised and observed as there is direct open defecation practiced near water sources and swampy areas, and it's much worse in Uror. According to county RRC coordinators, there were no sanitation and hygiene promotion services provided all these years in the counties, which made the community practice open defecation due to a lack of awareness on how to construct a latrine in flood-prone areas, therefore increasing the prevalence of WASH-related diseases. There are existing protection concerns for women and girls. With armed groups from neighbouring Pibor and nearby communities where cattle were raided and children were abducted anywhere, where women and children of both sexes fear, there are reported experiences of sexual gender-based violence (GBV), especially when collecting firewood, wild food, and fetching water. Women and girls are also lacking dignity kits, and the coping mechanism for MHM among women is risky. With existing persons with special needs, there is a need to strengthen protection action through WASH intervention by rehabilitating and drilling a borehole within the proximity of the human settlement.

2. Project activities

The key project achievable and deliverable activities to realise the achievement of the project are (1) community engagement meetings involving women, girls, boys, men, and people with disabilities for the project kick-off meeting. (2) Distribution of WASH NFIs such as soap, buckets with and without taps, menstrual hygiene kits, aqua-tabs, and water purifiers to children and caretakers at the nutrition site, IDPs, returnees, and host communities affected by crises in Uror and Canal/Pigi, (3) Training of 96 hygiene promoters and 16 supervisors; 6 hygiene promoters and 1 supervisor per payam, (4) Construction of 4 blocks of latrine in Uror and Canal/Pigi at nutrition site 3 stances per block. (5) Rehabilitation of 2 blocks of latrines in Uror and Canal/Pigi County at nutrition sites, each with 3 stances, including for people with disabilities. (6) Rehabilitation of 3 hand pumps in nutrition sites in Uror and Canal/Pigi, following WASH Cluster guidelines (7) Drilling and construction of 3 water facilities in IDPs and host communities in Uror and Canal/Pigi County. (8) Rehabilitation of 5 nonfunctional water points in IDPS sites and host communities in Uror and Canal/Pigi (9) Conducting hygiene promotion activities targeting nutrition facilities, schools, markets, IDP sites, and the host community. (10) Construction of 5 blocks of semi-permanent latrines with a flood-resilient design 8 stances each and 2 stances each will be designed for people with disabilities. (11) Rehabilitation of 2 blocks of semi-permanent latrine with flood resilience design (12) Conducting WASH barrier analysis at all the project sites (13) Conducting safety audits at all the project sites, (14) training of 3 community water user committees, 9 members per water point, for a total of 27; 60% of the members will be female; (15) training of 21 community pump technicians for the drilled water points in Uror and Canal/Pigi, 7 members per water point, as a sustainability and exit strategy of the project; and (16) construction and installation of hand washing facilities at all the rehabilitated and constructed latrines in the project locations, (17) refresher training of 8 community water user committee 9 members per water point, for a total of 72 members Pump mechanics 56 persons.
