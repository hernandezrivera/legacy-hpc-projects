Project Summary

Education management responsibility in Sudan is shared between the federal, state and
locality levels of government. According to Sudan Federal ministry of education, General
education sectoral strategy plan and report 2018/19-22/23,
. Basic
education GER remained stable at 72% between 2009 and 2016 before increasing by a
single point to 73% in 2017. In secondary, the GER increased by 5 percentage points from
34% in 2009 to 39% in 2016 before dropping a point in 2017 to settle at 38%. There are
more boys accessing basic schools compared to girls, the former recording a GER of 75%
while the latter posted 71%. In majority of the states, the gap between boys and girls is
considerably small but in some cases, the disparity is overwhelming. In secondary, girls
have a coverage of 41% compared to 35% for boys, this pattern is evident in all the states
expect Central Darfur, West Kordofan, Al Gadarif and West Darfur where boys have slightly
higher GER. The National Learning assessment conducted in 2014 established that 40%
of learners in grade 3 in Sudan could not read familiar words compared to an average of
20% from countries drawn from the Middle East and North Africa region. In terms of oral
reading fluency, measured by the number of correct words read per minute by the learners,
the assessment established an average of 14 words per minute. Significant number of
children are still excluded from a conducive learning environment. Only two thirds of
learners in government basic education schools are seated. 6% of learners in basic
education do not have access to water supply and the ratio of learners to toilets is very
high 1:102 for boys and 1:82 for girls. About half of learners in basic education are
enrolled in schools that are not connected to any source of electricity.
This project will enhance retention in basic education and improve quality at basic education. Using the child-friendly school strategy, schools will be safe, protective spaces where children learn
new behaviors and social norms related to protection. In addition to the construction and
rehabilitation of primary schools, this project will build the capacity of teachers and PTAs
to support an inclusive education agenda in 5 states (Central and West Darfur, Khartoum,
Blue Nile, and South Kordofan). The initiative is conflict-sensitive to avoid risks of harm to
vulnerable groups. Girls and other vulnerable populations will be given special focus for
protection. Measures will be taken to identify and mitigate the most salient barriers to
participation. Given the socio-cultural context and the risk asymmetries, female
participation and voice in all aspects of education will be prioritized, thus improving
inclusivity and access.
The project targets 35,961 children among whom are 17,261 boys and 18,700 girls.
28,275 Adult and 3626 Elders.

1 - Needs

According to UNICEF 2021 report, It’s estimated that over three million children here, aged5-13 years, are not in the classroom. Seventy-six percent of primary age children attendschool; in secondary that figure dips to 28 percent. At 43 percent, Blue Nile is the worstperforming state for children who are out of primary school. Conflict, a lack of awarenessabout the importance of education and prolonged under-development all contribute to thepoor schooling of boys and girls in Sudan. The inability to pay fees—even though schoolis free per government policy—prevents many poor families from sending children toschool. Finally, cultural pressures and the traditional views of the role of women resulted afewer girls attend, and remain in, school. In Sudan, more than 3.6 million children, or onethird of the school-aged population, do not go to school. There are disparities among thisfigure: girls, children affected by conflict, refugees, internally displaced persons (IDPs),children in rural areas, and children from poor households are most vulnerable to the lackof schooling. Poor access to schools is also a major issue in Sudan which contributes tolow schooling rates. Furthermore, the quality of the education is most often poor, with fewavailable teachers, the infrastructures of the schools are strikingly low-quality, and assuch school environments are often unsafe. (Only 45.5% of the schools have access toclean water.) Missing out on school can have very detrimental consequences for thechild’s physical and mental health: children are then more likely to suffer from child labor,poor mental health, and sexual violence. (UNICEF Sudan, 2021, “Education”). The literacyrate is particularly low: 45.2 percent of girls and women aged 15-24 are unable to read orwrite. Sexual violence and assault were also reported: women and girls in Darfur spokeout on the assaults they face when they are going to school. In addition, there isdiscrimination in class towards girls. In addition, one of the finding of WR protectionmonitoring in Nertiti was, there is no school in the area. Some of the families are sendingtheir children to Thur village school. But, the distance from Boldong village to Thur villageis very far away and insecure. In some areas, there is a school for children. But, it doesn’thave the facility it needs. Therefore, children will remain at home without education. Inaddition, adult education for women is another need mentioned in the discussion.Because of the cultural taboo before most women are illiterate that has an impact on theirdecision-making power. The continues conflict in in Central, West Darfur, Bule Nile, SouthKordofan and Khartoum has resulted in displacement, livelihood disruption, and hascontributed to high dropout rates in education. In addition, according to NHO 2022 report,all the localities in Central Darfur, West Darfur and Blue Nile and in South Kordofan andKhartoum leveled 4 in education program need. Furthermore, West Darfur state has one ofthe highest reported number of children in grade 3, 59%, who are not able to read. The lowperformance in West Darfur can partly be explained by the highest pupil-teacher ratiowhich leaves 84,907 children of school age 6- 13 out of school with only 18,529 childrenare provided education opportunities at Alternate Learning Program (ALP) centers. It isworth mentioning that 5 localities in west Darfur have a GER of less than 60% and 5 areaffected with conflicts and humanitarian situations. As per the Ministry of Educationrecords in the Blue Nile, the total number of children in Blue Nile State is 292,737 58% ofthose (121,770 children) are out of school because of the war between the Governmentand the SPLM-N, and due to inflation rates and family income (school fees). In all fourstates, the education facilities lack access to protective learning spaces, latrines, andwater and school materials.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Environmental factors may affect children’s access to education through protectionissues. In areas where there is a higher risk of either illicit environmental economies ormodern slavery related to environmental economies such as charcoal, mining, illicitcrop production, sugar production and seed nurseries, children may lose access toeducation due to child labour, smuggling or trafficking. School environment: WR willaddress issues related to the school environment, possibly to add new/temporaryclassrooms to expand schools. To that end, the environmental footprint of schoolconstruction will be considered, to avoid construction waste becoming a site for vectorpropagation or an accident risk. WR will consult USAID sector environmentalguidelines for primary and secondary schools and ensure than an environmentalmanagement plan is developed before expanding or modifying school facilities.Sustainably sourced construction materials will be a condition of tenders, regularremoval of construction wastes, regular elimination of stagnant water and any foodwaste to protect students, and ensure the implementation of water and energy systems to reduce consumption. WR will consult the WASH sectors Where installing orrehabilitating WASH facilities in schools.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

WR will ensuring that beneficiaries safely access their services, without causing harm,and for promoting meaningful access, accountability and participation of beneficiariesin the provision of education assistance. By mainstreaming protection into educationprogramming, WR will maximize the positive impacts of education programs onpeople’s safety and dignity and support affected populations access and enjoy theirrights. safety audit will be conducted to ensure the location of and access routes toschools, classrooms, teacher learning centers, playgrounds and child friendly spacespose no safety risks to children and teachers: In addition, will avoid constructingeducation facilities near military installations and conflict frontlines. WR will makesure the timing of classes is safe for children to travel to and from school withoutexposure to further risk of harm. Classes should take place in daylight hours givingchildren sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. WR will provide separate,secure and private toilet/latrines for boys and girls and separate facilities for studentsand teachers. Ensure toilet/latrine design accounts for children (e.g. size of drop holemay present a safety risk for children). WR will identify groups of children who facedifficulties accessing education (e.g. girls, children with disabilities, children fromminority communities, children belonging to disadvantaged or marginalized groups)and work with children, teachers, community leaders and parents to support equal andsafe access to education without discrimination. WR will create a system that istransparent with the affected populations by providing them with accessible andtimely information on selection criteria for targeted assistance, organizationalprocedures and processes that affect them. In addition, set up accessible, confidentialand well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with aview to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promotebeneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Eg. feedback box atlearning centres, phone lines. Student, teacher, parent and school managementcommittees are democratic and inclusive and members represent all groups within thecommunity (e.g. gender, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, persons with disabilities.
