Project Summary

Ongoing conflict in eastern Ukraine continues to weaken child protection systems creating
new risks for affected boys, girls and their caregivers. To respond to the urgent needs,
UNICEF is planning to strengthen child protection systems (C01) and to provide direct
services to affected children and their families (CO2). Specifically, UNICEF will continue to
provide psycho-social and protective services to children and their caregivers (including to
survivors of gender-based violence, to children affected by violence and to children
affected by mines and unexploded ordinance) (P02), invest in activities aimed at
prevention (violence, mine-risk education) (P01) and ensure capacity building to
community professionals working with children (P02). In addition, UNICEF will carry out
activities directed at improving parenting skills among caregivers to improve and
strengthen relationships between caregivers and their children. Taken together, these
activities are aimed to strengthen local response systems, including availability of
specialized services, as well as to ensure direct service provision where needed services
are not available.

1 - Needs

The situation in eastern Ukraine remains volatile. Some 3.8 million people, including 1million children, are currently affected and over 1.5 million are displaced. Nearly 11,500civilians have lost their lives or were injured in Eastern Ukraine because of the conflict. Ofparticular concern are those children living within 20 kilometres from the 'contact line’ inthe government-controlled and non-government-controlled areas. They continue toexperience recurrent shelling and violence. Disruptions in humanitarian access, the limitedcapacity of local institutions, weak rule of law and restrictions imposed on protectionmonitoring in non-government-controlled areas have all severely weakened the protectiveenvironment. Weakened family and community support mechanisms, daily stressors andloss of hope for the future continue to cause distress for children and their caregivers.This led to economic problems and continuous psycho-social needs. In addition,caregivers, highly affected by stress and hardships, often lack positive parenting skills,thus, failing to ensure the nurturing and caring environment for their children. Landminesand explosive remnants of war (ERW) continue to be a major concern in eastern Ukraineleading to death, injuries and disrupted livelihoods. While the largest concentration ofmines and other ERW are in areas along the 'contact line,' they can also be foundscattered throughout much of Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts. When children or caregiversare severely impaired from a landmine and/or ERW accident, it negatively impacts theirphysical and psycho-social health, their education/training and the ability to sustain theirlivelihoods. At the same time, limited services exist to support mine or ERW victims, andoften their needs go unaddressed. Furthermore, ongoing hostilities and economichardships led to increased family- and gender-based violence and neglect of children.While protection of children's rights, responding to gender-based violence and familyviolence are parts of the National Human Rights Strategy 2020, limited services currentlyexist for survivors of violence. Community services are understaffed and cannoteffectively respond to the problems, especially in the conflict-affected areas. Moreover, therisk of statelessness remains a key concerns, with thousands children born in nongovernment-controlled areas not having state-recognized birth certificates.

2 - Activities & outcomes

In order to address the above needs and achieve positive outcomes for children, thefollowing activities will be undertaken: - Awareness raising and information disseminationamong boys and girls, their caregivers and professionals on gender-based violence,violence against children, mine-risk education (including a digital campaign and directtraining sessions), key risks affecting children; sessions for caregivers on positiveparenting (P01) - Psycho-social support - UNICEF will continue to provide psycho-social,case management and referral services to boys, girls, men and women (including tosurvivors of gender-based violence and violence against children) (CO2/P02) - MineVictim Assistance - UNICEF will provide assistance to victims of mine and ERW accidentsand will work towards strengthening national and local response systems (C02/P02) -Capacity building/system strengthening - UNICEF will provide capacity building tocommunity professionals working with children, as well as to caregivers, to enable themto better respond to conflict-related child protection risks. The key areas include - genderbasedviolence, violence against children, mine risk, child protection in emergencies andconflict-related child protection risks. Additionally, as Sub-Cluster lead, UNICEF will do thefollowing: 1. Organize meetings to review child protection risks and interventions, currentaccountability practices, gaps and opportunities 2. Ensure ongoing capacity building oflocal partners on key child protection issues (Child Protection in Emergencies, ChildProtection Minimum Standards, etc.) 3. Organize and facilitate training for sub-clusterpartners to raise awareness and build capacity on accountability to affected population.Reporting on the results achieved will ensure that disaggregated data is available (sex,age, disability). The following Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) mechanismswill be used during implementation of the projects: -Core Commitments to Children(Emergency handbook) -Harmonized Approach to Cash Transfers framework -UN Protocolon Allegations of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (involving implementing partners) -ChildCentered Disaster Risk Reduction -UNICEF Guidance on Risk-Informed Programming -Guidance on Including Children with Disabilities in Humanitarian Action
