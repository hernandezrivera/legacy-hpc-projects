Project Summary

This project supports the overarching strategy for COVID-19 risk management in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States by identifying the humanitarian frontline workers in the high-risk LGAs and equipped them with the essential medical supplies. Procure and Prepositioning of PPEs at the LGAs levels and ensuring all humanitarian worker are provided with infection prevention and control materials. All the supplies would be received at the various central warehouses in the 3 States and sent to the selected LGAs stores for prepositioning for the staff. Within a period of 6 weeks, the supplies could be received and within 2 weeks distributed to all affected LGAs. The activities of this plan augment the three (3) preparedness and response plans of the SMoHs. These activities are vital in prevention and enhancing the level of COVID-19 response in Nigeria also.
The main objectives of this project are:
1. To provide Frontline Humanitarian Workers with essential Supplies for COVID 19 Response under the relevant interventions including coordination, epidemiological surveillance and Point of Entry surveillance, risk communication/community engagement and psychosocial as well as logistics services.
2. To support the Frontline Humanitarian Workers with guidelines for coordinated public health mitigation and response measures for the COVID-19 pandemic.
3. To procurement and preposition of COVID-19 PPEs, alcohol-based hand sanitizers, and other responses commodities to enhance testing, isolation, treatments, quarantine and prevention.
The project aims to reach 3,843 health personnel across 49 LGAs in the BAY States

1 - Needs

Data from the WHO Early Warning and Response System in June 2019 indicate that malaria compounded by malnutrition is the leading cause of morbidity and mortality for 34.4% of cases and 18.1% of reported deaths. This is either compounded by the fact that 50% of health facilities that would have provided malaria prevention or treatment are completely or partially destroyed. Similarly, congested living environments in IDP camps and host communities along with poor sanitary infrastructures contributed to the massive cholera outbreak in Borno State in 2018 with 6,439 persons affected including 74 associated deaths mostly among IDPs.The impact of the displacement for communities and persons displaced is huge and has remained key to increase in disease situation with consequences. Borno and Adamawa are currently experiencing an outbreak of measles with over 21,052 cases as at the end of December 2019. This is due to non-immunization amongst displaced children from locations that are security-compromised, which further puts at high risk children under 5 years already facing dangerously high levels of malnutrition, endemic malaria and other epidemic prone diseases. In 2018, there were extended cholera outbreaks across the BAY states (6,230 in Borno; 2,498 in Adamawa and 2,108 in Yobe). In 2019, only Adamawa experienced Cholera outbreak with 839 cases and CFR of 0.48% as of 6th December 2019.The deterioration of the security situation in the past months has exacerbated the situation especially for displaced women, children and the elderly being the most vulnerable. The humanitarian community continues to struggle to respond to urgent humanitarian needs with the already overstretched available capacity. Further, risk mitigation measures have been ongoing including enhanced surveillance through travelers screening at points of entry along at-risk borders of BAY states and the 19 States, Chad, Niger and Cameroun. In addition, the three Ministries prioritized LGAs in in the BAY States as being high risk for COVID-19 due to the geographical location and commercial activities, presence of more international humanitarian staff from affected countries, weak health systems and more vulnerable people in IDP camps within FCV settings in the northeast Nigeria.Currently Borno State has demonstrated the capacity to confirm COVID-19. As at April 29,2020, there have been 53 confirmed cases of COVID-19 in Borno; two in Adamawa and one in Yobe. Despite presence of capacity for laboratory confirmation of COVID-19 within Borno, challenges in community-based surveillance and lack of ideal isolation facilities may contribute to increased risk of spread and impact of the disease if it occurs.The success in the intervention requires continued WHO/State leadership and excellence in a wide-range of diverse but interrelated areas of implementing proven strategies for the prevention and possible control COVID-19 outbreaks. These include• strengthening the multi-sectoral coordination of the preparedness; Leadership, Advocacy and Partnership for operational readiness support within the government stakeholders and partners.• enhanced surveillance, including active case finding, case investigation, contact tracing and surveillance at Points of Entry (PoE),• Strengthening diagnostic capabilities; strengthening Laboratory services for confirmation.• case management- the care of COVID-19 patients as the response capacity of health systems is challenged; to ensure that COVID-19 patients can access life-saving treatment without compromising public health objectives and safety of health workers.• infection prevention and control in health facilities and communities, including safe and dignified burials,• risk communication, social mobilization and community engagement, psycho-social care,• operational support and logistics: Incentives to front-line staffs and procurement of testing kits and reagents, PPEs and commodities.

2 - Activities

1. Establishment of occupational safety and health procedures- WHO will work with the SMOH to organize sessions on establishing required safety and health procedures for front-line health workers across the BAY states through employer-provided occupational safety and health training to ensure that health workers are able to avoid exposing themselves and others to health and safety risks. These guidelines will be validated and disseminated across relevant health authorities and healthcare facilities across the BAY states for use by the front-line health workers. Periodic supervision and monitoring will also be conducted to ensure compliance with these measures. 2. Procurement and prepositioning of essential equipment and supplies for front-line health workers for COVID-19 response across the BAY states. A numbe rof required equipemnts and supplies will be procured to enable health workers keep safe all through the period of COVID-19 response. Standard WHO procurement guidelines will be strictly adhered to and these equipment and supplies will be distributed through a last-mile distribution matrix to identified healthcare facilities and staff who are at the front-line of the COVID-19 response.
