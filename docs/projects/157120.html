Project Summary

Using its rapid response mechanism, NGO X proposes an action of NFI distribution, using pre-positioned in-kind and cash assistance modalities; shelter repairs, and protection referrals to respond to these needs.
This activity is implemented in partnership with several NGOs whose name have been concealed for security reasons. Please note that one NGO will input into the OCHA HPC 2019 separately under this project. This decision was agreed between the two agencies and this submission has been coordinated to avoid duplication of targets and budget figures.
The proposed activities under Shelter/NFI will target approximately 30,783 unique households (153,915 unique vulnerable displaced and resident individuals) in Idleb and North Aleppo who will benefit from at least one assistance modality (in-kind, shelter repairs or cash) or will be referred to a Special Needs Fund run by NGO X for highly vulnerable households in need of further material support or referral to specialized services.
These activities have been informed by a rapid need assessment carried out by NGO X between October and December 2018 in Idleb and North Aleppo Governorates, lessons learned from previous emergency responses, regular monitoring, beneficiary feedback and other assessments conducted by NGO X, partners and other actors.
This project uses an integrated programming approach that includes NFI distribution, using pre-positioned in-kind and cash assistance modalities; shelter repairs, and protection referrals to respond to these needs. This submission is only focusing on Shelter and NFI Cluster related activities during 2019. Please see below the relevant activities form Shelter and NFI Cluster Results Framework 2019.
The funding for this project has not yet been secured, donor decision pending.

Needs

NGO X conducted two rapid needs assessments (RNA): one between 8 and 12 October 2018 in North Aleppo IDP camps: Ahel al-Sham Camp, Al Harameen Camp, Iğde-Ikda Camp, Kaiba Camp, Mukavema Camp, Risale Camp, and Yazibag Camp in the A’zaz district; and one between 14 and 18 December 2018 in Idleb: Ariha, Idleb, Harim, and Jisr-ash-Shugur Districts. NGO X conducted a rapid need assessment (RNA) in North Aleppo on 8-12 October to ascertain the major local needs and gaps. This RNA used two main tools for data collection: 12 KIIs with local councils and camp managers; and 39 FGDs with IDPs in four camps. The data collection was carried out by NGO X Syria team members who managed to secure permission to cross the Turkey border to Syria, as well as by Syria-based enumerators. The assessment outcomes and findings represent the following:Shelter issues:• Tents are the type of shelters that families live in for the majority of the HHs living in the visited camps.• Participants reported that tents do not have weather resistance, and the majority of the participants said that the tents are very old and very poor regarding protection from the cold, heat, wind, rain, snow.• Tents are overcrowded and have no privacy.• Participants indicated that there is no covered space for essential household activities (shower, cooking etc.),• They mentioned that they improve their shelter conditions by putting old clothes and blankets (which they buy using their own money) on the tent to insulate from the summer heat and the cold weather.• Assessment team encountered HHs who are staying/accommodated in the toilets because they do not have a tent or place to stay.NFI issues:• The quality of the NFI items is poor because they were distributed long time ago• Also, number of blankets and mattress are not enough for all HH membersAccess to NFI items:• There are demand and access challenges (participants do not have purchasing power), but • There are no supply and availability challenges because the market is very well functioning Fuel for cooking and heating:• kerosene is the main type of fuel used for cooking and heating• Participants indicated that kerosene is available in the local market and through mobile kerosene distributers, but • They suffer from the lack of the purchasing power.Urgent NFI needs:• Participants indicated that they are in urgent need of tents, plastic sheets as “Insulators”, blankets, mattress, kitchen sets and hygiene kits/items. The assessment report has been shared with SNFI cluster. NGO X conducted RNA in Idleb on 14-18 December in Idleb to ascertain the emergency, shelter and protection needs and risks (4 FGDs and 250 interviews with beneficiaries: 37% women and 33% men; 45% IDPs and 55% resident community members). NGO X sampled HHs reported living in owned property, housing without rent payment, housing through (in-)formal rental agreements and Communal Centres (CC) as the main shelter solutions. The majority of respondents owning houses were from the host community. Damage to shelter, lack of privacy and corresponding concerns regarding the shelter not offering adequate climatic protection were the most frequently cited shelter issue, referenced by 53% of survey respondents. High rent payments were among the highest concerns for households living in rented accommodation. Other issues cited relate to water storage wastewater management and other plumbing issues in addition to insufficient bathroom / toilet facilities for all household members. These pose risks to health and raise protection concerns (e.g. lack of gender-separation or privacy, lighting, etc.). Concerns over shelter and WASH conditions were also cited in assessed IDP camps.

Implementation of activities

Planned activities mainly fall under Shelter NFI Cluster Sector Objective 1: Provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI supportThe overall target under SNFI sector is 30,783 HHs (153,915 individuals). Responses will aim to cover needs in food and NFI through in-kind supplies, multi-purpose cash, or a mixed-modality response. Cash transfer is the preferred modality where markets are accessible and functional and security conditions allow. Responses will be designed to meet a family's needs for one month. When a shock occurs, an appropriate response is decided upon by working with existing verification mechanisms and using pre-agreed criteria for a rapid assessment. The modality decisions in each case will be made using the organisation’s modality flowchart. 1.1 Provision of core and essential NFIs 9,703 HHs (48,515 individuals)1.1.1 Provision of non-fuel core and essential NFIs No. of people whose core and essential NFI needs are met,• In-kind support will be provided through NFIs to 3,181 HH/15,905 individuals. amounting to a total $652,105 (3,181 HHs will be targeted by non-seasonal NFI kits)• Cash (multipurpose Cash grants MPGs) support will be provided to 6,522 HHs/32,610 Individuals, amounting to a total cash amount of - $804,984 (incl. money trader fees). 1.2 Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs and shelter assistance (20,950 HHs/104,750 individuals)1.2.1 Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs including heating fuelNo. of people whose seasonal NFI needs are met• In-kind support will be provided through Winter NFI kits to 6,950 HH/34,750 individuals. amounting to a total $1,049,450• Winter Cash (Winter MPGs) support will be provided to 14,000 HHs/70,000 Individuals, amounting to a total of - $2.077,600 (incl. money trader fees). 1.4 Rehabilitation of Life-saving and life-sustaining shelter spaces in collective centres, unfinished buildings, spontaneous settlements, and/or other emergency spaces (in-kind, cash, voucher, repair) (130 HHs/650 individuals)1.4.1 Rehabilitation of collective centresNo. of people assisted by rehabilitated collective centres (130 HHs/650 individuals), amounting to a total $130,000 (NGO X will target 13 Collective Centres (CC), 10 HHs per CC = 130 HHs/650 individuals) NGO X will also seek other donors’ findings and advocate for more fund for Shelter repairs and rehabilitations on the HHs level for approximately 500 HHs. NFI/Cash distributions will take place at designated sites selected based on a set of criteria, that include travel distance (<1 km), security, and protection and gender considerations. In extenuating circumstances, the team will distribute directly to beneficiaries with restricted mobility or high vulnerability. HHs headed by women and children are prioritised for assistance in recognition of increased vulnerability. Distributions sites are subject to distribution monitoring conducted by the MEAL Team. This includes monitoring distributions against quality checklists and gathering beneficiary feedback on the distribution process and assistance received. Remaining cash and in-kind assistance will be returned to the NGO X finance department/warehouse. Damaged items are returned to warehouses for replacement and investigation. Under Shelter, NGO X will provide the most vulnerable individuals living in collective centres (CC) and camps with shelter repairs and WASH rehabilitation based on needs; this component is also integrated with WASH facility repairs, sanitation, and wastewater and waste management. This Shelter repairs and rehab in CCs will include selecting locations and shelters, assessing their damage level and HH vulnerability, developing the Bill of Quantities and Scope of Work, signing agreements with Local Councils and Shelter Managers, and completing the repair and rehabilitation works via qualified contractors. These will be paralleled with Quality Assurance by NGO X’s qualified engineers and M&E activities.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:- Access to targeted areas and vulnerable populations remains permissive, supported by continued adherence to NGO X's acceptance strategy and the joint operating procedures; and security remains sufficiently stable;- The Turkish Armed Forces continue to provide stability in N Aleppo via their presence, their support for opposition factions under the banner of the “National Army” and their provision of security services;- NGO X maintains a level of cooperation with the Turkish authorities to secure safe access to the region;- Local markets continue to function with an adequate supply / diversity of items and retain sufficient capacity to support cash-based interventions; Risks:- Continued military offensives by GoS and inter-factional fighting presents the risk of death or injury to staff; damage to or destruction of NGO X premises or assets; reduced access to programme areas for NGO X staff or beneficiaries; border closure;- Continued targeting of certain opposition factions by international actors presents the risk of those factions directing their suspicions at NGO X;- Risks associated with operating in camp settings specifically in terms of GBV, child protection, armed recruitment;- Travel risks for NGO X staff and beneficiaries in N Aleppo due to insecurity, check points, roaming armed groups;- Remote control management from Turkey and Jordan presents the risk of international and Syrian staff losing legal status; sudden border closures disrupting operations; other regulatory changes potentially affecting the programme;- There is a risk of fraud including in banking, cash management, procurement, transport, and programming;- There is a risk that cash and market-based programmes in a conflict setting raises security and acceptance risks Contingency:- Safety and Security Evacuation Plans and SOPs in place, and an experienced security team which will continue to monitor trends, conduct actor mapping, and share information to enable rapid and appropriate changes to implementation plans;- Ongoing communication with actors operational across NGO X's areas; continued adherence to Acceptance Strategies: working closely with Local Councils to identify shocks and affected HHs, plan and conduct distributions, as well as maintaining open communications with communities;- Pre-emptive actions will be taken in camps to mitigate risks through mainstreaming protection, incl. GBV, child protection, protection from sexual exploitation and abuse;- Travel and other risks have been included in NGO X’s SOPs for risk assessment in N Aleppo;- To minimise the risk of fraud and its potential impact, NGO X has updated and strengthened its controls and assurances framework;- NGO X has registered as an INGO in Jordan and established an office in Amman.
