Project Summary

Due to decades of military battles around Jonglei, Central Equatoria & Western Equatorial States, it is anticipated that high contamination from explosive hazards is lingering around the areas and post a threat to the local communities, IDPS and returnees. The Project is to improve the ability of those IDPs, returnees and other community members, in particular children, to protect themselves from the risks they face in areas that have encountered previous fighting. This project falls under the cluster objective 1 & 2 and this will be achieved through the deployment of trained and accredited 8xMobile MRE/CL response teams (24xMRE/NTS Officers and 8xMRE/CL Team Leaders) and SLI-SS Technical MRE personnel. Each team will be deployed to different location such as Akobo, Nyirol, Uror (Jonglei), Mundri East and West (Western Equatoria), Yei, Lainya & Morobo (Central Equatoria) Through the use of both Trainer and Mobile RE Response SLI-SS intent to ensure that the project will:
 Provide Men, women and children in the areas affected by violence with an understanding of the danger of ERW and Mines, how to reduce it.
 Aim at improving school-based MRE targeting school children and teachers using the MRE School guide and different approach to ensure sustainable approach and this is to increase the sharing and adoption of safety messages.
 Ensure Community focal persons trained as well to ensure mines/ERW reporting and briefing of any new comer to the community they are made aware of the dangers of mines/ERW.
 Ensure that materials and equipment are made available to deliver RE Messages.
The implementation of the project will be In coordination with the National Mine Action Authority, UNMAS and Protection cluster, as the team will be expecting hazardous areas been reported during community liaison and RE presentation, this will be compiled reported through the IMSMA forms to UNMAS

Needs

Decades of conflict, still ongoing, have littered vast swathes of land with explosive hazards and 39.2 million square meters are thought to be contaminated by such hazards. The full extent of contamination is unknown with large areas of the country requiring survey. Approximately 150 previously unknown hazardous areas are discovered each month. In addition to the threats posed to the safety of conflict-affected communities, explosive hazards prevent the safe movement of the population including those who must flee active conflict. Explosive hazards preclude the delivery of humanitarian aid and inhibit socioeconomic development. (UNMAS Oct. 2018) There are currently an estimated 6.4 million people in need of humanitarian assistance across the country and nearly 3.5 million people displaced, including 1.6 million people who have fled to neighboring countries and approximately 1.9 million people who remain internally displaced and some are sheltering at UN Mission in South Sudan (UNMISS) protection of civilian (PoC) sites across the country. Due to the continued conflict, the contamination from the Explosive remnant of war has increased and affecting the livelihood and security of women, children and that requires a special intervention approach to mine action especially Non-technical survey and Mine Risk Education to ensure continuous and sustainable information collection, data gathering and mine risk education for all age and generations. In this case SLI – SS will Survey known hazardous areas and report new hazardous areas and seeks to disseminate Mine Risk Education across Uror, Nyirol & Akobo (Jonglei State) Mundri West & East (Western Equatoria) Yei, Lainya & Morobo (Central Equatoria State) to increase the safety of the most vulnerable population.

Activities

Activities will include the following.Capacity:a) Recruit a total of 32 Technical staff( 13 Female & Male 19) that will form 8 NTS/RE-CL teams and managed and monitored by an experienced NTS/RE-CL Project Manager.b) Conduct training for 8 NTS/RE-CL and accredited by UNMAS/NMAA, deploy to the project areas.c) Development and distribution of MRE materials and messages to support MRE deliveryMine Risk Education/Community Liaison (MRE/CL):d) Delivery of MRE sessions to 33,700 (Girls-9500, Boys-9000, Men-7200, Women-8000) vulnerable populations at the communities, returnees and IDP camps.e) Organist and conduct training for 800 (Men 500, Women 300) Teachers in 95 Schools in order to deliver and integrate RE in Schools (MRE School based program me)f) Organize and train 500 (Men 300, Women 200) Community focal persons in various locations of the project in order to coordinate dissemination of MRE and any reporting’s of hazardsg) Organize Mass media campaign by organizing 30 Radio broadcast in order to target 500,000 indirect beneficiaries.h) Community liaison to gather initial contamination information for non-accessible conflict-affected areasNon-technical Survey (NTS):i) Non-technical survey to identify hazardous areas for clearance in accessible conflict-affected areas and marking of hazardous areas identified.j) Collect and reporting of Hazardous items using IMSMA reporting forms (200 HA forms through data gathering by the end of the project.Quality Management System (QMS):k) Conduct internal Quality Assurance Monitoring at least once in six weeks as per National Technical Standard Guidelines (NTSG) and SLI - SS Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) Mine ActionReporting:l) Reporting of Daily, weekly, monthly and quarterly MRE/NTS progress reports to UNMAS NMAA, UNICEF, protection cluster and donors.
