Project Summary

The NGO proposes a 24-month (6 months of which are in 2024) integrated gender-responsive nutrition, WASH and SGBV project with a key focus on sectoral SGBV risk mitigation strategies in Aleppo (Al-Bab District and Sub-District) and Idlib (Jisr Al Shoughor District and Sub-District), NWS. Geographically, the NGO will focus on areas of greatest needs that have been identified in country Humanitarian Response Plans (HRPs) as accessible and under-served by other actors.
The project aims to address critical unmet nutrition, WASH, and SGBV needs. It targets the most vulnerable populations, including children, individuals suffering from moderate and severe acute malnutrition (MAM/SAM), and those at risk of SGBV. The project has a particular focus on women and girls in NWS and is aligned to each countries’ respective Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP). This project will build on existing efforts, including existing Global Affairs Canada (GAC) funded support (which will end across the same target areas in March 2024). In Aleppo and Idlib, NWS, the project will complement interventions related to the “Management of at-risk mothers and infants under 6 months” (MAMI).
Needs Assessment: Needs for women and girls in the targeted areas are exacerbated by harmful gender norms which restrict decision-making, movement and the ability to access health, nutrition and protection services. A September 2023 MSNA in target areas found child, early and forced marriage (CEFM), sexual violence and family separation as the three most common types of protection incidents. In Northwest Syria (NWS), 13 years of conflict along with the devastating 7.8-magnitude earthquake in February 2023, which affected 8.8M people, has left 105,000 households (HHs) displaced and 4.9M in need of urgent humanitarian assistance across all sectors (UNOCHA, 2023c, UNOCHA, 2023d). Across NWS there are 4.1M PiN (2.9M of whom are internally displaced persons (IDPs), 49% female) (UNOCHA, 2023e, UNOCHA, 2023f). 58% of IDP’s in the project target areas of Idlib (Jisr Al-Shughour) and Aleppo (Al-Bab) identify nutrition as a top need (UNOCHA, 2023f). As of 2022, in Jisr Al-Shughour there were 28,391 IDPs with 97,017 PiN with a need severity rating of five and 39,912 IDPs in Darkosh with 86,381 PiN with a need severity rating of 4. In Al-Bab there were 106,324 IDPs (36% in sites), with a need severity rating of 4 and 194,034 PiN (UNOCHA, 2023f).
GBV Integration in Nutrition and WASH: To respond to the increasing SGBV needs, the NGO will adopt a protection approach that encompasses SGBV response, ensuring that groups at risk of SGBV and SGBV survivors have timely access to quality services, and risk mitigation. Risk mitigation involves enhancing community stakeholders' engagement and participation to prevent and mitigate SGBV risks. The overarching goal is to enhance the safety of, and access to, SGBV services and risk mitigation. The NGO, in coordination with the local partners, will identify and strengthen safe spaces including women and girl friendly spaces in nutrition centres in NWS. Trained staff in the identified safe spaces will provide PSS including the provision of girl-friendly activities for women and girls with a focus on SRH, SGBV and life skills. In NWS, PSS will implement the Young Mother Club (YMC) model, which is a set of eight structured sessions on SRH, SGBV, and Life skills. Targeted women and girls will also be consulted regularly on their safety, meaningful access, and participation through the interviews held during the safety audit. In order to identify the main risks associated with project delivery locations and service provision, the NGO will conduct multi-sectoral safety audits covering nutrition, WASH and SGBV sectors in targeted areas.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Protection Risk Analysis: Risk 1: Lack of or inadequate information provision, feedback and participation of affected communities due to access challenges. Likelihood: high, Impact: medium. Mitigation: Establish/strengthen local partnerships, build the capacity of partners to effectively engage communities, strengthen community-based health and nutrition services, ensure beneficiary feedback included in TPMs. Risk 2: Barriers to access or exclusion of some vulnerable and minority from accessing health and nutrition services for different reasons such as children and adults with disabilities and denial of access to certain areas that have shifted lines of control. Likelihood: medium, Impact: high. Mitigation: Analyze and disaggregate all data by sex, age and disability. Analyze the needs of vulnerable and minority groups throughout the program cycle with identification of risk factors and rights violations impacting service provision for beneficiaries. Risk 3: Breach or leakage of sensitive data and information containing patient names, medical conditions and health care provider names into the wrong hands maybe can put people and communities at risk of targeting, shame, abuse and even death. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: Electronic data and information should be properly protected and backed up. PSEA: Risks: Women and girls not able to access services or participate in income generating activities due to sexual harassment; exploitation and abuse by aid workers; female heads of HHs and adolescent girls in particular risk of sexual exploitation in exchange to services (Voices from Syria, 2022); risk of not reporting SEA for fear of retaliation; lack of awareness on reporting channels; stigma and shame and fear of losing on the services making it difficult for the NGO to hold staff/partners involved accountable. Mitigations: the NGO has a Child Safe Guarding policy which includes PSEA. To mitigate the risk of PSEA during the assistance delivery, the following will be included: mandatory training and regular refreshers on Child Safeguarding, Safe programming and PSEA policy to the NGO and partner staff; working closely with the WoS PSEA TF to strengthen feedback, reporting and response mechanisms for SEA; conducting community awareness and sensitization sessions on PSEA with emphasis on reporting and ensuring safety of survivors through referrals; regular participatory safety audit to identify protection and safety concerns arising as a result of project implementation and developing action plans to mitigate identified risks collaboratively with the community; including PSEA messages and topics in all IEC materials and training sessions conducted under the project; ensuring that centrality of protection is embedded in all projects to mitigate risks and prevent potential harm.
