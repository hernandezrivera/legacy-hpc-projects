Project Summary

SPEDP proposes a 12 months humanitarian response plan for the year 2020 with main focus of providing access to safe water, adequate sanitation and hygienic behaviors to mitigate and reduce high levels of malnutrition, water related diseases (ARI, AWD, cholera etc) and gender based violence (GBV) issues among the affected populations in Unity, Jonglei, Western Bhar El Ghazal and Central Equatoria States. This response plan will complement SPEDP’s ongoing health project in Guit, WASH project in Old Fangak, food security and livelihood (FSL) project in Raga and shelter and non-food items intervention in Morobo County.
The proposed intervention will reach a target of 60,000 vulnerable people (14,400men, 16,800women, 13,800boys and 15,000 girls) among internally displaced persons, returnees, and host communities including 1,500 people with disabilities. The project interventions will target 25% individuals for WASH and Nutrition, 45% individuals for WASH and Health, 20% for WASH and GBV and 10% for Access to safe water focusing on four Counties of Guit, Fangak, Raga and Morobo , however, if the emergency would raise in other prioritized Counties, we are ready to change and implement the intervention where it will be most needed. The project aims at centralizing protection to promote principled access to basic services to vulnerable groups at risk, accountability to affected people ensuring that services are delivered in a gender sensitive manner and will consider the needs of different gender and age groups, people living with disabilities, etc. Regualr meetings will be conducted with older people and people with disabilities within the community to monitor safety and access issues to inform the design of WASH facilities. The activities will include, Construction of new water points after GBV risk analysis; rehabilitation of water points in OTPs and community level after GBV risk analysis; construction and rehabilitation of sanitation facilities in OTPs and community level after GBV risk analysis; installation of hand washing facilities to promote hand washing in order to reduce water and sanitation related diseases; engaging in cholera preparedness and response plan together with cholera task force in respective areas of intervention; hygiene promotion education through door to door visits, participatory hygiene sessions in public places for effective behavioural change; distribution of hygiene kits or household package for famine to mothers or care takers of children admitted for severe and moderate acute malnutrition (SAM/MAM) treatment at outpatient therapeutic programme (OTP)/SC. Menstrual hygiene management (MHM) kits will be provided to adolescent girls and women of child bearing age accompanied with menstrual hygiene management sessions so that girls will not miss attending classes during their periods. SPEDP will ensure ownership and sustainability of the response through involvement of the targeted community throughout the life cycle of the intervention. All the WASH temporary and sustainable services will address the vital needs, public health and protection issues through an accountability to affected populations (AAP) community approach to target the acute vulnerabilities based on inter/cross-sectoral evaluations and analyses. This project will focus on the various needs of men, women, boys and girls identified through focus group discussions (FGDs) with all the sex and age groups, people with disabilities and pregnant lactating mothers incorporating gender dynamics and specific needs of different groups into needs assessment and response recommendations. Throughout the response life cycle, SPEDP team will coordinate with the authorities, community and the key partners on ground who are implementing WASH, nutrition, health, and protection for integration of activities to ensure maximum utilization of resources.

1 - Needs

The South Sudan crisis is a complex and protracted humanitarian crisis resulting from 5 years of armed conflict, recurring natural disasters and severe economic crisis. The armed conflict has made local communities increasingly vulnerable and events have a severe impact, aggravating food insecurity, health risks and epidemics (e.g. Cholera) as well as disrupting local economy. Civilians also bear the brunt of the conflict and have been systematically targeted by the warring parties. People have lost their homes and livelihoods, and basic infrastructure has been badly damaged. Of the country’s estimated population of between 11 – 12.3 million people, new research by IOM DTM (30th of September 2019) found 1.5 million people internally displaced within South Sudan and more than 2 million South Sudanese have taken refuge in neighbouring countries like Uganda, Sudan, Ethiopia, Kenya and Democratic republic of Congo, with little hope of returning to their homes in the near future. Despite the peace-agreement reached on 12th September 2018. Nonetheless, the implementation process is very slow and fighting continues in many parts of the country by factions that are non-signatories to the peace agreement hence severe humanitarian crisis continues to threaten millions of lives. Over 7.1 million are in need of humanitarian assistance and protection. At least 6.35 million people faced acute or severe food insecurity during the month of August, even taking into consideration present levels of humanitarian assistance, according to the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) analysis released jointly by three United Nations agencies and the Government on 11 September 2019. Among these people, an estimated 1.7 million people faced Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and 10,000 people faced catastrophic conditions (IPC Phase 5). Some 5.50 million people – 47 per cent of the population – are projected to be severely food insecure in the January to April 2020 projection period. Despite slight improvements in food security, the number of acutely malnourished children is estimated to reach 1.3 million hence UNICEF called for a shift from treatment of malnutrition to prevention, including not only food assistance, promotion of breastfeeding and nutrition counselling, but also access to clean water, proper hygiene and health care. Among others, WASH-related diseases, such as cholera and diarrhoea, are widespread in the Country and often linked to limited access to safe water, adequate sanitation, and access to health care, population displacement, food insecurity, and poor hygiene practices. In Old Fangak, the community suffers the chronic effects of water related diseases like acute water diarrhoea, acute respiratory infection, typhoid, malaria, etc due to flooding and consumption of unsafe water, (SPEDP-22nd August 2019 assessment Report ). Gender-based violence (GBV) is endemic in South Sudan, where years of conflict have increased the vulnerability of women and girls. Many of these abuses occur when women and girls undertake survival activities, such as fetching water. Especially in displacement settings, they also face increased risks of gender-based violence (GBV) linked to overcrowding, lack of lighting and poorly designed facilities, such as latrines. In most cases in Raga County, women and girls don’t feel safe accessing water and sanitation facilities i.e. they are exposed to gender based violence (GBV) issues i.e. sexual harassment, (SPEDP-28th June 2019 Rapid assessment report). In summary the needs in the four Counties of Guit, Fangak, Raga and Morobo are attributed to limited or lack of access to safe water and sanitation facilities, high levels of malnutrition, GBV cases, high morbidity, mortality as a result of WASH –related diseases and displacements due to flooding.

2 - Activities

The key interventions SPEDP will be addressing in the targeted Counties of Western Bhar El Ghazal, Unity, Jonglei and Central Equatoria are as follows:– Water Supply:- SPEDP will perform drilling of 8 boreholes at Community level in order to provide vulnerable populations in Morobo and Raga with access to safe water supply. - Conducting rehabilitation or repair of 60 boreholes (hand pumps) at community level and outpatient therapeutic programme (OTP)/SC in the targeted project locations. SPEDP teams will consult with women and girls for the allocation of the sites for rehabilitation to mitigate gender based violence (GBV) risks.- Providing 5,500 HHs in Old Fangak and Guit with water purification products (PUR/Aqua tabs) to promote household water treatment to ensure safety of drinking water for human consumption. - Formation and training of water management committees comprising of 2men and 5women per committee to ensure effective and efficient functioning of water facilities.- Training of community based pump mechanics that include both men and women to ease operation and maintenance of hand pumps in the targeted project locations. - Conducting water quality testing and monitoring to ensure that water points constructed or rehabilitated are safe and free from pathogenic micro-organisms to mitigate against malnutrition for children under the age of 5 years. Sanitation:- SPEDP will construct 40 emergency latrines to provide vulnerable populations in IDPs and returnees settlements with access to safe excreta disposal with special attention given to the safety of women and girls.- SPEDP will also Rehabilitate 60 institutional latrines at outpatient therapeutic programme (OTP)/SC and community level in the targeted areas of response. - Continuous Community mobilization on the use of local available materials for construction of household latrines as well as promotion of cat sanitation in densely populated IDP sites without latrines to reduce open defecation.- Installation of hand washing facilities in public places and innovative utilization of existing community resources such as jerry-cans for construction of simple tippy taps to promote hand washing at household level. - Promoting safe domestic waste management at household level through open public clean up campaigns to reduce on public health hazards. Hygiene:- Training of community hygiene promoters’ volunteers and nutrition workers to disseminate key hygiene messages at community level and outpatient therapeutic programme (OTP) for those admitted for SAM/MAM treatment.- SPEDP will engage in cholera preparedness and response plan together with cholera task force in respective areas of intervention. SPEDP will train health personnel, teachers and community health promoters on case notification and investigation for timely response.- Training of school hygiene clubs with 40 members per club to promote hygiene in schools so that the same messages can be carried home by the pupils for behaviour change in the Community. - Engaging in distribution of hygiene kits or household package for famine prevention to mothers and care takers of children admitted for SAM/MAM treatment at outpatient therapeutic programme (OTP).- Conduct participatory hygiene and cholera awareness sessions in the targeted areas of response by disseminating key hygiene messages in line with cultural practices of the community i.e. hand washing with soap or ash at critical times, water protection (safe water chain), domestic hygiene (food hygiene), environmental hygiene (safe excreta disposal and solid waste disposal) etc. The response will promote door to door visits and participatory hygiene sessions in public places reaching a target of 75,000 people.- Distribution of MHM kits to 3,000 adolescent girls and 2,000 women of child bearing age accompanied with menstrual hygiene management sessions so that girls will not miss attending classes during their periods.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes, Internally displaced persons (IDPs) are particularly vulnerable as all their assets have been depleted or destroyed due to conflict and displacement. The categories of IDPs who will benefit from the intervention include:-Women: While attempting to save their children from starvation, malnutrition and disease, women are subject to conflict-related security threats including sexual violence; their vulnerability is enhanced when they are displaced and forced to migrate. Many women have fled the fighting alone with children.Pregnant and lactating women: This group is particularly vulnerable. With access to limited food, maternal and child health is compromised. At this stage the project will aim to improving this.Children and young people: There are many unaccompanied children and child or young adult headed households.Children under 5: This group remains vulnerable to malnutrition and access to sufficient support and services. With displaced parents of having access to limited foods supply, this group is most affected nutritionally.Older people: Older people often bear the brunt of conflict because of their frailty and reduced mobility.People with a disability: Like older people, are particularly vulnerable to violence due to reduced capacities (i.e. hearing, vision, mobility) and are often missed out of distributions due to access.Men: Most of the fleeing men have lost all their livelihoods and are unable to provide for their families. This results in anger and frustration sometimes leading to an increase of gender based violence. Targeting them focuses on restoring family relations and dignity.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, the project is linked to linked to government and community systems though the institutional capacity of government has been fragmented with the conflict and economic crisis and unable to strengthen their systems. The local community systems are triggered by emergency needs as there are a lot of drivers that include floods, localized insecurity, depleted community assets /WASH infrastructure. Therefore, the intervention will complement government's efforts for sustainable development.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, the project is aligned with the national objectives and it will recognize the urgent need to meet the social protection needs of specific vulnerable groups such IDPs, refugee returns based on UNHR 2019-2020 Yes, Regional RRP to prepare refugees returns to partake in peace building, promoting social cohesion between refugee and host communities and national reconciliation efforts in South Sudan. The objectives are taken from the WASH cluster strategic objectives intended to offer critical lifesaving interventions in populations defined under severity IPC 3,4&5 in the hard to reach Counties.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes, SPEDP has been working in these proposed locations and found the protection environment safe and stable for this intervention. Some of the areas are experiencing population returns both IDP and returnee population. SPEDP teams will ensure protection concerns are mainstreamed to ensure safety and stability of the project and ‘do-no-harm principle’ i.e. any form of assistance will not exacerbate or create more risks to already vulnerable populations, legitimize conflicts or fuel further conflicts rather direct assistance towards ‘connectors’ in affected populations to foster a sense of community cohesion. The intervention will ensure project participants’ of safety, rights and dignity alongside material assistance.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, the intervention will involve various stakeholders throughout the life of the project – including government, traditional leadership, and other community leaders –in building sustainable relationships between people, and between people and their environment, with a particular focus on encouraging peaceful relations between the host communities, IDPs and Returnees. Given access challenges, SPEDP will rely heavily on volunteers hired from within the local community for implementation of activities, including hygiene promotion and messaging. The formation of community-based pump mechanics and water management committees selected by the beneficiaries themselves will strengthen project ownership and participation of the community with minimal conflict. The project will be handed over to the community upon its successful completion.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

SPEDP upholds accountability as a core component of all its projects and as such, accountability to beneficiaries is mainstreamed in the design of this intervention, supporting our commitment to beneficiary protection (including protection of sexual exploitation and abuse) and communities will be continuously consulted on the process and contents of interventions conducted; local support, including males and females, will be recruited on a temporary basis to support the project planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation and ensure that the intervention is locally appropriate, sensitive and relevant. Complaints and feedback will be acted on according to established protocols agreed with stakeholders and incorporated into programme learning. SPEDP team will establish help desks at WASH-NFI distribution points to allow for illiterate beneficiaries to provide verbal feedback. The field teams will regularly engage with community members (including beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries, men and women, adults and youth) to invite their honest feedback. The feedback mechanisms are part of SPEDP’s Accountability to Affected Population commitment. In this approach, the beneficiaries are urged to lodge complaints/express grievances prior to and after distribution of the WASH NFIs including on the general programming of this intervention. Suggestion boxes will be placed at distribution points and brought out during meetings. Depending on the severity of the problem/complaint, the team will hold a meeting with the village committees for participatory solutions. Feedback will be provided to the community on amonthly or quarterly basis, through community meetings, on how the complaints raised have been resolved or are being handled without mentioning the individual(s) who raised the complaints.
