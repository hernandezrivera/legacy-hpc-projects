Project Summary

Kuwaiti patients Helping Fund (KPHF) proposes to assist vulnerable population affected by conflict, displaced and malnutrition by supporting 25 OTPs, 27 TSFPs and 4 SCs in El Fasher ,Tawella , Daresalam and Sarfomera in North and Dimsue , Greida , Nyala and Nyala North ,Tulus ,Kubum in South Darfur States. The project proposed to support 176,165 beneficiaries including (8,224 boys & girls with SAM,1,252 boys & girls with SAM and medical complication, 22,540 boys & girls with MAM and 12,870 PLW with MAM) comprising of IDPs, returnees and host communities experiencing with displaced and malnutrition problem. Malnutrition remains a critical problem affecting the North and South Darfur States. KPHF will provide health services through SCs and nutrition services through OTPs and TSFPs. Nutrition services to cover SAM and MAM cases for U5 and PLW which include screening at admission, medical treatment of severely acutely malnourished (SAM) children with and without medical complication, dietary management and individual child follow up, among others. It will also cover the administrative (support) cost of case management of moderate acute malnourished (MAM) girls and boys of 6-59months and pregnant and lactating women (PLW). Furthermore, nutrition education and counseling will be provided for mothers/caregivers in Infant and Young Child feeding (IYCF) practices and hygiene promotion as well as MSGs will built to facilitate of nutrition education and contribution in MUAC screening activities. Selected trainings will be provided on SC case management, CMAM, NiE, identification of medical complications at OTPs and TSFPs level, community screening and follow up. In addition to, women club, women cornea and community awareness session campaigns will be conducted under nutrition program.

1 - Needs

The nutrition need continue to persist in North and South Darfur States on the background of low coverage and access of nutrition services and inadequate response capacities. Malnutrition remain major cause of morbidity and mortality among children under five and PLW in above mentioned areas, particularly among vulnerable population. The nutrition services in both North & South Darfur state fragile and unable to provide essential life-saving services and respond to malnutrition situation. Poor socioeconomic status &food insecurity contribute to malnutrition rates in these localities, together with micronutrient deficiencies &lack of Vitamin A, iron, zinc &iodine. The Simple Spatial Surveying Method (S3M11) 2018 shows high rates of malnutrition in North & South Darfur above WHO emergency thresholds both GAM & SAM respectively 19.4% & 3.23 in North Darfur and 16.4 & 2.8 in South Darfur. Distribution of GAM prevalence based on MUAC follows a similar pattern to WHZ, and areas with high GAM by MUAC include North & South Darfur. In addition to, malnutrition is an ongoing problem throughout North & South Darfur localities, according to the S3M-II 2018 , for North Darfur localities indicate SAM , MAM & GAM respectively in Tawella locality (1.7% , 8.7% & 10.4 ) , in Daresalam locality (2.15% , 10.9% & 12.7) , in El Fasher locality (3.18% , 12.2 % & 15.6 ) and in Sarfomera locality (4.06% , 13.85% & 18.42%). While in South Darfur localities S3MII indicates SAM , MAM & GAM respectively in Dimsue locality (1.8 % ,7.54% & 9.52%) , in Gerida locality (2.66% , 8.8% & 11.36% ) , in Nyala North locality (0.8% , 5.2% & 6.32% ) , in Tulus locality (1.14%, 6.85% &7.99% ) and in Kubum locality (1.07%, 5.73% & 6.8%). Most of the children with SAM are found in these above mentioned areas and other States these States. A household survey conducted by KPHF in above mentioned localities revealed a very poor knowledge on Infant &Young Child Feeding practices, in addition to poor sanitation practices & a very limited coverage of community nutrition preventive package. The report indicated that in North and South Darfur, 92% of mothers begin breastfeeding within the first hour after delivery, but only 38% of mothers subsequently breastfeed their children exclusively, 24% of mothers prepare a separate meal for their children as a supplementary food & only 47% of children take meals three times a day. Moreover, lactating mothers who become pregnant report that they immediately stop breastfeeding, even if their baby is less than 12 months of age, due to a deep-rooted belief that a pregnant mother's breast milk is harmful & causes diarrhea. These above elaborated dismal nutrition situation requires urgent intervention to fill this gap and address the malnutrition status among children U5 and PLW. In addition to S3MII result and WFP VAM food security outcomes, significant food and nutrition gaps remain in North & South Darfur State. Low availability and accessibility of food (primarily due to drought), quality of diet, infant feeding practices and inadequate home management practices contribute to the poor nutritional status of children. Micronutrient deficiencies, including iron-deficiency anemia, vitamin A deficiency and Iron rich food consumption are serious nutrition issues facing the population. Anemia is suspected to be high among women and in addition to other micro-nutrient deficiency is a public health concern. The nutrition situation in the project locations is still poor persistently elevating levels of acute malnutrition.

2 - Activities / Outputs

•Rehabilitation of 25 OTPs, 27 TSFPs sand 4 SCs sites through using local environmental friendly materials and stabilized soil picks.•Conduct Community mobilization and sensitization for targeted communities on MUAC screening, active case finding and referral of acutely cases to OTPs, TSFPs and SCs.•Conduct regular MUAC screening at nutrition centers. •Conduct training workshop on CMAM protocol, NiE, SBCC and database management for project staff. •Conduct weekly focused nutrition education group sessions for male and female caregivers at OTPs, SFCs, SCs, & PHC clinics. Sessions in the clinic will include topics such as: IYCF practices, diversification of the diet with local foods, micronutrient supplementation, nutrition for a sick child, etc. In addition to establishment of MSGs. •Disseminate Information, Education and Communication (IEC) materials with key standardized nutrition messages. Conduct regular on the job training for project staff.Conduct regular monitoring visits.•Equipping and supplying all supported centers with medical supplies and non-medical supplies (staff, U5 drugs, RUTF, RUSF, F75, medical equipment’s, furniture, washing soap and disinfectant materials in the targeted areas.•In collaboration with SMoH KPHF will contribute in commemorate National breastfeeding week.* Establishment of MSG at targeted areas. •Provide supplementary food rations to children aged Under 5 years, pregnant and lactating mothers in SFC for treatment of Moderate Acute Malnutrition. •Provide therapeutic food rations to children aged under 5 years in Stabilization Center for treatment of Severe Acute Malnutrition (with medical complications). •Provide therapeutic food rations to girls and boys aged under 5 years in OTP for treatment of Severe Acute Malnutrition (without medical complications). •Provide treatment of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) in OTPs &TSFPs.Provide treatment and especially medical care for SAM with medical complicated cases at SCs.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

KPHF recognizes the importance of mainstreaming protection in its projects in order to make sure that its efforts would ensure full respect for the rights of individuals and groups. The organization recognizes the importance of the human rights of IDPs, returnees and all groups especially the most vulnerable groups such women, girls and minority groups. While implementing all projects, the organization will make conscious and intentional efforts to avoid all situations which expose beneficiaries to risk, threat, violence and abuse of their rights. Through the Community Committees, partner and staff members, KPHF work to foster trust and mutual understanding between various groups and to minimize conflict and reduce or stop violence. Also KPHF focus on building the capacity of the staff to strengthen their understanding on the fundamental human rights and serving beneficiaries with dignity , gender and protection mainstreaming to ensure that vulnerable beneficiaries (women, girls, men and boys and minorities) are respected and feel protected.
