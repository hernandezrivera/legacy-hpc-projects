Project Summary

The objectives of the refugee response plan by UNFPA are to address Gender Based Violence prevention and response, addressing the critical life-saving and essential reproductive health needs among the refugees and asylum seekers in the prioritized localities as mentioned for ethiopian, South Sudanese and Non-south sudanese refugees & asylum seekers in 53 high and medium priority areas identified by RCF. This plan will cover integrated response for SRH-GBV prevention and service deliveries, capacity building of local authorities, institutions & service providers, Community Awareness and Community Engagements, Advocacy for SRH-GBV services, and scaling up referral mechanism including the service coordination for at risk women and girls and GBV survivors irrespective of sex, age & ethnicity. The project consists of reproductive health and GBV service delivery and awareness activities. The implementation of activities will be conducted through NGOs and Government partners with the strong technical support from UNFPA, including maintaining the field presence. The project will target individual skills building, strengthening community structures and institutional capacity building of Sudan to respond to the needs of refugees.

1 - Needs

Sudan hosts more than 1.1 million refugees and asylum seekers in 12 states, including the new arrivals from Ethiopia. Among these vast numbers of refugees and asylum seekers, 73% are women and children and 70% are outside camps. 43% of the population are children under 18 years, 28% are women of reproductive age. Huge numbers of households in the refugee communities are female-headed (52%) with 1% child-headed households. This project will target 53 localities prioritized for 2022 by the refugee consultation forum (RCF). UNFPA will implement in Bahr El Arab, Assalaya,El Ferdous, Abu Jabra, Ed Daein, Abu Jubaiha, Leri, Dilling, Kadugli, Algoz, Altadamon, Al Meiram, Keilak, Al Lagowa, Babanusa, An Nuhud, Sheikan, Al Idia, As Salam – WK, Al Dibab, Ghubaish, Ailliet, El Jabalian, Kosti, Umdurman and Khartoum, to localities to support South Sudanese Refugees. To support the Non-South Sudanese Refugees, UNFPA has targeted Basundah, Al Fashagab, Al Mafaza, Gala'a Al Nahal, Madeinat, Al Gedaref, Reifi Kassala, Wadelhilew, Khasm Algriba, Kosti, Daralsalam/ ar rawat, ELJabalein, Um Algura, Umm Dukhun, El Radoom, Um Dafug, Bielel, Buram, Nyala, dar asSalam, Dimsu, Habila, El Damazine, EL Roseires, Wad almahi, Umdurman, Bahri, and Khartoum localities in 2022. The total population in these 32 localities is 1033422. As the project has focused on integrated SRH-GBV programmes for refugees, UNFPA has estimated the needs for SRH-GBV services for the target groups by using MISP calculator (IAWG-RH). The project will be able to serve 20203 pregnant women who are in need of critical and lifesaving SRHR services. This intervention, if implemented, should address pregnancy related complications including caesarean section and normal deliveries, prevention and treatment of STIs for both women and men, preventing neonatal complications. Besides, the project will serve approximately 59000 primary health consultations for both women, girls, boys and men, including the persons with disabilities, primary focus of consultation will be around SRH. This project targets approximately 4500 sexual violence cases with CMR services. In addition to that the project will address 50% of GBV survivors who received financial support while seeking other mental health and psychosocial services. Among other critical interventions, direct services such as dignity kits, RH kits, training and capacity building of service providers on both MHPSS, GBV case management, Clinical management of rape (CMR), establishment and strengthening referral mechanism, community awareness through community structures, such as women center, safe house, community based protection networks. UNFPA will also target 10% of the host communities to benefit from this project of refugees and Asylum seekers to mitigate conflicts between host and refugees. The key interventions are: mobile and temporary clinics, women centers, RH and Dignity Kits, Awareness Raising on GBV, Community Based Protection Networks, Capacity building, advocacy & referral services.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Output 1. Increased access to and utilization of quality GBV services and improved conducive community structure to prevent GBV in the targeted localities for refugee women and girls, especially GBV survivors. Activity 1.1. Establishment and operation of women centers to enable women with increased access to information and services . Activity 1.2: Vocational skills training for women and supporting with start-up capital for the most vulnerable women. Activity 1.3: Supporting women and girls with dignity kits, Psychosocial support and GBV Case Management Services, including Referral services cost. Activity 1.4. Training of GBV service providers and social workers, including the refresher training. Activity 1.5. Capacity Building of targeted community based protection network and women's groups, and direct Support to facilitate their work. Activity 1.6. Community sensitization session, public Awareness raising campaign- national and state level, develop IEC materials (flip chart development, including consultants cost to contextualize and translate materials and printing) Output 2. Improved access to, and utilization of lifesaving and essential sexual and reproductive health services. Activity 2.1. Timely deployment of temporary and/or mobile clinic services, referral services and health care providers to areas with limited access to lifesaving and essential SRH services. Activity 2.2 Strengthening the capacity of health care providers on provision of essential and lifesaving SRH services, such as EmONC, CMR, Post abortion care, family planning, ANC/PNC, etc. Activity 2.3. Improve the availability of essential and lifesaving SRH supplies including interagency reproductive health kits, personal protective equipment and infection prevention supplies, family planning, hospital equipment and supplies. Activity 2.4 Strengthen existing health facilities and improve their capacities through rehabilitation and refurbishments, hospital equipment and referral services for refugees. Activity 2.5 Engagement of target beneficiaries in demand creation for SRHR services as well as other emerging health issues such as COVID19, and ensuring their active role in establishing community based referral mechanisms for obstetric complication.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

1. Safety audit in a regularly basis with the beneficiaries. 2. Conduct joint assessment in the protection risks. 3. Enable women's social groups to speak on various protection challenges and risks in the refugee sites. The project will also keep community advocacy and consultations that will engage community opinion leaders to enhance deeper understanding of the risks at home and at the community level. The project will engage the community leaders to mitigate the protection needs. 4. Orientation on PSEA to the staff and enabling beneficiary knowing where to report. At the institutional level, the project will build the capacity of the service providers on GBV guiding principles, Do No Harm Approach. 5. GBV and PSEA focal points will be in place for each service delivery point 6. Risk reduction strategy will be integrated into each activities of the programmes 7. To mitigate host and refugee conflicts, UNFPA will address the programme following the principle of social cohesion, such as allowing the most vulnerable host communities to access the services. 8. The programme will target based on the gender analysis that reflects the different needs of women, girls, boys and men. At the same time, the project will also address the most vulnerable women and children, such as female- headed households, survivor of child marriage, pregnant women and GBV survivors. To mitigate the protection risks, the project will consult with women and men, young people separately to understand various protection risks while accessing services.
