Project Summary

Protection capacities for affected communities in Cox’s bazar are strengthened through community-based protection activities aimed at improving access to equitable and quality humanitarian assistance and resourcing the community to improve their response to Protection threats. Oxfam aims to support the community in mitigating and safely responding to protection threats through close collaboration with existing and newly formed protection structures who are working with Oxfam and/or partner agencies. Community self-protection capacities will be enhanced through assessments, protection monitoring, advocacy, coordinated responses and, building on the community’s protection awareness through training and provision of required resources to enable mitigation or response to protection threats. This project is guided by an age, gender and diversity approach to ensure maximum reach to most vulnerable groups and is in line with the sector principles of Do No Harm and Leave No One Behind. The activities will focus on working with the youth, gender diversity groups and persons with specific needs to be better informed on prevention measures to protection risks including those newly emerging or have further triggered by COVID-19. This project focuses on meaningful community engagement to increase knowledge and understanding of protection in a context-specific way that is safe to practice and relevant to the COVID-19 evolving situation. COVID-19 responses developed to date will be mainstreamed throughout this project including the administrative requirements as per national and international agreed regulations.

1 - Identified Needs

The COVID19 outbreak continues to pose a risk to Rohingya refugees; not only because of pre-existing conditions, including the overcrowded nature of the camps that make social distancing a limited option to many of the Rohingya Refugees, but also due to effects it is having on the protection environment. There is an imminent link between COVID19 and an increase in GBV, including IPV, denial of resources and violence towards sex workers, the finding point to an increase in child protection risks and stigmatisation of minority groups including transgender persons. Since March 2020, GoB restrictions to combat COVID19, led to reduction of humanitarian staff presence in the camps by 80% and in turn hindered refugees’ access to critical protection assistance. The restriction imposed on the affected persons mobility affected the flow of information particularly to most vulnerable groups on protection against COVID19 as well as protection against existing and newly emerging manmade protection threats. There has been evidence of increased social barriers on women and girls from accessing GBV services, as well as social isolation of persons with specific needs, including the elderly, persons with disabilities and transgender persons living in the camps and those from the host community of Cox’s Bazar. The latter examples are assessed as direct consequences of COVID19 misconceptions and its link to social stigma effecting achievements made to date towards gender equity and rather cause a backlash affected most vulnerable groups including women and girls, transgender persons and sex workers. The restriction in availability and access to humanitarian services coincided with an increase in criminal activities, especially physical violence and theft by armed gangs. Oxfam protection monitoring reports demonstrate a clear increase in theft and insecurity involving drug smuggling and consumption, as well as physical violence and the overall hostility across the targeted areas. There are reports indicating that men and boys are particularly vulnerable to physical violence and forced recruitment into criminal groups and attacked in accusations of belonging to rivalries to the dominant groups. Women and girls are also vulnerable to harassment, intimidation and forced marriages by the criminal groups. Oxfam consistently raises concerns voiced through interagency advocacy channels for collective action. COVID19 restrictions resulted in limited maintenance of the public solar lights installed by Oxfam during the lockdown, tens of public solar lights became non-functional. This led to spreading fear throughout the community, disproportionately affecting women and girls, but also boys and men and further restricting the mobility of most vulnerable groups in the camps. Due to the deterioration in the economic situation the potential for dangerous coping strategies and exploitation, including, child-marriages, child labour, sexual exploitation and trafficking of person surged. At the same time, tension between refugees and host communities as a result of competition for resources and general fear and misconception of COVID-19 is weakening social cohesion efforts. Oxfam Protection teams continues to assess emerging needs to address the new challenges to the protection environment of the Rohingya refugees and the host community in Cox’s Bazar. The concerns about involuntary relocation, the potential effects on the safety of refugees and ability to meaningfully access services, the camps fencing; the ongoing gang violence and increased criminality all remain priority points and Oxfam continues to advocate for solutions through activities proposed under this project. In recognition of the need and Oxfam expertise in the approach, community-based protection is the guide to all this project activities. Oxfam will work to enhancing community-engagement in protection focusing on community ownership and participation in the deliverables of this project

2 - Activities or outputs

A1. Strengthening self-protection mechanisms to better identify, mitigate and respond to protection risks: This activity will be done through strengthening Protection Committees (PC), Community based Volunteers (CBV) and Youth Leadership Groups (YLG) and through the coordination with external community structures. A2. Protection Monitoring and Advocacy: The teams will monitor thematic protection concerns. This activity monitors existing threats and its risks to the community, the types of vulnerabilities more likely to be exposed and community’s capacity to respond to the risks. The community’s feedback to Oxfam on the protection situation is referred to the advocacy department who then shares this information in in agency advocacy forums. CBV, PC and Youth committees will be involved in the protection monitoring process to inform reporting on protection concerns. A3. Protection NFIs Assessment and NFIs: Oxfam will provide in-kind assistance to support individuals, households or exceptionally groups to reduce exposure to emergency protection risks. The type of assistance will be determined following an assessment on the type of needs and suitable NFIs required. Oxfam will conduct risk assessments covering the safety of the distribution location, and assess any possible risks linked to receipts as well as to Oxfam staff and volunteers. The latter includes close safe and timely coordination with CiCs and Unions as a mitigation measure. A4. Anti-Trafficking Campaign: Oxfam will provide youth and women groups anti-trafficking ToT to improve outreach tackling. A5. Facilitating Referrals: Oxfam team including staff and volunteers, training and implementation of identification and referral of protection cases ensuring meaningful access to services. This is a crosscutting activity. A6. Safe Programming Sessions: All project team will be trained on ‘do no harm’ principles and protection mainstreaming. Under this activity’s local protection and civil society organisation will also be targeted for safe programming training. A7. Public Solar Lighting: Replacement and repairs of damaged, and stolen lights. Community prevention and mitigation mechanisms will be strengthened to reduce theft in the community through community maintenance groups, coordination with camp management and the Host Community. Oxfam will continue close collaboration with the SMSD sector, when assessing, validating and repairing solar lights (including during cyclones and other weather affected shocks) and when the maintenance group engagement with site management. A8. Social Cohesion: Consultation meetings with Host Community and Rohingya refugees Imams; women and youth groups will be held. The meetings outcomes will guide a social cohesion action plan. In Ukhiya, Tom Toms campaigns: social and religious events and community meetings will improve peaceful coexistence. Root causes and suggestions towards social strife will inform the activities. These initiatives complement other proposed activities. A9. Localisation Protection study: Oxfam will conduct a study to strengthen understating of the sector’s roles in protection and localisation, challenges and way forward. This study is intended to inform Oxfam’s protection strategy as well as a contribution with the wider sector’s localisation agenda. A10. IEC Materials (through multiple means including Radio Channels): Protection messaging/awareness raising on anti-trafficking, GBV/CP risks/response, legal awareness, protection of vulnerable persons during COVID-19 times (elderly, persons with disability, and gender diversity groups) using different ways. Oxfam will engage community structures in development and distribution of IEC materials to address concurring issues including how to address social stigma in the time of COVID-19. A11. End Line Survey: Protection assessments to understand the impacts on quality of programs and evidence-based programming at the end of the year to inform 2022 activities.
