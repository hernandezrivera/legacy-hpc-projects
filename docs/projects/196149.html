Project Summary

HEKS/EPER intervention is the continuity of shelter and settlement activities started in 2019. The project Objective is to ensure adequate living condition for Rohingya through more durable solutions for shelter and settlement and access to NFI. It is planned in camps 14,15,16.
The major outcome is that households live in a safe and dignified shelter by receiving Shelter Upgrade and Maintenance and Emergency support during monsoon, or in case of fire, landslides or relocation.
Both SUM and Emergency are planned in the 3 camps. 60% of HHs in each camp will receive SUM (about 40% already happened in 2022), which means about 13475 HHs, and an estimated 18% of HHs will receive Emergency support through RDV (4043 HHs). The cost of all activities includes operational and non-operation costs and materials are provided bythe IOM common pipeline. HEKS/EPER has a distribution point in camp 15. The assistance will include capacity building for the team and for HHs through a training on construction and repair technics. It will be a key step to receive materials and repair adequately their shelter by following the Minimum Performance Standards set by the SNFI Sector. Households will be supported by HEKS technical team through visits directly to HHs for technical guidance and will receive a two steps assistance with a minimum package and a voucher system that will allow them to choose the materials they want within a range of points. The program will follow the SNFI sector and RRRC approved approach and results will be shared regularly to the sector. EVHHs will receive a tailored assistance with porters and construction support according to their needs after participating in the training.
HEKS/EPER will be shelter focal point in camp 14,15 and 16 and will conduct RDV after referrals from SMS. The assistance will be provided within 72hrs from the referral time. HEKS/EPER Focal point will ensure good coordination at camp level and participate in DMC and respond to any emergency. Coordination at Cox’s Bazar will be ensured as well as participation in project development and technical working group.
HEKS/EPER will respond weekly to the SMS CFRM desk and provide a tailored support in coordination with all actors. A Help Desk is available at the distribution point to collect complaints raised by HHs. . It is managed by a volunteer in charge of coordinating with the distribution team to deal with issues.
HEKS/EPER team has received PSEA training and will be regularly updated about the topic to ensure PSEA measures will be taken. HEKS/EPER is part of the PSEA network and has a strong internal guidance.
All activities will be constantly monitored by the shelter team as well as a dedicated MEAL manager who support the team, monitor the CFRM desk for better accountability and conduct PDM. Community Engagement will be a major key with focus group discussion to explain the project to community leaders, households and ensure clear understanding. Government bodies, SMS and all key stakeholders will be informed before any activities and through regular meetings.
Outcome 2 focuse on DRR by providing HHs level site improvement assistance around shelters in camps 15 & 16 (Repairing stairs, protect damaged slopes and repair or build new drains). It reaches directly and indirectly about 10% of HHs (1792 HHs). Community Engagement is a key point as the work will be completed by CfW supervised by a technical team. The designs will follow Government and SMSD Sector guidelines and will not provide permanent structure using bricks and cement. Non-permanent materials will be used, such as geo bags, bamboos and ropes and supplied by HEKS/EPER and not under the IOM common pipeline..
As for shelter assistance, the third outcome aims at providing NFIs in regular assistance and emergency situations. An estimated 15% of HHs of the 3 camps (3369 HHs) will receive NFI emergency assistance and 100% for Regular assistance.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The need for distribution of shelter materials in the camps has not changed over time, as government restrictions force people to continue constructing shelter from non-durable materials (bamboo and tarpaulins) and limit access to income-generating activities. Rohingya refugees still rely on distributions from shelter/NFIs partners.The non-durable building materials degrade quickly and are susceptible to damage by the elements, such as monsoon rains, flash floods, landslides and regular tear. A regular assistance is needed and in 2021, 63% of the Rohingya population still reported shelter materials as a priority need (MSNA 2020), overtaking the number of refugees that named food as a priority need. COVID-19 restricted shelter partners to providing assistance for repair and maintenance between 2020 and 2021 and increased the needs to support. Only emergency assistance was provided, which increased to more than 30% oh HHs receiving emergency assistance.. In 2021, due to a large fire, materials dedicated to providing regular assistance were used as contingency materials and then reduced the regular assistance in camps by two. Only 70668 HHs received regular assistance from May 2020 to June 2021 when it was double from May 2019 to June 2020. Those situations caused a back up to meeting the needs that shelter partners need to catch up as soon as possible to return to a steady pace in the regular assistance provided.Since the beginning of the assistance, the GoB has restricted shelter assistance to bamboo and tarpaulins, which require annual replacement and has environmental impact on Bangladesh bamboo forests. To slow down the rate of bamboo deterioration and the need to replace bamboos, bamboo treatment procedures have been developed. But the need to find alternative solutions remain essential and shelter partners must join effort with Shelter Sector to advocate for alternative solutions in the camps.MSNA 2021 indicate that kitchen items, clothing, and lighting are persistent needs. To ensure better appropriateness, assistance must be provided through vouchers that will allow households to choose items from a predetermined range and fit their own needs.All Shelter and NFI partners must budget for and provide porter and construction labour support to extremely vulnerable households (EVHH). The assistance must ensure appropriate participation of refugees of all genders, ages and abilities in shelter design, training and construction. There is an important need to increase capacity building to be able to provide tailored assistance to different groups and focus on shelter strengthening and maintenance, including consideration of DRR, fire safety, and household-level site improvement.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

HEKS/EPER has been working in Camp 14,15 and 16 that forms a separated block from Kutupalong Mega Camp (3 camps geographically contiguous). In camp 15, HEKS/EPER has been the major implementing partner to provide shelter, NFIs and emergency assistance since 2019, as an implementing partner under the IOM common pipeline. The agreement is about to be extended in 2023. The shelter focal point responsibility provides HEKS/EPER a strong role in camps 14,15 and 16 with a coordination role with sector and governement bodies, emergency support in camps as well as at Cox’s Bazar level. HEKS/EPER has been working in the same sector, shelter sector sector, and aims at continuing for the coming years. In those camps, refugees are clearly informed HEKS/EPER provides the shelter assistance and it facilitate the understanding of the assistance. HEKS/EPER participates in Shelter Sector meeting and coordinates at camp level as a shelter focal point to ensure gaps are filled and avoid any overlapping between partners. As member of the PSEA network, HEKS/EPER comply with basic requirements, as well as for approvals at RRRC level

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

HEKS/EPER is shelter focal point in catchment 7 and 8 and work closely with Protection sector. The assistance will be provided without any discrimination, gender, disability or aged-barriers. The distribution point is meant to be easy to access to people with disabilities, proper waiting line for women is arranged, strong control to child protection is applied (with provision of a breastfeeding and child friendly area inside the distribution point). A tailored assistance with porters and labor is provided to EVHHs. HHs can refer to a Help Desk if they have any complaints or feedbacks on the assistance for better accountability. Trainings of beneficiaries will focuse on the importance of partitioning in shelters for privacy and the knowledge of the CFRM system to be able to refer any protection related cases if needed. If a shelter repair or construction or relocation is needed under the referral of a protection case, a dedicated HEKS Staff will supervise porters & construction support.

3.2 - Gender

HEKS/EPER Shelter team reached the balance in terms of gender. All staffs, male and female, are trained on PSEA. and HEKS/EPER female staff ensure support to women receiving the assistance to fit their needs and make them feel comfortable during the assistance. Female refugees are encouraged to attend trainings. Separated groups for women and female friendly sitting arrangement are in place to encourage female refugees to attend trainings and wait for distribution in a comfortable way. When invited, priority will be to minimize unintended adverse effect which could increase their vulnerability. Trainings will focuse on the importance of the shelter design and partitioning to ensure a separated space for women in the shelter and households are encouraged to chose an A breastfeeding corner and child friendly area are in place to facilitate their time in the distribution point when they come with children. Specific latrines for women are available in the distribution point

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

EVHHs include elderly and child-headed families, as per SNFI sector criteria. HEKS/EPER is committed to not hiring any children under the age of 18, as daily workers and not accepting any children sent from families for training and/or distribution. A specific support will be provided to children when they fall under EVHHs (child-headed households). Construction support and porters will be provided to repair their shelter And coordination with the safeguarding Protection Focal Point.

3.4 - Diversity

Rohingya refugees, across different age, gender and diversity groups will receive equal assistance, including PwD. They will be able to select their own assistance and a specific support with porters and construction team will be provided when they are considered EVHHs. Criterias considered for EVHHs are set with the shelter sector : Single female-headed household with no able-bodied male member within the household, e.g. no children over the age of 18 ; Person with disability with no able-bodied member within the household e.g. no caretaker, no children over the age of 18, no nearby relatives who can assist ; Person with a grave medical illness or chronic illness with no able-bodied member within a household, e.g. no caretaker, no children over the age of 18, no nearby relatives who can assist ; Elderly over the age of 60 with no able-bodied member within a household, e.g. no caretaker, no children over the age of 18, no nearby relatives who can assist ; Child-headed household.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

HEKS/EPER has set up a Help Desk to ensure rohingya families receiving assistance can provide feedbacks and complaints about the assistance provided. Regular messaging about the CFRM at SMS level is provided during trainings and distribution to ensure proper feedback mechanism at different level and not only related to shelter activities. SUM program is built to encourage households to selecting materials they consider the most appropriate for their shelter within a range of points and with technical support of the shelter team. Community orientation at different level is ensured so that households are clearly informed on modalities of the assistance.

This assistance will focus on repairing stairs, protect damaged slopes and repair or build new drains. It reaches directly and indirectly about 10% of HHs, which means 1792 HHs. Community Engagement will be a key point as the work will be completed by CfW supervised by HEKS/EPER technical team.
