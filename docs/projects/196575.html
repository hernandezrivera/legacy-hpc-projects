Project Summary

This project will respond to the needs of 216497 (IDPs:150998; HC:65499) (women:58454, men:51959, boys:51959, girls:4332, elderly male: 6496, elderly female: 43297, PWD: 43298) for 12 months through 2 interventions as follows:
The first intervention will target five informal settlements in Qourqeena and Maaret Masrin sub-districts to connect them to nearby water systems. Then support them with operating costs to pump the safe water from the network towards these camps daily. Also, this intervention will target another nine camps in the Maaret Masrin sub-district to be connected to the nearby sewage networks to ensure safer and more sustainable sewage services in camps.
On the other hand, this intervention will continue to provide full-package emergency WASH services to 86,374 individuals in 56 informal IDP settlements in Idleb and Aleppo, including providing chlorinated water via tankers or water networks. And installing more polyethylene tanks to accommodate the increase in water responded to the Cholera outbreak. Additionally, light maintenance activities for water storage tanks and handwashing points will be conducted to ensure their functioning; IhsanRD will test the water (at the source and communal tanks). Also, cash voucher assistance (CVA) and consumable hygiene kits will be distributed in all targeted locations to help apply the recommended hygiene practices.
In terms of sanitation services, Ihsan will construct new Latrines, complete with water tanks, septic tanks, and hand washing basins in the informal IDP settlements. In addition to connecting nine camps to nearby sanitation systems, IhsanRD will collect solid waste from 53 camps and remove the waste to landfills. IhsanRD will distribute waste containers to prevent littering. Important hygiene promotion activities, including COVID-19 and Cholera awareness materials, will be disseminated, accompanied by soap distribution. This component contributes to the mobilization of the community as the hygiene promoters will come from the community, taking into consideration the preventive measures.
The second intervention will provide sustainable WASH infrastructures in Kastanah Fawqa, Qminas, Falyun, Haranbush, Kelly, Kafrehmul, Ketyan, Martein, Tilaada, Majdaliya, Toum, Quneitra, Bennsh, Milis, and Batbu communities that have been considered as high-priority locations through the rehabilitation of 5 water stations, 5 sewage systems, and 2 wastewater treatment plant to serve 110,540 individuals. This intervention seeks mainly to provide sustainable and affordable power sources by installing hybrid solar energy. In addition to carrying out the other rehabilitation works required to ensure the proper functioning of the water systems. This intervention will cover the operations and maintenance costs for three months for the newly rehabilitated water stations. As efforts are made to establish/re-activate a tariff collection and accounting system and to sustain the water system in these communities in coordination with Services Units. The rehabilitation of sewage systems in the Ketyan, Martein Quneitra, Bennsh, and Milis communities. The works will include installing concrete pipes, excavation; backfilling; cleaning; unblocking sewage pipes and manholes, an extension of sewage networks, and repairing the concrete in existing drainage and manholes for 46,906 individuals. In addition, two wastewater treatment systems in Tilaada and Batabu (Kafr Nash) will be implemented. The wastewater treatment will use the ABR decentralized wastewater plant. This intervention has a great impact on the response to cholera response to avoid using wastewater in irrigation by farmers and to ensure a clean environment.

3 - Needs

According to HNO 2023 WASH needs of affected populations will continue to be high across 2023.Access to sufficient and affordable safe water, adequate sanitation, solid waste management and/or hygiene supplies will remain a challenge for more than 2 million people seeking shelter in IDP Sites. They are highly dependent on continued humanitarian assistance, with 95 per cent of interviewed residents of IDP sites having received some kind of WASH humanitarian assistance. Water trucking services are the only source of water for 73 per cent IDP sites residents and only 39 percent HHs are connected to (simplified) sewer networks, indicating a continued need for medium-term investment in IDP site infrastructure to provide more sustainable, affordable, and safer ways to supply water and dispose of wastewater. Despite the sector assistance, 43 per cent of IDPs in sites could not access one or multiple hygiene items, and 32 per cent faced barriers to effective handwashing. Also, 17 per cent of HHs reported issues with toilets functionality or wastewater disposal. Disruptions and poor quality of services in IDP sites have also direct negative physical and mental well-being and protection consequences, notably on women and girls, and can ease the spread of waterborne disease, as cholera, with 65 suspected cases reported in 10 IDPs sites by 15th October 2022.

4 - Implementation of activities

Work will be done to implement the 2 interventions of the project simultaneously. After making the project announcement and signing MoUs with the local councils, consulting with stakeholders, and updating the BOQs according to the existing gap, considering the needs of the most vulnerable people and people with disabilities. The necessary purchases and contracting with service and material providers will follow our approved standard procurement procedures.In the first intervention, work will be done to connect 14 camps to the nearby water and/or sewage network. At the same time, work will be done to continue providing 56 camps with life-saving WASH services within the project period. In the 2nd intervention, work will be done to rehabilitate 5 water systems and support them with operating expenses for 3 months, including fuel, chlorine, oil, and electricity, in addition to rehabilitating the sewage systems in 5 cities and establishing 2 waste treatment units.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

PSEA is at the core of IRD responses. All IRD staff will be complied with it. Effective systems are in place for preventing and responding to SEA acts by integrating PSEA in IRD policies and making sure that all personnel read and sign the code of conduct , and that all contracts with suppliers/contractors include clauses on PSEA and CSG. IRD also provides awareness training to all staff in the organization on PSEA and CSG principles.Also, IRD will ensure to provide awareness-raising sessions on GBV, PSEA, and CSG to consider all prevention measures in COVID-19. Moreover, the referral pathway will be carried out when needed.Implementation will be conducted through integrated WASH and Protection mobile teams during all the projects that apply for protection and humanitarian principles for all activities. Since all the project staff are well qualified and trained to deliver such activities, the quality of services under PSEA will be assessed will be guaranteed.This project is built taking fully into account: Do No Harm, Age, Gender, Diversity mainstreaming (AGDM), and commitment to safe and equal access to assistance. The project team will set the necessary participatory mechanisms for different groups in the targeted locations, including men, women, boys, and girls, in addition to the PWDs, elderly people who are the affected people in the COVID -19 and decide which stakeholders need to be involved in the different implementation stages.
