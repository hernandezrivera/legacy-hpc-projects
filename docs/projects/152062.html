Project Summary

Mercy Corps is proposing a 12-month project that will holistically address the basic needs of 3500 households targeting IDPs, host communities, returnees and refugees in Tripoli, Sabha, Sirte, Misrata and Benghazi to meet their urgent basic needs through the provision of multi-purpose cash assistance In line with the MPCA section of the HRP.
The project will respond to the acute humanitarian needs of the most vulnerable households while increasing their resilience to reduce ongoing and future humanitarian needs and to assist a durable recovery. The main objective of the project is to enable vulnerable individuals to access basic goods and services, increasing access to food security and health-related goods and services through the provision of multi-purpose cash assistance.
Mercy Corps will continue its support to the leadership of the Cash Working Group (CWG) and the Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI).

Needs

Libya has been in a state of turmoil since the overthrow of longtime leader Muammar Gadhafi in2011. Conditions deteriorated significantly after a power struggle between rival political factions escalated in the summer of 2014; civil, security, and social services have since collapsed in many parts of the country. Libya is experiencing an economic crisis, characterized by a drop in GDP, the drastic depreciation of the Libyan dinar, a severe liquidity shortage and rapid inflation. Findings from assessments within IDP and returnee communities have shown that issues related to accessing basic needs are more related to the lack of cash, and the inflation, than to the availability of the supplies. As a result, key commodities are no longer affordable to a large percentage of the Libyan population. Mercy Corps surveys in Tripoli showed that liquidity issues represent a main barrier to accessing services, and identified neighborhoods (such as Tarhuna in Murqub district) where families are struggling to cope with inflation and are confined into peripheral banlieue with extremely limited access to basic services and items.

Activities

The project is to address the urgent basic needs of highly vulnerable IDP, returnee, host community households and refugees, in Tripoli, Sabha, Sirte, Misrata and Benghazi, and improve their access to essential goods and services in a dignified manner by providing 3 cash transfers over the span of 3 months to 3000 households of highly vulnerable IDP, returnee and host community while also providing a one-off cash transfer to 300 refugees and 200 vulnerable Libyan households. MPCA under this project will support families to overcome the hardship that is caused by liquidity crisis and the conflict by directly providing cash assistance for vulnerable HHs to access goods and services. Mercy Corps will be using different delivery mechanisms according to the context to ensure the flexibility and the relevancy of the program. Mercy Corps will be deploying mobile money and electronic cards as delivery mechanisms. Mercy Corps' methodology to identify beneficiaries will be through conducting house to house visits or phone call surveys when security doesn't allow for visits. Mercy Corps will be implementing the agreed and harmonized targeting criteria of the Cash Work Group (CWG). Furthermore, as a co-lead and a member of the CWG, Mercy Corps will ensure a strong coordination with the CWG partners and other sectors like Protection, Shelter NFI, WASH and Food sector to ensure that other sectors are well represented and incorporated in the minimum expenditure basket and the targeting criteria as MPCA is a multi-sectoral intervention.

Access, safety and dignity of beneficiaries

In order to remain adherent to the do no harm principle, Mercy Corps considers necessary not to phrase potential differences in packages between host communities and IDPs as consequence of the status of the households. In a politically and socially sensitive environment, such kind of categorization will, likely, increase inter-community tensions while ensuring that all distribution locations are accessible by all the targeted population in a safe and dignified manner while making sure that there are feedback and complains mechanisms in place including a hotline and complain boxes at the distribution.
