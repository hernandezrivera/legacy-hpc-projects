Project Summary

The purpose of the project is to assure that the health status of refugees and asylum seekers residing in Tongogara refugee camp (TRC) is compatible with their achievement of self-sufficiency. Tongogara Refugee Camp clinic is often the first access point to health care for 14,050 refugees and asylum seekers (6,463 females; 7,587 males) from DRC, Rwanda, Burundi and elsewhere residing in Tongogara Refugee Camp. Among the health care barriers for this population of concern are communication and language barriers, acculturation challenges, divergent cultural beliefs about health, and difficulty establishing trust. The impact of such obstacles is magnified in patients with chronic health conditions and disabilities and is exacerbated by crises as the clinic becomes overwhelmed with locals seeking medical assistance at the clinic. The project intends to ensure adequate medicines and sundries for Tongogara Refugee Camp Clinic and expand secondary and tertiary health services and to facilitate the screening of new arrivals at the reception centre.

Needs

Refugees are at a higher risk for contracting certain diseases or having other health problems due to factors such as poor nutrition, poor sanitation and lack of adequate medical care. Displaced populations' health is mainly affected by infectious disease, mental health, and chronic diseases that maybe uncommon in the country in which they eventually settle. Refugee health status is largely due to factors such as the migrant's geographic origin, condition of the refugee camp where they lived, and personal, physical, and psychological conditions of the migrant, either pre-existing or acquired while traveling from their homeland to Tongogara Refugee Camp. Another obstacle that refugee women face in maintaining their menstrual health is limited to no access to an adequate amount of sanitary supplies. Tongogara refugee clinic experience significant challenges in caring for refugees including;-Facilities for screening of new arrivals-Adequate supplies i.e. medicines and sundries for the primary health care facility to adequately serve the population of concern-Resources to facilitate secondary and tertiary medical treatment for the population of concern- Insufficient time to address refugees’ needs is particularly difficult given that refugee populations are often unfamiliar with the concepts of prevention and long-term treatment of asymptomatic conditions and require greater amount of time for health education. - Health Promoters and Community Health Workers also may have knowledge deficits regarding refugeeexperience and common diseases hence the need for their capacity building.- Barriers to building rapport can impact provision of efficient and effective care due to patient reluctance to disclose important aspects of their health hence the need for psycho social counselling support -Poor health literacy impacting on adherence to medical recommendations by refugee patients

Activities

Health activities will include;-Procurement of medicines and sundries for Tongogara Refugee Camp Clinic to serve at least 14,050 refugees and asylum seekers-Establishment of screening facilities in Tongogara Refugee Camp-Facilitating General Practitioner and specialist nurses weekly visits to Tongogara Clinic-Health and hygiene education promoting heath literacy and preventative health education-Campaigns on Reduction of HIV Stigma and Discrimination, -Refresher Training for Community Based Councillors and IEC Materials-Cultural orientation, education on basic aspects of health care services and prevention and mental health screening-Psycho social support and mental health support
