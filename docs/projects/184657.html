Project Summary

Given the precarious food security, nutrition and livelihood situation across Somalia, conducting assessments and analysis on a regular basis and continuous monitoring of the situation remains vital. At present, the Government of Somalia has limited institutional and technical capacity to generate and provide such data. To fill the gap, the FAO-led Food Security and Nutrition Analysis Unit (FSNAU) provides critical and timely information and analyses on the food security and nutrition situation in Somalia to a broad range of stakeholders. FSNAU information and analyses facilitate and support effective evidence based decision-making in support of humanitarian, recovery and development interventions.
FSNAU identifies food insecure populations by livelihood and by district, pinpoints the specific challenges facing vulnerable groups, including women in achieving food security and reports on the population’s nutritional status to support targeted interventions. In addition to its early warning function, FSNAU carries out relevant baseline livelihood studies for an improved understanding of underlying causes of food, nutrition and livelihood insecurity. Gender analysis is an integral part of FSNAU’s data collection analytical process. FSNAU also provides food security and nutrition related technical and institutional capacity development support to Somali institutions and other key actors. The project has two key components:
I. In 2022, FSNAU will continue to provide information and analysis on current and emerging food security and nutrition situation in Somalia in a manner that supports prioritization and targeting of appropriate food security and nutrition response interventions. FSNAU will also provide additional information through its Early Warning Early Action Dashboard in order to support timely and prioritized response.
II. Staff of relevant government institutions at Federal level, Federal Member State level and Somaliland will also be trained and supported to participate in seasonal food security and nutrition assessments and analyses. On 21 September 2021, FAO signed a landmark agreement with the Somali National Bureau of Statistics (SNBS) to commence the gradual transfer of ownership of FSNAU to the Federal Government of Somalia. Under the agreement, FSNAU will build the capacity of the SNBS as well as government institutions working in areas such as agriculture, livestock, health, disaster management and statistics. The intended result is to ensure that these institutions gradually acquire adequate institutional capacity to assess, analyse, monitor and report on current as well as emerging food security and nutrition situations in Somalia as well as associated underlying factors that compromise food security, nutrition, markets and livelihoods. As part of the transition process, core skills training related to food security and nutrition assessment, monitoring, analysis and reporting, equipment and related capacity development support will be provided to government institutions.
FSNAU products are disseminated through the FSNAU website (www.fsnau.org), mailing list and through regular as well as ad hoc presentations to stakeholders, including the Somalia Humanitarian Country Team.
The main beneficiaries of the information and analysis generated by FSNAU under this project will be government line ministries, UN agencies, local and international NGOs and resource partners. Indirect FSNAU project beneficiaries will be the millions of food insecure people and acutely malnourished children who are expected to benefit from humanitarian assistance and livelihood support provided by the Government, UN agencies, local and international NGOs and resource partners.
FSNAU and REACH provide information and analysis to IASC Clusters in and the HRP/HNO process. FSNAU provides information and analyses that support the Food Security and Nutrition Clusters and REACH provides support to other IASC clusters in Somalia.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

FSNAU conducts seasonal assessments and surveys using a representative sampling methodology that ensures no bias in the identification and selection of households to be interviewed. As the data collected covers all members of selected (sampled) households and is disaggregated by age and gender, the results reflect the needs of all of the population surveyed, including the needs of marginal groups, women, girls and boys. In hard to reach areas where FSNAU staff are unable to travel, FSNAU Technical Staff conduct remotely facilitated interviews with the assistance of locally recruited enumerators who live and work in the communities that are being assessed.
