Project Summary

Somalia having been a country with protracted crisis, new and old IDPs continue to be in lack of shelter, both temporary and permanent shelters, this has on regular basis being posing a major challenge to the communities. Through the project WRRS will seek to achieve the cluster objective. WRRS will achieve this through distribution of emergency shelter kits, non-food items and construction of shelters. WRRS will also support the achievement through provision of sustainable shelters with improved security land tenure, NFIs distribution site planning and mapping of basic services. Facilitate access to durable solutions for IDPs who are willing to locally integrate and return to their places of origin. As a general approach, WRRS will promote linkage with camp coordination and camp management, and relief and development by promoting local building culture and building back safer. WRRS will ensure that assistance provided is appropriate and acceptable to the target people which includes the host community and the IDPs in kismayu by engaging on community consultations before project implementation and beneficiary accountability through participation and feedback. Accountability to affected people will be supported through formation complaint. The project will target IDP women, Men, boys and girls who are exposed to risks and other vulnerabilities. Women and girls from the IDPs will be given first priority as they are considered at risk and have no protection mechanism. The project will be implemented in three regions that is Lower Juba, Kismayo, Afmadow, Dhobley, , and Dolow . WRRS will be targeting the newly displace and settled IDPs that are in urgent need of temporary shelter and NFIs. WRRS will ensure that coordination is up to date with the key players being involved in resolving this long term crisis of shelter within the targeted regions.

Mitigation Measures

3b) Discrimination against women is widespread and systemic, they are subject to exclusion in many decision making processes and especially in Somalia, women are the most disadvantaged group in the society making it a risk factor to their protection. Women continue to face barriers to their participation, and are vastly under-represented in their communities and especially the minority clans. There are also gender differences in terms of inclusion in the labour market. Most women work in the informal economy, which is characterized by job insecurity, poor working conditions and low pay. The persistence and reproduction of women’s exclusion is also supported by social norms and religious and cultural values. 2b) In Somalia communities, traditional barriers still prevent women from going out of their homes to work. For some women, having primary or sole responsibility for household duties, including childcare, also prevents them from working outside their homes or areas of residence. The target population for this project will be the most vulnerable IDP women, girls and boys from the minority clans who are at risk of exclusion and discrimination. The design for the project will ensure that all are involved in the process from the design to the implementation of the project. They will be involved in major decision making process to ensure that there is ownership and project sustainability is achieved.4d) Serious protection concerns persist, putting IDPs and the most vulnerable host community’ lives at risk, forcing many to flee, exposing them to multiple risks while displaced, and impeding durable solutions. Compounding the conflict and its inherent risks to civilians, is a massive increase in drought related displacement and further exposed civilians to serious protection risks. Women, children and marginalized communities are especially at risk and face specific protection concerns. The protection crisis is also characterized by entrenched but fluid societal divisions, resulting in competition for resources across social groups. severe drought and the ongoing conflict continue to cause displacement. It is estimated that the number of IDPs have surpassing two million. Displacement dynamics have consistently been directed mainly towards urban centers and merging with urbanization dynamics of increasing pressure on services in larger towns and other urban cities. IDP settlements, many reside in overcrowded areas with poor living conditions and limited security provision. Where the basic needs of IDP are not met due to inconsistent service provision, exclusion.WRRS has a longstanding presence in Somalia, especially South Central Somalia with offices in Dhobley, Afmadow, Kismayo, Garowe While in other Regions, WRRS has offices in Baidoa in Bay region, Doolow and Luuq in Gedo region and Dhobley in Lower Juba since 2004 and has good understanding of the security context. WRRS has in place Standard Operating Procedure for security in Somalia, in order to guarantee a systematic approach to safety in humanitarian actions and specific rules of conduct in such a challenging context of operation. WRRS works with other agencies (i.e.UN and Government) on security matters by sharing information. Teams involved in operations are regularly informed on major and minor risks that may arise during the course of operation in field missions. A security checklist is provided on monthly basis by the Field Security Focal Points at WRRS's field offices across the regions that WRRS operates in. Before traveling to the field offices, especially in South Central where security level is at high risk, WRRS staff submit travel authorization requests to the Security Coordinator for approval. Considering the specific security threats related to operations in Somalia, the organization has conducted in 2017 a Risks Assessment and defined a set of risk mitigation measures, including security risks. This protocol is reviewed and updated on regular basis.
