Project Summary

The NFI project is an integrated project between WASH 33.3%, NFI 33.3%, and protection 33.3% sectors to cover the NFI (hygiene, kitchen, and dignity items) needs of the most vulnerable Syrian IDPs population, to meet the basic and NFI-related needs of the 7000 vulnerable Syrian IDPs families in Idlib (hygiene, and kitchen items) and 14,000 females through targeted cash assistance/ vouchers for a one-year period.

4 - Needs

WASH, (NFIs) and protection support remain a primary need for the crisis-affected population in Idlib and Aleppo, with the estimation of 2.8 million people in need of this kind of support. Access to and availability of Food, Non-Food Items (NFIs) and protection remain limited. Through its in-kind and voucher distributions, SoP has been addressing the crucial needs for (Food, NFIs items) of IDPs in Idlib since 2016 As the number of Syrian IDPs in area continues to grow and further needs are being identified by SoP teams through periodic field assessments and post-distribution monitoring, SoP now sets the goal to continue the targeted cash assistance to Syrian IDPs in the targeted area and reduce negative coping strategies. To Assess the situation in Northwest of Syria under three diﬀerent sectors and propose optimal response modalities and develop operational plan there, Space of Peace Organization conducted a multi-sectorial needs assessment in Idlib district during January-2020 assessing specifically in Idleb center, Harim, Dana, Sarmada, Bennsh, Darkosh, Kafr Takharim, Ma’arrat Tamsrin, Salqin and Teftnaz which belongs to Idleb. In addition to assesing Daret Azza in Aleppo. The conflict between the regime and armed opposition groups has intensified since the end of 2019 in the southern Idleb governorate, and southern Aleppo governorate, causing a wave of displacement. Over the last 2 months, there have been hundreds of airstrikes across the Idlib province, causing much damage and destruction. The Syrian regime captured Since October 22 more than 125 villages in Idlib, and South Aleppo. The majority displaced into central and northern Idleb as follow: 55,937 IDPs were displaced in Idleb center, 7,717 in Harim, 163,877 in Dana, 9,092 in Bennsh, 13,700 in Darkosh, 3,907 in KafrTakharim, 59,496 in Maaret Tamsrin, 6,229 in Teftnaz and 22,349 in Salqin. As for Daret Azza in Aleepo it has 11,655 IDPs.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

A lack of food and NFIs, WASH facilities and shelter create a myriad of protection concerns, especially for vulnerable groups, such as the elderly, women and adolescents. SoP will continue to monitor protection risks, working with protection colleagues if specific issues arise – and will ensure that any activities including targeting, distribution and monitoring do not increase these risks. Due to security threats from the conflict, such as aerial bombing, most relief assistance is being delivered in a low-profile manner, through the door-to-door processor in public buildings where IDPs currently reside. This distribution modality helps to limit the exposure of beneficiaries and the local association members. Furthermore, contextual and historical knowledge of the local associations assists in mitigating the risk in terms of on-going security threats. Where it is deemed appropriate by the local associations, there are distribution points where beneficiaries convene to collect distributions. In some areas, public distributions are unavoidable, but care is taken to arrange them at what is considered the safest possible location and time of day. When distributions are done in a more centralized manner, utilizing information solicited from beneficiary communities, SoP will ensure that they are done at times and in places that are safe and accessible to women and girls, as well as the elderly and people with disabilities and that any barriers to access as a result of gender norms or physical ability are taken into account when planning for the distribution. As needed, extra measures will be taken to minimize risk, such as buddy systems for going to and from distribution points. Community input will be solicited as to which measures would be most feasible and preferred, and support will be provided by SoP to ensure a sustainable, community-driven approach is adopted.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: The security situation remains stable and the center remains accessible. The acceptance of SOP and its center by the local community and the local authorities is sustained. The need for GBV programme in the area persists. Risks 1:military escalation erupts in the targeted location mitigation: we will train our staff on emergency preparedness and GBV closure SOPs. the situation will be monitored closely with the field staff and the local authorities. Risk 2: the confidentiality of the GBV is breached. mitigation: we will put in place strict disciplinary measures and train the staff on the code of conduct. Our staff have been working in this center for several years and they are aware of the sensitivity of GBV cases. Risk 3: beneficiaries will not attend the vocational training regularly. mitigation 3: beneficiaries will be consulted on the topics, the pace of the course and they will be able to give feedback. the reasons for their lack of commitment will be analyzed and addressed accordingly. Assumption: the security situation remains stable and the area is accessible.
