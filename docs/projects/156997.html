Project Summary

The IRC is proposing to directly implement child protection programming, to support with children being safe in their homes and in their communities. This will include through child protection case management, psychosocial support programming, parenting skills sessions, safe healing and learning spaces (SHLS) and mobile community based programming- namely ‘Reach Up and Learn’. In Northeast Syria, violence is pervasive, and protection issues—such as child labor and child recruitment, early marriage, intimate partner violence, sexual violence, economic exploitation and family separation, among others—are alarmingly prevalent . The 2018 Humanitarian Needs Overview reports that 52% of communities report child separation from their caregivers, with death of caregivers, divorce, financial stress, child marriage, and child recruitment as the primary reasons reported for child separation. The majority of separated children are cared for within kinship care, a pre-existing cultural norm, however there are indications that these arrangements are under-strain. The IRC is coordinating with Save the Children to drive forward efforts to support foster care in Northeast Syria. This is, unfortunately, coupled with the reality that there is a general lack of protection actors operating in Northeast Syria, challenging the ability to establish effective referral networks and adequately connect people to services. The needs of local populations overwhelm the services available, and a continuous absence of protection services compounds the long-term negative effects of conflict. IRC will support local actors to ensure the continuity of our programming, and long-term support to children at risk. Case management will be offered by trained caseworkers, including on reunification. Alternative care will be established and supported by IRC staff, with close collaboration from other child protection actors. Safe Healing and Learning Spaces (SHLS) will also support psychosocial support for children at risk, parents and caregivers. Parenting skills curriculum will be offered to parents and caregivers, and mobile Reach Up & Learn (RU&L) programming which supports parents and caregivers through home- visits, and teaches them techniques for working with their children.

Needs

As underscored by the Child Protection Area of Responsibility (AoR) for Whole of Syria: one in three girls and boys have known nothing but a lifetime shaped by conflict: 5.3 million girls and boys are in need of humanitarian assistance; 2.5 million girls and boys are internally displaced; over 1 million girls and boys live in hard to reach locations; two-thirds of girls and boys live in extreme poverty; 1.75 million girls and boys are out of school; and 1.35 million are at risk of dropping out. The 2018 HNO underscores the broad need of protection services in Syria, with 82% of surveyed communities reporting occurrence of child labour preventing school attendance, 69% reporting incidences of child marriages, 52% reporting family separation, 47% reporting child recruitment into armed forces, 27% reporting sexual violence, all contributing to the trauma and psychological distress of children. Children’s experiences of violence in their daily lives in their communities, schools and homes, along with repeated displacements, loss of or separation from family members and friends, dramatic deterioration in living conditions, divisions in their community, and lack of basic social services is profoundly affecting children’s well-being and development. Real and immediate concerns over the possible loss of a generation due to a combination of child protection risks, lack of access to quality education and limited opportunities for adolescents and youth to meaningfully engage with their communities underlines the importance of the “No Lost Generation” approach in placing these issues at the center of humanitarian needs analysis and the response to the Syria crisis. Reports of forcible recruitment of children have been prevalent in ISIL-controlled areas of Ar-Raqqa and Deir-ez-Zor governorates. The Humanitarian Needs Overview 2018 reports that children are especially vulnerable, particularly those who are unaccompanied and separated or living with older/disabled caregivers or out of school. Children engaging in child labour or recruited for military purposes will require specific consideration. Youth often lack employment and appropriate livelihood opportunities and may therefore be at particular risk of exploitation or engaging in at risk activities. Adolescent girls may face the specific risk of early or forced marriage.

Implementation of activities

The IRC will continue delivery of child protection responses in Northeast Syria through five Safe Healing and Learning Spaces (SHLS), which include provision of structured psychosocial support (PSS), basic literacy and numeracy for children, and parenting skills sessions for caregivers to prevent violence in the home and improve children’s wellbeing. Three mobile teams in Raqqa, Deir ez Zor and Tel Hamis will support children at risk. The IRC prioritizes the enrollment of vulnerable children in SHLS services with no registration cutoff. To ensure accessibility, the IRC will train staff on facilitation techniques, make SHLSs physically accessible to all abilities, conduct campaigns addressing children with disabilities, and collaborate with actors providing physical rehabilitation and mobility aids for children with disabilities. The IRC strives to ensure gender equity, working with families to improve access of girls to the SHLS and other CP activities. FGDs are used to understand the responsibilities girls and boys face in the home, with the reality of the child’s home life considered in the scheduling of SHLS activities. Multiple sessions for the same age-group are held throughout the day, guaranteeing that children will be able to access at least one of the available slots. The IRC will provide non-focused structured PSS to boys and girls in the form of structured Social and Emotional Learning (SEL) sessions, and to caregivers in the form of parenting skills education. Additional MHPSS support will be provided to adolescents under the IRC’s Now, I’m Stronger curriculum, working particularly with children exposed to exploitation and child labor. Both curricula fall under level 2 of the PSS pyramid as community and family support and have an explicit focus on reducing the negative impacts of toxic stress and dealing with personal and family issues. SEL and parenting education support children and adults in acquiring and effectively applying knowledge, attitudes and skills necessary to understand and manage emotions, set and achieve positive goals, feel and show empathy for others, establish and maintain positive relationships, and make responsible decisions. The parenting skills training primarily aims to promote safe, healthy and supportive homes and relationships, as well as improving parents’ stress management skills, positive parenting practices, and strategies for supporting boys and girls with psychosocial needs. ECD programming will also be integrated into the SHLS models with some sessions set aside for 3-5 year olds for the delivery of IRC’s Pre-school Healing Classroom model which first piloted in Lebanon in 2016 and has continually resulted in significant improvements in young children’s literacy, numeracy, motor skills and social-emotional outcomes. Trained facilitators will support young children in a range of activities, including art, literacy, social and emotional learning and numeracy, supporting them in developing key skills. Child protection case management will be offered for at-risk children and parents and caregivers, working with the family unit to strengthen children’s resiliency, empower parents and identify local solutions to protection concerns. The IRC utilizes a holistic and child-centered approach to case management, providing children with necessary services through regular service mapping and the establishment of robust referral pathways. Individual PSS sessions are delivered as part of the case management process, providing tailored support to children. Alternative care will be set up in coordination with other child protection actors. In line with interagency SOPs, IRC will work with communities to establish alternative care and ongoing support for foster families. This will begin in Al Hole and Raqqa City, and then after lessons learned are gathered from camp and urban settings, this will be rolled out across NES. CPIMS+ is also being rolled out in 2019 by IRC and Save the Children.

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection is a core objective of all humanitarian work in crisis affected areas. Given that affected populations heavily depend on humanitarian services for survival, it is crucial that services be delivered in a safe and dignified manner. While the operating environment in Northeast Syria is relatively stable, the programming during the award period is likely to face a higher level of instability due to the 19 December 2018 announcement of the U.S. intention to withdrawal from Syria and ongoing statements from the Government of Turkey on an intended military incursion. Additionally, concerns remain over the presence of IEDs in Ar Raqqa and Deir-ez-Zor governorate. Therefore, there must be a close working relationships with other actors in the Northeast to monitor the situation and coordinate on operational and programmatic responses. Additionally, IRC will work closely with the Mine Action Group and other actors to ensure safe programming and to prevent pull factors towards areas of potential harm. Security screening procedures remain important to ensuring return movements are informed and voluntary. Children are disproportionately affected by crisis and armed conflict, and have been amongst the most severely affected by the conflict in Syria. In Northeast Syria, violence is pervasive, and protection issues—such as missing documentation (e.g. birth certificates), child labor and child recruitment, early marriage, intimate partner violence, sexual violence, economic exploitation and family separation, among others—are alarmingly prevalent across the country. This is, unfortunately, coupled with the reality that there is a general lack of protection actors operating in Northeast Syria, challenging the ability to establish effective referral networks and adequately connect people to services. The needs of local populations overwhelm the services available, and a continuous absence of protection services compounds the long-term negative effects of conflict. Violence against children is pervasive, including in areas where hostilities decreased and contribute to the further deterioration of coping mechanisms. Girls are reportedly being married younger, with child marriage- sometimes involving girls of 10 or 11 years old-framed as a protective coping strategy in 11 out of 14 governorates within the 2018 HNO.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Program staff will be trained on PFA, communication skills, helping skills, GBV disclosures, child safeguarding, Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), ethical data management, and case management. The IRC’s supervision structure ensures that technical oversight is provided through regular internal program audits, and group and individual supervision sessions. Technical Advisors provide regular support visits, remote support and technical guidance on program implementation, review and design. Project monitoring will also include observation tools and file check lists that, alongside regularly updated Standard Operating Procures, will help to ensure adherence to best practice. All focused non-specialized services will have pre- and post-assessments to measure improvement and impact. Beneficiary satisfaction surveys will also enable the IRC to adjust programming to suit the interests and needs of the participants. Finally, recognizing the complex operating environment, self-care and staff care materials are shared with all Protection staff to encourage dialogue in the workplace and a culture of communicating challenges. All IRC employees are automatically enrolled in the Employee Assistance Program (EAP) which provides employees and their eligible dependents with 24-hour access to confidential behavioral health services and resources in Arabic. The CP team is taking seriously any data protection considerations and will have no paper trail in the centers with names of participants – all attendance will be captured with unique protection codes. Case management data is captured through CPIMS+ as the global best practice in CP data collection, with rollout led by IRC and Save the Children. IRC places focus on informed consent/ assent, the Best Interests of the Child and data confidentiality.
