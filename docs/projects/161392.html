Project Summary

Concern will continue to support preventative and curative services in Aweil West and Aweil North in NBeG, Bentiu PoC and Guit in Unity and Juba PoC in Central Equatoria. Concern will emphasise prevention of MAM and SAM in this response through focus on behaviour change communication to improve MIYCN practices. This will be done through individual counselling at nutrition sites and supporting lead mothers to support MtMSGs to improve MIYCN. Considering men’s roles as decision-makers, they will be supported by MCAs with trainings in MIYCN and understand how their roles impact on their spouses’ and children’s nutrition. Concern will also integrate ECD and GMP during OTP and TSFP. Curative services will be provided through the CMAM approach. Children under 5 with SAM will be treated in OTP and children with SAM and medical complications will be treated in Stabilisation Centres. Children under 5, PLW and elderly (in PoCs only) with MAM will be treated with TSFP. CNVs will screen and refer for malnutrition to ensure early treatment. As waterborne diseases are a main cause of malnutrition, the response will be integrated with WASH where Concern has WASH interventions with caregivers with children in OTP and TSFP being targeted for WASH activities. In NBeG, the Health programme will support lead mothers and MCAs to improve health practices to reduce common illness that result in malnutrition. Concern will support nutrition beneficiaries with nutrition gardens in PoCs and vegetable gardens in Guit, Aweil West and Aweil North to improve access to nutritious foods. Concern as a referral system for protection cases identified at nutrition sites. In Leer, Concern will support its partner, Nile Hope to provide curative and preventative nutrition services.

1 - Needs

Although the R-ARCSS has resulted in improvements in security, 7.2 million people were in need of humanitarian assistance in 2019. 1,301,000 children under 5 years of age are acutely malnourished, 292,000 children with SAM, 1,008,000 with MAM 352,000 are PLW with MAM and in urgent need of treatment (IPC, Aug 2019). 6.45 million people are currently at Crisis levels or worse of food insecurity and December 2019 it is anticipated that 50.5% of the population will be at Crisis or Emergency Levels of food insecurity (IPC Aug 2019). Although food security is likely to improve with harvests and malnutrition reduce, by April 2020 malnutrition is likely to increase due to high morbidity levels, poor access to health and nutrition services, poor water and sanitation infrastructure, poor nutrition, health and hygiene practices and poor food production. All of Concern’s operation areas of Juba PoC, Bentiu PoC and Guit in Unity and Aweil West and Aweil North in NBeG are currently at Serious levels or worse of malnutrition (IPC, Aug 2020).Households in PoCs remain vulnerable to malnutrition as they continue to live in the sites where there is poor sanitation and hygiene practices, limited income generating activities, increased disease risks due to congestion and persistent gender inequalities. IDPs in the PoCs are extremely vulnerable to food insecurity in absence of food assistance. In Juba PoC, most households are dependent on GFDs for their main food source. GAM increased from 12.8% in December 2017 to 16.4% in February 2019 (Concern SMART survey 2019) classified as Critical according to WHO. MIYCN practices are low, 17.1% mothers only fed their children from 4 or food groups which although shows slight improvement from December 2017 survey which was 15.4%, still remains low due to limited access to diverse food groups. 36.7% of mothers were not practicing exclusive breastfeeding during the first 6 months of child’s life. Mass MUAC screening for elderly population in Juba PoC conducted by Concern in April 2019 shows a GAM rate of 20.2%. In 2019, the GAM rate was 14.7% and SAM 1.9% in Bentiu PoC, while a decrease from a GAM rate of 16.4% and SAM 3.1% (Concern SMART survey 2019) still remains Serious. There are poor MIYCN practices in Bentiu PoC with a Minimum Acceptable Diet of 5.6% and Minimum Dietary Diversity of 32.1% (Concern SMART survey 2019). In Guit, the GAM rate in 2019 was 11.2% and SAM 1.7%, a decrease from 2017 where the GAM rate was 13.9% and SAM 2.9% (Concern SMART survey 2019). However these are post-harvest rates and are likely to have declined as hunger season progressed in 2019. In Guit, poor MIYCN practices persist with a Minimum Acceptable Diet of 5.6% and Minimum Dietary Diversity of 5.2% (Concern SMART survey 2019). Malnutrition is likely to increase during the lean period in 2020 in Unity due to poor nutrition, health and hygiene practices and weak health systems and food insecurity. NBeG counties to have high risks of malnutrition with all counties at Emergency levels of food insecurity and GAM levels above 9.9% (IPC, Aug 2019). This is due to delayed rains in 2018 production season and flooding in 2019 reducing harvests. In 2019, the GAM rate was at 12.7% and SAM at 2.5% in Aweil West and the GAM rate was 12.6% and SAM rate at 1.7% in Aweil North. This shows a decrease from 2017 where in Aweil West the GAM rate was 15.2% and SAM 3.6% and in Aweil North, the GAM rate was 14.5% and SAM 4.8%. MAM was at 10.2% in Aweil West and 10.7% Aweil North (Concern SMART surveys 2019). However, these rates are post-harvest and are likely to decline as the hunger season progresses. High malnutrition is due to high morbidity rates in children under 5 with 41% in Aweil West and 42.1% in Aweil North (Concern SMART surveys 2019), poor feeding and young child feeding practices with Minimum Acceptable Diet at 13% in Aweil West and 5.2% in Aweil North and Minimum Dietary Diversity at 5.1% in Aweil West and 13.6% in Aweil North.

2 - Activities

CO1: Behavioural Change Communication will be strengthened at nutrition sites through daily MIYCN counselling sessions. At community level, households will be accessed through support group approach. At community level, approximately 10-15 women will be grouped and one woman selected to be a lead mother be her members. The lead mothers will periodically (4-6 weeks interval) be trained on WASH, nutrition, FSL and health topics by the community outreach supervisors and focal persons for MIYCN. The lead mother trained will then return to her group and trains the mothers in her group with the same message she learned from the training. During the trainings, the lead mothers provide individual counselling sessions and develop plans with mothers to conduct home visits, to their designated households and shelters. MIYCN messages will be transcribed into drama and songs to promote nutrition and health behaviour change. Lead fathers will support other men with MIYCN trainings and to analyse how their role impact on their spouses’ and children’s nutrition. Concern will also continue to participate in Vitamin A Supplementation (VAS) campaigns in all the operational areas, providing human resources, vehicles and other pooled resources under the leadership of the VAS leading agent and MoH. Concern has a pool of experienced VAS campaigners and vaccinators trained and experienced from the bi-annual previous VAS campaigns in all its operational areas.CO2: Malaria testing and treatment will be conducted for all Severely Acute-Malnourished (SAM) cases on first contact at all nutrition sites. All Concern nutrition sites provide both OTP and TSFP services for SAM and MAM cases respectively, hence coverage for OTP malaria testing and treatment will be 100%. MAM cases presenting with signs and symptoms of malaria such as fever will be referred to the mainstream health service centres.CO3: All children with SAM and MAM will be screened for malaria and information documented. Concern will continue to integrate WASH with nutrition services. To strengthen quality of services, nutrition workers including nurses will be trained and supported through periodic support supervision mentorship and coaching sessions. WASH activities will be integrated with nutrition. Children identified with SAM will be given a preventive package including soap, Jerry cans and WASH kits if available. Concern is promoting establishment of demonstration gardens as well as nutrition gardens at the nutrition and at household. Concern will continue to explore acceptable, sustainable and environment friendly methods of farming in Bentiu PoC and greater Guit county. Mothers in their groups will be regularly engaged in trainings, clearing of the land, nursery bed preparation, planting, weeding and harvesting. Safety audit tools have been developed and designed to rapidly assess nutrition sites in identifying potential GBV related safety risks. Analysis of GBV needs and priorities will be integrated into SMART and KAP surveys that are periodically done. Concern has also a CFM in PoCs and outside PoCs a CRM support in identifying community need and priorities and timely address any GBV related issues.CO4: Periodically Concern will conduct KAP surveys and SMART survey to guide planning, resource allocation and decision-making. At nutrition sites, nutrition workers and CNVs will be trained on data collection to strengthen surveillance and timely reporting. Nutrition data collected on daily-based will then be compiled on weekly report formats and submitted for compilation. At the end of the month, the data collected will then be entered into the NIS.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Concern adheres to humanitarian principles including neutrality, impartiality and humanity and will provide curative and preventative nutrition services and support communities to become resilient to nutrition, health and hygiene risks based solely on need and in line with the MoH’s CMAM and MIYCN guidelines.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

NBeG has had no significant displacement, however if there is displacement, Concern will consult with local authorities and community leaders and nutrition partners such as planning CMAM services to ensure coverage to both host communities and returnees. Host communities in Guit often have to provide assistance to IDPs and returnees when they already are vulnerable, including sharing food resources and this increase food insecurity. Conflict and the resulting lack of services can be attributed to the inability of the resident IDP community to communicate in a productive and constructive way with humanitarian and state agencies, and this miscommunication may lead to antagonism. The programme will strengthening host and IDP community level monitoring on demands for service provision to ensure continued CMAM services in the programme and improve MIYCN practices. Concern will support community members to be accustomed to sharing their concerns and providing feedback on interventions, which is likely to reinforce their resilience and improve the quality of future humanitarian programmes.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Concern due its long presence in NBeG has build a strong relationship with the SMoH and CHDs under previous programmes to provide CMAM services to complement BPHS and has established relations with the SMoH and CHD in Guit. For this response, Concern has an MoU with the SMoH and CHDs to support CMAM at static and mobile sites including joint supportive supervision.. In Juba and Bentiu PoC, Concern coordinates with CHCs and in Guit, Aweil West and Aweil North, Concern collaborates with community leaders to plan CMAM and MIYCN activities. MIYCN activities are conducted by community volunteers including CNVs, lead mothers and Male Change Agents. SMART, SQUEAC and KAP surveys are implemented in coordination with the Nutrition Cluster, MoH and National Bureau of Statistics. Concern shares findings of these surveys to contribute to planning and sharing learnings to monitor malnutrition rates, CMAM coverage and nutrition, health and hygiene practices in Concern’s programme locations.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Concern adheres to the MoH’s CMAM guidelines in provision of OTP and TSFP and the MoH’s MIYCN guidelines for implementation of community level activities to improve nutrition practices. Concern submits monthly reports through the NIS to support the Nutrition Cluster and MoH to monitor performance of targets and trends in admissions to OTP and TSFP. Concern is also the co-lead of the national Nutrition Cluster and is involved in coordinating the nutrition response with partners, identifying gaps in nutrition services and sharing learnings.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Although Juba PoC and Bentiu PoC are protected by UNMISS and inside the PoCs by UNPOL, there are considerable risks of sexual and gender based violence to women and girls. In Guit, there are serious protection risks for beneficiaries where women and girls at risk of sexual and gender based violence and men and boys of forced recruitment into armed group. While NBeG remains relatively stable, there are still risks of sexual and gender based violence. Caregivers will be sensitised on protection risk reduction measures such as travelling to OTP and TSFP only in daylight hours and in mixed groups. OTP and TSFP will also be operational only during daylight hours. Concern will allow caregivers to administer RUSF and RUTF at nutrition sites if there is a risk that the therapeutic food will be sold in households.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

This nutrition response is a two year intervention that is designed to prioritise saving lives through treatment of MAM, SAM and SAM with medical complications. While there has been improvements in security in Unity, with the current situation remaining fragile and localised insecurity such as intercommunal violence, the prospect for IDPs in Juba and Bentiu PoCs returning is unlikely and continuing high food insecurity and morbidity rates in Aweil West and Aweil North, the GAM and SAM rates remain above or close to emergency thresholds, there is a need for nutrition services to continue to save the lives of children, women and elderly. However, there is a need consider sustainability and Concern will continue to engage with the Nutrition Cluster and other coordination mechanisms at national and state level in the planning process for the future of PoCs and humanitarian hubs. As the crisis and nutrition needs continue to remain acute, Concern does not anticipate exiting after the programme is completed. However, Concern will ensure sustainability of its nutrition activities. Concern has MoUs with SMoHs and CHDs to support their capacity to deliver CMAM services. The CMAM approach provides communities with the necessary skills and tools to assume management of acute malnutrition to reduce the impact of future nutrition crises in the project locations. Ownership of the project and sustainability will be increased hiring local staff and training and supporting community volunteers such as CNVs, lead mothers and lead fathers to train others on MIYCN and improve nutrition in their communities after the project. In addition, MtMSGs will be expected to continue to support mothers to improve nutrition once the programme is completed.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Concern will target women through MtMSGs, giving them with education and knowledge to make informed decisions on health and nutritional needs of their children and themselves. MCA approach will target men to actively engage other men in domestic work, and contribute to changing attitudes around gender norms and roles such as roles in decision-making on going to nutrition sites. Bentiu PoC and Guit there are considerable protection risks and in NBeG, while relatively stable there are still risks of sexual and gender based violence. Although Concern is not a Protection partner, protection needs to be considered at all stages of the programme cycle. All nutrition sites have latrines that are sex segregated and ensure privacy. Concern uses a triage approach where the very sick, elderly, disabled and caregivers with special needs receive consultations first before others. As per the MoH’s CMAM requirements, nutrition sites have adequate staff so that quality nutrition services are provided in a dignified manner. Having the sufficient staff will ensure waiting times are minimised for caregivers and children and so they are seen in the shortest possible time and reduce disruption to childcare activities. Each nutrition site in-charge will nominate a staff who will receive and compile protection concerns reported by beneficiaries for referral to Health and Protection partners. The CFM in Juba and Bentiu PoC and CRM in Guit, Aweil West and Aweil North will used for beneficiaries to register protection concerns in confidence and these will be referred to Protection partners with actions taken to minimise protection risks. During monitoring, separate female and male FGDs will be conducted that will provide a safe space for them to share any safety concerns or protection incidents and make necessary changes to the project as appropriate.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Caregivers attending OTP, TSFP and Stabilisation Centres will be asked to provide feedback on the quality of services. Beneficiaries such as MtMSG members, lead mothers and MCAs will provide feedback to Concern on quality of MIYCN trainings. Surveys including SQUEAC surveys allow communities to identify how they can access CMAM services and KAP and SMART surveys can identify communities’ knowledge and adoption of key MIYCN practices. During KAP surveys, separate female and male FGDs will allow for women and men to have a safe space to share information on impacts of Concern’s Nutrition programme on improving nutrition in their communities. In PoCs, while camp managers are responsible for the CRM, Concern implements a CFM for beneficiaries to provide feedback on nutrition interventions. In Guit, Aweil West and Aweil North, Concern has a CRM where beneficiaries can submit feedback and complaints through approaching a Concern staff, in writing and submission in CRM boxes or in NBeG through telephone. Complaints are assessed by a CRM committee that is independent of the programme and decisions on accepting or rejecting the complaint are made by the committee and recommendations to the Programme Managers while sensitive complaints such as protection or fraud cases are referred to the Country Director.
