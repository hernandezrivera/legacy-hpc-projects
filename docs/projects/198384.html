Project Summary

Since the beginning of the year, the number of people displaced has continued to rise in northern Mozambique. According to IOM/DTM Baseline Assessment Round 16, there were an estimated 946,508 people who left their homes (June), up from 784,564 reported in the last assessment (February 2022). In Negomano (Mueda district) there is a transit camp that receives Mozambican families who cross the border with Tanzania that need to increase the water supply highly insufficient.
During 2022 more than 38,244 people returned to their areas of origin in the district of Palma after the city was occupied by the insurgents who damaged the main water infrastructure.
The project aims at strengthening the WASH conditions for IDPs, returnees and hosting communities in Mueda and Palma through: rehabilitation of water infrastructures consisting of an urban water channel and boreholes distributed in the various "bairros" of Palma sede; awareness campaigns on the correct use of hygiene practices andpromoting activities for self-construction of household latrines and garbage pits

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

2.000 returnees households in Palma district and 1750 IDPs in Mueda district affected by the protracted conflict in Cabo Delgado have protected and reliable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene facilities and a safe and healthy environment, contributing to reduce the risk of transmission for WASH related diseases. Brief description of proposed methodologyThe key beneficiaries group of the interventions are households that have been displaced in Mueda as a result of the several attacks that have affected Cabo Delgado and returnees in Palma district. Following an initial assessment to determine specific areas where activities under this project will be implemented, the interventions includes the following outputs and activities

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Output 1: Access to safe water of appropriate quantity and quality (as per WASH cluster standards). Output activities are concentrated in Palma sede which have urgent WASH needs and include increased access to water through: (1) Improving access to water through new water systems or water points rehabilitation, and operation and maintenance of existing infrastructures; (2) support to FIPAG water treatment plant and network and (3) establishment / refreshment of water committees to ensure mechanisms for operation and maintenance of water systems are in place. Output 2: Access to appropriate sanitation and hygiene facilities, including management of solid waste (as per WASH cluster standards). HELPCODE will be in charge of: - the installation, maintenance and decommissioning of emergency latrines / toilets, washing /bathing facilities and handwashing points in public / communal spaces, child-friendly spaces and other critical points ensure frequent practice of handwashing with soap;- promoting activities for self-construction of household latrines and garbage pits (including the provision of sanitation supplies for most vulnerable households), including through training of local artisans in quality slab production. Helpcode identifies professionals to construct quality slabs as well as local artisans to be trained by the identified professionals participate in the construction as aprend;- improved drainage and the collection, removal and disposal of solid waste.- Conducting a gender-based-violence safety audit to identify risks to women, girls, boys, and men related to WASH infrastructure in each site- Vocational training of local artisans (including women) for production of slabs, soap, and maintenance of water points and other WASH related skills. Output 3: Dissemination of information on safe water, sanitation and hygiene to prevent spread of wash related diseases.Programme beneficiaries will benefit from hygiene promotion activities such as community engagement sessions and others that are required and possible, respecting the COVID-19 related mitigation measures and recommendations for SBCC activities. Participatory Health and Hygiene Education (PHHE) will be an integral component of the WASH activities. Community engagement sessions will be spearheaded by hygiene volunteers, who will be sensitised on safe water chain, good water, sanitation and hygiene practices, as well as prevention and control of communicable diseases such as COVID-19 and cholera. Hygiene volunteers engaged will receive token incentives, standardised and agreed by the WASH Cluster. Messages and modalities will be informed by the WASH’Em tools, following the WASH’Em processes.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Helpcode generally implements emergency programs directly, having an updated authorization to operate in Cabo Delgado. Particular attention is paid to building a meaningful, accountable relation with local institutions (District and Provincial Governments, SDPI, SDSMAS, SDEJT, SDAE), including through signing yearly agreements / MoU with which Helpcode supports the local development strategy and contingency measures and that also regulates the involvement of District officers in all stages of an intervention. For emergency interventions, a regular relation has also been established with the National Institute for Disasters Management.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Helpcode AAP’s strategy abides to IASC 5 commitments, according to the following: Leadership - Promote a culture among staff of acceptance of failure and negative feedback from affected people. - Ensure that relevant staff and managers collect feedback from affected populations in appropriate age groups (sensitive to gender & age); respond to this feedback; and report back. - Work with local and national partners, so that there is a coordinated plan and capacity which is sensitive to gender, age, disability and ethnicity. Transparency - Provide accessible and timely information on organizational procedures, structures and processes that may impact communities in order to support informed decisions, and engage communities in a dialogue as part of information provision Participation - Work with relevant local and national partners and actors to support and influence effective design, assessment, implementation and monitoring of the response. - Engage all parts of a community, including those who are vulnerable or at risk to draft robust, contextual vulnerability and capacity analyses. Inclusivity is ensured through a thorough analysis of local social dynamics, mobility capacity of beneficiaries, differences in the use of time by women and men, identification of “role models” among community stakeholders that can promote social change. Feedback and complaints - Provide information to all in affected communities about the organisation, the principles it adheres to, how it expects its staff to behave (including commitments made on PSEA), the programmes it is implementing and what they intend to deliver. - Communicate in languages, formats and media that are easily understood, respectful and culturally appropriate for different members of the community, especially vulnerable and marginalised groups. - Establish and maintain effective feedback systems, using a variety of communication channels that are accessible to all persons of concern and that are appropriate for both sensitive and non-sensitive feedback. The feedback is disaggregated by age and sex at minimum. Design, Monitoring and evaluation - Assess the implementation of the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) and other collective standards including the IASC AAP commitments through assessments of organizational performance on accountability to measure and improve accountability to all persons of concern. - Include persons of concern as partners throughout the operation's management cycle and report the results of assessments and follow up actions to them

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will work with the District Services of infrastructure and based on Sphere Humanitarian Standards in order to go beyond the minimum standards for disability inclusion in humanitarian action Community consultations will be held to inform about the location and segregation of facilities, as well as the identification of specific needs, such as adapted facilities for people living with disabilities, and the provision of adapted facilities to cover these needs.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Among its internal procedure, Helpcode has developed an environmental policy aiming to avoid, minimize and mitigate the environmental impact of its activities in the field, in line with Sphere Humanitarian Standards and global environmental standard, guidelines and tools. The construction of water and sanitation works will be carried out in coordination with local institutional stakeholders such as SDPI and FIPAG, that are responsible for controlling and monitoring the execution of the works in accordance with environmental standards. In construction activities that involve the use of local materials, priority will be given to material supplied by certified traders, avoiding, when conditions permit, inadequate use of informal material available directly in the intervention sites. The construction and disposal of sanitation facilities will be carried out by in compliance with standard hygiene and humanitarian intervention procedures.
