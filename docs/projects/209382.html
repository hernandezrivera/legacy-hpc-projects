Project Summary

This is a complex 6-month intervention that aims to Improve the food security and livelihoods for war-affected populations through various areas in Darfur, Khartoum and Jazeera. 3 months into the conflict, the total estimate of IDPs across Sudan reached 2.6 million. The critical needs of 5,855,848 IDPs and 1,532,350 mixed cross borders movements (Dec 2023, IOM) add onto those of 25M overall identified by OCHA.
Considering the inconceivable suffering of the Sudanese population, TGH have coordinated its efforts to present cash assistance to support the war-affected population in Darfur, Khartoum and Jazeera, as they are the most affected states since the beginning of the conflict in Sudan.
Newly displaced and conflict affected households are unable to meet basic needs for survival. Displacement and conflict result in both the immediate loss of households’ livelihoods and an inability to meet basic needs; it also affects individuals’ ability to regain their livelihood and access income. Economic decline across the country and high food prices resulted in conflict affected households having little cash to cover their food and other needs.
In line with the revised HRP strategic objective 1 (SO1), TGH believes the provision of MPCA is a timely multi-cluster (multi-sectoral) life-saving form of assistance. The use of MPCA as a response modality has been proven across the Horn of Africa to reduce the use of negative coping strategies and contributes to the mitigation of protection risks in support of the revised HRP strategic objective (SO2) on protection.
Multipurpose cash interventions play a crucial role in addressing immediate food needs by enabling beneficiaries to purchase nutritious food items from local markets. Given challenges with in-kind distributions and the setting up of commodity supply routes – partners propose cash and market support as the preferred modality to address food insecurity and access to basic non-food items.
TGH plans to conduct distribution of unconditional food assistance through Cash Based Transfers to contribute to the reduction in mortality and morbidity for food insecure households through unrestricted cash assistance. Unconditional Food Assistance will be targeted at the most affected populations and meant to cover emergency needs on a short-term basis. Where conditions allow, cash will be the preferred method for the delivery of Unconditional Food Assistance based on the rapid needs and market assessments. The program will provide targeted households (HH) with 100% of the required 2,100 kilocalories per person per HH per month for a period of 3 months. This is equivalent to 68% of the Cash Working Groups’ Minimum Expenditure Basket (Local Food Basket) for food related item. As of September 2023, the amount was set at 20,000 SDG per individual per month. TGH will cover 3 months of LFB value during the lean period (June – August 2024). In the HPC sheet, the unit cost has been revised taking into account recent exchange rate (63,8 USD per capita for 3 months + 9% of transfer fees = 69.5 USD) but will be updated as conditions on the ground change and in line with CWG recommendations. TGH will also rely on CWG methodology for beneficiaries’ identification, selection and verification. At inception, consortium partners will sensitize the targeted community by sharing information about the objective and methodology of the action. TGH will adopt community-based targeting, targeting HH identified by the community as most impacted by the conflict, food insecure and who are not receiving any other assistance. Once the beneficiary list is finalized, it will then be presented to the community for validation. Any individuals not considered to meet the criteria will be discussed and replaced with the participation of the community.TGH will conduct verification of beneficiaries to ensure they meet the target criteria.Targeted beneficiaries will receive 3 rounds of assistance for the peak of the lean period.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

TGH’s feedback mechanisms are designed to allow people of all ages, genders and abilities (including marginalized communities) to voice their feedback, suggestions or complaints. The current mechanism allows individuals to voice their feedback in a secured and anonymous manner. Once the complaint is received, it goes through a loop following a standard operating procedure. Feedback is systematically categorized, answered and closed once the beneficiary is satisfied with the answer. The mechanisms aim to guarantee the availability of appropriate communication channels between the beneficiaries and the project team, while ensuring effective handling of complaints. This reinforces understanding of and trust towards the proposed mechanisms.Depending on the context (e.g. availability of phone coverage) and to accommodate beneficiaries’ preferences and abilities (e.g. literacy skills), the CFRM mechanism in each location consists of one or more of the following channels:Regular consultations with community focal points / community committees by project teams, including on the best way to communicate with them - responses and ideas will help tailor the CFRM to this specific action.Suggestion boxes: will be available in sites where TGH & PUI intervene e.g. health/WASH/youth facilities. Project teams will explain their function. Feedback forms will be available by the box, can be left anonymous and will be collected once a week by the project team.Surveys and group discussions: will be organised jointly by the MEAL and the project team during and after the implementation of activities to monitor whether the assistance was delivered in a safe, accessible, accountable and participatory manner. This feedback channel is punctual and allows TGH & PUI to cross check information received through other channels.TGH team oral feedback: TGH teams are regularly present in the field and collect oral feedback from beneficiaries. Involvement of the affected populations ensures that the emergency response is tailored to actual and most urgent needs. Partners will engage with the community at all stages of the project cycle, including the needs assessment, project implementation and any adaptation or adjustment needed based on lessons learned. Participation is ensured mainly through 1) regular discussions with different stakeholders (community leaders, beneficiaries of the action, community members, etc.), 2) a comprehensive Complaint Feedback & Response Mechanism (CFRM); and 3) the involvement of the community in the implementation of the activities, either through their recruitment in the project team as volunteers or through community-based approaches. The CFRM will enable partners to collect beneficiaries’ feedback and complaints and respond to them. The MEAL teams will increase the opportunities for monitoring field visits, interviews and have Focus Group Discussions with beneficiaries to ensure that all of them are given the opportunity to voice their feedback, concerns or suggestions. The involvement of beneficiaries enables Partners to design, and continuously adjust, the project to adapt it to the context and to beneficiaries’ choices.The involvement of the affected population remains at the core of this action to ensure that the emergency response is tailored to actual and most urgent needs. The involvement of local partners, including neighborhood and community committees supports this purpose.
