Project Summary

The ongoing conflict is exacerbated education crisis as it was suspended for 6.5 million children have no access to education services. Attacks on schools and their use as emergency shelters further compromise the safety of education spaces and put enormous pressure on the already fragile education system for the host communities as internally displaced children.
Displacement has overwhelmed host communities, straining their fragile education systems and limited services. Access barriers remain significant, with an additional 6.9 million children out-of-school including children with disabilities, many of them living in vulnerable communities. Inadequate access to clean water and sanitation in schools remains a significant barrier to accessing education with many schools lacking functional facilities, running water, and soap supplies. Nearly 55 per cent of schools lack access to safe water and 49 per cent lack access to improved sanitation, with only 10 per cent with access to handwashing facilities. The crisis caused significant psychological distress among children, potentially impacting their long-term mental health and education progress if not urgently addressed. As well put more Gender-based barriers that further limit girls' access to education.
The project aims to ensure that 21,001 crisis-affected vulnerable children (aged 6-18) (10711 of them are girls and 2100 children with disabilities) have access to improved equitable quality education in safe and protective learning environment. This response targets children at risk of dropping out due to the crisis and ensuring retention for displaced and non-displaced children, particularly those from disadvantaged backgrounds and in hard-to-reach areas; in safer states and safe pockets within conflict affected states and children in conflict areas once safety and access permit, in 14 localities of 8 states:
- ND: 5194 IDPs and vulnerable children (2649 girls and 2545boys) in primary schools; - SK: 1850 IDPs and vulnerable children (944 girls and 906 boys) of primary education; WN: 4103 IDPs and vulnerable children (2093 girls and 2010 boys) of primary education; NK: 2409 IDPs & vulnerable children (1229 girls and 1180 boys) of basic education; Kassala: 4237DPs and vulnerable children (2161 girls and 2076 boys) in basic schools; Gedaref: 1476 IDPs and vulnerable children (753 girls and 723 boys) in basic schools and, Aj Jazirah: 1476 IDPs internally displaced and vulnerable children (753 girls and 723 boys).
The response will improve g access to education in safe, protective and inclusive learning environment for crisis-affected vulnerable children, through improving facilities in learning environments by establishing of TLSs, rehabilitation of classrooms and improved gender sensitive and inclusive WASH facilities. Adolescent girls will be supported by providing them with dignity kits and Menstrual Hygiene Management. The learning environments will be safer and more protective for vulnerable children by providing psychosocial support (PSS) for children, providing recreational materials and establishing children clubs. This is beside capacity building of teachers to prevent and respond to child protection and safety in learning environment by training them on child protection, provision of psychosocial support, and prevention and response to GBV, positive discipline, and building resilience. Target OOSC will be support to enroll in ALP. Moreover, improve quality education through supporting teachers’ training on learner’s centered pedagogy and provide them with teaching materials and children with necessary learning supplies. As well teacher’s wellbeing in target learning spaces will be ensured through providing them with emergency incentives.
The project will strengthen the capacity of the education system, partners, and communities to deliver a timely and coordinated education response through training staff of states’ MoE and education partners.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Plan International maintains high standards of Safeguarding and PSHEA (preventing sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse), more-so in emergency situations. Plan’s Safeguarding Policy and procedures are implemented during the design and implementation of project interventions with additional vigilance and we adapt and strengthen existing interventions to identify and reduce risks to girls, boys, children, young people and beneficiaries. Safeguarding and PSHEA risk analysis and assessment of project interventions will be conducted with mitigation measures/contingency plans identified for each risk. Plan will work to ensure staff, partners, and beneficiaries and communities are aware of the safeguarding policy, its minimum codes of behavior, and requirements to uphold high standards of professional behavior. The implementing partners were assessed to ensure the adequacy of the partner’s safeguarding and PSHEA policies and guidelines. Mitigation strategies take account of particular risks or vulnerabilities associated with girls, boys, children and young people. Children and young people are respected and their safety and protection is not compromised when obtaining and using information on and images of them.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Plan International will ensure accountability to affected people, as well as the donor, local partner, relevant government stakeholders. The project will involve the target community members, where appropriate working through existing community structures whilst ensuring representation of women, youth, people with disabilities, elderly and the related CBOs, in the project cycle management. Target groups and other related project stakeholders will be fully oriented on the project objectives and deliverables using safe methods and forums such messages to ensure that, a sense of ownership is created among target communities and increased accountability towards the beneficiaries targeted by the project. Plan International Sudan has a child friendly Community Feedback & Response Mechanism (CFRM) system rolled out at state level in all its locations. This CFRM are in line with the Core Humanitarian Standards and will enable community members to provide their feedback, concerns and reect their opinions on the project performance in a safe and confidential manner. The accountability systems have multiple entry points, designed to be accessible for young people, women, PWDs, and other vulnerable groups through introduction of different approaches in collaboration with the communities. Entry points will include hotline/Sim numbers, WhatsApp and boxes as safe entry points to allow target beneficiaries to share their feedback/complaints. Beneficiaries are oriented and encouraged to use the system, which established in each target community. The received feedbacks/complaints are analyzed and addressed following the internal procedures that are aligned to the Common Humanitarian standards. Feedback/complaints concerning other project stakeholders (government staff, partners) will be referred to their senior management as appropriate where concerned organization can take the necessary actions. Plan will also seek through safe methods through community volunteers and CBOs members to allow children to reect their opinions and feedback about the project as well as using logbooks at schools that target beneficiaries can register feedbacks. Externally Plan is a member of the AAP/PSEA network and mechanisms and interacts with UNCT/UNOPS call centers and AAP focal points in other organizations that is set up by the humanitarian actors in Sudan. Plan will contribute to enhancing technical standards and improving AAP and PSEA mechanisms and approaches based on lessons learned and experience from Plan globally, has strong internal Safeguarding policies, standards, procedures as well as robust training and capacity building manuals that can enhance AAP and PSEA roll out, reporting and responding. Plan will also ensure to participate in needs, information and assessments on AAP and PSEA as initiated by the AAP network as appropriate. Plan will commit to adhere to implement the AAP and PSEA in its design and delivery of projects and reporting requirements.
