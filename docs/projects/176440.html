Project Summary

The provision of safe drinking water helps to improve quality of life, as well as dignity of those served.
Safe and quality drinking water is essential element of life, water is protected by international law. It is the right of all human’s entitlement to sufficient, safe, acceptable and affordable water.
Access to safe and quality water is indicated as one of the most efficient instruments in promoting health.
In South Sudan. Only 36 percent of the household reported having access to an improved water source in under 30 minutes without facing any protection concerns. The remaining 64 per cent of the people across the country either rely on unimproved or surface water source, (41 per cent), takes more than 30 munities to reach an improved water source (19 per cent) are able to reach an improved water source in less than 30 munities but face protection concerns while accessing the water source.
In upper Nile 66 per cent, most of the proportion of households rely on surface water, followed by Jonglei with 43 per cent and Central Equatorial with 41 per cent, with 17 per cent of the people unable to collect enough water for drinking, and 39 per cent of the people reporting insufficient access to water for handwashing.
Water, sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) remains a major public health burden, in the absence of safe and quality water exhibits increase in diarrhea cases deaths , The cases of AWD for year 2014 are 194,844 cases including 11 deaths and for 2020 are 140,856 cases including 26 deaths across the Country , a 60 per cent increase compared to 2019, it is worth to note that even due in the increase there were no reported confirmed cholera case.
In early 2020, the Global Acute malnutrition prevalence (GAM) was high (16.2%) above the WHO recommended threshold of 15%.
An estimated 1.4 million children are currently facing malnutrition, 313,319 of them are severely malnourished.
A growing body of evidence indicates that access to safe drinking- water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services has important positive impact on Nutrition.

The world Health Organization (WHO) estimates that 50% of malnutrition is associated with repeated diarrhea or intestinal worm infection as result of unsafe water, inadequate sanitation or insufficient hygiene.

The World Health Organization, WASH water quality surveillance program aims to address the water quality technical issues faced by partners by enhancing water quality, testing, treatment surveillance. To provide timely data on water sources which are found to be bacteriologically contaminated, provide technical guidance to WASH cluster partners to enable them to implement the remedial action required to enhance equitable access to safe drinking, for all.
The WHO project for water quality surveillance will contribute the cluster collective outcome 12.1.1 and 12.1.3.

1 - Needs

Recurrent conflicts in South Sudan and lack of durable peace since December 2013 has led to social-economic marginalization, deprivation and lack of basic services. In 2020, the country relapsed into the triple shock of intensified conflict and sub-national violence in selected hotspots, a second consecutive year of major flooding and the impacts of COVID-19 pandemic leaving an estimated 8.5 million people in urgent need of humanitarian assistance (HNO 2021). An estimated 1.6 million people are still internally displaced and another 2.2 million are refugees and asylum seekers in the region. The country also hosts estimated 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers from Sudan, Democratic Republic of Congo and Ethiopia.Only 36 per cent of households reported having access to an improved water source in under 30 minutes without facing any protection concerns. The remaining 64 per cent of people across the country either rely on unimproved or surface water sources (41 per cent), take more than 30 minutes to reach an improved water source (19 per cent), are able to reach an improved source in less than 30 minutes but face protection concerns while accessing the water (4 per cent). The highest proportion of households relying on surface water were found in Upper Nile, with 66 per cent, followed by Jonglei with 43 per cent and Central Equatoria with 41 per cent. The quantity of water available per day is also below standards in many locations, with 17 per cent of people unable to collect enough water for drinking, and 39 per cent of people reporting insufficient access to water for handwashing. The cases of AWD for year 2014 are 194,844 cases including 11 deaths and for 2020 are 140,856 cases including 26 deaths across the Country.The country is currently experiencing one of its worst Food insecurity situations. An estimated 5.8 million people would be in emergency Phase 4 and 5 Food security situations through December 2020 to March 2021, this number will be approximately 7.2 million between April to July 2021 (IPC 2021 report). Consequently, an estimated 1.4 million children aged between 6 months and 5 years are currently facing malnutrition, 313,319 of them are severely malnourished. An estimated 483,383 pregnant and lactating women are also acutely malnourished. As a result, Global Acute Malnutrition in South Sudan remains persistently high, the FSNMS 2019 report recorded GAM rates among children under-five at 16.2 percent above the minimum threshold of 15%. An estimated 1.8 million people most of them from Warrap, Jonglei, Lakes, Unity and Upper Nile states and Greater Pibor Administrative area would be in emergency Phase 4 and 5 Food security situations through December 2020 to March 2021, this number will be approximately 2.5 million between April to July 2021 (IPC 2021 . Consequently, an estimated 1.4 million children are currently facing malnutrition, 313,319 of them are severely malnourished. An estimated 483,383 pregnant and lactating women are also acutely malnourished. As a result, Global Acute Malnutrition in South Sudan remains persistently high, the FSNMS 2019 report recorded GAM rates among children under-five at 16.2 percent above the minimum threshold of 15%. Major causes of malnutrition in the population range from high prevalence of diseases (up to 36%), poor quality and diversity of food, deficiency of safe and quality drinking water, sanitation and hygiene services, poor access to health and nutrition services, and COVID-19 pandemic.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Feedback from the most vulnerable members of the community will be used to support mainstreaming of vulnerable and at-risk groups needs into regular programming and advocate for inclusive programming that takes care of the needs It will also be used to increase representation and participation of at risk and vulnerable groups through community meetings, direct participation of at-risk groups in the day today implementation and monitoring of project activities Where protection risk has been identified in the feedback from affected people, WHO and WASH cluster will use the information to engage the protection cluster to increase protection assistance and safeguards for beneficiaries of WASH services. This can be in terms of walking distance to the water point, or queuing time at the water point to fetch water. Finally, feedback from vulnerable members of the communities will be used to strengthen enhancement of WASH service delivery. WHO will engage cluster partners for remedial actions.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

• How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism? WHO has a strong framework for humanitarian accountability given its Cluster leadership on Public Health and its role as the Health sector lead in development setting. As such the project will adopt context specific approaches to implement accountability to affected people and provide beneficiaries information about the project while ensuring the functionality of complains and feedback mechanism with the view of making sure affected people at all levels are consulted, informed, are participating and given timely feedback. Gender and age structured Focused Group Discussions (FGD) will be used to ensure that children, adults as well as male and female gender are able to inform the choices the project will take Project inception meetings will be used to provide information to WASH stakeholders, community leaders and selected beneficiaries. Important information such as the project purpose, target group and criteria, project benefits, donors and how people will participate will be provided during the inception meetings. Meanwhile community meetings will also be used to disseminate project information as well as receive and respond to community feedback/complains

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

WHO will analyze beneficiary feedback with the view of understanding best practices and things that are not working, Consequently, WHO will reintegrate recommended best practices to increase support for affected people. Where feedback exposes gaps in the water quality surveillance programing, WHO will design and implement remedial actions in line with needs expressed by the beneficiaries including provision of clarifications in the case of limited information. Sample remedial actions could be increased participation of affected people, increased engagement of community leaders to create a two-way communication with local partners, community and opinion leaders and beneficiaries. In some cases, beneficiaries’ feedback will be used to strengthen the WASH cluster mechanisms to ensure affected people have access adequate WASH services that are not provided through the project. For instance, lobbying with other clusters to provide support to gaps identified which are not water quality and WASH related.
