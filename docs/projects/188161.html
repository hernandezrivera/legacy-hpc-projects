Project Summary

The Syrian crisis has left many with little or no access to education, especially at Deir Ez-Zor governorate. The absence of quality education led many children to leave schools at an early age. Thus, these children might be affected by child labor, early or forced marriage, domestic violence, and abuse. These coping mechanisms are widely reported across Deir Ez-Zor Governorate both in local communities and IDPs informal settlement, representing protection risks for children, and a serious barrier to access education.
The project aims at providing ALP curriculum to out of school children and pupils that are studying in a level behind their age group. The project will increase the chances of 1500 vulnerable children, in three schools within Sur sub-district, to be re-admitted to the educational system and accordingly prevent protection risks related to education drop out.
The project will work towards meeting two objectives of the Education cluster. In fact, the rehabilitation of the three schools, procurement of teaching materials and the provision of ALP curriculum to out of school children as well as students that are behind their age groups, will ensure better access to Education. Furthermore, the teacher’s training and inclusion of coaches in the three schools will ensure the provision of quality education for the targeted children.
After completing the ALP cycles, children will be re-admitted to the correct level of Education and become able to continue their courses in formal schools. AFH will target 1500 children in the first year (2022) equally divided in two educational cycles at three schools while in 2023 the number of beneficiaries will be 2250.

4 - Needs

The Syrian crisis has left many with little or no access to education. The situation is worse in Deir Ezzor governorate due to the full or partial destruction of educational facilities, lack of qualified teachers and weak capacities, and the very limited resources of the local Education committee.Just after the security situation in Deir-Ez-Zor relatively improved, the Education Committee focused its efforts on rehabilitating schools and re-admitting children into the educational system without taking consideration of their academic levels or age groups. Furthermore, the education quality provided was very mediocre due to the lack of qualified teachers in the area.These factors have resulted on many children dropping out school as a result to the poor educational quality or the fact that they are studying with much younger age groups which often creates frustration among these children.Consequently, children being out of schools are more exposed to negative coping mechanisms and protection risks such as child labor, early or forced marriage, neglect, and abuse.AFH is proposing this intervention out of its belief that the right place for children is school and that no child must be deprived out of this universal right regardless of the causes.

5 - Implementation of activities

AFH will start by conducting rehabilitation activities in the targeted facilities to make the learning space safer and more appealing to children. Furthermore, AFH will provide equipment, stationeries and teaching materials that are required for the execution of the education activities. The organization will as well recruit teachers and make sure that all of them receive essential training at the beginning of the project in accordance with INEE international standards. Therefore, registration of students will begin with the development of defined criteria for selecting the most vulnerable children. Following that, all registered students will have diagnostic tests to determine their level. After that, AFH intends to provide two cycles of educational activities in 2022, each lasting four months. Further to that, AFH will provide structured Psychosocial sessions to children that are showing signs of depressions, aggressive temper, constant fear, have been bullied or subjected to abuses. this will reinforce the impact of the intervention and ensure that children won't drop out of the courses.Finally, and after the conclusion of each cycle, AFH will follow all graduates to ensure they resume their formal education.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

the main protection risks that might face the implementation of the project are the following:1- Child abuse or mistreatment by teachers or educational staff. In this regard, AFH will make sure to train all teachers and educational staff on safeguarding and child protection. AFH will make sure that all teachers and project staff will sign the Organization's Code of Conduct. Furthermore, AFH will make sure that all project stakeholders are aware of the Complaint and Feedback mechanisms to denounce any mistreatment or behavior that might violate the code of conduct and the children's safeguardingChild Safeguarding will cover all people who may interact with children. These could be organization staff, teachers, other school personnel, school bus drivers, contractors, vendors etc..2- children being victim to ERW or explosives on their way to school. in this regard, AFH will provide sessions related to raise the awareness of children regarding explosives and remnants of war through Mine Risk Education sessions. 3- Acts of sexual harassment conducted by boys on young girls in the latrines or unsafe places. AFH will ensure to rehabilitate the sanitation facilities in a way that provides privacy and gender segregation to protect girls from any SG or Harassment's risk.4- Services not matching needs of people with disabilities. in this regard, AFH will make sure that all the facilities are PWD friendly including classrooms and sanitation facilities.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

AFH has available policies on Child Safeguarding and PSEA. All staff that join AFH are properly inducted on the Organization’s Code of Conduct, PSEA and CSG policies. After the induction all staff must sign the documents. AFH has strong Complaint and feedback mechanism in place as well as a whistle blowing policy to denounce any misconduct or SEA acts. MEAL department ensures to share leaflets, banners to explain the feedback and complaint mechanism of the organization containing different channels of communication. AFH has zero tolerance towards any act of SEA or child protection violation.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

the main risks that might affect the execution of the project are the following:1- Parent resistance to children education and especially girls as a result of negative coping mechanism such as child labor for economic reasons or forced and early marriage as a cultural and traditional reason. In this regard, AFH team will conduct several awareness raising sessions to caregivers about the importance of education to their children. Furthermore, the team will engage the community leaders and the elderly in advocating for children's access to education2- the second risk is the lack of qualified teachers in the area. this risk can be mitigated throughout the capacity building program that would be integrated in the project so that recruited teachers will undergo a series of technical trainings that will equip them with the necessary skills to provide quality education to children3- possible theft of schools' assets and equipment. in order to avoid this risk, AFH will hire two security guard to ensure the protection of the school. Furthermore, the team will engage the community leaders and the well respected individual in the area in the project and increase their sense of ownership so they can advocate and exercise their moral power on the others which will result in the protection of the schools.
