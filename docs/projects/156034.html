Project Summary

Concern Worldwide will support the BAHAR RELIEF ORGANIZATION and SANAD to provide non-formal education in five NFE centres for 2,325 out-of-school children in two towns, Afrin and Jandairis, in the Afrin District of the Northern Aleppo Governorate. SANAD will target 300 children affected by disability in the towns of Jarabulus and Al Bab. Five safe and accessible learning spaces will be set up. 315 teachers will receive training in classroom management, positive discipline, and the integration of PSS etc. Six BTL campaigns will be conducted with the aim of reaching some 4,200 individuals. The overall aim of the action is to increase the attendance/enrolment rates of out-of-school children in formal education.

Needs

The ongoing conflict in Syria has had an outsized impact on children and adolescents. Barriers to accessing education are particularly visible in the Aleppo Governorate, where there is a large population of IDPs and returnees. According to the August 2018 REACH Situation Overview for Idlib and the rural areas of Idlib, Aleppo and Hama, 77% of communities surveyed reported barriers to accessing education. This area also has the second-highest dropout rate (39%) in Syria. According to the education offices of the Afrin and Jandairis city councils, Afrin, a city with a population of 100,000 (40,000 IDPs), has around 40,000 school-aged children and a dropout rate of 30%. Jandairis, to the southwest of Afrin with a population of 30,000 (10,000 IDPs), has 13,000 school-aged children and a dropout rate of 35%. The top three reasons adduced are a lack of income, child labour and lack of educational materials, books and stationery (REACH 2018). Across Syria, teachers, administrators and schools do not have the capacity to provide a quality education for students with disabilities. Overall, only 26% of schools accept students with special needs and disabilities. Across the Aleppo Governorate, 21% of schools accept or enrol children with special needs and disabilities (ACU 2018). In Al Bab, Aleppo, for example, a city of 100,000 between residents and IDPs, 1,353 people are affected by disability. Of these, 427 (41% females, 59% males) are younger than 18, and 319 (45% females, 55% males) are of school age (Al Bab City Council Census 2018). Throughout Syria, of the students with special needs and disabilities, 47% have a motor disability, 31% have a mental disorder and 21% have lost one or more senses. Though 26% of schools accept students with disabilities, there is a dearth of specialists to support the learning of these students—only 1% of schools have any kind of specialist to provide or assist with special education (ACU 2018). Per the 2018 HRP, other push factors that cause children to drop out of school are the harassment, bullying and violence that they experience in and out of the classroom. Teachers and students alike suffer from the effects of trauma, war-related stress and psychosocial disorders. Despite this, only 14% of schools have a psychological counsellor on staff (15% in Aleppo, 10% in Idlib), and only 21% of schools have teachers who are trained in the provision of PSS for children (13% in Aleppo, 27% in Idlib) (ACU 2018). Across Syria, though the vast majority of students have been exposed to some kind of psychological trauma, only 14% of schools have implemented PSS programming or services (theatre, art, motivational competitions, etc.) in schools (14% Aleppo, 22% Idlib). Poor psychosocial health and wellbeing manifests in many different ways. Reporting on negative phenomena affecting students, teachers communicated that the most common were poor marks (17%), difficulty concentrating (14%), difficulty memorizing (12%) and anxiety or tension (9%). Teachers also described witnessing poor personal hygiene, language problems, agitation, bullying, depression, loneliness and loss of appetite among their students (ACU 2018). Per the Whole of Syria Severity Scale published in the 2018 HNO, proposed areas of operation in Aleppo Governorate (Jarablus, Al Bab, Afrin and Jandairis) are classified as a 4 (severe problem), with some villages of Al Bab classified as a 5 (critical problem). Implementation of activities

Concern's Global Education Strategy is closely aligned with the INEE Minimum standards (Access, Quality and Wellbeing) and the objectives outlined in the ECHO HIP 2019. Provision of non-formal education to out of schoolchildren: Concern partners will provide non-formal education to 2,325 of the most vulnerable children (ages 6-18), both IDPs and host community, in Al Bab, Jarablus and Afrin, with the aim of reintegrating children into formal education opportunities. SANAD will specifically target out-of-school children with disabilities through two centres in Jarablus and Al Bab, while Bahar will reach the most vulnerable out-of-school children (including those with disabilities) with three centres in Afrin. NFE classes will focus on basic literacy and numeracy as well as other subjects including sports, art and IT skills. In collaboration with partners, Concern will develop a curriculum that utilizes Concern Turkey’s NFE curriculum, IRC’s Safe and Healing Learning Spaces (SHLS) curriculum.The NFE approach will prioritize play-based learning and integrated PSS and SEL. NFE activities will be held five days per week with the aim of providing participating children with the skills and confidence to enrol in formal education opportunities at the start of the following school year. In Afrin, Bahar will operate three NFE centres and implement three cycles at each centre over a 12-month period, while SANAD will implement two 5-month cycles at two centres. SANAD has elected to implement 2 x 5 month cycles because they are exclusively targeting children affected by disability according to their organizational mandate. When children enrol in the programme they will be directly referred to formal education opportunities and supported to register in the formal education system. Provision of protection services to children participating in NFE centres: Each partner will have a specialized Protection team to provide the necessary protection services for children participating in the NFE programme. These services will include outreach, Psychological First Aid (PFA), individual PSS, and counselling. Children can be referred from the NFE programme to the protection services provided by each partner organization.Teacher professional development: Concern partners will provide 315 school administrators and teachers participating in the project with professional development comprised of pre-service and in-service training, ongoing mentoring and teacher learning circles. Community outreach and advocacy: Awareness raising topics will be conducted through six BTL campaigns with the aim of reaching some 4,200 individuals to include formal and informal education opportunities in each district, back-to-school campaigns and the rights and needs of children to go to school. Participants for the NFE programme will be identified through outreach. In addition, SANAD will specifically focus on awareness raising with caregivers, community members and school administrators about the inclusion of children with disabilities in the formal school system.Caregiver Engagement Course: Each partner will implement a 12-week caregiver engagement course reaching 280 family members and caregivers. The aim will be to better equip parents and caregivers to support their children’s wellbeing, development and school readiness, a two-hour weekly session will be held at the NFE centres over a period of 12 weeks. Topics will include age-appropriate play, positive parenting, emotion regulation, child protection risks, the psychological impact of conflict and displacement on the wellbeing and learning of children.Five safe and accessible learning spaces will be set up. Bahar will set up three learning spaces in existing school buildings (two in Afrin and one in Jandairis). SANAD will set up two learning spaces in rented buildings (One in Al Bab and one in Jarablus)

Protection Risk Analysis

• Security risks to partner staff from BAHAR and SANAD - BAHAR, and SANAD will maintain their neutrality in spite of the pressures arising as a result of the conflict inside Syria. Concern will support the partners to strengthen their security management plans and to develop an appropriate duty of care policy to provide support to their teams and their family members in Syria in the event of a sudden change of control in the project areas, displacement, death or permanent injury. • There is a risk that the authorities will not remain committed to providing an enabling policy and institutional framework for education and that they will not continue implementation. Partners will continue to work closely with community leaders, UNICEF, humanitarian and development actors regarding how authorities can further assist to ensure successful implementation and sustainability of the education process. Agreements with the authorities regarding roles and responsibilities will be developed. Partners will meet the local authority officials on a regular basis and update them on the progress of activity implementation. Partners will involve the local authorities in implementation and different training courses. BAHAR, SANAD and Concern will individually represent the activities in this project towards the Turkish authorities responsible for cross-border activities in Northern Aleppo. Concern will support partner staff to provide training to their teams inside Syria on the OCHA-led joint operating principles (JOPs) to ensure that the individual staff and teams implementing this project are able to maintain their neutrality from the parties to the conflict. • Concern will work with the partners to develop a capacity-building plan and to support the partners in their organizational development in order to be able to maintain their organizational structure in Turkey and in Syria. Concern will also help the partners to apply for funding directly from other donors where appropriate in order that they might be less dependent on Concern. • Concern will support the partners to further develop their staff conduct and safeguarding policies and to roll out this policies and appropriate training to all programme staff in Turkey and Syria. Particular attention will be paid to the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). Concern will also support partners to strengthen their Community Response Complaints Mechanism (CRM) and to increase the awareness of the CRM among programme participants. Partner staff will also be made aware of possible risks, and their obligations to staff and beneficiaries through the initial CLOFAT and CLOSAT selection reviews.• Protection risks of mixing different age groups in the same centre/class. Partners will look to create separate times of attendance for each age group.• Resistance to gender mainstreaming from male leadership structures. – Partners will look ti ensure that projects will benefit all members of the community and will always seek prior agreement and the active involvement of traditional leadership structures.• The host community rejects the presence of partners or the Project because the host communities feel unequally treated or threatened/stigmatized disapprove of activities implemented or other reasons. The Partners have already built relationships with local leaders, communities and authorities. They will endeavour to keep nurturing these relationships through regular information and support that is sensitive to the structure of incentives in order to mitigate this risk.• Disagreements between organizations and local education authorities on data collected, data results, validation, etc. Possible physical l risk to aid workers during data collection. Partners will operate according to Joint Operating Principles (JOPs), including do-no-harm between humanitarian community and local authorities. Timely and accurate security analysis, and security training will serve to mitigate physical risks.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The fluidity and volatility of the security situation in Syria, particularly in relation to Manbij (where an assault on the city and a massive outflow of refugees are possible) are beyond the project's control, but were the fighting to escalate and encroach on Concern's partners' areas of operation, partners will implement their own security management plans. As partners will be operating in areas of high tension, they will maintain a low operational profile at all times where possible to minimize attendant risks and will work to maintain good relations with local authorities and organizations where possible. Day-to-day changes will also be made to working conditions and operations depending on tensions and security reports.Changes in the security situation, particularly in project target areas, affects the safety/mobility/ displacement of beneficiaries/staff and results in their being unable to participate in project activities This includes the risk of targeted attacks and bombing of schools/communities and the risks of mines, airstrikes and cross-fire. Child safety incidents in school due to bullying and overburdened teachers are also possible, while their vulnerability on the journey to school must also be accounted for. Mitigating measures in relation to these particular risks include close monitoring of the security situation in coordination with partners and relevant stakeholders, and the regular conducting of security assessments. This will ensure that staff members and teachers are trained in safety and security best practices, including mine-risk education. If and when the local security situation deteriorates, staff will look to immediately inform the internal emergency focal point about the situation; work to coordinate with partners and clusters and hibernate/suspend operations if necessary. They will also consider the possibilities of adopting different modalities to implement the project. In the event that such security threats did materialise, all, important documents will be digitised and saved to remote servers. Decisions will be taken in advance whether hard copies should be destroyed or not. Communication with donors will also be critical in assessing whether support is required in relation to the relocation of activities to safer spaces whenever needed. Alternative locations (community centres, homes, etc…) and methodologies of education should be considered to avoid attack-related risks.With regard to the transportation of children to school, the training of partners and local teams on child safety; regular security analyses and the development of schools-specific child-safety policies; the establishment of complaint mechanisms in school and the establishment of ECCE within the neighbourhoods, whereby children will be dropped to the centre by their parents will serve to minimise these risks.
