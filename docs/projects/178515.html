Project Summary

4 - Needs

Armed conflict, economic deterioration, displacement and the COVID-19 crisis all have a gendered and disproportionate impact on girls and women in Syria. This is manifested primarily through different forms of gender based violence (GBV), especially early/forced marriage, intimate partner violence, sexual harassment and sexual violence (including rape), denial of resources, emotional/psychological violence and physical abuse, in all walks of life. While UNFPA's GBV interventions reached around 1,000 communities across Syria (2020 4Ws), this is still only 12% of all communities. Economic deprivation, lack of livelihood opportunities, discrimination, exclusion, and socio-cultural norms have led to the continued normalization of GBV and severely impacted the psychosocial wellbeing and continued erosion of women’s and girls’ rights, including those with disabilities. The COVID-19 pandemic compounded this adversity. Restrictions to contain the disease severely affected employment, income, education, availability of and access to services, including specialized GBV services, limited access to basic hygiene and other essential supplies, and freedom of movement. These worsening conditions were linked to an increase in GBV within the home, including intimate partner violence and psychological violence against women and children. Additionally, sudden displacements and shifting lines of control continue to expose women and girls to heightened risk of GBV and to the likely need of emergency interventions. Where GBV services exist, women and girls conﬁrmed that they have a positive impact on their lives. Women and Girls Safe Spaces and Community Well being Centers are places to seek safety and receive life-saving services.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project aims to increase availability of and access to specialized GBV services across Syria through static facilities, mobile teams and remote services. UNFPA will also continue to increase its support and services, including through cash and voucher assistance and dignity kits distributions, to female headed households, adolescent girls, widows, divorcees and women and girls with disabilities (identiﬁed as most at risk of GBV in the HNO) and focus on empowering them, from a socio-economic perspective, through women and girls safe spaces (WGSS), community wellbeing centers (CWC), safe spaces within health facilities, integrated RH/GBV outreach services and remote/on-line services. UNFPA will continue to lead the GBV coordination and enhance the quality of the GBV response through building the capacity of members, development of IA technical guidance, strengthening referral pathways and SOPs. 1.1 / 1.2 Provide lifesaving and quality GBV response services including case management, psychosocial support and reinforce referral pathways throughout Syria. This will translate in an anticipated 806 community reached with GBV response services and 379,450 GBV response services provided. 1.3 The capacity of 1,220 service providers will be built to ensure quality GBV services 2.1 UNFPA will engage in strategies to empower women and girls and prevent GBV, with a focus on changing negative attitudes that foster harmful social norms and implementing the WoS adolescent girls strategy. UNFPA aims to reach 1,277,000 beneﬁciaries (510,800 women, 383,100 men, 255,400 girls and 127,700 boys) and to conduct 150,600 COVID-19 awareness raising interventions. 3.1 UNFPA will train up to 1,020 humanitarian actors in GBV risk mitigation.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. If GBV services are not conﬁdential, they may put survivors at further risk of stigmatization or even death. 2. If services are not survivor centered, this will have a negative impact on help-seeking behavior with dramatic consequences on survivor's health. 3. Communities may be resistant to engaging around topics of GBV and ultimately prevent women and girls from attending. To mitigate these risks, UNFPA carries out thorough risks analysis of its interventions and works with communities to reduce risks of GBV. UNFPA strives to ensure that all services are conﬁdential and survivor centered by building the capacity of its partners, but also to make sure that there are no negative repercussions in accessing them. In addition, UNFPA continuously conducts barriers to access of services assessments at the WGSS/CWCs in order to mitigate risks associated with services provided. Women and girls are at the centre of the decision making at every phase of the project implementation. UNFPA organizes and facilitates trainings for a variety of sectors to ensure that actions to prevent and mitigate risks of GBV are included in their responses. UNFPA has established strong M&E systems. In addition to UNFPA’s regular M&E standard reporting practices such as onsite visits, monthly and quarterly partner meetings, UNFPA conducts quarterly assessments using client feedback forms at health facilities and WGSSs, key information interviews with service providers, and FGDs at WGSSs and an annual impact assessment. Third party monitors are used to verify program quality and implementation (M&E framework available upon request)

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Given that the project targets women and girls in particular, which are those considered most at risk of SEA, the risk is considered high. Specific measures are in place to mitigate this risk such as IP careful assessments, regular capacity development, mandatory work plan shared in advance of the implementations and regularly tracked, availability of hotlines and feedback mechanisms for beneficiaries as requirements for the selection of IPs

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Access, permissions and bureaucratic hurdles may slow the implementation however, UNFPA actively works with its national partners to over come these challenges. Furthermore the possible non renewal of the SCR will have a tragic impact on women and girls’ life, not being able to access life-saving services anymore. Security issues and shifting lines of control will lead to a hampered access or even material damage. This is mitigated by a detailed contingency plan for every segment of the UNFPA intervention. Assumption that communities will continue to engage in preventing GBV. While GBV remains a diﬃcult subject in many communities, this can be overcome by actively engaging with communities. Another risk associated with the project is mainstreaming fatigue as various projects aim to mainstream GBV, gender, disability and other protection issues. The GBV AoR assumes that this fatigue will occur and has prepared to provide tools, checklists and technical support (Skype calls, email, meetings) to facilitate technical support in developing and implementing mitigating measures across sectors. The GBV AoR, as a mitigating measure, will work with colleagues addressing gender, disability and protection risks in order to synthesize and streamline processes e.g. conduct a FGD that addresses the GBV risks associated with person(s) with disabilities or pilot an indicator that addresses GBV and disability for women, men, boys and girls.
