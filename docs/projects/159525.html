Project Summary

The project aims to fill critical gaps in sexual reproductive health services delivery for
conflict affected population and enhance provision of essential quality SR health services
according to the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) for RH standards. The project
will target sexual reproductive health needs of women, and men and increase access to
quality maternal and newborn care, HIV and STI prevention and treatment, comprehensive
services provision to survivors of GBV and SGBV/rape and improve integration of SRH
service into primary health care level.

1 - Needs

As confirmed by the 2019-20 HRP, 1.3 million people in Eastern Ukraine are in need of essential healthcare and disease prevention, out of which 0.6 mln “along the Contact Line”. Access to essential healthcare, including reproductive health services is limited by the compromised travelling ability of the majority of the affected population in the areas close to the contact line due to the security reasons, unavailability of the public transportation and very often unaffordability, and the breaking down of referral systems ultimately adds to the decreasing quality of care and patient’s safety. Women and children (more than 60%) are disproportionately affected by a severe reduction in health services. Pregnant and lactating women continue to face compromised access to reproductive health and referral services, to antenatal care and postnatal care and safe birthing practices. This exposes pregnant women to a high risk of pregnancy-related morbidity and mortality. Throughout the last 6 months, health authorities in Donetsk and Lugansk oblasts are reporting a considerable increase in the non attended antenatal care for pregnant women and an increase of deliveries outside of health facilities (over 300 cases recently). Maternal health and psychological support network care are largely not available due to the lack of trained care providers and non-functioning referral system; health services for GBV survivors, including post-rape care, enhanced referral pathways, is either not available or not user-friendly.There is strong evidence of a marked uptick in sex work, including among adolescent girls, and an increase in the number of unwanted pregnancies as well as abortions among underage girls in Luhansk GCA (up to 10 cases monthly compared to 1- 2 cases before the crisis for the entire oblast). Without access to quality reproductive health services, they face an increased risk of life-threatening complications, including pregnancy-related morbidity and mortality. The high risk of increased sexual transmission of HIV and STIs in the affected regions adds to the needs in the area of reproductive health. Estimated 32,800 adults in GCA, NGCA and areas of high concern in need of STI treatment. Around 18,000 potentially unrevealed HIV infected in NGCA: every 2nd potential HIV case is not revealed. At the same time, local healthcare authorities report the lack of diagnostic equipment and treatment supplies. Since the onset of the crisis, UNFPA Ukraine is leading the humanitarian response to reproductive health needs of most vulnerable women and adolescent girls, providing life-saving supplies and ensuring accessibility of medical services to those in need, in particular in remote areas and near the contact line. UNFPA SRH mobile teams outreach work along the contact line shows very poor woman health conditions. Out of – 4276 woman examined during June-September 2019, 1070 have Pelvic Inflammatory Diseases, 567 -Urology Diseases, 606 - uterine prolapse, 324 - Uterine fibrosis. Around 450 woman has disability due to last 2 mentioned pathologies and requires surgical treatment. During last 2 years from state and donor moneys were procured laparoscopic equipment for rayon hospitals along the contact line, but unfortunately, there is a big gap in a number of medical staff, especially OBGYNs (before the conflict was 10-12, now only 1-3) and their qualification is very low to use the equipment. One surgery in referral hospitals cost for a patient with all legal and under the table fees around 400-500 USD plus travel and accommodation cost. UNFPA supported visit of qualified OBGYN surgeon to Popasna rayon hospital, he performed during 2 weeks 27 surgeries, that is more cost-effectiveness solution and do not require long travels for patients.

2 - Activities & outcomes

1. Essential supply of drugs and medical goods to increase capacity of local health facilities on provision of maternal and newborn life-saving service and treatment of complications of abortion and miscarriage. UNFPA will supply UNFPA emergency SRH kits to maternity and rayon/city hospitals, to assist normal and complicated deliveries and complication of abortion and miscarriage to 8,650 women of reproductive age (WRA).2. Provision of outreach minimum essential SRH and GBV services in rayons and cities along the contact line Donetsk and Lugansk oblast by three mobile SRH teams for 6 months. To address the problem of medical human resources shortage in the areas most affected by the conflict 3 mobile teams consisting of OB-GYN, general practitioner, nurse/midwife would be providing outreach services visits during 6 months May-October 2019. The mobile teams will be managed by IPs NGO, "Ukrainian Medical Mission". Waves of targeted screening visits to rural settlements, local educational institutions (colleges and schools) and workplaces would be scheduled with local health authorities. The scope of free services addressing acute SRH needs of women and girls and GBV/SGBV will include:-Provision of Maternal health services are mainly prenatal and postnatal care including measurement of blood pressure, weight, height, Leopold manoeuvres, Doppler fetal heart rate, assessment of high risk pregnancy and identify the referral cases; routine pelvic exam to assess for sexually transmitted diseases and identify and follow-up of the referral cases to the secondary level maternities. -Provision of Family Planning Counselling and Commodities including counselling and distribution of commodities; oral contraceptives, condoms, IUDs and injectable.-Provision of general and individual counselling on women's health issues including sexual and reproductive health, pre and postnatal care, maternal health, ultra-sound examination by portative machine, breast-feeding, nutrition, sanitation and childcare, cervical cancer screening.-Provision of outreach STI/HIV prevention, testing and treatment: Express testing of pregnant woman and population at risk of HIV, syphilis, hepatitis B and C- 5000 tests. Syndromic diagnostic and treatment of STI. Provision of condoms to population at risk, PLHIV, woman and man RA, adolescent -Addressing SRH needs of women with disabilities. In 2017, UNFPA provided adjustable OBGYN medical chairs with special functions to examine woman with disabilities 10 service delivery points along the contact line. After training, medical staff of 3 mobile teams and local physicians will be expected to provide SRH service to 300 women with disability.-Provision of Informational and educational materials (IEC): brochures, leaflets and other IEC materials about sexual and reproductive health, sexual and gender-based violence prevention, and referral pathways to 50 thousands of woman and youth-Provision of medical, psychosocial, legal support and referral of GBV/SGBV survivors. Health staff will be providing medical counselling, post-exposure prevention of HIV, STI and unwanted pregnancy, and referral service to GBV/SGBV survivors.3. Provision of qualified OBGYN surgeon to perform OBGYN surgeries to 6 rayon hospitals and Avdyivka city hospital. Besides direct service provision, it will be also in-service training for local OBGYN surgeons. Will be provided 7 two weeks visits or 14 one week visits. Around 210 OBGYN surgeries will be performed.4. Increase capacity of health providers and managers in planning and provision of emergency SRH service including STI, GBV/SGBV and addressing SRH needs of women with disabilities. conducting trainings for 180 health providers and managers: "The Minimum Initial SRH Service Package (MISP) in crisis" , "Comprehensive management of GBV survivals, including cases of rape", "Addressing SRH needs of women with disabilities".
