Project Summary

This intervention will support 400 of the most vulnerable households (HHs) in the five governorates of the Gaza strip who are impacted by the prolonged economic deterioration caused by more than 15 years of siege. This action aims to ensure the specific basic needs of these 400 HHs are met, notably through the distribution of Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA).
The project is informed by assessments ACTED conducted between September and October 2022, the 2022 Multi-Sectorial Needs Assessments (MSNA), conducted by REACH and OCHA in July 2022, extensive consultations with key stakeholders, and recommendations from the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) and the Cash Working Group (CWG) in Gaza. Specifically, ACTED and its implementing partners Arab Center for Agricultural Development (ACAD) and the Palestinian Environmental Friends (PEF) will increase beneficiaries resilience and improve coping capacities through the provision of multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) to help HHs suffering from limited access to income opportunities meet their basic needs and alleviate the impact of the last escalation in August 2022.
MPCA enables female-headed HHs and HHs with particular needs, such as pregnant and lactating women, families with PwDs and/or chronic diseases, to prioritize their specific needs (i.e. milk, instant formula, medicines, diapers) and to meet their differentiated priorities which are often left unmet through sectoral cash initiatives.
The implementation of this action will be based on past experiences implementing MPCA projects since 2021, funded by the HF pool fund and CDCS, as well as e-voucher projects funded by CDCS and Alliance2015, and implemented in coordination with WFP.
Furthermore, ACTED will rely on its 2 Community Emergency Response Networks’ (CERNs) existing capacity to implement activities throughout the project, to reach beneficiaries, share key PSEA messages, and support ACTED’s Complaint and Response Mechanism (CRM). These 2 CERNs were already established under former HF-funded projects, operate across 3 governorates, and are composed of 3 Community-Based Organizations (CBOs), 4 Community-Based Protection Committees (CBPCs), and 130 volunteers (176 individuals in total). ACTED will also rely on its community representatives in governorates which are not fully covered by the CERNs.
The project will be supported by the mainstreaming of an Integrated Protection Approach (IPA), with specific focus on the most vulnerable groups, including women, girls, boys, PwD and older people. Gender principles will be mainstreamed throughout the entire action and CBPCs will be trained on referral mechanisms of protection cases such as GBV with a particular focus given to women and children. Finally, Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) will be core to the proposed Action.

1 - Needs

The Gaza strip continues to be affected by the prolonged economic deterioration caused by more than 15 years of siege and sustained undermining of potential economic rehabilitation, with an unemployment rate reaching 44.1% for the second quarter of 2022. It must be noted that according to the Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS), unemployment in Gaza is still much higher among women than among men, reaching 65.1% in the second quarter of 2022, compared to 65.6% in the previous quarter and 64% in the second quarter of 2021. Regarding Gaza’s younger population (ages 15-29), the unemployment rate stands at 59.1%, compared to 62.5% in the previous quarter and 61.9% in the same quarter last year. Additionally, the recent escalation in Gaza and the global commodities prices volatility due to the Ukraine crisis, as well as the disruption of supply chains, and the shortage of supply compared to demand in the post-COVID era, have had a strong impact on the access to basic commodities in Gaza Strip. Since the beginning of the Ukraine crisis, wheat flour prices in Palestine have in fact increased by 32 percent, vegetable oil prices by 15 percent, and fuel prices by 10 percent. Based on the MSNA conducted by REACH and OCHA in July 2022, increased food prices and shortages have led to poor food-consumption levels among most vulnerable HHs, and negative coping strategies including buying food on credit (incurring debt) (75% of HH), borrowing money to cover food needs due to lack of food or money to buy it (54% of HH), reducing food consumption to cope with a lack of food or money to buy it (81% of HH); and reducing or ceasing payments on utilities (45.7% of HH). The widespread reported use of negative coping strategies is thus an indicator of the difficulties faced by households in meeting their most basic needs, which this proposed action attempts to address. Additionally, ACTED has conducted an assessment in September 2022, where surveyedHHs had a borderline food consumption score averaging 30.6; amongst them were 31% with a poor FCS averaging 15.4. This shows that most HHs suffer from food insecurity and do not consume adequate nutritious food. 70% of surveyed HHs relied either on social aid or financial support from friends or family to be able to cover their basic needs. About 94% of HHs interviewed had to buy food on credit or borrowed cash during the month preceding the survey as a coping mechanism. Moreover, reducing expenses on health (including medicines) and education was used by 34% of households in order to afford food. Those coping mechanisms left 94% of HHs with at least one adult member with debt to repay. Due to the accumulated debts from covering basic needs, paying rent, and some medical expenses, 92% of surveyed household prioritized cash over other types of assistance. This project will thus support the implementation of the MPCA objective: Vulnerable HHs in oPt affected by shocks and stresses due to conflict and natural disaster supported with MPCA are able to meet their immediate essential needs and access to lifesaving services, decreases the incidence of negative coping strategies. MPCA enables female-headed HHs and HHs with particular needs, such as pregnant and lactating women, families with PwDs and/or chronic diseases, to prioritize their specific needs (i.e. milk, instant formula, medicines, diapers) and to meet their differentiated priorities which are often left unmet through sectoral cash initiatives.It will also support objective 2 for the HRP 2023: “STRATEGIC OBJECTIVE 2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under International Humanitarian Law.”, through the provision of MPCA in local currency via a local Money Transfer Agent to 400 vulnerable HHs.

2 - Activities

A1) Verification of pre-identified list of beneficiaries in project design phase: ACTED, PEF and ACAD will coordinate with MoSD and obtain a list of the 500 most vulnerable HHs in need of MPCA in order to select 400 eligible households. The selection criteria will in fact prioritize extremely poor HHs in all the governorates of the Gaza strip, affected by the recent escalation in Gaza, as well as specific vulnerable groups such as female-headed HHs, HHs with children, older members, people with disabilities and/or chronic diseases. Particular attention will be paid to prioritise female-headed households and HHs with particular needs, such as pregnant and lactating women, families with children and persons with disabilities and/or chronic diseases, and/or HHs who are paying rent. ACTED will use its experience in conducting verification exercises, notably using the UVAT tool. A2) Conduct sensitization sessions: ACTED/PEF/ACAD, with the support of CERNs, will start sensitization sessions with the 400 beneficiaries under this activity to present them the project, its duration, selection process, and MPCA distribution process. In total, around 16 sessions will be conducted for all targeted HHs with 15-25 individuals participating on average. Through these sessions, beneficiaries will be informed about ACTED’s complaint and response mechanism (CRM) and will distribute PSEA leaflets to all beneficiaries. The project team will as such introduce beneficiaries to CERN members and community representatives and will notify beneficiaries of the closest complaint boxes available to them, which will be placed at the CBO offices closest to beneficiaries’ localities. During sessions, the project team will also request beneficiaries to inform them of any changes in their personal information (mobile number, location) to ensure the continuity of assistance delivery throughout implementation. A3) Provision of MPCA to support the most vulnerable HHs: The 400 HHs selected under activity 1 will be provided with multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) to ensure their families are able to meet their basic needs. ACTED will provide each HH with 3 instalments of MPCA over a 3-month period, in order to ensure their essential and urgent needs are covered immediately, giving them time to rebound and rebuild their resilience. As per guidance developed by the Gaza Cash Working Group (CWG), the agreed emergency transfer value for multi-purpose cash assistance is 1,081 NIS per instalment. These 400 HHs will thus receive 1,081 NIS per instalment (3,243 NIS in total). This MPCA will be distributed in local currency (NIS). MPCA is beneficiaries’ preferred method, especially for women and PwD, as it provides them with more choice and flexibility, while safeguarding their dignity. In addition to being a cost- efficient method for humanitarian actors, the multiplier effect associated with injecting cash within the local economy will contribute towards boosting the Gazan economy. Cash will be provided to beneficiaries through a money transfer agent. Relying on an MTA will ensure continuous access to withdrawing points throughout week and weekends and proximity to HHs. This method of distribution ensures accessibility for BNF, taking into account their specific needs, notably those of women-headed HH or HH with a member with disability. A4) Conduct field monitoring visits and PDM. Field monitors will be in charge of conducting monitoring of project implementation through close follow up and monitoring home visits of beneficiaries. This process will also be supported by ACTED/PEF/ACAD field staff and ACTED’s AME Unit, with support from the CERNs. The ACTED Appraisal Monitoring and Evaluation (AME) Unit, independent from the Program Department, will be in charge of the post-distribution monitoring (PDM). This will include standard post-distribution questions regarding the assistance received – whether there were any issues related to the implementation process, how the intervention impacted their household, etc. The PDM process will be led by ACTED’s AME unit. The AME unit will lead a team of enumerators that will collect the required data. The PDM will be used to measure the project implementation, satisfaction with the methodology, assess beneficiary targeting, collection of cash, use of cash and market behaviour, as well as beneficiaries’ perceptions of ACTED, PEF and ACAD. A5) Strengthening CERNs through refresher trainings. The CERN will be strengthened through refresher trainings, including on PSEA. CERN will ensure community engagement throughout the project’s lifespan as well as referring any identified protection issues. A CERN will be tasked with supporting awareness sensitization sessions. 40% of the CERN’s membership will remain composed of females. A6) Referral of the most vulnerable HHs with specific needs The proposed Action will involve frequent and meaningful consultation with a variety of stakeholders to exploit synergies and avoid duplication with other ongoing actions to maximize cost efficiency and facilitate the sustainable continuation of activities beyond project closure. This will be achieved by coordinating activities with relevant bodies in Gaza, to ensure alignment with their needs and objectives, and adherence to minimum standards for assistance. A key part of the project’s exit strategy will be the referral of vulnerable households with specific needs to Cluster sector specific cash interventions, e.g. health, food, protection and social protection or to the appropriate agency/government structure. This referral process will take place during the course of the project and include any unmet needs identified in the project’s PDM. CERN members will be briefed on MoSD’s social protection platform in order to support the registration of any identified household in target communities who are yet to be registered on the system and ensure assistance is available after the project’s closure.

3 - Indicators

Project implementation will be supported by a comprehensive monitoring system to gather all relevant data to steer the action, maximize its impact, measure its effects, anticipate and mitigate any challenge. Specifically, ACTED’s Appraisal, Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU) is independent from the Program Department and will be in charge of reinforcing the projects’ accountability throughout the project cycle management by regularly assessing project implementation. AMEU is an essential part of ACTED’s project management structure, providing a mechanism to crosscheck and give recommendations on the work of the project team and partners. AMEU leads assessment of baseline situations of beneficiaries through data collection by disaggregation based on age, gender and disability, monitors ongoing implementation through regular field visits and consultations with beneficiaries, and evaluates the outcome of the activities. This methodology ensures that the AMEU uses an outcome monitoring approach to analyze the effect of the action on target groups. The action will promote the use of best practices in accountability and transparency, in line with Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) principles, by using ACTED’s Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM). The CRM will enable beneficiaries and other stakeholders to provide feedback and suggestions directly to ACTED staff through a phone line, post, email, and complaint boxes in relevant locations, as well as directly to partner’s staff members. The CRM should ensure that beneficiaries can easily and without fear of repercussion communicate cases of abuse of power by financial institutions, community leaders and/or other key actors involved in the cash-transfer program and/or aspects of program and/or transfer modality that inadvertently have negative implications. ACTED’s AMEU is in charge of handling this mechanism to provide timely response or adequate action to address any concerns.Financial monitoring will be undertaken by all project partners and then consolidated by ACTED, with external audits of the action taking place on an annual basis. ACTED staff will request from ACAD monthly reporting on activities on top of contractual obligations to ensure all indicators are on track and register beneficiaries’ satisfaction and any difficulties encountered. This monthly reporting will minimise the risk of delays in the activities’ implementation, that any change in the implementation of the project is notified and finally that all indicators are on track and if needed corrective measures are taken to reach the target.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

As noted in the 2022 MSNA findings, cash assistance is beneficiaries’ preferred method (70% of HHs), especially for women and PwD, as it provides them with more choice and flexibility, while safeguarding their dignity. In addition to being a cost-efficient method for humanitarian actors, the multiplier effect associated with injecting cash within the local economy will contribute towards restoring the Palestinian economy. Furthermore, cash assistance ensures multisectoral coverage, while keeping beneficiaries involved all along the activities, as they can choose the more adapted items to cover their multiple needs.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

The project will be underpinned by ACTED’s programmatic approach of meaningful community engagement and participation of the population, in line with the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Five Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP), namely (1) leadership/governance; (2) transparency; (3) feedback and complaints; (4) participation; (5) design, monitoring and evaluation. More specifically, AAP will be ensured throughout the intervention by taking account of, giving account to, and being held to account by the affected population as follows. ACTED will be held to account by the beneficiaries in line with Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) principles, by using ACTED’s Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM). The CRM is overseen by ACTED’s AME Unit, which is independent from the delivery of activities. Beneficiaries can access ACTED’s CRM through a dedicated phone line, WhatsApp, email address and complaints boxes as well as directly to ACTED staff during field visits. Through this CRM, beneficiaries can raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on ACTED and co- applicants’ work in a secure and confidential way, and receive a response in a timely manner. ACTED’s AMEU is in charge of handling this mechanism to provide timely response or adequate action to address any concerns. Having a well-designed and well-managed mechanism for handling beneficiary complaints will improve the quality of the action, enhance the trust and confidence of beneficiaries and identify areas of the project which need to be improved, and ensure that ACTED teams learn from the feedback provided through this process.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

Beneficiaries can access ACTED’s CRM through a dedicated phone line, WhatsApp, email address, complaints boxes and directly through ACTED staff during field visits. This plurality of means aims to ensure CRM accessibility to all, including vulnerable groups with specific needs. During the assessment, field officers will provide a CRM card to each BNF, including all communication channels mentioned above, and explanations on the CRM, to ensure that all BNF feel comfortable and in measure to reach it if needed, including in case of SEA. Female staff, part of the team to which BNF will be able to refer to, will notably ensure female BNF feel safe to complain. AME staff will dedicate specific time to vulnerable groups during field visits to re-explain the CRM and emphasize that BNF can directly deliver the feedback or complaint to them during visits - if one person would not have the capacity to use/access a phone/email address or the complaint box (notably children, PwD, and the older people).

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

In all programming, ACTED ensures respect and promotion of the rights of all individuals and communities involved, in accordance with the relevant bodies of law (international human rights law and international humanitarian law). To take account of affected populations, the action will consider the needs and interests of beneficiary communities, particularly in regard to designing activities with their safety and dignity in mind. As part of protection mainstreaming, ACTED analyzes protection risks; anticipates possible consequences that may arise; and ensures that issues can be reported/referrals can be made to appropriate agencies/host government structures if required. In particular, ACTED, PEF and ACAD will undertake regular visits to the communities and beneficiary HHs to ensure that protection concerns are incorporated throughout the intervention, thus enhancing safety, security and privacy for women, children and other vulnerable groups. ACTED is firmly committed to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality, and strives to ensure the ‘Do-No-Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities do not inadvertently worsen tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance. To this end, all staff will be made aware of and in adherence to the Do-No-Harm principle as outlined in ACTED’S Code of Conduct and related policies including Child Protection; Anti-Fraud, Bribery & Corruption; Anti-Terrorism; and Policy Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Under this framework, ACTED has developed mechanisms that can contribute to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse such as: signing of the code of conduct by all staff in order to clarify unacceptable behaviour and in particular sexual exploitation and abuse; information to the beneficiaries of existing remedies (Complaints and Response Mechanisms); implementation of training and awareness sessions in order to train and empower the teams and identify risk factors on an ongoing basis.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In order to maximize the reach of the intervention, the project will adopt a deeply integrated protection approach (IPA) throughout all stages of the project cycle. The action fully acknowledges that different groups and individuals have specific needs and assistance will therefore be adapted accordingly. To this end, ACTED will ensure that information is provided through accessible channels, formats, languages and family and community support networks to ensure that the beneficiary community are kept fully informed of the assistance available under the project.ACTED’s AMEU will also ensure equal and impartial access to aid by overseeing the CRM, as well as by conducting various verification exercises. For example, ACTED’s AMEU will conduct a verification exercise to authenticate the fairness of the selection process and all applicants will be notified of the final decision and provided with the means to raise concerns regarding the selection process through ACTED’s CRM. Where applicable, at least one male and one female per HH will be consulted to ensure gender-specific needs and concerns are considered, as well as those of boys, girls, PwDs, the older people and other marginalized persons to ensure assistance reflects their unique vulnerabilities. ACTED will put particular emphasis on informing beneficiaries on the functioning and use of the CRM in the Gaza strip, since the last MSNA conducted by REACH and OCHA in June 2022, revealed that only 21% of HHs which benefitted from humanitarian aid, reported awareness of how to access a complaint or reporting mechanism. Finally, the project will deliberately target the most vulnerable groups for inclusion into the program. For instance, women in the targeted areas will be highly encouraged to participate in the CBPCs to ensure that membership remains at least 40% female at a minimum. Similar efforts will be made to ensure that vulnerabilities do not act as a barrier to accessing aid. For instance, CBPCs will conduct HHs visits to assist in the completion and collection of applications for the vulnerable (including older people, people with disabilities) that might be unable to reach the registration site.
