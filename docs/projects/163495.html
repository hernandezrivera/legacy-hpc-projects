Project Summary

In order to respond to needs of women and girls and provide appropriate response, the funds will help set up a robust humanitarian team in the South West and North West of Cameroon.
Low capacity of local actors in the two regions continues to hamper efforts to deliver timely and quality GBV/SRH services.
To address the key service gaps, UNFPA will Support the setting up of “safe spaces” for PSS, case management, social cohesion and life-skills for affected women and adolescent girls. Adolescent friendly spaces will be established at high density displacement areas to address the SRH/GBV service needs of adolescents. Working with local networks and service providers, 3 mobile clinics will be supported to address the needs of women of reproductive age especially pregnant women in areas with severe access constraints. Tents for service provision will be procured to support rapid response to emerging service delivery needs in the two regions.
Some indications about the calculations of the number of SRH kits requested.
First, we will require individual delivery kits to be distributed to about 10,000 pregnant women with no access to health facility delivery in the NW and SW.
Second, about 60 birth attendants in these areas will be given Kit 2B (Midwives and other providers within the displaced communities).
Third, an extra 15 facilities will need to be provided with rape kits.
Fourth, the block 2 kits will be used to support activities in the mobile clinics and select fixed clinics.
Last but not least, three functional hospitals have been targeted for support with Kits 11A, 11B and 12.

Besoins / Needs

Cameroon humanitarian is almost worsening in the 3 majors affected regions North West, South West and Far North. North West and South West regions are hitting by a social and political crisis with an armed conflict from these regions who are claiming the independence of Ambazonia. For Far North region, the crisis is due to insecurity caused by Boko Haram. According to last humanitarian newsletter , there are actually 4.3 Million people in need including about 2,3 Million targeted for assistance with 922,000 internally displaced persons and others from host communities.According to HNO 2020, many GBV incidents, affecting women and girls in particular, were reported. In this context of socio-economic fragility due to crisis, the use of sex as a negative coping mechanism is reported. Women have more responsibilities as heads of households, making them even more vulnerable. Access to legal services is very problematic, with lack of legal response to survivors. The closure of many health facilities limit access to reproductive health services. It has been reported that women and girls living with disabilities have been repeatedly raped and sexually abused. These people also have very limited access to services and protection due to insecurity. In terms of responses, UNFPA mobilized funds from UNFPA emergency funds and CERF to support activities related to prevention, mitigation and response. Its implementing partners was able to : - establish 9 “safe spaces in Far North, South West and North West for psychosocial support, case management , psycho social support and mental health; - distribute 7,000 culturally appropriate dignity kits to vulnerable women and girls , support 7406 GBV survivors to access one of the essential service and refer to adequate GBV specialized services, support 15 985 persons affected by the crisis to receive psychological first aid; facilitate sensitization to reach 154 064 community members with lifesaving messages and referrals pathways for SRH and GBV services, sensitize 650 people living with disability on GBV /SRH, capacitate over 373 actors on various thematic areas of SRH and GBV, finalize and disseminate service mapping of GBV actors in South West and North West, validate and disseminate GBV SOPs and referral pathways in the Far North, South West and North West . UNFPA is leading the GBV sub cluster in South West and North and co-lead GBV working group at national level and engaged to co-lead the GBV working group in Far North. GBVIMS is functional in Far North and efforts are engaged to extend GBVIMS in NW&SW.Access to services for survivors is limited due to insecurity, lack of services and hard reach localities .Despite the efforts realized to conduct interventions through affected populations, many needs related to GBV and SRH service delivery remained uncovered.In 2020, the GBV sector in the Far North, South West and North West will focus on prevention and response, as well as capacity building and increased coordination. Main interventions are related to establish and strengthen referral and holistic care mechanisms to enable women and girls to access quality services, with a focus on survivors of rape, sexual violence and GBV and those living with disability.
