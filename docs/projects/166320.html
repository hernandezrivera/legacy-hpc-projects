Project Summary

Since 2009, the Boko Haram insurgency has crippled Northeastern Nigeria. Borno State (Borno) has been particularly affected; of the 2.2 million people forced from their homes across the region, over half (1.5 million) are in Borno. AOGs also continue to attack LGAs in Northern Adamawa and Eastern Yobe states. The Global Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) compiled by OCHA estimated that there are 7.1 million people in need in northeast Nigeria, 80% of whom (approximately 5.6 million) are women and children. The protracted conflict has led to continual displacement with new arrivals and displaced populations residing in over-congested camps. Such populations face grave protection risks and violations such as exploitation and abuse, arbitrary and extended detentions, family separation, and restrictions on freedom of movement and insecurity. Of those affected by these realities, particular subsets including female-headed households and child-headed households, unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), and adolescent girls and boys face heightened protection risks. These risks are exacerbated by a lack of adequate services in sites, which is expected to be compounded by the COVID-19 pandemic. Family separation and bereavement, as well as social isolation as a result of the pandemic is also anticipated to significantly affect parenting support for children and adolescents, vulnerability to abuse and exploitation as well as neglect.
The IRC Child Protection COVID 19 response will focus on providing Case Management through direct services and linkages to specialized services as well as Psycho- Social Support (PSS) for children, adolescents and their caregivers who have been affected by COVID 19. IRC Child Protection (CP) intervention will focus on providing specialized case management services to Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC), other children that have suffered or are at risk of abuse, exploitation, neglect, violence and adolescents by providing them case management services. This response will also create linkages to specialized services that will cater for the peculiar and immediate needs of children and adolescents arising from the effect of COVID 19 on the one hand and those associated with the humanitarian crisis in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states on the other. The response will be closely coordinated with Health, Nutrition, Gender Based Violence (GBV) and WASH services including through conducting Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE) activities, mainstreamed through a collaborative approach. The IRC tool kits for Socio-Emotional Learning (SEL), Supporting Adolescents and their Families in Emergencies (SAFE) and the Families Make the Difference (FMD) parenting skills, which have been adapted to meet the needs that may emerge in the pandemic, will be used to meet the psychosocial needs of children, adolescents and their families. The adaptation of the PSS activities will aim to provide children and their caregivers’ the much needed social and emotional support to help them manage the COVID associated stress while observing the preventive measure of social distancing. The intervention will also strengthen community led structures who would support the IRC child protection team in implementing these activities in their communities.

1 - Needs

Since 2009, the Boko Haram insurgency has crippled Northeastern Nigeria. Borno State (Borno) has been particularly affected; of the 2.2 million people forced from their homes across the region, over half (1.5 million) are in Borno. AOGs also continue to attack LGAs in Northern Adamawa and Eastern Yobe states. The Global Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) compiled by OCHA estimated that there are 7.1 million people in need in northeast Nigeria, 80% of whom (approximately 5.6 million) are women and children. The protracted conflict has led to continual displacement with new arrivals and displaced populations residing in over-congested camps. Such populations face grave protection risks and violations such as exploitation and abuse, arbitrary and extended detentions, family separation, and restrictions on freedom of movement and insecurity. Of those affected by these realities, particular subsets including female-headed households and child-headed households, unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), and adolescent girls and boys face heightened protection risks. These risks are exacerbated by a lack of adequate services in sites, which is expected to be compounded by the COVID-19 pandemic Overall, the pandemic has resulted in reduced access to services available to children, adolescents and their caregivers affected by the armed conflict in Northeastern Nigera. Government imposed movement restrictions and consequent scale down of humanitarian activities has contributed to this issue. From the available data of confirmed COVID-19 cases recorded in Nigeria, the number of children infected is relatively low but they remain extremely vulnerable to secondary effects including a number of protection issues which may arise from their caregivers getting infected. Apart from losing caregivers and family members, facing family separation as a result of caregivers being isolated, their level of vulnerability to abuse, exploitation, neglect and violence will increase without any support from their caregivers, communities or services that existed in the communities before the pandemic. The pandemic has also affected the routine recreational and other activities that provided them with PSS that enable them to build resilience and positive coping strategies. Caregivers also have to deal with more psycho-social and emotional stress related to loss and difficulties associated with the pandemic. This affects their ability to provide parenting supporting to children and adolescents under their care.

2 - Activities

This project will provide case management to children including unaccompanied/separated and adolescents who have suffered or are at risk of suffering from abuse, exploitation, neglect and violence. It will include conducting assessments of cases identified, registering, working with children and caregivers to develop case plans and follow up. The project will also link children and adolescents to specialized services to meet their health, nutrition, WASH and GBV needs. Children and adolescents will also be provided with PSS by adapting the IRC SAFE and SEL activities to meet the social/physical distancing requirement in reducing the spread of the virus and also providing age appropriate PSS kits containing items which children and adolescents can use for recreation at the house hold level with community based facilitators working under the supervision of IRC CP staff to provide remote supervision. Caregivers will be supported with information that will strengthen their parenting skills and place them in a position where they are able to positively support children and adolescents under their care and also manage their personal stress. This is necessary in ensuring that children and adolescents have a safe and supportive home environment required for their well being. Information on prevention, identification and where to go for services will also be shared with children, adolescents and caregivers. The IRC CP team will work closely with its existing community led structures and incentive workers who reside within the communities and have access to personal protective items in providing these services to the targeted clients.
