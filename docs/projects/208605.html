1 - Needs

The proposed interventions will aim at maximizing coverage and access as well as improving timely referral from the community to all CMAM sites. SC will ensure that care is provided to malnourished children for as long as is needed while ensuring that caregivers, especially mothers and other vulnerable caregivers, are supported to be available at care centers in order to reduce the chances of children presenting malnutrition to deteriorate their condition.The interventions within this sub-sector will focus on improving MIYCN practices through capacity-building and social and behavior change interventions to improve knowledge, attitudes, and practices. These activities will focus on filling knowledge gaps and reinforcing positive behaviors. Through the proposed interventions, the community will be sensitized on IYCF and be encouraged to support PLWs and caregivers to uphold recommended feeding practices.

2 - Activities

SC plans to reach 118,200 children through the MUAC screening approaches. Children 6-59 months identified as SAM cases without medical complications during the screening will be referred to the nearest OTP sites (supported by SC or any partner) where they will receive treatment. SC will continue supporting 9 OTP sites, in health facilities and in outreach sites project. Of these, SC will construct two OTPs and refurbish one additional OTP, making a total of three OTPs that will be constructed and/or refurbished under this award. To ensure that quality treatment for SAM cases is being provided, SC will build the capacity of health facility staff (MOH) and mobile health workers to deliver CMAM, and ensure that an adequate supply of medicines, equipment and supplies is available. UNICEF will provide nutrition supplies for the management of SAM as per the existing agreement between SC and UNICEF. Children with medical complications will be referred for in-patient care to the nearest stabilization center, including Molai General Hospital, supported by this project. The Stabilization Center admits complicated SAM and MAM cases from 0 – 59 months, providing specialized 24-hour care, according to National Protocols. SC will treat an estimated 480 (230M; 250F) children with complications during the implementation period. SC will support mothers and infants <6 months of age who are nutritionally vulnerable through implementation of the Management of at-risk Mothers and Infants under six months of age (MAMI) Care Pathway. Health Workers will assess and monitor infant growth, feeding, clinical status and maternal nutrition and psychosocial factors, providing support, as needed, for infant feeding support, maternal feeding support, and/or maternal well-being support. Mothers and infants will be referred for health care where clinical issues are identified, and referred for specialized support where severe illness, malnutrition, and/or maternal mental health illness is detected. To ensure optimal coverage and early detection, nutrition volunteers will screen mothers and infants <6months in the community and refer to CNMs for a MAMI assessment when issues are identified. Infants and their mothers enrolled in the program will receive regular re-assessments and counseling at the community level while enrolled in the program. Additional contact points will also be leveraged for screening, for example vaccinations and IMCI visits. Mothers and their infants <6 months who are found through screening to be nutritionally at-risk, based on determined criteria (e.g. poor growth, LBW, pre-term, multiple birth, orphan, feeding difficulty, moderate maternal mental health difficulty and other key MAMI risk factors for the context (to be determined at a later stage) with no complications will be referred for enrollment into outpatient MAMI services within the program. This will include regular re-assessments and IYCF counseling at the community level by CNMs, and, as needed, referral to psychosocial services for the mother to support the infant’s nutritional situation. Infants within the MAMI program requiring health services will be referred to the health facility. Health workers and CNMs will receive one refresher training on CMAM to enhance their capacity and for quality service provision and a new training will be planned on the MAMI approach.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

By adhering to the principle of "Do No Harm" in all of its activities, SC will ensure the safety of its activities. In addition to key informant interviews, suitable sites and locations will be selected for meetings and focus group discussions. To minimize risks, secure sites near beneficiaries will be prioritized to ensure that people can return home before dark. In addition to safeguarding training, Save the Children's staff and enumerators will also receive training on gender equality, inclusion, and accountability. All new SC employees are oriented to and required to sign the SC Safeguarding Policy and the Code of Conduct as part of their contract. SC mainstreams protection, gender equality and inclusion across all programming. The food assistance program refers to vulnerable households with minors as caregivers over 65, households with children, households with disabilities, and households headed by women.

9.1a - Specify (and give URL link if possible to) the relevant needs assessment document.

Due to protracted conflict in Northeast Nigeria, majority of HHs in Borno are living as IDPs after multiple displacements; women and children make-up 82% of the total IDP population.9 HHs have not been able to return to their homes of origin and/or to their LGAs of origin due to insecurity. Most of the population is concentrated around Maiduguri town and neighbouring LGAs of Jere. In a recent assessment, IDPs indicated their biggest unmet need is food (76%) followed by NFIs (13%) and shelter (4%)

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

SC will promote transparency and accountability in all its operations and ensure that beneficiaries, community leaders and other community members have access to the feedback and response mechanisms (FRM) and SC will ensure confidentiality to protect beneficiaries. The feedback mechanisms will be established together with and for beneficiaries – including children, adults, persons living with disabilities and other vulnerable groups, and take timely corrective actions to address safety, dignity and access issues raised by users and/or to redefine their assistance. During IYCF sensitization meetings, SC will reiterate messages to beneficiaries, partners, local leaders and other community stakeholders emphasizing that SC assistance is free and impartial, and the right to aid as being unconditional and that no one should ask for any kind of favor in return for aid, particularly sexual and/or monetary. These messages will be reinforced in radio jingles on local radio stations in local languages to provide information to the wider population. SC will use the preferred feedback mechanisms selected by the community, these may include a toll-free mobile phone number, help desk, and community meetings. Within the shopping areas, a FRM desk will be set up and managed by two SC staff (male and female) to ensure beneficiaries can provide feedback on any challenges they face relating to this sector and other components of the program and that they are addressed efficiently. Community feedback meetings to engage with community structures and beneficiaries on the impact of program activities to the community and ways of improving on the support provided.
