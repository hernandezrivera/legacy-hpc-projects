Project Summary

WASH Interventions will aim at ensuring that populations affected by displacement have reduced risk of exposure to water and sanitation related diseases. NRC will provide access to safe water and sanitation as well as promotion of better hygiene practices that lead to behaviour change.
NRC will construct new water points and, in some cases, rehabilitate existing water points to increase access to adequate and reliable water supply. NRC will ensure that water is safe for drinking through water quality testing at competent laboratory and provision of water treatment equipment, regular monitoring and continuous engagement of the community in water quality management activities. Water committees will be established and trained in the maintenance of water points and water quality management. The establishment, training and continuous support to WASH committees through refresher trainings especially after rehabilitation of water points will be critical in ensuring project interventions are strengthened by a comprehensive understanding of community dynamics that may impact positively or negatively on the programme.
NRC will support communities to construct their own latrines through contribution and/or materials, training of community member and local artisans in construction methods, maintenance of the latrines and technical supervision of the construction of latrine superstructure. In addition, NRC will promote construction of family/household latrines as opposed to communal latrines that have proven difficult to clean and maintain. While latrine use, cleanliness and maintenance will be the responsibility of the beneficiaries, NRC will support the latter with training, sanitation cleaning/construction tools, and related hygiene promotion activities. NRC will also ensure that there is proper lighting for safety of latrines use at night through provision of solar lighting, lamps or flashlights to families.
Latrines will be gender-segregated and distinguishable to promote privacy, dignity and overall wellbeing of users. The location of latrines will be determined taking account of geological factors and following consultations with the community and addressing the safety of women and girls when accessing latrines, especially at night. Family latrines will be preferred over community latrines as the latter are difficult to keep clean and well-maintained. Handwashing stations will be placed at strategic points to promote handwashing. Latrine designs will ensure access by all, including people living with disabilities.
NRC will provide some suggested COVID-19 NFI kits which will include bars of soap, a bucket and disinfectant for household surfaces, in this case bleach which can be diluted with water to include IEC materials with instructions on how to use it. The household NFI kits that will be provided will not be any different than usual, additional soap and household bleach will be the difference.
Through hygiene promotion NRC will seek to improve community hygiene and encourage behaviour change via household visits, community mobilization and focus group discussions and using information, education and communication posters with messaging on proper hygiene practices, such as handwashing at critical times, safe excreta and waste disposal, safe water chain and safe water management. Messages will specifically address how to reduce the risk of exposure to and transmission of communicable diseases. NRC will provide menstrual hygiene kits and training on their use to women and girls of childbearing age.
Target group
NRC target group will include returnees, IDPs and vulnerable members of host communities. Persons living with disabilities will be part of the beneficiary selection criteria.

1 - Besoins / Needs

There is limited access to basic water and sanitation services – in the FN 75% of households displaced during 2020 do not have access to drinking water. This results in high incidence of water-borne and sanitation related illnesses. The majority of the population in displacement areas in the Far North and the environs rely on water from shallow/hand-dug wells or surface water. In most areas, the limited groundwater resources due to poor hydrogeological conditions significantly contributes to water scarcity and poor water quality in terms of high salinity values. The findings on water quality from an assessment in Logone-et-Chari carried out by NRC in January 2019 indicated that more than 50% of the population have difficulties to access to drinking water due to unavailability, and insufficiency of water sources. For those who have access, water sources are mostly located more than 600 meters away from dwellings. The use of improved water sources for drinking and cooking ranges from 56.6% in the Far North to 72.7% in Adamawa region. Finally, only 14% of the population in the Far North have access to adequate hygiene and sanitation infrastructure and 54% do not have access to water sources . Environmental sanitation is also a major concern with high rates of open defecation due to lack of adequate and secure latrines for IDPs and returnees in the Far North region and open defecation was already an existing negative cultural practice before the crisis. In the context of regular movement of IDPs, refugees and returnees, latrines are an urgent need, as the Far North region is also an area known for frequent cholera outbreaks especially during the rainy season. In July 2019 the Ministry of Public Health confirmed an outbreak of cholera in North and Far North regions of the country.Existing sanitation facilities are full, abandoned or in disrepair, and often there is a high user to facility ratio. Poor water quality at water sources is due to multiple factors including open defection around the water points and limited community management of existing water points, leaving them prone to algae contaminations and pollution from waste debris in unprotected water sources. A post distribution monitoring exercise will be done three months after the distribution exercise to ensure that the beneficiaries use the materials as intended. NRC already has Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) systems in place that ensures disaggregated data collection from the beneficiaries. The M&E department in NRC will assist the program team in the monitoring of outputs associated with gender equality.
