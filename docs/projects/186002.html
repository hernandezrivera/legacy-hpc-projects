Project Summary

The purpose of this project is to increase food security and nutrition for vulnerable households severely affected by the conflict in the North West South West regions of Cameroon. The specific objectives are to increase crop and chicken production for household food consumption and income. The proposed intervention is based on the premise that if food insecure farmer households are supported to recover and rebuild their agricultural livelihoods, they will increase food availability, accessibility and affordability for their households and community, and achieve sustainable food security. Supported with improved farm inputs (seeds, farm tools, local chicken breeds), trained on improved agricultural practices in production, post-harvest handling, and facilitated increased market access for their farm produce; households will increase agricultural and livestock production for food and income.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The North West (NW) and South West (SW) regions have an estimated population of 2,541,188 and 1,934,352 respectively. About 70% of rural households in these regions depend on subsistence agriculture as the primary source of food and income. Subsistence agriculture in these regions constitute crop production and rearing of small livestock mainly poultry, goats, sheep and pigs.The ongoing socio-political conflict in these regions has severely affected households and communities resulting in the loss of their livelihoods. The crisis has led to the displacement of the farming communities, erosion of household assets including loss of agricultural and livestock production assets, limiting access to farm inputs and support services. Additionally, the restrictive measures implemented across the country to curb the COVID 19 pandemic has further disrupted agricultural production and exacerbated food insecurity. The affected households increasingly face food insecurity in the face of severe food shortages, and sharp price increase of foodstuff. This has rendered life miserable particularly for women and children. Findings of an assessment commissioned by Nascent show that with failing efforts to revive their livelihoods, 97% of households interviewed reported to be coping by decreasing the number of meals per day for children and the elderly and minimum monthly household income deteriorated from 5000FCFA ($9) before the crises to currently 1,000FCFA ($1.8). (Nascent Solutions, 2021).The Harmonized Framework (Cadre Harmonise) of March 2021 reports that 10% (2.6 million) of the Cameroonian population faced acute food insecurity (Phases 3 to 5) during the period March-May 2021. The projected food insecure population in Cameroon for the period from June – August 2021 is 1,920,618, with approximately half being in the NW (561,394) and SW (393,224). Therefore, of the ten regions, the NW and SW regions have the highest food insecurity rates of 22% and 20%, respectively.
