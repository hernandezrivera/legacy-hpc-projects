Objectives

1. Expand and strengthen immediate access to equitable learning opportunities, in a safe and protective environment, to crisis-affected communities, children and youth (host and refugee populations) 2. Improve the quality of teaching and learning for refugee children and youth, and host communities and increase CPD opportunities for teachers 3. Increase refugee participation and engagement in the education of children and youth

Needs

- Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) are valued and learning is happening -however, there is a recognition by parents, children and CFS facilitators for the need to transition to more formal education provision - Access to education in Myanmar prior to displacement was low (approx. 50%) and there is very little correlation between those who attended school in Myanmar and those who are now attending learning centres in Cox's Bazar which has resulted in mixed ability - mixed age learners in one space. There is therefore the need to ensure access to quality education for all to meet Sustainable Development Goals (SDG) and Grand Bargain commitments - Language of instruction is a significant barrier: Rohingya is an oral language, however related to the local Chittagong dialect, so centre animators are able to communicate verbally with children; however, with Bangla not allowed to be taught/ used, Burmese and English are being taught. The refugee population indicates a strong preference for English to be taught - There is the need for a more formalised curriculum linked to the CFS and Temporary Learning Centres - this will be founded on the Learning and Competency Framework and Approach (LCFA) - but based on continued lobbying at national level for accreditation - Capacity building and Continuous Professional Development for animators and teachers is required - English is a priority, as is learner-centred pedagogy as the majority of animators are young people with a secondary school education level, but with no pre-service teacher training - Only 3% of adolescents are accessing learning opportunities, however, responses need to accommodate certain cultural norms and expectations, particularly around the need for segregated learning spaces for adolescent boys and girls - There is a lack of parental and community engagement in education provision and management - There is a high vulnerability to natural disaster - therefore there is a need for integration of disaster risk reduction (DRR) at all levels - centre, management and community outreach

Activities or outputs

1. Establishment and equipping of 17 multi-functional child protection spaces and 6 women and girl friendly spaces (WGFS) to deliver learning in line with the Learning Competency Framework and Approach (LCFA) for children ages 6-142. Establishment, training and mentorship of community-led protection and management committees for the Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) and WGFS3. Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) training and action planning and follow-up for children, adolescents, Temporary Learning Center (TLC), CFS and WGFS protection and management committees4. Continuous professional development encompassing conflict-sensitive education, learner-centred and subject-based pedagogy for learning animators and teachers5. Integrated safeguarding and child protection mechanisms and pathways, including integrated Psycho-Social Support (PSS), ensuring a protective learning environment
