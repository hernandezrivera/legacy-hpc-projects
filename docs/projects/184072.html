Project Summary

The project aims at providing comprehensive WASH response to most vulnerable communities in Sharg An Neel in Khartoum State.
Based on the needs described in the section below, the Action aims covering the major gaps that are currently preventing the access to a safe and cleaner water, adequate sanitation facilities and to basic hygiene practices. A soft component of sensitisation will be integrating the action and favour the adoption of best hygiene and sanitation practices. ACTED will rehabilitate 5 water pumps, and 5 water yards while ACTED will maintain additional 5 water points in terms of water quality assurance and water safety measures and complement the intervention by the distribution of 2000 Hygiene kits.

1 - Needs

As per BaNVA report of September 2021, the 33% of the host communities residing in Khartoum state experienced a high vulnerability level. The indicator took into account eight sectors, i.e., universal, monetary, education, food, health, shelter & energy, WASH, and protection. Focusing on WASH sector, 34% of Khartoum state vulnerable residents were classified as highly vulnerable, and the 3% as severely vulnerable. In terms of sanitation, the value indicates the lack of usable latrines for the population of reference. For HHs falling under the above 34%, the nearest source of water is more than one hour away by foot, while the 3% experiencing a severe vulnerability had no access to sufficient water or had to travel for more than 5 hours to reach the nearest water source. In relation to the hygiene gaps, the HHs falling into the highly vulnerable group had no access to soap, while the 3% were reported to lack handwashing tools. A more accurate breakdown of data confirms the major needs identified in the above-mentioned report. Taking into account the 2022 target of PiNs living in Sharg An Neel and shared by WASH Cluster in Sudan, more than 238,000 people are reported “in need” of WASH services. In Sharg An Neel only, it is said that 21,237 relies on unprotected water sources. 47% of PiNs of sanitation facilities have to use non-official water sources, thus facing a critical need, while up to 6,221 individuals, equal to 2.6% of the total vulnerable residents in the target area, are currently disposing their faeces in open spaces. Finally, in terms of hygiene, almost all the population in need is reported to experience a critical/extreme need.Moving from the figures listed in this section, the proposal aims at providing a comprehensive WASH response to improve the access to the WASH services for the vulnerable residents of Sharg An Neel in Khartoum State.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The Action will consider the project’s environmental footprint and possible adaptations or mitigations to limit a negative impact. Local resources and expertise as well as a greening approach in procurements of all the Items will be favoured. Overall, the proposal prioritises the rehabilitation of existing WASH infrastructures to the construction of new ones, whenever feasible and without affecting the quality of the intervention. Solarisation will be favoured, when applicable, for water sources and sanitation facilities (i.e., solar lamps). The response will also include sensitisation sessions on WASH-related DDR subjects, to be conducted both at community/local committee level and at HH level.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The Program envisages the specific needs faced by different socio-demographic groups direct and encourages gender-balanced participation of target communities– with a zero-tolerance policy towards discrimination– throughout the Action, which is embedding the “Do No Harm Principle” and follows a conflict-sensitive approach. Preparation Phase:- Before each intervention, ACTED conducts an assessment of the security context and protection risks, followed by a regular monitoring of the situation throughout the project’s lifespan. - The whole community will be sensitised on the scope and the activities of the project and fully informed on the selection criteria, which have been designed to address the protection risks and cover the sectoral needs of the most vulnerable (single women, PLWS, children, elders, PwDs, HHs with no source of income, HHs with PwDs, HHs with more children under 5 years old). Program Implementation Phase:- ACTED mainstreams gender-adapted and key protection messages during its community sensitization and trainings. - ACTED will ensure easy-to-access and gender-segregated latrines, with lockable doors and solar lamps. Sanitation facilities will be suitable for elders and PwDs. - MHM component included in the proposal. - Distances to fetch water, waiting time, safety during traveling will be addressed through rehabilitations and ad hoc protection measures. - ACTED will set-up a gender-balanced Program team to ensure a safer environment, improve protection mainstreaming and favour inclusionary mechanisms. In all activities, ACTED will promote an equal gender participation. - First aid kits and protective wear are also provided to ACTED’s staff, beneficiaries and daily workers, in line with the type of activities. Accountability:- ACTED has an independent Appraisal, Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU) with experience in setting up Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRMs). A dedicated staff will be responsible to manage the CRM at Area office and Field office levels, where beneficiaries can report complaints/feedback in a safe and confidential way. Beside its staff permanently deployed in the field, ACTED will also conduct regular spot checks. ACTED will evaluate if the complaint was triggered due to flaws, shortcomings or gaps in procedures or implementation strategy. In addition to case-by-case handling, anonymized complaints reports will be shared internally to possibly reshape the ongoing Action and inform the future projects’ design. A more detailed description of the CRM is provided under the AAP section of this proposalHuman Resources - ACTED has a Code of Conduct that is consistent with the U.N. Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Task Force on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse in Humanitarian Crises. ACTED also complies with The Code of Conduct for The International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement and the UNHCR Code of Conduct.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

As part of its accountability strategy, ACTED will mainstream protection and PSEA elements during all activities, particularly trough safety audit tools, CRM and inclusion of PSEA training modules into capacity development action plans for community governance structures. ACTED has appropriate and proportionate procedures and controls in place to prevent, report, detect, respond and report on safeguarding issues & allegations.Recruitment- ACTED prevents employment/deployment of unsuitable individuals through rigorous screening. People employed by ACTED already have obligation to read and sign the code of conduct and internal policies. Administration and employee cycle:- Signature of Code of Conduct for staff; - Monitoring of trial periods for cases detected,- Disciplinary measures follow-up on cases detected in conjunction with country coordination. Training program related to safeguarding issues. - ACTED has an in-house training related to safeguarding issues: i) Annual training on the Code of Conduct and Policies in all countries and at HQ for 7000 staff; ii) Pre-departure training for international employees. Iii) Specific training on SEA & Child Protection in intervention countries.ACTED Complaints and response mechanisms. - ACTED will establish Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) for beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback in a secure way, and receive response in a timely manner. A dedicated staff will be responsible to manage CRM permanently installed in Area Office as well as in all target locations throughout the project. At the beginning and during implementation, target communities will be sensitized on the mechanism, and on how to submit complaints. FGDs shall be organised. Complaints will be confidentially recorded and reported each month at country-level. CRM will ensure accountability to beneficiaries’ and build trust between ACTED and community, pay specific attention to gender-related concerns, and improve programs quality, moving from intention to prevent/mitigate any adverse social/environmental/health risks.Complaint’s investigation & disciplinary measures- ACTED has capacity to investigate safeguarding issues in an independent and confidential way, while protecting both the alleged victim and the alleged perpetrator (ACTED SEA Policy, Articles 19 to 21; ACTED Child protection policy, Articles 20 to 22). ACTED must always ensure that the author of a report does not suffer from any harm as a result. ACTED also endeavours to protect victims of prohibited acts (ACTED SEA policy, article 23). ACTED has psychologists available to support individuals who would have been impacted as a result of a failure to comply with ACTED's safeguarding policies. The HQ HR department will assess allegations to design an investigation plan and select an investigation team. Depending on investigation conclusions, different measures can be taken including disciplinary actions and criminal prosecution.
