Project Summary

The humanitarian crisis in the Southwest region of Cameroon has exposed the local population to large scale protection risks and human rights violations and impacted vulnerable households’ capacity to meet their emergency food needs. Through this Action, the IRC seeks to improve the protection status of vulnerable people affected by the crisis in North West and South West Cameroon, assisting the most vulnerable people to stay safe, access assistance, recover from violence and claim their rights. The IRC will do this through an integrated program of Protection prevention and response activities to mitigate further protection risks resulting from the use of negative coping mechanisms.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Cameroon continues to be impacted by three complex, protracted, humanitarian crises resulting in structural development deficits and chronic vulnerabilities that further impede the long-term recovery of affected people. Humanitarian needs continue to be extensive with an estimated 1 1.5 million people in need in the North-West (NW) and South-West (SW) regions . The ongoing security crisis in the NW/SW has resulted to the displacement of 1 million people. Protection partners reported increased GBV cases with 869 incidents in August and September, mostly in the Menchum and Mezam divisions in the NW and in Fako division in the SW. The cases reported include: physical assault, emotional abuse, denial of resources, rape, sexual assault, and forced marriages with survivors of GBV incidents being mostly women, (97%), including 49% IDPs. Children constitute 22% of survivors with a majority being unaccompanied and separated children (19.5%). In addition, 3% of survivors are persons with disabilities. (OCHA situation report). The ongoing violence, combined with pre-existing socio-economic vulnerabilities, endemic poverty, and cultural and traditional norms, presents significant risks for women and girls and boys. Women and girls are specifically exposed to sexual violence, rape and forced and early marriage and physical assault but also domestic violence and emotional abuse. Grave forms of child rights violations have continued to be reported including 9 attacks on education in August and September; abduction of 15 teachers in the NW and release after payment of ransom ; abduction of 24 children (18 in Boyo division NW and 6 in Bolifamba near Buea (SW) and the partial burning of 3 schools in the SW, amongst other grave rights violations. Prolonged absence of schooling exposes children to immediate protection risks such as child labour, trafficking, recruitment/use by armed actors, drug abuse, and developmental risks such as delayed entry into higher education or the job market because they lack the right set of skills. With the proposed Action, IRC will respond to the most urgent protection needs in underserved and hard-to-reach communities in NWSW, through increased access to basic services for the most vulnerable and at-risk individuals and the reinforcement of local capacities to build a safer environment.
