Project Summary

The need to sustain sexual and reproductive health “SRH” in Gaza, remains very high,
considering the prevailing instability and its impact on the health system at large. As evidenced in the 2023 HNO, 245,000 women and girls were identified as high vulnerability of high-risk pregnancy, anemic mothers, female adolescents, and women with unmet need for family planning etc.
This project is aligned with HNO 2023 Health Cluster Objectives 1 & 2 linked to Strategic
Objectives 2 & 3 respectively. The project contributes to preventing excessive maternal
morbidity and mortality, which have markedly deteriorated last year. The project will
increase access to SRH care and nutrition services and information, including family planning, female reproductive cancers, and ensure that safe delivery and emergency obstetric and newborn care services are available with specific needs of people with disabilities (PwD) and protection/GBV needs are mainstreamed.
The project will complement previous and ongoing UNFPA programs/interventions, covered by Canada and Japan etc., by expanding the coverage of health system capacity to ensure continuation of essential SRH and maternal care services at primary and secondary health care levels and community.
The project will:
- Ensure continuation of SRH services, enhance access to quality SRH care and strengthen SRH management and counseling, including the operationalization of relevant protocols and strategies i.e. ongoing SRH strategy for Gaza.
- Build the capacity of health providers in lifesaving SRH issues with a focus on anemic
pregnant and lactating women, high-risk pregnancy, detection and management of
gynecological and pre-menopausal disorders etc.
- Strengthen the preparedness and response of the health facilities, fill gaps and replenish
lifesaving SRH equipment, drugs, commodities, and supplies including contraceptives.
- Actively engage communities, support coping mechanisms, sensitization, awareness raising and outreach including amplifying midwives' role through midwifery-led home visits.
- Support linking and informing communities on available services, including protection
from GBV and psychosocial support, subsidies, and cash vouchers etc.,
with special focus on marginalized communities and vulnerable groups including people with disabilities (PwD), young girls, poor and elderly people etc.
- Improve coordination of SRH services among various stakeholders/providers, including
mainstreaming disability, adolescents’ needs, protection and GBV in SRH service provision
and community outreach.
While this project is designed as an immediate response to address urgent and critical needs of Gazan women and young girls, especially those in marginalized areas, it will also support men and other vulnerable adolescents and strengthen the preparedness and resilience of both the community and the health system in absorbing shocks, saving lives and recovering from crises. UNFPA will work with MOH, Palestinian NGOs as needed to implement this project.
UNFPA is in coordination with active partners i.e. WHO, UNICEF, PMRS and MAP-UK to ensure complementarity of this project with their intended interventions.

1 - Needs

Please describe the needs your projects aims to address: The population in Palestine remains vulnerable with structural disadvantage due to the protracted crisis emanating from the ongoing Israeli occupation and the intra-Palestinian political division between the West Bank and Gaza. Coupled with the shifting regional dynamics, the profound economic crisis facing the Palestinian Authority, falling aid influx and donor fatigue, 2.1 million of Palestinian people are dependent on some form of humanitarian assistance, of these, 64% (1.3 million people) are in Gaza. Poverty rates have risen since 2020, reaching 28.9%. which represents approximately 1.4 million people living in poverty (OCHA- oPt Key Facts and Figures May 2022).Gazan women, men, girls, and boys face a range of serious protection and humanitarian threats including threats to life, liberty, and security. Gender-based violence (GBV) continues to be a key protection and health concern in Gaza, further impeding women’s and girls’ access to resources including lifesaving SRH services, counseling, and information. According to MSNA 2022, almost all the 90% of households seeking health care have reported experiencing a barrier to access health care. Around 77% of these cases of barriers relate to the high cost of services. These persisting challenges, compounded by the frequent military escalations such as in May 2021 and August 2022, have led to a serious deterioration in the availability and quality of health services and are limiting access to basic SRH care. Even during emergencies, SRH including maternal health and FP, GBV and protection services are essential and must be sustained as they are central to the health, rights and well-being of women and girls. However, this becomes a challenge in light of the recent shift in attention and critical resources away from this type of service provision as revealed by research studies on how unmet need for family planning is generally greater among more fragile countries (UNFPA/2020: Impact of Public Health Emergencies on SRH and Reproductive Rights in the Arab Region: The COVID - 19 Case). The 2023 HNO has identified SRH needs for about 245,000 people of vulnerable groups including high risk pregnancies, pregnant and lactating women, women with unmet needs for family planning, and female adolescents etc. There is also an apparent lack of life sustaining SRH equipment, drugs, and disposables, as they are currently under-prioritized and inadequately funded. According to MOH September 2022 drugs report, 43% of essential MCH drugs are at zero stock level and 25 % will only cover a period of 3 months, which further increases the risk of death and disability as well as increases GBV for women and girls. A study by Women Affairs Center, supported by UNFPA on the impact of the May 2021 escalation on women, revealed that women, especially pregnant and lactating and those of chronic diseases including breast cancer survivors, have suffered from poor nutrition and inability to access health facilities to get their medications and needed health care services. Sexual and Reproductive Health issues continues to be of concern in Gaza, with instable maternal mortality ratios, as indicated by the rise of maternal deaths mounting to 34 cases, representing a national ratio of 60/100,000 compared to 24.2/100,000 in 2020. It’s worth mentioning that COVID-19 impact has significantly contributed to this rise. (MoH 2021 annual report).One in four pregnant women are registered as high-risk pregnancies. High-risk pregnancy, ANC and cesarean sections are reflections of quality of care that needs to be improved as to avert maternal mortality and morbidity. Cesarean section “CS'' rate increased to 25.5, compared to the WHO recommendation of 10-15%, increased percentages of Anemia among pregnant women mounted to 40.5% (MoH Gaza Annual Report 2021). Family planning services are of a particular concern, especially for IUD insertion, that is severely affected in closures, especially during peaks of pandemic or escalations, and when Health providers shifted to telemedicine approach. Unmet need for family planning has risen from 10.9 to 12.9 between 2014 and 2019, (MICS 6, PCBS 2019). Stock outs of 1-3 of family planning commodities continue to be reported in major health clinics (MoH Gaza 2022). High-risk pregnancy, ANC and CS are reflections of quality of care that needs to be improved as to avert maternal mortality and morbidity. Ensuring continuation and accessibility of essential SRH services in Gaza, given the instability of the situation along with the shortages of essential drugs and supplies, as well as human resources, becomes more needed than ever. There is a growing need to increase access to health care, by addressing its main barriers including the financial cost of medicine and services, which was reported among the major barriers to access health services in Gaza (MSNA/HNO 2022). In addition to enhancing coordination among various players, as recommended by a high-level technical mission in 2021 by a senior UNFPA humanitarian expert from the humanitarian office based in New York, who met with senior MOH management and major SRH providers and delivered MISP TOT training to a group of health providers. Accordingly, UNFPA is considering recruiting a dedicated SRH humanitarian coordinator to support and strengthen coordinated SRH needs, response, preparedness, information & networking in Gaza. Through this project, UNFPA and its partners are aiming to strengthen the resilience and coping mechanisms and support the well-being of people, especially women and young and adolescent girls. 2 - Activities

Please describe the activities that make up your project: Major components of this project have been supported, in part, by Japan and Canada as part of HRP2022. This project will build on previous work in line with ongoing UNFPA interventions, both under humanitarian interventions as well as joint development projects with WHO and UNICEF funded by AFD. UNFPA along with its partners will target major maternity and PHC facilities, through provision of essential SRH equipment, instruments and lifesaving pharmaceuticals and supplies including family “FP” planning commodities. Additionally, interventions will focus on expanding the method mix, based on the results of the ongoing assessment by UNFPA in Palestine, expected to be released early 2023, training of health providers on quality SRH services, counseling and launching community outreach campaigns. The project is designed to reach out to vulnerable groups, as identified by HNO 2023: PLW with Anemia, high risk pregnant women, and women with reproductive cancers, young girls, and men etc. In addition to supporting continuation of SRH services at MOH and major providers, the project will increase and support vulnerable people’s access to SRH services (imaging, lab tests and other procedures) through health vouchers/subsidies. Outreach will include awareness raising, targeting tens of thousands of people, including men, and adolescents (boys and girls) in vulnerable areas of the Gaza strip, through sessions, media, social media and other venues. Through this project, and building on lessons learned, and previous achievements, UNFPA will respond to urgent needs in SRH services, by filling gaps and preventing system collapse through sustaining and scaling lifesaving SRH services, providing critically needed supplies, community engagement including men and boys, and capacity building of staff and enhancing SRHcoordination and reporting among various actors in Gaza. Practically, and to enrich the intervention and expand its coverage, UNFPA will work on the following domains: ⮚ Support SRH coordination and networking humanitarian platform by active SRH actors and active linking to Health Cluster planning & reporting mechanisms. ⮚ Improve emergency preparedness and response of SRH needs in the health system andthe community in the Gaza Strip.⮚ Strengthen access to lifesaving SRH services, through supporting major maternities and PHC facilities, with critically needed equipment/instruments, drugs and supplies including family planning commodities “introducing new methods i.e., hormonal IUD, implants etc.”, as per the ongoing assessment, supplements, Iron and folic acid to support the response, and capacity building of staff including on counseling and adaptive service provision skills (tele-medicine etc.)⮚ Support preconception antenatal, premenopausal care and family planning services & information, mainstreaming disability, GBV and psychosocial support, prevention of sexual exploitation activities “PSEA” trainings of staff.⮚ Enhance proper referral of SRH services among various providers and levels ofcare. As chair of GBV sub-cluster, ensure appropriate linkages and referral of GBVdetection and management in the health facilities and the community, supporting accessof vulnerable women to specialized health services through subsidies and linking them toprotection services and women empowerment programmes i.e., cash voucher etc.⮚ Support networks of community activists and implement community mobilizationcampaigns including health education/awareness component, that will target the issuesfrom a community and women empowerment perspective via educating families; men,women, boys and girls on basic gender and human rights issues, including women/girlswith disability and their personal hygiene and RH needs. In addition to consequences “health and social” of early marriage and promotion of health outcomes and reproduction, supporting positive participation on relief and preparedness to emergencies will also be among the suggested themes. Activities/targets: 1. Provision of lifesaving SRH needs; drugs & disposables, equipment, instruments, and medical furniture to 10 (4 MOH and 3 NGOs) maternity and 40 PHC clinics (27 MOH and 13 NGOs).2. Improve the capacity/skills of 200 health providers, practitioners on various SRH issues including: Preconception care, ANC, FP including the new method mix, EmONC, infection prevention, MISP, and management of gynecological and pre-menopausal disorders, BC and other SRH issues. The training will be based on standard developed protocols by MOH and in accordance with the needs endorsed by the capacity building committee of MOH.3. Support access of vulnerable women to SRH services through health vouchers/subsidies. / 600 women 4. Provide midwifery counselling services and psychosocial support to women, men and girls “in households ” in 5 Gaza governorates, including distribution of supplements, linking/referrals to specialized heath, GBV, and women empowerment service. i.e., distribution of dignity kits, cash vouchers program etc. building on UNFPA’s and implementing partners’ experience and partnership with WFP cash voucher system through projects funded by Japan supplementary budget, CERF and HPF. 12600 home visits/18,000 people reached. 5. Support SRH coordination platform of major actors in Gaza to ensure complementarity, share experience, and sustain regular reporting and monitoring of gaps. increase access to SRH services and information in Gaza. 6. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The project components are based on national humanitarian plans, in accordance withIHL, and are guided by international guidelines on sexual and reproductive health (SRH) in emergencies, such as the SPHERE Standards, as well as the Minimal Initial Service Package (MISP) for reproductive health in emergencies agreed upon by the Inter-Agency Working Group on Reproductive Health in Crises. UNFPA takes the principles of “Do No Harm” seriously within its programmes including with its implementing partners to ensure safety and dignity of all served beneficiaries including women, girls and disabled people in lie with the international IASC standards and principles including GBV: Minimum Standards for Prevention and Response to Gender-based Violence in Emergencies. In addition, the project planned interventions including health services will be provided in a manner that keeps all service recipients away from any potential to face any hazards or threats, physically and emotionally

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project is targeting all Palestinians in need of SRH healthcare, without any discrimination based on gender, refugee status, disability, or ethnic background, in all Gaza Strip with a special focus on vulnerable and marginalized communities.Provision of supplies will be through the central stores of the project implementing partners and will be monitored by UNFPA according to internal M&E procedures. Areas for community outreach will be carefully chosen in coordination with implementing partners’ affiliated active CBO’s and key informants in the area to ensure free access of women, girls and disabled people to the sessions and available services supported by the project. Participation of both women and men in training and other capacity-building activities within the framework of the project will be strictly applied and monitored by UNFPA through field visits and requesting disaggregated data reporting.
