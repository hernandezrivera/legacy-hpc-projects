Project Summary

About 4% of the 5.5 million people in need of urgent humanitarian health assistance in South Sudan are from Twic East and Duk. These two are one of the counties currently facing severe flooding and frequent conflicts in Jonglei state. 20% and 30% are respectively in catastrophic and extreme need of health care assistance.
The flood waters, particularly in Twic East, never receded but has continued to increase in depth and width ongoing from 2020. This has caused precarious and protracted displacement of populations, destruction of health facilities, water hygiene and sanitation facilities, shelters and impediment of humanitarian aid delivery resulting to high disease outbreaks, low immunization coverage, poor hygiene and sanitation practices, with insufficient access to safe drinking water leading to water-borne diseases such as diarrheal diseases, snake bites and risks of cholera. The populations also face epidemics such as infections including malaria, risks of measles and Hepatitis E. All these have and continue to cause excess morbidity and mortality. Of the people most in need of health care services, 50% are female (including women of childbearing age:15-49 years), 52% are children (under 5 boys and girls, 6-17 boys and girls) and 15% are persons with disabilities. Due to multiple shocks and vulnerabilities, affected populations are at risk of or experience violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect including GBV and mental illness, especially women and girls. COVID-19 exacerbated scale and urgency of needs in a country where immunization coverage is the lowest.
With the existing capacity of using its own motor speedboats and over 2 years’ experience operating Panyagor County Hospital and mobile health services in over 28 islands of Twic East and Duk, CINA is technically and operationally positioned in 2022 to reach 14,000 vulnerable populations (including 1410 male children under 5 years, 1502 female children under 5 years, 2268 male children 5-17 years, 2100 female children 5-17 years, 2254 male adults 18-60 years; 2450 female adults 18-60 years, 1001 male adults above 60 years, and 1015 female adults above 60 years, including 15% (2100) persons with disabilities), both from the IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities.
Proposed interventions will include improving access to equitable lifesaving essential quality healthcare services including maternal, child and adolescent health services, sexual and reproductive health, treatment of common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services. These will be provided through static (1 hospital, 2 PHCCS, 4 PHCUs and 8 mobile health services), procurement of essential medical kits and training of health workers. To decrease excess morbidity and mortality rates from malaria, diarrhea, acute respiratory infections and vaccine preventable illnesses such as measles, CINA will step up immunization against vaccine preventable diseases and procurement of outbreak response commodities and emergency antigens for immunization and cold chain commodities; increase community service provision linking with facility-based services using Boma Health Initiative and ICCM; conduct IPC in health facilities through regular assessment using scorecards, provision of supplies and protocols and continuous monitoring of WASH in health facilities as well as enhancing epidemic disease surveillance, prevention and control including outbreak detection, investigation and response. To enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and copying mechanisms, CINA will conduct regular community consultative meetings, implement feedback and ensure compliance; provide technical and operational assistance to CHDs through supportive visits to ensure quality provision of health services as well as coordinating response with other humanitarian-development-peace partners on ground to promote synergies and optimize use of available resources.

1 - Needs

A population of 222,648 people in need of health care services in Twic East and Duk counties is without a doctor (Health Cluster HRP targets 2022). The health care system entirely relies on humanitarian assistance since the outbreak of conflicts in 2013. The drivers of acute healthcare needs include floods, food insecurity and conflicts causing limited health facility functionality and capacity and poor coverage as almost all health facilities are ravaged by floods. Women, children, elderly and persons with disabilities are particularly vulnerable and most frequently face limited access to health services. Only three of 21 health facilities are providing full package of BPHNS in Duk and Twic East, with only 42% and 20% of populations in Duk and Twic East having 1 hours’ time access to health facility respectively (HNO 2022). This implies inadequate access and unavailability of health services. Meanwhile, Malaria is the leading cause of morbidity and mortality, accounting for 52.6 per cent of all morbidities and 58.2 per cent of all mortalities in 2021 (HNO 2022).A report by FSNMS+ Qualitative Assessment indicated insecurity on the route to health facilities and closure of health facilities due to violence and flooding and long distance to facilities as the main barriers to accessing health care services by community members. Acute food insecurity, with Duk and Twic East being IPC4, with GAM rates of 26.61% and 13.83% respectively contribute to acute malnutrition among under 5 children and pregnant and lactating mother leading to poor health outcomes (Nutrition Cluster GAM rates June 2021). The IRNA October 2021 for Twic East reported that 58,069 people were displaced by floods to about 52 islands with populations in Panyagor displaced to schools, churches and government premises. Paliau, Pongborong and Nyuak PHCC sites, reported 970 cases of complicated malaria, 90 cases of acute respiratory infection, 14 cases of safe delivery, 12 cases of bilharzia, 13 cases of eye disease and 3 cases of snake bites in one month. There was a very low immunization coverage with Penta-valent, OPV, measles and TT and BCG vaccines due to lack of vaccine supply. This has made the under 5 boys and girls vulnerable to vaccine preventable diseases. In Duk county, an IRNA done in December 2021 indicated that 27,006 IDPs and host communities were living in the islands, displaced due cattle raids and abductions violence that occurred in July 2020 where 43 people were reportedly killed, 37 people wounded and others went missing. This was shortly followed by devastating floods in 2020 and 2021 which heavily hit the islands.Access to BEmONC is very low as only 1 facility, each in Duk and Twic East is availing these services. This contributes to pregnancy related and new-born morbidity and mortality. Teenage pregnancy is estimated at 30% among girls 13-19 years, and there is low rate of contraceptive uptake 2020 (FP 2020) among women of childbearing age (15-49 years). There is wide spread gender inequality and gender-based violence, affecting girls 6-17 and women 18-60 years, occurring within communities, especially in Duk where displaced populations live in clustered settlements. These vulnerabilities and violence heightened mental illnesses and psychological distress especially among the most vulnerable women, elderly, adolescents and PWDs. To address the above needs, CINA will provide interventions in line with health cluster objectives: to improve access to equitable lifesaving essential quality healthcare services including maternal, child and adolescent health services, sexual and reproductive health, treatment of common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services; decrease excess morbidity and mortality rates from malaria, diarrhea, acute respiratory infections and vaccine preventable illnesses; and enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and copying mechanisms.

1. Provision of life-saving essential health care services including management of non-communicable diseases through static response ( by operating 1 hospital in Twic East, 2 PHCUs in Duk and Twic East, 4 PHCUs in Duk and Twic East) and 8 mobile health services in Twic East and Duk islands; procurement of essential medical kits and training of health workers.2. Promote humanitarian-peace-development nexus bringing humanitarian, peace and development actors together to promote synergies and optimize use of available resources.

7.4. Community Engagement Strategy

Yes

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

CINA will use its existing beneficiary feedback mechanism to adapt the project and ensure that it is addressing the needs of the community. To this end, CINA will involve the community in all the implementation of the project including the target criteria for beneficiaries and mechanisms for referral. Complaint boxes will be placed in health facilities, mobile clinics and during awareness raising and beneficiaries will be informed on how to use it. Regular project reviews will be conducted together with the affected population to ensure that the project is on track and is meeting the expectations of the community and target vulnerable groups.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CINA will use its existing community engagement strategies to ensure that the vulnerable members of the community (including persons with disability, elderly, women, widows, women-headed households, and girls, adolescents, unaccompanied and separated children, survivors of GBV, etc.) have their voices heard. This will be achieved by using the existing community feedback and complaints mechanisms structures (complaint boxes, M&E visits, project focal persons, community leaders, etc. ). All vulnerable community groups will be oriented of these complaints and feedback arrangements and will be encouraged to provide feedback through any of these structures.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CINA's Human Resource department makes appropriate recommendations to senior management on enhancing SEA prevention efforts. CINA has a PSEA policy and a PSEA Focal Person who is known to all staff, regular clients and beneficiaries. The contacts of the PSEA Focal Person are made widely shared. In addition, CINA conducts awareness-raising sessions on sexual exploitation and abuse for all staff and beneficiaries and other personnel in on a regular basis. All contracts for CINA staff, volunteers, consultants and clients include a clause on zero tolerance to SEA.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

This project has an activity on ensuring consultative community meeting regularly and implementing feedback mechanism and ensure compliance. To achieve this, CINA will ensure that the communities (both beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries) receive an up to date necessary information about the project and in exchange provide their feedback and/or complaints or response as part of accountability. To this end, CINA will train all staff implementing the project to ensure that they understand and have the capacity to implement the Accountability to Affected Populations Mechanism.
