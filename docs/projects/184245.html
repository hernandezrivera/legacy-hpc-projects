Project Summary

According to the 2021 post-Gu assessment conducted by FAO's Food Security and Nutrition Analysis Unit (FSNAU) -nearly 3.5 million people are expected to face the food consumption gap or depletion of livelihood assets across Somalia, with the situation likely to increase in the absence of humanitarian assistance. Moreover, the overall media Global Acute Malnutrition ( GAM) remained serious at 11.1 % weight-for-height z-score due to high mobility, low Vitamin A supplementation, low immunisation coupled with reduced food security, and access to milk. Kismayo district of Lower Juba region is among districts that host some of the most vulnerable and prostrated IDPs in Somalia, are characterised faces limited livelihood assets, low social support and highly dependent on external aid, faces moderate to large food consumption gaps throughout 2021. The poor urban in the district continue to face food consumption gaps over the same time due to poor economic activities and the rising cost of food and other essential items. The media prevalence of Global acute malnutrition (GAM) in Kismayo has remained serious in urban, and IDP at 11.4% and 11.1%, with SAM at 3.0% and 3.3%, respectively, with crude Mortality rate ( CDR), recorded at 0.27% in urban and o.47% in IDP, with morbidity of 11.7% in urban and 17.4% in IDP, with coverage of Vitamin A at 80.7% and 84.3% and measles vaccine at 72.8% and 79.1% respectively. In the Mudug region, the chronic nature of malnutrition has a devastating impact on child survival and development; the current estimated total acute malnutrition burden for acutely malnourished children stands at 90,840 under five boys and girls while severely acutely malnourished children in at 13,440 under five boys and girls ( FSNAU 2021 and Nutrition Cluster GAM prevalence data). In the Addun Pastoral community of Hobyo and South Jariiban district of Mugu region, the media prevalence of Global acute malnutrition (GAM) remain serious in urban and IDP at 10.1% with Severe Acute Malnutrition( SAM) rate of 1.1% SAM respectively, with morbidity of 14.0% with coverage of Vitamin A at 45.5% and measles vaccine at 72.8% and 79.1% reactively 48.9% respectively. The immediate causes of malnutrition include high mobility rate, poor dietary diversity, inadequate infant and young care practices and their mothers, household food insecurity, poor access to essential services, and poor infrastructure in some rural areas. SAF-UK will focus on rehabilitation and improving the nutritional status of children under the age of 5 and PLW (pregnant and lactating mothers) through integrated treatment, reliance building basic health and maternal services, including Expanded program for immunization (EPI), BSFP, hygiene promotion including hygiene kits. and protection activities. It combines therapeutic and supplementary, nutrition activities that prevent malnutrition and support optimal nutrition behavior as a long-term goal in building the resilience of the targeted communities, in line with organizations community strategy and Accountability to affected population (AAP) Framework. Programs will integrate with health, WASH and food security and livelihood activities. The project will employ and train staff (Men and women) including community workers(Men and women) through a transparent process. community volunteers (Men , women and youth of both sexes) will be engaged so as to ensure sustainability of interventions and inclusivity. Government officials will take lead in most key decisions and will be involves through the cycle for sustainability and contribution to capacity building. The programs will adhere to set government strategies and policies related to interventions , and will also contribute to achievement of cluster 2020 HRP working closely with government entities and the cluster.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

For IDP the main risk is displacement. For this, the SAF-UK has maintained a database for all beneficiaries with telephone contacts and that of relatives. For those in rural Mudug, the risk is spontaneous clan clashes. While it is difficult to deal with this, we have ensured that services are provided equitably for the different clans in their areas. Interclan conflicts, therefore, have minimal effect on our programs. in all locations, the organization will seek to provide safety nets by implementing BSFP. Victims of GBV will also be provided with an organizational GBV focal point to link survivors to appropriate assistance. Programs will also ensure IDP has access to essential services equitable and dignified with equality observed in job allocation in projects serving the IDP population.Staff recruitment is not based on tribe but professional competence; this has produced a team from a hybrid of cultures who have learned to work together well. Some positions are also reserved to the target communities and community groups that are marginalized like IDPs and women, so that equity is achieved. All gender will be awarded the opportunity to benefit inequitably. Women and youth who are mostly left out in recruitment will be considered in all projects, and positions will be awarded equitably. The needs assessment will include all community groups so that all conclusive, fair and agreeable evaluation is reached. both men and women leaders with an additional youth representative will be part of the community health board as per organizational policy. This will ensure decisions made are all-inclusive. SAF UK policies have sought to put men at the helm of needs affecting their wives, daughters and female relatives. The organization's APP framework seeks to ensure men participate through affirmative action for men ensuring that men are not excluded from interventions targeting women beneficiaries. Children who are often left out will not only be seen as beneficiaries, but their views on service delivery ad need will occasionally be sought by a professional child specialist so that the whole community participates in program design and implementation. Community diagnosis, community dialogues, evaluations and assessment will include all community groups to ensure all are reached. Discussions, assessments and conversations will consist of women, children and youth with their own forum so as to ensure their views are considered and used in programming. The programs far and hard to reach areas in rural settings will be served by mobile teams so as to reduce risks and hazards faced by these groups enroute to accessing services.
