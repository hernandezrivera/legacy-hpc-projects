Project Summary

According FSNAU in June and July 2021, the overall median Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) in Somalia remained Serious (11.1% weight-for-height z-score). The drivers of acute malnutrition include high morbidity, low immunization, low vitamin-A supplementation, reduced access to milk, and food insecurity. Urgent treatment and nutrition support are required for approximately 1.2 million children under the age of five years (total acute malnutrition burden), who will likely face acute malnutrition between August 2021 and July 2022, including 213 400 who are likely to be severely malnourished. Integrated interventions should be provided to support recovery and prevent deterioration in the nutrition situation’s Post GU Season September 2021, also indicates; the total Acute Malnutrition Burden Estimates by Region (August 2021-July 2022), Specified 52,490 children 6-59 months will be acutely malnourished while 7,550 children will be severely malnourished in Gedo region. Additionally, Areas in Gedo region especially Gedo riverine, Dollow urban, Juba riverine currently have or projected to have a GAM prevalence of Critical, indicated by a weight-for-height z-score of 15-29.9 percent or by a mid-upper arm circumference below 125 mm of 10-14.9 percent:
The Nutrition cluster estimates a caseload of 3099 SAM, 3099 MAM and 2019 PLWs with SAM in Bardhere District, 1011 SAM, 3944 MAM and 1000 PLW with SAM in Dollow, 1272 SAM, 3964 MAM and 1026 PLW with SAM in Ceel waaq District while in Garbaharey 1384 SAM, 5415 MAM and 1470 PLWs with SAM .
HDC is an active nutrition/health partner and currently implementing OTP, SC and EAC simplified protocol in partnership with UNICEF and SHF in Ceelwaq District running one SC, two static OTP centers and 7 mobile sites in Ceel Waaq District. Furthermore, HDC is implementing emergency primary health care in Bardhere district through three mobile outreach sites. In Garbaharey District HDC is also implementing emergency primary health project through Burdhubo health centre and Muuri and Abgalow outreaches. However, there are gabs of nutrition project in Dollow, Bardhere Garbaharey. At the same time The nutrition project in Ceel-waaq will end 30th April 2022. Therefore this project will address the identified gaps in nutrition, enhancing access to nutrition services within their localities aimed at improving the physical and mental well-being of boys, girls, and women in the targeted locations. These specific needs have been outlined in the HRP and FSNAU and UNOCHA mulita cluster need rapid assessment gaps identified in the targeted locations. The proposed project is geared towards reducing the vulnerability of the beneficiaries and communities. HDC will work with MOH-Federal, JSS-MOH to address the nutrition gaps identified. The services will be both preventive and curative targeting vulnerable women, boys, girls, and persons living with physical disabilities in the targeted district.
Mobile and outreach implementation models will be adopted to address the needs of vulnerable beneficiaries in remote and geographically isolated and hard to reach populations in Dollow, Bardhere, Garbaharey and Ceelwaq district. While in Urban centres static OTP facilities will be operationalized. Screening for malnutrition will be carried out and children with SAM will be treated in OTP sites. Children with SAM with medical complication will be referred to the SCs. Through this project, HDC is planning to maintain and support the existing nutrition services (8 fixed OTPs, 1 stabilization center in the Ceelwaq general hospital with integration of health projects and 4 mobile teams to reaching 20 villages comprising IDPs and Hard to reach rural population mostly affected by crises in Dollow, Bardhere, Garbaharey and Ceel Waaq districts. 1. - Projects activities

Project ActivitiesThis project aims to deliver an integrated package of life-saving nutritional humanitarian assistance to children under 5 years and pregnant and lactating mothers. The response package includes outpatient nutrition programs to reduce and/or prevent the rate at which children fall into SAM (Severe Acute Malnutrition), Outpatient Therapeutic Programme (OTP) to address the treatment of severe acute malnutrition completing the full cycle of addressing acute malnutrition so as to avoid excess mortality. HDC will ensure the treatment of complicated SAM cases through a referral mechanism. We will further coordinate with active organizations implementing WASH, Food Security, and Nutrition, Protection, and Education projects to ensure a multi-sectorial integrated response to address nutrition causal factors affecting the communities, conforming to the whole-of-system approach.Activity 1.1.1 screening of children (10,000 boys and 10,000 girls) 6-59 months of age and pregnant and lactating women (PLWs 12,000) for acute malnutrition. Activity 1.1.2: Treatment of children (4000 boys and 4000 girls) 6-59 months with Moderate acute malnutrition using EAC special criteria. Activity 1.1.3 treatment of pregnant and lactating women (PLW) with acute malnutrition (7316). Activity 1.1.4: Treatment of children (2000 boys and 2000 girls) 6-59 months with severe acute malnutrition using nutrient dense RUFT food and routine treatment of malnutrition related complications. Activity 1.1.5: Making referrals to SC of children with medically complicated SAM 400 (200 boys and 200 girls). Activity 1.1.6: Printing of 5000 IEC material on key messages related to nutrition, booklets, HMIS registers, posters, OTP cards, referral slips printed. Activity 2.1.1: Training of 80 (40 male, 40 female) nutrition staff on National IMAM Guideline. Activity 2.1.2: Formation of 40 mother to mother support groups each with 10 members consisting of lead mothers who are pregnant or lactating. Activity 2.1.3: Training of community nutrition volunteers and led mothers 120 (100 female and 20 male) on Infant and young child feeding. Activity 3.1.1: Giving 4000 pregnant and lactating women first individual counselling on appropriate IYCF/IYCF-E. Activity 3.1.2: Providing 4000 pregnant and lactating women with Iron folate supplementation. 3.3. providing children aged 0-23 months who received Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) counselling Women 71,316.3.4. 8,000 providing girls and boys aged 6-59 months of who received vitamin A supplement in semester 1.
