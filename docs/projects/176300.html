Project Summary

The objective of proposed intervention is to improve the overall food security situation by providing emergency food assistance as well protecting livelihoods of targeted populations. The interventions will also be carried out to build the resilient community by agriculture resilience approaches and strategies. Communities will also be provided with agricultural inputs and tools and coupled with training to enhance their learning and capacities on improving agriculture practices, harvesting methods, reduce post-harvest losses and seed multiplications.
The proposed FSL activities have been designed to improve the food security of people living in IPC 3 and higher locations by increasing access and availability of food, Household purchasing power by distributing agricultural inputs, provision of cash-based interventions (CBI), and encouraging resilience through the support of income generating activities (IGA) and skills trainings.

1 - Needs

South Sudan is experiencing unprecedented levels of acute food insecurity, with an IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) acute food insecurity indicators observed in Akoko, Aweil South, Pibor, Tonj East, Tonj North, and Tonj South. Furthermore, areas in Pibor County, in the Greater Pibor Administrative Area have been identified as IPC Phase 5 (Famine Likely). Recent assessments completed by DRC MRT found that food and access to food assistance was highlighted as the primary need by respondents, with reports that some assessment participants are consuming meals less than twice a week. For households in Duk Padiet and Bor South County displaced by flooding and conflict, key livelihood assets and crops were destroyed both during the flooding and the organized violence. Due to the impact of recent flooding on the host community, opportunities for casual labour employment in the local market is limited. The high levels of food insecurity as observed in areas such as Jonglei State could potentially serve as a trigger for continued organized violence, as hunger forces households to raid neighbouring communities for livestock and livelihood assets. Similarly, food insecurity will likely deteriorate should the levels and severity of organized violence observed in areas in Jonglei and Warrap States continue into 2021. As per OCHA , more than 1 million people have been affected by flooding since July with some 480,000 people displaced. The flooding resulted in significant crop losses in Unity and Upper Nile state with negative impacts on the food security situation of the affected population. As per HNO 2021, the increase in needs is largely driven by the rising food insecurity. When consulted, food insecurity or lack of food was identified as the primary challenge or one of the primary challenges faced by the majority of affected people across sex and age groups. Needs do not exist in a vacuum, however, as food insecurity weakens people’s health and nutritional status and exposes them to greater protection risks. . Among the most vulnerable people are newly displaced families; communities hosting large numbers of displaced and/or recently returned people; and households that are headed by a single parent or looking after older people or people with disabilities. It has been also observed that the spike in sub-national violence and floods triggered new displacements in last year and as per HNO 2021, Preliminary analysis shows that more than 230,000 people displaced to a new location during the first nine months of 2020, including new displacement and movement of existing IDPs to the new areas of displacement. An estimated 7.7 million people are expected to experience acute food insecurity and worse (IPC 3, 4 and 5) in 2021 according to the latest IPC analysis published in December 2020. This includes 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers who need FSL support in 2021. Of these, 50% are female, 54% children and 15% people living with disability. The number of people in need increased 15 per cent, compared to the 6.7 million people who were acutely food insecure in 2020. The number of counties in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) is projected by the peak of the lean season (May to July) to increase to 45 as compared to 28 in 2019. The same comparison cannot be made for the 2020 lean season due to the delayed data collection. However, the projection for October to November 2020, usually a time when people harvest, 34 counties are in Emergency (IPC Phase 4).

2 - Project activities

DRC will improve the food security of vulnerable and conflict affected households by providing emergency food assistance with partnership of WFP under Mobile response team. DRC MRT team has building experience on providing rapid multisectoral response country wide. Similarly, under partnership with WFP, DRC will provide conditional general food assistance through Food for Assets will improve the community infrastructures, flood dykes and swallow wells to channel the water for irrigation. These assets would be identified by communities based on their needs and provision of general food assistance will also increase the food production and access to diversified food.DRC will provide the emergency access to agricultural inputs to most vulnerable to increase the food production in Unity and Upper Nile states. Households will be prioritized who rely on the agriculture, have lost the agriculture tools due to the displacement and or don’t have self -capacity to buy the seeds and inputs. DRC will also provide the training on best agronomic practices to increase the food production, reduce the post -harvest damages and building on seed multiplication approaches. The intervention will ultimately increase Households self- reliance capacity and will reduce the dependency on humanitarian assistance in coming time. DRC will also encompass efforts to increase the local seeds demand in the respective counties to develop the market systems. DRC will also use the Cash & Voucher assistance through seed vouchers to enable the local procurements of seeds. Both crop and vegetable seeds would be provided through seed vouchers so that beneficiaries can buy seeds on their preferences.DRC will continue working with local agriculture extension workers to extend the services on promoting climate smart agriculture practices to increase the food production, promote local seeds and seed multiplications approaches, reduce post harvest losses and increased crop diversifications. As mentioned above in objective 02, DRC will promote to local procurement of seeds and support the seed fairs through seed vouchers enabling communities to buy their preferred seeds for both vegetables and crops. The intervention also be imbedded with best agriculture practices to reduce the post harvest and seeds perseveration by using the hermetic storage bags. DRC will also conduct the market assessments in Rubkona and Guit to understand the market systems, identification of key value chains ( agriculture, fisheries and livestock productions and will engage youth, market actors and farmers in order to provide them income opportunities. Building on previous experience on vocational and life skills, DRC will engage project development committees to identify the youth and women groups for vocational skills in Rubkona, Jamjang and Guit in community services centres. The variety of life skills courses i-e tailoring, solar maintenance, butchery, baking, garments, leather crafts, and start up kits will also be provided to students to start their business and earn livelihoods. In Jamjang, DRC will continue to work with Natural Resource Management committee to decrease the deforestation and introduce different variety of seedlings for tree plantation. DRC will also provide the trainings on natural resource management to communities to build the capacities on tree planting, agroforestry and climate change impacts. Building on experience on energy saving stoves, DRC will also provide basic training skills on promoting the use of energy saving stoves to reduce the risk of GBV. Women are often on risk when they collect firewood’s from far flung areas.The Community Service Centre (SSC) will function as joint community livelihood training centres and business hubs that will provide permanent demonstration bases for IGA and FSL activities.DRC will support the needs of vulnerable households and the demand of locally established producing cooperatives through vouchers.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

DRC conducts regular awareness raising with beneficiaries on PSEA, including reporting mechanisms, as well as provides information on PSEA to beneficiaries in multiple formats.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

DRC has several methods for lodging complaints which are accessible to most vulnerable members of the community including those that are not literate. These include community visits to receive complaints (oral and written), FCRM desks which are staffed by both male and female staff, having gender disaggregated FGD during PDM including youth participants, and individual interviews during PDMs. DRC also receives complaints through community representatives who are more accessible to beneficiaries. Other methods include suggestion boxes and in person office visits. Having several mechanisms allows for beneficiaries to choose a method that is most comfortable and practical for them and ensures there is at least one mechanism in place in all locations. Apart from the aforementioned channels, DRC will also gather feedback from the vulnerable groups through community meetings and surveys. DRC Staff have been trained to handle personal complaints and inquiries in a confidential manner, and protection issues are referred to DRC protection team or to protection partners to ensure sensitive issues are handled by qualified staff only.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

DRC will form the project development committees during the inception phase of the project and conduct meeting with targeted beneficiaries and key stakeholders to inform them on project activities, including criteria, beneficiaries entitlements and complaints and feedback mechanism. DRC will establish strong community engagement mechanism by engaging them at each stage of project i-e project start up phase, community meetings, project development committee formation, beneficiary registration & verification, entitlements sharing, orientation on Complaint Response Mechanism and ensure that communities are being provided feedback with their complaints.. DRC will also ensure to provide details of each activity through visibility banners and also information on complaint response mechanism broadly.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

During the design phase of the project, DRC engages community and other relevant stakeholders in the need assessments and uses their feedback to design the project activities based on their needs. The regular engagement of communities and stakeholders informs the project design, their prioritized activities and modality of assistance. DRC has a functional Feedback, Complaints, Response Mechanism (FCRM) that allows beneficiaries to give feedback and complaints regarding the activities being implemented in their respective locations. The DRC F-CRM desks are set up to receive complaints and questions from beneficiaries and follow-up with feedback, including referrals to external service-providers or to the DRC protection desks when appropriate, either immediately or within a reasonable timeframe, depending on the nature of the complaint. DRC has a standardized FCRM form where all feedback and complaints are captured. All data captured on the FCRM forms is then uploaded onto a password protected database where all feedback and complaints are tracked until they are closed. Furthermore, beneficiary complaints and feedback will also be captured when conducting post distribution monitoring (PDM). Beneficiaries will be able to provide their opinions on how DRC interventions can be improved and they will be given the opportunity to provide feedback or make a complaint.
