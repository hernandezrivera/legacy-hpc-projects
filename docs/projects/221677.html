Project Summary

Islamic Relief Worldwide- South Sudan is a Humanitarian International Organization that has been responding to the humanitarian crisis in South Sudan since 2003 providing assistance to crisis affected population through key thematic areas of WASH, Food Security and Livelihoods, Health, Nutrition, Gender, Protection and Inclusion. Islamic Relief South Sudan responds to include manmade and natural disasters (mainly flood & drought) and conflict-affected people. Islamic Relief has been actively involved in addressing Gender-Based Violence (GBV) and Protection issues in South Sudan, focusing on both immediate support for survivors and long-term prevention efforts. The organization’s work is rooted in a comprehensive, community-driven approach that ensures vulnerable populations—especially women, girls, and marginalized groups—receive the necessary protection and care. Islamic Relief's GBV and protection interventions in South Sudan align with several Global Development Goals, especially SDG 5 (Gender Equality), SDG 16 (Peace, Justice, and Strong Institutions), and SDG 3 (Good Health and Well-Being). Through its work to protect survivors, prevent violence, and promote gender equality, Islamic Relief supports the broader international agenda of building safer, more equitable societies, where all individuals—regardless of gender or background—can live free from violence and discrimination. Through this HRP, Islamic Relief South Sudan intend to respond to and address human suffering at the targeted counties through provision of capacity building support through increased skill building and retention of girls in schools, especially in marginalized areas. Stronger economic independence for women, contributing to reduced poverty and improved livelihoods. Reduction in harmful gender norms, with greater community support for women’s rights and gender equality. Improved health outcomes for women and girls, including better maternal health and protection from GBV. while integrating intervention with WASH, Nutrition, and Health sector. This HRP will be implemented in Kapoeta East and Kapoeta North Counties of Eastern Equatoria state. Warrap state, gogeril County. Therefore, this project “Building Capacity of Women and Girls’’ GBV services for Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), Returnees and Host Communities in Kapoeta East and Kapoeta North, Eastern Equatoria state and in Gogeril County of Warrap state in South Sudan intends to achieve the following objectives:
1. Increase skill capacity in women and girls, especially in marginalized mentioned areas
2. Stronger economic independence for women, contributing to reduced poverty and improved livelihood.
3. Reduction in harmful Gender norms, with greater community support for women’s right and Gender equality.
4. Improved health outcome s for women and girls including maternal health and protection from GBV.
The proposed project will cover 21,850 beneficiaries (Men 5,462, Women 6,118, Boys 5,026, girls 5,244, PWD 1,093)

1. Needs

In 2024, the Protection Cluster and areas of responsibilities fully aligned the data and information gathering and analysis processes with revised global guidelines gathered in the Protection Analytical Framework and streamlined South Sudan’s Protection Risk Monitoring System. Based on the Q2 and Q3 PRMS assessments, the cluster and areas of responsibility (AoR) identified the top protection risks faced by the people of the country. The most prevalent risks include Gender-based Violence, Forced Eviction, Child Marriage and Family Separations, Discrimination in Accessing Resources, Attacks on Civilians and Restrictions of Freedom of Movement, alongside a significant presence of other risks. In 2024, the Protection Cluster and AoRs in South Sudan fully aligned its HNO/HRP processes with the revised global guidance and model in determining the population exposed to top protection risks. Out of 11.29 million affected populations (IDPs, Returnees, Residents), the model predicts that approximately 9.18 million people (81% of the affected population) are exposed to at least one of the top five protection risks. Further analysis shows that 98% of returnees (840K people) are the most protection risk-exposed population group, followed by IDPs at 81% (1,58M). In accordance with the HCT strategic guideline, the Protection Cluster provided data and justifications for the prioritization process of SSD counties. 39 counties and the Abyei Administrative Area (AAA) were classified as priority 1 and 2 counties and cluster partner organizations are advised to submit projects to the HPC covering targeting needs for the duration of 12 months. 21 counties have been labelled as priority 3 counties and humanitarian support should extended to the duration of up to six months. 18 SSD counties have been de-prioritized, and partners will not be expected to submit project proposals in those counties. Building on high-level analysis derived from community-based information gathering through the PRMS tools (Key Informant Interviews, Focus Group Discussions, Protection Risk Assessments, observation assessments, and AoR Expert Assessments), along with the strategic goals set by the HCT and the JIAF 2.0-based inter-sectoral assessment under the HPC, the Protection Cluster and AoRs in South Sudan have identified 5.34 million people in need (PiN) and advocate targeting 1.47 million individuals across population groups as outlined below: Based on IRNA findings, majority (68.2%) of the sampled households rated their communities as safe while 29.7% of the households rated their communities as unsafe and 2.1% of the households were neutral about the situation in their communities. Analysis by County of sampled household showed more households in Gogrial East felt their communities were unsafe compared to other Counties (Gogrial East=60%; Tonj North=25.2%; Wau=1.4%). In addition, more female headed households felt their communities are unsafe compared to male headed households (FHH=43.2%; MHH=19.1%). Intercommunal differences (44.1%) and unemployed youth (37.1%) were the two main causes of violence in the communities as mentioned by the assessed households. Analysis by County of sampled household also exhibited similar causes of violence with exception of Wau. majority (66.8%) of the assessed households mentioned there had been no victim of gender-based violence (GBV) in their community in the last 1 year whereas 33.2% (Gogrial East=42.0%; Tonj North=47.6%; Wau=8.7%) of the households reported there had been victims of GBV in their community in the last 1 year. The project will be implemented following the channel of hope methodology and will address the following. Strengthening local capacities of the community leaders, and local authorities through trainings on gender related issues, skill building activities, PSS session, Case management, Awareness raising on GBV related issues.
