Project Summary

Past successive drought seasons and a combination of intermittent conflicts between clans of South and North Galkayo and impacts of successive seasons of above normal rains (Deyr 2019 and Gu 2020) that caused serious flooding and destroyed crops coupled by desert locust invasion and economic repercussion of impacts of COVID-19 containment measures has significantly weakened the already precarious food security situation and aggravated malnutrition rates among boys and girls aged under 5 years and PLWs in Hobyo and South Galkayo IDP camps. The situation is compounded by anticipated La Niña conditions associated with projected high risk that drought conditions will develop by early 2021. (July/August 2020 post-Gu assessment).
Nutrition situation in South Galkayo IDP camps remains high with GAM 15.6% and SAM 3.3% (Post Gu Seasonal Food Security and Nutrition Assessment report 30 September, 2020). The situation is exacerbated by limited and poorly distributed nutrition and health centers owing to long distance covered to access the services coupled by poor breast feeding patterns among pregnant lactating women (PLWs),poor diet diversity and complimentary feeding among boys and girls aged 6-59 months as well as limited access to safe water and poor hygiene conditions and practices attributed to frequent cases of water borne diseases that worsen already precarious malnutrition status of Pregnant Lactating Women (PLWs) and boys and girls aged 6-59 months. The worst affected are IDPs and very vulnerable host communities living under poor conditions and rely on marginal unreliable livelihood strategies with significantly high malnutrition rates having lost all their livelihood assets to the current climatic shocks. Nutrition analysis in the project targeted locations showed an identical malnutrition rates of boys and girls at the rate of 50-50% with no clear cut difference in terms of their age as well as Pregnant Lactating Women (PLWs) hence need to prevent further escalation. The project targets Severely acute and Moderate malnourished 3500 children (1750 boys and 1750 girls) aged 6-59 months and 1062 Pregnant Lactating Women (PLWs).
Of particular concern are women, girls and people with disabilities that are further affected common cases of violations and discrimination when accessing humanitarian assistance hence need for their increased protection and improved consultation and inclusion of people with disabilities in the project planning phase as well as other subsequent implementation stages including monitoring to ensure that their needs are covered and that they equally benefit from the project.
The project is aimed at providing equitable access to quality lifesaving curative nutrition services among boys and girls (0-59) months & PLW through systematic identification, referral and treatment of acutely malnourished cases as well as Strengthen lifesaving preventive nutrition services for vulnerable population groups (marginalised women, girls and children as well as people with disabilities) focusing on improving nutritional status through provision of supplementary nutrition products, appropriate infant and young child feeding practices in emergency and micronutrient interventions through the most the most appropriate feeding and nutrition treatmentand ensure evidence based Basic Nutrition Services Package (BNSP) linked to Nutrition, Health, WASH, Food security and protection in the targeted locations in South Galkayo and Hobyo Districts, Mudud Region Somalia

Project Activities

1. Screening of 3500 children (1750 boys and 1750 girls) aged 6-59 months and 1062PLWs and treated through OTP/SFP program 2. Promotion of optimum IYCF targeting 1062 PLWs with emphasis to exclusive breastfeeding in project locations 3. Prevention and management of common diseases (anemia, diarrhea, malaria etc) through Vitamin A supplementation, deworming, multiple micro nutrients, supplements, iron/folate supplements and dissemination of hygiene promotion messages and practices to the targeted 3500 children (1750 boys and 1750 girls) aged 6-59 months and 1062 girls) aged between 6-59 months and 1062 Pregnant Lactating Women 4. Training of 40 OTP/SFP staff (20 men and 20 women) as well as volunteers in case detection throughMUAC measurements and weight measures 5. Training of 60 Community health workers (30 female and 30 male) on nutrition education and to enhance their knowledge in Malnutrition to mitigate increasing malnutrition cases among children aged 6-59 months and PLW 6. Conducting 12 Community education sessions for 100 community nutrition volunteers (50 female and 50 male) to increase awareness on malnutrition
