Project Summary

DRC will implement activities that support conflict and displacement-affected people in regaining their economic wellbeing: from meeting basic needs in emergency contexts to recovering and/or strengthening sustainable and resilient livelihoods; and with a strong focus on scaling up the use of cash and voucher assistance in support of these outcomes.
The concept of graduation underpins DRC’s approach to economic recovery. Across all activities, DRC aims at graduating people of concern from assistance to self-reliance and resilience or, in other words, to being able to cover their needs independently of external assistance and in a dignified and sustainable manner. DRC will target 23,586 beneficiaries in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States.
In order to ensure that the DRC programme response is relevant and appropriate it will be required to understand and address the needs of the different primary groups who are displaced and otherwise conflict-affected: - IDPs in host communities (official and spontaneous) - Returnees (from internal displacement or voluntary/forced repatriation) - Host Communities (hosting IDPs or receiving returnees). DRC strives to address tailored economic recovery needs of people affected by displacement. DRC will have a specific focus for women and youth in the economic recovery interventions designed for the proposed project.

1 - Needs

The number of people in need of urgent assistance in north-east Nigeria rose from 7.9 million at the beginning of 2020 to 10.6 million since the onset of COVID-19. Despite challenges, aid workers have already reached over 3.6 million people with life-saving assistance in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states since the beginning of the year. According to the Humanitarian Situation update of November 2020; The economic crisis as a result of the COVID-19 pandemic is affecting communities and local economies in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states. Lockdown measures and movement restrictions disrupted livelihood opportunities during the time and the effects of the disruption have been worsened by the lean season, inflation and market price increases of food and non-food items. Reduction of basic services and livelihoods are exacerbating the vulnerabilities of affected people such as increased unemployment and loss of remittances. Daily labourers and people engaging in cash-for-work activities are the most affected due to restricted labour migration and closure of small businesses. Youth and women in the informal sector are also hard hit. Supply shortages as a result of movement restrictions are also reducing economic opportunities and decreasing rural and urban incomes thereby affecting people’s ability to buy basic commodities. This economic downturn is making it more difficult to lay the foundations for sustainable recovery and a return to longer term development in the north-east. 71% of households in Yobe and 82% of households in Borno and Adamawa are reported to have insufficient income to meet basic needs in the Early Recovery and Livelihoods Sector according to the MSNA conducted by Reach in November 2020. DRC for this project will target Adamawa, Borno and Yobe States. Specifically for the target locations proposed by DRC in Adamawa 80% and 71% of households in Madagali and Michika respectively are in need of improved access to income under the Early Recovery Sector with majority of household relying on borrowing food and income as a coping strategy. As for Yobe 84% and 59% were the thresholds for Gujba and Gluani respectively for households with insufficient income to meet basic needs with the majority of households using similar coping strategies to Borno and Adamawa.

2 - Activities

DRC's potential donors for the selected activities are USAID Bureau for Humanitarian Assistance (BHA) and the European Union. Activities are planned to be implemented in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa. Activities for the project will include;Activity 1 - Small business management training and distribution of Business Grants for Small Scale Business Start-up: A total of 1,055 households (representing 6,330 individuals - women and youth) will be supported with small business management trainings and business grants to improve their skills and increase their access to safer livelihood options for income opportunities and future recovery. Beneficiaries will engage in the viable small businesses identified by DRC Livelihood Market Assessments for Adamawa, Borno and Yobe States. DRC will also provide business advisory and monotring for women and youth assisted with income generation activities.Activity 2 - Vocational Skills Trainings for Youth: DRC will train 905 youth (18 to 24years) representing 5,430 individuals in marketable vocational skills identified by labour market assessments. This activity will strengthen capacity of youth and create off-farm income generating activities mainly in Yobe state.Activity 3 - Support formation of Village Savings and Lending Associations (VSLA) groups to enhance access to financial services that include credit/loans, savings and fund growth for 852 women and men (representing 5,112 individuals).Activity 4 – Rehabilitation of community assets and improving access to income through Cash for Work activities: This activity will focus on rehabilitating and constructing community assets of significant value to communities. DRC has already commenced a participatory Community Based Planning (CBP) process focused on identifying the community assets for rehabilitation or construction utilising donor guidelines that may include water sources, agriculture irrigation systems, abattoirs, market sheds, innovative food security related assets etc, which will determine the exact nature of work. Workers monthly will work and receive cash payments for completed works. This activity will target 1,119 households (representing 6,714 individuals)

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

DRC in all proposed locations works with Protection staff that will mainstream protection issues before and during project implementation. DRC has Protection Mainstream strategy for all sectors to ensure protection issues are well mainstreamed. Assessments and community consultations will consider all age, gender and marginalized/minority groups during programme design and planning to ensure that they influence the decision making process for the project. DRC will ensure implementing a do no harm approach to its programming to minimise disrupting social cohesion and peaceful existence. DRC also has a complaints and response mechanism in place to facilitate the report of sexual exploitation and abuse also complemented by regular Protection Monitoring and Screening conducted by Protection staff.
