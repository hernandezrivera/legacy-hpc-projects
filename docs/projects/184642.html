Project Summary

According to the HNO narrative, there are 1,330,000 IDPs in Somalia who face acute food insecurity and are projected to face moderate to large food consumption gaps through late 2021. The potential of drought in 2022, compounded by continued armed conflict, political instability, diminishing livelihoods and population’s displacement is expected to worsen the food insecurity situation in 2022. This could result in further inflows of new IDPs into IDP sites necessitating the need for provision of secure durable, safe, accessible, and decent shelter to reduce chances of gender-based violence and spread of covid-19 disease. The lack of critical information on available assistance and the existing community feedback mechanisms (CFM) will likely exacerbate the situation.
The most affected IDPs will be those living in rural areas where majority of the population is experiencing multiple declines in food and income sources. Contributing to their vulnerability is that fact that most IDPs, both in rural and in urban settings, in Somalia have limited access to livelihood assets, few income earning opportunities, low communal support and rely heavily on humanitarian assistance. While the cluster key vulnerable groups have been identified as persons with disabilities and the elderly, women and women headed households are also among the most vulnerable. This is because the women work burden will be increased from being forced to take on or seek opportunities to provide food and water. Women headed households are generally more food insecure than male headed household due to their poor access to labour and income sources. Additionally, minority communities, girls and young persons are also among the most vulnerable.
The project will target 1,915 Vulnerable IDP households for food assistance and 2,188 Vulnerable IDP households for NFI and ESK assistance projects. The project will also support construction of 100 durable shelter to the recently forced evicted and deported from Laascaanood which now in Baidoa without having proper shelter, this will improve them to integrate to the other communities and reduce the vulnerability of the supported people. The project will reach (A total of 25,218 individuals, assuming each household has at least 6 individuals) prioritizing women and persons living with disabilities in the cluster locations of Banadir, Baydhaba and Beledweyne Beneficiaries will be disaggregated by their marginalization status, for instance, minority clan, elderly, or people with disabilities, in addition to gender and age disaggregation. These cluster locations were selected based on high population densities of IDP sites and SYPD's operational areas. Provision of Emergency shelter and NFI kits will improve the living condition of the targeted vulnerable people.
The project will target population age; infants (0-5 years), children (5-17years), adults (18- 60 years) and elderly (60+ years). The project will also target a special population namely chronic ill person. The project will adopt a flexible voucher to encourage aid recipients to have a level of choice on NFIs and shelter materials. This helpfully provides a sense of more dignified assistance to populations that better meets the unique needs of every household. The project will conduct PDM every two weeks after distribution. The PDM will involve the relevance the assistance and satisfaction of the beneficiaries. The project will conduct PCM through mobilization meetings with all the stakeholders to ensure the inclusivity of the groups and avoid exclusion of marginalized groups. SYPD will set up a feedback mechanism through setting hotline numbers for channel any complaint and or query. the project outcome is to increase the proportion of Somalis, particularly the most vulnerable and marginalized with equitable access to and benefiting from government-led and regulated inclusive, affordable, and quality and basic social service at the different state levels by 2025.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Minority clans/ethnic groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance. - The project specifically aims to include the marginalized, specifically persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls. The project deliberately will aim to involve these groups and the communities in which they live in the entire project cycle. - The beneficiary selection criteria of the project prioritize marginalized groups. - Project staff will work closely with the community to ensure the right beneficiaries are selected for the project, including a verification process with the community. - A complaint and feedback mechanism will be set up for the project. It is essential that the mechanism is all inclusive and gives voice to the marginalized. however, it the event that the beneficiaries have difficulties in sharing feedback to SYPD. the project will conduct routine meetings with the village relief committees to gather concerns and issues related to the programming, address amicably. Risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity in targeted project area. While the shelter cluster will not specifically focus on permanent shelter, SYPD will follow relevant protocols and due diligence to ensure land on which IDPs are settled have minimal risks of eviction. - Stakeholder involvement including with camp managers, host community representatives, relevant government institution to ensure reduced risks related to land tenure. Risk of exposure of violence as a result of distance to and location of humanitarian assistance/services or indiscriminate attacks from armed actors. -Distributions will be conducted at site. - Security assessments will be conducted in close coordination with community leadership structures. - The projects will integrate and benefit a percentage of host community to avoid related conflicts. - Use of flexible vouchers instead of cash minimizes the risks associated with distribution of money such as theft.
