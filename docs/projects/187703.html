Project Summary

IMC propose to continue implementing a mixed approach of direct service provision through its static (currently 3) and mobile facilities (currently 6), providing PHC services in various areas of Damascus, Rural Damascus and Dara’a governorates. Core funding for these facilities is secured until the end of the first and the second quarter of 2022 and IMC will aim at renewing existing funds for at least additional 12 months. The facilities provide comprehensive PHC package with preventive and curative services including reproductive and child health care, management of communicable and non-communicable diseases, health education and awareness individual and group sessions (including on topics related to COVID 19). All children under 5 and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) are screened for malnutrition and referred to the closest public facility for treatment. Medications are provided free of charge based on doctors prescriptions and IMC EML (Essential Medicines List). IMC doctors are trained on mhGAP and work closely to case managers who are also embedded in the health facilities. IMC also support referrals to higher level of care (consultations, surgeries, specialized diagnostic services) through private service providers, including specialists and hospitals, for patients identified as in need of further examination and/or surgeries, to reduce risk of deterioration of their health status as well as mortality and disability. Always through referrals, IMC supports provision of hearing aids and speech therapy as needed, mostly to children with hearing impairments. In addition to the above, two static clinics in Damascus and Jaramana, provide ENT and ophthalmology services, as well as basic laboratory tests. Furthermore, through these clinics IMC distribute mobility/disability consumables (diapers, catheters, colostomy bags) and items such as wheelchairs, crutches, walkers, air mattresses and toilet seats. All facilities are screening patients for COVID 19 symptoms and refer them to the closest designated facility based on the established protocol.
Although statistics vary among locations, the majority of the beneficiaries seeking services at IMC facilities are women (approximately 60-70%) while for age categories below 18 years the sex disaggregation is more balanced. This can be attributed to the availability of services addressed to women (gynecology) as well as cultural and contextual considerations. Furthermore, approximately 30% of IMC patients are over 50 years old and 2 to 3% are PWD. As such and although this percentage varies across the locations as above mentioned, IMC facilities provide services to all categories of the population, including the most vulnerable categories such as older persons, women, children and PLW.
In parallel with direct service provision, IMC will continue work towards increased resilience and strengthening of the public health system through enhanced collaboration and support to the PHC facilities managed by the Ministry of Health (MoH). IMC is currently targeting various locations in Hama governorate, however based on gaps, needs and capacities, might be expanding to other governorates as well. Support includes provision of supplies, equipment, furniture and capacity building, aimed at increasing both quality and type of healthcare services provided by the targeted facilities. Overall the proposed intervention is in line with the objectives and response strategy for the health sector.
Monitoring activities are conducted both by health technical staff, to ensure adherence to standards and protocols, and IMC MEAL team. MEAL related monitoring include, among others, visits to the facilities, patient satisfaction surveys, PDM and surveys for beneficiaries supported with referrals, shipment verifications.

4 - Needs

The extent and severity of humanitarian needs have continued to increase during the past year, with estimated 14 million people in need of humanitarian assistance according to the Global Humanitarian Overview and 12.2 million in need of health assistance according to the estimations from the Health sector. Similar to other basic services, access to healthcare services at all levels is hampered by a combination of factors: the impact of the conflict in terms of damages to facilities and infrastructures, displacement and shortage of staff, the effect of coercive measures and the Lebanese crisis on the deterioration of the economy. Local capacities to produce and import medical supplies, raw materials for medications, medical equipment and spare parts continue to be limited, while increased shortages of fuel and electricity further challenge service provision. The COVID 19 pandemic has posed an additional burden on the health system, stretching and diverting already limited resources. The ability of the majority of the population to adhere to preventive measures is compromised by sub-standard living conditions, while vaccination proceeds slowly. The majority of the population lives below the poverty line, and thus unable to access basic commodities and services, including healthcare and medications, the latter particularly affecting people with chronic diseases. Additional barriers are related to the distance, cost and availability of transportation.

5 - Implementation of activities

IMC will continue providing free of charge comprehensive PHC services at its static and mobile facilities, as detailed in the summary section. IMC will also continue support advanced health care services through private service providers and provision of disability/mobility NFIs, including hearing aids. Case management will continue to be available in all locations where privacy and confidentiality can be secured (which is not always the case for mobile facilities). Capacity building and supportive supervision will continue be provided to both IMC and MOH health workers based on gaps and might include the following topics based on the services provided at the supported facilities: chronic diseases, mhGAP, IPC, CMR, rational use of drugs, medical triage. IMC will also continue supporting the public system at PHC level, through provision of supplies and equipment to enhance both type and quality of services provided. Through its health and MEAL staff, IMC will ensure close monitoring of the supported health facilities, including among others: checks of diagnosis and related treatments, monthly consumptions reports, use of PPE and adherence to IPC measures. Patient satisfaction surveys are conducted regularly to inform programming adaption and identify areas for improvement and emerging needs, as well as follow-up and satisfaction of the referral services and PDMs for beneficiaries receiving NFIs.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

GBV related risks: Services related to sexual and reproductive health care can cause increased GBV risks to girls and women, or else, GBV survivors might seek support in health facilities. Main mitigation measures to reduce GBV related risks include: training to relevant health workers on how to deal with people at risk or survivors of GBV, confidentiality, survivor-center approach, CMR, safe referrals (including to IMC protection team). IMC gynecologists are always female; furthermore, doctors are always assisted by one nurse. Facilities, even the temporary ones used by mobile teams, are arranged as much as possible to secure privacy and confidentiality of patients.Service provision: Low quality services/not adherence to technical minimum standards might expose beneficiaries to further harm and deterioration of their health conditions. Main mitigation measures include: regular supportive supervision and monitoring of service provision; capacity building, formal and informal; effective feedback and complaints mechanisms. COVID 19: Not adherence to preventive and IPC related measures can increase the risk of transmission to beneficiaries. Main mitigation measures include: monitoring of adherence to preventive measures; provision of quality PPE and adequate IPC related supplies; trainings/refreshers; suspension of service provision if increase in the number of health workers with confirmed/suspected infection occurs.PSEA: please refer to the dedicated section.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SEA related risks include abuse and exploitation both towards staff and beneficiaries seeking services at IMC facilities, either in exchange for services/assistance or not. At organizational level, all acts falling under the definition of SEA are prohibited and considered a gross violation of IMC Code of Conduct, which all IMC employees must sign and commit to adhere to. The Code is supported by IMC Safeguarding and Whistleblower Policy. All employees and volunteers are required to complete a mandatory training on the Code of Conduct & Ethics and Safeguarding Policy, and on reporting mechanisms, with refreshers provided on annual basis. IMC HQ has established a Safeguarding Task Force while at country level there are designated focal points in charge of training and awareness to staff and beneficiaries and ensuring safe and appropriate referrals in case of any incident being reported and confirmed by investigation. Furthermore, IMC Syria MEAL team conducts mandatory and refresher trainings on IMC Syria specific community-based feedback and response mechanism for all IMC staff and volunteers. Awareness and information sessions to beneficiaries regarding PSEA and reporting mechanism are also provided, together with information on how to safely report any incident. Health service providers are always accompanied by at least another staff member and IMC gynecologists are always females.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1: Delayed/lack of approvals for operations, monitoring and dispatches. Mitigation: Regular follow up with relevant authorities; remote management system; buffer stock at facilities. 2: Deterioration of the security situation. Mitigation: monitoring and info sharing with relevant stakeholders and temporary closure of facilities if risks raise; security/surveillance at warehouses, facilities, premises; minimize value/quantity of items while ensuring balance with programmatic needs. 3: Unavailability of qualified medical staff. Mitigation 3: Maintain a roster; offer attractive benefits package. 4: Unavailability/shortage of medical supplies and pharmaceuticals. Mitigation: ongoing pre-qualification process to expand available suppliers; maintain long term agreements. 5: Delays in international bank transfers. Mitigation: Maintain more than one bank account; advocate for dedicated channels for humanitarian transfers; info sharing with partners and donors. 6: COVID 19 limit capacity to deliver services either because of lockdowns or infection among staff. Mitigation: request waivers to allow movement of staff and mobile teams; adherence to preventive and isolation measures; temporary suspension of services to protect beneficiaries from transmission. 7: Fraud, collusion, abuse. Mitigation: mandatory trainings; established processes for financial and procurement checks and controls at HQ and country, including compliance and internal audit departments; reporting mechanism.
