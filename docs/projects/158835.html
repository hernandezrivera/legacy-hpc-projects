Project Summary

This project is aligned to HNO 2018-2020 Cluster Strategic Objectives 1 & 2. It also contributes to strategic objectives 1 and 2 of Every Newborn Action Plan “ENAP” 2017-2020, developed and adopted as a joint collaboration among major UN agencies, WHO, UNICEF and UNFPA in addition to other active stakeholders i.e. MAP-UK. The project will complement UNFPA 2019 implemented CERF and recently approved HPF/standard allocation projects; by expanding coverage and duration of health system capacity to sustain essential sexual and reproductive health “SRH” and maternal care services through 2020.
Gaza situation remains very fragile with increased poverty and lack of resources that puts huge pressure on the health system and depletes its coping mechanisms. Different challenges impacting the health system; the massive casualties due to "Great March of Return", protests have led to 316 deaths and 34,137 injuries (August 2019 Health Cluster Situation Report), further draining the already exhausted health system. Humanitarian needs are exacerbated with weakened essential maternal and reproductive health care services, including severe shortage in equipment, drugs, and disposables, that are severely underfunded, which has increased the risk of death and disability for women, girls, and infants, requiring an immediate response. According to the Ministry of Health (MOH), the maternal mortality ratio (MMR) in Gaza increased from 8.6 in 2017 to 19.1 in 2018 deaths per 100,000 live births (MoH Annual Report 2017 and 2018). Notwithstanding under reporting, 2019 cases of reported maternal deaths as of September 2019, is more than the whole reported in 2018, clearly indicating a rise of MMR in 2019.
UNFPA aims to save women’s lives and ensure that, despite the toll on the health system, women and girls have access to essential Sexual & Reproductive Health (SRH) including maternal health services and information. This will be achieved through filling gaps in SRH and maternal health services, mitigating the impact of sever shortages of supplies and reinforce life-saving SRH and maternal care service provision and preventing excess mortality and morbidity through;
- Ensuring a continuum of care and provision of life-sustaining SRH services, counselling and information including strengthening community preparedness.
- Provision of essential life-saving SRH and maternal health equipment, instruments and supplies
- Mainstreaming and training on the minimum initial service package for reproductive health in crisis "MISP", emergency obstetric care, and safe birth etc.
- Actively engaging communities, supporting coping mechanism, sensitization, awareness raising and outreach.
- Support linking and informing communities to available service with special focus to marginalized communities “i.e. ARA” and groups including disabled and elderly etc.
While this project is meant to address immediate response to urgent and critical needs of Gazan’s women’s, and young girls, especially those in marginalized areas, it will also support men and other vulnerable adolescents and strengthen the preparedness and resilience of both the community and the health system for absorbing shocks, saving lives and recovering from crises.

1 - Needs

The situation in Palestine, especially in Gaza, is well recognized as a protracted protectioncrisis with humanitarian consequences, driven, among others, by a lack of respect forinternational law by the occupying power. Palestinian women, men, girls and boys in Gazaface a range of serious protection and humanitarian threats including threats to life,liberty and security. Gender-based violence (GBV) continues to be a key protection andhealth concern in Gaza, further impeding women’s and girls’ access to resources includinglife sustaining health sexual and reproductive services “SRH” and information. Following 13 years of tightened blockade on Gaza, internal political divisions and public employees' salary cuts, with no clear horizon for a way out soon, Gaza faces increased poverty and deterioration of living conditions, with a health system on the verge of collapse with limited access to essential sexual and reproductive health services. Maternal health facilities and units are facing severe constraints such as regular and excessive electricity outages, shortages in essential drugs and supplies, including lack of micronutrients, such as iron and folic acid. According to latest reports, 55% of essential MCH drugs at zero stock at MOH (MOH September drugs report), and 39% of women’s health drugs are at zero stock among the major NGO health providers in Gaza (PNGO September 2019). The impact of the "March of Great Return" comes on top of an already fragile health situation where the massive influx of casualties is hindering the health system absorbing capacity. Scarceresources are diverted to deal with the massive injuries and trauma care needs, overlooking aggravated needs of other vulnerable groups including 45,000 pregnant women at any time. Accordingly, pregnant women's access to life-saving maternal and reproductive health services including emergency obstetric care, safe birth and antenatal care decreases, exposing women to risk of maternal mortality and disability. Alarmingly and based on MOH, Health Cluster, and OCHA reports, there have been an increase in maternal mortality in Gaza from 2017 to 2018 estimated at 122% increase. 18% of pregnant women in Gaza are anemic and 14% of lactating women are malnourished (UNICEF/WFP/Save the Children May 2019). Coupled with lack of access to family planning services exposing more women to unintended pregnancies and putting them at higher risk of maternal mortality and morbidity, this rise is expected to continue in 2019, as initially indicated by Maternal Mortality Committee. UNFPA aims at saving women’s lives through strengthening system and community access and preparedness to life saving SRH services and information on; safe childbirth, Emergency obstetric care, preconception care, early detection of breast cancer, antenatal care with focus on high risk pregnancies, and enhancing knowledge of and utilization of essential RH services, during acute crises. This project will increase access of vulnerable women’s, men and girls to SRH services, support networks of community activists and implement community mobilization campaigns including health education/awareness component, that will target the issues from community and women empowerment perspectives via educating families, men, boys and girls on basic gender and human rights issues, including women/girls with disability and their personal hygiene and SRH needs. Through this project, UNFPA will build and complement its support; through CERF and HPF projects to expand coverage, reach more people in need, further reduce the chronic shortages of supplies and strengthen cooperation and coordination with major health actors in Gaza including UNRWA, WHO, UNICEF and Health NGOs, on SRH and maternal care through supporting the SRH platform to ensure complementarity, networking and synergism.

2 - Activities

This project complements previous and ongoing UNFPA humanitarian interventions, supported targeted major maternities and PHC facilities, through provision of essential SRH equipment, instruments and life-saving pharmaceuticals and supplies including family planning commodities, and safe delivery kits. UNFPA will expand the network of 106 health staff, who were trained on safe delivery and referral as to support safe childbirth at the community and/or PHC facility, during emergencies when access to maternities is blocked, or appropriately refer cases to further assistance, and community awareness campaigns reaching thousands of people in vulnerable areas of Gaza strip. Through this project, and building on lessons learned, and previous achievements,UNFPA will respond to urgent needs in SRH services, by filling gaps and preventing system collapse through providing critically needed supplies, community engagement and capacity building of staff and enhancing SRH coordination and reporting among various actors in Gaza. Practically, and in order to enrich the intervention and expand its coverage, UNFPA will work on the following domains; - - Strengthen access to life saving SRH services, through supporting major maternities and PHC facilities, with critically needed equipment/instruments, drugs and supplies as well as training on emergency obstetric care “EmOC”. - Support preconception care, antenatal care and family planning services & information, training of staff and equipping facilities with needed supplies, including family planning commodities, Iron and folic acid to support the service. - Support SRH humanitarian platform of coordination and Networking. - Support networks of community activists and implement communitymobilization campaigns including health education/awareness component, that will target the issues from community and women empowerment perspective via educating families and girls on basic gender and human rights issues, including women/girls with disability and their personal hygiene and RH needs, risks “health and social” of early marriage and promotion of healthy outcomes and reproduction, supporting positive participation on relief and preparedness to emergencies. Activities:  Provision of life-saving needs; drugs & disposables, equipment, instruments and medical furniture to 6 central maternities and 42 PHC clinics (27 MOH and 15 NGOs).  Improve the capacity/skills of 200 health providers, practitioners on EmOC, safe delivery, MISP, preconception care, and other RH issues.  Provision, distribution and deployment of individual safe delivery kits to major PHCs, trained staff, and ambulances.  Support SRH coordination platform of major actors in Gaza to ensure complementarity, share experience and increase access to SRH services and information in Gaza.  Improve community emergency preparedness in isolated communities through enhancing awareness and /or linkages with appropriate service delivery points and available members of the safe delivery and community networks.  Conduct community-based interventions; supporting community networks, outreach sessions, and media campaigns advocating for universal health coverage and right to health and access to services.

The monitoring framework shall track progress against achieving key deliverables and milestones both on partners and UNFPA directly implemented activities. Also, field monitoring visits may be planned and done jointly with the donors upon their request. 9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The project components are based on national humanitarian plans, in accordance with IHL, and are guided by international guidelines on sexual and reproductive health (SRH) in emergencies, such as the SPHERE Standards, as well as the Minimal Initial Service Package (MISP) for reproductive health in emergencies agreed upon by the Inter-Agency Working Group on Reproductive Health in Crises. UNFPA takes the principles of “Do No Harm” seriously within its programmes including with its implementing partners to ensure safety and dignity of all served beneficiaries including women, girls and disabled people in lie with the international IASC standards and principles including GBV: Minimum Standards for Prevention and Response to Gender-based Violence in Emergencies. In addition, the project planned interventions including health services will be provided in a manner that keeps all service recipients away from any potential to face any hazards or threats, physically and emotionally.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project is targeting all Palestinians in need for SRH healthcare, without any discrimination based on gender, refugee status, disability or ethnic background, in all Gaza Strip with special focus on vulnerable and marginalized communities. Provision of supplies will be through the central stores of project implementing partners and will be monitored by UNFPA according to internal M&E procedures. Areas for community outreach will be carefully chosen in coordination with implementing partners’ affiliated active CBO’s and key informants in the area to ensure free access of women, girls and disabled people to the sessions and available services supported by the project. Participation of both women and men in training and other capacity building activities within the framework of the project will be strictly applied and monitored by UNFPA through filed visits and requesting disaggregated data reporting.
