Project Summary

Secured project:
The project providing life-saving humanitarian food assistance to 2102 HHs as follow:
1- GAN shifted the beneficiaries from the emergency to regular assistance to the most vulnerable families (1000 families - 6200 individuals) in two communities: Mare' and Salama.
The modality is cash for food, as it is considered the most effective especially under the context of COVID-19 epidemic, where cash modality requires less staff during the delivery of assistance and reduces touching with the beneficiaries, according to our experience in this modality the beneficiary does not need more than 5 minutes to receive the assistance.
The assistance tailored according to the family size to achieve the benefit and the fair distribution of the assistance (12$ per person monthly) for 4 months.
2- The project target 1102 families with cash for food, the cash intervention is feasible in these camps as they all have access to different main markets in the targeted communities, four rounds of cash for food distributed in Jan, Feb Mar, and Apr 2021, each round consists of 2 weeks for distribution and 2 weeks for validation and PDM, 50$ allocated for each family as recommended by cluster and CWG, although the data gained from the FSL cluster indicate to more than 90% respond in Dana but no partner plan to target the same vulnerable and underserved camps, the camps were targeted by GAN bread campaign in Aug and Sep 2020, according to the interviews and assessment, 99% said that the first priority is food, the proposed modality is cash for food, as it is considered one of the most effective modality since the markets are available near camps.

4 - Needs

Syria remains one of the world’s most complex humanitarian emergencies characterized by ongoing hostilities, triggered one of the worst displacement crises of our time, and led to the widespread destruction of civilian and agricultural infrastructure.More than 800K have been displaced since Dec19. Nearly1.5 M people live in 1160 camps/sites in NWS (79%women and children). Between December 19 and early March 20, 960k people were displaced in NWS.The SP is in virtual freefall having lost 78%of its value since Oct2019, while price increases for staple goods are at an all-time high. More than 90%of the population is now estimated to live below the poverty line.The COVID-19 pandemic has accelerated this economic downturn by further reducing already sparse income-generating opportunities.The crisis continues to have a gendered impact, with women and adolescent girls paying a high price for harmful and discriminatory gender norms, including gender-based violence.Facing deteriorated living standards, families are increasingly adopting harmful coping mechanisms. 71%of households and 75% of female-headed households have taken on more debt.28%of families now adopt crisis or emergency food related coping strategies, including withdrawing children from school to have them work instead, selling property, migrating due to lack of food, and early child marriage. negatively impacted due to households facing barriers resulting from both access and affordability, HNO 2021

5 - Implementation of activities

Monthly food assistance for the assessed food insecure people: 1- The project will provide life-saving humanitarian food assistance to 1000 HHs (6200 individuals), (900 IDPs/100Host) HHs in the village in Mare' and Salama communities. The project implemented through cash for food modality using (e-voucher) with the aim of saving lives and ensuring continued and regular access to food by providing a monthly 12$ per person which will last for 4 months2- The project will provide life-saving humanitarian food assistance to 1102 HHs, in the camps The project implemented through cash for food modality using (e-voucher) with the aim of saving lives and ensuring continued and regular access to food by providing a monthly 50$ per family which will last for 4 months. We used beneficiary selection criteria that prioritize vulnerable HHs, including female or youth-headed households, those with elderly or disabled family members, and households with more than six persons.They will be able to use the e-voucher card immediately after the orientation sessions. GAN chose to implement through e-voucher system based on many reasons, such as saving the dignity of beneficiaries, as to prevent of food basket selling which is frequently happened and GAN implemented and used this modality in 2019 and the beneficiary satisfaction rate was 98%.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

GAN is a member of PSEA network, attending a monthly meeting and following the guidelines, so to mainstream the protection from sexual exploitation and abuse in the project, GAN have developed a policy stating standards of conduct, including acts of SEA, and a work plan to implement the policy in place, GAN will ensure all staff members are signed and adhered to a code of conduct policy, PSEA and will support the members to access relevant pieces of training (PSEA, GBV), ensuring that non-humanitarian stakeholders who will be in touch with beneficiaries such as (suppliers) will be informed and adhere to the humanitarian principles and code of conduct, PSEA posters will be available at distribution points. GAN recognizes the rights of all stakeholders, particularly the vulnerable, to access humanitarian programs without the risk of sexual exploitation or abuse. GAN intends to prevent abuse by implementing procedures to protect beneficiaries through best recruitment practices, staff induction, creating an open and aware culture and assigning clear management responsibilities, ensuring that victims are supported, and perpetrators held to account.Information on the referral pathway will be circulated through posters and banners at the distribution points.The distribution points will be inside the camps itself, during daylight hours, and safeguard that there is appropriate segregation for women to create a culturally sensitive and safe operational environment

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption:- The money transfer agents using the e-voucher modality are available and are functioning.- Markets are functioning properly in the targeted areas.- Access to Syria remains possible and safe through the neighboring countries.- COVID context is continued.Risk:1: the risk of that one money transfer service is responsible for transferring the cash amount:Likelihood: low, Impact: high.GAN has a prior fundamental condition for a money transfer agent: the payment is conducted after distribution. 2: COVID-19 spread in target communities:Mitigation: GAN team takes all necessary measures to avoid the transmission and spread of the disease infection, such as wearing a face mask, gloves, providing hand sanitizers, maintaining social space spacing, and measuring the temperature of the beneficiaries before entering the distribution point, GAN will use the latest version of recommendations in regards of distributing NFI under the cotext of COVID-19. 3: Lockdown and curfew in the targeted communities due to the COVID-19: Mitigation: In case that a curfew is imposed, there will be permitted from the local authorities for humanitarian workers to work and movement, and GAN will change the cash assistance with in-kind assistance and will deliver assistance to the beneficiaries' homes (Door to door). 4: Due to the unpredictable situation in NW Syria, there is a risk that markets are not functioning properly due to lack of access, or other unexpected issues. Likelihood: low, Impact: high.Mitigation strategy: GAN confirmed the markets are functioning and the security situation allows the implementation of the project. The situation of the market will be monitored on a regular basis by the program team.
