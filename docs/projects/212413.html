Project Summary

Samaritan's Purse will continue supporting households (HHs) by protecting and building productive assets and restoring/creating income-generating opportunities to save and sustain life. The project is in accordance with the UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs targeted activities for 2022. The program developed a comprehensive list of vocations (over 35 market-relevant skills training areas) that beneficiaries will select. In order to expand the varieties of apprenticeship providers, the program will first identify the businesses/apprentice providers and match trainees to the training providers. This will minimize the risk of saturating the labour market with a few types of trades, e.g. barbers and tailors. Besides, project beneficiaries have not been exposed to a wide range of vocational and apprenticeship skills training options. Therefore, the urban livelihood project will focus on two Specific Objectives (SO):
SO1: Strengthening small and medium-size businesses with high growth potential creates jobs for unemployed or underemployed youth. Samaritan's Purse will allocate grants for the 23 most viable business plans (Small Medium Enterprises). In addition, SP will explore possibilities to support at least 1 Large Business Enterprise in addition to the small enterprises. Mentoring and support of business owners on the growth journey will be provided although the programme cycle. The businesses selected will receive essential soft skills and business management training, including writing a budget and calculating revenue, offered to all business owners. Selection criteria: 1) based in Hassakah city, 2) defined as small businesses, 3) willing to attend mandatory sessions (business planning & budgeting), 4) willing to take between 1-5 apprentices for five months, 5) not receiving aid from another NGO for their business. See the image with a list of viable business opportunities within NES.
SO2: Increasing employability of unemployed youth in the local economy through market-relevant vocational training. The project will accept 46 apprentices and provide a stipend for six months of work (22 days/per month). The apprentices will be matched with the businesses according to their skills and experience. During the program, apprentices will receive on-the-job coaching from the businesses. As a result, the program will create a pool of skilled laborers that the market could absorb. Basic soft skills and business management training (formal five-day training and on-job learning), including writing a budget and calculating revenue, will be offered to all apprentices. In addition, apprentices working in a sector requiring more skills may be provided additional trade-specific vocational training, such as construction-related skills. The program will also include multi-stakeholder workshops/showcases in the form of events, bringing together multiple beneficiaries and private sector employers into a single joint dialogue, discussion session, or presentation - strengthening the skills and networking ability of participants and increasing business opportunities and connections within the region.
The program is largely guided by the Early Recovery Livelihood working group guidelines and the good practices of other existing humanitarian actors in the program area. Samaritan's Purse will develop context-relevant training monitoring tools to track progress in skills development. Selection criteria: 1) not currently employed, 2) non-consistent employment in the last six months, 3) priority to IDPs but not exclusively, 4) no other income in the HH. SP will provide a start-up kit at the end of the program, i.e. construction toolbox, sewing machine, to the apprentices graduating from the program. The kit will encourage those who want to start their own business. 0 - Project Summary (extended)

Protection Mainstreaming:SP will prevent, as much as possible, any unintended negative effects of the intervention that might increase people’s vulnerability to physical and psychological risks. For example, SP will ensure that routes to and from any training or program sites are safe and accessible. Also, SP will prioritize the safety and dignity of participants by respecting the social norms of the target area and ensuring that interventions are culturally appropriate; for example, norms around male and female interaction for the vocational trainings and apprenticeships. The SP program will coordinate with other non-governmental organizations (NGOs) in the local target areas to ensure no duplication of activities that could be harmful or make program activities redundant in the livelihoods and markets sector in terms of training and capacity building of apprentices and businesses. SP will ensure that program staff are capacitated to respond appropriately to protection or prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) concerns or complaints arising, and are trained to follow all policies and guidelines in responding with a survivor-centered approach. An awareness of the unique needs of vulnerable groups will be incorporated into program interventions for IDPs and relevant host community members, in an effort to ensure safety, security, accountability, dignity, and effective access to the program across genders and age groups. Sensitization will be provided to the community to ensure that women businesses and apprentices are supported and the wider community understands the value of women being able to earn money through livelihoods/markets. Programming will ensure that sexual violence and exploitation are mitigated through cultural sensitivity and by navigating local gender norms. The program will provide training and education on safe practices for safeguarding and PSEA, as well as provide a safe place for women and girls to receive help and counsel as needed, and a hotline to report incidents.
