Project Summary

While harvests improved in 2017 and 2018 due to the good rain seasons, food insecurity increased due to a rise in food prices without a proportional increase in income especially for the displaced continues to be a major shock for IDPs, returnees and some host community members in Yaseen and Shaeria localities in East Darfur. With peace having been experienced in the last two years displaced communities are beginning to return to their original home areas.
Conflict in Darfur resulted in the displaced people losing their productive asserts in terms of livestock and land for cropping. Humanitarian needs in 2018 have increased by one million people due to the South Sudanese refugee influx, disease outbreaks, food insecurity and malnutrition. Left untreated, acute malnutrition can have debilitating consequences on children, such as impaired physical growth and cognitive development
In times of stress or shocks the vulnerable households dispose of asserts starting with livestock which in turn increases cases of malnutrition and they tend to sell these at very low prices as a coping strategy. In some cases households resort to early child marriages to get income for food negatively affecting the health of the girls. Children under 5 years and the pregnant and lactating women are most vulnerable to malnutrition. Girls and women may be forced into prostitution for survival and this is detrimental to their health due to the risk of HIV and child trafficking for sexual exploitation.
Food prices are expected to further rise in 2019 and food access among poor household will be much lower than. Seasonal Monitor: Dry and significantly hotter-than-normal conditions across Eastern Horn and South Sudan. The projected reduction in rain fed yields and in pasture or grazing will result in families selling off household assets to buy food, which is demonstrable through a major decrease in the price of meat.
Poor macroeconomic conditions persist, fueled by the scarcity of foreign currency, and the Sudanese Pound (SDG) stands at 61 SDG/USD on the parallel market. According to Sudan’s Central Bureau of Statistics, the December 2018 national inflation rate was 72.94 percent, compared to 68.93 percent in November 2018 and a five-year average of 27.16 percent.
Sorghum and millet prices are on average 150 percent above last year and over 270 percent above the five-year average. Food prices are expected to further rise in 2019 and food access among poor household will be much lower than normal during the 2019 lean season when poor households are reliant on markets to access food. This will lead to negative coping mechanism, triggered stress and in depth livelihoods shock amongst farmers and pastoralist households mainly due to water and grazing shortages. This has put increased stress onto an agricultural system struggling to rehabilitate itself after decades of war.
So the food security and nutrition strategy should focus on promoting food production and assisting with access to quality seed to increase productivity.
The impact of the drought on education is likely to lead to a negative trend in children’s school attendance. As the scarcity of food at household level is forcing children to contribute to the families’ income and tasks, children are beginning to drop out.
The proposed intervention aims to take steps to promote and support market access for vulnerable people using the voucher and cash transfer systems. Local purchases will serve to promote local production and create employment for the vulnerable households. The use of the cash transfers will ensure that the households buy the products as when they need them reducing the risk of massive prepositioning of imported food stuffs and other NFIs that may drive commodity prices down negatively affecting the local producers or push when buying locally demand may outstrip supply pushing prices up beyond the rich of other vulnerable households not receiving support.

Needs

Harvests improved in 2017 and 2018 due to the good rain seasons, food insecurity increased due to a rise in food prices without a proportional increase in income especially for the displaced continues to be a major shock for IDPs, returnees and some host community members in Yaseen and Shaeria localities in East Darfur. With peace having been experienced in the last two years displaced communities are beginning to return to their original home areas. Conflict in Darfur resulted in the displaced people losing their productive asserts in terms of livestock and land for cropping. Humanitarian needs in 2018 have increased by one million people due to the South Sudanese refugee influx, disease outbreaks, food insecurity and malnutrition. Left untreated, acute malnutrition can have debilitating consequences on children, such as impaired physical growth and cognitive development. The proposed intervention aims to take steps to promote and support market access for vulnerable people using the voucher and cash transfer systems to access production inputs and supplementary food to keep the vulnerable households well nourished. Local purchases will serve to promote local production and create employment for the vulnerable households. The use of the cash transfers will ensure that the households buy the products as when they need them reducing the risk of massive prepositioning of imported food stuffs and other NFIs that may drive commodity prices down negatively affecting the local producers or push when buying locally demand may outstrip supply pushing prices up beyond the rich of other vulnerable households not receiving support. The years of cutting down of trees and mining of the cropping lands without replacing the soil fertility crop yield have been declining and so are the pasture. The impact of droughts is on the increase and water shortages for domestic, cropping and livestock are worsening year by year. There is need to change the cropping practices so as to adopt more effective water harvesting techniques that will recharge the underground water table and also adopt soil management techniques that do not destroy the soil structure and maintain soil fertility. Returnee households are not able to meet all their food security need by buying from the market given the high inflation rate driven by fuel shortages and increasing exchange rates.

Activities

Activity 1.1: A community level basic response and quick needs assessment will be implemented to identify the most deserving cases to receive support. 2,750 individuals will receive support through cash transfers to increase their resilience to shocks by supplementing their income to buy food, agriculture production inputs for crops or livestock depending on the household preferences and needs Activity 2.4: 20,000 individuals will receive USD 200,000 vouchers for summer field crops mainly seed and some tools where necessaryActivity 2.3: 5000 individuals are to receive USD 20,000 for winter vegetable production for improved household nutrition. Activity 2.1:Improve productivity of households to meet their basic needs and access to essential services through institutional development at community level and household training of women and men to empower them with key livelihoods skills. 15 Community volunteers will be selected to lead up to 20 leaf farmers for cascading trained on improved agriculture techniques using the farmer field school approach/20 demonstration plot to reach 25,000 individuals. Activity 3.1: Training of Community health Promoters and care group members of the target localities on improved practices that promote essential health, nutrition and hygiene practices. A total budget of USD 77,500 is allocated for the capacity building and networking activities. Capacity building of the community health workers is also included in this budget. Activity 3.2: Up to 650 individuals will receive direct support for transport to the nutrition treatment centers (OTP and stabilization centers) and CCFL training to improve the nutrition of the children and PLW screened to have SAM and MAM cases. USD 50 is budgeted for each beneficiary household.
