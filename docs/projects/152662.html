Project Summary

UNMAS will field four Quick Response Teams, with embedded MRE/Community Liaison Teams (CLTs)—to provide explosive ordnance disposal (EOD) spot tasks, survey and clearance of land areas (calculated in sq. meters), technical and non-technical survey, as well conduct MRE and CL. The 40,000 MRE will cover 12,000 boys, 12,000 girls, 8,000 women, and 8,000 men. During the sessions, further information is gathered by the MRE/CLTs on suspicious hazards which is shared with the operations team members for survey, marking, and clearance. MRE alerts communities how to identify, avoid and report explosive hazards they may encounter to reduce their threat of injury or death. Additionally, the teams will survey and clear 600,000 sq meters.
Deployment of mobile clearance capacity to respond in urgent and prioritized locations throughout South Sudan to provide survey and clearance of explosive hazards as well as provide risk education to women, men, boys, and girls to mitigate the threat of injury or death from explosive hazards.

Needs

*As of October 2018, there are 531 known hazardous areas recorded in the IMSMA database, but the total extent of contamination across South Sudan is unknown, particularly in areas of recent or ongoing conflict. There is a need to survey and locate unreported or suspected hazardous areas as well as to conduct clearance and removal of explosive hazards.*The legacy of protracted conflict has resulted in over six million people living in close proximity to landmines and explosive remnants of war (ERW) which threaten their lives, particularly those fleeing active conflict. It further inhibits the return of displaced persons, the resumption of livelihoods, and the delivery of emergency and life-saving assistance. *In parallel to the removal and clearance of explosive hazards, the provision of risk education (RE) will ensure that communities and individuals are able to develop strategies to cope and reduce the threat of landmines and ERW. *The teams will be highly mobile and able to rapidly deploy to situations which need immediate or emergency response in priority locations to enhance protection needs, life-saving assistance, or support the returns of internally displaced persons (IDPs) or refugees.

Activities

*Survey, clearance and risk education (RE) staff are trained and accredited. *Ensure effective prioritization of tasked locations (in coordination with key stakeholders, including relevant cluster partners in addition to Protection: WASH, FSL, etc.). *Technical and non-technical survey to locate the presence or absence of explosive hazards. *Ensure known hazardous areas (HA) are recorded, marked and/or cleared and closed as well as that the surrounding communities and informed and are aware of the status. *Update IMSMA database to record progress and update maps; information to be shared with stakeholders. *Community Liaison Teams (CLT) provide RE tailored to the needs of the audience (internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, children, and women receive targeted messaging and teaching methods).

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Community Liaisons (CLs), embedded with the Mine Action Teams, notify the community structures (eg. local authorities, community elders, women and youth groups, etc.) on the mine action operations and provide RE on the ways the community members (targeted and disaggregated by gender and age) can recognize, avoid, and report explosive hazards.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

CLs tailor and target RE content and messaging for audiences disaggregated by gender and age to ensure all members of the community who approach their environs from different perspectives and for different needs.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

UNMAS has a strict zero-tolerance policy on PSEA, implementing the UN’s code of conduct and reporting mechanisms. All staff are informed routinely on PSEA and it is mandatory for all staff to attend in-person trainings as well as complete on-line exams.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Through the provision of RE and general consultation during the notification by CLs to the community on mine action operations, community members are encouraged to share information on suspected explosive hazards and suspicious objects with the CLs. They feed this information into the Mine Action Teams who can then respond by surveying and clearing the suspected hazards based on the new information--which enhances the overall project's outcome and impact.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Feedback/complaint mechanisms are available to the community through initial consultation, ongoing MRE sessions, and through community authorities/elders/women and youth groups—to alert UNMAS to issues which may be overlooked or require intervention.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

As mentioned above, CL's gather information during provision of RE and consultation with a variety of community members throughout the mine action operations.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

During mine action operations, with embedded CL's who consult with the various community members and frameworks as well as provide RE targeted for age and gender, information is gathered to inform survey and clearance. Furthermore, the Information Management System for Mine Action (IMSMA) is reviewed and updated throughout operations, including RE, to provide better analysis of the response needed.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Through operations, including RE and consultation with community members, plus IMSMA, the teams are able to identify and address barriers of access due to landmines and ERW and intervene appropriately to enhance and ensure the safety and security needs of the different population groups within the area of responsibility. Furthermore, gender and age considerations are taken into account when considering barriers to access, addressing protection threats, as well as addressing such protection threats. Based on analysis of recent and current investigations of explosive hazard accidents, UNMAS targets boys and girls for MRE, but also engages women and men with MRE, recognizing all members of the community approach their environment for different reasons and thus messaging is inclusive to cover the different needs and risks for age- and gender-specific contexts.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project's main objective is to reduce the threat of injury or death by landmines or ERW for all members of a community through both the removal and destruction of explosive hazards as well as through RE to inform civilians (disaggregated by gender and sex) of how to identify, avoid, and report explosive hazards.

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Through daily, weekly, and monthly reporting to IMSMA, the project will detail the number of explosive hazards (including type, location, etc.) removed and destroyed, the number of hazardous areas closed, the amount of sq meters cleared and handed over, and the number of women, men, boys, and girls provided with RE to identify, avoid, and report explosive hazards they may encounter.
