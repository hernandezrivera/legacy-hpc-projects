Project Summary

According to the IPC analysis released on 3rd of November 2022, during the lean projection period (April-July 2023), an estimated 7.76 million people (62.7% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 43,000 people likely to be in catastrophe (IPC phase 5) acute food insecurity in Akobo, Fangak, and Canal/Pigi counties of Jonglei State and Leer and Mayendit Counties of Unity State. In the lean-season projection period of April to July 2023, 47 counties will likely be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, 29 counties will likely be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, and one county will likely be in Stressed (IPC Phase 2) acute food insecurity.
The nutrition situation remains a major problem. According to FSNMS survey round 28, Prevalence of wasting among young children in South Sudan remains above the World Health Organization emergency threshold in four State (Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity and NBeG) of the ten states, while the national average as per the latest FSNMS is estimated at 16% percent, showing no improvement since 2019.
Between July and October 2022, 57% of the counties were classified in crises (IPC phase 3) or worse acute malnutrition. During the second projection period (lean season), the situation is expected to deteriorate further where 71 percent counties are projected to be in crises or worse IPC acute malnutrition (IPC AMN Phase 3 and phase4). This represents the highest number classified in IPC AMN Phase 4 (Critical) for the past 3 similar seasons.
Key drivers to acute malnutrition include high prevalence of diseases. 62.5% of children reported to have been ill two weeks prior to the assessment. The childhood diseases recorded include Diarrhea, fever, and cough. These diseases are among the most common and fatal diseases among children and cause rapid deterioration in health. Poor sanitation conditions are likely contributing to the high prevalence of illness, with open defecation as high as 50% of households in 56 counties.
In 2023, an estimated 2.14 million people in South Sudan (SS) will be at risk of acute malnutrition, including 345,893 children with severe acute malnutrition (SAM), 1,057,703 children with moderate acute malnutrition (MAM), and 737,812 pregnant and lactating women with acute malnutrition (AM-PLW).
In response, UNICEF will collaborate with actors in health and WASH sectors to reinforce preventative activities and treat children under five with severe acute malnutrition. These activities will include securing safe water through: rehabilitation of non-functioning boreholes; distribution of water treatment kits to secure safe water; hygiene promotion through distribution of soap; early childhood development (ECD); counselling on appropriate infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices; promotion of kitchen gardens and cooking demonstrations to ensure adequate physical and cognitive developments; malaria testing and treatment; vaccinations, micronutrient supplementation and deworming to boost immunity; and community engagement to encourage greater utilisation of available services.
To improve community participation and awareness among caregivers and other community stakeholders, UNICEF will use multiple channels to mobilise communities around key behaviours to prevent undernutrition through network of CNVs (community nutrition volunteers) and MtMSG (mother to mother support groups).
The cost of delivering this UNICEF nutrition project 345,893 children in South Sudan (SS)at risk of severe acute malnutrition, Vitamin A supplementation for 2.7 million, and MIYCN counselling for 1.7 million PLW. To ensure timely and adequate availability of essential nutrition lifesaving supplies at point of use, the estimated total cost of delivering this UNICEF nutrition project is US$ 76 Million.

2 - Project activities

UNICEF and nutrition partners will implement numerous interventions and activities to respond to needs. The planned activities are:  Train health and nutrition workers on malnutrition prevention at national, state, county and community levels.  Provide targeted individual and group counselling on MIYCN to caregivers of children aged below 24 months, including men.  Support establishment of demonstration kitchen gardens at facilities and in communities.  Promote dietary diversity, food demonstration with locally available food, and cooking methods through cooking demonstrations.  Support growth monitoring and promotion at facility and community levels.  Provide micronutrient supplements, specifically vitamin A, and deworming through integration with National Immunisation Days (NID), as a standalone activity or with acute malnutrition screening. Conduct malaria testing and treatment at nutrition sites (OTP).  Distribute long lasting insecticidal nets (LLINs), WASH non-food items, and conduct hygiene promotion at OTP sites through the WASH cluster.  Train health and nutrition staff on how to provide psychosocial support to families and distribute ECD kits at nutrition sites (OTP).  Train health and nutrition workers on community management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) to improve care for children aged 6-59 months with malnutrition.  Conduct community mobilisation activities and active screening for early case detection and referral of children with SAM. Provide integrated treatment to children with SAM, both at static facilities and through community outreach.  Provide integrated care for children with SAM in OTP/Stabilization Centres in health facilities, including WASH services and ECD at nutrition sites.  Create awareness and provide gender-based violence referral services at OTP sites, focusing on women’s empowerment in community meetings. Conduct timely emergency preparedness and response. Monitor the progress and quality of nutrition interventions and services at the static nutrition facilities and outreach sites.  Provide integrated on-the-job coaching and mentorship on SAM treatment, supplies management and MIYCN. Routinely manage nutrition programme data and information.  Conduct nutrition assessments throughout the country twice yearly: FSNMS.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

UNICEF has a zero-tolerance policy for child safeguarding violations and sexual exploitation and abuse perpetrated by its employees, personnel, sub-contractors and implementing partners. In South Sudan, a combination of various factors (i.e., high prevalence of Sexual Gender Based Violence, conflict, the high vulnerability of beneficiaries dependent on humanitarian assistance to meet basic needs, elevated presence of humanitarian staff and related personnel, weak rule of law…) makes the country a high-risk environment for (SEA. Following the endorsement of the United Nations Protocol on Allegations of SEA Involving Implementing Partners in February 2018, UNICEF has incorporated child safeguarding and prevention of SEA standards in contractual clauses of all partnership agreements signed between UNICEF and Civil Society Organisation (CSO) partners. In addition to that, in February 2020, UNICEF South Sudan has started to roll-out UNICEF new global Procedure on Managing Risks of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse in Implementing Partnerships which includes a more stringent assessment for all CSOs partners, as foreseen by the above-mentioned UN Protocol. The preventions of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) assessment rates organizational capacities to prevent/ respond to SEA and covers six core standards: Policy, Management and Human Resources systems in place, Mandatory training, Reporting, Assistance and referrals for survivors and Investigations. The organizational capacity rating converts to a SEA risk rating (High, Moderate, Low); the principle being that UNICEF cannot enter in a partnership with a CSO with a High SEA risk rating. When the procedure was rolled-out in February 2020, all UNICEF partners rated at Moderate and High risk of not having systems and risk mitigation measures in place for PSEA were invited to develop an action plan to identify and address the organizational gaps identified during the assessment. In parallel, the UNICEF PSEA Toolkit was launched in to support partners to improve PSEA organizational risk mitigation measures. This comprehensive tool is used by partners to develop additional PSEA policies, procedures and systems within their respective organisation. UNICEF also held numerous workshops and one-on-one support to partners.
