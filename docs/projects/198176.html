1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The UNICEF programme will focus on responding to the needs of conflict affected children and their families with a particular focus on the needs of adolescent boys and girls. The main pillars of intervention will respond to the child protection risks of sexual violence, recruitment and use of children in armed groups or armed forces, unaccompanied and separated minors, mental health and psychosocial needs, environmental hazards. UNICEF will use a strategic approach of strengthening community based mechanisms, strengthening systems of referral and case management and provision of a variety of focused group activities. Where possible UNICEF will work with local actors, civil society and have a strong focus of child participation and working within the best interest of the child. A strong emphasis will be placed on child protection monitoring to provide evidence based programming. Prevention programming will include working with children, communities and security forces. Response will look to increase access to and quality of services. Quality will be based on the global standards such as the child protection minimum standards and GBV guidelines. 5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

This project will be based on a needs assessment that looks at the protection risks of boys and girls in the context of the northern provinces. The assessment will look at barriers to services for both boys and girls to ensure access to services and that services provision is tailored to the needs of the individual accessing the service. The programme will target under 18s with a particular focus on the needs of adolescent boys and girls. The programme will run safety audits that target girls and boys separately to fully understand the needs of this population, this information will also be triangulated with caregivers. Adolescent girls and boys will be consulted separately in the design of the programme and timing of activities to influence the design of the programme. The programme will also look at the needs of child mothers, caregivers with a focus on mothers but also including fathers. All data will be disaggregated by age, gender and diversity.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

UNICEF uses child rights framework in the planning and design of programmes. This approach places children at the centre of a response and is based on principles relating to the best interest of the child. This involves a process of designing programmes based on assessment of needs, child participation and triangulation with communities and caregivers. MHPSS programmes are designed in consultation with the persons targeted for the programming including time, location and consultation on activities. The programmes have feedback sessions with the participants at the end of each session to ensure amendments can be made throughout the cycle of programming. Child protection monitoring is completed in consultation with communities and action plans are developed with communities to implement protection strategies, these are reviewed with the community on a monthly basis through community groups that are accessible to men, women, youth and children with disability. All UNICEF partners have access to training on how to work with persons with disabilities and in the use of inclusive approaches. Programmes will be tailored for age and gender and inclusive of abilities considering the use of language, literacy levels and will be adjusted accordingly. The programme will target at risk groups through community outreach and by engaging youth, community leaders who are from different backgrounds and gender.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

UNICEF parnters with AIFO and Light of the World to support training to implementing partners on disability inclusion and also to link referral for assistance for specialized support. Structures, facilities and recreation kits and other forms of kit are designed with consultation of disability agencies to ensure the design is accessible for all and items can be used by all.Data is also disaggregated by age, gender and disability. Awareness raising is implemented at the level of the community to ensure that communities are aware of disability inclusion and support members of their community to participate in activities.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The programme will use an approach of resilience building of communities to ensure that communities can build on their own resources and strengths to limit the need of outside materials and support. The design of the multipurpose centres will be based on local natural materials. The programme aims to empower communities to participate in the building of the structures. Implementing staff will be recruited locally to provide sustainable approaches to knowledge development and resilience. Where possible, kits will be locally procured to put back into community business and use materials that are familiar and used by communities.Emergency preparedness measures will be supported at district and provincial level to support local response capacity and regional responses to reduce need and use of flights in case of further crisis or natural disaster. The programme is also looking at the set up of satellite offices to reduce the travel by car or flight.
