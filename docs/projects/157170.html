Project Summary

This project will address the food security and livelihoods needs vulnerable households in the Jebel Mara area of Central Darfur through improving agricultural and livestock production and providing vocational training and start-up materials for youth. This project will also respond to the ongoing protection needs of both the displaced and vulnerable host community members by improving community-level protection networks, strengthening case management and referral mechanisms, and supports for persons with special needs.

Needs

The context in Darfur is a result of years of layered active conflict resulted in displacement and disruption of community structures. IDPs in protracted displacement lost their household assets during the displacement and food insecurity exposes them to negative coping strategies including begging and eating less meals a day. Women and girls who are heads of households are at greater risk in these conditions given the culturally limited opportunities they have at income generation and food production. They are exposed to sexual exploitation as a coping strategy and possible GBV in addition to all the other negative coping strategies. Vulnerable host households are also exposed to similar risks. Children face specific protection risks, including exploitation, malnutrition, removal from school, GBV and abuse. Many children who have experienced or witnessed traumatic incidents have experienced psychological harm. For the population at large, but particularly for women and children, cultural and community barriers and stigma in addition to lack of support services may prevent them from coming forward to seek protection services. Specifically, women and girls may face abandonment by their families, forced marriage, and face psychological trauma. Men and boys can also be survivors of assault and face greater taboo in seeking assistance. Building trust between duty bearers and vulnerable populations as well as significant gaps in the referral pathways remains a barrier to managing protection cases. As a result of conflict and displacement, livelihood assets have either been destroyed or looted over time and farmers are often challenged to invest adequate amounts in staple crop production. Furthermore, capacity challenges with the Ministry of Agriculture means that extension services that would provide training and knowledge sharing on improved agronomic practices limit crop productivity, can result in unnecessary soil and environmental damage and result in harvest loss due to poor storage conditions place additional pressures on households. Markets have been destroyed and trade systems disrupted due to poor state of roads and insecurity. This has resulted into many vulnerable populations resorting to coping mechanisms such as burnt brick-making which is environmentally damaging.

Activities

DRC has been implementing protection, child protection, and GBV in Nertiti, Rokero and Golo under previous SHF funding, ECHO and DANIDA support. As of 2019, DRC has commenced protection activities in Um Dukun with DANIDA support. To continue to support community-driven capacity and response to protection needs, DRC proposes continued support community dialogue and referrals for GBV survivors and children at risk in conjunction with MoSA. These community services compliment Women Centres and Child Friendly Spaces that have been established by continuing to formalize, strengthen and build trust between communities and the Community Based Protection Networks (CBPNs) and Community Based Child Protection Networks (CBCPNs). DRC is currently in the process of strengthening referral systems within the organization to ensure vulnerable populations receive complimentary programme support and individual protection assistance from DRC. This is a priority as referral pathways in existence are not sufficient to meet the breadth of protection concerns and may not exist in all locations. In addition to weak state mechanisms, there are limited humanitarian partners operating in DRC’s Area of Operation.In addition to providing key community-level protection services, DRC recognizes that the main source of income for communities is agriculture, whether it be farming or livestock and, given the disruption to local livelihoods, will focus on supporting communities in Nertiti, Golo and Rokero with agricultural support in the form of crop input and training as well as livestock support in the form of vaccinations and fodder. Given the rapidly approaching timeline for staple crop planting, DRC proposes a flexible approach to either staple crops (as a preference) and winter vegetable production should the proposed action start to late in the season calendar to proceed with necessary procurements and training plans.

Impact on environment/natural resources

Agricultural activities are resource intensive, requiring land alteration and water use. DRC proposes to mitigate environmental impacts by including environmental best practices training as a component of all agricultural activities. For crop production, this includes best practices for crop rotation, intercropping, water use, and natural pest control options. For livestock production, this includes understanding the negative consequences of overgrazing and the importance of effective water use.
