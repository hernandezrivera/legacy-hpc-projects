Project Summary

This project seeks to provide access to Safe, Equitable and Sufficient water for drinking and other domestic uses to about 55,000 vulnerable populations of Host and Returnee Communities. The project will also sensitize these targets emergency affected populations on the benefits of practicing good personal and environmental hygiene (including Menstrual Hygiene Management - MHM), through House-to-House and mass session hygiene promotion campaigns. During this; information, education and communication materials (IEC) will be used. Similarly, this project will integrate WASH services in the delivery of timely, effective, efficient and quality Health Care system in the target locations, through developing and strengthening the Emergency Response capacity of the Health care workers, including understanding referral pathways for difficult health cases. The vulnerable people in host communities will be supported to have increased access to safe sanitation facilities in some selected Health facilities. The Health Facilities will also be supported with medical/health consumables, which will be affordable and accessible by the target population. This will consequently increase access to timely and quality health care by about 20,000 people living in Host and Returnee communities. Furthermore, this project will mainstream Protection (GBV and Child Protection) into the WASH and Health interventions. The project seeks to mainstream protection in the delivery of WASH and Health care services, therefore targeting about 5,000 vulnerable persons living in those locations. The project shall therefore develop and strengthen the capacity of Health Care workers on emergency response. The mainstreaming will be done in the communities and in institutions (including in Schools and Health Centers). The overall objectives of the project is to improve the living standard of Host and Returnee communities, through the provision of timely, quality and accessible WASH and Health services, without discrimination of any class of the beneficiaries, based on either sex, age nor disability status.

1 - Needs

In line with 2022 humanitarian response plan, the conflict stemming from the insurgency of non-state armed groups (NSAGs) in north-east Nigeria continues as intensely as ever. The attacks and insecurity have displaced millions of people, devastated agricultural production and other livelihoods, cut off essential services, and caused a crisis of protection. Some 8.4 million people in the north-east states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY states) will need humanitarian aid in 2022, only slightly fewer than a year ago. Of these, 2.2 million are internally displaced; 1.5 million are returnees who lack essential services and livelihoods; and 3.9 million are members of communities affected by their hosting of internally displaced people. This figure also includes the majority (an estimated 733,000) of the 1 million people in areas currently inaccessible to international humanitarian actors. Hundreds of thousands of IDPs are concentrated in ‘garrison towns’ where the Nigerian Armed Forces defend perimeters that are risky to venture beyond. This creates congested, unhealthy living conditions, and constrains displaced farmers’ livelihood options. A major cholera outbreak in the BAY states in 2021 has underlined affected people’s vulnerability and the difficulty of precluding grave threats to life and well-being. Displaced population in Adamawa state continue to experience challenges in accessing quality health services largely because indigenous IDPs hardly adapt to the culture of dwelling in formal camps but rather prefer to lodge with relatives, rent low profile accommodation depending on the income of the household. This compound health challenges except in recent times (December 2019) when a bill which considers unemployed youth, IDPs and other vulnerable groups to have access to medical care services. • Available at; Nigeria: Northeast Response - Adamawa Health Sector Working Group Bulletin No. 10, October 2019 | Humanitarian Response • Data Displacement Matrix.LABDI has identified the following needs:a. Vulnerable people in host communities and returnees lack sufficient access to safe and equitable water for drinking and other domestic uses.b. People in host communities lack adequate knowledge on good Hygiene practice to prevent common hygiene related diseases like Cholera and Malaria. They also lack WASH NFIs to use in promoting good hygienic practice.c. IDPS in Host communities lack adequate access to safe and disaggregated sanitation facilities.d. Host communities and Returnees, particularly women and children, are increasingly susceptible to disease outbreaks, especially Cholera, Malaria including GBVe. Host communities and Returnees face serious challenges in accessing health services due to various reasons, including inadequate Emergency Response Capacity of medical staff, unavailability of drugs and supplies. f. Challenges in accessing quality health care services, as a result of direct and indirect costs, including the price of quality medicine.

2 - Activities

The following activities will be implemented:i. Construction of 10 New Hand Pump Boreholes and 2 New Solar Powered Boreholes to Create access to safe drinking water to 25,000 host and returnee populations.ii. Rehabilitation of 40 Dysfunctional Hand Pump Boreholes to Restore access to safe drinking water to 30,000 host and Returnee populations.iii. Training and Equipping 12 Local Area Mechanics (LAMs) for the repairs of dysfunctional water facilities, to ensure continuous working and supply of the water facilities.iv. To form and Train 94 Water Sanitation and Hygiene Committees (WASHComs) to ensure the management and maintenance of newly constructed and rehabilitated community water facilities.v. Sensitizing 55,000 IDPs and Returnees with improved water sources on Practicing good personal and environmental hygiene, to prevent the outbreak of WASH related diseases, through Community-tailored House-to- House and Mass session Hygiene Promotion and Messages.vi. Construction of 20 Blocks of 2-compartment emergency Latrines to 2,000 Host communities.vii. Strengthening Health Care system by Provision of quality and timely life-saving and life-sustaining Health Care services in 16 Health Facilities, including Maternal and Child Health services, Sexual and Reproductive Health Services, services for victims of Gender Based Violence, Psycho Social Support and Strengthening of referral services in the target locations.viii. Making Health Care system accessible and affordable by Procuring and Providing essential drugs to 16 Health Facilities in the target locations.ix. Develop and strengthen the capacities of 32 Health Care workers in 16 Health Facilities on disease surveillance, disease outbreak prevention, preparedness and increase their response capacities for key communicable diseases.x. Rendering Psycho-Social Support counselling to at least 10 victims of SGBV, including referral to obtain medical services.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

LABDI will ensure protection mainstreaming through the involvement of Women in decision making and implementation of the project while we also integrate Menstrual Hygiene management in the course of implementation of the project. Also, there will be a collaboration with the appropriate government/private agencies advocating the prohibition of violence against the disable, girls and women in communities. Religious and Traditional leaders will also be involved in identifying most vulnerable community members and groups.
