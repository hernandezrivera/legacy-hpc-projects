Project Summary

The Blue Nile region in Sudan has long history of protracted conflicts. However, the fighting between the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) and the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) which broke out initially in Khartoum on 15 April 2023 and quickly expanded to other areas across the country continued for nine months and have resulted in a cumulative effect on the humanitarian situation with big number of internally displaced people. On 15 December 2023, Fighting broke in the outskirts of Wad Medani at Aljazeera state. This resulted in more displacements. According to the International Organization for Migration’s Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) December 28 Flash updates, more than 250,000 – 300,000 people have fled Al Jazeera State following the clashes between the RSF and SAF. Reports show that displaced people from Wad Medani and other parts of Al Jazeera State have arrived in Gedaref, Kassala, Red Sea, Sennar, Blue Nile and White Nile states. Many IDPs reportedly arrived at the existing IDP gathering sites (collective centers) the majority sought shelter within the host communities. In Blue Nile, HAC reported that about 500 IDPs arrived in Ed Damazine.
In addition, the tribal conflict that was erupted between July and October 2022 between the Hausa community and the Funj, Hamaj, and Berta communities in Roseiries, Wad Al Mahi, and Geissan localities and resulted in a displacement of 127,961 persons with 60,000 displaced within the Blue Nile Region. Besides, there was spontaneous refugee returns from South Sudan and Ethiopia with some 30,000-refugee returnees verified by UNHCR and partners in Baw, Alkurmuk localities.
Education is vital for safeguarding children through psychosocial intervention, fostering a secure environment, and restoring normality, dignity, and hope.
The conflict has adversely affected the education of girls, boys, and adolescents, especially those with disabilities, hindering their access to inclusive and quality education in a safe learning environment.
Facing the dread of losing friends, homes, and education, many children live in fear and trauma, having witnessed the death of family and friends, leading to incidents of GBV and numerous health and psychological challenges. If the war continues, no child in Sudan and Blue Nile can return to school with complexity of the situation including lack of teachers’ salaries, resulting in a lost generation deprived of essential learning opportunities. In Blue Nile Region, Teachers are not trained, emergency learning and teaching materials have not been provided, damaged classrooms and teachers’ offices, Wash facilities need rehabilitation. There are no well-established TLS, adolescent girls need hygiene kits, and teachers have no salaries or incentives to deliver teaching, additionally many students need school-based psychosocial support. The purpose of this education support project in Blue Nile is to provide access to education in safe and protective learning environment and Improving equitable quality education within a protective environment for crisis-affected vulnerable children for 12,507(6,379 F, & 6,128M) at 16 Targeted schools in the four localities. out of which 7,504(4,502F) are IDPs while 5,003(3,001F) are Host community, these include children with disabilities. The response focuses on improving access to quality education in safe environments. It targets children at risk of dropping out of school due to the crisis and ensuring retention for displaced and non-displaced children, particularly those from disadvantaged backgrounds and in hard-to-reach areas. This will involve providing psychosocial support including Social Emotional Learning (SEL), teaching materials, teacher training, safe learning spaces, sanitation facilities, menstrual hygiene education, safe drinking water school meals, and emergency preparedness.
