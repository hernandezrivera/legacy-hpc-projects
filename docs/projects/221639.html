Project Summary

This project is a flagship multi-sectoral nutrition project aimed at reducing morbidity and mortality related to acute malnutrition among children under five, pregnant and breastfeeding mothers by providing life-saving treatment and prevention care. This approach recognizes that malnutrition is influenced by multiple contributing factors, including health, agriculture, education, water and sanitation, and social protection and this will involve collaboration across various sectors to address malnutrition comprehensively. By integrating efforts from these different sectors, the project will address both the nutrition specific and nutrition sensitive interventions. Nile Hope proposed response will implement both the nutrition prevention and treatment approaches, the nutrition sensitive that align with sectoral objective 1 will include a broader social behavior change and awareness communication encompassing maternal, infant and young child nutrition, health education, promotion of optimal feeding and care practices through kitchen gardening, cooking demonstration, Vitamin A campaign and deworming supplementation in addition to zinc tablet and iron /folic acid as may be required. Nutrition specific interventions that in line with sectoral objective 2 will compose of; Screening activities for under five boys and girls, and PBW/G, Treatment of Severe Acute Malnutrition with and without medical complications in children under five, Treatment of Moderate Acute Malnutrition among children under five and Supplementary Feeding Program for Pregnant, Breast-feeding Women and Girls (PBW/G) and other vulnerable persons as well as IYCF in Emergencies, also this project will focus on strengthening on the job training for nutrition frontline staff and the CNVs. Nile Hope under nutrition sectoral objective 3 will focus on strengthening nutrition information system to ensure data quality and program monitoring. Nutrition integrated program in line with sectoral objective 4 will include effective coordination and collaboration with other sectors such as WASH, FSL, Health, Education and protection, all these sectors will be mainstreamed to ensure the other contributing factors to acute malnutrition are addressed.

1. Needs

The crisis of Malnutrition in South Sudan, Upper Nile State especially in Nasir County continue to deteriorate and influenced by multiple contributing factors, these factors continue to erode the desired foundation needed for optimal maternal and child nutrition outcomes and health. Nasir County is one of Upper Nile State counties where GAM rates remain above the emergency threshold of 15% according to the WHO standards and generally about 1.4 million children at risk of AMN and about 4.3 million children and women will need life-saving treatment and preventive care in 2025. According to the FSNMS survey conducted by FAO/WFP/GOSS and UNICEF between July and September 2024 indicated a GAM prevalence of 12.0% in Nasir. The historical data for FSNMS in 2023 indicated a GAM rate of 21.10%. This shows improvement in the GAM rate but the IPC Acute Food Insecurity for the same period is in phase 4. This GAM rate expected to deteriorate due to the contributing factors to AMN that remain high and these will have impact on nutritional status for women and children. The recent FSNMS round 30 shows that in Nasir, the food consumption indicators such as MDD, MMF and MAD are very poor and high risk for AMN; 20.7%, 9.1% and 3.9% respectively. Diseases among children under 5 are a major contributing factor as well with Malaria (of those with Fever) prevalence at 41.3%, and acute respiratory infections at 19.7%. The sanitation situation in the county is also very poor with access to improved sanitation facilities standing at only 9.4% and only 1.9% can access drinking water from an improved water source; consequently, diarrhea was 16.9%. Food insecurity is phase 4 as a result of poor harvests and inadequate support to produce due to insecurity and issues around clan revenge and child abduction threats have ensured many households with inadequate food reserves. Most of the goods and services to the County are imported through the Ethiopian border; however, these products are beyond reach of most households as market prices continue to raise and the value of the South Sudanese Pound continue to depreciate and currently the community in Nasir trade in Ethiopian currency (Ethiopian Birr) this has made the operational environment becomes difficult to tackle the determinants of malnutrition in the county effectively. Nasir County is vast and the accessibility is a challenge in which some of the facilities are along the river corridor and other are in Inland, however out of 20 sites only 8 facilities are accessible through the river and the rest of the 12 sites are accessed by walking or through air during supplies transportation. Movement from the base in Mandeng to Kier which Is one of the 8 facilities in along the river can take 7 hours on a speedboat. Supervision in the 12 inland sites involve walking around for long hours. While all these challenges are being tackled, inadequate funding has not enabled the project team to conduct as many outreach services as should be the case because the staffing structure is lean. In terms of social protections services, Nasir does not have any Cash Assistance services, BSFP is minimal but GFD is provided but sometimes intermittently. Care and feeding practices such as Exclusive breastfeeding 65.4% and ISSSF 49.3 prevalence are low, while CBF81.1% meets the target. The sub-optimal exclusive breastfeeding and complementary feeding practices will likely contribute to acute malnutrition. The nutrition sensitive interventions such as heath, WASH and FSL are fragile and need further support. Integrating efforts from these different sectors, the project will tackle both the direct and underlying causes of malnutrition. While also coordinating with other stakeholders and strengthening the leadership for health and nutrition intervention at the county health department. The project will seek to address inequalities by supporting boys and girls, women, IDPs, Returnees and host population.

2. Project activities

1. Protect, support, and promote optimal breastfeeding and complementary feeding practices. 2. Provide micronutrient supplements to young children and women. 3. Screen and treat girls and boys under five years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by severe and moderate acute malnutrition. 4. Integrate child stimulation and early childhood development services in the provision of care for children with acute malnutrition. 5. Work with the WASH Cluster to ensure provision of WASH kits to children with acute malnutrition and sensitize communities with hygiene and sanitation messages. 6. Work with FSL Cluster to promote chicken raring, establish kitchen gardens and promote seed multiplication for vegetable gardens and support fish preservation. 7. Collaborate with health cluster to ensure prevention, screening and treatment of malaria and management of diarrhea and provide Zinc and ORS to children with diarrhea at the nutrition service delivery. 8. Work with the Protection Cluster to ensure mainstreaming of services and referral of child protection and Gender Based Violence (GBV) cases identified in the nutrition sites. 9. Link with Cash and Voucher Assistance programs to bene t nutritionally vulnerable children and women. 10. Undertake community engagement activities during the design, implementation, and monitoring of nutrition services to strengthen Accountability to Affected Populations. 11. Conduct regular program and field monitoring and supportive supervision to strengthen evidence-based decision-making. 12. Conduct nutrition coordination meetings with the government, implementing partners, and donors.
