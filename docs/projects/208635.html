1 - Needs

The proposed interventions will aim at maximizing coverage and access as well as improving timely referral from the community to all IMAM sites. SC will ensure that care is provided to malnourished children for as long as is needed while ensuring that caregivers, especially mothers and other vulnerable caregivers, are supported to be available at care centers in order to reduce the chances of children presenting malnutrition to deteriorate their condition. The interventions within this sub-sector will focus on improving MIYCN practices through capacity-building and social and behavior change interventions to improve knowledge, attitudes, and practices. These activities will focus on filling knowledge gaps and reinforcing positive behaviors. Through the proposed interventions, the community will be sensitized on IYCF and be encouraged to support PLWs and caregivers to uphold recommended feeding practices.

2 - Activities

In partnership with UNICEF, Save the Children international will continue lifesaving treatment of children with Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM), while encouraging appropriate Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) and care practices. SC plan to reached 287,000 children through monthly screening of children 6-59 months for early identification, referral and 20,818 children admitted for treatment of Severe Acute Malnutrition in 29 Out-Patient Therapeutic Program (OTP) sites. 48,000 children 12-59 months will receive two rounds of deworming and Vitamin A supplementation for children aged 6-59 months will be provided while12,000 pregnant and lactating women and adolescents will be provided Iron Folic Acid (IFA) to prevent anemia and improve their immunity. 128,950 Mothers with children 6-23 months will be targeted to improve complementary feeding through cooking demonstration for food diversification and 1,950 children will receive homebased fortification using MNPs/SQ-LNS to enrich family food for prevention of micronutrients deficiency. Moreover, this partnership includes the implementation of strategies for behavior change communication, 128,950 support group members will be reached through Mother-to-Mother Support groups, peer groups for adolescent girls as well as community engagement sessions targeting community leaders, male caregivers, mothers-in-law, and other stakeholders.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

By adhering to the principle of "Do No Harm" in all of its activities, SC will ensure the safety of its activities. In addition to key informant interviews, suitable sites and locations will be selected for meetings and focus group discussions. To minimize risks, secure sites near beneficiaries will be prioritized to ensure that people can return home before dark. In addition to safeguarding training, Save the Children's staff and enumerators will also receive training on gender equality, inclusion, and accountability. All new SC employees are oriented to and required to sign the SC Safeguarding Policy and the Code of Conduct as part of their contract. SC mainstreams protection, gender equality and inclusion across all programming. The food assistance program refers to vulnerable households with minors as caregivers over 65, households with children, households with disabilities, and households headed by women.

9.1a - Specify (and give URL link if possible to) the relevant needs assessment document.

After more than 13 years of conflict, violence, and insecurity, Northeast Nigeria continues to remain in a large-scale humanitarian and protection crisis, affecting the lives, livelihoods, and well-being of millions of people in the region as well as cross-border. In Borno and Yobe states, an estimated 4.2 and 1.6 million of people are in need of humanitarian assistance in 2022 respectively. Of this number, 1.8 and 0.2 millions are internally displaced. The conflict in Northeast Nigeria continues to have a direct impact on the nutritional status of both states, which is extremely alarming according to the latest Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance (NFSS) round-12 (December 2022) conducted in August and September 2022. In Borno State, malnutrition has continued to rise in the state, with levels of prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (WHZ) rate increasing from 11.8% to 12.3% in 2022, and SAM increasing from 1.4% to 1.9% compared to the same period (NFSS R10) in 2021. Northern Borno and Eastern Borno domains have amongst the highest GAM and SAM rates in Borno State, with 16.5, 15.2 GAM rates and 3.6, 2.9 SAM rates respectively (NFSS R12). In Yobe State, the prevalence of GAM has reached a rate of 12.0%, and SAM cases of 2.5% in Northern Yobe domain, which shows a slight decrease from 16.0 GAM and 3.3 SAM rates of the same period (NFSS R10) in 2021. However, slight decrease in Northern Yobe domain, the rates are considered high/serious as per WHO-UNICEF emergency/severity threshold (10 %-< 15%). Moreover, an increase in organised criminal group activity in the northeast Nigeria in recent months has contributed to additional displacement and reducing households’ ability to engage in income-generating activities. Levels of food insecurity in conflict-affected areas have been aggravated due to the intense flooding experienced in 2022 and the continual rising costs of food, with most LGAs in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa expecting to remain in either Crisis or critical.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

A complaints response mechanism will be reinforced at all sites and communities in line with core humanitarian standards (CHS). AAP sessions will be integrated into the monitoring system of the partner; all the training curriculums, community sessions, and sensitization meetings to ensure knowledge of AAP principles. To successfully implement the project activities, Save the Children international will coordinate with relevant health, protection, WASH, and other actors within the community to establish efficient cross-referral pathways. GBV and protection referral mechanisms will be strengthened in close collaboration with the nutrition sector and protection sectors. In addition, Save the Children international has an established Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) to ensure that program beneficiaries can raise complaints and receive feedback from Save the Children International. The CRM will also have a system that can report concerns back to UNICEF. At all the project sites, there will be a dedicated CRM desk. The participation of women will be ensured by positioning male and female volunteers at the helpdesks and posting the complaints box in a secure location near guard posts. Additionally, female community volunteers will approach mothers and caregivers for feedback, and a hotline number will be posted at health facilities through which mothers and caregivers can provide feedback anonymously.
