Project Summary

This project will seek to provide immediate lifesaving response to vulnerable crisis affected children from IDPs and host communities of Ayod and Fangak counties through strengthened comprehensive case management including family tracing & reunification; referral for services; provision of critical age and gender appropriate psychosocial support services in a timely, holistic and child friendly manner.
The project will also seek to enhance the protection environment of vulnerable crisis affected girls, boys and youths to enable durable solutions through establishment of community based child protection networks, foster family groups, youth/adolescent clubs, Child Friendly Spaces, Youth friendly spaces, child rights clubs, school based child help desks and recreational centres. This will create enabling conditions for realization of solutions and prevention of further displacement which leads to family separations; identification of risks, dangers and impediments; advocacy and mitigation measures; psychosocial support and peaceful co-existence initiatives.
A total of 7,500 people (3,750 females and 3,750 males) will benefit from this project. 4,500 of them which is 60% will be children (2,250 girls and 2,250 boys). 3,000 which is about 40% will be adults. 3,000 (40%) will be host community while 4,500 which is about 60% will be IDPs.

Needs

Ayod county has experienced an increase in internally displaced persons (IDPs) as a result of numerous fighting incidents in many parts of Jonglei state since 2013, the latest incident happening in mid-January 2018. Ongoing conflict in Jonglei continued to negatively affect humanitarian needs among the population in the first quarter of 2018. Clashes between armed groups and pervasive insecurity, particularly in northern Jonglei caused displacement among affected communities, negatively impacting the ability to meet their primary needs. According to a report by REACH South Sudan, Situation Overview of Jonglei state, January to March 2018, it is reported that conflict and deteriorating humanitarian conditions continued to drive displacement within Jonglei in the first quarter of 2018. Focus Group Discussion (FGD) participants interviewed in Bor PoC site in March reported that some displaced populations from Uror and Nyirol travelled to locations in Pagil payam in Ayod county, reflective of conflict driven displacement from these areas. FGD participants interviewed in Bor PoC site in February also reported displacement within Ayod, driven by fears of armed groups advancing from Wau town into areas surrounding Mogok and Jiech. Displaced populations then reportedly moved to Pagil Payam, placing further strain on resources in these areas. This population is facing greatest need from the compound effects of displacement and recently displaced people are facing greatest protection risks due to actual absence of frontline services. Fangak county has been experiencing tension and sporadic violence following the influx of IDPs which has led to the increase of IDPs population in the county. The continued violence in Mayiendit , Leer, Koch and Guit county of southern Unity State has displaced thousands of civilians. Many IDPs are expected to return back home from Bor PoC and Juba PoC to Fangak county. A huge influx of IDPs from Waat and Walgak of Akobo West, are also seen in Fangak. The May 2018 incident of Southern Unity State topically increased the Fangak IDPs population and this was verified by NRC, IOM and ADA assessments and verification exercise conducted in May, June and July, 2018. Due to humanitarian access constraints of many locations, the IDPs continue to remain in dire need for humanitarian services as many locations are very remotely located and difficult to reach due to the fact that there is no availability of vehicles in the area. The IDPs population summed up to 45% percent of the total population in Fangak county. It is evident from the foregoing findings that children and youths from Ayod and Fangak counties are prone to numerous forms of abuse including recruitment and use in armed groups, family separations, early marriage, killing/injury, abduction and domestic violence among others. These forms of abuses often have life long effects on the physical and psychological well-being of affected children.

Activities

There are other actors implementing child protection activities in Ayod and Fangak like INTERSOS and CAO but who do not cover the whole counties. ADA is already aware of specific locations that they are working in and will implement the following activities in alternative payams/bomas like Menime, Pagil, Haat and Mogok in Ayod county; and Juaibor and Keew in Fangak county to avoid overlaps:Activity 1: Conduct induction training on case management and psychosocial support to 16 staff (8 case workers & 8 PSS facilitators) for 3 daysSixteen new staff will be recruited and trained. These will be 8 case workers and 8 PSS facilitators (8 males and 8 females). The induction training will provide them case management skills to enable them deliver on their work. Case workers will identify; document; assess the protection needs of children; plan and implement the plan for intervention on the identified needs including referral to other actors for services; and follow up of cases; using global child protection Case Management minimum standards, that the project manager and one child protection officer has been trained on. The knowledge acquired will be cascaded down to all case workers so that they will be better equipped with necessary skills to enable them deliver the project.PSS facilitators will be trained on basic skills like how to communicate with children, signs of children facing distress or other forms of abuse, Psychological First Aid, and planning and management of mobile Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) activities, which are age, gender and culturally appropriate.Activity 2: Provide comprehensive case management service to 600 children (300 boys and 300 girls).Case workers will identify 600 children (300 boys and 300 girls). Identification will be done using priority list from standard operating procedures for case management in South Sudan. Once identified, children with critical protection concerns or those at high risk will be supported through comprehensive case management procedures in line with minimum standards of case management. Children with health needs and those malnourished will be referred to other actors in-charge of health and nutrition service provision; and those out of school will be referred to actors providing education for school enrolment. All child survivors of Gender Based Violence (GBV) will be referred to GBV actors in the target locations. Cases of child recruitment and use in armed groups will be reported to UNICEF through Monitoring and Reporting Mechanism (MRM) focal person. ADA will ensure that children released from armed groups will benefit from release and reintegration services. The well-being of all children with critical protection concerns will be monitored through regular follow up visits to ensure that new protection needs and risks are prevented or responded to in good time. Unaccompanied or separated children in need of family tracing and reunification (FTR) will be referred for family tracing through child protection/FTR national coordinating agency, which is Save the Children.Activity 3: Establish and train 20 Community Based Child Protection Network group (comprising of 20 members; 10 men & 10 women), on prevention of further harm to crisis affected children for 2 days.Activity 4: Establish and train 1 foster parents group comprising of 20 people (10 men and 10 women), on interim care, positive parenting and general child protection for 2 daysActivity 5: Conduct Psychosocial support (PSS) activities targeting 1,400 children (700 boys and 700 girls) through mobile Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) ADA will support 1,400 distressed children by targeting them through mobile CFS activities which will be age, gender, culturally appropriate and child friendly. Through mobile CFS the facilitator will be moving in different but safe locations with activities or materials in order to reach 1,400 children (700 boys and 700 girls) from far corners of the project sites

Beneficiaries will receive information about the assistance during project inception meeting and regular meetings with beneficiary representatives to update them on project implementation progress. Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The ADA adheres to Age and Gender Diversity Mainstreaming (AGDM) in all its activities and in recognition of the fact that women and children are generally disadvantaged not just because of cultural norms but also because of the nature of the current displacement wherein women and even children bear the burden of looking after the family. Women, men, boys and girls will be provided with context, age and gender specific messages. Focus Group Discussions will be conducted with women and men separately; as well as with specific age groups of boys and girls to ensure equal participation.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

ADA has two Prevention from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) focal persons who were recently trained by IOM on PSEA 6 core principles, prevention, reporting and response mechanism. ADA will seriously consider protection of beneficiaries and project staff from sexual exploitation and abuse. All staff will be trained on the same and alleged cases of PSEA will be responded to expeditiously in a confidential manner; and in a way to promote safety, respect and dignity of survivors. All newly recruited staff will be taken through ADA code of conduct policy, GBV and child safeguarding policies all of which stresses the need to protect beneficiaries or children from all forms of exploitation and abuse. Reporting structure and channels including contacts of PSEA focal persons within and outside the organization will be made know to all staff and all will be encouraged to report allegations of sexual exploitation and abuse.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Consultations with vulnerable people will be the core principles during the implementation of this project. ADA will encourage participation of community representatives in decision-making and project planning process. ADA will work to eliminate existing barriers (physical, institutional and attitudinal) to consultations with communities and instead encourage inclusive ways of seeking and disseminate information. For example use at least different means of communication (Megaphones, churches, events and house to house visit) and simple language or drawings to be sure to reach everyone. ADA will ensure assessments are objective and inclusive; identify the most vulnerable people; collect and provide disaggregated data by sex, age and type of impairment taking into account contextual factors such as family or social support, discrimination, economic situation, distance to services etc. ADA will also map existing services to refer and respond to urgent basic and specific needs of the most vulnerable children with protection concerns.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

ADA will use Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) tool to ensure the community have multiple ways to share complaints and information about their lives with beneficiaries, with respect to this project. ADA will improve an inclusive system of interacting with communities and promote minimum standard for all mechanisms for regular tracking, reviewing and action feedback for the project. A complain desk during project implementation will be installed in ADA offices and Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) to allow complains and feedback from the beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries. All cases will be recorded and addressed accordingly, confidentiality will be assured across the process. Complains will be managed with care, dignity, confidentiality and respect whilst reporting to complaints desk. During the project implementation ADA will set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, or address concerns and complaints.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

ADA will carry out activities that aim at reducing and mitigating protection risks including the cases related to Sexual Gender Base Violent, family separations and recruitment of children into armed groups. ADA will continue promoting an environment where protection and assistant are delivered in safety and with dignity. Risk, Vulnerability and Capacity Analysis: ADA will create a context-specific protection risk and benefit analysis. ADA will analyse relative importance (likelihood and impact) and manageability (prevention or mitigation) for different groups and individuals. Desk Review and Needs Assessment: Gather situational protection information on the major risks for the affected population (what and for whom), sources of risks, and any community-based or self protection mitigation mechanisms. This information could come from protection needs assessments, case management, and feedback mechanisms. ADA will build a monitoring system and an accountability framework on the basis of identified protection risks and benefits. ADA will continue to ensure that protection is mainstreamed in all of its activities as noted in the previous sections. ADA being protection actor will plan to further strengthen its links with protection cluster in 2019. ADA is a protection implementer and will remain as protection actor to continually monitor the protection needs in order to adequately serve groups at risk, such as youths, adolescents, child headed households especially female headed ones, separated and unaccompanied children, children engaged in worst forms of child labour, exploitation and abuse; and other most vulnerable children. In line with protection referral systems, ADA will continue to conduct comprehensive case management and PSS activities for especially vulnerable children. ADA will also continue to coordinate with Mobile response protection Teams to ensure protection representation on missions in project target locations and inform protection risk analysis.

However, particular attention will be paid to those with specific needs including elderly, female head household, unaccompanied girls and boys, person with no community links. Time and the speed of response: Use of participatory approaches in an emergency situation normally takes too much time. Participatory processes also require a commitment in terms of time from the affected population. This factor can be especially important when populations are under dire need or other forms of stress. By giving up their time, they may allow opportunities to pass - time spent in meetings with you is time not spent earning a living, collecting water or foraging for food, and so on. Successful participation activities will take into account participants’ own schedules and obligations, and successful participatory project teams will demonstrate awareness of, and gratitude for, the time that people give to the project.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

The ADA will carefully consider and tailor each response to the verified needs on the ground, paying much particular attention to the most vulnerable, and with an overarching focus on protecting the safety, security and dignity of populations targeted with assistance. The location we are targeting has always remained a safe heaven for IDPs because it’s surrounded by swamps and it’s not easily accessible to the military using their heavy weapons. The chances are very low for the government to pursue them. In addition to above explanation, this area is inhabited by the same community and they don’t fight each other. This area remains a safe haven for IDPs who come from different parts of Upper Nile, Unity and Jonglei states who are mainly of Nuer ethnic group same as the hosts and are welcome within the community. ADA would continue providing appropriate emergency and maintained a suitable permit–to-work system where appropriate. The project would take into account of the relevant potential for violation of rules by employees and the factors that influence the potential for rule violation (e.g. workload, time pressures and practicality of the rules) and address the same accordingly.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

ADA will continue to focus on protecting the safety, security and dignity of populations targeted with assistance. Selection of project beneficiaries will be done through an objective in-depth assessment and project beneficiary representatives will be involved in planning, designing, implementation, and decision making process of the project. This will enhance community ownership of the project. Project beneficiaries will also have opportunities to provide feedback on project execution process through planned feedback and response meetings. All feedbacks from beneficiaries will be taken seriously and used to review project design and implementation. Project monitoring will ensure that project staffs treat beneficiaries with respect and dignity by ensuring that they do not take pictures of nude children, they protect all information of every child through sharing on need to know basis and through password protection; and that all information of every beneficiary is treated with utmost confidentiality.
