Project Summary

WFP will provide life-saving food assistance to the entire Rohingya refugee population (approximately 860,000 people) through General Food Assistance. Beneficiaries will receive entitlements that can be redeemed from WFP-contracted local retailers across the camps. E-vouchers provide more flexibility, dignity and greater dietary diversity for the refugees while also creating market linkages with the host community and the wider Bangladeshi economy. To ensure an effective and efficient emergency intervention, WFP will preposition enough in-kind food to rapidly respond to the needs of approximately 13,000 households should they be displaced by monsoon, cyclone or conflict.
In addition to providing large-scale food assistance, WFP makes sure to address the needs of the most vulnerable households through providing access to fresh food and promoting income generating activities in the camps and host communities. WFP will provide complementary food vouchers to 260,000 extremely vulnerable individuals through Fresh Food Corners and Farmers’ Markets. WFP will also engage 32,500 Rohingya refugee households in skills development training and provide inputs for mask making, sanitary pad production, homestead vegetable production, community aquaculture and the recently launched recycling project. In cooperation with the Government of Bangladesh, WFP will support 30,000 women with monthly subsistence allowance and business development support in Ukhiya and Teknaf.

1 - Identified Needs

Three years after the influx, Rohingya refugees and surrounding host communities are more vulnerable than ever with limited livelihood opportunities and dependency on external assistance. Even before COVID-19, in late 2019, 94 percent of refugees were largely or entirely dependent on external assistance and while host community households are slightly better off than Rohingya refugee households, 41 percent were vulnerable in late 2019 with households headed by women more vulnerable (52 percent) than those headed by men (38 percent).Food consumption outcomes for both refugee and host communities improved in July compared to April 2020 thanks to expanded humanitarian assistance, highlighting the continued need for assistance (WFP, August 2020). Furthermore, at the height of the crisis, 50 percent of host community households were dependent on external food assistance and only 3 percent had their own produced yields to consume.One of the biggest impacts of COVID-19 and related national and local lockdowns on both Rohingya refugee and host communities has been the economic consequences with a reduction in livelihood opportunities, restrictions on movement to search for income opportunities, depleted savings and asset stripping. In June, only about half the refugee and host community households reported being involved in some form of income generating activities (WFP COVID-19 Impact Monitoring Survey, July 2020). The promotion of self-reliance and livelihood opportunities for Rohingya refugees and vulnerable host communities is therefore essential in addressing the economic repercussions of the pandemic.COVID-19 and consequent restrictions also exacerbated pre-existing vulnerabilities and exposed vulnerable households, in both refugee and host communities, to increased risks. The most affected households include single women-headed households and households without a working age man; households with at least one member aged under 5 and above that require assistance to complete daily activities (e.g. people with disabilities, older persons and people suffering from chronic illness); and large economically vulnerable families (generally with more than five members). Gender norms and roles have not improved during the pandemic as containment measures have negatively impacted how women and girls negotiate the restrictive social norms that govern their lives. Women, who are mostly engaged in the informal economy, and vulnerable groups dependent on daily work have been hit the hardest by the economic consequences of the pandemic (ISCG Gender Hub, October 2020). For instance, pre- and post-crisis vulnerability of host community women-headed households increased significantly compared to men-headed households with close to half women-headed households found to be highly vulnerable following the crisis (WFP, July 2020).
