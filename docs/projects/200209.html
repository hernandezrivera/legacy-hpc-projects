Project Summary

The overall objective of the proposed project is to address the emergency protection and basic assistance to the most vulnerable Migrant Workers in Lebanon.
The deteriorating socio-economic situation added to their challenges, making the most basic of services and needs, including shelter and healthcare, unattainable for many. This project aims to reduce the risk of evictions, exploitation, abuse and human trafficking through the provision of NFIs, Voluntary Return, Cash for Rent, access to protection services (case management and socio-legal services), MHPSS and awareness raising on preventive measures by contributing to the Migrant Sector Outcomes under the ERP 2023:
Outcome 1: Vulnerable migrants have improved access and knowledge of protection services, legal assistance and MHPSS assistance. The proposed project’s Objective 1 “Provision of Protection Services” contributes to this goal through:
Act. 1.1: Delivering psycho-social support (PSS) and legal awareness sessions to at least 2080 migrants, including referral to individual therapy where relevant. This includes awareness raising material provided to landlords for prevention of abuse in rent increase.
Act. 1.2: Providing legal counselling and assistance to at least 300 migrants including legal aid, negotiation and access to justice, provided by Amel's specialised in-house legal team focusing on issues such as salary theft, detention, lack of legal residency, employer-employee disputes.
Act. 1.3: Protection services to least 1800 vulnerable migrants with urgent protection case management who face violence incl. GBV, abuse, exploitation, and human trafficking, among other challenges.
Act. 1.4: Screening and registering all migrants requesting support (though Amel hotlines, and referrals from NGOs, migrant groups, embassies, etc.) (at least 4460) to ensure they meet the vulnerability criteria to be eligible for Amel services.
Outcome 2: Vulnerable migrants have improved access to multi-sectoral, dignified and lifesaving assistance. The proposed project’s Objective 2 “Temporary shelters” contributes to this goal through:
Act. 2.2: Provision of 3040 NFIs including Blankets, hygiene kits, dignity kits, luggage, and baby kits.
Act. 2.3: Providing 1860 at-risk migrants with Cash for Rent (CfR), especially migrants with children to reduce risks of eviction, homelessness, violence and abuse. CfR is a sub-form of MPCA that supports all migrants including undocumented migrants who face the highest vulnerabilities.
Act. 2.2: Provide training and awareness raising on cultural sensitivity and migrant inclusivity to build the capacities of 60 humanitarians and service providers
Outcome 3: Vulnerable migrants have access to Assisted Voluntary Return and Reintegration (AVRR) assistance to their country of origin. This goal will be contributed to through Objective 3 of the proposed project: “Voluntary Return” through:
Act. 3.1: Facilitating counselling and Voluntary Return (VR) for 300 Migrant workers, including those in detention, back to their home countries managing all necessary processes including waivers of penalties and documentation to travel, in addition to coordination with GS offices, embassies, employers
Outcome 4: Efficient and evidence-based humanitarian programming is enabled across sectors due to coordination, and improved data collection and analysis
Act. 4.1 Conducting a nation-wide assessment on migrants extracted from its database of already supported cases to be agreed upon with the migrant sector
Act. 4.2 Conducting a thematic assessment which findings will be utilized for dissemination to other partners and beneficial for increased support to MWs
Act. 4.3 Amel was voted by the migrant core sector members to co-lead the migrant sector as co-coordinator. Accordingly, Amel sustains its effort for the remaining 9 months of the year by including its technical and 12+ years’ experience in the support of MWs, due to the large amount of work required.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

The project is in the first place designed to be responsive and remedial, to counter the protection concerns of the target population, whilst ensuring that they are involved in creating their own safe protection environment. No intervention will be taken without the expressed will, consent and involvement of the targeted individual. The activities are rolled out by trained and specialised staff. Confidentiality of cases is ensured where out and the do-no-harm principle is embedded both within this project as well as more broadly throughout Amel's program design. Gender sensitivity is also engrained within Amel's approach - and the team is able to provide gender-sensitive services throughout its centres.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Amel is a member of the CHS alliance and a strong advocate of Humanitarian Accountability to beneficiaries, in addition to donors. Amel has a number of well-practiced quality assurance mechanisms and accountability strategies in place. Constant communication between the project coordination team and field staff, as well as regular field visits ensures good interaction between all staff involved in the project. This allows feedback for rapid responses to changing dynamics in the field. Additionally, this program has a hotline for (P)VoHT, including vulnerable migrants at risk of sexual/labor exploitation. The Do No Harm principles are embedded in the project design, and social stability factors are taken into account. The project builds on existing deep-rooted relationships developed with local communities. Services have been identified by engaging with over 26 migrant communities in Lebanon. The team who developed the proposal has a deep understanding of the context & risk mitigation
