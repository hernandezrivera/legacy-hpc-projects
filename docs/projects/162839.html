Project Summary

This project aims to improve the living conditions of 50,638 refugees in Shagarab camps in Kassala and SSR in North Darfur through the following WASH, Livelihood & NFI responses:
- repair and extension of water distribution pipelines to rehabilitate disused sections, eliminate leaks and reach new distribution areas;
-installation of new elevated water tanks to increase storage capacity and ensure greater access to water;
-chemical-physical and microbiological testing system for water and constant monitoring system to ensure water quality in all refugee camps;
-strengthening of the solid waste collection and disposal system through adequate equipment and training of the personnel involved;
-implementation of awareness campaigns in hygiene and sanitation to spread the knowledge of correct hygienic-sanitary practices and prevent the spread of diseases;
-construction of household latrines.
-provision of livelihood assets.
-Vocation training.
-Extension service.
-NFI provision for both new and old caseload replenishment

1 - Needs

By the end of 2018 a joint participatory need assessment lead by UNHCR and COR has been conducted to identify gaps, challenges and define future programming in support of improving the living condition of refugees both in camp and out of camp context at national level. As part of that exercise Al lait participatory need assessment identified the following main pressing need and gaps:• Personal hygiene kits to support health and protection of over 4,100 refugee and host community women and girls;• Community-based protection and livelihoods programming to support well being of 2,400 single women-headed households;• Shelter materials for over 4,600 refugee families in need and vulnerable host community members; and• Scale-up of mobile clinic and nutrition services to improve access for 15,000 refugees living across Al Lait localitySince COOPI has been supporting on improvement of water supply, sanitation, hygiene and livelihood and through different projects, COOPI will complement the activities currently being implemented extending the scope and focusing on enhancing self reliance related actions. On the other hand water supply systems and piping networks in East refugee camps are older and in need of rehabilitation and extension. Water shortages are common in camps due to broken systems, inadequate water storage tanks and increased demand. Shagarab camps have poor drainage, and stagnant water issues require vector control support to mitigate vector- and water-borne illness. Household latrines remain insufficient and do not guarantee safe access to dignified sanitation to all. The health situation is still critical also because of an ineffective solid waste management system that exposes refugees to the risk of infection and contagion.

5 - Environment Marker

B+: Medium environmental impact with mitigation

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Through the rehabilitation of water structures, an optimization of water resources is obtained. The careful management of this resource plays a crucial role in the environmental sustainability of refugee camps because it is a scarce commodity. Moreover, the project will have a positive contribution towards environment conservation derived from the management of solid waste management activities since the waste is an important cause of environmental pollution.
