Project Summary

Most vulnerable populations in Jonglei, Western Equatoria and Eastern Equatoria states continue to endure multiple shocks that have continued to erode and undermine lives and livelihoods with consequent in poor food and nutrition security outcomes. A combination of poor harvest, protracted conflict with recent escalation of hostilities in the Equatorias, economic meltdown, and now floods which have persisted in Duk, Twic East and Bor South among other counties have conspired to drive food and nutrition insecurity to unprecedented levels. Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates are above emergency threshold of 15% in most of the target counties with 1.3 million children under the age of 5, and at least 676,000 pregnant and lactating women at risk of acute malnourishment (FSNMS, 2021).
The situation of vulnerable communities is compounded by COVID 19 pandemic that has resulted into significant impacts on businesses including negative economic implications that have further eroded livelihoods. This coupled with increasing levels of inter-communal conflict, especially along Bor South- Pibor border; and renewed waves of violence in Western Equatoria continues to displace most vulnerable in addition to access contraints and impediments; further limiting livelihood activities. Recent skirmishes in Bor South County have further led to multiple displacements and disruption of critical livelihood activities (UNOCHA, 2022 Updates).
Community Aid for Relief and Development (CARD) proposes actions targeting 10,000 households who make up 60,000 beneficiaries (10000 men, 10000 women, 20000 girls and 20000 boys) includes people with disabilities and will respond to prioritized activities via livelihood protection support through prepositioning crop, vegetable and fishing livelihood kits supplies through FAO pipeline and livestock interventions, which are in line with the second and third FSL Cluster strategic objectives emergency livelihood and livestock support and supporting capacity building, asset, market support and value addition.
CARD has been operating in the targeted counties since 2014 and has recently supported at least 36,000 beneficiaries through FAO, SSHF among other donors. CARD has other programs with competent staff, logistics and community goodwill; good relations with communities, local leaders, RRC officials and state/county ministries of Agriculture and Animal Resources. CARD’s strategy will also ensure upholding core principles or programming via quality interventions, ensuring Accountability to Affected Populations, inter-cluster synergies – via linkages with nutrition, health, protection, protection of beneficiaries including GBV and an innovative integrated multi sector approach. AAP and PSEA will be mainstreamed including putting in place measures of sharing PSEA, AAP feedback among target beneficiaries. CARD will in addition regularly review and adapt ways of working with affected populations and increase levels of transparency and participation of affected boys, girls, women and men. CARD will also mainstream cross cutting measures-gender equality and women and youth empowerment, protection, environmental conservation, AAP including taking into account GBV mitigation measures.

1 - Needs

Most vulnerable populations in Jonglei, Western Equatoria and Eastern Equatoria states continue to endure multiple shocks that have continued to erode and undermine lives and livelihoods with consequent in poor food and nutrition security outcomes. A combination of poor harvest, protracted conflict with recent escalation of hostilities in the Equatorias, economic meltdown, and now floods which have persisted in Duk, Twic East and Bor South among other counties have conspired to drive food and nutrition insecurity to unprecedented levels. Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates are above emergency threshold of 15% in most of the target counties with 1.3 million children under the age of 5, and at least 676,000 pregnant and lactating women at risk of acute malnourishment (FSNMS, 2021); and 8.3 million people, including refugees, expected to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2022 lean season, a 7 per cent increase from the 7.7 million in 2021 (HNO 2021; FSMS 2021). The situation of vulnerable communities is compounded by COVID 19 pandemic that has resulted into significant impacts on businesses including negative economic implications that have further eroded livelihoods. This is in addition to an unstable economic situation in the country which continues to impact most vulnerable –including those with disabilities ability to access key markets, while conflict, flooding and seasonal effects are further limiting flow of both commercial and humanitarian goods and services, especially in Equatorias (Access Constraints, 2021/ 2022; NGO Forum Updates 2021/ 2022). This coupled with increasing levels of inter-communal conflict, especially along Bor South- Pibor border; and renewed waves of violence in Western Equatoria continues to displace most vulnerable in addition to access contraints and impediments; further limiting livelihood activities. Recent skirmishes in Bor South County have further led to multiple displacements and disruption of critical livelihood activities (UNOCHA, 2022 Updates). Unprecedented food insecurity levels have further contributed to women, children, older persons and persons with disabilities to protection concerns including cases of gender based violence including rape, with cases reported in Tambura, Mvolo, Duk, Twic East and Bor South; while they collect firewood and gather wild foods; in addition to the adoption of negative coping strategies among adolescent youth and women (Protection Trends, 2021). Community Aid for Relief and Development (CARD) proposes actions that will support livelihood protection through prepositioning crop, vegetable and fishing livelihood kits supplies through FAO pipeline and livestock interventions, which are in line with the second and third FSL Cluster strategic objectives emergency livelihood and livestock support and supporting capacity building, asset, market support and value addition. CARD has been operating in the targeted counties since 2014 and has recently supported at least 36,000 beneficiaries through FAO, SSHF among other donors. CARD has other programs with competent staff, logistics and community goodwill; good relations with communities, local leaders, RRC officials and state/county ministries of Agriculture and Animal Resources. CARD’s strategy will also ensure upholding core principles or programming via quality interventions, ensuring Accountability to Affected Populations, inter-cluster synergies – via linakges with nutrition, health, protection, protection of beneficiaries including GBV and an innovative integrated multi sector approach. CARD will in addition regularly review and adapt ways of working with affected populations and increase levels of transparency and participation of affected boys, girls, women and men. CARD will also mainstream cross cutting measures-gender equality and women and youth empowerment, protection, environmental conservation, AAP including taking into account GBV mitigation measures.

2 - Project activities

Prepositioning through Cluster Pipeline and distribution of agricultural livelihood kits - crop, vegetable and fishing kits as well as farming tools to 10,000 households to facilitate emergency food production and rehabilitation of livelihoods for both main season and dry season (fishing and vegetable kits). 2. Establishment of 30 kitchen gardens at Nutrition facilities including capacity building on agronomic practices including cooking and nutrition. 3. Support to seed fairs using locally sourced seed; seed security and multiplication strengthening/ Support market functionality 4. Provision of livestock treatment and vaccination campaigns targeting 100,000 livestock including training and equipping of 120 community animal health workers to reduce livestock mortalities, morbidities and increase productivity and access to animal source foods.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiary feedback including any complaints raised will be tackled CARD in consultation with implementation committees including making appropriate changes where necessary to ensure beneficiary views are taken into consideration.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CARD will ensure vulnerable members including people with disabilities are engaged and actively participate throughout the project cycle, including being part of implementation committees and have their feedback taken into account.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CARD staff and associated personnel have signed a code of conduct that includes PSEA including having in place communication to target beneficiaries on their rights and reporting mechanisms in case of violations, which include PSEA.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

CARD will hold consultative sessions at the beginning of the project including throughout implementation in addition to formation of implementation committees to spearhead beneficiary messaging on the project including collection of feedback and complaints that will inform implementation.
