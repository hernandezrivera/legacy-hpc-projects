Project Summary

This project aims at supporting women and girls survivors of GBV or those at high risk including women and girls with disabilities through the provision of quality, accessible and acceptable GBV multi-sectoral services in six safe spaces, two women Hubs, and 14 community Women-Led organizations across Gaza Strip and West Bank governorates. Also, the project focuses on promoting the role of grassroots community organizations by supporting their solidarity and community outreach in combating GBV issues, women's rights, and PSEA. The project has three main outcomes:
1- Improved accessibility, availability, and quality of GBV services for 5000 women, girls, men, and boys, with a focus on women and Girls with disabilities, Elderly women, and GBV Survivors in the affected communities in the Gaza Strip and West Bank.
2- Enhanced capacity of 300 GBV governmental and non-governmental specialized and non-specialized service providers on GBV case management, GBV detection and referral, CVA in case management, and PSEA.
3- Increased awareness for 10,000 local communities' members about GBV available services, human rights, cyber-crimes and violence, and the developed standard operating procedures of PESA.
The project is built based on the affected community needs and feedback, considering the vulnerable groups and women and girls at risk, in addition to UNFPA partners' experiences and updates from the field, the established safe spaces, and home visits. Moreover, this project builds on the robust experience and evidence of the importance of CVA in case management as part of the GBV multi-sectoral services to guarantee the protection of women and girls GBV survivors and at risk, and enhance the role of integration, consistency, and coordination between the service providers in the referral pathways. Additionally, the project will meet and cover the needs and requests from other clusters for capacity building about GBV safe referral, PSEA, and CVA for protection, and from the GBV Sub-cluster for advanced and technical services’ capacity building.
Moreover, the project will reflect UNFPA experience and focal points' capacities in PSEA to spread the knowledge and reporting mechanisms, and awareness within all target groups through WLOs and CBOs IPs.

1 - Needs

According to HNO 2022, the occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) remains a protracted protection crisis, where too many Palestinians struggle to meet their most basic needs and live in dignity. The crisis is characterized by more than 54 years of Israeli military occupation, lack of respect for international humanitarian and human rights law, internal Palestinian political divisions, and recurrent escalations of hostilities between Israel and Palestinian armed groups. COVID-19 has exacerbated the crises in the past two years. This year has witnessed further deterioration in the humanitarian situation in the oPt due to: 1- The most serious escalation of hostilities between Palestinian armed factions in the Gaza Strip and Israel since 2014. 2- Movement restrictions imposed by Israel, citing security concerns, which impede the access of people and goods into and out of Gaza, hamper the implementation of infrastructure projects and delay economic recovery. 3- An increase in confict-related Palestinian casualties in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, and in settler-related violence against Palestinians and their communities. 4-The restrictive and discriminatory planning regime applied by Israel in Area C and in East Jerusalem, which continues to prevent Palestinians from addressing basic housing, livelihood and basic service needs. 5-The intra-Palestinian divide between Hamas and the Fateh-led Palestinian Authority (PA), which remains unresolved, deepening territorial fragmentation and reducing the capacity of local institutions in Gaza to deliver basic services to the population. The 2022 People In Need (PIN) was calculated based on the first-ever household-level Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA), rather than on a harmonized dataset generated from the various cluster needs assessments, as was the practice in past years. This year’s more precise inter sectoral estimate of needs has resulted in a slight reduction in the PIN for 2022. The People in need of Protection (417,435 Boys, 392,152 Girls, 482,369 Men, and 463,453 Women), Elderly (27,829 M, 35,650 F ) , PWDs (2.4%/ total: 42,130) (10,018 Boys, 9,412 Girls, 11,577 Men, 11,123 Women), with a total of 1,818,888 (1.2M Gaza, 630K West Bank).The reduced PIN is not indicative of a reduction in needs; on the contrary, the socio-economic situation on the ground has deteriorated in 2021, due to the sustained impact of the COVID-19 Pandemic, exacerbated by the escalation of hostilities and unrest across the oPt in May 2021, deepening the severity of humanitarian needs. Of the total, 64 per cent of people in need live in Gaza, and 36 per cent of people in need live in the West Bank. Within Gaza, 63 per cent of residents need humanitarian assistance, while 21 percent of West Bank residents need humanitarian assistance. Based on the GBV SC SDR 2022, a number of factors contributed to and in some cases increased the risk of GBV for women and girls. These factors include Israeli occupation and confict-related violence, the May escalation, prevailing traditional cultural norms in Palestinian society, the increase in economic and social deterioration, and the outbreak of COVID19 pandemic. Women and girls face a number of risks, including discrimination and various forms of GBV such as early/forced marriage, intimate partner/family violence, sexual harassment, rape, incest, denial of resources, psychological abuse and sexual exploitation and abuse. 31 per cent of households in the West Bank and 19 per cent in Gaza are concerned about the safety and security of girls, while 24 per cent and 17 per cent in the two areas respectively are concerned about the safety and security of women. According to the latest available violence survey published by the Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistic (PCBS) and was updated in July 2022, 59.3 per cent of (married or previously married) women between the ages of 15 and 64 experienced violence in the 12 months preceding the survey – 70.4 per cent in Gaza and 52.3.3 per cent in the West Bank. Of those, 57.2 per cent experienced psychological violence, 18.5 per cent physical violence, and 9.4 per cent sexual violence, while 9.6 per cent experienced the emerging issue of cyber violence. Most importantly, the SDR highlighted the 2022 key priorities: Harmonise and unify women protection legislations, reform and strengthen legal protection to women and girls, introduce laws that criminalize domestic violence, sexual harassment, and marital rape, reform the male guardianship laws, address political factors that hinder implementation and enforcement of women protection, mainstream GBV as a crosscutting priority in the oPt humanitarian response. On the one hand, the key vulnerable groups are: Women and girls in general, Women and girls with disabilities, Female-headed households, Bedouin women, Internally displaced women, and Women and girls living in refugee camps.There are various factors affecting the status of women and girls in the Palestinian society. Cultural and social attitudes and beliefs around women’s roles remain powerful barriers to effective prevention and response to GBV. In consonance with one of 2022 protection cluster publications, it is estimated that 1 million people will need GBV protection assistance in 2022, with the highest number of identified people in Gaza. Women and girls continue to face widespread and varied forms of GBV, including intimate partner/family violence, sexual harassment, rape, early/forced marriage, incest, denial of resources, psychological abuse, and risk of sexual exploitation and abuse. These protection risks exist in a context where GBV services are limited and access is restricted due to the imposed restrictions on movement by the occupation as well as COVID-19 measures.

2 - Activities

Output 1: Enhancing access to comprehensive and multisectoral services for 6 safe spaces, 2 women Hubs, and 14 Women-Led organizations across Gaza Strip and West bank including (health, legal, PSS, dignity kits, vocational training courses, and cash assistance) for the most vulnerable women and girls and with disabilities. Activities include;1. Provide case management services for 800 women, girls, including those with disabilities through the six safe spaces and two women hubs. The case management includes health and sexual and reproductive health, cash assistance in case management, mental health psychosocial support, individual psychosocial counseling, shelter, couple counseling therapy, psychosocial services, dignity kits, individual legal counseling, and legal documents for women, court representation, assistive devices and occupational therapy for women and girls with disabilities.2. Provide psychological support services for 5000 women, girls, men, and boys including those with disabilities through the 6 safe spaces, 2 women hubs, and 14 WLO which were targeted by previous UNFPA projects to enhance their technical and managerial approaches in the most vulnerable areas across Gaza Strip and West Bank.3. Provide legal counseling and representation for 500 women of the most vulnerable women, girls, boys, and men in the five governorates in the Gaza strip, Nablus, Hebron, Tulkarem, and Jenin.4. Provide case management fund for 500 GBV survivors who are GBV survivors with different risk levels. The high-risk cases will be considered for emergency cash assistance to save her life and meet her protection needs urgently (within 72 hours), avg. 200$. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

“Do No harm” is one of the main GBV guiding principles and approaches according to UNFPA minimum standards and all partners of GBV service providers know how to apply these principles in their work with GBV survivors. UNFPA IPs service providers and GBV SC members received in September 2022 a case management session with UNFPA consultant about the global standards of case management, to protect the identity, personal information, and safety of GBV survivors by not disclosing any personal information about the survivor or the incident to anyone without the survivor’s explicit permission and consent. Also, GBV service providers have robust experience in ensuring GBV survivors are protected from harm and in providing psychological and emotional safety to survivors who may be at high risk from the perpetrators or from their family members due to notions of family honor. Additionally, UNFPA targets men and boys in most of its interventions to balance the male to female dynamics in the family and community, and to solve any risky challenges through the male counseling units and the couple therapy. Also, CVA in case management is basically designed to contribute to the safety, dignity, and resilience of women and girls in humanitarian contexts as it offers discretion and flexibility compared to in-kind assistance. Additionally, UNFPA conducted a CVA risk assessment in October 2022 to get feedback from beneficiaries and case managers about the potential risks linked to CVA and the response mechanisms that are adopted by women in different communities, and how to mitigate the negative mechanisms such as early marriage, forced marriage, selling their assets and jewelry, and beggary from the community. On the one hand, UNFPA IPs case managers received two induction sessions from UNFPA international humanitarian team about CVA in case management and risk mitigation.0UNFPA conducted different capacity building trainings to GBV SC and other clusters’ members on PSEA, and establish safe and confidential pathways such as SAWA hotline that covers Gaza and West Bank, where we inform women and girls and all target groups to use if any incident of SEA occurred or was suspected.

On the other hand, the awareness, outreach initiatives, advocacy campaigns and the social media materials will cover wide areas to ensure all targeted communities have a better knowledge about GBV, PSEA, Cyber Crimes, rights of PWDs and other important topics.
