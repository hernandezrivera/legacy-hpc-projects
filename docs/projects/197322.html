Project Summary

As part of SAMS mandates in Health sector, SAMS 2023 programs will be in line with Health Sector 1st , 2nd and 3rd strategic objectives, and as SAMS runs 33 HFs in Idleb, Aleppo, Al Hasakeh and Ar Raqqa governorates, SAMS will continue to provide health support for population in 2023 through continuing implementing activities of strengthen Health capacity to prepare for, detect and deliver a timely response to disease outbreaks, including Cholera and strengthen health system capacity to support continuity of care, strengthen community resilience, and respond to IDP movements. In this program, SAMS will prioritize the reduction of morbidity and mortality through improving the access to quality health care (primary and secondary, specialized services, MCs, and an ambulatory system, C19 and Cholera response) in 33 HFs and MCs, they are:

SAMS will support facilities on different levels including coverage of staff incentives, running costs, provision of pharmaceuticals and other medical and non-medical commodities, and rehabilitation/maintenance of the HFs. SAMS will provide recruitment support, capacity building, and technical supervision to ensure the HFs are staffed with qualified personnel and to enhance compliance with standard treatment protocols. SAMS will provide the following services at the primary healthcare level: SRH services (antenatal care, postnatal care, family planning, normal deliveries, gynecological consultations, and clinical management of rape), EPI, communicable and non-communicable diseases, Nutrition, MHPSS, health awareness, and basic laboratory and radiology services. Services at the secondary health care level will include gynecologic surgeries and cesarean sections, High-risk pregnancies trauma management, major surgeries, hospitalization services, blood transfusion, hemodialysis, and ICUs, in addition to C19 and Cholera response (CM, Triage, PPE/IPC, CHWs). This program will also provide cancer treatment to Breast, colon testicular and ovarian, ALL, and Lymphoma patients (Hodgkin, and non-Hodgkin); Cath Lab, Pathology, GI endoscopy, Dental services, Paediatric ALL, ICU, NICU, PICU, Kidney transplant medications. Additionally, SAMS will support the ambulatory system (5 ambulances). In addition, as part of SAMS' strategy in building the capacity of the health care professional in NWS through advancing SAMS’ investment in its medical education program: Suppor Residents' Doctors; Support Midwifery and Nursing Education; and Mental Health Education. SAMS will coordinate Health Cluster through 4Ws and HeRAMS and constant participation and active membership in TWG: COVID-19 TF, Cholera TF, SRH, MHPSS, Non-Communicable Diseases, SIG, Midwifery Capacity Building Strategy for NWS, Secondary Health Care, and Drug Supply Line, in addition, to coordinate with other NGOs on the referral, and the contribution to DHIS2, notwithstanding its membership in the NGO Forum, SNA, and others.

3 - Needs

Situation in Syria continues 3.1 million people out of the population in NWS are in need of health assistance (OCHA Northwest Syria Sitrip, 24 October 2022), with only 372 out of 541 facilities remaining fully or partially operational (Q2 HeRAMS 2022). Furthermore, it’s important to mention that in NWS, much more funding is needed to continue these life-saving activities and reach all people in need. Without sufficient and timely funding, an estimated 1.7M people are at risk of losing access to primary health care services, medicines, emergency obstetric care, neonatal care, and vaccines, among other health interventions, where the Health Cluster in NWS suffers gaps of 71% of its required funds for essential life-savings services for almost affects all health facilities NWS Funding Gap Analysis, October to December 2022. Moreover, the fragile health system in Syria continues to face concurrent emergencies and chronic challenges which affect the availability and quality of health services across Syria, as well as the physical and mental wellbeing of the population. With the cholera outbreak spreading in NWS starting from 19 September, the health needs in the area increased and the outbreak added addition burden on the HFs. The total suspected cases in NWS 9,108 (8 deaths) as per Daily Updates of Cholera Outbreak in NWS as of 12 November 2022.

4 - Implementation of activities

SAMS will work on the following activities: Recruitment and Support the salaries of the HF staff, Rehab works for HF and supports the operational costs, Capacity building for medical and non-medical staff, Providing PHC services according to updated EHSP including CD, NCD, child health, SRH and mhGAP, provide trauma, surgeries, SHC, and in-patients services in SAMS hospitals and specialized centers including ICU, PICU, NICU, maternal and child health, IM, hemodialysis, oncology, MHPSS, and Internal Medicine hospitals as well as Cholera response. In addition to procuring medical/non-medical commodities for the HFs, Raising awareness sessions, health promotion, to the community through CHWs, self-care and staff care activities, Provision of the ambulatory and referral services, Implementation of safe and effective medical waste management protocols, Sustaining services of EPI, Provide medical education (midwifery, nursing, and mental health) and Midwifery deployment, in addition to the support of the resident doctors’ project, and Provision of PSEA and safeguarding training and support the DHIS2. MEAL Field monitors will conduct quarterly visits using checklists/spot checks, including verifying attendance sheets, payment receipts, accuracy, and patient data entry, in order to monitor activities and assess impact. Besides SAMS CRM channels, the MEAL team will conduct patient satisfaction surveys (including protection mainstreaming indicators).

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Safe Closure of Facilities and Protecting Data. Possibility: Low. Impact: High Mitigation: Electronic data and information is properly protected and backed up according to SAMS SOPs (SAMS EMR system is already in place). Risk: Women and girls are prevented from attending certain training or sessions. Possibility: medium, Impact: High, Mitigation: Coordinate and communicate with the community leaders to identify our work, in addition, to conducting outreach visits. Risk: Persons with disabilities, older persons, and areas away from centers. Possibility: High. Impact: High. Mitigation: Outreach teams will be allocated to reach beneficiaries who cannot access the centers to participate in activities for people with severe disabilities and elderly people with mobility difficulties in remote areas. Beneficiaries' access will be facilitated by providing transportation as needed to ensure their effective participation.Risk: C19 and Cholera outbreaks may affect the implementation and increase the risk of infection transmission to beneficiaries & stafflikelihood: HighMitigationUpdating the regulations continuously and promoting vaccination among the staffProvision of PPE to staff and BENIPC training for the staff


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Beneficiaries are subject to PSEA risk. Possibility: Low. Impact: High. Mitigation: The availability of a clear complaint mechanism within the service centers and the definition of it during the definition of the beneficiaries of the services of the Center and explain the mechanism of its flag "complaints box and mechanisms" follows the complaints section directly to the private line and mail moderator complaints section and brochures and the mechanism of regular complaints, in addition to the availability ofperiodic training for service providers and ensure supervision of effective technical service.SAMS displays various posters to enhance the awareness of both the health facility staff and the beneficiaries regarding the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), the services provided, the patients’ rights and responsibilities, and the Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM). The CRM poster includes the complaints/feedback channels (Phone hotline number, WhatsApp, Viber, and email), and SAMS installed a designated ‘Questions/Comments/Concerns’ box. Moreover, SAMS developed a short animation video displaying patients’ rights and SAMS’ CRM channels, the video is published via SAMS social media channels.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Control by unaccepted groups / GoS. Mitigation measures: (ATC), Information security SOP; Relocating the activities locations; Inform the partner civil authorities (HD and Civil Local Authorities), of the suspension and the reasons, in order to play their role in negotiation and advocacy, and apply the agreements of neutralizing humanitarian facilities.Risk: UNSC XB non-renewal. Impact: High. Mitigation: SAMS has multiple channels of funding and has cross-border access to pass commodities to Syria with its wide local coordination with local authorities. Alongside contingency plans from OCHA and UN in place prior to implementation.Risk: Potential high turnover of staff receiving salaries. Mitigation measure: Potential high turnover of staff receiving salaries; Repeating the training regularly to ensure that all new staff delivering it; Adopt appropriate salary-scale to retain the staff as possible.Risk: Theft. Mitigation Measures: Safety and security upgrades for facilities, including CCTV cameras and guards; Photo verification of received shipments, signed waybills, and notification if any missing items noted in HF count within 72 hours; Review of stock/inventory records.Risk: Financial risks (Cash Transfer/ Financial Diversion). Mitigation: SAMS utilizes official channels, authorized by the Government of Turkey, to transfer money from Turkey to Syria.
