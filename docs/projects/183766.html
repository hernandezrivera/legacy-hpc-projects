Project Summary

Libya still faces multiple challenges, including low cash liquidity and fuel shortages, further compounded by restrictions due to COVID-19. The Libya Food Security Sector based on the 2021 Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment and other secondary data analyzed that 26% of refugees in Libya are in need for food assistance. The proportion is the highest among all population groups: 25% for IDPs, 19% for Migrants, 4% for Non-dispaced persons, and 10% for Returnees.
Due to a lack of access, transparency, the impossibility to monitor, and given that, according to international law, individuals under detention in a country must be provided with the means to live with dignity by the government of this country, WFP, along with the partners of the Food Security Sector took the decision not to intervene in detention in order not to legitimize detention and provide assistance blindly where access and monitoring are impossible.
Consequently, alternative forms of assistance to vulnerable refugees have been developed and this project, targeting food insecure migrants outside detention centres, has been designed to support the food security and nutrition status of refugees and asylum-seekers while preserving them from any form of exposure, stigmatization and discriminations, ensuring their protection and promoting their inclusion within the local communities or other sustainable solutions.
WFP and UNHCR have built their functioning partnership since June 2020 to provide food assistance to vulnerable refugees and asylum-seekers in Libya based on analyzed data. The most food-insecure non-detained refugees and asylum-seekers will be identified in close partnership with UNHCR. WFP will mobilize its full capacities to procure and distribute its ready-to-eat food assistance to vulnerable refugees and asylum-seekers in urban settings. For every distribution, COVID-19 precautionary measures such as masks, disinfection, social distancing, temperature checks will be systematically applied.
