Project Summary

A Rapid Assessment Team (RAT) of MERCY Malaysia was deployed to Cox’s Bazar from 24th June to 10th July 2018; and findings revealed that the needs and gaps are still exists especially access to critical services especially for pregnant mothers and children. Thereby, in order to fill the gaps and complement other recovery initiatives, MERCY Malaysia together with CIS recommends to build a Comprehensive Primary Health Care (CPHC) Centre in Jamtoli Camp 15. Main objectives of this program are to improve access to comprehensive primary health care services of Rohingya and host communities, with maternal and child health services included as well as 24/7 emergency care and labour ward (for only delivery). While the expected outputs from this are: 1) one (1) CPHCC will be established and operationalize for at least a year: 2) to provide safe, adequate and integrated service to comprehensive primary healthcare and 3) to advocate, promote and facilitate implementation of agreed Essential Health Services Package in camps. The program will be run together with local partner, Community Initiative Society (CIS) or also known as Dhaka Community Hospital Trust (DCHT). Target beneficiaries for this program are refugees and host communities in Camp 14, 15 and 16 with approximately 140,159 beneficiaries.

Objectives

To improve access to comprehensive primary health care services of Rohingya and host communities especially in Camp 14, 15 & 16

Needs

Based on assessment conducted in June 2018 in Jamtoli Camp 15, the team found out that: 1. Most reported that they were aware of health facilities that provide these services but the distance to travel was too great. Another concern is regarding their safety when travelling back to home, from health facilities especially when they have to wait for long time to see the doctors and finish late evening; 2. Another major issue is that less than 3% of the Rohingya population had been vaccinated before displacement. This is clear from the epidemics of diphtheria and mumps in the camp; 3. There are a reasonable number of primary health posts available in the area (camp 14, 15 & 16) but lack of specialist services. There is also a lack of facilities that can provide 24/7 service; 4. The nearby International Organization for Migrant (IOM) primary healthcare facility in Block A is being relocated; 5. Due to long time of waiting, some mothers who have small kids stop seeking for healthcare services - no one to look after their kids at home.

Activities or outputs

Outputs:Output 1.0: One (1) CPHCC is established and operationalized Sub-activity: Strengthening the establishment and provision of healthcare services in the affected community Output 2.0: Safe, adequate & integrated service to promote CPHC Sub-activity 2.0: Strengthen the technical capacities of the health workforce (including medical officer, nurse, paramedic, field worker and other supporting staff) in line with the national standard and protocolsSub-activity 2.1: Advocate, promote and facilitate implementation of agreed Essential Health Services Package in camps Effective services that are going to be provided: 1. Curative care 2. Maternal, newborn, child and adolescent heatlh 3. Nutrition 4. Communicable disease 5. Mental Health and Psychosocial Services 6. Non-communicable diseases 7. Emergency referral 8. Support services 9. Community Health

Key project/performance indicators

1. Responsiveness - a) Reduced time waiting at the clinic b) Improved quality time spent with doctor 2. Continuity of care - % patients that satisfy with the services received (Will be based on three (3) which are information, relationship & management) 3. Community Empowerment - One (1) Community Health Worker (CHW) Team is been trained and deliver services 4. Effective Referral System - Emergency Referral (ambulance 24/7) & rapid two-way referral between PHC facilities and hospital are established
