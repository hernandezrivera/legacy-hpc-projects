Project Summary

The overarching goal of the Emergency Telecommunications Cluster (ETC) is to ensure that humanitarian actors have access to vital telecommunications services that allow them to perform their work in common UN operational areas. Effective communications systems are critical to support and enhance the safety of the humanitarian community.
Telecommunications infrastructure throughout Syria has suffered significant and ongoing damage due to a decade of devastating conflict. While Damascus maintains good coverage by Mobile Network Operators (MNOs) and humanitarian radio networks, other locations including Aleppo, Deir-Ez-Zor, Homs, Hama, Qamishli and Tartous require continued upgrades and maintenance to improve their coverage. In 2023, the ETC will continue to maintain and enhance the operational coverage of Security Communication Systems by enhancing and expanding the existing VHF network and Deploying Push To Talk (PTT) technology in common operational areas.
Reliable connectivity also remains a major challenge throughout the country. In 2023, the ETC will continue to provide data connectivity services in Aleppo, Deir-Ez-Zor, Hama, Homs and Qamishli and improve ETC data connectivity services in all interagency hubs where these services are provided. As the fuel crisis and a lack of mains electricity remains an urgent issue in the whole country, the ETC will maintain and upgrade the existing solar power systems to provide reliable power for the ETC’s data connectivity services in common hubs. Further, in support of the United Nations Department for Safety and Security (UNDSS), the ETC will upgrade the essential connectivity equipment in all Security Operation Centres across Syria.
There is a need for the continued provision of shared connectivity and telecommunications services to facilitate cross border movement and operations in neighboring countries where Syrians have been forced to flee. The ETC will continue to provide dedicated ICT helpdesk support personnel in the inter-agency response area in Gaziantep, Turkey, and provide technical guidance and support to the response community in Turkey, Jordan and Lebanon as required.
In 2023, the ETC will further develop the technical capacity of national humanitarian ICT staff through the delivery of a technical workshop and simulation exercise. The ETC will continue to assess the needs on the ground to support and enable humanitarian activities where and when required. Contingency resources may also be required to support new common operational areas or the extension of common services, based on assessments conducted periodically throughout the year.

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

In the Syrian context, the provision of reliable inter-agency communication services is key for the delivery of humanitarian assistance. In the case of underfunding, the ETC will be unable to fulfil its mandate to provide all operational areas with vital communications services, which will seriously hamper humanitarian operations in Syria and will affect staff safety in the field. Capacity development is a critical element in Syria, as in some areas of operations rely entirely on skilled national staff due to the difficulty in deploying international staff. A shortfall in funding would restrict the ETC’s ability to organize required training for national staff in 2023.
