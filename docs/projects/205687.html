Project Summary

The emergency support project is a 1 year response plan to assist vulnerable IDPs and host community comprising of 7500 households and 45,000 population in Gengeng,Abang and Yirol payams of Yirol West, Malek and Adior payams Yirol East and Dor, Bunagok payams in Awerial county of Lakes state .The intervention aims to ensure that lives are saved and human suffering is reduced through provision of emergency dry and main season vegetable, crops and fishing kits for vulnerable households and livestock interventions.
The prioritized objectives to achieve the overall goal will include:
Cluster Objective 1:Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5 and 4).
Cluster Objective 2: Enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock support (in IPC 3, 4 & 5).
Cluster Objective 3:Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb and adapt shocks contextual changes.
The main activities to be carried out to realize this objectives and achieve the overall goal will include:
CO1: Food distribution through in kind and cash voucher assistance.
CO2: livelihood support: crop & vegetable seed kits, tools, fish kits and livestock vaccination and treatment in-kind except nutrition vouchers (cash + poultry + goat) and seed trade fairs (vouchers);
CO3: local procurement of seeds & cereals (aggregation centers); value chain addition and training: improved agronomic practices, post harvest losses.
WAV needs assessments, as well as Post Distribution Monitoring from ongoing programs, have identified significant unmet needs for both IDPs and host families who have lost productive crop agriculture or fishing assets due to ongoing conflict or displacement and as a result has reduced purchasing power and incomes that has hindered food security. It is critical to support these households to begin to restore lost crop agriculture or fishing livelihoods, thus enabling them to have increased food availability at personal production level, as well as small income for increased food access. The project will distribute fishing kits and then train fishermen on the usage of the new fishing equipment. Early distribution of fishing kits will support fishermen to take advantage of the fishing season. Similarly the project will distribute vegetable seeds and tools to beneficiaries (farmers) during early February for planting in the same month. In addition, the project will do post planting monitoring and provide periodic technical advice and follow up as necessary. During April and May, WAV will distribute staple crop seeds and tools to farmers. The beneficiaries of staple crops and tools will take advantage of planting season and will do the planting between May and June, 2024. The majority of IDPs in Yirol West,Awerial and Yirol East traditionally men take care of the livestock and they are also involved in the fishery business, while women do most of the agricultural work. Due to these cultural and traditional approaches, the majority of agriculture beneficiaries will be female headed HHs.

1. Needs

Recurrence of violence Intra and inter communal conflicts and floods in parts of Lakes state has had detrimental effects on the food security and livelihood situation affecting mainly women and children. 5.5m people in South Sudan are currently food insecure (IPC October 2023-July 2024) a trend expected to rise due to seasonal forecast in locations prior to multiple crises, already below thresholds in food security, WASH, Health, Education and Nutrition . The county targeted in Lakes is Yirol West in Gengeng,Yirol and Abang payams,Malek and Adior payams Yirol East County and Dor, Bunagok payams Awerial county currently in IPC 3 and jointly have a populations of 96,000 people with 5% of the population currently IDPs. (IOM DTM, ’18). These Counties host the IDPs integrated into the host community without access to work, with food distribution as the main source of livelihood (WFP,NPA and CRS 2021 ). FSNMS round surveys indicate that at least two thirds of respondents in Yirol West,Awerial and Yirol East Counties reported having received some humanitarian assistance. Multiple displaced IDPs have significantly higher levels of food insecurity at households (43%) than residents (35%). Female headed households also has higher prevalence of food insecurity (45% versus 33% for male headed households), a possible indication of gender based vulnerabilities. Significant decrease in the amount of households that receive income from casual (skilled and unskilled) labor passed from 20% to 30% to merely 6% to 8% in Yirol West,Yirol East and Awerial counties as a result of multiple displacements. Populations are now engaged in alternative coping livelihoods options such as sale of crops, livestock and unsustainable natural resources (poles, grass, firewood, charcoal) and/or begging. National production is expected to be below the five-year average, which will lead to low domestic supply in 2023 and a continuation of extremely high food prices. At the household level, food availability is now expected to be lower than previously projected due to crop damage from Fall Armyworm infestation. Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence rates are above emergency levels malnutrition thresholds (GAM >15%) in the targeted payams of Yirol West,Awerial and Yirol East counties. Disease outbreaks such as cholera and other AWDs have had significant effects on food security. Populations have resorted to less preferred dietary alternatives due to loss of livelihoods to both natural and conflict fueled causes, crop failure and multiple displacements. Prices of commodities have presently increased by over 200% due to local currency depreciation. Inflation currently stands at over 600% (SSNBS, 21). With over 61000 people said to be flood prone and inter tribal/intra tribal conflict in the targeted counties (52% Female) alone, this has affected road accessibility within the aforementioned county causing food prices to skyrocket in the local markets. Livestock conditions have also been impacted negatively due to changed migration patterns to cattle raid conflict, insecurity, and concentration of animals in small areas has raised concerns about disease outbreaks, reduced access to resources and veterinary extension services, as well as increased tensions between herders and farmers. Importantly, the terms of trade between livestock and cereals have drastically declined across all markets, undermining the purchasing power of aggro-pastoralists in Yirol West,Yirol East and Awerial counties.
