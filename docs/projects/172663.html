Project Summary

In Sudan, DRC's country-wide program purpose is designed to Respond to Conflict-Induced Emergencies, Protect Human Rights, Promote Sustainable Livelihoods and Foster Social Cohesion for Displacement Affected Communities and Host Communities.
The program revolves around three platforms which contribute to 1) immediate humanitarian assistance through emergency response, 2) recovery and resilience-building through durable solutions , and 3) helping communities working towards longer-term goals by addressing the root causes.
Activities funded under this project would allow DRC to work alongside local authorities and affected communities in providing social and protection services and meeting basic needs (encompassing food security, shelter and non-food items and WASH).
Under this project, DRC will primarily be targeting Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and returnees either in current areas of interventions or in newly accessible areas in Central Darfur and South Kordofan. DRC will also build on its current program (Protection, GBV, Livelihood) currently delivered mixed-background communities living in under-served areas of Khartoum state. With that in mind, it is worth noting that host communities are also support where and when needed as our projects are implemented using community-driven and conflict-sensitive approaches
In addition to direct and immediate humanitarian assistance, DRC remains committed to supporting communities in achieving durable solutions following years of conflict and displacement. Recognizing that durable solutions are not something that can be achieved in isolation, DRC’s strategy in Sudan is to build partnerships around durable solutions projects, working closely to offer holistic opportunities on durable returns, by assisting displaced and returnee communities.
Throughout its interventions, DRC works closely with national humanitarian actors and relevant Government of Sudan ministries to ensure that the activities planned are in line with government broader standards and plans as well as community needs, and that local duty bearers have the capacity to build upon the integrated program that DRC will implement and sustain its positive impacts.

1 - Needs

The context analysis underpinning DRC’s 2021 programme for Sudan relies primarily on external sources such as: the Sudan 2020 Humanitarian Response Plan and Humanitarian Needs Overview, Sector/cluster reports, the country-wide MSNA led by REACH, which we cross reference with DRC internal sources including: DRC Sudan’s Strategic Programme Document for 2020–2021, baseline Conflict Analysis (Central Darfur), regular situation reports, rapid assessments, as well as localized needs assessments conducted which have been conducted by our Programme and MEAL teams in the second half of the year. Indeed, preliminary results from DRC’s 2020 Central Darfur MSNA have influenced activity selection for 2021. They indicate prevalence of complex, multi-dimensional gaps in target communities. Similarly, DRC’s implementation approach and planned activities in Khartoum state are informed by both the area-based profiling analysis and a rapid shelter assessment conducted in Q3 and Q4 2020. As leading humanitarian actor in Sudan addressing Protection related needs for displaced populations, DRC will continue to focus on addressing Protection and GBV needs in particular in Central Darfur but also in Khartoum and South Kordofan. Needs identified in terms of food security (including general food distributions), in-kind agriculture assistance, reconstruction and rehabilitation of livelihoods assets will also be central to the needs addressed by DRC's integrated programming in 2021. Additionally, in Central Darfur, DRC will increasingly shift focus on durable solutions and addressing root-causes under which resilience-building interventions, so crucial for restoring livelihoods and promoting sustainable agriculture programming, will be developed on top of the FSL emergency support provided to vulnerable households most in need. WASH needs, primarily in terms of access to essential hygiene items, COVID-19 prevention, and improvement of community infrastructure and hygiene facilities will be in addressed in both Central Darfur and increasingly, in South Kordofan where DRC is currently conducting an assessment. While there has been a greater investment in generating evidence on the humanitarian context in Sudan, an evidence gap remains. More research and studies are needed to build upon and contextualize the results of MSNAs available to date. DRC, has resourced its MEAL team with this gap in mind and is now positioned to better inform its programming and approaches but also, to support the humanitarian community in this regard. Complementary to this, the Mixed Migration Centre (MMC) will also expand its research work among migrating populations in Sudan and strengthen the evidence base underpinning DRC’s programme strategy.

2 - Activities/Outputs

In 2021, DRC Sudan will continue to focus on four sectors (based on OCHA’s coordination clusters in Sudan) these are: Protection, Food Security & Livelihoods (including Economic Recovery), Shelter & non-food items (SNFI), Water Sanitation & Hygiene (WASH).More specifically, DRC is planning on delivering the activities outlined under section 4 of this project. With regards to the GBV response in Sudan, DRC is planning on opening Women and Girls Safe Spaces to complement wider protection programming with PSS services in a confidential and private setting. These spaces can be utilized to increase community outreach for awareness and sensitization and to provide individual case management services. DRC will select the locations of the space based on need and priorities of communities, with priority being given to Khartoum (Sherg al Nile locality) and South Kordofan (Kadugli, Dilling, Abu Jubaiha, Abu Karhsola, al Rashad, Um Dorain, Heiban, Al Abbasiya. In addition to establishing new centers where needed, DRC will rehabilitate and support existing centers. Activities run at the centers will include: recreational activities, life skills activities, individual and group counselling (non-specialized), Psychological First Aid (PFA) and skills building/vocational activities within the context of psychosocial support programs. DRCs structures CBPN at the local community level, such as village or neighborhood, as opposed to at a larger locality level, thus increasing the buy-in and participation of local communities and taking into account local dynamics in a conflict sensitive manner. Dignity kit distribution is not limited to distribution but is coupled with sharing information on GBV, available GBV services, GBV helplines numbers, and messages on PSEA. Dignity kits distribution is entry point to raising awareness and reaching women and girls.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Shelter and NFI distribution can have an environmental impact as many materials contain single-use plastics. DRC will continue to look into sourcing more local and environmentally friendly NFI and shelter materials whenever possible. In addition, DRC will seek to source locally available food assistance to avoid the environmental impact of importation and transportation over long distances.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

DRC develops activities and selection criteria based on a context specific risk analysis and continued engagement with local communities. All activities and delivery methods are developed in consideration of the risks and vulnerabilities of local communities and target individuals/groups. DRC approaches are built around community-based protection which are particularly effective at mitigating risk factors and strengthening protective factors in the community. Throughout implementation, DRC conducts ongoing protection monitoring in order to identify new risks and vulnerabilities and assess the impact of our programming on the protection environment. In 2020, DRC has strengthen protection mainstreaming through trainings of all its programme staff.
