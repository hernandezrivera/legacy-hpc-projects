Project Summary

UNICEF will reach 167,123 beneficiaries (10% PwD) with child protection services, among which are 116,484 children and adolescents, and 50,639 adults. Women and girls will constitute 60% of beneficiaries. 70% of the project target will be in Rohingya refugee camps and 30% in host communities. UNICEF will reach the above mentioned target through 16 camps in align with rationalization following one partner one camp with maximum 50% coverage of each camp.
UNICEF will adopt a public health approach to preventing abuse, exploitation, violence and neglect of children, within a social-ecological framework and according to fundamental Protection principles including Do No Harm and the Best Interests of the Child.
UNICEF will work with government institutions to strengthen child protection systems. We will maintain partnership with the Department of Social Services (DSS) to build capacity of the social service workforce, and provide technical support to justice sector stakeholders including judges, police, probation officers and social workers to improve children's access to child-friendly justice, while promoting juvenile justice principles such as diversion and alternative measures to detention. UNICEF will provide human resources and technical input to the Office of Deputy Commissioner to accelerate Birth Registration through upgraded systems and financing.
UNICEF will ensure that survivors of abuse, exploitation, violence and neglect - including Unaccompanied and Separated Children and other vulnerable children - have access to case management, care plans and referrals. Family-based alternative care will be promoted. UNICEF will ensure that adolescent girls and boys have access to Life Skills-based learning and are engaged as agents of positive change in their communities through access to information, social mobilization and participation. UNICEF will provide access to structured and unstructured Mental Health and Psychosocial Support for children and adolescents to build resilience and their coping skills, and give Positive Parenting guidance to caregivers including awarness and community sensitization in prevention and respond to any violence against children and women. Covid19 mitigation measures embedded within regular programming.
UNICEF will establish and make functional Community-Based Child Protection Committees through trainings, technical guidance and logistical support and ensure community led awareness raising and sensitization in camps and host community.
UNICEF will embrace its role as a lead agency in the Child Protection Sub-Sector Strategic Advisory Group, Protection Working Group, Case Management Technical Working Group, Anti-Trafficking Task Force and other coordination forums, ensuring close collaboration with Government, NGOs, Civil Society and the private sector. UNICEF will also provide technical guidnace and organizational development support to implementing partners and government counterparts.
Child Safeguarding and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) will be mainstreamed. UNICEF will lead the training of implementing partners and ensure mechanisms are in place in line with the humanitarian code of conduct.
UNICEF will mainstream Emergency Preparedness and Response through DRR capacity building for service providers and communities, and through pre-positioning supplies.
This project will give priority to the inclusion of Persons with Disabilities (PWD) ensuring equal access to services in dignity.
UNICEF will promote social cohesion and peaceful existence within and between communities through promoting free flow of accurate information, dialogue, coordination, joint activities, sport and recreation.
UNICEF will strengthen Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) by facilitating complaint and feedback mechanisms, and monitoring to ensure that feedback improves programming. Children, adolescents and caregivers - including those with disabilities - will be consulted.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

According to the Protection Working Group, 896,879 Rohingya refugees currently living in camps in Cox's Bazar require humanitarian assistance in 2023. An additional 541,021 girls, boys, women and men require similar assistance in the Bangladeshi host communities of Teknaf and Ukhiya upazilla. There are many child protection concerns in the refugee camps and host communities. The Child Protection Sub-Sector Assessment in Rohingya Camps in September 2021 established that the most prevalent child protection concerns perceived by respondents are Child Labour (cited by 64% of respondents), Neglect (59%), Child Marriage (57%), Risk of trafficking (32%), Separation of children (25%), Physical abuse (23%) and Sexual and gender-based violence (12%).Case management trend analysis from CPIMS+ as of June 2022 has shown 91% children are neglected, 54% girls are more affected by child marriage with increasing cases, 93% boys are engaged in child labor but trend is decreasing, both 53% boys and 47% girls are affected by physical and emotional abuse which is also increasing . Among them 13% children are living with disability whereas very limited services available in camps and they are facing social stigma and deprivation. Therefore, children are needed to have continuum care to address the increasing trend of child protection concerns such neglect, child marriage, child labour, physical and emotional abuse in both camps and host community. As COVID 19 impact 5% of households in the refugee community reported an increase in violence against children; and Mental Health and Psychosocial distress needs has increased, affecting both children and their caregivers. There is a need to increase community-based, unstructured and structured Psychosocial support, as well as specialized Mental Health services. Parents and caregivers need guidance on positive parenting and coping skills both at the household level and through community-based mechanisms. Adolescent boys and girls face unique challenges that call for tailored measures to mitigate the impact of limited access to education and livelihood opportunities exacerbated by Covid-19. Adolescents are not able routinely to engage and participate in decision-making, and live under the spectre of child marriage, further refugee movements, and exposure to criminal activity such as drug dealing. Child protection systems in Cox's Bazar are under strain. The justice system is not child-friendly and requires investment to bring it in line with the Children’s Act 2013 (amended 2018). Capacity building is needed to operationalize diversion and strengthen the monitoring of children as they move through the justice system. Children’s Courts, Child Welfare Boards (CWBs), Child Affairs Desks at police stations requires further strengthening and coordination. Technical support to the Department of Social Services (DSS) to strengthen the social service workforce is ongoing but requires further support to raise quality standards especially around case management. Birth Registration calls for technical assistance to, and advocacy with, the Office of the Registrar General at national and sub-national levels, and the District Administration. Additional human resources and capacity building of Local Government Units is required on using the upgraded BDRIS and accelerating Birth Registration in the district. Cox’s Bazar District is vulnerable to natural hazards (monsoon, cyclones and landslides) which regularly impact safety, livelihoods and infrastructure.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The Project will ensure that Rohingya refugees have equitable access to child protection services in a predictable, efficient, timely, transparent and accountable manner. UNICEF will work in 16 camps follwoing 2 partners in each camp and two Upazila in host community and will ensure common strategies, systems, protocols, and tools wherever possible. UNICEF and implementing partners provide a consolidated package of services in the camps and host communities thus ensuring equity in service provision. The Project will work with the Government, UN, NGOs and donor community together with the relevant sectors and working groups to further avoid gaps and overlaps.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

The Projet incorporates protection principles and promotes meaningful access, safety and dignity to the children, adolescents, families and members of the community in the Rohingya refugee camps and host communities. Through service delivery nodes (Multipurpose Centres, Adolescent Clubs, etc.) and ensuring robust outreach to the most vulnerable following do no harm and inclusion of diversity, the Project will ensure access to assistance and services without discrimination or barrier. Embedded within project design is an information and feedbackmechanism through which the affected population can demand accountability for the adequacy of interventions, and for UNICEF and implementing partners to address concerns and complaints.

3.2 - Gender

The Project has been tailored to meet the needs of girls, boys, adolescents, parents/caregivers, and female and male community members. Gender equity has been considered in all facets of project design, recognition of the protection concerns of girls and women espciacially GBV that prevail. Girls and women will benefit from dedicated Safe Spaces for Women and Girls providing bespoke case management, Psychosocial support and skills development services by trained professionals. Adolescents will benefit from life skills-based learning including Adolescent Sexual and Reproductive Health guidance, pre-vocational preparedness, social cohesion and resilience guidance, and recreation activities. The support to adolescents will recognize their capacities as agents of positive change in their families and communities. Mothers and fathers will benefit from positive parenting support. Overall, the Project will respond to all forms of abuse, exploitation, violence (including GBV).

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

The Project aims to prevent and respond to all forms of abuse, exploitation, violence and neglect of all girls and boys from birth to 18 years old. Age-specific interventions are provided throughout, including Psychosocial support activities and adolescent life skills-based learning. UNICEF has stringent standards and protocols related to Child Safeguarding and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Implementing partners are assessed for rigor in this regard and are required to be rated as low risk. UNICEF is a key member of the PSEA Network.

3.4 - Diversity

The Project aims to provide equitable access to prevention and response services across the full diversity of the target population. While the Project does not have a specific focus on encouraging diversity (e.g. sexual orientation), all actvities are undertaken free of discrimination or judgement. However, project will prioritze targeting at least 10% people with special needs to access the child protection and other referral services.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

UNICEF will ensure that AAP feedback and response mechanism is established following the AAP principles of participation, inclusion, communication and transparency that child protection is made central in all stages of the programme cycle. UNICEF will ensure all staff, volunteers including children, adolescents, caregivers and targeted community are accessible, reliable and timely information about organizational procedures, structures and processes on affected population capturing age, gender and specific groups at risk of discrimination and vulnerable. UNICEF and partners will take measures to reduce barriers so that children of all age groups and those who are at risk of discrimination are participating in planning, implementation and monitoring process while implementing the projects by the partners. UNICEF will support partners to strengthen organizational child safeguarding policy through conduct risk assessment and increase staff capacity to take appropriate measures.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Project overall aim is to ensure sustainability and resilience in child protection programming and systems-strengthening approach with community-based child protection. Aims to make children, caregivers and communities more resilient to shocks, thus contribute to DRR with a child protection lens. As active member of PERU UNICEF will prepositioning child protection emergency items and capacity building support to partners implementing contingency plan. Will introduce greening concept in all facilities and engage children, adolescent on awareness and sensitization to prevent environmental degradation, reduce use of plastic, tree plantation and waste management in camps. To reduce the impact of climate change will ensure all structures are built with environment friendly and disposable materials such bamboo structure, install solar panels. Will do advocacy and lead campaign initiatives to reduce risk of landslides and increase natural protection during monsoon and cyclone seasons.
