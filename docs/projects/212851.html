Project Summary

According to the 2024 Humanitarian Needs Overview, the overall NFI PiN has increased by 15 percent to 6.2 million. Unaffordability is the main driver of need. In 2023, the estimated number of people requiring NFI support has increased by 15 percent to 6.2 million. With funding shortages currently at a historic low, the NFI sectors’ limited response in 2023 is further expected to exacerbate needs as 2024 unfolds. Harsh winter conditions affect further the most vulnerable and access to basic NFIs is needed to ensure that health, well-being, and protection concerns are met. Key NFI items such as winter clothing, thermal blankets, and heaters are essential and accessible yet unaffordable to most individuals.
The primary driver of displacement was reported as a worsening economic situation and lack of access to livelihood opportunities, household ability to meet basic needs is projected to further decline thus increasing the severity and intensity of shelter and NFI needs across population groups. Evidence from 2023 already points to this assumption/expectation, as the average monthly shelter and NFI expenditure has risen to 26% and is further expected to rise in 2023 forcing the population to rely more on humanitarian assistance. the response will focus on (IDPs, host community and retirnees )
activities
The proposed winter intervention will use the most appropriate modality – in-kind distributions, commodity vouchers, or cash through Financial Service Providers, depending on context. . A total of 5000 HHs (2000 core NFIs , 3000 HHs winter NFIs) are targeted during 2024. Seasonal voucher assistance is for vulnerable families to access core relief items to meet their additional needs during winter months. Vouchers are exchanged for fixed quantity & quality of specified goods from vendors at local markets. In kind distributions of multifuel heaters and fuel will be distributed at the start of the winter
Monitoring is the responsibility of the country based Manager of Program Quality, supervising MEAL officers the HQ. The collected data will be sex & age disaggregated. selects beneficiaries according to set criteria, distribution is through distribution points and if needed door to door. After each distribution does PDM and wherever possible organizes focus group discussions.
risk analyses
Risk 1: Increased community tension due to the assistance, and/or unclear beneficiary selection criteria leading to perceived discrimination. Mitigating measures: Provision of standardized assistance packages with guidelines followed by all sector partners .Harmonized and transparent selection criteria are used by all partners. Awareness raising conducted with communities. Regular monitoring, consultation, post-distribution monitoring, and adjustments to the program to prevent and manage any tension which may arise. Risk 2: Interference with beneficiary selection may lead to potential fraud and aid diversion resulting in financial or reputational risks. Mitigating measures: Capacity building of partners to improve vulnerability assessments in accordance with AGD. Advocate with relevant authorities and partners to apply the sector criteria in the selection process. Emphasize the importance of impartiality to partners and systematically monitor adherence and feedback from beneficiaries, with corresponding corrective measures. Establish an active and informed complaint mechanism. Risk 3: Abuse of Authority by distribution actors (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion). Mitigating measures: all staff members are required to sign and comply with the Code of Conduct, and also ensure procedures are in place to report perpetrators.
