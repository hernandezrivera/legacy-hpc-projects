Project Summary

The project is planned to address lifesaving shelter and NFI need for conflict/new displacement and flood affected (displaced and lost shelter and asset) people in Twic East and Duk counties of Jonglei State, and Yriol East and Yirol West of Lakes States. The project will address 617 HHs /3,700People/ with lifesaving shelter support, and 1050 HHs (6,300 people) with lifesaving NFI support. Shelter needs will be addressed with conditional restricted cash based intervention and NFIs Pipeline supplies.
In case of locations where access is compromised and there is need of joint approaches LWF will make use of the humanitarian hubs, or will conduct its operations with the mobile teams to reach deep filed locations. The project will ensure durable solution and resiliency of communities to manmade and natural hazards/disasters through building capacities of customary institutions, local administration, women and youth groups. The project will ensure the sustainability of its outcome through working together with customary institution, line departments, women groups, youth groups, and local administration from the beginning of the project. Eventually a proper handover of the project will be conducted. This project will contribute to objective 1 and 2 of shelter and NFI cluster in South Sudan

1 - Needs

According to IPC Alert April –August 2019 in South Sudan 6.35 million people was under IPC 3 (Crisis) i.e. worse acute food insecurity, 1.7 million was under IPC 4 (emergency), and 10,000 people are under IPC 5 (catastrophe). It was expected that the situation will improve from September- December 2019 due to harvesting season and new harvest will be available. In the August it was projected that Jonglei state will have highest number of people under crisis (IPC 3), the estimate was 1.25 million. The same report projected that from September –December 2019 in Duk County will at emergency (IPC4) will persist among other counties in the country while Twic East from Jongle, Yirol East and Yirol West from Lakes status to be in crises (IPC3). However the situation is further deteriorated due to the unprecedented floods which have affected those areas. At country level in South Sudan an estimated 2,442,716 individuals require shelter and Non Food Items (NFIs) assistance in 2020. The categories of needy people include returnees, Internally Displaced People (IDPs). According to Counties RRC office, in Jonglei state in Duk, and Twic East counties alone are hosting about 32,000 IDPs and about 11,000 Spontaneous Returnees related to conflict displacement and returning from refugees and IDPs. Similarly Yirol East and Yitrol West host thousands of IDPS displaced due to tribal clashes and returnees. Through the SSHF 2019 second allocation LWF is supporting about 818 HHS in Twic East, and Duk, the IDPs and returnees in dare need of assistance for shelter building/ repair and NFIs. Many are living in the open, other in temporary or severely damaged structures lacking of the basic NFIs facilities such as mosquito nets, plastic sheets, blankets and kitchen utensils. The situation of IDPS, returnees and host communities has worsened due to flooding in the recent months. For example the flood has displaced communities in Payuel , Ageer and Panyang of Duk County, and Pakeer, Ajoung, Lith, Nyuak and Konor counties of Twic East county. A multiagency assessment of flood disaster in Twic East came up with a total of 8722 flood affect people , however a recent record is in October the people affected raised up to 29,259 and a multiagency assessment this October 2019 in Duk confirmed that 6349 HHs were affected by flood. The flood caused damages/collapse of Tukuls (grass hatched houses), damages to household items, and pit latrines collapse, damage of crop field and even loss of human life.

2 - Activities

• Conduct a joint Rapid Need Assessment to identify and register beneficiaries with other stakeholders including Government. The assessment will be made with cluster partners and stakeholders in the project areas to validate existing information and to have update information for upcoming responses and prioritization within the counties of intervention. • Conduct Rapid Market Assessment (RAM) for Shelter and NFI: The RAM is to be conducted by LWF technical team with the support of external expert to validate existing RAM report• Form and Train CDC (Cash Distribution Committees): In each payam on intervention Cash Committees comprising of nine members (including women, youth representative, chiefs representative, among others) will be formed and trained on beneficiaries selection , and registration , cash program and cash disbursement methods (direct cash, and vouchers) and their roles. • Form Cash committee complain Response at Each payam or community • Lifesaving NFIs assistant to most vulnerable 1050 HHS (60% female headed) from IDPs and Returnees in Kind NFI kits. LWF LWF get NFI kits from Pipeline Supplies • Lifesaving Shelter assistance to most vulnerable 617 HHS (60% female headed) from IDPs, Returnees and at affected host communities members to build Shelter (Tukuls) through conditional cash provision (Restricted cash): The assistance will focus to provide the most vulnerable female, child and elderly headed IDP, and returnee a in Twic East, Duk ,Yirol East and Yirol East Counties, who lack access to safe shelters, and those vulnerable households lack shelter due to protracted conflict displacement, returnee back home from refugees and those who lost shelter with natural disaster. The assistant will be done with two rounds (tranche) cash payment for accessing the materials to meet their unique shelter needs. • Training of 617 HHS on life skills and shelter building: Beneficiaries and community stakeholders will participate in six training sessions to discuss optimal shelter design. The shelter program will deal with target community on HLP (Housing Land and Property right). Returnees mostly deal HLP with their community and no challenges as these groups come back to their own areas. In case of IDP HLP issues mostly handled as chiefdom level in Dinka community where the project operates. LWF will facilitate discussion with chiefdom and needy IDPs on HLP. All shelters will be built in accordance with SPHERE standards and include protection mechanisms such as doors with locks. • Training community member of resilience building towards natural and manmade disaster (local disaster risk reduction body at payam level, seventy individual). The training will focus on customary institution , local administration, women groups and disabled person• Monitoring of construction of Tukuls: This is step by step follow up an advisors support with CDC and stakeholders to enforce the beneficiary build the tukuls and finalize. The Cash disbursement will start when the beneficiary identified the building site and lay foundation (clearing and measuring), then the construction of the shelter at individual beneficiary level will be monitored and reported. • Post Cash Distribution Monitoring and Reporting: This monitoring will be conducted from 2weeks of cash disbursement. Relevant questionnaires will be developed for the shelter and NFI response and used. The data will be analyzed and documented to adopt lesson learned, understand challenges and success in cash programming. • Training on Staff Communications & Community Engagement (CCE) and AAP

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Lack of access to/awareness of referral services for survivors of protection incidents (GBV, child protection, for example) High .LWF will coordinate with John Dau Foundation (JDF) in the project area to to support possible referral survivors for survivors. Contamination of targeted project areas with explosive hazards Medium. LWF with coordination county and payam administration and demining agencies (like MAG) will inform for target population on identified critical explosive areas, so that the people need to take care not to move in those areas and their animals too. Risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure in targeted project areas Medium. In some border areas there will be tension of land tenure or nature resources focused disagreements, clash or conflict. LWF will work with county peace committees (that used to be supported by LWF in Twic East and Duk counties), CARE International (who working on peace building and local cohesion in the project areas) and local administration to settle disputes and pave ways for smooth implementation of the project. Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance Medium. LWF will work with customary institution, Youth and administration to safeguard girls and womenRisk of exposure of violence as a result of distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and services Medium. The project will deliver its support or inputs at each community centers or with radius of one-two kilometers radius. In Local context this can highly minimize the risk
