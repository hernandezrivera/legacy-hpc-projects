Project Summary

This proposal is in line with the objectives and the framework of the OPTI country HRP strategy under objective 2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL. Further, this project is also aligned to the WASH Sector HRP objectives as follows: (1) Ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable access to safe and sufficient WASH services in accordance with dignity of the unserved, underserved and most vulnerable Palestinians under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank. (2) Strengthen WASH preparedness and response capacity to the unserved, underserved and most vulnerable Palestinians under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank to reduce threats and to cope with emergencies and shocks. The specific objectives of the project are: (1) Provision of service operational interventions for service providers to maintain existing levels of essential WASH services in the Gaza Strip. (2) Provision of capacity building interventions for service providers to maintain existing levels of essential WASH services. The objectives are in line with the needs identified in the HNO and Oxfam’s recent assessments.

1 - Needs

Since 2006, the population of Gaza has experienced deterioration in their economy, lack of basic services and destruction of infrastructure due to the Israeli occupation. Poor water quality, along with the deteriorating infrastructure and services, continue to negatively impact the quality of life. Gaza has been affected by a chronic electricity deficit for over a decade, due to the low capacity of the Gaza power plant, and constrains on access to fuel. According to the Palestinian Energy and Natural Resources Authority (PENRA), electricity need in the Gaza Strip is 450-500 megawatts (MWs) of which 140-160 MWs are currently available. The increase in electricity supply in the Gaza Strip since October 2018 has improved the delivery of water and sanitation services (OCHA Bulletin of August 2019), but the problem causes are still in place. Moreover, as per the Gaza Water Security Mapping carried out by the Palestinian Water Authority (PWA) in 2019, Gaza’s electricity demand is projected to grow at an average annual rate of just above 3.5% for the coming years. It is estimated that the water sector will require 137.8 MW by 2030, and that if no further power generation options are developed, the unmet demand for electricity will increase to 63% by 2030. Given the scarcity of fuel for generators, there should be proactive investment in coordination with donors, humanitarian agencies and private sector, on the application of solar power options. The average amount of piped water supplied during the first half of 2019 is still below WASH and WHO recommendations of 100 l/c/d with 77,5 L per capita per day (Updated HNO 2020). Furthermore, piped water is unfit for human consumption and is primarily used for domestic purposes other than drinking and cooking. Despite having more electricity hours, the unreliable supply is still specifically affecting women, intensifying their responsibility for unpaid care and domestic work, which often entails intensive and physical work. In addition, the restricted access to basic services, the restricted mobility and the lack of resources also add up to the difficult situation. In response, the WASH Cluster recommended that organizations invest in solar energy sources to support WASH facilities to enhance sustainability, operation, and access to services. Based on the needs highlighted in the updated HNO 2020 and Oxfam rapid care assessment (May 2019), which included 5 focus group discussions (FGDs), participants proposed solar/alternative energy as a solution to address electricity dependability which would improve care responsibilities and house chores. In addition, Oxfam’s gender analysis (May 2019), clearly highlighted that women and children bear the brunt of securing water in their HHs. In 2019, in coordination with the WASH cluster and under the Solar Energy Coordination Task Force (SECTF), Oxfam conducted a comprehensive solar system study to assess and prioritize the WASH facilities that could be operated by solar system. The study identified that 280 out of 409 WASH facilities are feasible and moderately feasible to be operated by solar system. As per Islamic Relief study (2018) and Oxfam solar system study (2019), CMWU and Oxfam agreed to prioritize six critical water wells that serve vulnerable communities in Gaza (Shujaia, Zayton and Sheikh Ejleen) and Rafah governorates (Mosubih, Al Zuhour and Kherbat El Adas). Accordingly, a technical field assessment was conducted in September 2019 to identify the specific needs of each well by CMWU, Oxfam, MA’AN and PEF to the Al Zuhour and Al Seka wells in Rafah and Sheikh Ejleen No.2, Sheikh Ejleen No.5, Sheikh Ejleen No.6 and Shujaia No.4 water wells in Gaza. Oxfam's local partner, MA’AN and PEF established community engagement activities to receive feedback and ensure ownership of the intervention. The need to promote women’s participation and representation was highlighted to influence the attitude of predominantly male participants.

2 - Activities

This project will respond to the needs of male and female beneficiaries in the targeted areas including children, elderly and people with disabilities by installing solar system and conduct maintenance work in the six selected wells in order to mitigate the electricity shortage and enhance the sustainability of the wells operation. These actions will improve access for targeted people to sufficient quantity of domestic water for cooking, personal and domestic hygiene and will also contribute to alleviating the burden on women and children.MA'AN and PEF work closely with vulnerable communities in diverse locations including farmers, women, children and youth and have extensive experience in access to sustainable and safe water and sanitation services in the Gaza Strip. MA'AN and PEF were fully engaged in the project’s design, needs assessment and community consultations. The two partners will implement the project in coordination with Oxfam with joint responsibility for the monitoring and evaluation activities and for stakeholder engagement. MA’AN will lead the implementation of the activities in the Gaza governorate while PEF will lead in the Rafah governorate. 3 - Indicators

Based on Oxfam’s Common global Approach to MEAL and Social Accountability (CAMSA) the project will integrate a comprehensive Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) framework. The MEAL framework will promote gender sensitive, collaborative and effective, results-focused and accountable MEL and social accountability practices throughout the project implementation. Learning and feedback will also be key practices that will be an integral part of the MEAL framework and will be documented by Oxfam in close coordination with implementing partners. In case of emergency, Oxfam’s minimum standards for MEAL and gender in emergencies are applied. Gender-relevant needs, actions, and issues will be monitored during the project implementation ensuring gender sensitive project reviews and evaluations. Monitoring tools, data gathering analysis, and reporting will encompass qualitative and quantitative gender and age disaggregation and analysis. This will help in tracking performance against indicators. Monitoring tools such as baselines and endlines will be used. key Informative Interviews (KII), Stories of change, Focus group discussions (FGDs) and others will be included as relevant. In OPT, Oxfam developed a comprehensive Feedback, Complaint, and Response Mechanism (FCRM) under its Saving Lives Program (including under this project) and will be piloting a mobile case management tool to capture real-time data from the field through the use of Information Communication Technologies (ICT). The FCRM ensures access for women and men to submit their feedback with safety and privacy. Feedback may be received either online, verbally or through hotlines and feedback boxes. The system automatically refers feedback to relevant program staff and is monitored by Oxfam’s MEAL Officer. Furthermore, complaints related to safeguarding, GBV, misconduct, or fraud, will be referred to Oxfam’s Country Director and safeguarding team and will be treated with confidentiality to ensure privacy. 9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Protection principles are considered in the project as part of the humanitarian principles governing Oxfam’s humanitarian interventions. Oxfam premised its project design on the ‘do no harm approach’ ensuring that project activities do not present any harm to the targeted population by facilitating access to the essential WASH need which would have put them at risk, had it not been available. Technical assessment will be conducted in a way that takes into consideration the safety, dignity and confidentiality of the served population while also keeping the protection of data and humanitarian actors uncompromised. Preserving dignity is essential to maintaining self-esteem and confidence, which is important to cope in stressful and potentially overwhelming humanitarian situations. Supporting women’s self-esteem and confidence will reflect positively on and enhances the roles each member of the household fulfills and will result in improved wellbeing of the family, especially children as care receivers.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

On a community level, local community committees (with equitable gender and age representation) will be established to ensure their direct engagement with targeted communities and beneficiaries throughout the project, thus enhancing community engagement and reducing any potential protection risks at community level. Building the capacities of targeted communities to improve their self-protection mechanisms will be ensured through conducting orientation sessions to inform targeted communities about services that will be provided as well as on safety measures and access modalities. Moreover, the project design has considered gender and protection principles by emphasizing on the participation of women in the project’s committee as well as further exploring women and people with disabilities needs. More over, Oxfam will ensure safe exit for the project through providing more sustainable solutions for the identified needs and the proposed intervention by following an inclusive approach which ensure the targeted women, men, PWDs and other vulnerable groups are part of our interventions as the main strategic way of working to ensure sustainability.
