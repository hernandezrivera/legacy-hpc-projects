Project Summary

Elaf organization will provide shelter support through rehabilitating of 300 households in Idleb and NFI distribution for IDPs for 1000 HH
No. of Households that will be supported 1300 approximately (Individuals) 6500. The SADD as the following: (#of Males 1690, #of Female 1950, #of Boys 1560, #of Girls 1300, #of PWD 975)
In this project Elaf will respond to two main needs in SNFI cluster response plan as the following:
1- Elaf will support the capacities of 1000 households for NFI core and essential kits, for the IDPs and especially newly displaced where applicable who are living in Dana district (Idleb governorate).
Elaf plans to distribute NFI essential items for the new IDPs, as we will target IDPs who are the most vulnerable and since the they were not able to carry NFIs with them, besides many of them have been moving from one place to another seeking safety, therefore they kept on leaving and losing their belongings over and over again especially what they will need to survive the cold of the winter.
The need of kits is immense as the repetitive displacement prevented them from carrying most of their items in displacement trip. Therefore, the NFI essential items will support those families and alleviate the suffering of cold and help them to start over after moving continuously seeking safety.
Elaf previously implemented same activities in and outside of camps in Dana and Idleb sub districts and due to the huge needs and lack of funding we planned this particular intervention in Afrin and Dana sub districts
Elaf will support rehabilitation of 300 HHs in Dana sub-district as well, This suggested intervention will be, as you may see in the activities in line with Shelter Rehab Guidelines (SRG) of GRADE 2 damages houses, the design of program, considering protection issues, designing the activities and engaging the community, all was designed in line with SRG.
A focus on moderate damage (Grade 2) allows a maximization of impact, in order to assist as many beneficiaries as possible especially IDPs and returnees which their shelter units were damaged as a result of aerial/land attacks or unfinished units that needs basic rehabilitation to sustain their lives, windows and doors. Units that will be targeted will be checked for the HLP issues and no rehab will be done without it.
Additionally, we will give 1000 IDP households in such locations NFI kits. Based on our prior work execution, the project would target the most vulnerable HHs, particularly women-headed HH. We concentrated on reaching them as one of the most vulnerable HH in the community.

3 - Needs

Eleven years of conflict have affected people’s coping ability across resident and displaced communities, making timely and principled provision of humanitarian assistance for them all the more critical. Based on HNO 2023 It is estimated that 5.5 million people in Syria will require shelter support. In accordance with the MSNA 2022 findings, 34 per cent of the overall population live in ‘sub-standard’ shelter, of which 19 per cent reside in finished residential buildings classified as damaged and/ or displaying ‘adequacy issues’. Lack of access to electricity/lighting, insulation from heat and cold, deterioration of shelter integrity (notable increase in damages to windows/doors), in addition to lack of privacy, are among the top shelter adequacy issues identified. In conjunction with this.Additionally, nearly 85% of the population as a whole indicated a decline in their capacity to meet basic necessities. A 30% dependence on either shared or private generators exposes homes to high fuel costs and increases the demand for good winter clothes. As a result, as households' average monthly income versus expenditure gap hits 60%, basic necessities become even more out of reach. With a forecast worsening of the economic situation in 2023, the number of people in need of NFI support is predicted to rise even higher. The most vulnerable people are further impacted by the harsh winter weather, and access to Core NFIs is required to address health, well-being, and protection concerns

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption 1 – Access to Syria remains possible and safe from the neighboring countries. Risks: A change of government policy/attitude in Turkey could lead to a closure of the borders or and/or negatively impact on humanitarian access to Syria Mitigation strategy: As a registered NGO in Turkey, ELAF is expecting that this legal framework will, at best, give the leeway to operate. If the general attitude towards Cross-Border humanitarian assistance should change. Assumption 2 – Markets are functioning properly in the area identified Risk: Due to the unpredictable situation, there is a risk markets are not functioning properly due to lack of access and/ orsupplies. Mitigation strategy: Elaf teams have confirmed the market is functioning and the security situation allows the implementation of the project. The situation of the market will be monitored on regular basis by the program team. Assumption 3: The project does not create distortions in the local economy Risk: As consequence of the project, there is a shortage of materials in the area and the prices increase. Mitigation strategy: contractors will be identified at the initial stage of the project and provided with necessary information. Assumption 4: The area of operation still need the intervention Risk: other entities may implement in the area before the acceptance of the project Mitigation strategy: the project may be shifted to other subdistrict in which the cluster has marked by the HNO 2023
