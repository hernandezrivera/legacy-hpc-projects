Project Summary

The regions surrounding the Lake Chad Basin have been embroiled in a decade-long conflict and the three North East Nigerian states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) have withstood the worst of the violence. As of August 2019, an estimated 1.8 million internally displaced persons (IDPs) had been identified (International Organization for Migration, National Emergency Management Agency, Displacement Tracking Matrix [DTM], October 2019). This has devastated the region’s fishing industry, causing severe shortages and driving up the prices of fish products across the country. As a result, the demand-supply deficit of fish products in Nigeria is estimated at 2.2 million metric tonnes (Nigeria Fisheries Statistics Report, 2016).
The armed insurgency has further eroded food security and, as a result, malnutrition rates have skyrocketed. Findings of the October 2019 Cadre Harmonisé analysis highlight that, between October and December 2019, approximately three million people in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States face acute levels of food insecurity (Phase 3 and above).
Therefore, the overarching objective is to provide conflict-affected fisherfolk households with the means by which to resume environmentally friendly, fisheries-based livelihoods, thereby enabling them to progressively meet their food and nutritional needs. In doing so, the action will lay the foundation for longer-term resilience building and sustainable economic and social development. This will be achieved through a combination of skills development and productive measures supported by conditional cash transfers in the targeted communities.

1 - Needs

Before the insurgency, fishing on Nigeria shores of Lake Chad was a vital livelihood as well as an important protein source at the household level. Most communities along the Nigeria shores of Lake Chad are considered as fishing communities or villages i.e Abadam, Kukawa, Monguno, Marte, Ngala, where the majority of households are involved in harvesting, processing, distribution, marketing, and sale of fish resources (FAO Fisheries Technical Paper. No. 481. Rome, FAO. 2007, 125). However, the conflict has disrupted the whole fishing sector affecting income from fishing. With the increased military operations along the lake chad basin a general embargo has been placed on fishing activities and thus disrupting the fish supply chain. FAO discussions with the State Ministry of Animal Resource and Fisheries Development (MARFD) indicate the need to re-establish and develop fisheries through aquaculture interventions with the goal of reducing redundancy among fisher folks in the conflict affected areas. The increased land and areas retaken by the military, a large-scale, state-led return of IDPs to their communities of origin is ongoing. To date, approximately 1.6 million returnees have been identified in the three states (DTM Round 28, October 2019). This has enabled some access to fishing grounds, enabling fisherfolk to resume some level of fishery production but the lack of fisheries inputs remains a limiting factor to fish production for the majority of fisherfolk households who have little choice but to continue relying on humanitarian assistance to meet their food and livelihood needs. Prior to the crisis, the fisheries sector had significant economic importance and fish from the North East were supplied inter-regionally to the rest of Nigeria. FAO has been operational in the North East since 2014, implementing lifesaving interventions in the form of emergency food security, nutrition and livelihoods support to IDPs, returnees and host communities affected by the crisis. However, in the absence of sustained and expanded humanitarian assistance, the number of people experiencing acute food insecurity is projected to increase by up to 50 percent by the onset of the 2020 lean season (June – August) The project will address the following objectives;• To restore and regenerate fisheries-based livelihoods affected by the conflict.• To increase production of fish-based products (fish, fingerlings, local fish feed formulation). • To regenerate and promote value addition to fish products through the provision of assets and training for processing.• To promote the formation of cooperatives and facilitate market linkages for sustainable inputs and outputs. • To support the rehabilitation/reconstruction of fish markets in the BAY states.

2 - Activities

FAO will collaborate with relevant government institutions, the State Ministry of Animal Resources and Fisheries Development, National Institute of Freshwater Fisheries Research (NIFFR) and Federal College of Freshwater Fisheries Technology (FCFFT) Baga to provide support in communities targeting fisheries extension services,research, training and mentoring. The beneficiaries will include a minimum of 35% inclusion of women and at least a 60% inclusion of youth, throughout the activities.Their specific needs in terms of training, resources, communication with communities and all aspects related to their successful participation in the project will be considered. For fish processing, FAO will prioritize women-headed HHs and female youth and equip them with innovative and sustainable skills in fish processing and link them to market for increased income generation & nutrition security, taking into account their limited access to crucial productive livelihood assets. The specific activities proposed for the intervention include: Provision of fish farm starter kits (tanks, fingerlings and feed) for aquaculture production mainly targeting male youth Establishment and/or scale-up of private/community based fingerling production enterprises in close collaboration with the Federal College of Freshwater Fisheries Technology Provision of smoking kilns to women’s groups to promote food security and income generation through the sale of processed fish products Formation of beneficiaries into clusters/cooperatives supported with training and seed money towards establishing a Group Savings and Loans (GSLA) scheme Reconstruction/rehabilitation of market stalls and shades in key fish markets in the region Beneficiary identification and targeting criteria: Target beneficiaries will be identified in accordance with FAO criteria following a participatory methodology in collaboration with LGAs officials, nominated community and socio-cultural representatives and State fisheries staff. Based on these, Beneficiaries will be identified through community social mobilization and targeted using the following criteria;i. Minimum requirement:- IDPs, returnees, and host communities households with access to land for cluster fish farming - Households resident in the accessible fishing community.ii. Priority Households:- Vulnerable/food insecure households among IDPs, returnees and host community.- Households with keen interest in fish farming as means of livelihoods diversification.- Men headed households for fish farming starter kits- Women headed households for fish processing/marketing starter kits- Youth headed households- Households whose main livelihood is fishing or fisheries related activities such as boat building, net mending, trap making, rope weaving, fish processing, transportation etc- Households with presence of malnourished children FAO has developed a rigid protection sensitive strategy in the northeast, based on a do no harm, approach, boosting meaningful access and integrating principles of the accountability of affected populations and communications with communities to enhance participation. The goal of FAO’s approach is to boost participation in the project cycle, curb SEA and GBV. FAO implements feedback mechanisms including toll free numbers, suggestion boxes, focus group discussions. In the M&E of the project, FAO will undertake yield assessments & PDM which will collect beneficiary feedback concerning acceptability, timeliness, transparency of the selection process etc. Women, men and youth’s feedback will be deliberately targeted as part of the above processes, in consultation with local authorities and interest groups such as women and youth cooperatives.A minimum of five per cent of total budget will be allocated towards protection mainstreaming activities.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

FAO will ensure that feedback mechanisms such as toll free numbers, complaint boxes, complaint desks are available throughout the life cycle of the project and that vulnerable community members have access to these mechanisms. The organization will also ensure that the views and experiences of the most vulnerable are integrated in post-activity monitoring and reporting. FAO will engage a wide cross section of stakeholders to identify the most-vulnerable and those least likely to utilize feedback mechanisms . These individuals will be identified and reached in person where possible and their specific requirements and needs will be taken into account in project design, implementation and appraisal.
