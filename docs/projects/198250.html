Project Summary

The crisis in the South-West and North-West Regions of Cameroon has compounded pre-existing vulnerabilities. Since 2016, political and social instability, exacerbated by sporadic violence, has had a negative impact on the civilian population of Cameroon’s South-West and Northwest Regions, hosting four million inhabitants (16% of the total population). In November 2017, the socio-political crisis progressively translated into insecurity and armed violence. Since then, the escalation of tension and upsurge in hostilities between non-state armed groups, defence and security forces have triggered humanitarian needs across the two regions, linked to significant internal displacement. The destruction of food crops, water supplies, health services, and subsequent large displacement of families and communities takes a heavy toll on children and women especially. Even greater numbers have died from malnutrition and disease during this period in the SW and NW Regions of Cameroon.
To assist conflict-affected populations in western Cameroon, FFP enables WFP to distribute in-kind food rations to IDPs and host community members in Northwest and Southwest with the use emergency food vouchers and essential relief commodities to vulnerable people in affected Regions.Thousands of people have been killed, three times as many have been seriously injured or permanently disabled and countless others have witnessed or taken part in violent acts. The destruction of food crops, water supplies, health services, and subsequent large displacement of families and communities takes a heavy toll on children and women especially. Even greater numbers have died from malnutrition and disease during this period in the SW and NW Regions of Cameroon. Overall, 3.9 million people in the country require emergency assistance to meet their basic needs, according to the UN. In 2019The rapid Emergency Food Security and nutrition Assessment (EFSA) and Mobile Vulnerability Assessment and Mapping (MVAM) conducted by WFP revealed that 1.5 million people in SW and NW are food insecure. To assist conflict-affected populations in western Cameroon, FFP enables WFP to distribute in-kind food rations to IDPs and host community members in Northwest and Southwest. FFP also jointly supports an NGO with USAID’s Office of U.S. Foreign Disaster Assistance to provide emergency food vouchers and essential relief commodities to vulnerable people in Northwest and South West
However, it is worth noting that Lebialem Divisions is one of the areas that have been hardest hit by the crisis. Since the coming of the Humanitarian Response Plan in 2019, Lebialem has hardly received assistance due to the high level of insecurity and enclave nature of the area. Even so various cluster reports show that there are no interventions in this area because development agents are unable to penetrate the area due to insecurity. This current year with the increase in prices of all agricultural inputs ranging from fertilizers, seeds, farm tools has caused a great drop in both the farm sizes for cultivation and their production. This is so because many farmers who are IDPs or Returnees don’t have enough money to cultivate larger farm sizes which will need more labour and more farm inputs. This was very challenging for the farmers to meet up with the recent increase in prices because they lack income to purchase the required need for the cultivation of their crops which is mostly potato, maize, garlics and ginger. This has greatly affected the agricultural productivity of the upper Lebialem(M'mouckmbie,M'moucklete and M'mouck fosimondi) farmers.
This project aims at supporting 150 households IDPs and Returnees in 3 communities to rehabilitate their potatoes and backyard livestock farms for income generation and improve living conditions of inhabitants and encourage more returnees to come back home.
