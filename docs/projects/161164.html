Project Summary

The following proposal is intended to fill critical Shelter-NFI gaps to most in need population in Mundri East, Mundri West , Mvolo and Lainya and surrounding locations in CES and WES, following the displacement resulting from the ongoing arm clashes between the opposition forces believed to be loyal to NAS of Thomas Cirilo during the last months of May to August around CES particularly areas of Yei region including predicted new IDPs that might arise if situation persist as well as expected Returnee in the event there is meaningful peace that can convince those in refuge in the neighboring countries to return. Actions for Rapid Development-Africa (ARD-Africa), with this project will target 1300HH undeserved individuals (men, women, boys and girls) in Yei, Morobo and Lainya counties to be assisted with life saving S/NFI based on their assessed needs. While ARD-Africa (through its static office in Yei) provided similar emergency responses on behalf of other partner organizations including Concern Worldwide and UNHCR in Yei River State to many people displaced due to protracted insecurity in the area, we are preparing to respond to any emergency related to SNFI in the State. As it remains yet unpredictable despite slight calm in the past few months, ARD-Africa presumes that continued needs in the area will persevere and if tensions persists, further waves of displacement could grow, increasing vulnerability and the needs for additional assistance in the coming months and Year. Additionally, in the case of realization of meaningful peace, there shall be waves of vulnerable returnees including IDPs and those who sought refuge in neighboring countries who maybe critically in need. Most of whom will find nothing in their residents i.e. houses and materials burnt, broken, or looted. This may call for preparedness either way. ARD-Africa, through this plan – given funding, will secure additional dedicated team to both coordination and front-line responses the target locations and the surrounding areas. We have set overall objective to have a team that have certain capability of flexibility to respond in neighboring areas and states where the effects of the conflict in the State may extend to other locations. The response shall typically be based on in-kind SNFI assistance to the needy population in the target areas. The items shall be secured from the pipeline through coordination with the pipeline management and the logistics cluster to estabish availability of items and delivery mechanisms to the target locations.
In a hard to reach areas, ARD-Africa will use its Community Liaison Officers in areas it has including coordinating with CBOs on ground to deliver services. we shall work in collaboration with line partners to deliver coordinated service delivery to such areas. Services that requires attention of Protection, settlement etc shall be coordinated with partners including CCCM, Protection partners, OCHA, including local authorities on ground to effectively deliver assistance to in-need population in hard to reach areas. where ARD-Africa is responding to areas with humanitarian hub for instance Kajo-Keji, it shall coordinate with the hub management and establish landing point within the hub for its responses (in-case of mobile), partners available in the hub shall be consulted on situation and possibility of delivering assistance in the humanitarian hub to avoid duplication and inconveniences while on ground.

1 - Needs

There are estimated 46,000 vulnerable people in need of Shelter NFI assistance in Yei, Morobo and Lainya including 76% in Yei, 9% in morobo, 15%. while in Mundri East 98000, Mundri West 11100 and Mvolo 10700 in need of SNFI assistance (REACH, Situation Overview: Central and Eastern Equatoria, South Sudan, April-June, 2018 and OCHA Humanitarian needs overview). As the Shelter-NFI Cluster expects the emergency situation in CES and surrounding areas to persist and potentially worsen if arm clashes continued, most populations in WES and CES will require more comprehensive support in the coming year while Yei, given stability/peace, will be storm with returning population and perhaps IDPs if clashes persist in its surrounding areas. There are only three static SNFI parners in yei (TITI, Concern WW, ARD-Africa & UNHCR (refugee focus)) with little case loads to specific areas mostly within Yei town and perhaps Mugwo. Some of them including LCED, PAH, SPEDP, NRC, etc are responding on mobile though others are preparing to establish static presence in Yei. Services outside Yei capital are delivered in a rapid response form which at-times covers minimal of the needs. Most areas outside Yei town covered including Mugwo, birigo, koyoki, tore, goli, lasu, Koyoki, etc unlike in Yei town, greater Mundri and Mvolo, requires comprehensive assessment and support. Some of these locations are supported by UNHCR through ARD-Africa with few caseloads compared to the needs on ground and our experience is that, huge population especially in greater Kupera including Mukaya and Bereka of Lainya are in-need of SNFI assistance. While in Yei, areas of Rasul (complicated), kundru, tore, umbaci including Goli (Returnee) also need proper attention. Morobo areas of Lujulo, Kimba/panyume, wudabi, and gulumbi needs assistance. About 5,098 in gulumbi, 4,226 in kimba, 1,447 population in panyume in morobo are estimated to have dire need for SNFI, these figures are obtained from previous assessments (obsolete) made in the areas without response due to security issues which again requires in-dept analysis and verification of the figures. Concern WW is covering Mugwo with small caseload but still needs exist though they planned to reach Morobo (per the 2019-SA2) also with small caseload leaving huge gap that may be found and anticipated with return population in-case there is relative calm. As predicted, if there is more arm clashes in the State, more IDPs shall storm Yei town and if relative calm is seen, waves of Returnee will too storm Yei and the surrounding areas. This again will increase vulnerabilities and calls for advance preparedness 9contigency plan) for hot-spot areas of IDPs and Returnees. For this plan, ARD-Africa established itself to reach 10,000 people in greater Kupera (Lainya), Mundri West, Mundri East and Mvolo. The assistance include in-kind Shelter NFIs assistance as well as resilience activities including capacity building on fuel efficient energy cooking stoves and improving coping mechanisms.

2 - Activities

1.1: Identify populations in need by conducting consultations meetings, needs assessments/analysis and verification of the kind of Shelter/NFI needs (materials) required and accessibility; ARD-Africa shall employ S/NFI Cluster tool for the data collection including FGDs, KII, household questionnaires including general observation. All groups of vulnerable people including IDPs, Returnee, Host communities, PLW, PLWD, Men and Women etc shall be engaged in the consultation/assessment process. 1.2: Conduct awareness sessions, beneficiary selection and registration that subsequently inform distributions of NFI and/or shelter materials and subsequently distribute NFIs to identified registered beneficiaries; ARD-Africa shall lay a selection criteria that includes PLWD, PLW, female and child headed household, people with limited community linkages, new IDPs, Host with critical vulnerability to NFI, Returnee with critical vulnerability to NFI, households with malnourished children under, place a pipeline request for the needed items and coordinate with log cluster on delivery on site or to locations and subsequently distribute items to registered and verified beneficiaries 5. Complain and Feedback Mechanisms shall be ensured, a communication line shall be established as well as CFM desk shall be set during registration and distribution to collect and attend to new info or raised concerns. Referreall pathways, in coordination with other partners, shall be established to guide on other concerns beyond the reach of this response. SNFI response committee shall be established with representation of both male and female, these groups shall design the SNFI response that will suit all their groups in the selection criteria. This is to incorporate inputs from all groups in the response design and ownership. 2.1: Train selected beneficiaries on efficient cooking energy and briquette construction; a selection criteria for trainees on construction of efficient cooking energy and briquettes shall be established based on willingness to train others, female/male above 18, IDP, Host, Returnee, can mobilize other raw materials accessible on ground e.g. clay soil, grass, etc. group trainees into 25-50 member per each group and conduct trainings on both sessions (stove and briquettes) 15 days per group. 3.1: Participate in regular Cluster meetings with all partners and attend relevant meetings at site level to share information with stakeholders, ensuring continuity and efficiency of response relative to the broader humanitarian response. 3.2: Regularly report to the stakeholders including the Shelter NFI Cluster. 4.1: Conduct rapid monitoring and post-distribution monitoring exercises in all response areas, ensuring results are fed back into ongoing implementation. And final completion reporting. PDM will collected disagregated data based on beneficiary satisfaction, concerns about the response including identification, selection, registration, training and distribution stages of the project. Information gathered shall be used to inform future response, while lessons learnt shall be shared with the SNFI Cluster. Prior to distribution, conduct HLP tenure and power relation assessment and identify vulnerabilities and needs of especially widows, single mothers, PLWD, minor headed households. Depending on the identified needs, undertake activities aimed at supporting statutory and customary justice and dispute resolution mechanism including raising awareness among local authorities and communities on their rights and obligations, strengthening skills and knowledge on HLP of community leaders and providing assistance with directing/referring to applicable procedures to access such structures (especially for affected persons who face obstacles in reaching them).mm

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

This project is linked to South Sudan Interim Country Strategic Plan 2018-2020, particularly Strategic Outcome 1and 3 on food transfers, Cash base responses, capacity building; SSDI Priority Action Plan (PAP) 2013-2020. The plan is lined up with the laid frameworks on social protection including PSEA. Given the specific security dynamics in CES and surrounding areas, ARD-Africa will continue to ensure that protection of beneficiaries including IDPs, Returnees or Refugee, humanitarian staff and assets are at the forefront of planning and implementation throughout the project period. ARD-Africa will continue linking with protection actors both at national and Yei/CES level. Staff will work with protection actors to closely monitor the potential risks associated with conducting distributions in particular areas, and in the identification of protection cases in order to adequately serve them with NFI and shelter materials, such as unaccompanied minors, pregnant women, the elderly etc. Through protection referral systems, ARD-Africa will remain abreast of developments in field locations and where special needs may exist, provide assistance on an ad hoc basis as necessary. ARD-Africa will ensure that, ahead of any provision of assistance, protection concerns in the project location have been adequately understood and addressed and meanstreamed in response, ensuring that the interventions do no harm to the beneficiaries or the communities in which they reside. In addition, ARD-Africa will continue to work with the Protection Cluster to identify ways in which the Shelter-NFI team can better serve protection needs.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Despite of insecurity in other parts of the state, there are hopes with the upcoming peace progress. With the hopes of formation of new unity government, insecurity will hopefully subside and situation will be sufficient for implementation of sustainable projects. ARD-Africa will exploit opportunities to ensure coordination with the national and State levels Education Cluster to ensure that Security Risk Assessments are coordinated Vise-versa from the ICWG, UNDSS, OCHA at all levels including etc. and implementing partners on ground levels. Also we shall ensure security analysis is conducted prior to deploying any response, and will take in to account exceptional risks faced by our local staff of certain tribal groups when traveling to specific sites. Particular risks during transport of stock and field staff will be mitigated through effective coordination with other humanitarian actors to travel in convoys where necessary, and with request for forced protection escort as a last resort. Meanwhile during response/distribution, ARD-Africa shall maintain space for feedback on protection concerns data collection

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Upon distribution of NFIs, community members/target beneficiaries will be educated on their usage and management. The skills they may acquire will help them maintain the supplies for longer period. They shall also be trained on making efficient cooking energy stoves and briquettes which shall raise them incomes as well as improve their resilience (coping mechanisms) to cooking energy. The project will support other cross-cutting emergency needs in these counties, and will provide continuity and value for money within the response. This project will also fill a critical gap for the cluster both in terms of covering coordination in parts of Yei, Morobo and Lainya in addition to having a static team covering any further needs arising in the State. ARD-Africa already focussed its responses with expanded approaches that improve resilience and contribute to a strong base for long-term activities and sustainability. This includes more HLP engagement to ensure security of tenure for returnees, more capacity building and practical trainings to enhance resilience building and copying mechanisms. As usual and as shall be factoed in this plan for exit, ARD-Africa will pool all community members including beneficiaries, community leaders including local authorities in exit event. This event shall inform on the benefits of the response, opportunities and wayforward linked to mitigating some of experiences including barriers as a result of the response. Lessons learnt shall be shared with the stakeholders including donors and SNFI cluster to inform on future planing to such already targeted areas.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Right from onset of response, communities and their leaders including local authorities shall be consulted, engaged in planning and implementation of the project. Authorities and communities shall be sensitized ahead of response to create prepared mindset of the response and continuity of co-existence. List of registered beneficiaries shall be posted on board to allow community verify prior response to promte transparency and avoid anticipated problems during response and beneficiaries shall be informed of their entitlements. During needs analysis, ARD-Africa will ensure that information are gathered in a dis-aggregated manner i.e Gender, IDPs, Returnee, Refugee, Host, including children under 5; • elderly (> 60 years); • Persons living with disability (PLWD); • Female headed households; • Child headed households and/or unaccompanied minors; • individual adults with no effective community linkage. • Chronically ill people. People with special needs especially the disabled group and their associations (if exist) shall be engaged right from the needs assessment so that their needs are factored in response plan. They shall be involved in planning as well as in implementation of response. Response sites shall be established closer and in safe places to the people so they can access them without challenges. Awareness in communities shall be localized in understandable languages of the community, complain and feedback desk and hotline shall be created for inquiry and attention on matters arising. Informant data and identity shall not be revealed/exposed. Beneficiaries shall be trained on handling and use of tools provided to minimize injury. Complain and feedback mechanisms through hotline and front desks shall be established to receive and attend to raised matters, informant details shall be kept confidential and a general guidance and advice shall be delivered based on maters raised to reduce risk of individuals feeling targeted with response to complaints.
