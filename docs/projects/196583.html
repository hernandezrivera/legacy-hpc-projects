Project Summary

The proposed project aims at enhancing access to water, sanitation, and hygiene services for conflict-affected populations across Northern Syria, with emergency, life-saving WASH assistance mostly provided in camp settings, and longer-term, resilience focused assistance mostly provided off camp. ACTED will provide a variety of assistance modalities toward this aim. In-camp, ACTED will provide life-saving water supply (through a mix of trucking and pumping), sanitation services including wastewater and latrine management, and solid waste management services, and water quality monitoring to several camp clusters in Northwest Syria (NWS), as well as Washokani and Areesheh camps and multiple informal settlements in Northeast Syria (NES). Complementary to this assistance, ACTED will provide hygiene promotion and awareness sessions to all targeted camp residents, and distribute WASH NFIs to highly vulnerable displaced persons. Off-camp, ACTED will rehabilitate, through contractors and in coordination with all relevant stakeholders (including WASH Cluster, WASH Working Group, and local councils and technical departments), numerous assets, including water pumping stations, water treatment stations, water towers, etc. ACTED will also install new assets and/or facilities to fit existing assets, in areas where the need for water is highest. ACTED will look to hand over these assets to relevant local stakeholders while providing operation and maintenance support, and capacity-building trainings, in order for WASH-related basic services to remain operational through these assets after the completion of the intervention. In Northwest Syria, ACTED will conduct repairs and rehabilitation of WASH facilities in health centers. ACTED will also provide emergency water trucking out-of-camp in Northeast Syria. Lastly, in the context of the cholera outbreak, ACTED will meet the critical WASH needs of vulnerable individuals in and out of camps, by ensuring the continuation of the provision of WASH services and improving WASH infrastructure to prevent the further spread of cholera in NES. In total, ACTED expects its activities to reach 1,667,924 individuals under the WASH sector in 2023. ACTED will seek synergies with its response in other sectors, primarily Early Recovery activities, notably through activities falling under indicators from both WASH and ERL sectors, such as WASH asset rehabilitation activities. Specifically, ACTED will seek to rehabilitate key water supply assets and systems off-camp in 2023, particularly in NES, in a effort to restore and sustain communities' access to this service, and to hand it over to local stakeholder where possible. This approach is geared for increased sustainability of the WASH response and is expected to increase cost-efficiency in the upcoming years (2023 and beyond) by reducing the need for live-saving assistance off-camp, particularly water trucking. With regards to in-camp interventions, ACTED will work through targeted communities in camp, notably through community-based structures, to build the sustainability of the WASH activities conducted. In NES, this include involving Water Committees from the resident population to participate in water quality monitoring, whilst informing camp residents on how to raise maintenance and water supply issues. In NWS, ACTED is also evaluating the prospects of cost recovery mechanisms. The approach considers social and economic dimensions, including the enabling environment, willingness to pay, and financial considerations such as fixed and variable costs. Active participation in the Cost Recovery in WASH Infrastructure Technical Working Group at the WASH Cluster will further promote informed decisions are taken.

3 - Needs

Across Syria, over 13.5 million persons are expected to be in need of WASH assistance in 2023, a 2,6 per cent increase over 2022, as per the (unreleased) 2023 HNO. In Northern Syria, access to WASH services is compromised by prolonged decay of the existing systems, caused by years of conflict, limited or no maintenance, continuous drain of technical staff and poor natural resource management. Lack or inadequate quantity of safe water is leading people to revert to unsafe or costly water sources, and to highly rely to on humanitarian assistance: in both NES and NWS according to REACH, private water trucking was the main source of drinking water. The lack of stable power supply due to high cost of diesel remains one of the main barriers which affects the functionality of water systems, while prolonged drought and the extremely low water level of the Euphrates River has led to a second consecutive year of water crisis. In camp and settlements, access to sufficient water, adequate sanitation and solid waste management and hygiene supplies is a challenge for IDPs. Critically bad sanitation conditions, poor hygiene practices and deterioration of water quality have a direct impact on increasing public health risks, including AWD/Cholera outbreak, as well as an impact on the livelihood and protection of households. The Governorates with lower sewer connection rate (Ar-Raqqa, Deir-ez-Zor, Al-Hasakeh, Aleppo) are currently the most affected by the cholera/AWS outbreak.

4 - Implementation of activities

ACTED will continue its extensive in-camp WASH response, benefiting camp residents in Areesheh, Abu Khashab, Washokani camps in Al-Hasakeh governorate, informal sites in Ar-Raqqa, Al-Hasakeh and Deir-ez-Zor governorates, six camp clusters in A’zaz and three in Jarablus in Northern Aleppo governorate, and 11 camp clusters in Idleb governorate, providing life-saving water trucking/pumping, sanitation and sewage services as well as solid waste management for some 374,910 IDPs. ACTED will facilitate access to in-kind WASH NFIs for 4,000 IDPs. Off-camp, ACTED will continue repairing and rehabilitating WASH assets, including communal water stations, improving water access across Aleppo, Idleb, Ar-Raqqa, Al-Hasakeh, and Deir-ez-Zor governorates. ACTED will conduct repairs on WASH facilities in upward of 23 health centers in Northwest Syria and provide emergency water trucking in urban setting in Al-Hasakeh. To respond to the AWD/cholera outbreak, ACTED will conduct water quality control of water stations and filling points, distribute cholera kits and support infection prevention at communal facilities in Al-Hasakeh, Ar-Raqqa and Deir-ez-Zor governorates. Lastly, in complementarity with its Early Recovery activities, ACTED will look to establish multi-sectoral synergies to strengthen community-level resilience, particularly with regards to linkage between WASH asset rehabilitation and productive asset rehabilitation (irrigation assets, communal bakeries, grain silos, etc.).

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Non-inclusion of vulnerable group needs during project design/implementation. Likelihood: Low – Impact: High. Mitigation: The proposed intervention will be implemented in accordance with ACTED’s gender approach, which follows the Gender Handbook for Humanitarian Action reviewed in 2017 by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC). ACTED incorporates gender analysis and considerations into the full project management cycle, from project design to implementation and monitoring. During the community sensitization, ACTED encourages the identification of special needs of most vulnerable groups including women, children, elderly, or people living with disabilities and conducts FGDs and/or community meetings prior to implementation. ACTED addresses where possible gender and protection concerns. In areas out of the scope of programming or expertise, ACTED relies on referral procedures to specialized agencies. Risk 2: Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation/abuse. Likelihood: Low – Impact: High. Mitigation: All ACTED staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct which directly refers to Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Gender Based Violence (GBV). As part of efforts to improve accountability, ACTED is committed to implement Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its projects to allow beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on ACTED work, as well as the work of the contractors, and to relay any arising issue.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

All ACTED staff receive induction and regular refresher trainings on the Code of conduct, PSEA, and Child Safeguarding policies. Contractors have the obligation to sign an ethical declaration and respect ACTED Code of Conduct. Contractors are briefed on PSEA before the implementation of the project. ACTED staff will be present to monitor daily the work of the contractors but also the behavior of the contractor employees towards camp residents. Information on the Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) will be communicated to camp manager and community leaders and as much as possible directly to camp residents, especially those who may be directly impacted by the rehabilitation works. Information will also be displayed on the channels available to raise complaints. Mitigation measures are monitored through regular on-site monitoring at the rehabilitation sites. The presence of women staff, in the program and monitoring teams, will be guaranteed particularly during monitoring and evaluation activities conducted at household-level. ACTED staff, program and monitors, are trained on protection and gender mainstreaming and on how to refer SEA incidents and/or suspicion internally. All SEA incidents are investigated by ACTED SEA Committee and communicated to the PSEA Network, as part of ACTED responsibility as member of the network.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Security and Political: Expansion to new areas: in-depth access mission from the ACTED Security team, formal approval required for daily movements and daily sharing of security updates. Conflict escalation: movement suspension, relocation of staff Community acceptance: monitored daily by liaison assistants, regular meetings with local community leaders’ network in order to prevent tensions. Regular dialogue ensured with all relevant authorities, with support from various coordination mechanisms at area level. Risk of interference: low visibility, recruitment and procurement processes, information sharing sensitivity, clear communication with local actors regarding expectations and responsibilities. - Resource availability: Road closures: ACTED diversified its network of suppliers through tender processes and reinforced local suppliers’ capacities to ensure that items are procured regularly and prepositioned. Procurement related risks: 2-week local advertisement in multiple locations and subsequent transparency control and safeguards: analysis of all offers at capital office level, evaluation of technical and financial offers conducted separately between field team and capital. Recruitment related risks: process entails 2-week advertisement with inputs from field, regional office, area coordinators. - Community and cohesion dynamics: CRM inside Syria: stakeholders can safely provide feedback and report any project issues or concerns.
