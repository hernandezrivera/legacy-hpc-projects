Project Summary

Under this project IOM will focus on shelter/NFI activities that ensure protection for population at risk of being affected by the COVID 19 pandemic, targeting internally displaced people, returnees and host communities.
To this end, IOM will:
(A) Support CCCM in enhancing reception capacities by providing quarantine facilities
(B) Ensure access to different types of emergency shelter solutions for displaced populations through the distribution and prepositioning of emergency shelter kits,
(C) Contribute to decongestion through construction of emergency shelters, and rehabilitation of damaged public buildings, etc.;
(D) Ensure that the care, maintenance and upgrading of existing shelters are conducted for continued safety and comfort of the affected population;
(E) Ensure that households in host communities have access to materials and labour for repairing their damaged houses;
(F) Ensure IDPs in host communities are supported through cash for rent and security of tenure
(G) Ensure that households in IDP camps and host communities and returnees have access to basic and improved NFI kit
(H) Support IDPs in camp, host communities, new arrival households and individuals at quarantine and Isolation centres with loose items
(I) Supporting IDPs living in camp and host communities through Cash for improved NFIs
IOM’s shelter activities will be implemented in two States of North East Nigeria; Borno (11 LGAs) and Adamawa (9 LGAs), targeting 88,000 individuals. IOM ensures that all shelter activities taken are accountable to the affected population and that the gender-specific needs of project beneficiaries are integrated. Shelter solutions are harmonized at sector level and coordinated with partners.

1 - Needs

The ongoing crisis in North-East Nigeria that has affected millions in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states, is further threatened by the recent global health pandemic and outbreak of the Coronavirus (COVID-19). With over 1,500 cases confirmed in Nigeria, the need for more specific interventions that focus on protecting the most vulnerable members of the crisis-affected communities is of the highest priority. In this regard, IOM is submitting this addendum to the HRP 2020 with specific activities for the prevention and response of the spread and containment of COVID-19, as well as to ensure that critical life-saving assistance continues to be provided where overwhelming humanitarian needs persist to ensure appropriate responses. There is a high risk of the spread of the virus in the camps and camp like setiing. High shelter density in several camps compounds the risk for IDPs due to high levels of congestion. Decongestion of highly-congested camps and camp-like settings is even more necessary in light of the COVID-19 pandemic in order to reduce risk of transmission, with a priority to be given to camps having a surface area of less than 20m2 per person. Assessments of the Borno Adamawa and Yobe State IDP camps conducted by the CCCM, Shelter, and NFI Sectors indicate that one in four of the camps, which host 430,000 IDPs, is highly congested with per capita space of less than 15m2. Consequently, almost all of the LGAs hosting the over-congested IDP camps in Borno have been identified as ‘high risk’ areas by the Borno COVID-19 Preparedness and Response Plan. In three of the camps assessed, located in Jere, Gwoza, and Kala Balge LGAs for a total of about 64,000 people, the static crowd per capita space is less than or equal to 1m2. Physical distancing measures are impossible to enforce in these camps. In addition, almost 50% of the IDP shelters in the camps and out of camps are self-made makeshift shelters which provide inadequate living conditions to accommodate IDP families that average 5 individuals per shelter. Inadequate shelters require repair, rehabilitation and refurbishment due to the temporary nature of the materials used. Due to self-settlement of IDPs, the majority of camps lack adequate site planning to address issues such as proper infrastructure erection, poor or no drainage systems, inadequate living space and spaces in between shelter units which are very close . While efforts are underway to ensure sound site planning, significant efforts are needed to expedite this process to reduce risk of COVID-19 propagation in camp settings Logistics arrangements and diﬃculty in transporting shelter and NFI materials procured outside the region often lead to a delayed response which further increases the vulnerability of IDPs. This is also compounded by effects of COVID-19 and the lockdown, security and poor road networks. Although all IDPs are one of the most affected population groups, there are groups with more significant vulnerabilities among the IDPs such as the elderly, women, and girls with specific needs, children and persons with disabilities, and those with underlying illnesses. It is also important to highlight that IDPs living in host communities and host communities without CCCM site facilitation or out of camp are equally under the category of the most affected.

2 - Activities

In consultation with the Health sector and partners, IOM will support Camp Management agencies to enhance reception capacities of camps and entry points.This will be done through the by provision of quarantine facilities, which will accommodate newlyarriving -arriving IDPs and host communities who require a 14-day monitoring period before they can join the community. Quarantine facilities/ shelters are non-medical facilities that aim to provide self-isolation for new arrivals with either travel or contact history. IOM proposes 1) Constructing quarantine shelters, which are self-contained units with living areas, integrated with WASH facilities, and are segregated by gender. 2) Rehabilitating, upgrading or re-purposing existing public buildings to be used for reception purposes and to quarantine individuals in the case of a mass influx. PROVISION OF EMERGENCY SHELTER (ES) SOLUTIONS: 1) Without a proper shelter, households will not be able to protect themselves and may contribute further to the spread of COVID-19 within the community. In addition, with restricted movements world-wide, as well as restriction on inter-state movements within Nigeria and most recently the lockdown of parts of Borno, the delivery of essential goods can experience severe delay. IOM will preposition ES kits in strategic LGAs. 2) To contribute to the decongestion efforts of camps and in order to maintain appropriate physical distancing, IOM will also construct emergency shelters, provided that land is secured . Priority will be given to the camps with the highest congestion levels with the minimum surface are per person. PROVISION OF REPAIR, REFURBISHMENT OR REHABILITATION OF SHELTERS IN CAMPS : 1) Repairing damaged shelters: as inadequate shelter can increase respiratory diseases and exposure , which increases the risk of contracting and spreading of COVID-19, IOM will prioritize repairing existing damaged shelters in the camps through reinforcement kits, community carpenters and technical guidance provided by IOM specialists.. 2) IOM also seeks to provide partitioning for any mass reception structures that do not have family units for proper physical distancing between families. In addition, there are many ES in the camps that are without internal partitioning and provision of such partitions will enable families to protect vulnerable members of the families 3) In the absence of adequate land for the construction of ES, rehabilitation and creation of partitions within of damaged public buildings can provide a solution for providing shelter for households without shelter as well as to decongest highly-congested camps and reception centers. PROVISION OF REPAIR, REFURBISHMENT OR REHABILITATION OF SHELTERS IN HOST COMMUNITIES: 1) shelter repair kits and cash grants are directed to returnee families or families in the host communities who are living in inadequate shelters or damaged shelters. The support will consist of in-kind shelter materials for rehabilitation as well as cash grants to support with labor and additional materials. PROVISION OF CASH FOR RENT FOR IDPs: 1) While many IDPs in Borno and Adamawa rent houses with host families, support in the form of cash for rent will protect them from eviction and provide security of tenure, as well as exposure to weather. This will also avoid unnecessary movements from one location to other. PREPOSITIONING OF IMPROVED AND LOOSE ITEMS: To respond timely to areas of high-risk transmission of Covid-19, IOM will preposition and stockpile NFIs in the deep field locations.DISTRIBUTION OF NFI (IN-KIND): IOM will continue to provide newly arriving IDPs and families in quarantine centers with basic NFI kits to address their immediate needs upon arrival.PROVISION OF NFI (CASH)I: Due to unforeseen circumstances and the ongoing lockdown the non-availability of items in the country it will be a challenge. To this end it is essential to do cash distribution in accessible locations.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

IOM will ensure accountability to the affected population and most vulnerablecommunity throughout all stages of the project life cycle. During the design of theproject and throughout the planning stages, IOM will promote active participation andfeedback from the targeted population in assessing their needs and specicvulnerabilities, including those of women, youth, and persons with disabilities, amongothers. This will ensure, for example, that the physical abilities of the elderly andpeople with disabilities are factored into any forward planning in terms of accessibilityto services provided. IOM will also seek regular feedback from the targeted populationduring the implementation of the project, most notably through the complaint andfeed-back mechanism team and tools . Affected populations may also providefeedback or submit complaints through the suggestion boxes or other elements of thecommunity-based complaint and feedback mechanism. This mechanism is managedby the IOM Camp Coordination and Camp Management Unit. After the project hasbeen completed, IOM will conduct post-intervention surveys to ensure furtheraccountability to affected populations and promote learning.
