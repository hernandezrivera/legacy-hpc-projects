Project Summary

This project focuses on activities that support the health of FDMN and the Host Community in Cox's Bazar. Our work is primarily focused at two facilities:
(1) The MedGlobal Health Post in Camp 24, Leda, Teknaf - here, we provide minimum services required as per health sector guideline e.g outpatient curative care, maternal and child health, adolescent health, family planning, gender-based violence, diseases, mental health and psychosocial services, capacity building for local health care professionals, and community health worker such as case-follow up and public health education keeping sex, gender, disability and diversity inclusion considerations in programming. The activities & outputs will be monitored using KOBO toolbox & Internal clinic census, and reports will be made available to the Health Sector.
(2) The MedGlobal Maternity and Birthing Center in Somitipara, Cox's Bazar - here, we provide pre- and post-natal care, normal vaginal delivery services, medications, and education to mothers and mothers-to-be.
The project also intends to provide crucial medical supplies and equipment to other camp facilities. All activities will be prepared in conjunction with the Health Sector to ensure that they are consistent with the Health Sector's priorities and other health-related activities, especially those in Camp 24. MedGlobal will prioritize global advocacy through showcasing outcomes and important conclusions from the activities.
To ensure accountability & health sector strategic objective for JRP 2022, the following steps will be taken:
(1) A continuing logic model planning process based on needs assessments and baseline surveys with the goal of involving the community;
(2) Rigorous audits and budgets to ensure financial transparency; and
(3) Program monitoring and evaluation to assure program quality management.
(4) Protection, Age, Gender and Diversity (AGD), disability inclusion, accountability to affected populations, PSEA, disaster risk management, and environment and ecosystem rehabilitation for addressing the impacts of climate change to be mainstreamed in the MedGlobal 2022 sustainable operation strategy.

At the health post, MedGlobal's work concentrates on 51 percent women and includes maternity care for young mothers, as well as family planning. Gender, sex, disability, diversity and age are reported in the statistics. MedGlobal is already performing needs/baseline evaluations for 2022, with women, girls, disability, diversity and other age groups in mind, and we will aim to incorporate the results into our next logical framework model & project management cycle. We already conducted multiple COVID 19 capacity projects in 2020 & 2021 including supporting to Bangladesh government health facility e.g the Upazila Health Complex with Central Oxygen Systems and other medical equipment and supplies to health facilities in the FDMN camps, and we spent roughly $505,000 on the projects in conjunction with other stakeholders. All the host and FDMN activities, including the yearly Helping Babies Breath (HBB) and point of care ultrasound (POCUS) training course in conjunction with the SRH WG, were included in the budget line for 2022. The construction of the health post at Camp-24, Leda, had a budget of approximately $97,600 USD.

1 - Identified Needs

Approximately 907,766 Rohingya refugees reside in Cox's Bazar Refugee Camp. 27,115 live in Camp 24, where MedGlobal’s New Health Post is located.So far, in 2021, with the support from Latter-day Saint Charities, MedGlobal has offered 17,433 consultations to Rohingya refugees, compared to 26,809 in 2020.While Health Post attendance was reduced due to the COVID-19 pandemic, it continues to increase to pre-COVID levels. Patients who did not seek care in the last few months are returning to the Health Post. In the camps, Rohingya refugees face gaps in healthcare services. These gaps are caused by shortages of financial, human, and supply resources, plus insufficient land to build facilities on.MedGlobal is constructing a health post in camp 24, ensuring that the population living there get wider access to health care. As per the health sector gap analysis report of 17 August, there are lots of spaces where medical intervention is needed and critical. In particular, as per the need assessment done via our data collector and IPC team there is a shortage of maternity, NCD, ID, laboratory, and mental health and psychosocial support services. NCD needs:27.46% of camp residents are estimated to have hypertension and diabetes (However, these rates are likely underestimated due to a lack of testing. )Maternity needs:More than 70% of pregnant mothers suffer from lack of access to proper maternity, ANC, PNC care. Family planning services are more often neglected and young mothers don't have proper education on FP in the refugee camps.ID needs:Sexually transmitted infections are thought to be very high amongst Rohingya refugees, but insufficient screening prevents accurate estimates.Gender Needs:According to our need assessment & clinical reporting MedGlobal planned to include population by disaggregated sex, age, religion, diversity data.Others:- It is challenging for Rohingya to find accurate information to educate them about healthy habits, disease prevention, and when/how to seek care.- Local health care providers struggle to access training resources.- Capacity building is needed in order to improve the care that providers give to Rohingya refugee patients.Future steps:MedGlobal is planning to address the gaps by building healthcare infrastructure inside refugee camp 24. Moreover to support the 26,585 population inside the camp 24 & 3,415 population from the host community at Leda, Teknaf among which 59% is female and 35% Children, Elderly and vulnerable community. We will provide maternity, ID, NCD, general medicine, Lab services, Pharmacy and other services under the minimum service package of health sector guidelines.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: Provision of comprehensive health care services in line with the minimum essential service package. Integrate mental health services. Provide mental health and psychosocial services through MHGAP trained doctors and closely link with protection. Promotion of access to curative care tailored as necessary to the specific needs of people of all age groups, including persons with disabilities, adolescents, LGBTI and people who sell or exchange sex.Activity 2: Provide Sexual and Reproductive Health Services to women, adolescents, and men through a health post, and provide education on SRHR within the community. Provision of Clinical Management of rape for survivors including following the referral pathway to protection, legal and psychosocial services. Activity 3: Maintain psychosocial counsellors at the health post and provide community-based points of access and care in client homes - the community-based facilitators will refer patients to the health post and psychosocial counsellors. The psychosocial counsellors will supervise and monitor newly recruited volunteers who are trained in mental health community outreach - these volunteers will assist in identification and referral of refugees with mental health needs. Implement the community PSS facilitator modality. Activity 4: Provide direct nursing emergency care and referral services to higher level care.Activity 5: Provision of all basic maternal and newborn health services as per minimum packages for HP.Activity 6: Sustain our community health worker program linked to the facility in compliance with the Community Health Working Group (CHWG) ToR and standards. Activity 7: Monitoring, evaluation and reporting: The project will collect daily and monthly service data disaggregated by age, sex and disabilities and report to MedGlobal clinic census, Health sector and DHIS2. The project will monitor the quality by using different tools and survey.Activity 8: Maintenance of health care waste management and infection prevention and control (IPC) system in the HP premises. Community engagement and awareness raising, counselling on COVID-19 and patient referral in person.
