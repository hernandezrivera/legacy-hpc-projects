Project Summary

Bangladesh is one of the most vulnerable countries worldwide to natural disasters. Two districts, Cox’s Bazaar (Teknaf) and Bandarban (Alikadam) are two of the most affected regions, suffering cyclically from cyclones, flooding and saline infiltration, and landslides, rodent attacks and earthquakes; all of which negatively impact of local population’s livelihood opportunities. The populations in these regions are mostly reliant on agriculture, livestock and daily labour as their main form of income, with limited additional means of income diversification. Additionally, there is a general lack of preparation in regard to natural hazards, at household, community and local authorities’ levels. Awareness on risks and mitigation measures is low, while early warning systems remain incomplete despite some means and efforts deployed from governmental and non-governmental actors. In Teknaf specifically, the considerable influx of Rohingya refugees fleeing Myanmar exacerbated existing vulnerabilities within the population, impacting the labor market and depleting local resources such as water and wood, creating a rise in social disaccord. Therefore, this project will be able to achieve the following objectives;
* To promote diversified and resilient livelihoods for marginalized farmers and livestock owners.
* To enhance the economic opportunities and livelihoods diversification of the most vulnerable HHs through disaster resilient IGA and market assessment.
* To strengthen individual, households and communities’ capacity to adapt and recover from natural disasters and stresses/shocks.

Needs

Climatically Teknaf suffers with annual cyclones and flooding which greatly reduces the extent to which agricultural production can take place due to prolonged flooding and salt water intrusion, increasing the level of salt present within soil and irrigation water. Although saline resistant rice varieties are available, the expense means they are often inaccessible, not available on local markets or not distributed/supported by local authorities. The arrival of the refugees in 2017 further reduced land available for farming, whilst placing a higher strain of demand on the limited farming that does take place7. As a result, there is increasing dependency on external markets which means populations are more vulnerable to price fluctuation. The influx is also impacting the local economy by introducing a labour surplus which has driven daily labour wages down. The majority of the workforce in Teknaf was dependant on daily labour for their income, this loss on household incomes will in turn reduce food security. Support should address the lack of financial capacity through diversified livelihood activities or further develop existing ones. The refugee population remains 100% reliant on external food assistance, while the host community (HC) is increasingly food insecure. Economic vulnerability remains the main driver of food insecurity, with 78% of refugees unable to meet the monetary value needed to cover their essential needs. The majority of the refugees see in their food assistance the main, resource to access all their essential needs. Inadequate food consumption affects around 4/10 refugee HHs, with highest rates among the old unregistered (50%) and new arrivals (44%). Female-headed HHs or HHs with People with disabilities (PWDs), chronically ill people, and with numerous children are the most vulnerable. The HC have acute needs for income generation activities. Cash support, rebuilding agriculture sector, and creating opportunity to have work through previously known skills remains their major demand. Resilience building both in terms of resources (capacities, livelihood) and in terms of infrastructures (disaster resilient facilities) remains a key need across host communities and refugee camps. The project will address the following issues in the project design and implementation from gender and age perspective; The need assessment identified the needs of men and women for young adults, middle-aged adults and old people. The project designed to address specific needs of women and men. However, at the beginning of implementation SI will carry out gender mainstreaming analysis for the project to identify power, roles and responsibility difference among different gender and age groups. Tailored project activities will be designed in order to address the differential needs of men and women. Female specific activities will be designed to strengthen economic empowerment of women which ultimately contribute to break the social obstacles which women are entertaining because of the power dynamics in the targeted communities.The project will involve affected communities in the project cycle management. Their full participation in decisions that affect their life will be ensured. Affected communities will be engaged in project design to identify their needs, involve in project implementation, and consult with for any change in the programme. This will also be linked with the CRM mechanism that will be established to improve the quality of our services.
