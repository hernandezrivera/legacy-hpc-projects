Project Summary

Context and justification
According to the UNOCHA report in Cameroon for August 2020, 162,726 people are displaced in the West region in 2020 compared to 123,308 in 2019. The Noun accounts for 32,047 of these and the Mifi division 20,000. The two divisions have two main categories of Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs): those displaced from the North-West and South-West (NWSW) regions to the West, and those displaced within the two regions due to attacks by non-State armed groups (NSAGs) in communities close to the NWSW. Coupled with an increase in the number of IDPs and the emergence of COVID-19, the lack of a real humanitarian response in the West region has further deteriorated the conditions of IDPs. The presence of IDPs in the West is causing significant pressure on existing social services. The displaced, about 80 percent of whom are women and children, face a grave humanitarian situation in their host communities. Their presence in impoverished host communities is straining already limited education, WASH, shelter and health facilities in these areas. Food resources have been exhausted and the protection environment weakened. More than 65 percent of IDPs need direct food assistance. More than 90 percent of IDP children (be they unaccompanied or not) are involved in some form of child labour. There are reports of rape, sexual harassment, sexual exploitation and physical violence. There has been an escalation in the abuse and violation of women and girls due to different vulnerabilities. Some students sit on the floor to study. Some children have limited access to school because of financial constraints. Many IDP children go to school hungry and some faint in schools.
In general, the coexistence between the host community and the refugees progressively deteriorates in such a way that we need to build a new confidence amongst them. So a relevant and sustained program for the resilience of those two communities should tackle the trhee following pillars: food secutity, pauverty, and peace.
PURPOSE AND OBJECTIVE
The aim of the actual project is to develop a sustainable food security through a pacific coexistence of displaced and host resilient populations both commited to work together to increase their incomes as well as their living conditions.
OBJECTIVES
- -Strengthen food security among the refugee populations of Foumbot and Bafoussam;
- To sustainably increase the income of women and youth through income-generating activities along the value chain of agricultural and agri-food products, as well as assistance in setting up an internal mechanism for mobilizing funds to co-finance their micro-projects;
- Promote living together and the culture of peace among refugees and host populations.
ACTIONS
Our project will be built around three pillars.
1. The cornerstone of food security and nutrition (zero hunger),
The actions will focus on Agriculture and the value chain of agricultural products, including small-scale animal husbandry.
2- The pillar of income security for women and young people, both refugees and hosts (zero poverty).
The actions here will focus on supporting the generating activities of women and young people, particularly in food processing, small-scale agro-food trade, and marketing of their products, as well as on the issue of internal mobilization of funds to ensure the sustainability of the actions undertaken. Here we will also include assistance to young schoolchildren to take off-school courses through ICTs.
3- The Pillar of the search for social cohesion and peace (zero conflict)
The activities here will focus on education for peace and conflict resolution. Here, local and refugee communities will be trained in peace education and living together.
Project implementation approach
In the case of agricultural inputs and Income Generating Activities, we will practice the "Pass on the gift" method of support, which will allow in the future to multiply the actions to more displacements.
