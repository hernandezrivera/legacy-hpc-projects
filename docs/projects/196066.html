Project Summary

IRC’s proposed project will provide inclusive response and prevention services to children, adolescents, and their caregivers in Rohingya refugee camps and host communities in Cox’s Bazar. The IRC targets children who experienced violence or at risk of violence. The project is aligned with IRC’s global strategy, the country strategic action plan 2022-2024, and objective 4 of the 2023 JRP. As an active member of SAG, co-chair of PSEA network, leads the CP-GBV TT, IRC will support CPSS to ensure the program delivered are in accordance with sector objectives and to enhance the capacity of partners. The IRC’s project is premised on the social-ecological model and Do No Harm principle. IRC will mainstream gender, equality, diversity as it has a fully-fledged sector as well as PSEA, and safeguarding in every activity. IRC will mainstream EPR and conduct multi-sector gender sensitive assessment on protection risks associated with climate change. IRC has established AAP and inclusion of PWD is imperative in IRC’s project to ensure equal access to services. The IRC will promote social cohesion and peaceful existence within and between communities. The project will be implemented in the following camps 1E, 1W, 6, 8E, & 25 and host communities of Teknaf and Ukhia Upazila; will reach 46850 (10% PwD) target population including 28034 children and adolescents. IRC prevention intervention is largely local partner led, where IRC will provide technical and handholding support to the implementing partners to implement IRC’s flagship program on Safe Healing and Learning Spaces (SHLS). Children aged 6-11 years can learn, develop and be protected by conducting age, gender specific, and culturally appropriate structured or non-structured PSS, it helps children to cope with and heal from trauma; and participate in recreational activities to return to normalcy. Support activities for adolescents includes: IRC’s structured curriculum SAFE(Supporting Adolescents and their Families in Emergencies) for boys and girls aged 10 to 19 years to increase their safety and participation, strengthen family and peer support, and equip them with positive coping strategies. Adolescents are also supported with "Learn to Lead" structured curriculum that support building resilience and civic engagement, particularly on issues related to leadership, social cohesion, advocacy, conflict resolution and peace building. They will also be engaged in "Learn to Earn" sessions to equip them with transferable business and life skills. Awareness raising activity is designed based on the child protection needs identified through CPIMS, and regular child protection situation monitoring in each camp and host communities using comprehensive community outreach module. Community-based child protection mechanisms will be established and or strengthened in community. child protection risk mapping and risk mitigation plan will be developed by the adolescent club members and CBCPC member. IRC will also work on Systems strengthening and Capacity building; IRC will facilitate the training and capacity building scheme for social workers, local/national NGOs, non-CP actor, community leaders and GOB structures. IRC will ensure that survivors of abuse, exploitation, violence and neglect - including unaccompanied and separated children and other vulnerable children have access to case management services. This system to provide age, gender and culturally appropriate processes and services and the system will serve as a basis for national child welfare system as the country moves into recovery or development phases. Early Childhood Development (ECD) intervention will also be introduced with the integration of Education component of IRC.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Needs among the host community in Ukhiya and Teknaf arise mainly from existing development challenges but have been compounded by the refugee influx. While basic services have been provided in the camp, children still face the risks related to neglect, exploitation and violence including gender-based violence risks, child marriage and child labour. The most reported CP concern revealed from child protection trend analysis of CPSS are neglect, child Labor, child marriage, separated from family and physical, emotional or psychological abuse. Adolescent girls are most at risk of child marriage whereas adolescent boys are at risk of child labor. It is also observed in 2022, the rate of child marriage is increasing among the Rohingya community. The trends of physical and emotional abuse is increasing and children are from 5-11 years are mostly experiencing this.The most child protection concerns perceived in the camps were child labour (64%), neglect (59%), child marriage (57%) and risks of trafficking (32%). These were followed closely by depression (27%), separation of children (25%) and physical abuse of the child (23%). Additionally, 59% of respondents have noticed an increase in violence against children in the community in the last 12 months. (Findings from Child Protection Assessment, October 2021). This revealed that parents using negative coping mechanism and child labour and child marriage found as a result of negative coping strategy. According to the Child labor study, IRC, September 2022, 22% of boys and 5% of girls were involved in paid employment in the host community, whereas 15.6% of boys and 1.5% of girls worked in the Rohingya community. In both communities, limited family income is the primary reasons for sending children to paid work. Moreover, in both communities, the prevalence of dropout rises with age. Around 40% of host children (15 to 17 yrs.) and 70% of Rohingya of the same age group were not attending school or madrasa. Programming to support adolescents have not been enough over the years forcing many adolescent boys and girls not to be engaged in any meaningful skill development programming. This is leading the adolescents, especially boys to easily engage in negative coping mechanisms (criminal groups, substance abuse, etc). There is limited access to national protection systems including response services (case management) formal, non-formal and tertiary education in both Rohingya and host community children. The current level of interventions for adolescents who are out of school is inadequate to fully empower them with skills and mitigate the tendency among them, to engage in high-risk behavior. The consequences of COVID-19 have proven to have a significant impact on parental stress, with caregivers and parents expressing their level of anxiety and psychological distress, economic hardship, and competing priorities, make it challenging for caregivers to fulfil their responsibilities. The Global protection cluster (2022) reports chain of causality as a lot of climate impacts are linked to a longer chain of causality to protection risks such as livelihoods questions, migration or movement, etc. Limited multi-disciplinary strategies to understand the links between climate impacts and protection risks requires working with actors with different areas of expertise. climate risks and protection risks reinforce each other – communities in conflict are unable to take the required adaptation, develop resilience, and mitigation needed to reduce climate risk. Increased climate risk increases complex and muti-dimensional protection risks.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The proposed project is aligned with rationalization principles including principle-3 adopt an integrated approach and principle-4 Ensure predictability and continuity. Following the rationalization principles, CPSS completed rationalization process. CPSS prepared rationalization mapping following some criteria such as 3W matrix, 5w data, agency’s priority, fairness of distribution, bilateral and trilateral discussions between/amongst agencies to agree on presence in camps etc. With the active involvement of SAG, CPSS finalized rationalization mapping having two agencies in one camp. Final rationalization mapping was shared with appealing partners and SAG. IRC child protection covered camps (1E, 1W, 5, 8E, 14 18, 22 and 25) and two host community locations in 2022. Following CPSS Final rationalization mapping, IRC will work in Camp 1E, 1W, 6, 8E & 25. where IRC’s integrated protection (GP, CP, GBV) along with other sectors Health, SRH, DRR, Education already are in place. The main purpose of the IRC’s rationalization is to ensure service integration and reduce the service gaps and costs with quality maintenance. IRC has a good presence, and coordination with the government, and area of responsibility organization (AOR) including UN, INGO, NNGO, CBOs since 2017. The IRC will participate and support the inter-sectoral camp-based coordination and camp-level coordination platforms, also maintain good relations with the Government (in particular, with CiCs). Coordination and collaboration will be highly maintained between two cp partner. IRC will build the capacity of implementing partners including women-led organizations/local organizations to improve their capacity in delivering services to refugees. Following the fund-raising strategy, IRC will work to ensure stability of program operation. IRC operates its program in same camps for multiple years and this strategy will be followed in 2023 and onwards.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

The proposed project will focus on child protection interventions. The IRC will ensure access to protection mechanisms to increase capacity to stay safe, exercise basic rights, and access essential services. The activities will enhance rights-based, reliable, and quality humanitarian programming that considers age, sex, disability, specific needs, and cultural appropriateness throughout the project. IRC will ensure integration within the protection (CP, GP, and GBV) and with other sectors of IRC and partners. Particular attention should be dedicated to all mechanisms to protect refugees from Sexual Abuse and Exploitation by humanitarian workers.

3.2 - Gender

The patriarchal representation of masculinity is very strong in Rohingya, and host communities, men and boys are leading decision, bread winners for the family and protector for any emergency. The project will support both women and girls and men and boys in their respective locations. The approach to engage boys will be done through a global curriculum called SAFE, that support boys with knowledge related to gender. In addition to facility-based activities, block-level initiatives will be provided to reach the most marginalized including children with disabilities and adolescent girls. The IRC will reach the age, gender, and diverse population throughout the project and disaggregate the data in line with AGD approach. IRC will follow the gender ratio in staff and volunteers’ recruitment and management representation, train on gender mainstreaming and PSEA, and design and implement a gender-sensitive intervention for changing situation through shifting the norms and cultural practices.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

The IRC will analyze the likely risks and needs of the clients in-terms of age and diversity. A risk matrix with strategies to prevent, minimise and or mitigate the consequences of the risks will be developed. The services are designed to address the specific needs of target population. Child safeguarding is dealt with highest organizational priority, this falls under mandatory reporting protocol. Both preventive and response mechanism are in place to ensures safeguarding, reporting in-terms of clear flowchart and anonymous reporting process in every facility where IRC operate, response includes immediate support to the survivor including functioning referral pathways. Content and materials are mainstreamed with existing curriculums to ensure safeguarding sessions with clients on a regular interval to empower them to report any concerns. Child safeguarding risks are regularly assessed, and mitigation strategies are designed throughout the project cycle management.

3.4 - Diversity

Under this Action, age, gender, diversity (AGD) and inclusivity approaches will be mainstreamed. Diverse people including PWD, will be engaged throughout the project. IRC has a strong presence at PWG and ADWG, and good relations with other specialized organizations. All the tools and formats of IRC to review through the gender diverse lens and train the staff on gender, diversity, and PSEA. The protection mainstreaming will help to identify the risk for the diverse population, and service delivery to ensure according to the diverse population. The integrated programming approach will contribute to expediting cross-referrals and will allow for assistance to be tailored to the individual needs of beneficiaries. Intervention for children and adolescents will be contextualized and customized considering the different age groups of children. Diverse groups will be considered whether it is recruitment of staff or selection of project staff and volunteers.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

IRC will ensure solid community feedback mechanism, which is closely aligned with the Core Humanitarian Standards. IRC will promote and functioning feedback and accountability mechanism amid children, parents, caregiver and community people. Child participation will be strengthened at different level of the project stage. IRC will continue the separate CP hotline number to hear beneficiary voice, conduct regular group discussions with the beneficiaries, protection monitoring, visit door to door to collect feedback and complaints from the community and response to mitigate child protection concern. IRC will ensure that CP referral pathways are regularly updated and disseminated it to related stakeholder. IRC will also orient IRC and implementing partner staff on AAP-related actions and principles, including PSEA; ensure the implementation of child safeguarding policy. As a co-chair of the PSEA network, IRC will strengthen SEA reporting system.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

DRM has been incorporated to reduce the risk of negative effects and adverse impacts on life and the environment; the project will establish an information management system to ensure early warning, multi-hazard preparedness messages to increase awareness, enhance knowledge and skills of frontline staff, volunteers and community people to implement disaster risk reduction interventions effectively and identify the risk of natural hazard through community risk assessment periodically by building community-based CP system, to do advocacy with the relevant actors and also to provide emergency child protection services during a disaster. IRC is well-positioned to scale up its existing emergency programming in Cox’s Bazar given its unique capacity to implement integrated protection programs. IRC will also work with PERU team for effective representation for pre and post emergency response. The project will ensure environmental sustainability and climate change mitigation measure.
