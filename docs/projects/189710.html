Project Summary

In this project, Save a Life will seek to improve access to safe, appropriate emergency shelter and lifesaving non-food items to 12000 individuals through general in-kind assistance in Aweil South county in Northern Bahr Ghazaal. To enhance the coping mechanism of the affected populations, SLI will support most vulnerable returnees and host communities/affected non-displaced with durable solutions such as training on Fuel Efficient stoves and how to make shelter and non-food items using locally available materials such as grass, soil and trees. Through this project, SLI will support communities affected by natural disasters such as flooding to recover by building their resilience to recover from disasters. In locations experiencing Land, Housing and Property issues, SLI will seek to build the capacity of local authorities and affected populations with skills and knowledge on resilience building and access to land, housing, and property.

1 - Needs

The 2022 South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview Report estimate that 2.4 Million persons will face S/NFIs needs in 2022. Of this, 50% are women, 53% children and 15% individuals living with disability. Key drivers of S/NFIs needs include latest flooding whose impact started in the last quarter of 2021 and is expected to last up to 2022, general and localized conflict, disruption of supply routes and markets, macroeconomic instability (hyperinflation), food insecurity that is leading families to spend the little they have on food than buying shelter or nonfood items, time poverty and Impact of Covid-19. Some of the places that are likely to experience serious S/NFIs needs in 2022 include Northern Bahr El Ghazal, more so in Aweil South County. The HNO report has classified 49,700 people are facing severe needs in the county. Also, 21,300 and 14,200 individuals are facing extreme and catastrophic needs in the county. Further, between 50,000 and 100,000 people are seriously impacted by flooding which ravaged the country in the last quarter of 2021. Other factors causing SNFIs needs include inflow of displaced persons from IDPs and Refugee camps to their home of origin. In these Counties, the IDPs and Returnees are coming home to find shelters they left behind destroyed, looted or burnt as a result of conflict. Others find their original home occupied and have no means of generating income to establish new shelters. As a result, they are forced to sleep under trees, occupy abandoned structures and institutions, or share living and sleeping spaces with host community or relatives. In such circumstances, the affected individuals live in inhumane conditions and are exposed to elements of weather and protection risks. Women and girls sharing sleeping spaces with male counterparts or sleeping outside lack privacy and at risk of exposure to sexual gender based violence. OCHA projects that the impact of the floods will last into 2022 necessitating provision of S/NFIs items to affected populations. Beyond flooding, food insecure households are most likely to utilize the little resources or money they have to buy food instead of shelter materials or other household items such as mosquito nets, cooking sets or clothes. Others are likely to sell the little items they have or exchange them for food. As a result of fighting, markets, supply routes and livelihoods got disrupted. Vendors vacated insecure areas while transporters removed their vehicles from the road for fear of being attacked. Job opportunities shrunk as private, public and non-government organizations stopped or scaled down their operations. The cumulative impact on such disruption has resulted in reduced production, collapse of markets, unemployed population that rely on aid and reducing purchasing power among populations. This would mean that essential shelter and non-food items are not found in some of the rural markets, are too expensive or affected communities who rely on aid have no means of buying the items. In locations where local markets are well stocked with essential shelter and non-food items, the community lack the means to buy the items due to diminished income generating activities courtesy of Covid-19 and general unemployment.

2 - Project activities

OUTPUT 1.1: 12000 beneficiaries comprised of Host Community, 7000 Internally Displaced and 3000 host communities and 2000 Returnees provided with shelter and non-food items through General In-kind Distribution (GIK). ACTIVITY 1.1.1: Conduct community communication, consultation and sensitization meetings on the S/NFIs assistance with local authorities, community leaders and members to provide project information and the role of the community in the implementation process. ACTIVITY 1.1.2: Community Mobilization, Registration, verification and selection of 12000 beneficiaries with the participation of community members, leaders, and local authorities, and beneficiary’s representatives. ACTIVITY 1.1.3: Prepositioning of Shelter and Non-food items from core pipeline ACTIVITY 1.1.4: Conduct distribution of Shelter and Non-food Items to 12000 using S/NFIs cluster Criteria in target counties. ACTIVITY 1.1.5: Conduct PDMs on Shelter and Non-food items with 17% beneficiaries randomly selected to ascertain use, appropriateness and benefits of items distributed. OUTPUT 2.1: ACTIVITY 2.1.1: 12000 most vulnerable returnees and host communities provided with awareness and training on durable solutions, rebuilding lives through shelter and NFI solutions and disaster risk reduction. ACTIVITY 2.1.2: Conduct awareness on disaster risk reduction through local authorities, project staff, chiefs and community leaders targeting 12,000 affected persons. ACTIVITY 2.1.3: Establish and train three (3) Disaster Management Committees and assist the committees develop and implement their own Climate Change Adaptation Plans.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

SLI believes a good project design should ensure that targeted beneficiaries have been consulted at the start of the project design period. This ensures that the project addresses a need that is of relevance for the beneficiaries and stakeholders. Inclusion of targeted beneficiaries during project design fosters relationship with the communities and also beneficiary involved in beneficiary feedback mechanism report a feeling of empowerment. It also ensures that the most vulnerable are included and they benefit from the project. SLI adapted to the following feedback mechanisms, Focused Group Discussions, Key Informative Interviews, Project design meetings, regular beneficial feedback meetings, routine community visits, suggestion boxes and Hotline Numbers. A variety of feedback mechanisms helps to suit different groups of people, i.e illiterate, PWDs, aged and youth. Every SLI staff handling feedback/complain mechanism is fully trained in psychosocial support, these enables them handle PWDs as well as GBV victims in a professional manner. (Creating clear referral pathways with stakeholders’) To achieve this SLI considers sustainability and exit strategy for the feedback mechanism as part of the initial design phase. SLI Feedback/complain is handled as follows; All complaints or feedback received through any complaints channel (Feedback and Complaint Desk or toll free telephone number) during implementation of the project will be recorded on a Feedback and Complaint log. The Programme Manager will maintain this log.All complaints or feedback received will be processed appropriately, timely and ensuring upmost confidentiality, privacy and safety of complainants and those affected. The Feedback and Complaint log will be password protected, securely stored to ensure confidentiality, privacy and safety of complainants. Hard copies will be printed and led on weekly basis within a secure complaints & feedback folder. The register will be reviewed on a weekly basis to ensure any open complaints or feedback addressed, processed and closed appropriately and the log is being kept updated. Key learning's or changes to policy, process or working practices arising from received complaints or feedback will be extracted monthly and shared with the SLI’s senior management for wider organizational learning. Beneficiaries and communities, especially the most marginalized and vulnerable will be periodically consulted to ensure they are satisfied with the complaint and feedback mechanism and is accessible, effective, confidential and safe.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Lack of access to/awareness of referral services for survivors of protection incidents (GBV, child protection). SLI is part of referral pathways in Aweil Counties. All GBV, PSEA and other protection cases will be referred through existing referral pathways for specialized services. On Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance. SLI will work with local authorities, Religious Leaders, youth leaders and create awareness on importance of girls/women not on to walk in groups but as well be accompanied by men (fathers, brothers, religious leaders or local authority) to safeguard girls and women at risk of exposure of violence as a result of distance to and from location of humanitarian assistance and services. The project will deliver its support or inputs at each community centers or with radius of one-two kilometers radius as well conducting activities during the daytime to allow the women/girls walk during safe hours of the day. In Local context this can highly minimize the risk. To ensure individuals living with disability do not miss on humanitarian assistance, SLI will work closely with local authorities (chiefs) and religious leaders who will help in identifying PWDs living within the area, this is considering some of the PWDs have problems walking or even getting information prior.
