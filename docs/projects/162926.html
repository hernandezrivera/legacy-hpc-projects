Project Summary

The project’s overall objective will be to improve capacities of vulnerable refugees and host communities to access education and FSL services and materials. The project will target to reach at least 2000 families (approximately 10,000 individuals) in El Jabalein and El-salaam localities (for White Nile State); Omdurman and Um bada localities (Khartoum State).
For Khartoum state, the focus will be education, while in White Nile state, the focus will be education and FSL.
Under FSL, the project activities will focus on provision of agricultural inputs, provision of vocational trainings, provision of business start-up kits (in cash or kind), and conducting of trainings, linkages and networking activities to such entities as financial institutions, cooperatives and SILCS.
Under education component of the project the project will seek to improve access to quality primary education for vulnerable refugee and host community children.
Specific project objectives are:
1. To improve learning environment through construction/rehabilitation of classrooms, WASH in schools (rehabilitation/construction of latrines and water points; and hygiene promotion activities through Sanitation Health Clubs) and capacity building of School Management Committees;
2. To increase enrolment and retention of school-age refugee children through enrolment campaigns and provision of retention support (school fees, textbooks, uniforms and female hygiene kits) for children.
3. To improve learning outcomes through re-training and provision of incentives for teachers, and provision of teaching and learning aids material (chalk, pen, exercise books and instruction charts) for target schools.
Project will place special focus on people with special needs such as girls and women who are disproportionately at risk (due to their refugee status), thus vulnerable to early marriage, child labour and exploitation, and SGBV. The project duration will be 12 months.

1 - Needs

A UNHCR Participatory Needs Assessment (December 2018) highlights the following education needs in the 9 camps in White Nile: - Syllabus are not completed in time.- High school dropouts (especially girls, who end up being married off or exploited through various forms of child abuse) are high due to financial constraints. Majority of girls at puberty stage stay away from schools when experiencing their monthly periods.- Widespread shortage of school materials such as school uniforms, exercise books and text books. - Teachers are absent from the schools most of the time.- Poor sanitation enviroment and limited number of latrines in the refugees schools.- Lack of school recreational activities.- Insufficient sources of safe and clean drinking water for the schools.- Refugees are not able to provide breakfast meal for their children who attend schools ,thus poor class attendance and participation- The few classrooms are in dilapidated conditions and are congested. The impacts of the economic situation on refugee communities is compounded by limited access to livelihoods for South Sudanese refugees across the response. Against this backdrop, the increase in the cost of living due to high inflation has affected the purchasing power of refugees and undermined their capacity to earn enough income to become self-reliant, heightening assistance needs and dependency across the response. This inability to cover basic needs is also accompanied by several knock-on effects, including increased tensions with host communities, school drop-out and low enrolment, prevalence of child labour, early marriage and other child protection issues, and sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) risk.South Sudanese refugees interviewed during participatory assessment (UNHCR; December 2018) also cited poverty as a major challenge they face in Sudan. This is driven by lack of productive assets that can be utilized to support their livelihoods, insufficient technical and entrepreneur skills, lack of access to work permits and documentation, limited employment opportunities, and gaps in financial services to support entrepreneurship. For refugees living in rural areas, both in camps and out-of-camp settlements, key constraints include prohibitions on land ownership and/or limited access cultivation via crop-sharing, and limited market accessibility.
