Project Summary

The project endeavors to implement social cohesion activties and empowering both refugees and host community in Western rural of of Aleppo province.
SYCAC saw the implementation of its project, entitled Safe and Friendly Spaces for Community Dialogue, as a fundamental action to increase stability and support for more stable livelihood of both refugees and host community, based on the following Outcomes:
Outcome 1:
-Provide protection and guidance services to people affected by GBV, empowering women and girls (the most vulnerable) to earn livelihoods and job opportunities; The following vulnerability criteria will be adopted as a condition of registration:
• Women, adolescent girls and boys at risk either face or are at risk of discrimination, genderbased violence and denial of services, including for persons with disabilities.
• Single-parent families, the elderly, and people with disabilities face neglect and denial of services.
• Men, women and children with chronic diseases and mental disorders
• Pregnant and breastfeeding women
• Healthy/disabled family members
Outcome 2:
- Reducing conflict and tension between the locals and refugees’ communities by creating safe spaces for dialogue and building channels of communication;
This intervention will support 310 direct beneficiaries, and more than 100,000 indirect beneficiaries (family members, relatives, personal network, work network, local authorities, organizations, associations) of protection services and interventions to reduce tension and conflict.

3 - Needs

Growing tensions between locals and refugees are exacerbated by a context of economic deterioration with the exchange rate of the Turkish lira against the dollar, the resulted in loss of purchasing power and wages, as well as a decrease in job opportunities.in addition to a significant increase in cases of aggression and violence against IDPs, for these reasons, the conditions of life for refugees have become indescribably difficult .Based on a recent needs assessment carried out by SYCAC in the Rural Aleppo in April 2022, targeting a group of 1464 young men and women included in an age range from 18 to 50 years old, including 849 man, 615 women, 682 employed, unemployed 782, 1068 refugees, 396 host community members; the results showed that: i) more than half of the respondents are unemployed despitehaving obtained university degrees, ii) the percentage of HHW who want to learn a craft exceeds 60% of the respondents, indicating their preference indicating their preference in joining small project andinitiatives engaged in learning, iii) people interviewed suggest that activists and actors should play a greater role in awareness, cohesion and integration for the local community of refugees and host community.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

All SYCAC programming is based on the “Do No Harm” principle. SYCAC recognizes that providing any service during a conflict in which needs are dire and resources are scarce, has the potential to cause conflict and protection concerns for all actors involved. We will conduct outreach to clarify to the community the scope of the specific targeted communities that we will be targeted and expect that this will generate an understanding amongst the host community. We will focus concerted communication on making sure that the target of the civil society actors understands the nature of the intervention. This will focus on preparing them for the end of the intervention from the beginning so that they make best use of the time that this will support them. This information will also be reiterated during each coordination meeting and coaching session. Program and M&E staff will be present during program implementation to address any concerns that beneficiaries may have. Additionally, during the needs assessment process, program staff will make note of any particularly vulnerable cases or protection concerns and refer those affected confidentially to relative services providers SYCAC is coordinating with.SYCAC employees will also raise awareness of the importance of the presence of people with disabilities within the stages of the project, and ensure that all activities are fair to them with participation, resources, and their special needs, to facilitate their access to resources.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SYCAC will recognize the risks faced by beneficiaries, and the difficulties they face in reporting harm they experience by the organization will not tolerate our staff, partners or associates to engage in or support any type of sexual exploitation and abuse or child abuse. The principle of ‘best interests’ will guide our actions in response to concerns and breaches of this policy. SYCAC understands that, in order for the PSEA/Child Safeguarding policy to be well understood and effectively implemented, it is essential that there are high levels of awareness regarding the policy and that staff and others are clear, confident and competent in putting the policy into practice. Disseminate and promote the policy widely. Copies of the policy will be made available and accessible to all stakeholders, both in hard copy and electronically in order to ensure that the policy commitments, principles, and practices are publicized and that everyone is clear about SYCAC position in relation to PSEA and safeguarding children, In addition, all papers and brochures distributed by the PSEAH Network will be published within all project activities, ensuring easy access to all complaints channels, especially for women and people with special needs.the affected population will have access to the intervention services equally as appropriate with the need and without any discrimination.
