Project Summary

The purpose of this project is to reduce hunger, improve nutrition, and increase food security for 900 blast-affected and other food insecure households in Beirut. This project will expand MERATH's ongoing voucher project to an additional 900 food insecure households affected by the August 4 explosions in Beirut and Lebanon's macroeconomic crisis in Beirut.

1 - Needs

On 4 August 2020, two massive explosions at warehouses containing ammonium nitrate at Beirut’s port resulted in widespread damage to buildings and infrastructure across the capital. As of August 17, the explosions had resulted in the death of 1780 individuals and, injured over 6,000 others, while at least another dozen remained missing, OCHA reports. OCHA confirms that the price of essential items, such as food—which were already expensive before the explosion—are anticipated to continue to increase. MERATH’s local church partners have been assessing needs, helping clean streets and damaged houses, and/or providing hot meals to displaced and affected populations since August 5. MERATH and some of its partners undertook preliminary rapid needs assessments on August 6 and 7 in which potential beneficiaries identified food assistance as a key need, which is in line with assessments undertaken by other organizations.

2 - Activities

MERATH's network of up to 15 local church and community-based partners will assess needs, select beneficiaries based on MERATH's vulnerability criteria, and distribute 900 food vouchers and COVID-19 prevention leaflets (the leaflets will be distributed for 3 months only). The assistance provided will be in addition to MERATH's ongoing food voucher program and utilize MERATH's existing partnerships with grocery stores. MERATH will coordinate with the FSSWG and OCHA, cross-reference beneficiary lists with WFP, and require partners to closely coordinate with other organizations at the local level to prevent any duplication of assistance. MERATH will conduct household surveys in September 2020 and March 2021 to measure progress against project outcomes. The assistance provided will be needs-based, unconditional, and in line with humanitarian principles and MERATH’s core values of impartiality, dignity, and stewardship.
