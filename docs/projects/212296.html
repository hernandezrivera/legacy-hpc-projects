Project Summary

The ongoing Syrian crisis, compounded by earthquake aftermath, severely impacts education. A 4% rise in People in Need (PiN) highlights the urgency for emergency education, affecting 7.1 million, including 10% with disabilities. NWS sees significant challenges with approximately 69% of children in Idleb and 38-48% in Ar-Raqqa, Damascus, Rural Damascus, and Aleppo being (OoSC). Economic factors impact 6.9 million children (46% girls, 54% boys), compelling families to withdraw children, emphasizing a 4% increase in PiN compared to the previous year. Overcrowded classrooms, inadequate WASH facilities, and electricity shortages hinder the learning environment, necessitating strategic intervention aligned with Qatar Charity's (QC) 2024 plan. Addressing the specific challenge of OOSC is crucial, focusing on inclusive education and creating conducive learning environments, especially in NWS.
QC is planning for a comprehensive response under the education sector in NWS includes:
- Rehabilitation of 275 classrooms in formal schools, with a specific emphasis on improving 30 WASH facilities (water networks, doors, tanks, Tiles) , accessibility for (CwDs), electricity, doors, windows, and beautification for all targeted schools (19250 Bnfs, 10,010 girls).
- Construction of 30 concrete classrooms in crowded and already existed schools (expansion) to increase access (Bnfs 2100, Girls 1092)
- Construction of 32-classroom school from scratch in Azaz sub-district (Bnfs 2240, girls 1165)
- 5000 school bags will be distributed to children (2600 girls)
- 9 schools (3000 students, 14750 girls) in Idleb camps will be supported by teacher stipends and operational costs for 9 months (170 education cadre, 110 females)
- Cash for education will be provided to 160 children (72 girls, 1 USD a day) affected by child labor in Albab camps
- Provision of heating fuel for 1250 classrooms for 3 months/ (87500 Bnfs, 45500 girls)
- Winterization clothing kits support to in-school students (5100 Bnfs, 2652 girls)
Activities will be implemented directly by QC if possible. But when it requires big # of staff or wide geographical spread, QC will rely on IPs due to access and logistic barriers.
Monitoring will be in place through field monitors who visit regularly and conduct surveys, PDMs, tools etc. Complaints are welcomed through boxes, WhatsApp and hot-line.
In NWS, education sector is confronted with various protection risks. Under safeguarding, Overcrowded classrooms and inadequate supervision create an environment susceptible to instances of bullying, abuse, and exploitation. Here QC will Implement rigorous teacher training programs on child protection, establish clear reporting mechanisms, and conduct regular audits to ensure a safe learning environment. Also, under Gender-Based Violence (GBV) Risks: Vulnerable populations, including girls, are at risk of experiencing gender-based violence, both within and outside the school environment. To mitigate this risk QC will Develop and implement gender-sensitive policies, conduct awareness campaigns to empower students, and establish safe environment within schools with trained staff to address GBV incidents promptly.
Moreover, negative Behavior of Teachers including discrimination or harassment by teachers, can contribute to a hostile learning atmosphere. And to prevent this risk, QC will focus on positive discipline, cultural sensitivity, and inclusivity. Establish confidential reporting mechanisms to express concerns.
Under SEA, QC believes that Power imbalances between teachers, school staff, and students can create an environment conducive to exploitation. QC will Implement and enforce a strict code of conduct addressing power dynamics. Also, Lack of accessible and confidential reporting mechanisms can discourage victims or witnesses from coming forward. Thus, QC will establish multiple reporting channels, and conduct awareness campaigns to educate students and staff about the reporting mechanism.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

QC, in coordination with the Education Cluster and education authorities inside Syria, will rehabilitate 275 classrooms affected by the earthquake in Idleb and Aleppo. Prioritization will be based on collaboration with the Education Cluster, targeting the most substantial schools to maximize the impact of project activities. List of schools will be coordinated with EDs in both Aleppo and Idleb to check their priorities (%50 Idleb : %50 Aleppo) and to be confirmed by Edu cluster to avoid duplication. The focus is not only on repairing physical structures but also on making schools accessible for CWDs by incorporating mobility facilitation aides such as ramps, tubes, and accessible toilets. All rehab works is planned to be done during Summer vacation 2024.Also, and in response to overcrowded schools, QC will construct additional concrete 40m2 classrooms in high-demand schools, aiming to enhance learning environments by reducing student-teacher ratios. In Azaz, where approximately 200,000 Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) reside, QC will build a new 32-classroom primary school with full furniture, contributing to reducing the number of (OOSC) in the region.Moreover, and as part of the project, 5000 school bags will be distributed to children in camps, catering to various age groups. Each bag includes essential educational materials such as A4 notebooks, pens, pencils, erasers, coloring materials, a pen case, ruler, and a drawing notebook. This initiative not only provides necessary supplies but also encourages and supports children in their educational journey.QC will also ensure the continuity of education in 9 schools within camps, encompassing 75 semi-permanent education spaces and involving 170 school staff. This support includes teacher stipends for an academic year and covers running costs for essentials such as water, electricity, internet, hygiene, and refreshments for teachers.Additionally, and to address the urgent winterization needs, QC will support 1250 classrooms in NWS with fuel during winter classes, providing 4 liters per day for 50 days. Additionally, winterization clothing kits, including shoes and jackets, will be distributed to 5000 in-school students in camps. This dual approach not only ensures a comfortable learning environment but also empowers students to attend school and engage in safe outdoor activities during the winter season.Activities will be implemented directly by QC if possible. But when it requires big # of staff or wide geographical spread, QC will rely on IPs due to access and logistic barriers. This comprehensive project seeks to create a positive and inclusive educational environment in Idleb and Aleppo, NWS, addressing immediate infrastructure needs while strategically aligning with QC's commitment to the holistic development of the community. The coordination with the Education Directorate and the Education Cluster ensures a well-rounded and impactful intervention in the region's education sector.
