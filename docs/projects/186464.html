Project Summary

The ETS will provide humanitarians across Borno, Yobe and Adamawa states with reliable communications services to enable an eﬃcient and safer response, ultimately saving more lives. ETS services allow humanitarians to implement eﬃciently their activities and carry out their jobs safely in areas where services from local services providers are unstable or non-operational. In addition, the ETS will continue to build the capacity of local partners, will map the communication needs of the affected population in North-East Nigeria and evaluate the possibility of implementing a pilot project to address identified needs for communities.

1 - Needs

More than a decade into the crisis, communications infrastructure remains severely affected, characterized by a lack of – or non-existent – reliable and safe telecommunications, mobile networks and Internet services, especially outside of urban areas. This gap was identified as of one the main impediments to enable humanitarian programming and ensure the safety of humanitarian actors when operating in a complex operational environment. To enable a coordinated humanitarian response and the safety and security of humanitarian personnel, the ETS was activated in November 2016 in Nigeria to provide vital common communications services - Internet and security communications - to the humanitarian community responding in the most-affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe. Shared security telecommunications services are provided in the capital states in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa states, and Internet and security telecommunications in the eight humanitarian hubs gradually deployed across Borno State. Given the criticality of the ETS communications services, especially in deep-ﬁeld locations, a continuous power solution is required to ensure provision of services 24/7. In August 2020, the ETS received the required hybrid power solution equipment for its infrastructure, the hybrid solar power solution but the installation has been delayed due to the COVID-19 travel restrictions. In 2021, the hybrid solar power solution was installed in all ETS location to ensure ETS services are available 24/7. ETS services currently being provided will be maintained, upgraded and expanded in line with the evolving needs on the ground. The ETS continuously assessed the communications needs across North-East during frequent ﬁeld missions and consultations with partners through dedicated local ETS Working Group meetings in Maiduguri.

2 - Activities

In 2021, the ETS worked closely with its partners, including United Nations (UN) agencies and International Non-Governmental Organisations (INGOs), to ensure a coordinated response and services to the entire humanitarian community. Throughout 2022, the ETS will maintain this approach to: 1) Continue the provision of coordination and information management activities To ensure a coherent response and minimise duplication of efforts, the ETS will continue the provision of coordination and information management services. The ETS will participate in relevant sector meetings and will organise dedicated ETS coordination meetings in Maiduguri. Updated ETS information products, including dashboards, situation reports, infographics, operational documentation and meeting minutes, among others, will be produced, timely disseminated among global and local partners and posted on the Nigeria emergency page on the ETCluster.org platform.2) Maintain Internet and security telecommunications services in 10 areas currently covered with ETS services in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa states. In 2022, the ETS security telecommunications services covering Maiduguri, Damaturu and Yola metropolitan areas as well as the ETS Internet connectivity and security telecommunications services deployed at the humanitarian hubs in Bama, Banki, Damasak, Dikwa, Gwoza, Maiduguri, Monguno and Ngala will be maintained. 3) Map the communications needs of the affected population and evaluate how ETS can fulfil them.The ETS will conduct an assessment to identify the communication gaps of the affected population in North-East Nigeria, evaluate how the ETS can fulﬁl them and implement identified activities. 4) Continue delivering capacity-building activities to humanitarian actors.The ETS will provide training on security telecommunications procedures for the entire humanitarian community on a demand basis for an eﬃcient use of the ETS security telecommunications services. Technical radio training and capacity building activities for humanitarian partners will be conducted;
