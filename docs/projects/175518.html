Project Summary

This proposed project will address emergency shelter and non-food items needs of vulnerable populations affected by conflict, flooding or in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) acute food insecurity both displaced and in secondary displacement. The project will target 37, 000 individuals (Men 7088, Women 9338, Boys 9870, Girls 11,704) both newly displaced and protracted IDPs and host communities, for emergency shelter and NFIs activities response in Lakes (Rumbek North, Yirol East, and Yirol West) and Warrap (Tonj North and Tonj South ) most vulnerable populations in need (after an assessment). The project will reach 15% of the target population (6,030 individuals) as People with Special Needs. About 93% of the target population will receive Gift In-Kind assistance which will be prepositioned from the Cluster Core Pipeline. After an assessment has been done, and an analysis report submitted through the SFP to the Cluster at the National level, a pipeline request will be submitted. After approval, items will be transported to respective CCOSS storage facilities and subsequent distributions. CCOSS will coordinate will other cluster partners in respective locations for effective response. 7% of the target population will benefit through the CBI approach – Restricted voucher modality. A market assessment will be done, participants identified and issued with vouchers before the redemption process is done
The convergence of multiple shocks in already vulnerable areas in 2020 and 2021 could lead to severe outcomes. Crucial information gaps remain a challenge, with poor access to many parts of Lakes and Warrap due to insecurity and inadequate infrastructure. Lakes and Warrap states have continued to remain one of the most difficult locations especially for humanitarian agencies to operate. This is due to drivers and multipliers of the crisis that have remained present over time. These include insecurity and violence, local and inter-communal violence (IVC), ongoing displacement, sparse basic services, disease, climate shocks, flooding, economic instability, and insecure access to food and livelihoods.
CCOSS will also ensure that there is proper coordination especially in locations where there is more than one implementing partner. County cluster meetings will be held weekly to ensure proper coordination, avoid double-counting, and as well reduce duplication of efforts. CCOSS is a key member of other clusters working within the same locality as well as a State Co-lead for Lakes and Warrap state.
CCOSS will use humanitarian hubs for coordination meetings and other related humanitarian activities. CCOSS will be working with other partners who are operating in the same areas or locations on the issues affecting vulnerable populations in hard to reached areas through mobile response in collaboration with other partners on the ground. CCOSS will also work with other partners on area-based approaches through the open forums during cluster meetings where partners would highlight activities being implemented and locations of operation to avoid duplication activities. This would be achieved through coordination of the cluster and support of CCCM, coordinating the activities of partners to avoid overlaps of the same activities in one location.
General Operation & other direct costs include costs associated with providing direct management & other support functions at the Main office in Juba. This includes management staff salaries, communication, stationery, rents, etc. The Indirect project costs include bank charges, staff recruitment & servicing costs, auditing and consultancy costs.

1 - Needs

According to the IPC report for October 2020 – July 2021; in the lean season of April to July 2021, the food security situation will deteriorate in Lakes and Warrap states and an estimated 13,000 people will likely be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Tonj North County. During this period, all counties are classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, except for Twic County which is classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. This deterioration will be driven by the seasonal depletion of food stocks, likelihood of high food prices, reduced market functionality, and access challenges occasioned by the onset of rainfall that leads to the worsening of road conditions, as well as the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic, economic shocks and currency devaluation that will continue to erode households’ purchasing power. A Shelter and Non-Food Items assessment conducted by REACH in April 2020 found out that Rumbek North was among the top five counties where the highest proportion of assessed settlements (31%) reported any severity of shelter damage as a result of the conflict. The County also reported 25% of the IDPs living out in the open. IDPs and Host Communities in Rumbek North, Tonj North, Yirol East, and Yirol West have the highest severity of needs. Within these population groups, some of the vulnerable groups that may have specific needs include children, women at risk, the elderly, people with disabilities, single-headed household members, and the extremely poor. Being unable to support themselves through communal connections and without coping mechanisms from other resources, people who are outside camps have very limited or no access to essential life-saving shelter or NFIs. In both protracted and new displacements, people are forced to leave behind basic household items and shelter materials as a result of conflict compounded by food insecurity and economic deterioration. Those to be targeted include women and children who will remain especially vulnerable due to their roles in society and the protection risks inherent in being without shelter or social support networks. A small number of host community members do not have sufficient emergency shelter or non-food items (ES/NFIs) as they have used their already limited resources to provide for the displaced. People’s ES/NFI needs are primarily linked to conflict-induced displacement, but at times also to food insecurity, economic deterioration, and disease outbreaks. While people outside of camp-like settings have better access to shelter materials, their overall ES/NFI needs are traditionally broader due to the absence of functional and accessible markets and lack of communal connections or coping mechanisms from other resources. This is particularly the case for IDPs who have been subjected to multiple displacements. CCOSS will focus its interventions on communities in crisis and emergency in the four Counties namely; Rumbek North, Yirol East, Yirol West, and Tonj North. The beneficiaries will be populations in need and will be targeted using the cluster targeting criteria: • Households with children under 5; • Pregnant or lactating women (PLW); • Unaccompanied elderly (>60 years); • Persons living with disability (PLWD); • Female-headed households; • Child headed households and/or unaccompanied minors; • Chronically ill people; • Persons with no effective community linkages. CCOSS will use participatory approaches to identify the needs of women, men, boys, and girls. In this proposed project, barriers to the participation of women and other vulnerable groups will be discussed and taken into account in both the assessment and implementation stages. Gender and age considerations will be included in needs assessments prior to implementation. CCOSS will also train staff, community leaders, and community members on AAP methodology. This will ensure that AAP/CCE will be incorporated in all the stages of the project cycle.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

CCOSS has conducted several market assessments in Yirol, the latest being in August 2020. Key summary findings included the following:-• There are no functional markets with S/NFIs in Adior and Malek (Yirol East) apart from small scales retailers as a result CCOSS will contract vendors who will use voucher fair modalities in order to provide S/NFIs to an area near IDPs settlement. The only market where there are NFIs is Yirol Main Market. • Of assessed settlements that reported the presence of new IDPs, 70% reported that IDPs were housed in temporary shelters, highlighting that shelter conditions were often worse for IDPs than for host community residents. • Traders confirmed they have the ability to meet the customers’ needs by supplying the NFIs within TWO WEEKS TIME; it is likely markets can absorb gradual increases in demand. Supply may increase faster if road conditions or security along the routes improve. Due to heavy rains that have been pounding South Sudan since May, roads have continually been eroded, thus rendered almost impassable. • Market access especially to affected populations out of Yirol town will remain a bigger challenge. Of the settlements that reported access to a market, only 4% reported traveling less than 30 minutes to reach it. Some take about six to seven hours to access the Yirol Main market and they move in the group which was difficult for the vulnerable person to access, and in this, there was no issue of death cases which have been reported as IDPs walk back from the market. The cost of transportation between settlements are It is based on these findings that CCOSS proposes to use Voucher fairs where traders/vendors supply the agreed non-food items and the participants are allowed to redeem their vouchers. CCOSS will also conduct monthly price monitoring targeting the Yirol market to help adjust the transfer value

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

From our previous experiences using Voucher Modality, the approach used has been voucher fairs where vendors supply the agreed non-food items and the participants are allowed to redeem their vouchers during the voucher fairs. This arrangement has ensured the following are achieved; the modality will increase the supply of goods and the presence of traders in the under-served areas with no functional markets; Increase safe access to markets of NFIs for program participants in these locations; Increase access to quality goods available to the under-served populations or introduce new goods to these areas and this modality also catalyzes the market relationship between vendors and program participants or communities in the underserved areas. This voucher fair will aim to increase networking between market actors, or bring in new goods to the areas, thus will be open to the general public.
