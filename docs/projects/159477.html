Project Summary

The project aims to increase access to safe water and improve sanitation and hygiene in an effort to reduce the transmission of AWD/cholera and water borne diseases for the host community, IDPs and returnees in the regions of Middle Shabelle, Bay and Bakool.
Over 47 per cent of the Somali population does not have access to safe drinking water. Field reports (from UNICEF) also suggest that 40 per cent of existing water sources are non-functional in addition to the high cost per cubic metre of water. The background to the malfunctioning of water supply systems are weak water supply management models, high operation and maintenance costs and lack of supply chain of spare parts coupled with technical limitation of service providers. The other problem is lack of inadequate knowledge and practice of good water, sanitation and hygiene behaviours. Most Somalis neither know the health benefits of using improved sanitation facilities nor are they aware of the health benefits of practicing handwashing with soap at critical times. (UNICEF Somalia Wash strategy note 2018-2020).INTERSOS intends to intervene to ensure rapid and sustained WASH services in order to scale up hygiene promotion to prevent potential AWD/Cholera outbreak at key health and nutrition centers as well as in any strategic basic services' provision facility in areas highly affected by displacement. During the intervention, Water Committees, composed equally by women and men to represent equally the community, will be trained to manage wash facilities.
INTERSOS is committed to ensuring that its programs are gender sensitive and achieve the Gender Marker scale. Women and girls have been adversely affected by the conflict, mainly due to breakdown in community protection mechanisms. Therefore, particular attention will be given to Lactating, pregnant women and children (0-5 years old) visiting health facilities and nutrition Centers, who will be provided with Hygiene Kits. Menstrual hygiene products and specific targeted items such as sanitary pads will be included as part of the hygiene kits. To guarantee communities' engagement, consultation in all planning and implementation phases of the project will be constant. In addition, water distribution points and sanitation facilities will be established in areas that are safe and appropriate for women to access and will be designed considering people with disability in the community. INTERSOS will use its established female community network to divulgate hygiene promotion messages.
The project’s key activities will be
-Emergency water trucking which is life-saving intervention that is used to cover interruptions in water service or access to sufficient quantities of water to meet survival requirements.
-Provision of donkey carts to improve access to water for vulnerable HHs (including HHs headed by people living with disabilities).
- Construction of communal latrines to ensure that each family has a latrine.
-Construction and fencing of waste dumping site.
-Provision of sanitation kits, Hygiene promotion through education forum, CLTS and awareness campaigns, -Rehabilitation of shallow wells followed by water management committees training to ensure maintenance and sustainability of the works.
- Rehabilitate/construct and maintain WASH infrastructure
-In addition to this innovative technique of manual drilling wells will be introduced and local technicians will be trained and given material and tools to support and start sustainable profitable businesses.

Activity 4: Emergency latrine construction and latrine desludging for communities & IDPs respecting the one latrine per 50 people (8 families) and when possible one latrine per 30 people as per (WASH Cluster standard). (1,030 men, 2,200 Women, 1,300 girls and 1,000 boys).Activity 5: Establish Water Management Committees (WMCs) at community and IDP level to manage WASH facilities and conduct hygiene promotion. Please provide further explanation

The Project aims at promoting durable solution/resilience of displacement affected and host communities. This objective will be achieved by ensuring that the targeted communities have access to clean water and to sanitation facilities. In order to achieve this result, the project will be integrated with other ongoing actions in the areas of intervention to promote access to basic services for the targeted communities such as schools and hospitals in order to build their resilience and to promote long term solutions to displacement. The proposed Action is linked to the second IASC criteria on durable solutions to guarantee an adequate standard of living. The targeted population group will be the IDP community, as well as returnees and vulnerable host community members in areas affected by displacement. INTERSOS will transfer responsibilities to the relevant local authorities/community members during the project implementation through enhanced coordination and engagement with the government and with the affected population. INTERSOS is optimistic that as the government grows stronger, its institutions will be able to absorb increasing responsibility for the delivery of humanitarian services. Addition to this the project introduces innovative solutions to sustained access to water through manual drilling of wells with PVC pumps. This is a new technique that can be very effective where river bed is perceived to have high yield of water and lower water table are drilled using hand drilling machine. The drilled shallow wells will be equipped with pvc pumps to extract and discharge water to the consumers. This simple technique has the potential to ensure a great impact despite the limited available resources by transferring to the community to know how thus promoting durable solutions. INTERSOS recognizes that the test of a truly successful initiative is how well and long its positive impacts are sustained following the exit of the intervention. Our presence will also ensure continuation and support of the already implemented initiative. A key component of this project is capacity building of communities and community groups. The knowledge and skills that they will be equipped with will remain as assets in the community beyond the project period. Some of the sustainability measures incorporated in the design of this project include; Establishment and training of WASH committees and Water service board members on operation and maintenance of water point will ensure the sustainable management of the water points, particularly the large scale water scheme and avoid over use of water points which is common in Somalia. Involvement of community members at all levels of project implementation will promote ownership and thus contribute to sustainability. From INTERSOS’s experience, community members are willing to sustain interventions that they were involved in from the onset. Where relevant, particularly in host communities, beneficiaries will sign agreements on community ownership, management and maintenance. Based on the agreements, relevant beneficiaries will have their knowledge, skills and capacities enhanced and strengthened. The water service provision Board will also sign contract AGREEMENT/ MOU to manage and maintain the Borehole and the water supply network in the town.
