Project Summary

This activity aims to improve the living standards of people affected by displacement in Cabo Delgado by providing emergency shelters including household NFI kits and construction tools.
In addition to the emergency shelter items, this project supports the shelter improvements, early recovery shelter assistance and/or upgrades mainly in the returnee areas.
This project targets both displaced affected populations and recently returned populations with priority to the most vulnerable and in need, including those living in host community and IDP sites.
The disaggregation by sex/gender, is contemplated in the project as part of the selection criteria to select the most vulnerable groups, such as children head of household, women head of household, women lactating, etc.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

For this project, the shelter's key activities include the provision of Shelter NFI Kits for shelter type B or improving the existing ones depending on their conditions using Cash modality whenever feasible or in kind modality in the context where the cash modality is not adequate.As NRC has experience with Cash modality in Mozambique and prioritizes flexible approaches for shelter assistance, the Cash modality will be the preferable modality. Only in contexts where the cash modality is not applicable, NRC will use Voucher or in-kind modality to assist.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

NRC will implement this humanitarian project through and with the affected community using community-based approaches and safe and inclusive programming. NRC integrated protection-specific questions in each assessment and selection criteria to ensure prioritization of safety, and dignity, and avoid doing harm throughout shelter activities. The safety needs of men, women, boys, and girls will be identified through assessments prior to shelter interventions. In alignment with SPHERE standards, the privacy and safety needs of women and girls will be particularly important in the design of space usage to reduce any protection risks. Some of the highest protection risks reported in camp settings include lack of privacy, risk of intruders, and sexual violence, due to lack of lighting at night. NRC teams will engage in consultations through relationships with community leaders, FGDs, and representatives of different ages, gender, ethnic, and marginalized groups to ensure intentional safe, and inclusive approaches to prevent and reduce exposure to protection risks and promote safety and dignity. Through community engagement, NRC will identify approaches that do no harm and promote gender equality, ensuring the inclusion of women in decision-making, community consultations, and design. The GBV risks will be mitigated throughout programming and informed responses will be identified in consultation with communities through FGDs and ongoing staff engagement to monitor any potential harm or threats.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

NRC will implement this humanitarian project through and with the affected community using community-based approaches and safe and inclusive programming. NRC integrates protection-specific questions in each assessment and selection criteria to ensure prioritization of safety, and dignity, and to avoid causing harm throughout project activities. Programming is designed with specific attention to individuals with particular vulnerabilities who may face barriers to accessing assistanceTo ensure accountability to the affected people and create community acceptance prior to the implementation of the project, NRC will conduct community engagements to present the project to the communities,.This includes discussing the design of shelters and planning distributions of NFIs to identify accessible locations and times for distributions that accommodate all individuals in a safe and dignified manner. In addition to the community engagements, the activities will be monitored by project teams to proactively ensure meaningful access and through post-intervention monitoring to assess potential gaps or community feedback to inform implementation.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Through FGDs and assessments, NRC will identify and include the PwD in the decision-making and ensure their protection and inclusion in all shelter and NFI-related interventions. During distributions, People with Special Needs /PwD’s committees will be included or ad-hoc focus groups will be consulted for the revision of shelter designs, adapting sectorial shelter designs to their specific needs. This may include, among others, shelter access adaptation, privacy screens, kitchen unit dimensioning, wheel-chair drainage friendly design, lighting, ramps, handrails, etc... to be determined based on PwD’s community consultation. To reach out to a representative sample of PwDs who might be unable to attend consultation sessions in the community, the NRC team will take care to include household visits at their dwellings during assessment, planning, implementation, and M&E activities.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Due to the nature of the proposed activities under this sub-sector, the localized environmental impact is relatively low. However, NRC prioritizes the use of Cash to give flexibility in best respond their unique needs including usage of few conventional material for the roofing, and walls such as mat mixed with Portland cement. This approach reduces the usage of items such as plastic tarpaulins. Shelter materials such as bamboo and wood will be procured locally through authorized suppliers by local authorities, who have been interested and insisting on the use of sustainable and controlled sources, mitigating the environmental impact. Other mitigation measures have already been taken by local authorities and humanitarian partners, such as the limited/reduced number of families per site or the use of efficient cooking stoves, in order to reduce the environmental impact in the surroundings of each reallocation site.
