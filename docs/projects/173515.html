Project Summary

"Safollomoy Samaj Kollyan Unnayan Sangstha" aims to reach 3200 Rohingya and 150 host children (3-17) with early childhood development and learning opportunities by maintaining the operation of existing 49 learning centers. It will upgrade these LCs aimed at contributing to increasing access to safe and inclusive space for the refugee and host population in case of disaster. The learners will grab provisions of quality learning teaching to excel their latent talents, indoor games, recreation, awareness sessions, different participatory events, psychosocial counselling and so forth.
The project is gender sensitive and address the age group 3-17. The total targeted beneficiaries are 3350 where at least 50% are girls. Having the consideration of gender issue, gender segregated wash facilities has been included. The awareness sessions for caregivers where 50% women and 50% men would be included.
This project will involve the affected community in different phases of the implementation specially in delivering assistances. Having different assistances like dignity kits, nutrients and hygiene kits the children and adolescent girls and boys will influence their whole community. Besides, the caregivers will be involved in our awareness sessions where they influence the whole community.
Some of basic training and other capacity building activities in the project will ensure the quality of teaching. The Project also emphasis on monitoring through continuous data collection and field visit to ensure the delivery of quality education and logistics support. Safollomoy will be concern and ensure the preparedness regarding any natural calamities in accordance with contingency plan.
This project bridges with child protection, GBB and food security (HEB) sectors so that it will require inter sectoral communication and participation during the project implementation.
All these operations require coordination with education sector that will be ensured through participating different trainings conducted by sector, 5w reports and any other help regarding education enhancement.

1 - Identified Needs

"Children have a right to learn and a right to an education that will support their development, growth, and ability to build their future. Education is a basic and universal human right, which should be enjoyed by all children, without distinction or discrimination though they affect by natural or manmade disasters. Indeed, every child deserves a quality education, which includes a gender equality dimension. Such education, focusing on human rights, will allow fighting against inequalities, injustice and oppressions, which are deeply rooted and enshrined in most societies especially the FDMN community. The existing needs in FDMN community has been traced out which are following.The key challenges found by parents accessing to education include distance from learning facilities and protection for children aged 3-5; restrictions for children aged 6-17 due to the responsibilities in the household; lack of sufficient education kits and insufficient WASH facilities. In addition to a lack of female bathing facilities that leads to a lack of basic personal hygiene. Rohingya adolescent girls are less likely to attend learning centers due to safety issues for adolescent girls' inside the camps. To avoid dropout of age group 3-17 its highly needed to feed snacks or provide nutrients support including hygiene and dignity kits.Due to restriction of permanent or semi-permanent structure, temporary constructions are made for LCs with bamboos and tarpaulins, most LCs are already in need of renovation and this is predicted to incur significantly during the recurring monsoon and cyclone season (April-October). Land slide is a key issue severely hampering the response, and requires innovative approaches like double stories facilities, and home-based facilities to meet the vast education needs.While students have a structured curriculum, learning outcomes are unlikely to change without the availability of skilled and well-trained teachers, education kits support, and the development of a social and emotional learning atmosphere. In the Rohingya camps, hiring adequately trained facilitators with a proper understanding of child development remains a challenge. The situation is further complicated by the low levels of teacher skills, most of which are under-qualified. Rohingya adolescent girls (aged 15-18 years) only 3% were not used to go to schools before COVID 19 outbreak due to the lack of concern guardians, qualified female teachers and insecure routes to school (J-MSNA RR July-August 2020). There is a need to strengthen engagement with parents, caregivers and communities in order to motivate them to send the adolescent girls for education. There is also a need of providing additional classes for adolescent girls in cross-sectoral facilities (such as girls friendly spaces) in order to support girls' access to learning in line with social-cultural norms of segregation for unmarried girls after puberty. Children with one or no caregivers live in vulnerable, overcrowded spaces face risks related to SGBV and prostitution trafficking, particularly for adolescent girls. Adolescent girls are especially vulnerable to early or forced marriage and sexual exploitation, while boys face increased risks of child labor, abuse and trafficking of human beings. Teenage boys out of school are more likely to be involved in anti-social activities, extremism, bullying, sexual harassment, and so on. It has been estimated that after the COVID-19 situation 12% adolescent boys and girls will not be willing to go to schools because they are needed at home to help their family where 10% girls won’t attend schools for early marriage (J-MSNA RR July-August 2020). In order to achieve high enrollment and attendance rates in education facilities, sessions with parents, caregivers and Learning Center Management Committee (LCMC) are needed to engage the communities in promoting education.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: The continuity of education in protective environments through existing 49 LCs.Activity 2: Establish WASH facilities, Hand washing facilities, provide PPE for COVID-19 hygienic kits like soap, mask, sanitizer etc.Activity 3: Provide equitable and inclusive education for host and Rohingya community girls and boys aged 3-17 years old, aligned with Education Sector standards, and increase teaching related professional development opportunities.Activity 4: Learning facilitators and other education personnel in camps will receive periodic, relevant and structured training, professional development and support according to needs and circumstances. Activity 5: Safollomoy will provide indoor games, sports and all possible materials. G&B (3-17) in camps and (4-17) host community will receive adequate education materials, supplies and equipment aligned with Edu Sector standards. Activity 6: Recreational activities will be ensured to Rohingya and Host children and they will participate in music, dance, drama, drawings, sports, and cultural shows. Activity 7: Awareness sessions conduct regularly on health, recreation, learning by doing, Sexual and Reproductive Health, abuse, exploitation etc. to children especially for Girls. Activity 8: Conduct teacher competency assessment and develop teachers’ competencies. Provide level-specific teaching material.Activity 9: Safollomoy will arrange guardian meeting in every learning center on bi-monthly basis for ensuring community participation, raise awareness on education, personal health and hygiene, gender based violence etc. issues; Activity 10: Training and discussion session will arrange with the Members of LCMCs in camps on learning facility management, DRM and participatory community engagement. Activity 11: Organize forums and committee to ensure, participation, ownership, and the improvement of practices on girl’s education, child protection, care giving, life skills, disabilities, social cohesion, DRR. Activity 12: Engaging parents and community stakeholders in ‘Participatory Monitoring’ to improve LC’s teaching-learning environment.Activity 13: Provision training programs with Monitoring and Evaluation Officer for strengthen monitoring system.
