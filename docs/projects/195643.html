Project Summary

South Sudan is one of the poorest countries in the World. More than one third of the population lacks secure access to food, clean water and poor infrastructures such as roads, electricity, health centers and schools etc. South Sudan is facing one of the worst food security and nutrition crises globally. Almost 63 percent of the population is likely to be in acute food insecurity, of whom 87 000 people facing extreme hunger with no or limited coping mechanisms. Such high numbers have not been seen in years in South Sudan. Urgent and sustained multisectoral humanitarian support must be provided to save lives, prevent famine and re-establish livelihoods in time for the next harvest season.
An anticipated fourth consecutive year of extensive flooding, coupled with incidents of conflict and insecurity, and macroeconomic challenges, is likely to further increase already very high levels of food insecurity in South Sudan, as the lean season peaks. This includes the continued presence of people in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5).
Under this project, SSGID plans to offer support to resumption/recovery of crops and fisheries production for sections of farming populations engaged in crop and livestock activities in Twic, Tonj East and Gogrial West counties. The project aims to increase production of food; nutritious vegetables and fish rich protein sources in the locations during the dry season and expand the livelihood activity options among the vulnerable, increase dietary diversity for the Agro pastoralists and fisheries. The project will upscale seed production and provide support to seed fairs using locally sourced seed; seed security & multiplication strengthening/ Support market functionality e.g. assist traders to re-supply markets/ local procurement & aggregation of produce for WFP assistance. Farmer groups will be formed and trained on crop and vegetable farming; traders trained on seed-voucher system. This project will benefit the vulnerable households in most vulnerable; Child-headed, Elderly and Female-headed households will account for the 40 .5% of the households under IPC 3 and 4 suffering under food insecurities and malnutrition. 47947 through receiving food, non-food items and vouchers. 47,947 people will be reached through distribution of farming tools and fishing kits and guided to start and upscale production of food nutritious vegetables and fish. 96000 livestock are planned for treatment and vaccination; particularly in Twic , Tonj East and Gogrial West counties. Overall, 47,947 people within the communities will be capacitated to ensure they are strengthened to sustain activities beyond this project. To ensure accountability to affected persons, a project committee (AAP) will be formed through which the affected persons will participate all the stages of the intervention (planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation). A complaint and feedback mechanism (CFM) will be established and communicated to all beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries for receiving and providing feedback. The CFM will be safe, accessible and developed in a way that takes into account vulnerabilities of each represented group: men, women, boy and girls. Strong focus will be made to ensure that the most sensitive groups (e.g. persons with disabilities, pregnant and lactating women, and unaccompanied elders/minors) in the community will be served appropriately to their needs and in the manner not exposing them to additional stress. To ensure individuals living with disability do not miss on assistance, 14% of all beneficiaries will be individuals living with disability.
To integrate the response, SSGID will establish demonstration gardens across nutrition sites, participate in joint training on sources and benefits of balanced diets. Health and WASH partners will be involved

1 - Needs

The 2022 ICP, 740,000 people in Warrap are estimated to be in IPC phase3 in October to November 2022, (57.1% of the population) are facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 413,000 people are in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity and 327,000 people are in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. During this period, all counties are classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. The main drivers of food insecurity in Warrap State are conflict and insecurity, climatic shocks in the form of floods and dry spells, high food prices, animal disease outbreaks, crop pests and diseases, and reduced income at household level.During the post-harvest period of December 2022 to March 2023, the food security situation is expected to deteriorate slightly, with an estimated 746,000 people (57.6% of the population) likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. Of these, 413,000 people will likely be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and 333,000 people will likely be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). During this period, all counties are classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. The food security situation is expected to deteriorate further during the lean season from April to July 2023, with an estimated 885,000 people (68.2% of the population) expected to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. Of these, 472,000 people will likely be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, and 413,000 people will likely be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. During this period, all counties are classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. The deterioration of the food security situation is attributed to the seasonal depletion of food stocks, likelihood of continued high food prices, reduced market functionality and access challenges caused by insecurity and the worsening of road conditions due to rainfall, and disruption of livelihoods and displacement caused by insecurity. According to the current 2022 IPC report, Gogrial West is projected to have 98,000 people food stressed, 98,000 in crisis, 65,000 in emergency in December to March 2023 and from April- July 65,000 will be food stressed, 131,000 in crisis and 82,000 under emergency. Tonj East 55,000 will be under emergency, 74,000 crisis, 37,000 food stressed by July 2023 and Twic county will be most hit, 108,000people will be in emergency, 96,000 crisis and 41,000 people will be food stressed by July 2023.Based on these numbers, a robust intervention in needed and SSGID propose FSL intervention is lifesaving and will seek to increased food production and enhance access to livelihood opportunities for Food Insecure families in the targeted counties. The interventions will be based on rapid FSL needs assessments preceding any particular response. This will allow FSL teams to respond to the specific needs of the given displaced community, being fully accountable for the identification of the beneficiaries and of their needs according to the FSL cluster strategy. During all interventions, SSGID FSL and Monitoring and Evaluation teams on the ground will collect information from the beneficiaries regarding their needs to be used in the ongoing response, as well as for future reference. Other data sources to be referred during the intervention shall include REACH surveys, IOM DTM, IPC data, IRNAs, FEWSNET, C.F.S.M.A and SMART SURVEYS that already show significant Food Security needs within the affected communities in Tonj East, Twic and Gogrial West. SSGID has been in the three counties for more than 5 years implementing protection, livelihood and resilience projects. The Organization has offices, staff, assets and community networks it will rely on to support the proposed project.

2 - Project activities

1. Distribute FSL kits to 47,947 people or 7,991 households during the lean season. This will improve the physical and mental well-being and reduce morbidity, mortality & suffering (HRP SO1 and FSL CO1). 2. Distribute seeds and tools to 7,991 households (47,947 people) in order to boost crop production and address dietary diversity - the major underlying cause of malnutrition - with vegetable production. SSGID will deliver practical training on basic agronomic practices, agribusiness and post-harvest handling to build capacity and resilience that will in turn enable communities recover and absorb shocks and establish kitchen gardens at sites near boreholes and other water points (swamps, along river beds) to encourage vegetable production during the dry season. 3. Livestock vaccination; disease prevention & treatment especially zoonotic e.g. RVF planned for 96000. 4. Support to seed fairs using locally sourced seed; seed security & multiplication strengthening/ Support market functionality e.g. assist traders to re-supply markets. 5. Training & capacity building: Both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production (farmer field schools, demonstration plots), income generation (business skills), vocational training etc. 4,133 people and groups planned. SSGID will integrate Health/Nutrition activities through screening and referral of malnourished children and lactating and pregnant mothers to receive packages of farming/or fishing kits. 6: Formation and training of Crop and Vegetable Groups (Trainer of Trainers-TOTs): Twenty (20) Kitchen gardens will be formed and trained in the three counties. Training will focus on how to establish gardens and seed beds , land management, planting methods, seed bed management, mulching, soil and water conservation, integrated pest management, harvesting and harvest preservation. 7: Formation and training of Fisher Fork Groups (TOTs): Three (3) fisher fork Groups comprising of 30 (15 Females) members will be established and trained on net making, fishing techniques including how to protect small fish in rivers and ponds, hygienic handling, transportation and storage, packaging of fish, catch loss reduction, and fish preservation (smoking, salting and drying). 8: Conduct Post Distribution Monitoring: Two PDMs (one for vegetable and crop kits and the other for fishing kits) will be conducted through random sampling. PDMs will target 20% of beneficiaries reached. 9: Conduct Joint field visits and stakeholders forum: SSGID will conduct joint field visits together with local authorities, officials from MAF, Community leaders, beneficiary’s representatives to monitor project activities and gather views and perceptions of beneficiaries on the FSL project. Stakeholder’s forum will also be conducted to review the progress made on the FSL project. 10: Conduct post planting and post-harvest surveys to obtain information on level of preparation by farmers before planting and utilization of the FSL kits distributed. Post-harvest survey will seek to determine the yield of the crops and management of harvests by farmers and fisher forks.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

According to the food security and nutrition monitoring system (FSNMS) survey Oct 2022 7.76M people are projected to to be food severiorly food food insecure in April- July 2023 and 271,000 in Twic County, 184,000 people in Tonj east and 327,000 people in Gogrial west. In light of this, SSGID will take specific measures to ensure protection and inclusion of disabled persons in the intervention. For example, 15% of all beneficiaries will be reserved for people living with disability. Also, 15 % of AAPs will be disabled persons. Meetings, Focus Group discussions will be conducted with this category of vulnerable people to ensure their needs are captured and met. 15% beneficiaries participating in PDMs will be disabled persons to ensure their feedback on level of assistance, quality of implementation and satisfaction is obtained. Also, enumerators and staff hired in the project will be disabled persons. SSGID is a member of referral pathways in Twic, Gogrial West and Tonj East. As such, the organization will make sure that cases exhibiting protection and health concerns are referred to service providers. All project activities (demonstration gardens, distribution points, meeting points and training venues ) will be conducted in locations where disabled persons can easily access.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Information on feedback and complaint mechanism will be provided during inception meetings with community leaders, beneficiary’s representatives, local authorities, and other relevant stakeholders. All trainees including PIC and local enumerators will also be provided with information on CFM. Further, posters bearing a toll-free number and banners will be displayed prominently at registration and selection of beneficiaries’ sites, distribution points, during meetings and training. Further, public announcement will be made by gender protection Officers and project staff on avenues beneficiaries and non- beneficiaries can use to submit complaints and receive feedback.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

SSGID will deploy three gender protection officers who will be responsible for gender mainstreaming during implementation. The gender officers have received training on PSEA and are well versed with GBV sub-cluster standard operation procedures while dealing with GBV cases. The GBV officers will be responsible for conducting GBV awareness and communication during project activities. They will also conduct training to project implementation committee and locally hired enumerators on PSEA mitigation, prevention, and reporting. SSGID is a member of GBV referral and will be able to refer any cases of PSEA to relevant referral pathway in order for survivors to access services. All new SSGID staffs, locally hired enumerators and project implementation committee members will receive training on organisation’s PSEA policy. They will then be required to sign the policy prior to be included as part of implementation team. SSGID PSEA policy clearly stipulates that the organisation has zero tolerance to PSEA. The policy is clear on steps to be taken if any staff or individuals working for and with SSGID is found to have contravened the policy.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

SSGID interventions are right based and informed by need to ensure dignity, privacy and comfort of people in need when accessing humanitarian assistance. As such SSGID will embed accessible, effective, and safe feedback and complaint mechanism throughout the implementation process. During registration and selection of beneficiaries, SSGID will avail feedback and complaint desk in every beneficiaries’ selection and distribution sites. SSGID will also provide lockable suggestion boxes where complaints can be submitted.Desks and suggestion desks will be manned by a trained enumerator who will ensure all complaints received are submitted to SSGID protection and Safeguarding officer, who in turn will feed the complaints to a complaint and feedback file for redress. Also, SSGID will provide and display toll free phone numbers which beneficiaries can use to submit their complains and receive feedback. All received complaints must be addressed within two weeks with feedback provided in safe and protective way to ensure security and privacy of complainants.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The first working assumption of the project is that the political and security situation remains conducive to the project. The associated risk is that the security situation may deteriorate in a way that the continuation of the project is no longer possible. To mitigate against this likelihood, SSGID will sensitize key stakeholders and capacitate them in a way that enhances the likelihood that the protection of vulnerable groups will be improved, even in the absence of the project. There may also be a situation of difficulty for women to participate in the intervention. Their active participation in the social protection of vulnerable children and youth may be undermined and effectively undercut. SSGID will engage in community outreach to explain the project’s objectives and importance of engaging women and girls. A third assumption is that there may be issues with legitimacy and functionality of central government in parts of the targeted Counties. Direct assistance at local levels including, if viable, working with local authorities may be impeded. The project will not address issues which require changes in policies and legal frameworks. In this early recovery and transitional context, SSGID’s life-saving intervention will use food assistance, fish and crop production and livestock treatment and vaccination to assist in the restoration of food security systems, to encourage the return of internally displaced persons (IDPs) and refugees, and to promote social cohesion among communities. The project will focus primarily on IDPs and their hosts to encourage adaptation and integration. All project locations remain safe for carrying out project activities.
