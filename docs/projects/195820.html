Project Summary

DRC is proposing to provide multipurpose cash assistance (MPCA) to vulnerable individuals of five family average size in the targeted areas in (Rural Damascus, Daraa) to cover their basic needs.
According to the HNO 2022,The economic deterioration has financially squeezed families further. Based on the HNO of Syria 2022, 76% of assessed households in Syria report a significant deterioration in their ability to meet basic needs since mid if 2021, mainly due to price increases and a loss of income.
Only WFP, UNHCR, and UNRWA are providing CVA in most of the targeted areas and the accessibility to functioning markets and FSPs make the MPCA the most suitable intervention to address the basic needs of vulnerable HHs. The targeted beneficiaries will be selected in coordination with the main NGOs working in the area as well as the local authorities among the most vulnerable and underserved populations. The target will be calculated based on the number of families in the waiting lists of other UN agencies such as WFP, local NGOs and MoSAL .The intervention will align with the capacity of the local markets and FSP, the project implementation timeframe, and the budget availability.
Based on the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) figure provided by the Cash Working Group(CWG) and harmonization efforts made with INGOs, and as per the feedback of MoSAL's Director of Planning and International Cooperation, DRC will provide cash assistance in 3 rounds transferred per House H during the length of the response ( 630,000 SYP every Month during the implementation period). DRC will aim to maintain harmonization with the agreed CWG rate.
The project will be implemented directly by DRC in selected areas of implementation, where has been present for 10 years and is already carrying out economic recovery interventions. This will allow DRC to target particularly HHs with compounding vulnerabilities, including those facing protection concerns and those with members who are socially and economically marginalized and in need of assistance, such as women-headed households, people with disabilities, and elderlies. The beneficiary selection will be coordinated with WFP, who is providing food assistance to the most vulnerable HHs in the area. WFP will share their beneficiary lists with DRC to avoid overlapping and to provide a coordinated response in the target area.
DRC received written approval from MoSAL to implement cash-based interventions (CBI) on April 21st, 2020. Since then, DRC has developed Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs), beneficiary selection tools, and guidelines conducted a monthly risk analysis, and provided training for national staff.
DRC has signed a framework agreement with a financial service provider (FSP) and held several coordination meetings to confirm their capacity to absorb the caseload and their commitment to follow COVID-19 prevention measures and humanitarian principles

3 - Needs

The economic deterioration has financially squeezed families further. Based on the HNO of Syria 2023, The assessments and analyses carried out across the country continue to show a general deterioration in access to livelihoods, ability to meet basic needs due to gap between incomes and expenditures on essential needs, availability and affordability of infrastructure and services, threatening the resilience and social cohesion of households. In Daraa 1,546,169 individuals are in need for ERL and food security Assistance, while in Rural Damascus 4,861,843 are in need of the same assistance.Inflationary trends on key goods and services, widespread low productivity of inputs, low wages, and lack of employment, among other factors, continue to impact households’ priority needs and exacerbate the income-expenditure gap across the country.Families are increasingly adopting harmful coping mechanisms such as dept, emergency’ food-related coping strategies, child labor, selling property, etc. The average price of the standard reference food basket is increasing on monthly basis. According to Sept Market monitoring exercise, The average price of the standard reference food basket in the monitored governorates in September 2022 is 632,000 SYP, which is a 1.9% increase compared to the previous month and above the average of the last three months by 3.8%.Food security continues to worsen, aggravated by decreased crop production, and economic and climatic shocks.

4 - Implementation of activities

- Selection of MPCA beneficiaries: DRC will conduct a beneficiary identifications process. The process will include coordination with line ministries(MoSAL), UN agencies operating in the same areas, local initiatives and beneficiaries’ self-referrals to DRC community centers and DRC hotline. Following collecting and checking the potential beneficiaries list, DRC will start the registration process using the DRC vulnerability assessment tool.- Provision of MPCA to the selected beneficiaries: Following the selection of beneficiaries, DRC uses a local FSP for the delivery of cash to a beneficiary. The FSP branches are well distributed in the targeted areas, also. DRC will ensure that the same process is applied and beneficiaries have access to FSPs in their locations. The distribution process takes around one week per location/city.- Market Monitoring: will be conducted by DRC staff, using the tool Caritas has put in place if permission from the local authorities is granted, on a monthly basis and will allow measuring price inflation and availability of basic goods.DRC will monitor the distribution through conducting post-distribution monitoring of HHs who received the MPC and complementary focused group discussions with non-beneficiaries, where possible.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

DRC will conduct on monthly basis a risk analysis updated and followed up with the participation of the Economic Recovery and Protection teams as well as safety, supply chain, and finance departments under the leadership of the Cash specialist. Mitigation measures are put in place and continuously monitored.R1: The Safety related situation is volatile and could hinder the implementation of the project by reducing or limiting movement for beneficiaries and consequently their access to shops .Likely hood: High, Impact: High, Mitigation: DRC to closely monitor the situation with the safety team and to consider different options such as house-to-house distribution in some cases or switching to in-kind distributionR2: Limited access to beneficiaries by DRC staff because of Covid-19 restrictions or Safety situation. Likelihood: Low, Impact: medium, Mitigation: to conduct some activities remotely, including registration of beneficiaries, baseline-endline, and PDMs.R3: Increase of social tension because of the uneven distribution of assistance litigation measures. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium, Mitigation: DRC to inform clearly about the beneficiary selection criteria to all applicants and community leaders and to coordinate with other local and international NGOs in the area for referrals.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

1. Sexual exploitation and abuseDRC is a protection agency and protection will be mainstreamed throughout the project. The implementing team will work closely with the protection team for referrals and identification of protection risks. Protection staff will participate in the beneficiary selection and will be present during the cash distribution process at the FSP offices for monitoring and support. 2. sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV)For the beneficiary selection, victims of GBV and applicants exposed to protection risks, such as unaccompanied minors and female-headed households will be prioritized, through case management and psychosocial services. MPCA has been shown to contribute to the prevention of and response to GBV, including intimate partner violence.3. Gender InequalityDRC integrates age, gender, and diversity (ADG) considerations in its organizational setup and in all project phases from needs analysis up to implementation, monitoring, and follow-up. Gender components are mainstreamed into all project assessments and evaluations, and data is gender and age disaggregated. DRC also aims to ensure a balanced representation of women, men, and other marginalized groups among those benefitting from its services and strives to emphasize to community representatives and other project stakeholders the importance of this approach during beneficiary selection and consultation, for example during CL and CFP activities.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions• The security conditions as well as the economic situation do not further deteriorate and remain stable • Beneficiaries have access to the markets and the Hawala offices throughout the duration of the project• Markets keep working normally and essential goods are available• Access for DRC´s staff is secured throughout the duration of the project• Initial approval to DRC by MoSAl to implement Cash-Based Interventions is maintained.Risks• High inflation could reduce the impact of the standard monthly assistance on beneficiaries • Substantial fluctuation in the exchange rate would affect the amounts available for cash transfers• Mismanagement of cash assistance by beneficiaries Mitigation:• To conduct monthly market monitoring to assess the availability and cost of basic products and to coordinate with CWG members for an increase in the transfer value if needed.• To follow-up on the exchange rate fluctuation and to coordinate with donor and CWG members to provide a harmonized response.• To identify potential cases of mismanagement through the PDM and feedback and complaint response mechanisms.
