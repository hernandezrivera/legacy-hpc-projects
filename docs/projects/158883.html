Project Summary

The aim of the project is to support 4 communities in Ramallah and East Jerusalem governorates identified by the WASH Cluster by providing them with new water network extensions, rehabilitating current water networks, and installing transmission pipelines and new water reservoirs.

1 - Needs

West Bank and Gaza is a very water-scarce lower-middle-income territory with a relatively water-dependent economy and is vulnerable within its geopolitical setting. Assuring water security is thus a priority. Water security requires adequate water resources that are well managed, including management of risks, and water supply services that provide sustainable, efficient, and equitable. The geopolitical context places severe constraints on the water security of the Palestinian territories. Political economy factors have constrained the ability of the Palestinian Water Authority (PWA) to improve service delivery. These constraints affect the provision of services both in accessing adequate bulk water supplies and in investing in and implementing projects for developing water supply and sanitation infrastructure. In West Bank, access to piped water on premises does not translate into quality services. Consider the measure of “access when needed,” defined as “not available for at least one full day during the previous two weeks.” By this measure, access to improved water in the West Bank drops from 93 percent to 80 percent, with the biggest decreases in the governorates of Jenin and Nablus, as well as Ramallah and Al-Bireh governorates, where service is frequently intermittent. Sanitation coverage is high, but the connection to sewerage networks is much higher in Gaza than in West Bank. In the West Bank, despite near-universal access to improved sanitation (94 percent), access to sewage connections is only 30 percent, with rates varying widely by governorate, from zero (in Tubas) to 59 percent (in Qalqilya). Access also varies strongly by household income: only 13 percent in the poorest quintile are connected to sewer networks, compared to 42 percent in the richest quintile. Only 36 percent of the population of West Bank and 22 percent of the population in Gaza receive a continuous supply of water every day of the month. Despite this high intermittency, nearly 50 percent of the population in West Bank report no problem with their service – indicating that they have settled for low standards due to developing different coping strategies. Inefficiencies in the distribution of water through service providers add to the challenges with bulk water supply. Physical losses, theft, and unbilled water supply are very high. Overall, service providers receive revenues on only about 70 percent of the water they supply; the rest is so-called non-revenue water (NRW). While a rate of 30 percent for non-revenue water may be sustainable in a water-rich country, it is not in West Bank. These levels of non-revenue water are a dramatic loss for a very water-scarce nation. According to the rapid assessment conducted in cooperation with the WASH Cluster and PWA WestBank water department, it has come to an agreement on what locations and what activities need to be implemented while ensuring the adherence to the National Water Sector Plan. The situation of the targeted locations is deteriorating; rustic old networks, high losses and spills and a shortage of water, which sometimes leads to water pollution. There have been many efforts to do the needed repairs. yet, they are not being implemented properly. The suggested interventions will protect human health, minimize water losses, which will directly support the increase in water availability and decrease the cost of purchasing.

2 - Activities

Activity 1. Rehabilitation and construction of new households’ domestic water supply networks and installations of household connections. Global Communities with the support of the WASH Cluster, Municipality of Ramallah, Jerusalem Water Undertaking and PWA will construct and rehabilitate domestic water networks, install main trunk pipeline and installations of new households water connections as follow: • Connect targeted households at Khelat Al-Qadi - Ramallah by installing household connections and sub water distribution networks through pipes diameter of "1" inch and a total length of 2500m. • Construction and rehabilitation of domestic water networks with pipes diameters of "1", "2", and "3" inch with a total length of 4000m at Umm AL Safa - Ramallah. • Construction and installation of domestic water networks of “2" inch diameter of a length of 3000m and “4" inchdiameter of a length of 3500m at Sur Bahr (Al-Muntar، Wadi Al-Hummus ،Dier Al-Amoud communities) in Jerusalem. It should be noted that comprehensive field assessments, engineering designs, and tender documents will be prepared by Global communities and its Partner PHG in close consultation with the Municipality of Ramallah, Jerusalem Water Undertaking and PWA along with the engagement of the local communities. Further, when implementing new water networks and rehabilitating others, field coordination with other actors working in those communities will be ensured to avoid any duplication. Activity 2: Construction of a water reservoir with a capacity of 500 m3 in Qbia. This activity will benefit the targeted communities by the WASH Cluster through the construction of a water reservoir in Qbialocated in the Ramallah governorate. Meanwhile, Global communities in coordination with PHG, WASH cluster, PWA, Municipality of Ramallah and Jerusalem Water Undertaking will conduct a technical assessment and hire a technical consulting firm to design and prepare tender documents for the reservoir and associated piping work. Those activities (1 and 2) will contribute to the well-being of women and girls in the families as they bear most of the sanitation and hygiene responsibilities, where such burden increased due to lack of access to water. Hence, this intervention will support better water access to different age and gender groups. Activity 3: Community mobilization and hygiene promotion for practice change. This activity is designed to target women, children, and men at the targeted communities. The targeted beneficiaries will be provided with hygiene promotion activities using various approaches based on the communities' assessments. This will include behavior change approach and communication for Development (C4D) approach. The households will be targeted with 2 home visits to deliver the main key messages (safe water handling and storage and good personal hygiene practices) in order to influence good WASH practices. Specialized IEC (Information, Education, and Communication) material will be adopted – including responsibility calendars, informative stickers and follow up sheets. Men will be encouraged to take on roles in safe water handling, safe drainage of wastewater and protecting the hygiene of their community in order to reduce the burden on women and girls.

3 - Indicators

Global Communities has extensive experience in monitoring WASH programming activities. The Monitoring, Evaluation, and Learning (MEL) Plan outlines the anticipated results and the tools and mechanisms through which Global Communities and implementing partners will monitor and evaluate progress over the life of the program. This illustrative plan will provide information about the overall approach to performance monitoring, a description of the proposed methods for collecting, analyzing, and reporting high-quality program data. Global Communities’ proposed plan will ensure that gender and youth are fully integrated into the MEL system. Further, an illustrative learning and adaptive management plan will be put in place to promote stakeholder collaboration and capitalize on best practices and lessons learned. Monthly written reports will be submitted by the partner to the Global Communities will be involved in developing both the interim and final reports to the donor. In order to assess the appropriateness and effectiveness interventions in line with the overarching goal, Global Communities has designed a holistic MEL approach that employs rigorous quantitative and qualitative methods to inform an array of performance indicators that include: index/composite indicators, indirect/proxy indicators, complexity-aware monitoring, standard foreign assistance indicators, as well as several custom indicators to monitor the program’s success in designing and implementing an inclusive approach to achieving sustainable WASH outcomes. Regular field visits and on-going monitoring will be conducted during the implementation of activities and will be verified through PHG progress reports, in addition to partner's daily and weekly reports. In regards to the supplies, they will be purchased directly by Global Communities based on the list reviewed and shared by WASH Cluster. The installation work will be carried out by contractors that will be supervised by Global Communities and its partner site engineers on a daily basis in the field. Further, Global Communities will monitor the project activities on a regular basis to ensure that the needed equipment and materials are delivered as planned. In addition. Global Communities will closely coordinate with the Wash Cluster and other stakeholders to ensure no overlapping occurs and enhance coordination with other relevant agencies working on similar interventions. 8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

People have a right to have their voices heard in judging our response to their emergency. Asking for the views of the affected population can help us understand the difference we are making during the course of the response, and not just at the end of a project. Complaints will be processed by our MEAL Officer who has suitable qualities and is trained and supported to deal with them. They should be confident in directing complaints through the right channels and judging the sensitivity of complaints. Even less serious complaints demand greater or lesser degrees of sensitivity or a more objective view. A bi-monthly meeting will bring key staff together. Regular progress reporting and decision-making forums will be used to raise issues and complaints from the community and to determine the necessary actions and by whom. Where appropriate, local government, community leaders and other stakeholders should be involved in the investigation and resolution of complaints. It is important to make sure each complainant receives a response and appropriate action, and those similar complaints receive a similar response. The complainant will receive a response that comprises a clear answer and explanation (even if no action will be taken) as well as an indication that the complaint has gone through an established process. People need to know they have been heard and answered. We at Global Communities believe that receiving a clear explanation satisfies the complainant. In other instances, if the complainant is left unhappy with the result, here the complainant should acknowledge that they have understood the answer, and know that if they do not accept it they can complain again.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The project will ensure the safety and dignity of the beneficiaries by ensuring the availability of safe water supply and creating a clean living environment in the targeted locations. The project services will be allocated based on the number of beneficiaries. Target beneficiaries will be informed of the project objectives and its impact on the allocated quantities of water to be supplied to their respective areas In addition, areas to supplied by the selected wells are known to be highly vulnerable and groups like people with disabilities, the elderly and women with young children will highly benefit due to minimizing the burden of buying and attaining un-piped water. Global Communities has a specific security manual that includes safety and security guidance for working in Palestine and details the processes, procedures, and risk mitigation measures staff are required to take to ensure their and beneficiaries’ safety and security. All staff expected to work in or travel to the targeted locations will receive security training from Global Community Operations staff. We have established coordination and security protocols working closely with Global Communities DC-based international security team and other organizations, such as UNRWA, on an ongoing basis to understand changes to the security and operational context in West-Bank and advise staff accordingly, including in real-time as needed. In the event of a security incident that affects the project, Global Communities will work closely with Wash Cluster, international and local entities such as the UN, OCHA, AIDA and local partners to respond accordingly.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Global Communities understands the interlinkage in human rights and that social inclusion stretches beyond focusing on the direct needs of marginalized groups. Promoting the rights to access safe drinking water and sanitation, directly impact the rights of education, health, women‘s right, child rights and the rights of People with Disabilities (PwDs). The unserved and underserved communities in Palestine fall under three main social inclusion barriers; Attitudinal: stigmatization and misinformation including the different roles between men and women, which negatively impacts the decision-making process. Environmental: issues in accessing infrastructures and facilities and lack of communications. Institutional/ organizational: lack of policies, strategies, skills and information to support social inclusion. While designing the technical intervention, GC was committed to using a participatory approach and non-discrimination principles to empower the marginalized by addressing WASH barriers. To ensure successful intervention, cultural sensitivity matters were taken into consideration and were incorporated in the monitoring and evolution plan so that unforeseen obstacles can be dealt with in an effective and efficient manner. Participation of stakeholders at the appropriate time during the program cycle is imminent; it enhances ownership, power relationships, accessibility, accountability, and sustainability. GC ensured that identified WASH stakeholders in general and beneficiaries in specific are fully engaged in the program design. Further, GC is committed to disseminating information to all stakeholders throughout the program to ensure informed decisions are adopted.
