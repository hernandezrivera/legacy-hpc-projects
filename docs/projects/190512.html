Project Summary

Somali Region continues to host one of the largest IDP populations in the country, with many who have faced multiple waves of displacement or remain in protracted displacement. Throughout 2021, conflict reignited along the Somali-Afar and the Somali-Oromia regional borders, resulting in new waves of displacement, largely along ethnic lines. According to DTM round 27, there are an estimated 871,176 IDPs (142,671 households) identified in the Somali region across 427 sites, where conflict is the main reason for displacement with an estimated number of 506,089 IDPs (58.1%). Round 27 saw an increase of 36,453 IDPs from round 26. Despite the emergency response efforts in the target region, there exists an increased need to continue with a sizeable WASH to ensure that minimum lifesaving humanitarian standards are maintained for the affected population.
Ensuring enhanced and safe WASH services to the affected population remains one of the main priorities for the WASH Cluster strategy as well as HRP 2022. The locations and activities considered for this WASH intervention are identified in the HRP 2022 prioritization list to address the immediate and life-saving needs of conflict-affected communities in line with the WASH cluster’s priority activities. The action will be WASH services and carried out in line with cluster objectives CLWSH/CO1 (Deliver conflict-sensitive essential life-saving WASH services to affected people), and CLWSH/CO3 (To enhance vulnerable households’ ability to withstand recurrent and future shocks in Ethiopia through early recovery and resilience interventions).
This project is submitted to address the immediate and life-saving needs of crisis-affected communities in line with the cluster's priority activities. The project aims to enhance the protection, health, and well-being of the conflict and conflict-induced IDPs and affected host communities and will be focusing on increasing their ability to access safe water supply, improved hygiene and sanitation practices in a right based manner through context-specific approaches that take into consideration the limited access for WASH services.
This project will improve the WASH situations of conflict IDPs in the Somali region. This project will directly reach 43,765 IDPs and non-displaced people aiming at increasing the accessibility of the WASH services for IDPs in general and the most vulnerable, including women, girls, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities in particular. DRC is targeting affected areas where the highest needs for WASH exist and where the risk of disease outbreaks is likely to be significant due to lack of basic services. Through the proposed lifesaving and durable WASH activities, DRC will contribute to the risk mitigation of disease outbreaks due to critical water shortage, limited sanitation, hygiene facilities, lack of essential WASH NFIs as well as physical safety and GBV.
The project will ensure the active participation of the targeted beneficiaries and stakeholders throughout the project cycle. Implementation of WASH activities will be coordinated together with the beneficiaries particularly women participating in decision making and the intervention will increase access to women., children, and other vulnerable groups in particular. Protection and safety concerns will be given priority and considered providing safe and easy access to the facilities by women and children as well as disabled people.
The project strategy considers the needs and concerns of all vulnerable groups by preventing and mitigating protection risks. Protection concerns will be reflected in the assessment, design, implementation, and monitoring, of WASH programs. As a result, DRC will take measures to ensure that WASH approaches will promote and respect the rights and dignity of women, boys, and girls.

1. - Projects activities

Project activities Water supply scheme rehabilitation work (motorized schemes operation, extension/upgrading of pipeline, shallow wells): DRC proposes maintenance of motorized water supply schemes, to continue providing safe water to the targeted population. This includes; maintenance of electro-mechanical equipment, pipeline extension/extension, rehabilitation of hand-dug wells/shallow wells, and installation of plastic tanks. DRC WASH technical staff will maintain the non-functional water distribution points and construct new distribution points based on a thorough technical assessment. WASHCOS: DRC will work with the community members to establish or re-establish the scheme WASHCo structure. WASHCo will be trained and strengthened to manage the schemes rehabilitated under this intervention and will be responsible for the operation and maintenance of community water supply systems. The training will focus on water scheme management, sanitation, and hygiene, and minor maintenance works. DRC will support and encourage the committee to increase women members to 50%. Tools for maintenance of water supply schemes will be provided to WASHCOs so that they can carry out minor maintenance independently. Fuel Support: Due to the limited capacity and resources from local government, many water supply schemes are non-functional and require fuel support to run the motorized schemes. In the intervention locations where IDPs are sheltered, water supply need has increased significantly so that generators work long hours a day to pump water. As a result, the water offices have a budget limitation to maintain the supply to IDPs and host communities. DRC will provide fuel to run generators and avoid water supply interruption so that the pressure on the water supply due to the increased number of conflict IDPs is relieved. Sanitation: Targeted people will be provided with better access to improved sanitation facilities. Access to sanitation facilities is expanded through the construction of emergency semi-permanent communal latrines with handwashing facilities, bathing facilities, desludging and decommissioning of filled or abandoned latrines, distribution of cleaning, and environmental cleaning kits. Hygiene promotion: Hygiene and sanitation awareness-raising activities designed to reinforce and encourage positive health and hygiene practices that reduce water-borne and sanitation-related diseases will be promoted and conducted throughout the targeted sites. In addition, hygiene promoters will receive training on basic principles of protection, gender-based violence, and referral pathways and support the hygiene promotion activities such as mass awareness and house-to-house visits and act as community actors for protection concerns. Distribution of WASH NFIs: DRC, adhering to the national WASH cluster guidance, will provide a full package of WASH NFIs mainly including containers (jerry-can, bucket, and washing basins), water treatment chemicals, and hygiene items (multi-purpose soap). Proper targeting, verification, and compliant handling mechanisms will be established in coordination with the MEAL department.

DRC will actively participate in humanitarian coordination meetings including the WASH cluster conducted at Somali and national level. DRC works and shares an update, information, and lessons through these platforms and discusses with partners and IDP representatives/community structures upon the implementation of the project activity like beneficiary selection and targeting and distribution for WASH NFI in a participatory way.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

DRC will provide accessible and timely information to affected populations on DRC procedures, structures, and processes that affect them to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices. DRC will establish feedback and complaint that actively seek the views of affected populations to improve policy and practice in programming, ensuring that feedback and complaints mechanisms are streamlined, appropriate, and robust enough to deal with complaints about breaches in the policy. DRC will ensure the communities play an active role in the decision-making process through the establishment of clear guidelines and practices to engage them appropriately and ensure that the most marginalized (women and children) and affected are represented and have influence. Prompt response to complaints will be made. DRC will organize awareness-raising sessions so that people know how it works and will consider a joint complaints mechanism with other sectors (e.g. Protection) to minimize confusion.
