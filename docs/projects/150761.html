Project Summary

This project aims to deliver quality and lifesaving primary and secondary health services which include maternal, neonatal and child health through static facilities and outreach services in 5 Districts targeting men, women, U5 and over 5 years children. The project will address the wide health gaps in the areas by fully supporting 9 health centres and four (4) mobile/outreach services which boost access to health services and serve difficult-to-reach populations in Gedo. The intervention will cover the five target districts and surrounding villages with free curative, preventive, and promotional health services through 9 health facilities, 2 facilities in Bardhera District including Bardhere District Hospital and Ali Ato MCH, 4 facilities in Elwak District including Elwak District Hospital, Damasa MCH , Geriley MCH and, Husqurun MCH, Dollow health centre in Dollow District, BeletXawa MCH in BeletXawa District and Berdale MCH in Baidoa where else the health outreach teams will provide much needed primary healthcare. The services will be targeting to enhance reproductive health services which include access to Family planning promotion and integrated method mix counseling and service delivery, Focused Antenatal Care (including complete physical examination of the mother and her foetus, systematic screening for syphilis, urine analysis; hemoglobin as well as establishment of delivery plan including referrals when appropriate, Sexually Transmitted Infections and HIV Testing and Counselling which shall enhance Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care Services. The intervention shall also counter risks of communicable and non communicable illnesses including childhood illness. Through outreach services, the intervention shall strengthen surveillance system and enhance emergency response to populations affected by frequent occurrence of epidemics, offer referral services in addition to increasing awareness of essential services, increased awareness on nutritional and hygiene behaviours, increase health service coverage especially hard to reach populations. Capacity building for health workers shall improve capacity of the health workers in service delivery and handle emergency cases. HDC will closely with community leadership and the MoH regional and district health’s teams. We are the Health Cluster lead for Gedo region will facilitate the monthly meetings in which local partners and where regional health teams will be invited to the meeting. We shall also liaise with other partners working in the area of health and nutrition (SC) for the management of severely acute malnourished with medical complication as well. This intervention will build a more resilient healthcare system and strengthen community resilience in Somalia by aligning core service provision with the Somali Essential Package of Health Services (EPHS) anchored in Health Sector Strategic Plan (2017-2021).

Needs

The health sector in Somalia is in critical situation with one of the worst health indicators in the world. More than 5.7 million people require basic health services, including critical needs in maternal and child health, as one in seven Somali children die before the age of five while 1 in every 22 women die during birth. Disease outbreaks such as acute watery diarrhoea AWD/cholera and measles continue to represent a major threat to children with over 5,892 suspected measles cases (72 percent being children under five) and 3,590 cases of AWD/cholera reported in as at May 2018 while over 78,000 of AWD/cholera and 23,000 cases of measles were reported in 2017. Exclusion and discrimination of socially marginalized groups continue to exacerbate high levels of acute humanitarian needs. Gedo is estimated to be approximately 508,405 of whom approximately, over 40 percent of IDPs in Dollow have “poor” to “borderline” consumption. The region host highest number of vulnerable populations including IDPs and returnees. Large population especially in Elwak, Dollow and Bardhere are overwhelming and limited access and availability of free healthcare while amplifying the risk of outbreaks of cholera and other communicable diseases, hampering health services due high IDP populations and low coverage of immunization services has resulted unvaccinated children under-5 at high risk of preventable diseases such as polio and measles exuberated by the high movement among vulnerable populations. Since December 2017, HDC faced funding gap to support the 8 facilities which has affected quality delivery of services due to lack of support to sustain staffing costs including medical supplies. The facilities are facing shortage of the supplies which pose risk to further sustain the services while CEmONC Services project in partnership with Federal Ministry of Health/ UNFPA is due to end in December 2018 which will further put risks to populations in need for the services.

Activities

Activity 1: Recruit qualified health workers in each facility to provide health services in static and outreach facilities; Activity 2: Providing curative consultations for communicable and non communicable diseases through static health facilities and outreach services to target populations; Activity 3: Conduct outreach based activities to be conducted by 4 outreach teams to promote access to health services and nutrition and health education and consultations including child immunizations; Activity 4: Conduct community outreach to increase, attitude and practices of essential child health, nutrition and WASH awareness among households in Elwak, Dollow, Bardhera and Belet-Xawa;Activity 5: Provide comprehensive reproductive health services including Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BEmONC) and Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric Care (CEmONC) in Bardera Hospital including quarterly reproductive health campaigns and referral of complicated cases i,e fistula cases for advanced treatment; Activity 6: Provision of Immunization services (EPI) within primary health services integrated with nutrition services through HDCs nutrition sector. Children and pregnant women with malnutrition will be referred to nutrition centers for nutrition care and support. Activity 7: Capacity building training of health workers to enhance skills in service delivery including integrated management of childhood illnesses (IMCI), service delivery during emergency responses and enhanced disease surveillance; and Activity 8: Provide support to the facilities with essential drugs, vaccines and basic facilitate equipping facilities to render life saving services.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

This project seeks to cement by supporting health sector objectives which are in line with the national strategies anchored in Somali Health Sector Strategic Plan. Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

We have put in place measures to ensure equitable and impartial access of health services especially the displaced populations. Through outreach services, we shall be able to reach the IDPs or hard to reach populations who might not be able receive the services due to limited availability of services or are not able to afford transportation cost. The reproductive health campaigns are specifically meant to reach the IDPs and host communities to create awareness of available services. Complain mechanisms shall also be put in place to handle any dissatisfaction among the populations targeted.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The project has considered gender mainstream as well as age disaggregation among the beneficiaries. The services have been packaged to ensure children , those under age of 5 year and those above are able to get the services, well as men and women. The project implementation will incorporate. To better inform service provision, HDC will take into account the customs and religious practices of target communities to ensure services provided are acceptable to all sectors of the population. HDC will provide female-friendly health services by deploying female midwives at service delivery points where women feel insecure or prefer female health workers due to societal norms. Apart from that, while this project specifically targets women of child bearing age, it is recognized that service uptake by the target audience will be impossible without male involvement. This is especially important in the religious and cultural setting existing in Somalia. Males will be specifically targeted for reproductive health services and encouraged to accompany their spouses where possible. Furthermore, gender roles will be addressed through ensuring a clear understanding of the cultural and religious norms in the target community and using appropriate interventions. All data that will be collected at the facility level will be dis-aggregated by age and sex to ensure that the needs of the male and female population are addressed

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Considering the impact of security challenges in Somalia, most of areas affected by armed conflicts do not have easy access to services such as health services. Considering the impact of such conflicts we are going to deliver services through outreach teams to promote access to services among those affected. Secondly, HDC has the experience in areas affected and we plan to use local staff familiar with the area to avoid tensions and resistance. We shall also use the community resource persons to create awareness and importance of the services and use existing as well as using community structures that work in those set ups.
