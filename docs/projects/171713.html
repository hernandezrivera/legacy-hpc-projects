Project Summary

6.3 million Somalis are acutely food insecure through June 2020, a 36% increase since late 2019. This includes 2.1 million Somalis in IPC 3-4 (double an average year) and 4.2 million in IPC 2 (the highest on record). The situation deteriorated most significantly in rural areas, where the population in IPC 2-4 doubled and IPC 3-4 tripled. Since 2016, six out of seven rainy seasons have been poor, including Gu 2019 which yielded Somalia’s lowest cereal harvest in decades in southern Somalia. Pastoralists also struggle to recover from significant livestock losses and debt incurred from the protracted drought. ADA’s project is expected to directly assist 20,549 households (123,294 people) spread across the two regions of Gedo and Galgaguud, Somalia hence contributing across FSC’s three strategic objectives (SOs).
To improve immediate access to food (SO1),ADA will deliver cash assistance to 7800 households through cash-for-work (CFW) to repair community infrastructures and 8200 households through integrated cash and livelihood Programmes (Cash+). Beneficiaries will receive 3-6 monthly cash transfers, based on duration of the lean season and their vulnerability. To support transition out of emergency cash programmes, ADA will offer lighter and longer-term cash and livelihood assistance to the most chronically vulnerable households in the target districts with high recurrence of IPC 3-4. ADA’s cash programme assists severely food insecure people to meet their food immediate needs, while giving them choice and re-injecting cash into the local economy. ADA delivers cash through mobile money operators and money vendors, adopts rates recommended by the Cash Working Group and regularly monitors the availability and prices of selected food and non-food commodities in local markets through the support of FSNAU.
To protect and restore livelihoods (SO2), ADA will provide quality production inputs and training to Cash+ beneficiaries and an additional 2460 vulnerable households. Tailored livelihood support packages will enable farmers to secure a Gu/ Deyr harvest, pastoralists to enhance milk production and coastal communities to improve fish catch and processing. The project will further enhance production and promote climate smart practices through field school approaches, targeting 1044 farming and pastoral households that will pass on the knowledge to their wider communities. To protect livestock assets, ADA together with FAO and the Somali Ministry of Livestock and Veterinary Services will conduct region-wide livestock treatment and vaccination campaigns to protect thousands of animals; and deliver emergency water supply and animal feed in the case of severe drought (1045 vulnerable households).
To build resilience and mitigate new shocks (SO3) at a community level, the project will improve water management and related infrastructure to protect rural populations and livelihoods against flooding (repaired river embankments) and cope with drought (fenced water catchments, animal troughs, canal sluice gates).

Project Activities

Through ADA, the project is expected to directly benefit 6839 HH’s (41,034 people) disaggregated as follows. 1. CFW/UCTs: 2290 HH. Households with members able to work will engage in 12 weeks of paid labour to rehabilitate community infrastructure (e.g. water catchment, canal) or undertake work to improve their productive environment (Prosopis clearing, contour bunding). Households unable to work (pregnant, elderly, ill, disabled people, etc.) will receive UCTs of the same amount and duration. 2. Cash+ Livelihood Packages: 4549 HH. ADA will provide 3-6 monthly cash transfers and tailored livelihood packages for food insecure farmers, pastoralists, including: (i) Cash+ Agriculture for 2460 HH (cereal, legume, veg seeds, tools, tractor/irrigation hours, fertilizer), (ii) Cash+ Livestock for 2089 HH (mineral blocks, deworming, mazzicans). ADA will provide all beneficiaries training in climate-smart practices, complemented by sessions in nutrition (infancy to old age) under Nutrition Cluster efforts.3. Enhanced Community Infrastructure for Flood & Drought Resilience. ADA will contract works to repair river breakages (10) and improve the efficacy of infrastructures already repaired by communities through CFW, including water catchments (25 enhanced with fencing & troughs) and irrigation canals (8 enhanced with sluice gates, pumps, supported canal committees).

Mitigation Measures

Exclusion and marginalization:ADA verifies and ensures that beneficiaries identified in the tripartite (ADA, elders and council members) participatory selection process meet the selection criteria (female, male, age, IDP, household heads, etc.). ADA holds sensitization and communication sessions with all targeted communities about the selection criteria of the project. The same information is broadcasted through local radio before activities start. ADA selection criteria ensures that at least 30% of beneficiaries are women-head households for each programme activity. Moreover, ADA has formal complaint and feedback mechanisms in place, including a Call Centre and a toll-free Hotline. Third Party Monitors provide further opportunity for beneficiaries to report on the selection process, provide feedback and complainExternal stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: In addition to the above, FAO heavily engages with district, state and federal authorities to facilitate and solve issues of interference by external stakeholders. ADA triangulates information on alleged cases of interference and seeks mediation from relevant parties (elders, ADA staff, local authorities, etc.). If the case cannot be resolved, ADA reprogrammed activities to other districts. All contracts with ADA include a clause on zero-tolerance of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA), and obligate beneficiaries and other stakeholders to report such cases. ADA staff and counterparts receive dedicated trainings on AAP, Prevention of SEA and gender inclusion principles of ADA activities. Protection mainstreaming: In addition to the above, ADA mitigates these protection risks by avoiding large gatherings of beneficiaries, through conducting registration and input distribution at village level. ADA has recently developed a mobile payment system with a certain money vendor in the region that mitigates protection risks related to travel, extortion and intimidation, among others.
