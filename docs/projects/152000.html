Project Summary

The project aims to address critical WASH gaps in Dikwa through maintenance of critical sanitation facilities. Through close coordination with site facilitators, the WASH sector, humanitarian actors, and the IDP community, ACTED will identify prioritized sites for desludging latrines, in order to ensure IDPs have access to sanitation facilities. To ensure sustainability of the intervention, ACTED aims to promote good hygiene practices through community-led awareness and sensitization activities, supported by capacity building initiatives to volunteer community hygiene promoters, with equal gender participation. This activity will be complemented by the distribution of gender-sensitive hygiene kits to 800 vulnerable IDP households in order to ensure access to items and equipment required to adopt better hygiene practices.

Needs

According to REACH Initiative’s LGA Profiling Assessment, most new arrivals indicated the reason for relocation to Dikwa was the ability to escape AOG controlled villages, or to be closer their location of origin. Due to the strategic location of Dikwa it has increasingly become a point of first arrival for IDPs fleeing rural villages where Nigerian Security Forces and AOGs are in conflict. As it remains a front-line location, the number of IDPs and new arrivals is expected to increase, placing greater stress on already over-extended local resources and over-congested sites. According to a recent rapid assessment conducted in Dikwa by ACTED, and discussions with WASH sector partners, critical gaps in latrines have been identified due to large-scale desludgement needs, and flooding during the rainy season remains a prevalent risk to sanitation facilities, posing sever risks of disease outbreaks.

Activities

- Latrine desludgement: 200 blocks of latrines will be desludged, equivalent to 1000 latrines, calculated at 5 latrines per block. It is expected that this will benefit 20,000 beneficiaries, at SPHERE standards of 20 individuals per latrine - Gender-sensitive hygiene kits are procured and distributed: The distribution of hygiene kits in Dikwa aims to address hygiene and sanitation needs, critically exacerbated by the impact of heavy rains during the wet season on overall health and sanitation levels. The standard hygiene kits have been designed to provide critical life-saving hygiene products, with consideration for dignity preservation. All women and girls will be issued with sanitary pads as part of the hygiene kit. Volunteers will provide menstrual hygiene sensitization to the target users.- Capacity Building of WASH Committees to maintain WASH facilities: will identify existing WASH Committees or establish WASH Committees in the sites of implementation, and will provide training to committee members on good hygiene practices and on WASH facility maintenance. Committees will be composed of IDP community members, will have 50% female gender, and will also have representation from people with disabilities and special needs. The committees will be provided with latrine cleaning materials – detergents, brooms, disinfectants, protective items, etc. TORs will be developed in order to solidify the objectives of the committee, and to provide the foundation for activities that will be conducted by committee members. The committee members will be trained on community mobilization and participation, awareness raising, good hygiene practices, and WASH facility maintenance. The WASH Committee will conduct regular Committee meetings in order to discuss identified WASH challenges, and will attend regular joint coordination meetings with humanitarian partners in order to establish direct communication links between the IDP community and the relevant humanitarian sector.- Promotion of good hygiene practices: Community Hygiene Promoters (community volunteers) will receive training on community mobilization and participation, awareness raising, good hygiene practices, and menstrual hygiene practices. The CHPs will be responsible for hygiene promotion, including door to door campaigns at the household level and community hygiene sessions at the community level, aimed at promoting good hygiene practices among the IDP community, including: promoting safe water storage through clean containers, solid waste management (safe waste disposal practices), ORS preparation, promotion of hand washing at critical moments (before eating, before cooking, after visiting the toilet, after changing babies’ nappies, and before breastfeeding), and to discourage open defecation. Outreach activities will reach households twice during implementation, and will conduct 40 community sessions with approximately 20 attendants each. CHPs will also provide menstrual hygiene trainings for women and girls at household level and at community level for female attendants only. Information disseminated will also aim to enhance community overall knowledge of the importance of good hygiene practices, as well as on sensitization of AWD and Cholera in order to encourage afflicted households to report any suspected cases to appropriate humanitarian partners immediately, and to decrease stigmatization surrounding such cases. The volunteer Community Hygiene Promoters training program is designed as a capacity building program in order to enhance sustainability of programming, and to enable future employment opportunities. These individuals will also be trained on reporting tools to ensure that visited households and conducted awareness raising activities can be captured correctly.
