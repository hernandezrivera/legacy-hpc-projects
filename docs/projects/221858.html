Project Summary

AHC has been supporting education sector in Maiwut County through construction of semi-permanent learning spaces, capacity building initiatives for teachers, pupils and PTA/SMC, provision of MHM kits for adolescent girls in schools in collaboration with UNICEF, SMoGEI and CED. AHC will integrate the proposed education intervention with our ongoing FSL and Protection projects in Nasir County. The project is aligned to Education Cluster 2022-2025 Strategy and objectives for 2025 HRP.
The proposed interventions are aimed at increased access to dignified education for 4160 children; Nasir 3160 (M2000 F1160) Maiwut 1000 (M600 F400) and youth (aged 3-18) in the target counties. AHC plans to support 50, 35 and 15 percent of the total beneficiaries (4254) being Host communities, Sudan/Ethiopian returnees and IDPs respectively.
The interventions will be implemented in accordance with Ministry of General Education and Instruction (MoGEI) and other quality standards as set in INEE minimum standards of Education in Emergencies. Community participatory mechanisms will be utilized to promote school enrolment, retention and acceptable transition of learners to next class/school at the end of the school year. The establishment of the 6 semi-permanent learning spaces (2 Nasir and 4 Maiwut) shall be based on the approved MoGEI’s establishment designs, specifications and all engineering requirements stipulated in the design.
AHC will also construct 6 gender sensitive WASH facilities (2 Nasir and 4 Maiwut) in the selected schools. Protective fences will be erected around semi-permanent structures and schools for added safety. The established infrastructure shall also be disability sensitive, gender friendly and in such a way to withstand adverse weather conditions. AHC will work with communities, local county officials, State Ministry of General Education and Instruction (SMOGEI) in the in project implementation through community participatory approaches.
In regard to protection mainstreaming, safety audits will be conducted in the three target locations to map existing and potential risks and possible impact on project activities. The findings will be embedded in project activities and used to advocate for protection of staff, beneficiaries, project supplies and assets. AHC will rely on protection cluster, partners and UNOCHA for security updates. Local authorities will be informed of staff’s movement and project activities well in advance and requested to provide security. AHC will liaise with Community Social/Case Workers to refer any GBV case they come across to the referral pathway.
AHC will orient AAP committees and community leaders on the rights of PWDs and the project’s intention to incorporate CwD as participants and in supporting the project team to identify potential barriers not already considered to be incorporated into project activities. The project will provide equal opportunities to youth, women and girls and other marginalized or at-risk

1. Needs

South Sudan has the highest rate of primary school-aged children out of school in the world, with an estimated 3.3 million children under the age of 18 facing education barriers in 2023, 53% of whom are girls (HNO 2023). Education quality in South Sudan is impacted by the insufficient retention of qualified teachers, who often face irregular and low wages where 60% of teachers nationwide currently lack qualifications and work, in effect, as education volunteers. An IRNA conducted by AHC in Maiwut County identified barriers to accessing quality education including lack of schools within a reasonable distance from learners, school facilities such as classrooms in poor condition, inadequate WASH facilities and supplies, lack of female teachers (affects girls’ enrolment), lack of assistive devises for Children with Disabilities (CwD) and issues with teacher absenteeism, inadequate teaching and learning materials. Schools in regions like Jonglei (where Akobo is located), with high needs and limited access, are often overcrowded, with an average qualified teacher-to-pupil ratio of 1 to 100. This demotivates teachers and discourages children from attending or staying in school, leading to poorer educational outcomes. According to National Education Census 2023, the pre-primary enrolment in Upper Nile State had a 7% decrease in 2023 in comparison to 2022 with 5929 pupils (3303 M and 2626 F) enrolled in ECE facilities in 2023. This is attributable to lack of qualified teachers, education materials, conflict and limited access to resources. Social behavioural change is also required as some parents believe children are too young to attend school even beyond the ministry’s recommended of 3-5 years. A case study by UNICEF in 2023 notes inadequate physical infrastructure to support ECE, financing, poor coordination mechanisms at all levels (national, state and county levels), lack of alternate mechanisms for the most marginalized leading to poor quality services and drop out and repetition of primary education. The proposal directly responds to the priorities of the General Education Sector Plan(GESP) 2017-2022 and seeks to address issues of access and equity, quality of education from early childhood development, primary, secondary and AES, and to contribute to the Dakar framework on education for all (EFA), the Sustainable development Goals number four on Ensuring inclusive and Equitable Primary Education.

2. Project activities

Linked to Education Cluster 2025 HRP CO1: Emergency-affected children and youth have access to safe, protective, inclusive education services that are appropriate to their age, learning level and need Activity 1.1: Establish 6 learning spaces (4 Maiwut and 2 Nasir) in line MoGEI with INEE's Minimum Standards; The semi-permanent classrooms will be constructed in 6 selected schools in collaboration with CED and SMoGEI to promote school children protection, mental health and wellbeing. Activity 1.2: Increased community awareness for inclusive enrolment and retention of children, youth and adolescents To boost enrolment and maintain attendance, AHC will engage community members through a variety of activities to reach a total of 5114 children; Nasir (M1719 F1360), Maiwut (M1142 F 761) and youth (aged 3-18) from five schools in the target counties. Incentivized community mobilizers and facilitators will lead community dialogues. These mobilizers will undergo a comprehensive training on the Community Action Cycle (CAC) model. Activity 1.3: Rehabilitate 2 (Nasir 1, Maiwut 1) learning spaces in line with INEE's Minimum Standards; The intended outcome is that crisis-affected children and youth in the target counties/schools have access to education services that is appropriate for their age groups and level of learning. Activity 1.4: Rehabilitate 6 gender sensitive latrines (2 Nasir and 4 Maiwut) in line with INEE and sphere standards; Activity 1.5: Distribute dignity kits to 600 adolescent girls Specific focus will include Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) and comprehensive sexuality education. 600 MHM kits will be procured and distributed, accompanied by awareness campaigns on MHM. Each kit contains: re-usable Medium size sanitary Pack (each has6 Pads); re-usable maxi-size sanitary pack (each has 6 Pads); 3medium size pieces women underwear; 3 small size pieces women underwear; 800gms multi-purpose bar soap. AHC will also conduct awareness raising on key issues for the girl child and transition to post-secondary education; Activity1.6: Distribute assistive devices to 100 (33 girls and 67 boys) children with disabilities (CwD) AHC will conduct annual community awareness campaigns to promote inclusive education and enhance access for children with disabilities (physical and psychosocial disabilities, ensuring inclusion of children with learning difficulties/additional learning needs) and young mothers Activity 1.7: Establish and equip Child Help Desks within the selected schools These will be trained on psychological first aid(PFA), identification of children needing CP support and safe referrals, to identify and provide immediate support to vulnerable and at-risk learners and refer them to dedicated case workers when necessary. Linked to Education HRP CO2: Children and youth are provided with quality teaching and learning services that support retention and progress through the education cycle. Activity 2.1: Conduct in service training for 48 teachers; Nasir (M5 F33), Maiwut (M2 F8) on child safeguarding, MPHSS and GBV risk mitigation, referral mechanism; Activity 2.2: Distribute ECD Kits to school children in 6 ECD Centres; distribute Recreation Kits to children in 6 primary schools;Activity 2.3: Support 393 Nasir (M193F128), Maiwut (M43 F29) volunteer teachers with financial incentivesActivity2.4: Distribute teachers Kits to 393 teachers in 12 schools; Linked to Education HRP CO3: Coordination and crisis management capacity is strengthened to promote efficient, accountable, and timely education in emergencies response.Activity 3.1: Conduct training for 25; Nasir (M12 F8), Maiwut (M3 F2) school management committees (SMC) members, Parent Teacher Associations (PTA), and education authorities trained on school management, governance, climate change and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR)
