Project Summary

The proposed project by Plan International will contribute to improved livelihood for at risk 1400 adolescent girls and boys (15-17) and young people (18-24) to access vocational training and livelihood opportunities for older adolescents and young people identified through case management systems. The project will contribute to protection and risk reduction of vulnerable adolescent youths through restored livelihood systems by providing access to vocational training/apprenticeship on trades hair dressing, tailoring or computer. Training will be followed up with providing access to start-up tools to develop small scale economic activities to build resilience. The project will target 65% female and 35% male.

Besoins / Needs

Background• The Anglophone Crisis in Cameroon has been ongoing since Oct 2016, when violent clashes erupted between the military and armed groups fighting for separation of the predominantly English-speaking North-West and South-West (NWSW) regions from the rest of Francophone Cameroon. This has driven over 530,000 women, girls, men and boys into internal displacement, including an estimated 35% women and 40% children (UNHCR/CP AOR, Apr 2019). • The humanitarian situation continues to deteriorate with serious protection incidents, excessive human rights abuses, and violence against civilians with men and women, boys and girls loosing their means of subsistence. Displacement is increasing with the continuous destruction of villages and infrastructure by warring parties, fueling a climate of insecurity with the brunt of the conflict affecting the general population. Despite the massive scale of the crisis and immense needs, international attention has been scarce, leading the situation to be described as “the world’s most neglected displacement crisis” (NRC, Jun 2019). • The escalation of violence has prevented schools from operating normally as parents fear for the safety of their children. Disruption of education is putting children at increased risk of exploitation, including child labour or early marriage. Particularly at risk are adolescent boys and girls, who are at additional risk of recruitment into the NSAGs. The loss of critical years of education creates gaps around financial knowledge, life and business skills. • The escalation has also led to major destruction of villages, properties and national identity documents including birth certificates and titled deeds. One of the disheartening results is family separation, child headed household, livelihoods of families including young people are destroyed as a result of the crisis and many adolescents and young people find it difficult to meet their basic needs. Vocational training needs• In October 2019, Plan international conducted market studies for vocational training for adolescent and young people in the Northwest region in 10 communities including Ngomgham, Ntarinkon, Mulang,Ntamulung, Azire, Nitop I, Nitop II, Nitop III, Mile 2 and Mile 5. • The aim of the study was to identify relevant and viable vocational skill areas and opportunities in Mezam Divisionn that increase the employability of youths affected by the crisis. The specific objectives of the market trend analysis were to: Map specific viable areas for skills training and apprentice for girls on one hand and for boys on the bases of community needs and market, To determine who the major service providers are and access their capacity to train and position their graduates for employment and self-employment, To identify specific market challenges affecting prioritized vocational trades for youths. • The findings of the study point to tailoring, Hair dressing, Mechanic and carpentry as the most available Vocational trades for boys and girls. Generally from the study, Mechanics, computer, iron works are the highest preferred by boys and tailoring, hair dressing and computer are highly preferred by girls. Also, other youths signified interest in engaging in livestock farming specifically poultry and piggery farming. • Through the proposed project, Plan will contribute to improved livelihood for At risk older adolescent girls and boys (15-17) and young people (18-24) have access to vocational training and livelihood opportunities. With reference to the report of market analysis on profitable trades and their own preferences, older adolescents and young people identified through case management systems, access vocational training/apprenticeship on trades hair dressing, tailoring or computer. Training will be followed up with providing access to start-up tools to develop small scale economic activities to build resilience. The project will target 65% female and 35% male.
