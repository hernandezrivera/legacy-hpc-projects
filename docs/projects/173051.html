Project Summary

This project will respond to protection needs related to Gender Based Violence GBV of IDPs, returnees, refugees and their hosting communities in who faced threats of physical violence, exploitation, abuse, neglect in particularly to the current economic and health deterioration in light of COVID 19 in Umburu and Allaiet in North Darfur, Sharg Alneil in Khartoum, Aj Jabalain and As Salam / Ar Rawat in White Nile. NADA embraces community based protection approach by building capacity of targeted communities through equipping them with necessary skills and knowledge to implement community driven solutions to their local communities. The project will carry out extensive consultations with local community and line ministries as an entry point for smooth project implementation. The intervention will enhance local capacity on GBV through training of community based protection networks (CBPNs) and social workers ,establishment of referral mechanisms , operational of multipurpose women centers , provision of direct support including case management for GBV survivors and distribution of personal dignity hygiene kits (PHKs) .The project will link the protection, health and legal response needed by GBV survivors through provision of case management and referral services. Construction and running of women centers is core activity as lack of having women center with well-designed space for provision of PSS hindered the GBV survivors from accessing the urgent support /assistance. Project team will conduct services mapping and organize monthly meetings with relevant partners and services providers to ensure better access of GBV survivors to other services. Women and girls will receive direct support through provision of PHKs. The project team will organize round-tables forums with police, health cadre, community leaders regarding the coordination and strengthening the reporting and referral of cases. Project team will provide weekly awareness raising /PSS sessions through microphone in the women centers to ensure social distancing, individuals will be reached with similar messages through structured FGD sessions. Awareness raising messages on GBV and SGBV risks will be delivered through mobile public awareness campaigns. The project will consider APP through community participation on conducting needs assessments and implementation of activities as well as establishment of community based feedback and complaint mechanisms, composed of Community Volunteers (CVs). Process and time for response will clearly be communicated to beneficiaries. The intervention will follow GBV guidelines, GBV Standard operation procedures to response to the needs and the identified gaps and will use vulnerability criteria applied for protection sector.
NADA will commit to collaborate and coordinate with the GBV Sub-Sector on the national level and the GBV Working Group in ND. NADA will attend the coordination meetings and report to the 4Ws.

1 - Needs

According to the GBV sectorial analysis for 2021 HNO, Gender Based Violence (GBV) is a key concern within the different communities affected by different emergencies. Women and girls among the IDP communities and refugees are especially at a risk of GBV. Analysis shows that Conflict-Related Sexual Violence (CRSV) is especially common in IDP camps, in the vicinity of camps and in conflict zones. Girls and young women in Darfur face a high risk of sexual violence. In Darfur, 32 per cent of the community perceive their environment as unsafe due to GBV. Domestic violence, child marriage and Female Genital Mutilation (FGM) remain common practice. In a recent GBV study by UN-Women, four out of five women interviewed in Darfur recognized domestic violence as a problem, 70 per cent identified economic violence as an issue, while five per cent cited rape as a concern. Lack of services, negative coping mechanisms and negative social norms were identified as some of the exacerbating factors contributing to GBV. Survivors of GBV lack access to quality specialized lifesaving GBV services, such as the Clinical Management of Rape (CMR), case management, psycho-social support, legal aid and functional referral mechanisms. These services continue to be unavailable or of low quality in over 90 per cent of localities. CMR services are available in less than one in five health facilities. The gap in services is made worse by the lack of trained personnel and weak referral pathways. Survivors face procedural obstacles e.g. complicated and lengthy police form. According to the partial MSNA, only 29 per cent of households have access to GBV services with 87 per cent of households preferring reporting GBV cases to the police, while 24 per cent preferring leaders, 4 per cent preferring social worker, and only 1 per cent preferring Health staff, community midwife, Teacher or NGO staff.There is also a gap of integrated services for survivors which are survivor-friendly, such as one stop centres with only three states in Darfur having function GBV confidential corners . The collapse of traditional community support structures has increased the vulnerability in particular people with specific needs, including those living with disabilities. COVID-19 exacerbated GBV risks for girls and women with anecdotal reports suggesting an increase in domestic violence and sexual abuse. Few localities have functioning community-based protection networks and women centers which have limited capacities and thus they do not function optimally in GBV prevention and response. Low levels of GBV awareness among communities makes it challenging to ensure prevention, mitigation and access for survivors to the relevant multi-sectorial services.There are 2.3 million people in dire need of GBV prevention, mitigation and response activities. Women and girls continue to suffer disproportionately from GBV, poverty and violations of basic rights due to insecurity, low economic status and lack of livelihood opportunities, lack of community awareness on women’s rights due to cultural and societal norms. About 55 per cent of IDPs are women and girls, and 27 per cent of those women below the age of 18. According to available information on GBV cases, women and girls account for over 90 per cent of survivors, and although grossly under- reported, cases of sexual violence against men and boys have been recorded.The impact of COVID-19 differs for women, men, boys and girls. Women provide most of the care within families and in some areas they are the main breadwinners. Female-headed households and households with many dependents are among the most vulnerable because they now have additional burdens. More women are expected to fall into poverty during the COVID-19 pandemic, severely affecting female-headed households in the country.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Through this intervention, NADA is committed safeguard Right holders from violence and discrimination and is aimed at obtaining full respect for the rights of the individuals. NADA will implement the project in impartial and non-discriminatory ways that promote the safety, dignity and integrity of the people receiving assistance during assessment, design of project and delivery of services. The project team will ensure all elements of accountability to affected population to be captured through extensive engagement and consultation with the community. Project is designed to inform all displaced about the availability and location of services. The project ensures safe and equal access to inclusive and non-discriminatory provision of services. The intervention will also carefully apply Do No Harm approach to avoid any negative impact that it might bring to the area or the targeted communities. Manage information in a sensitive manner so that the security of informants or others who may be identifiable is not jeopardized. The assistance and protection measures will not undermine local capacities for self-protection as well as support the efforts of the affected population and local communities to find security and restore dignity. Project intends to respond to the root causes of the protection concerns in the area. To achieve this, NADA embraces community based protection approach, for building capacity of local communities, service providers and government officials. Their capacity will be enhanced in analyzing & finding solutions to protection problems, inclusive of reducing threats& risks, reducing vulnerability of affected population and increasing their capacity to cope with protection problems following exposure to shock and disaster.
