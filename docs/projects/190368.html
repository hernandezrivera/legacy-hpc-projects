Project Summary

The Joint Emergency Operation (JEOP) is an emergency food assistance program funded by the United States Agency for International Development (USAID), Bureau for Humanitarian Assistance (BHA). JEOP targets transitory or acute food insecure households through a participant targeting process led by the Government of Ethiopia’s (GoE) National Disaster Risk Management Commission (NDRMC). JEOP complements emergency needs outlined in the GoE’s Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP). Over the last decade, JEOP has served as a food pipeline for up to 33% of the emergency-affected woredas in five regions and one city administration, flexing to meet evolving need.
Currently targeting over seven million people nationwide, JEOP is one of the largest food operators in Ethiopia, working with the GoE alongside the World Food Program (WFP). JEOP’s principal objective is to ensure that targeted communities in need of emergency food assistance have access to sufficient, appropriate, food in a timely manner. This is done primarily through round-based distributions of commodities to targeted project participants (15kg. wheat, 1.5kg pulses and 0.45kg oil). Since 2018, JEOP expanded its modalities to include cash transfers in two forms; a) cash in lieu of wheat in line with seasonal availability of grains on local markets, and b) transfers to offset cost of transportation for families living far from final distribution points. Modality decisions are based on market and cash feasibility assessments looking at market conditions, access, preference, risk analysis, cost efficiency and effectiveness, with attention to intra-household and community conflict dynamics.
Led by Catholic Relief Services (CRS), the JEOP consortium includes CARE, Food for the Hungry Ethiopia (FHE), Organization for Rehabilitation and Development in Amhara (ORDA), Relief Society of Tigray (REST), and World Vision Ethiopia (WV). CRS implements through its local Catholic Church partners, the Hararghe Catholic Secretariat (HCS) and Meki Catholic Secretariat (MCS). CRS is responsible for overall consortium management.
In August 2012, USAID/Food for Peace (FFP), now the Bureau for Humanitarian Assistance (BHA), approved a two-year award through Cooperative Agreement No. AID-FFP-A-12-00009, initially effective from August 1, 2012 through July 31, 2014. Subsequent modifications extended JEOP through July 2022, the current end date of the program. Meanwhile, CRS is the Apparent Successful Applicant (ASA) for follow on funding for JEOP under another 5-year award and has applied for additional funding under another BHA Emergency mechanism to cover 18 months of the current caseload in Northern Ethiopia Response (implemented in Tigray and Amhara).
As such, from mid-2022, JEOP’s current geographic coverage area will be served by two funding sources: 1) JEOP, operating in Dire Dawa, Oromia, Sidama, and SNNPR (JEOP 2.0) and 2) JEOP-Northern Response (JEOP-NR), operating in Tigray and Amhara. It is anticipated that within two years of the start of the JEOP rebid, the caseload in Amhara and Tigray will have normalized, and these two regions will be enveloped into the multi-year award as before. While each introduces novel interventions and approaches, the foundation of the programs remains largely unchanged. Both will continue to provide in-kind food assistance to vulnerable populations across Ethiopia.

1. - Projects activities

Joint emergency operation is planning to serve food for approximately 4.1m (2 Male and 2.1 Female) disaster affected people in five regions of Ethiopia: Amhara, Oromia, Sidama, SNNP and Tigray; and in Dire Dawa City Administration. Food cluster projected that 20.4 million people would need lifesaving humanitarian food assistance in FY’2022, due to various driving factors including conflict, and drought. JEOP will distribute assistance on a round basis (a round is about 1 and a half months) and the first round is expected to start in the first week of April 2022. The Cash transfer value will be informed by the revised wage rates for 2014 EFY. However, JEOP will continue market monitoring activities to advocate adjustments on transfer value and to monitor impacts of commodity/cash distribution on markets. JEOP have also a plan to continue transportation cash assistance to needy project participants.JEOP will continue implementing an accountability mechanism by placing multiple options (suggestion box, direct call, community help desk, and complaint and feedback committees) for the project participant to voice their concern. Currently JEOP is setting up a call center which will be onboarded by multiple language speaking call center operators to directly receive feedback and complaint from the project participant in a free call service. JEOP implements a robust early warning system (EWS) for context monitoring purpose. The EWS will also continue to provide evidence from primary and secondary sources to better inform need estimation and programming. JEOP will also provide a capacity building training for ~10,000 woreda and Kebele level targeting taskforces on areas of early warning, humanitarian principles and national targeting guideline. Besides, JEOP would also support the local Government and kebele task forces to do a need-based targeting at community level through supporting the household registration and validation processes. In all its programs JEOP will ensure that cross cutting teams are mainstreamed including disability, protection, safeguarding, and environment. Other activities of JEOP includes: -- Train consortium members and government EW staff on EW system and data analysis- Train consortium members and kebele EW staff on EW system and data analysis- Organize awareness sessions for communities on risk awareness- Conduct quarterly EW data review and analysis for kebele and woreda Disaster Management Committee (DRM) members- Compile monthly report from the weekly data - Review monthly report from weekly data and respond accordingly- Participate in the seasonal multi-agency assessments- Conduct fast onset hazard/rapid assessments- Refresher training on national targeting guidelines- Prepare sales orders/call forwards/ and follow up commodity pipeline- Train commodity distributors and supervisors on ration size, entitlement, and distribution process- Train staff on JEOP commodity management minimum standards and best practice- Dispatch food commodities from central warehouse to final distribution points (FDP)- Conduct distributions (prioritize vulnerable groups and female-headed households) - Strengthen complaint response mechanism (establish PSEA accountability mechanism)- Train JAM community help desk members and government staff for new help desks- Engage community decision makers in awareness activities for the JEOP Accountability Mechanism (JAM)- Train JEOP staff on protection mainstreaming and PSEA- Train kebele and woreda officials on PSEA- Develop food distribution schedules/calendars- Conduct registration for flexible resource transfer beneficiaries- Distribute flexible resource transfers (top-up for transportation + in lieu of food)- Distribute flexible resource transfers (in lieu of food)- Financial education sessions for flexible resource transfer beneficiaries- Distribute commodities for rapid-onset emergency caseload

2.1.1 - Is it better to use cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

JEOP prefers cash assistance over voucher in order to give project participants more power in terms of selecting vendors and type of commodity they want to purchase. However, in accordance with the GoE-led plan for integrating cash transfers and food in selected woredas, JEOP will proceed with providing approximately a mixed modality transfer (cash for cereals and in-kind for YSP and oil) for two rounds of distribution and will provide four three of only food commodities in 2022. However, the decision for mixed modality will be carried out by thorough analysis of revised wage rate against the JEOP market data. The schedule of transfers follows the agriculture calendar for each of the targeted areas, market functionalities and price trend. Given the start-up required for setting up cash programming, and with an anticipated scale-down to support closeout in the latter half of Year 2022, JEOP will continue to focus cash-in-lieu support in Dire Dawa and Kersa (HCS), Arsi Negele (MCS), and South Gonder (FHE), given respective partners’ experiences with cash transfers to date, the needs that have been identified, and the market assessments and monitoring already conducted for program design. Project participants selected for cash-in-lieu support will be selected using the same criteria used by local governments for selecting HRP project participants to receive JEOP support. Market assessments and monitoring conducted in Year 2021 demonstrated that these rates would need to be increased in order to cover the entire basket provided by JEOP. Should PSNP wage rates be adjusted in Year 2022, these adjustments will be absorbed by the existing project budget. Recent experience shows that the PSNP rate set by the GoE may not allow households to purchase JEOP’s food basket/ration amount (15kg of wheat, 1.5kg of YSP and 0.45kg of oil) from the local market. For example, 2021 market values of 15kg of wheat, 1.5kg of field pea and 0.45kg of oil for Lay Gayint (cash transfer woreda in South Gondor), is 442 ETB based on average retail price collected in three markets of Lay Gayint in 2021 (January, February and March). However, the PSNP rate per person for Lay Gayint is set at 245 per person per month, 45% lower than the values of commodities in local markets. To further illustrate for this plan period, JEOP attempted a price forecast analysis for wheat using recent five years wholesale data. Accordingly, the price of wheat is expected to increase by up to 10% as compared to 2021 and 34% compared to 2020 in 2022. Retail prices are also expected to increase in similar pattern. However, the PSNP rate currently under work is expected to increase by up to 20% for 2022 (given Ethiopia’s average inflation of ~20% since July 2020). PSNP to be revised may not still allow households to buy JEOP food basket equivalent from markets. JEOP will do similar analysis to determine on mixed modality for each cash transfer locations.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

National and local actors are part and parcel of JEOP programming through out the JEOP project life cycle. At national level, JEOP works with the Ethiopian Disaster Risk Management Commission, food cluster, donor, cash working group and other relevant stakeholders as appropriate. JEOP collaborates at higher level with these group in need identification, prioritization of resource allocation, selection of response modalities, etc. At local level, JEOP closely works with the local EDRMC structure which includes zonal, woreda and kebele level DRM and food security task forces. These entities play a leading role in terms of leading geographic and household targeting activities at woreda and kebele level. JEOP also collaborates with local organizations for the implementation of the project, which includes REST, ORDA, Meki and Hararge catholic secretariats.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

JEOP Accountability Mechanism (JAM) - to ensure open channels of communication for project participants, community members, contractors, and staff to share feedback, including safeguarding concerns, JAM will be revised to include hotline service so that confidential feedback is reported safely. Each Accountability Officer will oversee Accountability Focal Points at woreda level, in turn overseeing up to 15 kebele level CHDs. This increased proximity will support a more consistent service provided by CHDs to communities. Furthermore, Accountability Focal Points will be present at each distribution, with a roaming role, observing processes and personnel, meanwhile supporting CHDs. Checklists for best practice will be developed for the use of these staff and integrated into the compliance and risk monitoring team checklists. CRS is also piloting a shared hotline across consortium members. Accountability Focal Points will be charged with sensitization on these mechanisms among others; CHD, Accountability Focal Points, suggestion box, telephone, speaking to staff, using IEC materials, and speaking at community meetings. Feedback and Response System Set up, Maintenance and Training: CRS and consortium staff will review accountability mechanisms and feedback mechanisms to ensure they are adequately serving the target communities. JEOP will provide for travel, per diem, accommodation. This will include participatory meetings with beneficiaries, printing of materials (banners, posters, feedback boxes) and translation of materials. This is in addition to contribution to the countrywide hotline pilot outlined in the preceding section, the aim being to provide as many possible potential accessible channels for a wide variety of project participants, contractors, community members and staff. The telephone hotline will involve engaging a high number of additional staff at CRS and partner level. CRS will facilitate training on the integrated feedback mechanism across partner and GoE staff engaged in its implementation. Safeguarding and JAM - the JEOP Accountability Mechanism (JAM) has been under review during PREP IX and will be revised in PREP X to add additional mechanisms such as phone hotline, accountability focal points and safeguarding staff. Alongside the JAM, PDMs will be a means to capture and better understand any safeguarding concerns. JEOP is contributing to partner level safeguarding assessments and action plans – these shed light on adaptions and support the referral mapping process – such that if, and when, concerns arise appropriate support is identified in each geographic area. At consortium management level, these initiatives will be overseen by the Program Quality (PQ) Unit, under the oversight if the MEAL technical unit. A Gender and Protection TA will also be included within the Program Quality team, a Safeguarding Senior Program Officer, in addition to the Accountability Officers at partner level. These staff members will handle sensitive and confidential feedback reported. In addition, this team will ensure that MEAL best practices, particularly those connected to safeguarding, protection and Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), are understood, embodied, and prioritized.
