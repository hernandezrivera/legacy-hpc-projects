Project Summary

In 2019, Cameroon had over 900,000 internally displaced people, 51% of whom were children with many ending up on the streets of cities like Yaoundé and Douala and to a lesser extent Kumba and Tiko in the south-west province. In the past 10 years, the number of street children in Cameroon has increased from around 1,000 to over 10,000 and this surge has, at least in part, been attributed to the separatist crisis in the country's English speaking north-west and south-west regions in addition to Boko Haram's terrorist attacks in the region along Cameroon's northern border with Nigeria causing many to flee their homes. Many other children arrive with nothing having fled the troubles in the Central African Republic and end up doing whatever it takes to survive, putting themselves at risk of exploitation and trafficking often ending up working on agricultural plantations without compensation as well as in factories, street selling and fishing. Of note is that historically Cameroon has not appeared to accept this as a problem, and whilst, under duress, some arrests have been made for the trafficking of children, few prosecutions have followed. A recent survey of street children in Cameroon indicated that 80.2% were boys and 19.8% girls, with most (77.7%) aged between 15–17 years, though some were much younger. Around 80% stated they were Christians, while under 10% said that they were Muslims with the majority of those coming from Yaoundé. When education was discussed with them, 21.3% said they had never been to school while 77.44% had received some element of primary education whilst only 1.3% had attended secondary school.http://www.african-volunteer.net/cameroon_street_children.html.
The Street2school project will seek to identify and support 5,000 street connected children (boys and girls) within the ages of 6 – 16, to be enrolled into formal education classes during the 2023/2024 school calendar year.
Our proposed methodology is to recruit, train and equip 200 street workers (social workers), who will daily visit street corners in Douala, Melon, Babadjou, Yaounde, Buea, Limbe to identify and engage with 5,000 Street connected children (boys and girls) aged between 6 – 16 years old. The daily engagement by the street workers is aimed at building trust with the street connected children through a voluntary child protection case management-based approach that will be provided in the streets, at street corners identified by the street workers; with accessibility to the street connected children a key priority. Of the 5,000 street connected children targeted to be engaged (identified and registered) by the street workers, at least half of the number of children (50%) who are willing to be enrolled in school, will be referred to the back-to-school component of the project and provided with services and support necessary for enrolment into Government schools. The remaining street connected children who were not enrolled will be referred to other relevant child protection services that will bolster their right to survival, development, protection, and participation. The parents/caregivers of the 2500 children willing to be enrolled to school will be traced, assessed, documented, and provided with positive parenting training and family-based livelihood services to ensure the children stays safe while at home, remain in school and continue learning. All the street connected children will be provided with MHPSS and life skills. The teaching at the right level methodology will be used to improve the literacy and numeracy skills of the children. Comprehensive child protection case management will be provided to all the street connected children.
