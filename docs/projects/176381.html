Project Summary

The project aims to provide integrated, safe, equitable, and dignified access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) services to IDPs, host communities, and returnees in the most affected areas of South Sudan. It builds on IOM's existing WASH service provision systems in camp settings while capitalizing on IOM's well-established emergency and preparedness response mechanism. Where possible, IOM will seek to complement humanitarian activities with transition and recovery elements to assist the establishment of sustainable solutions. Activities will be complemented with GBV risk mitigation and accessibility inclusion, designed in a conflict-sensitive manner and with protection considerations in mind. As the largest direct WASH service provider in camp settings, IOM will continue servicing conflict-affected populations in the Bentiu and Malakal Protection of Civilians (PoCs) sites and Wau's Naivasha Internal Displacement Camp (IDP), benefitting a total of 137,579 people. IOM will ensure the provision of WASH services through the operation and maintenance (O&M) of safe and accessible water supply systems, care and maintenance of accessible and secure sanitation and handwashing facilities, and management of solid and liquid waste. IOM will also continue investing in cost-effective and climate-resilient means of ensuring adequate service provision within these settings by setting up, maintaining, and scaling up water supply solar systems and biogas schemes. To account for the ongoing PoC re-designation and transition process, IOM will focus on transitioning from WASH service provision based on humanitarian assistance towards community-based ownership, through a participatory approach, with a focus on women’s empowerment and on the strategic needs of the most vulnerable. This will be done by: professionalizing the existing cadre of WASH volunteers’ and setting up new and/or build on existing community-based WASH groups; training and providing institutional support to local pump mechanics and representatives of local authorities to gradually transfer O&M responsibilities to the mandated authorities; and. exploring and designing durable solutions based on pro-poor cost recovery principles and market-based programming elements, where possible. As an extension of service provision to urban centers within the vicinity of PoCs and IDP camps, IOM will target 268,486 people. In tandem, IOM will also continue operating its Emergency, Preparedness and Response (EP&R) program, expanding access to WASH services to underserved and vulnerable populations in remote and hard-to-reach locations with high morbidity/mortality related to nutrition and health, and/or where WASH is the main stand-alone driver of the crises, and/or where the population has been affected by a sudden- or rapid-onset disaster (flooding, high food insecurity). Temporary and semi-permanent WASH solutions will be prioritized, and where identified, they will be designed in a climate-resilient way while focusing on instilling community ownership for operation and maintenance. This potentially includes borehole rehabilitation and/or drilling of boreholes, and/or rehabilitation of surface water treatment systems, hygiene promotion, and WASH Non-Food Items (NFIs) distribution, including training and distribution of menstrual hygiene management (MHM) kits to women and girls of menstrual age. IOM will also ensure the sustainability of water supply through the formation and training of water management committees. Geographical targeting will be done through WASH Cluster updates and/or using various assessment findings (IRNAs, NAWG recommendations, WASH Vulnerability maps, etc.). IOM estimates reaching 75,000 people. IOM will ensure that prevention and mitigation of Gender-Based Violence (GBV), Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP), and COVID-19 prevention measures are mainstreamed across the project cycle.

1 - Needs

Ranking 185 out of 189 countries in the 2020 Human Development Index (HDI) [ ], South Sudan's context remains complex, with socio-economic development being severely curtailed by several compounding crises (conflict, transition in service provision, public health emergencies, and climate change). Although symbolic advances towards political stability have taken place, the peace process remained characterized by a marked escalation of organized sub-national and localized violence, accompanied by cattle raiding and revenge attacks between ethnic groups [2]. UNMISS's re-designation of Protection of Civilians (PoC) camps into Internal Displaced Population (IDP) sites in November 2020 also triggered a transition in the service provision mandate; however, it is unlikely that the Government of South Sudan will be in a position to coordinate the humanitarian services delivery due to lack of human resources, with reliance on humanitarian aid likely to continue throughout 2021. The above was further compounded by climate change, whereby abnormal rainfall levels led to widespread flooding and triggered a new wave of displacement. As a result, WASH service provision levels in South Sudan remain amongst the lowest globally, being marked by low WASH infrastructure functionality, high incidence of vandalism and destruction, poor maintenance, and care. Increased displacement will also continue to burden existing WASH infrastructure in displacement sites and host communities where availability is already limited. The latest HNO (2021) estimates that 5.6 million people will be negatively impacted by lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices in 2021, with 310,000 additional refugees and asylum seekers likely to experience a lack of access to sufficient and quality WASH services. Such coverage signals several issues. It highlights the potential for increased vulnerability in accessing the scarce infrastructure, including exposure to protection and GBV risks. For example, only 36 percent of households reported having access to an improved water source in under 30 minutes without facing any protection concerns, and 16 percent of the people using water facilities reported safety concerns (HNO, 2021). High open defecation rates (73 percent at the national level) also expose women, children, and people living with disabilities at a higher risk of violence and discrimination, particularly in IDP sites and other crowded settlements. It also puts populations at risk of cholera and other diarrheal or vector-borne diseases. For example, the quantity of water available per day was below standards in many locations, with 17 percent of people unable to collect enough water for drinking (ibid). Simultaneously, low access to water also indicates the limited capacity of communities to prevent and respond to COVID-19, with 39 percent of people reporting insufficient access to water for handwashing (ibid). PoCs camps and IDP sites continue to lack sufficient access to hygiene and sanitation facilities and are at risk of disease outbreaks due to congestion (ibid), with an estimated population of 139,658 IDPs being put at risk. Poor access to WASH services combined with elevated levels of food insecurity will have a detrimental impact on children's health, leading to malnutrition (ibid) and stunting. Overall, the country's developmental path must continue enabling access to safe, equitable, and dignified access to WASH services while mitigating the risk of gender-based violence and ensuring people with disabilities can access safely to the infrastructures. Sources:[1] UNDP, Human Development Report 2020: Overview, http://hdr.undp.org/en/content/latest-human-development-index-ranking [2] Humanitarian Needs Overview, South Sudan, https://reliefweb.int/report/south-sudan/south-sudan-humanitarian-needs-overview-2021-january-2021, issued in January 2021, page 16

3 - Project indicators

The project's success will be measured through specific activity outputs and overall results, using a mix of quantitative and qualitative data collection methods, captured through a vast network of community-based volunteers directly engaged in IOM's activities, and compiled through Fulcrum (an online management database). For example, when these hygiene promoters conduct information sessions in the community, they will complete regular reports on what was covered in these sessions, what type of feedback was shared, and how many people attended the sessions broken down by age, gender, and disability. Similarly, there will be regular monitoring at water points by the trained water management committees to ensure all water points are being maintained and provide safe water to the community. This information will be shared back to field officers to track and monitor any changes or increased needs in the locations where IOM is working. Similar monitoring of all sanitation activities and infrastructure will be done with community volunteers to promote better sanitation practices in the community and monitor whether these practices are being implemented. IOM will continue to monitor all distributions and tracking how many WASH non-food items are being distributed and to whom and how many households have been reached compared to the targets. Post-distribution monitoring reports will be conducted by the WASH Core Pipeline, considering materials are sourced from here. In PoC sites and IDP camps, KAP (Knowledge Attitude Practice) surveys will be undertaken to improve programming, in addition to household observations and water quality monitoring. Weekly, IOM tracks critical indicators and progress of activities through a WASH indicator matrix and narrative reports, including a Water Quality Database submitted by the field teams. Those reports enable management to identify issues promptly. Outside of the PoC sites and IDP camps, IOM utilizes mobile data collection software to track the project's activities and progress (Fulcrum). All field teams are equipped with mobile devices to collect relevant program information on hygiene promotion activities, NFI distributions, and technical information related to the construction and rehabilitation of WASH facilities. In addition, all means of verification (such as geotagged pictures of the activities or facilities) are submitted through Fulcrum, allowing technical management and supervisors to track the progress and quality of the work remotely and in real-time. WASH indicators are also reported to the WASH Cluster monthly (e.g. 5Ws) to measure standards and assess needs. Overall, data and information gathered will be used to complement regular feedback loop mechanisms (weekly reports and weekly team meetings) as a way of ensuring it shapes programming and allows for corrective measures at an early stage.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

IOM will ensure that beneficiaries will be informed about their rights in accessing humanitarian services, that they are protected against exploitation and that there are reporting mechanisms through which complaints can be done in a safe and secure way. To ensure that, IOM's HRP proposed activity package will be guided by IOM's protection mainstreaming guidelines and the IASC centrality of protection (2013), IOM's Institutional Framework Addressing GBV in Crisis, and the IASC CBV guidelines. IOM has also an operational PSEA system enforced through the PSEA Policy (IN/024) and is an active member of the South Sudan PSEA taskforce at national level; co-leading State level groups in Wau, Bentiu and Abeyi. IOM will also ensure that staff and volunteers are trained on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, in line with IOM's Code of Conduct, and that they will be held accountable. The activities are designed based on the Humanitarian Sphere standards and South Sudan's WASH Cluster Protection Mainstreaming tip sheet. This helps ensure that beneficiaries have access to safe, secure, dignified and sufficient potable water and sanitation facilities. In addition, IOM will provide the UN in South Sudan hotline where complainants are able to report the cases and will train all staff and volunteers on the reporting mechanisms.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Throughout the project cycle, IOM will ensure sub-groups representatives, such as women, girls, men, youth, people with disabilities (PwD), people with special deeds (PSN), elderly and minority groups are fully engaged from the start through specific FGDs and targeted conversations. Aside from their involvement at the earlier stage, IOM will ensure they are fully engaged in conducting safety audits, thus ensuring they are able to access to the services and feel safe. Feedback will be shared through meetings with sub-groups representatives that will be responsible to spread it through their respective groups. In addition, these sub-groups will be informed about the anonymous complaints and feedback mechanisms that will be implemented so they can share their opinion in a safe and secure way.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

IOM promotes community participation and engagement throughout the project cycle in order to increase the ability of affected population to give feedback, have access to adequate and timely information and participate in WASH programs design, implementation and evaluation. In line with the WASH Cluster AAP Checklist, IOM will ensure that communities are fully engaged throughout the assessment and implementation process to ensure the needs are clearly identified, activities are adapted, and beneficiaries feel safe to access services. This process is conducted with the representatives of sub-groups, such as women, girls, men, youth, people with disabilities (PwD), people with special deeds (PSN), elderly and minority groups. IOM will also ensure beneficiaries are fully aware on the duration and the targeted location of the project. In addition, IOM implements an established complaint and feedback mechanism where individuals can reach out to the organization to provide feedback on the intervention and complain on the quality of the work.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

IOM's activities are based on a participatory approach that puts the beneficiaries at the center of its interventions, ensuring their proactive involvement at each stage of the project cycle: from the identification of general WASH needs and those of the most vulnerable populations, right through the analysis, strategic planning, implementation, and monitoring, as well as evaluation and learning. This will be done by collecting community feedback through community entry engagement, disaggregated focus-group discussions, surveys, semi-structured interviews, daily interactions and discussions, community meetings, and daily engagement based on the humanitarian principles, as part of activity implementation and monitoring. IOM will also ensure that feedback from women, PWDs and other minority/under-represented groups is considered, by engaging them separately and in a safe way. Such information is analyzed, collated, and discussed with the project team, thus consolidating feedback loops and assisting field teams, WASH officers and WASH Management taking stock of the activities and making adjustments that lead to evidence-based interventions, tailored by the beneficiaries' themselves.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

To mitigate those risks, IOM will conduct a safety audit with vulnerable groups within the community to determine the safest location and time of day to conduct distributions or build infrastructure. In addition, IOM will liaise with protection actors in specific locations to help identify the barriers to access services especially for persons with disabilities and other vulnerable groups, and determine ways to improve access in a safe, dignified, and accountable manner. When there is no protection partner on ground, IOM forms a body of well-respected individuals (such as teachers, religious leaders, elderly) who supports IOM in ensuring protection concerns are taken into consideration during the entire process of distribution starting from verification/registration up to the distribution. The same body also supports to address any complaints coming during the distribution process (e.g. missing token, missing items, etc.). IOM WASH staff is trained in Protection and GBV risk mitigation and safe and ethical referral of protection cases. As per general practice in IOM, ahead of any WASH deployments, IOM GBV and Protection specialists ensure the referral pathways information for specific areas are shared with the team. WASH teams are then able to share these with the recruited volunteers within targeted locations, who can then, in turn, share with their community members, so that GBV survivors are able to seek medical and psychological support as soon as possible. To address risks in the context of WASH programming in host communities, IOM informs the community leaders about the CFM during community entry dialogues. This information is also shared with direct beneficiaries of program activities. To address risks linked to WASH NFI distribution, IOM will inform the beneficiaries about the CFM during the WASH NFIs registration process. After completing the verification and registration process, IOM conducts a safety audit with the women group and PwD group to understand the most secured location and most suitable time (of the day) for distribution. At the same time, when there is no protection partner on ground, IOM forms a body of well-respected individuals (such as teachers, religious leaders, elderly) who supports IOM in ensuring protection concerns are taken into consideration during the entire process of distribution starting from verification/registration up to the distribution. The same body also supports to address any complaints coming during the distribution process (e.g. missing token, missing items, etc.). To address risks in the context of PoC WASH service delivery, IOM will liaise with respective CCCM agencies to ensure that transparent information dissemination, and clarity of procedures and complaint and feedback mechanisms through which the affected population can regularly express their concerns are shared with beneficiaries. In WASH response at community level, IOM liaises with the highest level of governance body (County administration office/payam administration office, County WASH department, RRC representatives etc.) to explain the mission objectives and geographical coverage. This exercise is done to ensure the governance body understands the geographical coverage that IOM has been mandated to target. As a follow up, for WASH NFI distribution processes, IOM conducts the verification and registration exercise in the locality itself rather than bringing the community to a certain location. Led by IOM staff and supported by the locally hired volunteers, IOM maximizes the number of field visits during the verification exercise, where accessibility is possible. At this point, local level community leaders, women leaders, and vulnerable groups are consulted. Selection criteria for selecting the beneficiaries are well explained to the community leaders. One or two days before the exercise, the community is informed through miking, in collaboration with the community leaders.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

Although IOM's WASH HRP proposal is not aimed at providing protection services, IOM WASH staff is trained in Protection and GBV risk mitigation and safe and ethical referral of protection cases. As per general practice in IOM, ahead of any WASH deployments, IOM GBV and Protection specialists check with the Protection cluster and GBV sub-cluster for protection partners responding to the crisis, and WASH assessments also check contacts for services on the ground. If there are any protection partners providing services, the contacts or available referral pathways are shared with the team for that specific location. WASH teams responding are trained on psychological first aid and also share these contacts with the recruited volunteers within targeted locations. In turn, they can share with their community members so that GBV survivors can seek medical and psychological support as soon as possible.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

With guidance from the National WASH Cluster, IOM's WASH HRP activities will target locations experiencing both high WASH vulnerability (4 and 5) and IPC 3, 4, and 5 levels. Interventions will be done in coordination with other sectors such as Food and Security Livelihood (FSL), Health and Nutrition, to address the humanitarian needs of the most affected population. As part of the response, IOM will increase access to water by rehabilitating or drilling boreholes at community and/or institution level, including cattle trough for livestock; provision of WASH NFI kits to the most vulnerable households; conduct hygiene promotion activities to mitigate the risk of waterborne diseases, COVID-19.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

IOM WASH programming is designed to ensure that WASH services are equally accessible to all, especially the most vulnerable populations. This is ensured through inclusive participation of beneficiaries in program design and implementation and decision-making processes developed by dialoguing with men, women, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, elders and/or others without effective community links. For example, throughout the project cycle, IOM conducts community engagement through focus group discussions, surveys, and semi-structured interviews. This creates a platform for sub-group representatives, such as women, girls, men, youth, people with disabilities (PwD), people with special needs (PSN), elderly, and minority groups in the targeted communities to voice their opinions. These tools are usually used during the distribution of WASH NFIs or as part of safety audits that help inform the selection of distribution sites and locations for the construction and/or improvement of WASH facilities. Throughout these activities, IOM's Protection and GBV specialists are fully engaged, ensuring that processes and final decision-making lead to WASH facilities that are inclusive and adapted to the needs of the most vulnerable populations. Overall, such a feedback loop provides critical inputs to the field teams, officers, and managers, guaranteeing that IOM continues to deliver evidence-based interventions that enhance equitable access to WASH services.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

Good access to WASH services is a critical element to achieve the above, as it helps secure nutritional outcomes since unclean water, inadequate sanitation, and poor hygiene practices are linked to undernutrition. As per activities highlighted in previous sections, IOM's HRP WASH program directly contributes to decreasing the prevalence of acute malnutrition, as its focus is on increasing access to safe drinking water at the community level. For example, IOM will restore broken boreholes at community and institution levels (nutrition sites), by either rehabilitating existing water points and/or installing additional ones in locations with low coverage within IPC 3 and 4 areas. Such risk of flooding be identified, IOM will also build raised (increased height and width) platforms that help reduce infiltration and/or proceed to disinfect flood-affected boreholes with chlorine. To further ensure safe water, IOM will also build fences around the improved boreholes and cattle troughs constructed/rehabilitated to reduce contamination from livestock. Throughout the project implementation period, water quality testing will be conducted at the borehole and household levels. If there is a need to increase free residual chlorine (FRC) at the household level, IOM will train bucket chlorinators positioned at the targeted boreholes to support users with bucket chlorination. Ensuring caretakers have access to continuous safe, equitable and dignified water sources will ensure that children under 5 will also be able to consume safe and treated water while ensuring water availability for hygiene purposes. Increased access to water sources will complement hygiene promotion at the community level, focused on emphasizing disease prevention (WASH-related diseases and C-19), safe excreta management, and handwashing with soap. Where possible, IOM will also work closely with Nutrition partners operating in IPC 3 and 4 locations by improving water points at nutrition sites, providing WASH NFI kits for water treatment and safe handling, as well as storage (buckets/jerrycans), soaps for handwashing practices to SAM and MAM caregivers, and strengthening hygiene messaging on personal hygiene, including babies' feces disposal.
