Project Summary

The eastern regions in Cameroon continue to face a complex and unprecedented humanitarian crisis with growing insecurity in the Lake Chad Basin region, the conflict in the Central African Republic (CAR) and the significant deterioration of food security and nutrition situation. The resurgence of violence in CAR has continued to drive vulnerable households towards the eastern regions of Cameroon, which are host to the largest number of CAR refugees (total 264,578 refugees, UNHCR, September 2018). This influx is exerting significant pressure on natural resources and basic social services in host areas, exacerbating pre-existing vulnerabilities. Therefore, WFP will target total 10,000 vulnerable population to engage in community asset creation and income generating activities that promote resilience and provide opportunities for women and men in vulnerable communities from March to August 2019. Resilience building activities will use food assistance for assets (FFA) to mitigate community stresses so those who engage in this activity will be equal participants, decision-makers and beneficiaries in food (5,000 beneficiaries) and cash-based support (5,000 beneficiaries) to elevate the resilience of local communities from the crisis and shocks.

Needs

Since 2014, Cameroon has severely affected by the Central African crisis that has led to the influx of approx. 250,000 Central African refugees in Adamaoua, East and North regions. The displacements of refugees tend to decrease recently but still the host communities suffer from the shocks from the influx of refugees in addition to the chronic vulnerabilities. The prevalence of food insecurity is 11% in the North region, 9% in the East region, and 7.5% in Adamaoua region (Food Security Monitoring System(FSMS) , Feb 2018). The proportion of households with poor food consumption remained relatively stable in Adamaoua (1%) but still high in the North (9%) and East (5%). In Adamaoua region, the economic vulnerability was particularly notable in February 2018 (25%, of which 12% extremely vulnerable) compared to October 2017 (17%, of which 5% extremely vulnerable). In the East, economic vulnerability deteriorated with 13% of affected households in February 2018 compared to 10% in October 2017. The use of livelihood strategies remains high in the East (30% in February 2018). The livelihoods of the populations of these regions remain precarious since the influx of CAR refugees. Food insecurity also results from the abandonment of agricultural activities by the populations for mining activities, specifically in Kadey, Lom-et-Djerem (East) and Mbéré (Adamaoua) divisions. The insecurity perpetrated by theft and the road cutters and the recurrence of agro-pastoral conflicts also discourage agro-pastoral activities that affect the food insecurity. Therefore, it is important to promote asset creation and income generating activities that will support the host communities, local population, and refugees to recover from the shocks and satisfy their food needs.
