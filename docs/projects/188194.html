Project Summary

Within Salamieh District of Hama Governorate, local markets and economies continue to function despite significant disruptions in supply chains, limited financing, and irregular access to productive assets. Furthermore, the misalignment between labour supply and limited demand has resulted in segments of the population experiencing chronic unemployment. Employment opportunities within vocational trades have emerged as an area of opportunity. Nevertheless, the preparedness of the labour market in terms of skill set has resulted in limited ability to meaningfully engage with these emerging employment sectors. Besides, the enabling environment for business creation has been disrupted since the onset of the crisis.
Despite the multiple layers of challenges placing pressure on local markets, several businesses have emerged filling gaps in social services (e.g. water trucking, communal power distribution) and other market needs (e.g. food services, clothing, etc.). These micro-businesses can capitalize on growing market demand and become important actors within the local labour market. E4R seeks to build the current local economic conditions within Salamieh District to improve the employability of affected people and
vulnerable groups and contribute to the creation of local enterprises. The overall objective of this project is to strengthen coping mechanisms of affected people and vulnerable groups, especially women, youth and People with Disabilities (PwD), through vocational training and business development services. During the life of the project, 300 affected and vulnerable people will be reached, of which 45% and 4% will be female and PwD, respectively. The project target is reflective of the number of unique project beneficiaries and has been determined based on the maximum capacity of vocational training courses as well as the number of new enterprises which can be supported during the life of the project.
Specific Objectives:
• Increase access to market-relevant skills/vocational training for 175 affected people and vulnerable groups, especially for women, youth and PwD. All trainees will be equipped
with kits and tools related to their respective vocation.
• Improve business development and management knowledge for 300 emerging and existing entrepreneurs. In addition to business to business training, all trainees are required to partake in Life Skills courses which include modules on communication, leadership, personal financial management, conflict resolution.
• Support socio-economic establishment and recovery of 80 micro and small-scale enterprises through grants, loans, and asset replacement with a particular focus on vulnerable groups. Upon completion of the entrepreneurship and business management training, individuals will be invited to submit a business plan for funding consideration. In total, 80 business plans will be selected and funded with a blend of grants, loans, and business facilitation services.

4 - Needs

In 2022, 14.6 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 1.2 million 8.9% from 2021. (HNO 2022)– with needs increasingly being exacerbated by economic decline. ERL needs remain large in scale, severity, and complexity across the country, with an estimated 13.8 million people (50% Female) in need of dignifying early recovery and livelihood support (HNO 202). As per the UN Inter-Agency 2020 Covid-19 Socio-Economic Impact Assessment, before the pandemic, 41.9% of households could cover their expenses, while currently, only 9.7% can do so. About two-thirds of households lost one or more income sources, and about half lost 75% to 100% of their income. Salamieh District has a population of 285,987 people, (52.1% Female) 30.3% PWD 11.4% IDPs and 7% returnees. The living cost increased greatly over the last year as the prices of commodities and services have more than doubled while the average income remained the same making it difficult for vulnerable families to afford minimum requirements. According to AKF Syria’s 2020 (SCORES 2021) Rapid Need Assessment conducted in Salamieh District, a total of 37,261 (41990) households/162,995 (176617) individuals (50,35% (51.6%) females) are vulnerable; diversity of income sources remains tenuous with the majority of vulnerable households being engaged in seasonal employment and exposed to protection risks.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will provide vocational and skills training opportunities to 175 vulnerable young individuals (40% females) and equip them with kits related to their respective vocation through AKF established Continuing Education Center (CEC) which offers a variety of market-relevant courses. A 2-week course will be developed on entrepreneurship and business management. 300 individuals will be equipped with the knowledge to generate business ideas, start a business, conduct a market analysis, create and accurately price product offerings and maintain basic accounting records. The target group is 175 CEC trainees and 125 vulnerable individuals interested in starting a micro-enterprise or growing their business. The project will launch a micro seed start-up fund to increase access to financial resources for those individuals Trained individuals will be invited to submit a business plan for funding. 80 business plans will be funded with grants and loans in partnership with the First Microfinance Bank (FMFB). AKF will provide a grant through enterprise-related assets by disbursing grant financing directly tothe entrepreneur and business facilitation services such as mentorship, and legal advice. Process and outcome monitoring activities will include baseline assessments of the target group, rapid labour market assessments, ongoing feedback with project beneficiaries, post-vocational training monitoring, labour market transition monitoring, and FGDs with project stakeholders.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

•Breakdown of social cohesion within the district due to increased sectarianism. Likelihood: Low. Impact: Medium. AKF will ensure diversity by targeting various groups in coordination with CBOs and the inclusion of all stakeholders. •Female beneficiaries may face discrimination due to negative cultural perceptions of work types. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Low. AKF will promote project benefits in the local communities in collaboration with CBOs & LNGOs to raise awareness on gender, social roles, new work types and identify vulnerable individuals willing to take such work. Feedback and Complain mechanism will provide updates on community reaction during the implementation and reduce complaining barriers preventing female beneficiaries from raising their concerns.•Gender-related norms may limit women's participation in some works. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium. AKF will ensure community participation through engaged LNGOs, CBOs, villages committees members, and female families in awareness sessions on gender, social roles, and positive results of females participation in this kind of work. AKF will ensure females are comfortable by choosing what they prefer. AKF-Syr will maintain regular monitoring of women working environment and active feedback and complaint mechanism.•LNGOs accused of bias in selecting beneficiaries, Likelihood: High, Impact: Medium. AKF will manage the selection process to ensure precise implementation based on the selection criteria.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk 1: There is a risk that the project activity may lead to safeguarding issues and might expose beneficiaries to sexual abuse or social stigma. Risk 2: The project activities time and locations might be unsafe for vulnerable groups, especially women. In line with AKF safeguarding policy, manual and standards, the project PSEA strategy will ensure the following mitigation measures are in place: • Train all project staff on safeguarding policies. • Ensure all involved partners adhere to AKF's Safeguarding Policy and Code of Conduct • Develop gender-responsive referral pathways and ensure project beneficiaries and vulnerable groups are aware of safeguarding policies and can access reporting mechanisms. • All data collection will consciously abide by robust ethical standards, guided by AKF’s Codes of Conduct and Safeguarding Policies. •Conduct an assessment of safety aspects in spaces used for activities implementation and the surrounding environment. The project will adopt measures and mechanisms to protect beneficiaries and hold superiors and subordinates accountable. AKF follows specific processes and procedures to integrate safeguarding in the Project Cycle. These are: • Determine expected safeguarding risk using (Risk Register tool) • Determine safeguarding measures to reduce the risks through (mitigation plan and indicators). • Evaluating safeguarding at the project Cycle before, during and after implementing the project, using Project Cycle Checklist.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk1: The deterioration of the security situation limiting the movement of staff and beneficiaries. Mitigation: AKF regularly monitors the regional and local security situation using a variety of primary and secondary sources. If the mobility of staff and beneficiaries in rural areas within Salamieh District is deemed high risk, AKF will delay the implementation of activities until the situation is reassessed and determined safe for travel and implementation. Risk2: Misalignment between labour supply and demand widening. Mitigation: Conducting a rapid labour market assessment to validate the current and future selection of vocations will mitigate any potential labour market gaps widening. Risk 3: Low desire to start micro-enterprises. Mitigation: Given the context, the decision to start an enterprise takes into account several factors. Individuals who have completed the entrepreneurship and business management training may wish to not start an enterprise.
