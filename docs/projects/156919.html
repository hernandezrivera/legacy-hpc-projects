Project Summary

Mercy Corps (MC) will respond to priority needs with life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI assistance across the governorates of Al-Hasakeh, Aleppo, Ar-Raqqa, Deir-ez-Zor and Idleb.
MC will improve access to dignified shelter through rehabilitation of unsafe shelters. MC will take into account home ownership along with vulnerability criteria to ensure HLP rights are considered, and vulnerable HHs are not further displaced or taken advantage of after receiving assistance. Shelter interventions will focus on small scale rehabilitation upgrades, including repairs and measures to enhance security and privacy. MC will further provide NFI and/or winterisation kits when needs arise. This may include: Winter kits, Clothing Kits, Hygiene Kits, Dignity Kits, New Arrival Kits, Infant Kits and Basic Shelter Kits

Needs

NES: Administered largely by the Kurdish Self Administration (KSA), NES contains pockets under Islamic State in Iraq and ash-Sham (ISIS) control. The large coalition presence is signaling a reduction, with the potential to destabilize a delicate equilibrium between conflict actors which has been an enabler of humanitarian access since 2017. Ongoing clashes in DEZ continue to cause periodic access problems. Tens of thousands of IDPs remain in camps and informal sites, unable to return to their homes. Across Syria, one in three households is now headed by women, which highlights different needs. Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) and large-scale destruction pose a serious risk to the physical safety of civilians, housing, land and property (HLP) and for the restoration of basic services.Destruction of homes and infrastructure has resulted in acute unmet shelter needs with only 4 INGOs currently providing shelter rehabilitation in Tabqa, Ar-Raqqa, Kisreh and Menbij. According to MCs MSNA, 35% of assessed populations (including 12% female-headed HHs) in Ar-Raqqa City and 13% of communities in DEZ live in damaged shelters or tents, with 55% of the host population (including 4% female-headed households) living in partially damaged houses. The majority do not have the financial resources for shelter repair. NFI’s remain a critical need, particularly during winter months with an average of only 36% of camp residents reporting having blankets and only 21% reporting having winter clothes. Every season, the lack of heaters and fuel keeps households unprepared for the cold winter months. NWS:With multiple waves of IDPs, humanitarian conditions deteriorated in 2018. The establishment of the demilitarised zone (DMZ) in Sep 2018 saw the numbers and frequency of displacement subside, however, inter-AOG conflict in January 2019 has the potential to further de-stabilize the area. According to the HAT’s Dec 2018 Report, “The DMZ Agreement”, there is likely to be a partial or complete breakdown in the political process if key provisions are not fully implemented, leading to a surge in IDP movementBoth the HNO and the shelter cluster identify shelter NFIs as a critical gap. Access to shelters is very limited and the hosting capacity of local communities has already been overstretched. The most common problems are lack of insulation from the cold, leaking roofs, lack of privacy and inadequate security. Over 65% of households cannot afford to repair their shelters due to the costs for repairs and materials. 90% of the communities in Atareb, 60% in Daret Azza and 65% in Dana, report an insufficient number of shelters. MCs assessment (June 2018) in 87 IDP sites in Jabal Saman and Al Mara Districts highlighted their poor conditions. Over 66% of the sites received new IDPs in 2018 without the capacity to absorb them. 81% of the communities in Atareb report the lack of privacy, in particular for women and girls, as a major protection challenge. A shelter response that is flexible encompassing non-food item assistance, shelter repair and winterization remains appropriate and warranted.

Implementation of activities

1.1 Provision of core and essential NFIs (e.g. mattress/mat, plastic sheet, blankets, jerry can, kitchen set, solar lamps, fuel; including cash/voucher for these items): MC will provide one-off, in-kind assistance to 200 newly displaced HHs (1,200 individuals) within 72 hours of displacement (access and availability permitting). In NWS, all the selected HHs will receive a kit comprised of: 1) new arrival kit; 2) hygiene kit; and 3) kitchen set. The composition of the kits is in line with SNFI cluster guidance and take into consideration age and gender considerations. Distribution is carried out with local organisations, who support MC with warehousing, mobilisation of the beneficiaries, set up of the distribution sites and transportation of the kits, with MC’s Program, M&E and Access staff present during all distributions.1.2 Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs and shelter assistance (e.g. winter clothing, fuel, winter-specific shelter upgrades; including cash/voucher for these items): MC will provide winter kits comprising of mattresses, blankets, floor mats, tarpaulin and a solar lamp, in line SNFI cluster guidance. MC will also provide winter clothing., taking into consideration age and gender. Target locations will be identified through an MSNA. An economic vulnerability assessment will ensure the most vulnerable are selected. MC will provide winter NFIs through the most suitable modality (cash/voucher/in-kind) based on the targeted areas and market functionality.1.3 Provision of life-saving and life-sustaining shelter (e.g. tent installation in camps, transit sites and spontaneous settlements with associated infrastructure, distribution of emergency shelter materials and kits; rental assistance, including cash/voucher for these items): MC will provide emergency shelter kits to recently displaced HHs and/or protracted displaced HHs living in informal settlements. The emergency shelter kit will answer acute shelter needs - either first phase response to displacement, or in response to a prolonged sub-standard shelter situation or additional winter needs, where no other options are available. MC will procure and distribute emergency shelter kits, consisting of materials and items, which enable flexible use during the displacement or recovery phase, and in case of multiple displacements. Kit content is in line with SNFI Cluster guidance for emergency shelter i.e. Tarpaulin sheet, floor insulation mat, nails and hammer, etc.1.4 Rehabilitation/upgrading of Life-saving and life-sustaining shelter spaces in collective centres, unfinished buildings, spontaneous settlements, and/or other emergency spaces (including: in-kind, cash, voucher, physical repair, etc.) and 2.1 Support to sustainably repair/rehabilitate housing and related community/public infrastructure and facilities to owners/tenants/host families (can include: materials, cash, voucher, cash-for-work, labour, etc.): MC will assist HHs with small scale rehabilitation upgrades for individual shelters, informal settlements and collective shelters. The rehabilitation works will be dependent on an engineering survey and consultation with the HH beneficiaries (female/male). BoQs will be drawn up for each shelter. Shelter repairs will focus on light rehabilitation. Typically, the type of emergency repairs includes provision of wooden doors to separate rooms and provide security and privacy, secure windows, and repairs of sanitation facilities and water connections and electrical works. As part of local assessments, MC will coordinate with local councils, local and international NGOs to ensure that HLP rights are respected, documented, and codified. The modality of shelter support many vary; in some locations MC will provide cash/vouchers for HHs to purchase shelter inputs and local masons will provide technical support, whereas in other locations rehabilitation works will be carried out through contractors.

Protection Risk Analysis

Mercy Corps Syria has rolled out a protection risks analysis tool for staff and partners since 2018, as part of the protection mainstreaming strategies. Noting that the Mercy Corps submission is reflective of protection programming incorporated into several donor grants (often multi-sector), each project and key interventions will have a protection risk analysis. The principle of risk analysis and protection assessment is a corner stone to Mercy Corps protection mainstreaming. Mercy Corps staff and partners will be trained on key principles, staff will be gender balanced and complaints followed up on. The following illustrates the main protection risks associated with this program, and mitigating measures that will be taken by Mercy Corps. Mercy Corps acknowledges that there are a significant number of protection risks that individuals and communities can face as a result of poor programming or programming in a difficult environment. This does not capture them all.1. Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers/partners: Mercy Corps monitoring and evaluation systems include accountability frameworks, and Mercy Corps has a beneficiary complaints mechanism. Mercy Corps is committed to the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and Child Safeguarding through appropriate policies (Code of Conduct) which are enforced through on-boarding and training to all staff, suppliers and partners. Distribution are carefully planned to ensure they are done at times and in places that are safe and accessible to women and girls, as well as the elderly and people with disabilities, and that any barriers to access as a result of gender norms or physical ability are taken into account when planning for distributions.2. Programming exacerbates community tensions due to perceived discrimination/favouritism: Mercy Corps upholds a conflict sensitive and DNH approach throughout programming. MC utilizes appropriate and transparent targeting techniques, prioritising the most vulnerable and those at risk, ensuring services will be delivered without discrimination, ensuring inclusion of minorities. Mercy Corps has invested heavily in the tools and capacity to understand the conflict and power dynamics of where we work through regular analysis at the of communities through to regional geopolitical politics to ensure all interventions are conflict sensitive and in line with a DNH approach. Our approach is grounded in analysis and, accountability (ensuring transparent and inclusive targeting, participatory design, and transparent feedback and complaints mechanisms. Mercy Corps staff and partners are representative of the demography in each area and have close community ties which facilitates community acceptance and aids accountability. Staff and partners are trained to ensure conflict sensitivity approaches are mainstreamed across interventions.3. Local authorities restrict activities of partners due to lack of support for protection interventions. Similarly, where programming is sensitive to authorities but a priority for local communities, Mercy Corps will endeavour to engage with local actors to increase opportunities for operating. 4. Persons without documents refused assistance: As part of a protection mainstreamed intervention, Mercy Corps does not require beneficiaries of protection or non-protection projects to possess national ID documents. In line with protection cluster guidelines, information can be triangulated using local verification processes. Protection interventions can increase stigma and risk to beneficiaries. Mercy Corps support project designs which mitigate risk to beneficiaries. Mercy Corps also is supporting projects targeting youth and adolescents and persons with disabilities who are historically excluded from target interventions.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Mercy Corps closely coordinates our contingency planning alongside the Humanitarian Access Team (HAT) which is hosted by Mercy Corps. Along with INSO, OCHA and cluster analysis, Mercy Corps operational planning strives to minimise risk to beneficiaries, partners and staff. 2019 is a challenging operational context in northern Syria.The humanitarian situation in NWS deteriorated in 2018. The establishment of the demilitarised zone (DMZ) in Sep 2018 saw the numbers and frequency of displacement subside, however, inter-AOG conflict in January 2019 has the potential to further de-stabilize the area. Administered largely by the Kurdish Self Administration (KSA), NES contains pockets under Islamic State in Iraq and ash-Sham (ISIS) control. The large coalition presence is signalling a reduction, with the potential to destabilize a delicate equilibrium between conflict actors which has been an enabler of humanitarian access since 2017. Ongoing clashes in DEZ continue to cause periodic access problems. Tens of thousands of IDPs remain in camps and informal sites, unable to return to their homes. This has meant that there is questions of operational viability across northern Syria in 2019. Mercy Corps remains in close coordination with our donors and partners for appropriate contingency planning to enable and adaptable programming.
