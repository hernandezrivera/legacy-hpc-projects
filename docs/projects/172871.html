Project Summary

This project is focusing on addressing the needs of school children, promoting community (men and women) protection from GBV and providing physical safety and psycho-social wellbeing support to boys and girls in conflict-affected areas of West Darfur( Sirba, Bida, Habila, Forbringa, Kulbus, Genina and Kerenik), Central Darfur (Rokero,Golo, Nertiti, Um Dukhum, Bendasi and Zalingi) and Blue Nil (,Damazine, Kumruk, Bau, Roseires and Geissan) localities. The project targets 178,000 people including 38377 men, 39943 women, 50837 girls, 48843 boys.
Sudan has one of the largest numbers of out-of-school children in the Middle East and North Africa region. UNICEF estimated that over three million children aged 5-13 years are not attending school. Seventy-six percent of primary age children attend school, but in secondary school, that figure drops to 28 percent. At 43 percent, Blue Nile is the worst performing state for children who are out of primary school. The illiteracy rate in Sudan stood at 50 percent for women and 30 percent for men in 2016. Women and girls face major barriers to education. An estimated 49 percent of girls in Sudan do not attend school. Women are not protected against discrimination in classrooms, which subjects them to fewer opportunities and maltreatment. Sudanese society also adheres to gender norms that women only belong in the house to care for children and undertake domestic responsibilities. These stereotypes have affected women’s access to higher education because they are typically culturally bound to domestic and maternal duties.
Conflict, a lack of awareness about the importance of education, and chronic under-development all contribute to the poor schooling of boys and girls in Sudan. Many teachers in Sudan were found to be untrained, unsupervised, and unequally distributed between rural and urban areas. While on some measures, Sudan has recorded steady progress in education, from an increase in primary school attendance to gender parity in primary classrooms it is clear that much work remains to be done.
Continued fighting and insecurity in the Blue Nile, West, and Central Darfur has resulted in displacement, livelihood disruption, and has contributed to high dropout rates in education. Children and youth have been made increasingly vulnerable, as they have discontinued their academic activities to search for food and safety, with increased exposure to protection risks such as sexual violence, child labor, early marriage, and recruitment into armed groups. Thousands of children were separated from their families and caregivers and have had limited access to basic social services, which left an enormous emotional impact on children. Gender-based violence (GBV) – including rape, sexual assault, harassment, domestic violence and forced marriage, have been also a problem in Sudan. According to one report, gender-based violence (GBV) threatens the overall well-being of the population in Sudan, particularly women and children. Gender-Based Violence Increase in Sudan During COVID Lockdown, which put negative consequences in the life of the victims. Under former President Omar Al Bashir’s 30-year rule, there were no laws to protect women and girls, which also exposed them to abuse within their families. GBV has not only negative consequences for those who experience violence, but also has a negative impact on efforts to promote peace, resilience, economic prosperity for women/girls and men/ boys, and for development across all sectors. This needs collaborative effort at all levels and intentional planning of protection intervention across all the sectors. This project will address the existing gaps in education and protection in the three states selected localities.
WR has protection and GBV programing experience globally and in the country. WR has gender and protection focal person and protection coordinator who are responsible for implementation, monitoring and reporting of protection activities.

1 - Needs

According to EMIS-based annual statistics from MoE book, 2017/2018,the gross enrolment rate in basic education (GER) in West Darfur is 68.5% which is below the national average of 72.5%. There is a significant difference in the enrolment of girls (61.4%) and boys (75.7), with only 43% net intake rate for Grade 1 students. The performance of grade eight exams was at 70%, which is also below the national average. Furthermore, West Darfur state has one of the highest reported number of children in grade 3, 59%, who are not able to read. The low performance in West Darfur can partly be explained by the highest pupil-teacher ratio which leaves 84,907 children of school age 6-13 out of school with only 18,529 children are provided education opportunities at Alternate Learning Program (ALP) centers. The movement from conflict-affected areas to relatively safer towns such as Al Gineina, Kerenieik, Forbrnga and Habila has created a situation of overcrowded classrooms that range between 75 to 85 students per class with a high pupil to teacher ratio. The low performance of the education sector in West Darfur has been further exacerbated by internal population displacements and an influx of refugees returning from Chad. It is worth mentioning that 5 localities have a GER of less than 60% (Habila, Bida , Forobranga, Kulbus ,Sirba ) and 5 are affected with conflicts and humanitarian situations. Two of the localities (Habila and Kulbus) have a very low GER of 32% and a very high dropout rate among localities. As per the Ministry of Education records in the Blue Nile, the total number of children in Blue Nile State is 292,737 58% of those (121,770 children) are out of school because of the war between the Government and the SPLM-N, and due to inflation rates and family income (school fees). In all three states, the education facilities lack access to protective learning spaces, latrines, and water and school materials. Child protection challenges are prominent in all states of Sudan. Children in Sudan experience widespread violence. Family and Child Protection Units (FCPU) reported over 30,000 cases of violence, abuse, and/or exploitation in 2016, with 60 percent of those cases involving sexual violence. Grave violations against children, including killing, maiming, and sexual violence, continue to be documented in the context of ongoing conflicts in Sudan. Additionally, 64 percent of children aged 1–14 years old are victims of violent discipline, equally divided between psychological and physical violence, according to MICS 2014. Suicide and bullying among adolescents is an emerging issue for which limited data exists. More than 33 percent of children under-five are not registered at birth in Sudan, a situation that has improved only slightly since 2010. Children who do not have birth certificates face difficulties registering for school, retracing family if separated, assessing their age if they are in contact with the law, and determining their age for marriage.The GBV activities mainly focus in WD ( Bida,Habila, Genina and Kerenik) localities, CD( Gharb Jabal marrah,Um Dukhum and West Jabal Marrah) localities and Blue Nile( Baw, Al Kurmuk, Al Rusayri and Gesan) localities.
