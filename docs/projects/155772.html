Project Summary

Between 2017 and 2018, some 60,121 families have returned to areas of origin in five tribal districts of KP . This translates into a population of 162,326 children in age-bracket of 3-18. The erstwhile FATA EMIS report for 2016-17 shows that more than one million (1,038,933) children in the age group of 04-14 are out of school in the tribal areas. The overall drop-out rate in Government Primary Schools over a cycle of 6 years is 76% (74% Boys, 80% Girls). Per the FATA Vulnerability Assessment (August-September 2017), half of the respondents expressed dissatisfaction with the quality of education due to lack of basic facilities, lack of teachers, teaching materials and damage to school infrastructure. In terms of damaged infrastructure, the RRU data shows that 943 educational institutions were partially and fully damaged in five tribal districts.
Under the project, UNICEF will support the returnee children to resume education in a safe and protective environment by addressing the unmet needs in education for some 80,000 school going children in all merged districts of erstwhile FATA, preferably in the newly returned areas of Khyber, Kurram, Orakzai, North and South Waziristan. The children in return areas need access to safe, inclusive, and quality education to reduce the risk of exploitation, establishing a routine and normalcy through psycho-social support, providing a child friendly environment and equipping them with life skills. The available data indicates that need to increase girls’ access to education institutions in the merged districts. To increase the girls’ access, it is pivotal to ensure female teachers’ attendance, quality of education, availability of supplies and engagement of community especially women in TIJs/School Management Committees. UNICEF will provide prefabricated school buildings, develop the capacity of Education staff, provide school supplies, strengthen TIJs/School Management Committees, and support Alternate Learning Program.

Activities

Under the project, UNICEF will support the returnee children to resume education in a safe and protective environment by addressing the unmet needs in education for some 80,000 school going children in all merged districts of erstwhile FATA, preferably in the newly returned areas of Khyber, Kurram, Orakzai, North and South Waziristan. The children in return areas need access to safe, inclusive, and quality education to reduce the risk of exploitation, establishing a routine and normalcy through psycho-social support, providing a child friendly environment and equipping them with life skills. The available data indicates that need to increase girls’ access to education institutions in the merged districts. To increase the girls’ access, it is pivotal to ensure female teachers’ attendance, quality of education, availability of supplies and engagement of community especially women in TIJs/School Management Committees. UNICEF will provide prefabricated school buildings, develop the capacity of Education staff, provide school supplies, strengthen TIJs/School Management Committees, and support Alternate Learning Program. Activities: The project will support 80,000 children, including 44,355, girls through the following package of interventions: 1. Construction of 100 prefabricated classrooms at 50 fully damaged schools.2. Rapid community based assessment of school-going and non-school-going children, which would contribute to strengthening the school enrolment campaigns at beginning of the academic session in April 2019.3. Provision of essential school supplies including school in a box kits, recreation kits, student learning kits and black-boards.4. Strengthening/Reactivation and strengthening of approximately 100 TIJs, including 42 women TIJs; One-day training of TIJ members on their roles and responsibilities to strengthen school-community linkages. 5. Capacity Building of 200 teachers, including 84 women teachers on psychosocial support and child friendly teaching.6. Provision of Alternate Learning Programme for over-age dropped out children. 50 prefabricated structures will be established to provide safe and child friendly environment to the children of merged districts. Schools/space for prefabricated will be identified and finalized with consultation of education department and district education office and TIJs. Each prefabricated structure will have two class rooms along with veranda and washroom which will accommodate 50-70 students per class room. Schools will be selected in coordination on the line departments. The proposed project intervention will support 55% girls and 45% boys. The proposed project will provide supplies to 100 schools (45 Boys & 55 Girls) which will benefit 80,000 children, including 44,355, girls. The standard School in a box (teaching materials) two to each of the targeted school will be provided. The project proposal will also provide training on child friendly teaching, psycho -social support, The training program will be focused on capacity building of teachers’ knowledge, skills to use supplies provided by UNICEF and to give effective psycho-social support to the students. Strengthening of 100 Talimi Islahi Jirga (45 male TIJs’ and 55 female) is one of the key component of the project. Close coordination will be maintained with Education Department, and district education offices in all planned activities. It was noted that there is a need for initiating a fast track or second-chance condensed education programme to support and mainstream over-age children, particularly girls, complete their primary/secondary education. Therefore, Alternate Learning Program will be initiated in the merged districts with special focus on girls’ education. All the stakeholders including; education department, district education office, TIJs’, District Administration, schools’ management, teachers and students will be engaged for effective implementation of the project activities.
