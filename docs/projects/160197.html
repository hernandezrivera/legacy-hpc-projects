Project Summary

The main purpose of this project is to increase access to and provide curative, preventive and promotive healthcare services with focus on general outpatient consultations for children, women and men; antenatal and postnatal care services, delivery in health facilities; immunization of children under five and pregnant women to mitigate high burden of vaccine-preventable diseases. The project also aims at providing mental and psycho-social services to mentally challenged people and victims of sexual and gender-based violence including management of rape cases. In addition, the project will also provide and ensure equal access to essential health commodities, basic medical equipment, health education using IEBC materials and laboratory services to diagnose, treat and reduce the high morbidity and mortality rates caused by the common killer diseases such Malaria, diarrheal diseases, malnutrition in the target population. Services will be delivered in static health facilities and outreach campaigns will take place in mobile clinics to all the population in need including individuals in hard-to-reach areas. Referral system will be strengthened to improve provision of specialize health services at different levels of healthcare facilities. In addition, the project will employ integrated approach with water and sanitation and lives-saving nutrition activities to increase access to other basic services such as clean water, sanitation and hygiene facilities in health facilities.
Furthermore, the project will also strengthen the capacity of skilled health workers through refresher training to improve the delivery of quality health services to the target population. Monitoring and evaluation framework will be developed to monitor the performance of the project. Regular collection of data and reporting will be clearly planned and followed throughout the project cycle. Surveys, periodic assessments and sharing of findings will be established.

1 - Needs

South Sudan has suffered enormously from complex humanitarian emergencies resulting from protracted armed conflict since 2013. Central Equatoria and especially Yei, Morobo and Kajokeji counties were severely affected between 2016 and 2018. Consequently, the population of three counties was displaced into refugee and internally displaced people (IDP) camps. Most infrastructure was destroyed, personnel displaced and health services were completely shut down in Morobo, Kajokeji and some parts of Yei County. Recently, the country has generally witnessed relative reduction in armed conflict. Central Equatoria region is currently secure and stable following the signing of the Revitalized Peace Agreement on 12 September 2018 (ACCORD Conflict Trends 2018). United Nations Mission in South Sudan (UNMISS) has also stepped patrols in Yei, Morobo and Kajokeji County (United Nations Peace Keeping, 2018) resulting in perceived peace and stability in the region. As the result, refugees and internally displaced persons are returning home. According to Humanitarian Needs Overview (2019), a total of over 100,000 returnees have come back to their homes; Yei (75,000), Morobo (15,800) and Kajokeji County (13,800). The returning population is composed mainly of children, men and women of different age groups and they come with enormous humanitarian needs that include access to medicines, skilled health personnel, antenatal and postnatal services; immunization, referral, reliable diagnostic equipment and health information. However, availability and accessibility to the basic healthcare services is very limited in Yei, Morobo and Kajokeji County. Availability of healthcare services is reported at 27% in Morobo, 53% in Yei and only 24% in Kajokeji (Health Service Functionality Monthly Bulletin in August 2019). This means the population in the outskirts of the towns has no access to basic healthcare services. Accessibility is further affected by the lack of infrastructure and poor status of the existing health facilities. In addition, more than 50% of the functional health facilities are run by the county health departments (CHDs) with limited medicines, inadequate medical commodities and lack of financial support leading to frequent stock-outs and interruption of essential service delivery (World Health Organization, 2019). Again, the increasing demand for healthcare resources including skilled health personnel are further constrained by the increasing population of returnees and host communities. Epidemiological Week 36 (2019) documented that up to 2,500 people accessed health services (IDSR Annexes Week 36, 2019), indicating increase from 1,000 people in the previous weeks and months (IDSR Annexes Week 16, 2019). This means the demand for essential healthcare services among the returnees and host population of Yei, Morobo and Kajokeji is exponentially increasing (REACH - Situation Overview: Greater Equatoria, South Sudan January - March 2019). The common health conditions affecting the target population are malaria, diarrheal diseases and acute respiratory infections (ARI) (IDSR Reports, 2019). High rates of malnutrition, limited access to clean drinking water and gender-based violence exacerbate vulnerability to killer diseases (REACH Assessments, 2019). To reduce excessive mortality and mortality rates among the returnees and host population of Yei, Morobo and Kajikeji County, provision of quality and accessible healthcare services is therefore needed.

2 - Activities

- Screening of under 5 children for malnutrition - Screening of pregnant and lactating women for malnutrition - Rapid testing for malaria for under 5 and pregnant women - Screening for Tetanus vaccination of pregnancy - Screening for vaccination needs to all under 5 - Conduct outpatient consultations for common diseases - Provision of antenatal care - Conduct assisted uncomplicated/normal deliveries - Referral of complicated deliveries- Conduct routine EPI and mass vaccination campaigns- Provide basic dressing for war-wounded - Conduct treatment of sexually transmitted infections (STIs) and wounds from sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) - Establishment of ORS Corner - Establish a medical waste management system at health facilities - Distribute Folic Acid for pregnant women - Provide Vitamin A supplementation for under 5 children - Provide malaria prophylaxis for pregnant women - Provision of deworming treatment for under 5 children - Distribution of Condoms - Refresher training of skilled birth attendants - Conduct Education/ promotion of breastfeeding - Conduct health education for health-seeking behaviour - Conduct health education on the use of clean water and sanitary facilities - Awareness-raising of the availability of services for SGBV survivors, TB and people living with HIV - Mobilization for vaccination campaigns - Establishment of surveillance/EWARN system in supported health facilities - Establish Sentinel sites of the early warning system of epidemic-prone diseases, outbreak response (EWARS) - Provision of essential healthcare commodities

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes. Part of the information to be disseminated as awareness will involve informing beneficiaries about their rights in health. Other partners including inter-news are running projects that support information sharing on rights including for health. By working closely with the government and the local community leaders, the project will ensure that the rights to health for the beneficiaries will be respected and acted upon in case of abuse. Examples of the rights include; blocking access to healthcare by armed personnel, misuse of assets such as laboratory equipment, diverging medical supplies for financial gains meant for the treatment of patients for free.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project is majorly designed to support more of the returnees who were previously displaced wither to refugee camps or IDP camps. These project will provide services to these people as they settle in their home areas in order to reduce morbidity and mortality.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

All health services will be provided with overall involvement of the national and state ministries of health. Direct link will be established during support supervision and supply chain management through the County Health Department systems. Reports will be shared through the national reporting lines that is the DHIS2. The Boma Health Initiative introduced by the government enhances community level healthcare. Training for community level engagements and other levels will be done using national guidelines to fit in to the national systems. The project therefore directly linked to government systems through these strategies.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is aligned with national objectives such that, the national guidelines including the Expanded Programme on Immunization, the Basic Package for Primary and secondary Healthcare and others will be employed. These documents were developed after a though analysis of the national priorities and strategies to be employed. The project will employ these documents to inform standards such as dosages to be administered, the line of treatment for certain diseases, the target age groups for immunization and adopting the policies on antenatal care including testing and counseling.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The anticipated/looming peace deal comes with reduced conflict and violence especially against children and women by armed personnel. In addition, UNMISS has increased patrols in the areas of project implementation. The government has established law enforcement sites that could be utilized to seek legal procedures against perpetrators of violence. All these dynamics provide increased access to protection. Coupled with increased messaging through the different platforms especially the internees community radio network in the areas, the protection environment will be adequate for the implementation of the project.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, the project will be implemented with close consideration of the government personnel and systems in place. Support supervision visits, training sessions and assessments will be conducted together with government personnel. Planning and coordination meetings and workshops will be done together with CHD personnel to orient them on the project to promote sustainability. Proper handover including of assets in accordance with polices will be done.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Contamination of targeted project areas with explosive hazardsThe project locations are areas previously active for fighting. There is a possibility of UXOs and remnants of dangerous materials. The project will work with mine groups and the national army to ensure that areas to be visited for implementation are cleared by experts before establishment of sites in the locations.Beneficiaries risk exposure to violence during registration/distributionWhere there is armed conflict, the possibility of violence including gender-based violence is high. Consultations and working in close coordination with bodies that handle cases related to violence will increase vigilance and holding to account perpetrators. The project will actively engage the police and other bodies in the implementation phase of the project to reduce such cases. Working hours will be restricted to hours when such cases are reduced or not eminent. Project locations will be established closer to where the majority of the population is located to reduce risks.Forced taxation of humanitarian assistanceHumanitarian work is often targeted for gains by armed groups. Working in coordination with UNOCHA and Relief and Rehabilitation commission for South Sudan will ensure all cases are reported and acted upon that will reduce re-occurrence.
