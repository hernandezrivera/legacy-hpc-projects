Project Summary

Exacerbated by the aftermath of devastating earthquakes, amidst this adversity, women and girls endure significant hardships but also display remarkable resilience (Voices from Syria 2023). The prevalence of GBV shows an increased tendency due to widespread displacement and the breakdown of social structures. Women and girls face heightened risks of violence, exploitation and abuse. From April - November 2023, RI registered 76 GBV cases in NWS, 21% of which were emotional/psychological abuse; 62% physical assault, including domestic violence; 8% denial of resources; 1% sexual assault; and 1% rape. Of the cases, 92% reported the offender was a male family member, with 90% of these cases including the husband as the perpetrator. Furthermore, results within the CP Assessment carried out in August 2023, led by CP Area Of Responsibility (AOR), indicate a rise in child labor, behavioral and psychological problems, child marriage, violence against children and family separation, which risks have been further amplified by both the earthquake and the issue regarding renewal of cross bording resolution.
Considering the identified protection needs in NWS, RI will provide comprehensive protection services in response to GBV and CP risks . Protection services will be implemented through 4 Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSSs), one 1 stop center and 7 protection teams deployed in RI supported health facilities. GBV response services for survivors and women and girls at risk will include case management, focused PSS, referrals, and Psychological First Aid (PFA) while GBV prevention includes outreach, awareness-raising through the GBV AoR toolkit, and vocational training. CP services will include CP case management and CP community-based activities.
Protection risks identified for the project are: Risk 1: Societal norms, gender inequality, and cultural barriers may cause loss of support from local communities and authorities for GBV programming and impede access to services for women and girls. To mitigate this risk, the project will regularly consult communities, provide awareness sessions and enhance integration of protection and health services Risk 2: Economic instability and lack of sustainable livelihood options present a significant risk, potentially limiting the project's success in improving the well-being of vulnerable groups. Mitigation measures will involve close coordination with livelihood and economic empowerment service providers to facilitate referrals, supported by an updated service mapping.
In terms of safeguarding , RI has zero tolerance toward safeguarding incidents with its robust system, enabling the safe submission and handling of sensitive information, and assuring that service-users/staff are aware of how to report a violation of safeguarding or Code of Conduct (CoC) provisions employs a zero-tolerance policy for any harm or abuse to clients perpetrated by our staff or partners . SEA risks identified : R1: Safeguarding incidents could increase due to a rise in negative coping mechanisms as living conditions deteriorate. Mitigation measures: ensure participants at RI activities are aware where to disclose safeguarding incidents; mainstream PSEA across key program activities; refresh training session for all RI staff.
Risks, assumptions and mitigation measures
Assumptions: CFSs and WGSSs are located in relatively safe areas; communities will engage in CP and GBV program activities. Risk1: deterioration of the security situation due to conflict escalation including armed Clashes, arrest and detention, shelling/artillery, VBIEDs/IEDs threatening RI’s staff and service users. Mitigation: RI team will follow the contingency plan prepared based on the situation development, considering staff safety and clear communication with management.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

RI will support 5 WGSSs and 1 one stop center in the following subdistricts: Teftanaz, Harbanoush, Darkoush, in Idleb, Azaz and Turkman Bareh in Aleppo. RI will continue to support the one stop center in Sarmada in Idleb that includes RH basic services in addition to GBV and CP specialized services. Additionally, RI have protection teams presents in some of supported health facilities to respond and provide psychological support to the GBV survivors and other women and girls at risks. RI will coordinate with other actors through the referral pathway developed by GBV AoR and complementing the localized service mapping will be conducted regularly by the team in the target areas to ensure safe referral and avoid duplication in service provision. RI will integrate awareness-raising for men and boys and psychoeducation to address behavioral problems to enhance their engagement in the GBV programming. Hotline service will be used to provide online information sharing, PFA, and referral. RI will establish community committees under the GBV components and CP to enhance community-based protection mechanisms. The GBV committees will be established or identified through women-led organizations and will receive technical and financial support to carry out advocacy and tailored activities for women and girls. The same approach applies to CP committees: its members will be community focal points advocating for child rights; they will be also trained to identify and refer CP cases to available services To strengthen support for children with disabilities and their caregivers, RI will partner with SENED to implement a focused PSS for them through a comprehensive special needs rehabilitation (SNR) program. SENED will deliver specialized services for children with disabilities, which seeks to improve their well-being whilst promoting inclusion within their communities and mitigating CP concerns in the future. GBV services will be monitored, measured, and evaluated with recommendations to improve the services delivered in the targeted areas according to the GBV MEAL toolkits developed by the GBV AoR focusing on the quality of service benefiting from the supervision mechanisms and monitoring tools including the survivor satisfaction survey CP services will be monitored following an age, gender and disability data disaggregation. Protection Risk analyssi (extended)R3: Provision of life skills, vocational training services for women and girls may also increase the risk of exploitation by service providers, including RI staff. To mitigate such risk RI will: conduct awareness sessions on PSEA and other forms of exploitation, emphasizing reporting procedures; establish positive relationships with local stakeholders, emphasizing adherence to humanitarian principles; ensure that complaint and feedback mechanisms are easily accessible to all women and girls
