Project Summary

This plan “2021”, named “Enhancing Sustainable Housing, Land and Property Solutions in Mvolo , Mundri East and Mundri West Counties” seeks to provide HLP solution to total of 16,034 beneficiaries including 7533 men, women 5116, boys 1570 and 1815 at cost of 443,400 USD. The plan will be implemented in three counties of Mvolo, Mundri East and West in Western Equatoria State. This plan responds to Protection Cluster Objective 3 “Enable durable solution for IDPs and other Populations”.
The key activities for this plan include
1.1. Establishment of one Legal Clinic in each County provide legal counseling and assistance to the beneficiaries.
1.2. Provision of free legal counseling and assistance to the vulnerable individuals and groups.
1.3. Hiring legal firms to represent the clients in courts in Mundri and Yambio. or institutions.
1.4. Organize community level awareness sessions with COVID 19 prevention protocols observed
1.5. Establishment of Community Committee to advocate for fair treatment of the vulnerable.
1.6. Establishment of Monthly Women Land Forum at level of three counties to increase awareness of women and girls’ land rights
1.7. Organize and Facilitate Women-Local Authority Dialogue..
1.8. Awareness creation through radio talk shows Use of Mini drama used to create awareness
This plan aims to contribute to finding durable solutions. AYA as an organization has established offices in Mundri and Mvolo counties. AYA is currently implementing one Food Security and Livelihoods in Mvolo in partnership with Norwegian People’s Aid and we have easy access to all Payams. In Mundri West and Mundri East Counties AYA is currently implement two project: A child protection Project in partnership with War Child Holland with funding with UNICEF and a multi-year Resilience Project (FSL) in partnership with Mercy Corps. Over the years AYA has been a member organization of NPA supported Western Equatoria Land Alliance

1 - Needs

Celebration of partial formation of south Sudan government in early 2020 with huge expectation from citizens was short-lived as measure to contain spread the deadly Corona virus ushered in a new era of uncertainty. The impact of this virus on national income globally and South Sudan in particular is worrying and has undermined government’s constitutionally mandated delivery of basic services. This means critical services that a peace-government is widely expected to provide will always remain as a critical gap further complicating already acute situation. Year 2020 witnessed one of the worst flooding in decades which displaced closed to one million, destroyed houses and property, triggered host community- IDPs conflict over land in places like Mangalla in Central Equatoria State where thousands flood displaced persons are currently settled. The transition of Protection of civilian sites into ordinary IDP camps under management of the government in Bor, Bentiu, Malakal and Wau. This has given opportunity for the owners of land on which these sites are established to demand for eviction of IDPs from their land. The country is also witnessing voluntary returns of both IDPs and refugees and this has brought in al lot of issues such as need for shelter support, conflict with secondary occupants and demand for reintegration package amid intercommunal violence. Insecurity restarted in the second quarter of 2020 (April-July 2020) witnessed armed confrontation between IO/SSPDF and armed insurgency believed to be associated to NAS in Singo, Wiro, Lozoh, Molo, Minga, Gabat, Dibin, Iye’ba, Kyerekyere. Reports (source: extracted from IOM Data tracking record) indicated more than 20,331 people have been displaced in Mundri East and about 14,688 population are displaced in Mundri West. In early 2021 Mundri East county has seen influx IDPs from Terekeka County in an area which is witnessing returns particular of IDPs . The county continues to witness occupation of civilian property by armed forces. Most houses of those displaced have been occupied by other people. The Governor of Western has asked the local Authorities of Mundri East to provide thousands acres of land for establishment of a farm. While this is a positive development, it is our concern if the land owners will receive a fair compensation and if those who will be displace will receive a fair resettlement packageMundri West is known for malpractices when it comes to land registration affecting the vulnerable men and women. Mvolo county experiences violence between farmers and cattle keepers dry season every year. In all the three counties where this plan will be implemented, bad cultures limit women’s access to, ownership and use of land and property. Women and girls can still not inherit houses, land and property left behind by their deceased parents and spouse. Majority of population still do not understand their housing, land and property rights and do not know where to seek redress in case of violation. Most vulnerable households cannot afford to take cases of land to court or even pay for a services of a legal counsel. Host communities have also seen a lot of un-procedural encouragement on their land by IDPS and also government. Public facilities such as schools are still being occupied by armed forces and IDPs. This plan therefore seeks to establish a legal clinic to provide legal counseling and assistance to vulnerable families, hire a Law Firm to represent beneficiaries before the court of law, create awareness on HLP rights, organize monthly Women HLP Forum, Coordinate Women-Authority Dialogue on women access to land, provide cash shelter support to IDPs to easily reintegrate in their communities as part of durable solutions and use of media particularly Amadi FM in Mundri and EveryVillage FM in Mvolo and drama to create awareness.AYA as an organization has established offices in Mundri and Mvolo counties.

2 - Project activities

1.1. Establishment of one Legal Clinic in each County provide legal counseling and assistance to the beneficiaries. This cleaning will be a centre where those in need of legal counseling will get help. This clinic will be run by two experienced Lawyers. The clinic may conduct outreach to distant payams. The Lawyers working in this clinic will be responsible to forwarding cases to court and monitoring court proceedings. 1.2. Provision of free legal counseling and assistance to the vulnerable individuals and groups. Counseling will be provided by two experienced Lawyers who will study each case independently and advice the clients according. The Lawyers may carryout field visits to document facts and make recommendation for actions including forwarding them cases to be considered for litigation support and or cash assistance 1.3. This plan aims to hire legal firms to represent the clients in courts in Mundri and Yambio. This is important because land cases take long in courts sometimes even after end of the project. Secondly AYA staff may not be the direct target of any threat from land-grabbing individuals or institutions.1.4. Organize community level awareness sessions with COVID 19 prevention protocols observed. This activity is aimed at educating communities on their HLP rights, means of redress when such rights are violated. This activity will be conducted by Community Outreach Officers.1.5. Establishment of Community Committee to advocate for fair treatment of the vulnerable, provide protective accompaniment to the vulnerable groups while processing their documentation and to launch complain with local authorities with view to stop malpractices. 1.6. Establishment of Monthly Women Land Forum at level of three counties to increase awareness of women and girls’ land rights, discuss challenges affecting women’s access to, ownership of and use of land. This activity will draw women from faith based groups, civil society, government and communities. The recommendations drawn here will be presented and discussed in Women- authority Dialogue Sessions.1.7. Organize and Facilitate Women-Authority Dialogue. This activity will be a follow up to women land forum and will discuss issues raised there. This activity is expected to be organized quarterly. The County Commissioners of three Counties expected to attend. This is a forum where cultures hindering Women’s fair access to HLP will be discussed.1.8. Provide cash based shelter support to most vulnerable returnees so as to allow their smooth reintegration in to communities. This cash based shelter support can be used to buy grass and poles and pay labour for thatching. This support can also encourage voluntary and safe returns.1.9. Awareness creation through radio talk shows where officials from land administration will be hosted to explain process of registration and how vulnerable people can be supported. The sessions will also host field experts to explain legal provisions related to HLP rights including women’s rights, inheritance and litigation processes. 1.10. Use of Mini drama used to create awareness

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Market assessments have been conducted by the use of MIRA and it is integrated into preparedness and planning for this project. For example in Mundri East particularly in Jambo, Buagy, Lui and Kedi’ba and Lakamadi, in Mundri West Amadi, Mundri Town, Kotobi, Karika and in Mvolo Yeri, Mvolo town, Bogori markets are functional and available goods are readily on the local markets. There are available vendors who will be contracted to support delivery of cash to beneficiaries which includes using cash vouchers, mobile phones such as Nilepay, and there are many small banks to deliver cash assistance. Therefore, there has been basic understanding of market structure, capacity of traders, prices of commodities or services in the different market locations in project area and financial service providers. There has been already MEB calculated for Mundri East, Mundri West and Mvolo Counties and there is available information in FSL Cluster that AYA makes its use to support the process. Mundri East in particular borders Juba County which hosts South Sudan capita. All the three counties are crossed buy Juba-Mundri-Yambio and Juba Mundri Mvolo-Rumbeki highways

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash assistance is cheaper than in-kind support. People are free to choose what they want to use their money for. Study has shown that cash assistance restores dignity and choice even in most difficult circumstances. Therefore, giving cash help to easy burden on the staff. The major aim of the cash transfer in our context is to meet basic needs of individuals who might have protection concerns. It can help them get to health facility, provide for food during referral and provision of life-saving support.Given the difficulties in transportation and movement in South Sudan, essential services will be difficult to reach the beneficiaries in early and timely manner. Cash will also support the empowerment of local market and enhances the market functionality based on the assessments conducted in regards to market. Cash assistance is cost effective.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

AYA has a robust policy on Prevention of Sexual and Exploitation. All AYA staff sign commitment not to engage in SEA related activities. PSEA forms important part of cross cutting issues that are core to our ability to ensure do no harm principle. In line with this, PSEA will be central part of project meetings and continuous community engagement. AYA will organize a series of one day trainings / meetings to educate AYA commitment of SEA free humanitarian environment. During these meetings / trainings AYA’s reporting channels will be explained, how whistleblowers are protected will be explored in more details to encourage safe reporting. AYA will print leaflets explaining meaning and practices that encompass SEA and the reporting channels. AYA will use drama and media

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

This plant assumes that the current feedback systems employed by most organization do disadvantage the most vulnerable members such individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls. In response to this, AYA has adopted Community Advisory Committee with membership from all sectors including people with disability as a comprehensive means to gather feedback. In furtherance of this commitment, AYA will organize feedback session separately for the vulnerable groups or individuals so that their views can be heard directly. AYA will also use a participatory approach that increases involvement of vulnerable groups. Beneficiary satisfaction survey will be organized separately for women , girls and those with disabilities. This way all the views will be collected and analysed and incorporated

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

With support of the Advisory committee formed from among from among the communities will form a direct information link between AYA and communities. Majority of staff will be from communities and will be assigned with responsibility of collecting feedback and communicating with the beneficiary. Beneficiary satisfaction survey will be carried out. Suggestion box will be established for beneficiaries to unanimously give feedback. Information table will be established to receive complains and provide feedback

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

AYA considers beneficiaries feedback as an important part of tailoring the project assistance to meet the needs of the beneficiaries. AYA will carry out beneficiary satisfaction survey, collect, collate and analyse beneficiaries’ feedback and use such feedback to adapt new approach to delivery of the assistance without project losing the core goal. As part of this Plan AYA will establish Community Level Project Advisory committee whose views will form part of project assistance adaptation to meet beneficiary needs. In situation where such feedback requires substantial changes in the way project assistance is delivered, AYA will seek guidance from the concerned donor on how to proceed. The purpose is that the project assistance delivered meets the needs of the beneficiaries. Beneficiary views will inform similar projects in future. 8.11. Mitigation Measures

Proper planning, consultation of every group of beneficiaries, disseminating PSEA messages, making every staff sign Code of Conduct before starting any engagement with the community, strengthening community complaint mechanism, dissemination of humanitarian principles to the key stakeholders, training staffs on key GBV guiding principles and conducting assessments prior to the distribution of items. Community awareness shall be done to communities on issues pertaining sexual exploitation and sexual abuse. The contacts for the focal points shall be given to the community members to enable reports of any act constitute SEA. Therefore, the proper feedback mechanism to the community shall be established to generate and disseminate some feedback to the communities. All these will help to mitigate the risks that are anticipated.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

Most acute malnutrition in communities arise due to displacement. This project will try to address issues relating to access, ownership and use of housing, land and property resources. This will increase agriculture hence food security impacting acute malnutrition positively
