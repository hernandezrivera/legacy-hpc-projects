Project Summary

In 2021, DRC will focus on speci􀂦c issues of boys and girls from both Rohingya and host
communities through addressing protection risks and needs, interventions according to
the age, gender, wealth, ethnicity, disability differences and other variables that impact
their risk and resilience. Based on DRC’s experiences and related to JRP, it will promote the
full participation of children, adolescents, and adults for improving access to existing
government and community resources and opportunities. The project focuses on
Community based Child Protection Mechanisms, case management including alternative
care, family tracing and reuni􀂦cation, PSS along with awareness package, positive
parenting trainings for caregivers to reduce the negative coping mechanism and
strengthen child protection systems. All activities will ensure the do-not harm principle and
mainstream the COVID-19 messages. To improve the socio-economic conditions of
children and strengthen their resilience, “Multi-purpose Cash Distribution” component will
be added through the coordination with DRC livelihood sector. DRC will continue to be an
active member of CP sub-sector and multiple inter-agency coordination fora and will liaise
regularly with the local authorities and will focus on partnership development with the
local CP actors. In order to strengthen child safeguarding measures including PSEA and
code of conduct, DRC will outline the child-friendly, accessible feedback and reporting
mechanisms through direct consultations with the children by trained CP staffs who will
conduct sessions in camps, schools, health centres etc. and establish accessible
helpdesks suggestion/complaints boxes to ensure that all interventions are safe for
children. DRC will provide capacity building trainings for local authorities, and local
organizations. Instead of using any new physical spaces DRC will use existing community
space and mobile intervention at community level. Besides this intervention, DRC’s
existing camp and community structures constructed according to the safe and green
engineering guideline and environmental impact of the project will be minimal.

1 - Identified Needs

The Danish Refugee Council is working to improve the lives of boys and girls living in dangerous conditions worsened by the COVID-19 crisis in the refugee settlements of Cox’s Bazar. Different variables and inequalities between the sexes has been exacerbated during this period and increased child protection risks and vulnerabilities. The socio-economic impact of COVID-19 has highlighted pre-existing protection risks leading to the use of negative coping mechanisms. This has resulted in increased child labor, child marriage, and decreased household incomes as reported in the Protection COVID-19 report (April 2020). 55% of children and adolescents need protection support for psychosocial distress, neglect, abuse, separation, sexual violence, child marriage, child labour and tracking. As of September 2019, there were 8,596 unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) in the camps with multiple child protection risks. A larger proportion of the vulnerable group is girls (57%), who are at risk of child marriage, neglect, sexual exploitation, and abuse. On the other hand, boys are at high risk of child labour, tracking, and exploitation.Adolescents and youth are a high-risk group due to the absence of education, life skills, and livelihood opportunities, as well as a lack of access to participate in decision-making processes that affect their lives (Gender and Intersectionality Analysis Report 2020). According to the JMSNA (Nov 20) child labour, child marriage, child missing, and PSS distress have increased within both host and refugee children in 2020. According to the Bangladesh Child Protection Assessment (Rohingya Refugee Response), May 2020, keychild protection risks are sexual harassment (50%), going missing and lost (82%), child marriage (49%), exploitative work (47%), kidnapping and tracking (72%). 17% of adolescents and 25% of caregivers believe that it is acceptable for parents to arrange a marriage. Besides these, the increasing concern during the COVID-19 pandemic period is also emotional or psychological abuse, distress, serious medical condition (including serious injury), and other child protection concern for which children are becoming more vulnerable during the COVID-19. According to the ACAPS Report “4 Months of COVID-19 Programming Restriction”, August 2020, while these protection issues already existed before COVID-19, the pandemic resulted in limited access to essential resources for host communities and refugees, further reducing the effectiveness of existing coping mechanisms. This has been highlighted by the Protection Sector which reported increasing criminal activity, intimate partner violence, and GBV in the camps due to adecrease in income, limited resources, fear, and a reduced humanitarian presence in the camps (ACAPS, Impact of COVID-19 on Gender Programming, June 2020). To meet the urgent CP needs and gaps in Cox’s Bazar, the priorities are three-fold: identifying and meeting the needs of approximately 7,500 unaccompanied and separated children in camps, providing PSS to those needing it and engaging the community to improve child protection services and governance. According to CP Sub-Cluster Report “Child Protection Risks During COVID-19”, May 2020, violence against children in the camps increased, withchildren experiencing or witnessing violence at home, which has great risk on their mental health and wellbeing. Girls faced maintained risks of GBV, including rape and domestic abuse due to the limited oversight and avenues to report during COVID-19. Risks of tracking and dangerous onward movement are increasing due to closure of educational facilities, increased anxiety/stress, and limited learning and recreational activities, according to ISCH Cox’s Bazar Situation Report, July 2020.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: Strengthening the capacity of community-based child protection committeesand community group formation to identify CP risks; to refer and ensure a protective environment for children at the community level. Activity 2: Local-level advocacy with the government for effective participation of children with disabilities (child led and child focused advocacy briefs, meetings and issue-based capacity strengthening). Activity 3: Capacity strengthening of government social workers on CPIE and case management for host community intervention (to engage government (DSS) for host community case management). Activity 4: Ensure a child-friendly police desk at Upazila Police Station through sensitizing the police officials for smooth service delivery for children. Activity 5: Establish child-friendly corners at school level for increasing knowledge, skill, and change the behavior through peer-to-peer approach. Activity 6: Case management support for children and families access to child protection appropriate service including specialist SGBV, alternative care, and Family Tracing and Reunification (FTR) services for children at risk. Activity 7: Age-appropriate recreational and psycho-social activities for children and adolescent in a safe space facilitated by trained staff and community volunteers (static Multi-Purpose Child and Adolescent Care Centre-MPCAC and other mobile child-friendly spaces, protective behavior, child resilience, social-cohesion through specific adolescent led activities), peer-to-peer PSS support groups, integrated protection intervention (GBV and General Protection). Activity 8: Self-reliance and skill development package for adolescents (girls, boys) and their family members to reduce the risk of resorting to negative coping mechanisms.
