Project Summary

According to “Health Sector Syria Annual report 2023” PiN of live-saving and life-sustaining assistance in 2023 are over 15.3 million Syrians with 13% increase from 2021 — of whom 29% are women, 46% are children, 17% are PwD, and 4% are elderly. Аround 4% of the affected population are pregnant women who are in need for pre- and post-natal care as well as safe delivery. Weak, fragile and disrupted health systems, with concurrent public health emergencies, affect access, availability and quality of health services across Syria.
The intervention is in line with the framework of the HRP 2023-2024 objective 1 taking into consideration the health system capacity in the context of Cholera and complementing the efforts of the health sector in all locations. The intervention will focus on supporting primary health care (PHC) services in Rural Damascus and Aleppo by providing light rehabilitation, essential equipment, medicines, supplies, mental health consultations, and primary health care consultations to three PHCs in each area, all operated by local partners. The aim is to offer medical consultations, including diagnosis, treatment, and care, as well as promoting good health, preventing health problems, and managing ongoing conditions. Specific services encompass internal medicine, reproductive health, and pediatrics clinics, addressing chronic diseases, family planning, pre-post-natal care, nutrition, and disease prevention. Additionally, physiotherapy services and training for caregivers of persons with disabilities (PWD) will be delivered through physiotherapy centers and mobile clinics. Recognizing the psychological impacts of trauma, specialized personnel including psychiatrists, psychotherapists, social workers, and psychologists will provide mental health and psychosocial support.
Risks related to interventions could, include Risk 1: Inadequate infrastructure and limited resources in targeted areas may hinder the effective provision of essential health services/ Mitigation1: TDH will develop contingency plans and alternative delivery methods to overcome infrastructure limitations and ensure service delivery continuity. Impact: Low
Risk 2: lack of fuel and shortage of power supply which could be hinder the facility services/ Mitigation 2: TDH has installed renewable energy sources such as solar panels and batteries. Impact: Medium
Risk 3: Security risks, including conflict, violence, or instability in the project area, may pose threats to the safety of healthcare personnel and impede access to healthcare facilities/Mitigation 3: TDH has Security Officer who provide update on the security situation/SARC collaborate with local authorities and community leaders to enhance security measures and establish safe access routes for healthcare teams. Impact: Low/
External risks include insecurity, inflation, sanctions and continued instability, further depreciation of the Syrian currency, and intervening events as COVID-19, Cholera and EQ.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The project seeks to implement PHC services covering medical services for host communities, returnees and IDPs in areas with severity score “4”. These locations are in RD (Sahnaya, Kisweh) and Aleppo (Az Zahrah and Hellok), not only by establishing new PHC centers with three main clinics but also by providing internal medicine, genecology & obstetric, Pediatrics and laboratory to provide medical counselling, treatment, and Cholera management and referral if needed in coordination with the implementing partners, but also by supporting physical rehabilitation activities including Physiotherapy/PSS for PwDs through mobile teams and also covering medical treatment of beneficiaries. So unmet needs of the most in need amongst the vulnerable individuals in RD and Aleppo governorates can be served. Also, in the context of cholera, supporting the primary health care system in areas of implementation will increase the capacity to response to medical cases. The project plans to conduct focused trainings for the staff and volunteers of SARC and MoH to build their capacity, and provide structured trainings on GBV, MHGAP, CMR, and disseminate updates on Cholera shared by Health sector. The total target will be 50,000 individuals in both locations. PSEA: TDH as an origination adapted a zero-tolerance policy against sexual exportation and abuse, and to ensure all PSEA standard are respected through staff and volunteers’ trainings, code of conduct, and clear communication channels (Hot-lines, Complaint and Feedback Box, On-site observation), including GBV. Implementation of the project follows established SOPs, including planning, reporting, procurement, and quality assurance. Continuous M&E activities are conducted to assess the effectiveness of interventions and gather feedback from stakeholders. TdH IT has extensive experience in similar projects and has strategically located PHC to effectively reach communities in need. TdH IT commits to providing monthly reports, and adjustments are made based on M&E findings. TdH IT aims to hire a gender-balanced team to collect protection-related data disaggregated by sex and age. Child protection and safeguarding standards are upheld, and a safe environment for children is ensured. A comprehensive risk assessment is conducted, and mitigation measures are implemented for different age groups. M&E, and beneficiary CFM are well utilized. following the principle of 'Do no harm' and promotes inclusive participation. Data collection tools consider gender and age, and community participation efforts incorporate gender considerations.
