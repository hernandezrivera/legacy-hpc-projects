Project Summary

Venezuela is currently experiencing a major humanitarian crisis with unprecedented challenges. In March 2019, the United Nations estimated that approximately 7 million people require humanitarian assistance in Venezuela, of which 2.8 million require health assistance.
Within the health sector specifically, the infrastructure is collapsing with a shortage of supplies, medicines, equipment, and medical and technical staff:
• A shortage in essential medicines
• Preventable diseases such as tuberculosis, diphtheria, measles and malaria have re-emerged in the country and are increasing
• Maternal and children under one year of age mortality rates have significantly
• An assessment of 40 hospitals in 24 states of the country found that 95% of tomography and magnetic resonance services were non-functional
• The Venezuelan Medical Federation estimates that approximately 22,000 doctors have left the country; this figure is about 33% of all doctors reported in the country in 2014.

In June 2019, International Medical Corps further assessed the most pressing needs at health facilities and hospitals, reaching the Maternal and Child Hospital of Caricuao, “Dr. Pastor Oropeza” and Children’s Hospital, “José Manuel de Los Rios” in Caracas. The needs were immense, ranging from essential and basic items such as antibiotics, toilet tissues, to medical equipment like x-ray machines. There was an overall gap in accessible medications, functioning medical equipment, and support to the medical personnel who remain. Many medical personnel are paid only $30 each month. Nosocomial infections are becoming one of the common causes of sickness and death. Our team reports grave concerns for the needs of pregnant women and newborns.

While the needs in and around the capital are vast, remote areas are particularly affected by the ongoing crisis. This includes Bolivar and Amazonas states, where many indigenous communities live. For inhabitants of these communities, access to qualified medical care is challenging due to transportation and insecurity, which leads to growing mortality and morbidity rates. Additionally, with low vaccination coverage, there are high risks of contracting diphtheria, measles, and other vaccine-preventable diseases. Many of the prevalent diseases could be resolved if treated properly.
International Medical Corps goal is to address the humanitarian needs in Venezuela through improving access to health and health-related services, by reaching communities that lack medical supplies, medications, medical equipment, and medical staff. Across activities, International Medical Corps will target the most vulnerable women, men, and children.

For this, IMC will provide medical support in the communities of Bolivar to improve the access to health care for 2,207 women, men, and children with our local partner, and IMC mobile medical unit team which consists of 28 medical staff. It is noteworthy that this state include much of the indigenous population of Venezuela, and in these more remote areas access to medical care is extremely limited or non-existent.

Further, IMC proposes to support 9 public health facilities in Caracas, Falcon, and Zulia, which will increase access to care and services to approximately additional 4,000 individuals in need.
