Project Summary

This proposed project, intends to scale-up Sexual Reproductive Health and menstrual hygiene Management support and care services for vulnerable displaced girls, young women and women living with disabilities in targeted camps, host communities and selected schools of Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe State. The project is aimed at building capacity of young girls and women with the confidence, knowledge, and skills to manage their reproductive and menstrual health challenges safely, with dignity by breaking down stifling barriers to information, supplies, and access to services, and thus reducing the dangerous risk that exposes these population.
The project is founded on the four pillars of programming for MHM captured in the core -package of intervention, and it proposed to reach 15 LGAs, 7 Camps, 18 communities with a total of 32,200 girls/young women in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe State within its project life span. Basically, two sets of two sets of beneficiaries will be targeted: displaced adolescent girls between the ages of 9 to 20 years, and women of reproductive age between the ages of 22 – 36 and those living with disabilities. We will be partnering with the Ministry of women Affairs and Social Development in the implementation of this project with technical support from the Borno, Adamawa and Yobe GBVSSWG and the coalition of Women led NGO. A total of about 13,300 women and girls are the direct beneficiaries of this project: with over 18,900 women as indirect beneficiaries.
These beneficiaries comprise women and girls who are currently affected or are at high risk of been affected by complications resulting from reproductive health issues as well as poor menstrual Management practice. With non-existent livelihood source and coupled with the harsh consequences of the COVID 19 global pandemic that further plunged them into more deplorable conditions.
The project will evoke a participatory approach by involving not only the beneficiaries, but Community Based Organizations, health personnel, community leaders/members, educational authorities, corporate bodies, and business as well as hundreds of volunteers and women youth leaders. The program will strengthen linkages to other related services particularly those of reproductive health nature such as HIV/AIDs, STDs, etc.
It will comprise of community awareness and outreaches to target beneficiaries by mostly health workers and other volunteers. The fundamentals of sexual reproductive health (SRH) management by women and girls and that of menstrual hygiene will be co-mainstreamed and taught in a club format (30 persons per club) for women and girls ages to run for a period of 3 months per each cohort group/persons using certified modules.
Women and girls with disabilities will be reached via a one-on-one and door to door outreach by volunteer coaches with abilities to engage with them. Within this period, beneficiaries will be supported with free menstrual reusable pads. Free SRH services comprising of medical tests, counselling support, and treatment will be made available, and a community improvement team comprising of multi-stakeholder’s forum of the target community will be set up, trained and supported to engage in sustained advocacy to related authorities (governmental and non-governmental) on access and availability of medicals and SRH services in their local communities. EGI have set up a reusable pad production centre which is almost at 95% production ready.
The project will seek to expand this centre and make it responsible for the provision these of pads to the target beneficiaries. An emergency livelihood component will also be mainstreamed to support and assist most vulnerable and at-risk women and girls.
The project is designed for a 24 months’ time-frame intervention duration, with a propose total budget of $139,500.

1 - Needs

Communities in Borno, Adamawa, Yobe and NE entirely have long been characterized with high level of poverty, deprivation and ignorance, compounded with a culture that is repressive to the reproductive health, needs and rights of women and girls. The decade long crises only exacerbated this, to a level that has become much more disastrous to the life, safety and dignity of women and girls. Women and girls have little or no knowledge and access to services of reproductive and menstrual hygiene management. As a result of the current crises, SRH and menstrual products are luxuries that only less than 21% could afford, coupled with an increasing occurrence of SGBV that put these women and girls at a further disadvantaged position. UNFPA estimates that more than 1.4 million affected women are of reproductive age, with 276,000 becoming pregnant yearly and will require reproductive health services and support. These communities were cut off from both access and services, due to terrain, security, socio-culture norms and paucity of funding that places priorities on live saving intervention like food and shelter. In 5 communities (urban and rural), an official survey by EGI indicated that more than 592,000 women and girls of reproductive age are with infections and they do not know what to do, much more it causes. In displaced communities and camps, 83% of girls and women don’t have enough money to buy menstrual products. For the absorption of menstrual blood, 79% per cent use cloth, 2 per cent cotton wool, 7 per cent sanitary pads and 2 per cent ash (Water Aid) Many use unsanitary materials such as old rags, husks, dried leaves, grass, ash, sand, or newspapers because they do not have access to affordable, hygienic and safe products and facilities. Shops in own villages or the environment often do not have sanitary pads for sale, because there is no demand for it. For 43% of the girls in rural area’s having their period is one of the reasons to quit school, with irreversible effects for their personal development, economical status and eventually their health. 56% of the girls–in and out of school- did not receive any information about menstruation (UNICEF MHM). The conflict has resulted in the destruction of health facilities and the displacement of women and girls, including over 1.8 million women of childbearing age, isolating them from necessary reproductive health services. Abduction and sexual violence are defining characteristics of the ongoing conflict, with about 5 in 10 women in the north-east having experienced gender-based violence (GBV). Supporting vulnerable women, providing mobile health teams, and developing and maintaining a strong, sustainable intervention will make a lifesaving difference for adolescent girls and women. The project will seek to provide access to right information and The project will develop comprehensive support and care services for vulnerable young girls and women in order to break existing barriers to information, supplies and services as it regards Sexual reproductive and menstrual health in displaced communities and camps.

2 - Activities

1. Advocacy visits will be conducted to all relevant stakeholders concerned and targeted at the state and community level. This community entry strategy is aimed at intimating the stakeholders, sensitizing them on the project and most importantly seek their support in mobilizing much needed resources – but human and material for effective programme delivery in all communities. Mobilization for the involvement and participation of the various stakeholders will be of key focus here too. Aside community and traditional leaders, other stakeholders to be mobilized include the Ministries and Director of health at State and LGA levels, Ministries of Education; Religious Leaders, Head of Corporate Organizations, NGOs (Local and International) etc. 2. The project will engage in comprehensive trainings of volunteers, staff, peer educators, Traditional birth Attendants, Women leaders and selected adolescent girls from targeted communities schools and young women from communities on the Minimum Initial Service Package comprehensive menstrual hygiene and reproductive health education. The training aims at effectively building the capacity of these girls on the basic and critical aspect of SRH and MHM via a Training of Trainers (TOT) model. 3. Community awareness and outreaches to target beneficiaries by mostly health workers, volunteers and other Staff where fundamentals of SRH management and that of menstrual hygiene will be co-mainstreamed and taught in a club format (30 persons per club) for women and girls ages to run for a period of 3 months per each cohort group/persons using certified modules. 4. Women and girls with disabilities will be reached via a one-on- one and door to door outreach by staff and volunteer coaches with abilities to engage with them. 5. Provision and support with free menstrual reusable pads. The project plans to create easy and consistent access to safe, healthy and hygienic menstrual products in a routine basis to all the targeted communities and communities with the aim of eradicating period poverty and mitigating against the numerous life-threatening vulnerabilities that comes with the lack of these products for the young girls and women 3. Construction of safe menstrual health friendly toilets in girls’ schools 6. Routine provision of Free SRH services comprising of mobile medical tests, counselling support, SRH products and treatment will be made available, with collaboration with related partners and organizations. Beneficiaries will be reached with Reproductive health dignity kits 7. Setting up of a community improvement team comprising of multi-stakeholders forum of the target community, trained and supported to engage in sustained advocacy on access and availability of SRH services in their local communities. 8. Expansion of EGI’s Re-useable Menstrual Pad Production outlet for more viability, expansion and reach.

CNF will ensure that protection mainstreaming is realized through regular consultation with the target communities, training and capacity building of staff on PSEA effective reporting channel case management modalities.
