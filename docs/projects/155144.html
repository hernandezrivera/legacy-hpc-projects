Project Summary

In 2019, World Concern with the technical support of Medair plans to operate 2 integrated health posts to provide access to lifesaving primary health care for Rohingya refugees in the makeshift camps living in relocation/extension sites, areas with gaps in health services or facing extreme congestion, with very high risks of natural disasters. These 2 integrated health posts are in camp 20 extension (facility number 056) and 8 West (H facility number 075). World Concern is currently the only primary health care partner in camp 20 extension and Camp 8 West has gaps in the number of health facilities for its population and one of the current main health providers, MSF-OCA, is planning to handover their health post in 8W to World Concern (#075) as of the 1st January 2019.

Needs

Ongoing formal and informal assessments continue to show high unmet needs across core sectors – health and nutrition, shelter/NFI and WASH. Many services are still clustered along the main road and remain close to the centers of the main camps, Kutupalong and Balukhali, but deep within the makeshift settlements/camps and within the extension sites there is limited humanitarian assistance available. Although overall there is adequate planned coverage of primary health care services within the existing camps based on the Bangladesh Essential Health Service Package's (ESP) minimum standard of one health post per population of 6,000 individuals, services remain fragmented and poorly coordinated (approximately 219 health facilities according to EWARS reporting in November, 2018). Site management, protection actors and nutrition partners in many camps struggle to understand which health facilities are viable for referrals. Unreliable hours of operation and unclear availability of services complicate efforts to direct Rohingya refugees to receive assistance and discourages timely health seeking behavior. Both the health and nutrition sectors’ strategy is to integrate services where feasible but not all existing partners have the capacity or the expertise to offer a comprehensive package. Some key health partners are already at the strategic planning stage of reducing the number of facilities in order to focus on fewer but fully capable primary health centers. The closure of health facilities, particularly those in the densest part of the camps will create a large barrier to accessing essential health services and will impact good health seeking behavior. There remains need for a strong multisector partner to step in to facilitate the transition to improved coverage of comprehensive integrated primary health care and nutrition services with complementary WASH support at facilities.

Activities or outputs

Outcome 1. Increased access for vulnerable women, girls, boys and men to lifesaving quality primary health care servicesEach integrated health post will serve an estimated population of 6,000 individuals. Comprehensive curative and preventative services will be integrated including: primary healthcare, SRH, BCC and nutrition screening for under 5s and PLWs. Each health post will have a PSS staff member in the facility to provide Psychological First Aid and to refer individuals requiring further MHPSS support or protection services. Strong referral pathways will be identified at the start of the project to ensure consistent secondary and tertiary health care can be obtained. The focus of the health posts will be children under-five years old and PLWs, who have the highest risk of illness and death. The health posts will provide services six days a week, for 5-7 hours every day depending on patient volume. Essential drugs for health posts (equivalent to a Community Centre by the Essential Health Service Package by the MoHFW), will be procured and delivered to health posts based on average consumption rates to ensure there are no stock outs of essential drugs. Having reliable and consistent opening hours are essential for the community to trust the health services provided by World Concern as well as to ensure timely and early treatment for illnesses and referral if needed. For these 2 health posts, a total of 62 staff will be hired, trained and supervised; this includes 19 health staff currently employed. All health staff will be given refresher training on national and global guidelines during their induction. Regular supportive supervision will be given to staff by clinic supervisors, health managers and health project manager to ensure staff provide quality care. Exit interviews will be conducted monthly to verify correct diagnosis, treatment and health education is given to care givers of children under five. Due to the ongoing risk of natural disasters such as cyclones, landslides and floods within the refugee camps, as well as disease outbreaks, World Concern will retain the capacity to deploy to disaster affected areas or to be able to provide additional support if disease outbreaks occur. Instead of keeping dedicated mobile medical teams (MMTs) World Concern will train certain key staff within each of the health posts to be able to deploy as needed with the skills as first responders and the response will be able to scale up if the need arises. All health staff will be trained on anthropometric screening for under-fives and PLWs and all community health staff will be trained on MUAC screening, IYCF and referral from the community to the health post. Supportive supervision will be provided by the clinic supervisor, health managers and health project manager to ensure staff adhere to national and global standards. Children under five and PLWs presenting to the health posts will be screened for malnutrition using MUAC and children under five will also be screened using weight for height. For nutrition services not available within the World Concern health posts, referrals will be made to the nearest functional nutrition site. Outcome 2: Increased health, nutrition and hygiene awareness and practice of key health and IYCF behaviors for vulnerable women, girls, boys and men An integral component of the integrated health and nutrition services is community health and social behavior change comminution (SBCC). CHVs will be recruited and trained from within camp 20 extensionand 8 West. Each health post will have 12 CHVs from the catchment area who will be trained on SBCC methods. Health messaging will focus on maternal and child health (ANC, PNC, SBA, FP, EBF, EPI, health seeking behavior, GBV, and IYCF), hygiene and early referral to health facilities. World Concern will collaborate with the CHW working group as well as other organizations to ensure utilization of standard messages and data collection tools.
