Project Summary

According to Save the Children, Plan International and World Vision’s Joint Briefing Note on the situation facing children in Northern Mozambique (June 2021) , there are horrific reports of grave violations against children, including killing and maiming, abduction, recruitment and use, and sexual violence, especially against adolescent girls.
According to UNHCR’s 2021 implementation reports, displacement caused by armed conflict in Cabo Delgado has exposed young persons to human rights violations and posed various challenges and protection issues and risks on them. In places where they sought refuge in southern districts of the province, limited resources and capacity of the government and humanitarian partners have made them at a greater exposure to abuse and exploitation, failing to meet their basic protection needs. This conflict compounded by the high level of fear and suspicion among local authorities towards the displaced populations, though mostly reacting to possible infiltration of armed elements, has created a breeding ground for abuse and exploitation against displaced populations, including youth. Lack and loss of identity documentation among displaced populations fuelled xenophobic attitudes and sentiments against them. Displaced youth have reported harassment and physical violence incidents due to failing to show identity documents that they have lost or never had access to or the means to acquire in their place of origin. As a result, displaced young people reported being humiliated by being called names such as insurgents or criminals; young girls, specifically, are referred to as prostitutes by the host communities. The ongoing conflicts and tensions prevent them from developing a social network through which they gain critical information and peer support for survival. Both aspects are key in buffering against adverse life events and creating positive coping mechanisms. In these volatile environments, displaced youth feel isolated without having opportunities to rebuild their lives and actively contribute to the welfare of their families and communities. This situation worsens or contributes to their sense of helplessness. Issues such as difficult or lack of access to food, adequate shelter, social and cultural activities, education, and formal and informal employment opportunities to support themselves and their families are of concern to promote the recovery, self-reliance and local integration of displaced youth.
The project will address differentiated protection risks and community-based protection needs through establishing community-based structures including protection focal points. Plan International will work with community-based structures to monitor protection risks for the purpose of risk mitigation and protection incidence and persons with specific needs for service provision. Additionally, Plan International will build the knowledge of the displaced youth on human rights, which supports an umbrella goal of empowering and training youth to become peacebuilders in their own communities. The project will also provide MHPSS services to populations affected by psychosocial distress caused by the experienced lived in the attacks perpetrated by NSAGs. Considering that affected populations left their homes fleeing the conflict, many could not carry with them their civil documents and may not access basic social services or humanitarian. The project will facilitate access to civil document to individuals who lost their them as a result of the conflict.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project will target beneficiaries form from IDP, returnee and host communities with protection services. Specific interventions will be implemented to address protection needs for children below 18 and adult aged 60+ years old. 47% of the beneficiaries will be males while 53 will be females.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The project activities will be implemented in the following districts:Chiure, Macomia, Mocimboa Da Praia, Mueda, Muidumbe, Nangade, Palma and Quissanga. The targeted population will receive support to get civil documentation through the relevant government institutions. The project will foster information sharing and awareness raising in the targeted community for the population to now their rights and how to claim them. Cash to access civil documentation will be provided on a case-to-case basis for transport and payment of the fees. In communities not reached by such programs, mobile brigades will be deployed to register children. These mobile brigades will also sensitize and advocate among families and wider communities on the importance of registration for themselves and for their children to ensure they can have access toservices and civil, social, and economic rights.The project will implement case management of people with specific needs, incl. people with disabilities and older people (incl. victims of explosives), survivors of torture, detainees. The will be identified through the community based structures and volunteers. Referal pathways will be established in each targetted community and this will be properly communicated so that the population are aware of where to seek assistance. The dissemination of referral pathways is prioritised, both through mainstreaming in trainings and activities and through specific actions to ensure they are as widely known as possible so more children can be referred and provided withadequate support. The action will use a community-based approach, key institutions and local government officials will be involved in activities and provided with capacity building, while coordination with peer organisations will be constant.Mental health and psychosocial support will be provided to the - MHPSS (Psychological first aid, individual counselling, group activity) - level 2 and 3Referrals to protection services and information on services will be established through community consultation, engagement with clusters and mapping of relevant existing structure. The project will build/strengthen prevention and response capacity through on providing support to community mechanisms and structures, empowerment activities, awareness on protection risks, CwC, social cohesion, explosive ordnance risk education - OERE. The project will then facilitate the linkages between the CCPCs and the District Child Protection Reference Groups, thus reinforcing the CP Systems at Community and Local Government level. This also improves the sustainability of the action by creating greater institutional knowledge and collaboration between stakeholders, thus making way for potential exit. strategies or a handover to longer-term development interventions. Capacity strengthening, training and advocacy for local authorities, humanitarian actors and other stakeholders will part of the key interventions. Training, mentorship and technical support will be provided in areas such as protection, SEA, referral mechanism etc.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The project will identify risks through risk assessment and safety audit and devise mechanisms to mitigate them. Plan International had go risk assessment and safety audit tool which are used to conduct the assessments. Through the rapid needs analysis, the project will also identify potential risks by interviewing different stakeholders and the affected population and community level. The project will regularly conduct risk assessments to update the risk register and make programme adjustments.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The project will identify and address barriers that limit access to assistance, such as cultural, physical, or economic constraints, for example by providing assistance at accessible locations or offer transportation for women, elderly persons and children at risk access protection services in distant place. The design of the project will culturally sensitive outreach to encourage women's participation. The project will also ensure physical access to children with disabilities.Safeguards will be established to prevent discrimination, exploitation, or abuse when accessing assistance, for example ensuring private and safe spaces for women andgirls/adolescents to discuss sensitive issues. The staff engaged in case management will be trained in age- and gender-responsive approaches. The project will actively involve women, elderly, children, adolescents and their families in the planning, implementation, and monitoring of the project through conducting regular consultations and community feedback sessions.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

To effectively identify people with disabilities (PWDs) and remove barriers to their access to assistance, the project can incorporate the following measures: Persons With Disabilities will be identified through Community Engagement and Outreach by working closely with community leaders, disability organizations, and local health/social services to identify PWDs. Participatory approaches will be applied, such as focus group discussions and home visits, to locate individuals who may not self-identify due to stigma or isolation. The project will conduct disability-inclusive assessments integrate questions aligned with the Washington Group Short Set of Questions on Disability into needs assessments to systematically identify functional limitations across populations. Disaggregated data by age, gender, and disability will be collected to better understand specific needs. Referral Networks will be established linking mapping partner with healthcare providers, schools, and social services to establish a referral system that flags PWDs requiring assistance. Accessible Communication Channels will be fostered with diverse disabilities can identify themselves and their needs. The project will pro-actively remove barriers to access by retrofitting facilities and distribution points to accommodate PWDs, including ramps, handrails, wide pathways, and accessible transportation. Priority lanes or designated support teams will be ensured for PWDs to minimize waiting times and logistical challenges, apart from providing at-home delivery or mobile services for those unable to travel. The project will also promote awareness and sensitization by training project staff and volunteers on disability inclusion and stigma reduction to ensure respectful and equitable treatment of PWDs. Community awareness campaigns will be conducted to reduce stigma and encourage the participation of PWDs. Child friendly, accessible and inclusive feedback Mechanisms will be establish e.g.,anonymous suggestion boxes, toll-free hotlines with voice/visual options) to ensure PWDs can report barriers or suggest improvements. The project will conduct regular monitoring and adaptation, assessing the barriers PWDs face during project implementation and adapt activities based on their feedback and evolving needs.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The protection project, while primarily focused on safeguarding the well-being of affected people in crisis settings, can have unintended environmental impacts. These may arise from activities such as shelter construction, supply distribution, transportation, or establishing child-friendly spaces (CFS). It is vital to incorporate environmental considerations to mitigate harm and enhance the project's sustainability. The project will conduct preliminary EIAs as part of project planning to identify potential environmental risks. This may entail partnering with environmental experts or agencies to integrate findings into program design. The project will train staff and community members on environmentally sustainable practices and involve children and families in environmental conservation activities to foster awareness and responsibility.
