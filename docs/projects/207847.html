Project Summary

"The project's main objective is to improve protection, safety, and security, enhance access to justice, and ensure peaceful coexistence between refugees and host communities in Cox's Bazar. The project will also facilitate an ongoing secondary sources conflict analysis to determine the current crisis and vulnerabilities for sharing the results with the relevant agencies for effective crisis mitigation. It will focus on vulnerable groups such as women, the elderly, and people with specific needs. The project will enhance the capacity of the Armed Police Battalion (APBn) and district police for improved protection and legal support services for the host and refugee communities. The project will also support a forum through which the host communities will have improved access to protection services and conflict resolution mechanisms and access to legal assistance through formal and informal mechanisms. The expected results will reinforce the capacities of actors that are currently working on community safety and security, justice and protection in both refugee and host communities, focusing on addressing crime prevention, access to relevant safety and legal services, promoting women and youth empowerment, and responding to any violation of rights. The proposed intervention is based on the findings of a community safety pilot project carried out in camps and host communities in 2020 and 2021 and experience and lessons learned from 2022, which emphasized the need for increased police presence, especially female police officers in the camps and capacity building of actors on refugee law, GBV, counter-trafficking, protection principles, community outreach, human rights, and conflict resolution. The project will focus on coordinating with law enforcement agencies, judiciary, and relevant justice sector actors, including local government institutions, to ensure refugees and host communities have equitable protection services and access to justice equipped with knowledge of reporting mechanisms. The project will contribute to improving the governance of the relevant institutions. In implementing the project, the agencies will ensure that persons of different genders and other backgrounds benefit equally from the activities. UNDP, UNHCR, and IOM will jointly implement the project in close collaboration with the RRRC and Camp in Charges, APBn, District Police, local government institutions, and the Judiciary in Cox’s Bazar. The proposed proposal will synergize with many projects implemented by the UN agency, INGOs, and NGOs to contribute to the peaceful coexistence between and among the host and camp communities. The GAM tool will assess the context in which programmatic action integrates gender and age consideration with a view to improving the quality of humanitarian action.
The activities will be targeted at all gender in the targeted camps and host community areas irrespective of their gender, sexual identity, and orientation. The needs assessments will identify the specific needs of different gender groups and provide tailored support. The project will institutionalize and integrate gender into the entire spectrum of activities -projects, programmes, and staff policies. For gender issues to be fully integrated into the UN values, all staff need to understand and implement the strategy in their work. This mainstreaming process is not exclusively related to women's needs and concerns. It also includes initiatives for men when needed, provided they promote overall gender equality. The project will target 50% men and 50% women in the program activities. Since women are left behind in many societies, it will focus on increasing women's empowerment and leadership throughout the project."

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"Cox’s Bazar District in Bangladesh, originally home to 2.2 million people, faced a transformative event in 2017 with the Rohingya influx, now hosting 956,467 refugees, constituting one-third of the population. This demographic shift created minority status for Teknaf and Ukhiya residents. The rapid growth of the Rohingya population, marked by 150,000 births since 2017, strained infrastructure and heightened social tensions. The 2017 migration profoundly impacted governmental service delivery institutions, leading to internal conflicts among the Rohingya community, prominent leaders' killings, and refugee involvement in cross-border actions, complicating the situation. Overwhelming demands on local institutions caused scarcity in services, hindering justice delivery and amplifying security and social issues such as increased intra-Rohingya political rivalry, the emergence of armed conflict groups, mal power structure, drug smuggling, human trafficking, and violence against women. As per Bangladesh Police, Cox’s Bazar stands out as one of the most conflict-ridden districts in the country. From 2017 through December 2022, there were over 116 recorded homicides in camps. As per UNDP's - CARU report, the count has reached 69 murders and 30 injured in total 86 incidents in 2023. Notably, the scale of drug seizures between January 2022 and August 22, 2023, amounts to 331,69,132 pcs of YABA, 178 kg of Crystal meth, 14262 liters of Alcohol, and 758 kg of Cannabis. These substantial seizures raised the number of cases filed, resulting in the arrest of 953 Bangladeshi nationals and 62 Rohingyas. During this period, a total of 192 Arms cases were filed, leading to the arrest of 204 Bangladeshi nationals and 176 Rohingyas. CARU reports 108 incidents in Ukhiya and Teknaf in 2023, comprising 52 kidnappings, 55 assaults, and 1 child marriage. A significant but underreported prevalence of gender-based violence (GBV) persists, encompassing dowry-related violence, rape, assault, abuse, and harassment. Due to social stigma, women seldom report GBV to authorities, handling it within families, posing challenges for data gathering. Social surveys consistently highlight GBV as a high-risk insecurity. Fear of harassment and assault, particularly for girls, often compels families to opt for early marriage. Widespread trafficking, especially of women and children, compounds the issue.The formal judicial system in Bangladesh grapples with a colossal backlog, surpassing 4 million cases as of September 30, 2022, with a pool of approximately 1,900 judges to deal with. This backlog significantly impedes the prompt dispensation of justice. In the Cox's Bazar District Judiciary, a daunting 65,000 cases await resolution, the Women and Tribunal Court has 9,000 unresolved cases, and the Chief Judicial Magistrate Courts of Cox's Bazar has 35,000 pending criminal cases. Ensuring community safety and security is essential for fostering social cohesion and peaceful coexistence in Ukhia and Teknaf Upazila of Cox's Bazar. This project aims to activate and implement the Community Policing mechanism, a vital support system for host communities, and enhance the capacity and skills of APBn forces to improve safety and protection services. Additionally, it will engage the Informal Dispute Resolution and Legal Aid mechanism, ensuring equitable access to justice and enhanced protection services for host communities. Strengthening these mechanisms involves capacity-building for the Justice Sector Institutions and forum members. Their active involvement is crucial in addressing social tension, resolving conflicts, and maintaining safety and security in host and camp areas. Collaboratively, UNDP, UNHCR, and IOM will spearhead these initiatives, with UNDP focusing on implementation in host areas and UNHCR and IOM managing the camp-related aspects of the project."

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project will be implemented in building trust and relationship with proper engagement of refugees, host-community members, local government authorities, police, civil administration and judiciary. The partner agencies will implement the project for three years ending in 2025 with funding from The Bureau of Population, Refugees, and Migration (PRM), USA. The safety, security and access to protection services are critical as the conflict between and among the host and Rohingya communities increased significantly. Due to control over the camp for illegal generating fund and political interest, vested interested groups are involved in different types of criminal activities. As a result, 69 killings, 108 violence incidents, 13,041,235 pcs of YABA, 100 kg of Crystal meth, and other drug seizures in 2023 (CARU-UNDP) created panic to the mass camp and host communities for safety and security. And also, the escalation of tension and fear of insecurity increased among the communities. The project will map to identify the potential communities who would participate at the higher level of the programme, and other communities will participate directly through the project activities. The various capacity-building initiatives will enhance all levels of communities and other relevant stakeholders' capacity to attain the project objectives. The project will ensure the quality and higher level of participation of the stakeholders and the communities from its inception across the entire implementation period.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

"The 3 agencies (UNDP, UNHCR & IOM) will ensure mainstreaming of protection throughout the implementation of the activities. ""Do no harm"" is the guiding principle for implementing the activities for the vulnerable people. The privacy and confidentiality of the beneficiaries will be considered while designing and implementing the project. Beneficiaries will also receive awareness-raising sessions to protect them from any unwanted incidents.The agencies will deliver services in ways that preserve the physical integrity of individuals and communities, that are culturally appropriate, and that avoid any unintended negative consequences. The project will provide assistance and services according to needs and not based on age, sex, gender, nationality, race, or ethnic allegiance. The project will provide training on PSEA to the project staff members and partners to ensure PSEA aspects. "

3.3 - Age & Disability

"The project has an explicit age, gender, and disability focus. Key strategies include supporting the capacities of women, youth community leaders, persons with disabilities, and elderly persons to prevent the escalation of inter-communal tensions. It will strengthen the capacity of local actors in addressing and preventing GBV, community safety and security, child marriage, conflict resolution, and human and drug trafficking, including local government, women mediator’s forums, youths, and NGOs. In all interventions, the project will seek to address the issues of different age groups and sensitize incorporating age and disability concerns throughout its initiatives.Integration of Gender and GBV (Gender-Based Violence) issues into all developed training modules intended for District Police, Community Policing Forum, Mediators Forum, and Union Legal Aid Forum incorporating gender-specific sessions.Prioritization of male and female students from schools, women from marginalized and indigenous communities, and individuals with disabilities from different age groups for sensitization with comprehensive info on safety and security issues such as drivers of community conflicts, gender-based violence, peaceful coexistence, cybercrime, local dispute resolution mechanisms, legal aid support, and redress mechanisms.Conducting consultations with the Community Policing Forum particularly involving female CPF members, elderly individuals, and persons with disabilities from various age groups and genders to identify local security issues and gather insightful info concerning security concerns, recent incidents of domestic violence against women, sexual harassment, and rape, for developing a community security plan and its implementation.Identification of survivors of GBV and violence, particularly women and girls, elderly individuals, persons with disabilities, and members of indigenous communities, linking them with need-based service-providing institutions."

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

"Accountability remains a vital and integral component of this proposed project to comply with the AAP. During the needs assessment, the affected populations in all the targeted locations will be consulted in designing, planning, and implementing activities in camps and host communities. The affected people will take the lead in deciding their roles and local resources mobilization considering safety, security, and gender dynamics. Affected women and girls will be consulted, in addition to and separately from men and boys. The project will practice the following tools: a. PSEA- Accelerating the PSEA plan at the community level. b. Conduct Need Assessment.c. Ensure Disability Inclusion. d. Ensure Gender Age Marker. e. Feed Back Mechanism.f. Effective coordination."

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

"The project employs a comprehensive and strategic approach to mainstream Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). Explicitly incorporating PSEA into the objectives of the broader community safety and access to justice project demonstrates a commitment to fostering a safe environment for all community members. Existing PSEA guidelines and protocols, encompassing reporting mechanisms, confidentiality measures, and a survivor-centered approach, are introduced and integrated into the project framework. These guidelines are effectively communicated to all project staff, community members, and relevant stakeholders.It proactively integrates gender concerns into all programmatic initiatives, incorporating PSEA issues into training modules. Training sessions on PSEA awareness, prevention, and response are conducted for project staff and relevant stakeholders, equipping them with the skills to identify, address, and report incidents appropriately. Engagements with host and Rohingya communities through meetings, workshops, and awareness campaigns disseminate information about PSEA, emphasizing a zero-tolerance policy for such misconduct. The project establishes accessible and confidential reporting mechanisms for incidents of sexual exploitation and abuse, ensuring widespread awareness. A robust monitoring and evaluation system focuses specifically on PSEA, regularly assessing preventive measures, response mechanisms, and the project's impact on reducing incidents.Collaboration with local authorities, non-governmental organizations, and international agencies working on PSEA ensures national and international standards alignment. Periodic reviews and adaptations of PSEA strategies respond to evolving community needs and emerging challenges, fostering flexibility and responsiveness. Thorough documentation of PSEA-related activities, incidents, and responses, along with regular progress reports, contributes to accountability and transparency. "

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The project will analyse the community disaster risks and vulnerabilities and will address the associated protection and safety concerns in coordination with key stakeholders. The project has scope of contingency funding (5%), if any disasters happened, the project would respond to ensure the protection and safety of the affected communities. The project will also address the gender-based violence that raising from disasters and climate impacts. The project ensues that the community protection and safety activities are risk informed and contribute long-term development and reduce the humanitarian burdens. The project will also build capacity of local government, police, security stakeholders and communities on the protection and safety concerns raised by and during disasters.
