Project Summary

This project comes to address the urgent needs of the health sector in Gaza as a result of COVID-19. Not to dwell much on figures (as these will be explained further in the needs section) but Gaza is now suffering from the third COVID-19 wave with the highest numbers of infected cases and deaths recorded since August 2020. The MoH has identified many needs to respond to the COVID-19 pandemic and the HNO 2022 identified more than 300,000 people and health workers at need. MAP will focus on case-management to avoid any duplication of service and to complement the efforts of others. This project will be part of the overall coordinated response with the MoH, HNC and other organisations to respond to the most urgent health needs that are currently surfacing in relation to COVID-19 such as the provision of drugs, medical disposables, and lab materials for case-management and PPEs for health workers. This will also be coupled with Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE) awareness messages through several platforms and printed materials about COVID-19 awareness and vaccines’ intake through a community partner that MAP has had a good experience within this field.

1 - Needs

This project is in line with Cluster objective 2 related to Strategic Objective 3On 24 August 2020, at 11 pm, the MoH in Gaza held a press conference in which they announced the detection of the first cases of positive COVID-19 patients from within the community, indigenous cases. Since then and as of 9 October 2021, Gaza went haywire. As per the most recent statistics from the MoH, 176,141 cases of COVID-19 have been reported: 15,544 are still active cases 169,142 cases recovered and 1,455 deaths were reported. Of those infected, there 3,725 were health workers. The gender breakdown of these is 51% females and 49% males (WHO (COVID-19) Situation Report 81), while 23.3% are children (Abed SN et al., J Gynecol Oncol. 2021; 4(3):1062.). Currently 200 patients remain seriously ill and in need of ICU or critical care, while another 202 need medical care inside hospitals but are less severe. Recently, and as part of the ongoing assessment of the COVID-19 situation in Gaza, MAP interviewed an MoH official from ICD asking about the situation at EGH (the largest designated MoH hospital for COVID-19) and he described it in one word “catastrophic”. As per the most recent WHO COVID-19 report covering up until 23 September 2021, it was indicated that the percentage of patients who need ventilation increased by 85% in Gaza while ICU patients increased by 6% in comparison to the previous reporting iteration. The positivity rate is high at 33% and coupled with the chronic zero-stock shortages. Before the detection of the indigenous cases, a need to urgently fill gaps related to lab reagents, drugs related to the ICU and respiratory system, medical disposables, personal protective equipment (PPEs) for the health workers, and materials for Infection Prevention Control (IPC) was persistent. The most recent updates from the MoH’s Central Drug Stores (CDS, September 2021) indicate that there are shortages in drugs and disposables equal to 39% and 21% respectively including a shortage of 19% and 7% in ICU related drugs and disposables respectively. While the laboratory unit reported that there are shortages of around 73% in immunity, and epidemics related items in which COVID-19 is part of (CDS, September 2021). To add, during 2021, MAP addressed many of the needs of the MoH for oxygen supplies. While we were able to support MoH with two large oxygen generation stations that were installed at a crucial stage during the second wave; MoH has been in dire support for alternative O2 support in the form of cylinders and we supplied MoH hospitals with 60 cylinders. However, more support is needed in the niche category. A senior MoH official told MAP that the fourth wave is expected to start end of November with the start of the winter season, and he stressed on the fact that the preparedness levels are not adequate in terms of case management or even testing. As for vaccines, it must be noted that only 123,248 people, 6% of the total population of Gaza, have received their second dose. When it comes to public opinion, the Palestinian Center for Policy and Survey Research, in their latest poll dated 21 September 2021, indicated that still there is public hesitation to take the vaccine as a mandatory procedure and this percentage reaches 35% of the population in Gaza and West Bank. While 24% of the poll’s audience indicated that they will not take the vaccine even when it becomes available. More is still needed to address this issue through RCCE. Thus, it is extremely important to try and alleviate the suffering of the people and Gaza and help the health system respond rapidly.

2 - Activities

The activities that MAP will implement are: • Procure and deliver urgently needed: drugs, disposables, and lab reagents essential for case management of COVID 19 of 3,600 ICU and pre-ICU patients. • Procure and deliver urgently needed PPEs to the front-line health workers. The PPEs will be used by health workers at any given time of the delivery of the items. MAP sends Tenders to all suppliers we have in the data base of vendors. Quotations/Offers are all collected and technical sheets for tender analysis are filled then sent to the technical committee members which are formed by MAP and MoH. We finalize the selection analysis based on: delivery time, price and reputation of the vendor and the history they have delivering items on time without delays. Preference will be given to local suppliers who are able to deliver fast. Please note that the activities have been coordinated with the health cluster, MoH and WHO. We made sure to speak with both stakeholders and to complement each other. While WHO will mainly focus on the testing and other fields, MAP will focus on the case management for ICU, pre-ICU, and also the other stages of hospital care for COVID-19 patients.• Implement an RCCE campaign through our partner Social Development Forum (SDF) to reach the public and increase their awareness about COVID-19 and the vaccination intake. MAP has implemented a similar campaign with this partner during 2020 and 2021 and we were able to reach more than 1.5 million citizens via more than 100 contributors to the campaign. The campaign will include a mixture of social media messages via Facebook, Instagram, and other famous platform while also there will be posters and billboards printed in addition to radio sports and SMS.a) Produce 3 radio spots about COVID-19 b) Print 20,000 posters and 5 billboards. c) Produce 50 social media messages via famous platforms. d) Send 25,000 awareness messages via SMS. MAP has a unique and adaptive procurement that enables us to procure in the fastest fashion possible while also completing the needed and required paperwork. Please note that MAP will take extra care to ensure that gender mainstreaming and disability inclusion are taken into consideration in this project. Gender and people with disabilities will be considered during the planning, delivery, monitoring and evaluation of all project activities. Such consideration will include, but not necessarily be limited to, the accessibility, acceptability, staffing, reporting and impact of those activities. This will be done via the community component with our partner via the different activities mentioned above. For example, women at reproductive age could be vulnerable to the disease and that this vulnerable category should be addressed uniquely in all the activities such as the SMS, radio spots, social media posts and other printed materials. Another example would be messages targeted for children who compose 23.3% of the target group of COVID-19 patients. Careful consideration would be put into drafting proper messages for this age group through consulting stakeholders and the children themselves. Accountability:In terms of accountability to beneficiaries, please note that MAP has a dedicated team combined of in-country and over-seas staff dedicated to Beneficiary Feedback Mechanism and Accountability. We hold weekly meetings and discuss the BFM in all the projects implemented with community partners and the MoH. We have recently held meetings with MoH staff and agreed that it would be very important to have BFM tools in projects where we have direct interaction with beneficiaries. The tools that we agreed on are a mix of WhatsApp number, FGDs, handing mini leaflets with data about BFM and giving the beneficiaries a mini scoring tool to measure satisfaction of services provided. The tools were discussed and chosen carefully to make sure that we reach as many people as possible regardless of the literacy level and also to make sure that there are secure ways of reporting without the feel of being exposed. MAP has also a page on our website dedicated to feedback and complaints in both Arabic and English. The algorithm at the organisation has a triage system that makes sure that complaints are all handled in complete confidentiality but also ensuring that serious complaints would reach the highest levels of management in the organisation to examine and investigate.As for the MoH, as a partner and beneficiary, we make sure that they are involved with us in all aspects of the project from the selection of the lists to the technical committees and the examination of items once being received. We also make sure that we inform them of the donors (if any) and how to provide feedback to us or our donors at the start of each project.As for the community partner, all that applies to MAP allies to the partner and we make sure that the BFM and accountability measures of the partner are on par with the standards needed. We also make sure that partners are trained and oriented on all aspects of BFM and AAP. That being said, SDF have already got all related trainings needed but MAP does refresher trainings for partners every year and we will make sure that SDF receives those trainings.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

MAP applies strict rules to ensure that the items procured are what is needed by MoH. We provide testing as part of the contracts, and we reject any items that do not match the technical criteria ensuring that the medical items are of the most benefit to the beneficiaries.The provision of the items will make sure that availability (free donated items with no charge) and accessibility are guaranteed for the targeted population. In addition, we make sure that the quality of the items procured is top notch as we have many clauses in our contracts defending this right. As for acceptability, MAP always seeks feedback from the beneficiaries and the partner and will make sure that the intervention is tailored to the need and fills a critical gap. MAP will also make sure to coordinate the response with the MoH, health cluster, other stakeholders in order to avoid duplication of services which in a way is considered as a harm being brought on the health system in general and the beneficiaries in specific.Safeguarding of children and vulnerable adults. MAP has developed an organisational algorithm for responding to Safeguarding concerns and region-specific algorithms for the initial investigation of such concerns. These documents were developed in conjunction with safeguarding experts and are considered fit for purpose and consistent with current sector guidance and the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS). They contain clear lines of responsibility and reporting and, where appropriate, confirm the timelines to be followed when responding to safeguarding concerns. All these documents are reviewed at least every two years. An incident report template is used to report any suspected breaches in MAP’s Code of Conduct or Safeguarding policy to MAP’s Safeguarding focal point. MAP partners all signed the code of conduct related to Safe guarding and all have got a training in 2021 that will be refreshed in 2022.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

MAP is neutral and does not lean toward any group over the other in the context we live in. MAP is impartial, and we provide aid to those who need it regardless of any other consideration such as gender, age, religion, etc. MAP is an independent organisation and is not affected by commercial powers. MAP is humanitarian and provides aid to those vulnerable and suffering all the time. We deliver directly to the CDS which in turn distributes to all MoH facilities in need ensuring access to all services by all vulnerable groups regardless of their age or gender. MAP monitors the delivery at the facility level to ensure that items are made available for the beneficiaries. In addition, the BFM in place will make sure that any breaches to the above principles are to be addressed immediately by the organisation in order to avoid any partial and un-equal access to the services.
