Project Summary

After 12 years of hostilities and persistent economic crisis, people in Syria saw their situation worsen when a series of earthquakes hit northern Syria and Türkiye on 6 and 20 February 2023. The earthquakes uprooted hundreds of thousands of families, resulted in almost 5,900 deaths and more than 12,800 people injured in Syria, and severely damaged infrastructures already weakened by the conflict.
Over 6.7 million Syrians are internally displaced, with 2.8 million in the northwest region alone, living in dire conditions. Access to essentials like safe water, food, medicine, healthcare, and livelihoods is limited, particularly in northwest Syria.
Over 2 million IDPs are living in last resort camps, Informal settlements, reception/transit, and collective centers (HNO, December 2023), 90% of the 1,510 IDP active IDP sites in NWS are self-settled and lack organized camp management systems. (CCCM Cluster, Dec 2023). The needs of those who are displaced are increasing rapidly and critical basic services such as health, shelter, food, WASH, and protection persist for hundreds of thousands of men, women, and children. Additionally, economic hardship, insecurity, and the threat of continued displacement leave more than 2 million people in NWS vulnerable and in need of essential, multi-sectoral assistance in formal and informal sites.
IOM will assess the needs of the most affected communities, engage with multiple and diverse stakeholders, provide small-scale care and maintenance activities, and coordinate the provision of multi-sectoral support in reception centers (RCs) and IDP sites.
IOM will implement the Area Based Approach to target the most vulnerable and underserved self-settled sites through Monitoring Assessment Team (MAT) to identify, assess, and refer for a sectoral response within Cross-Border. Furthermore, the project will strengthen emergency preparedness and fire response by establishing and training fire committees, distributing fire points and fire mitigation tools, refilling used fire extinguishers, and providing awareness courses on fire mitigation/prevention for displaced people. In addition, IOM will focus on strengthening community engagement to enable camp populations to participate in the decision-making process as well as to play an active role in camp management, by establishing inclusive camp management committees and delivering capacity-building training to them.
IOM will conduct community-led integration and skills-building activities in the targeted locations, emphasizing the inclusion of women and youth in the process. As part of these efforts, people in camps will be provided with vocational training, equipping them with the necessary expertise to pursue sustainable livelihood opportunities. Additionally, the initiative will support individuals with start-up grants to rehabilitate, develop, or start social or business entrepreneurship initiatives within the camps. Through these endeavors, IOM aims to empower individuals and foster resilience and self-reliance within the camp communities. These initiatives will be conducted in coordination with the early recovery (ERL) cluster to ensure alignment with broader development goals and maximize impact through collaborative action.
Finally, through the MAT, IOM will continue identifying and assessing the needs of most vulnerable informal sites and refer them to the IOM Cross-Border Unit for targeted assistance.
PSEA strategy: IOM recognizes the risk of SEA by IPs and their sub-contractors during the implementation. Key mitigation measures include ensuring that all IPs staff have read and signed their Code of Conduct, have taken part in PSEA training, have a dedicated complaint mechanism in place, and delivering PSEA awareness-raising activities and materials for beneficiaries and local communities. Furthermore, IOM, through its TPM teams, will ensure that activities are carried out in a principled manner.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Protection Risk Analysis Risks 1. Continued operations of RCs, and any possible coordinated relocation assistance will create negative pull and push factors for IDPs. 2: Needs assessments or intentions surveys will raise unrealistic expectations among IDPs living in camps and contribute to assessment fatigue. 3: The management structures could provide legitimacy to actors who are not truly representative of the IDP communities. 4: IDPs living in RCs and self-settled sites are evicted by landowners, local actors and security forces without prior notification. Mitigation Measures: 1: Maintain the CCCM guidance and position on camp expansion, establishment and support ensuring that camps are the last resort. 2: Ensure that the intention surveys and needs assessments are brief and inform operational guidance. 3: Ensure that any management structure is representative of the entire community. 4: Establish communication between camp management and local actors; develop guidance materials to support IPs communication with local authorities; maintain referral and incidents mechanism; ensure RCs closure and exit planning involve the affected community and local authorities; develop guidance for exit plans and camp closure and ensure IDPs are voluntarily leaving and other shelter options are identified. Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures. Assumptions: IOM assumes that the security situation remains calm, and IPs can access the locations that will be targeted. IOM assumes that a consent-based approach will continue to enable humanitarian access across borders. IOM assumes that humanitarian principles will be adhered to by all actors. IOM and IPs will monitor, track and report back on any incident that indicates a significant violation of humanitarian principles. IOM assumes that the IP will be able to obtain the necessary approvals from the local authorities to implement the project activities. IOM assumes that the population of targeted camps will be encouraged to identify and cooperate with camp management committees. Risks: Access restrictions and insecurity hinder the inability of partners to operate in the target location. Non-renewal of the consent-based approach. Local authorities’ interference in activities. Disengagement of IDPs during the implementation stage. Mitigation measures: Reporting security and access-related issues, establishing a security and accessibility plan for the targeted areas, and developing evacuation/contingency plans. Adequate and experienced IPs are trained on CCCM to ensure coordination of services continues. IOM through the IP will ensure the engagement of the local authorities from the initial stage. Furthermore, IPs must report incidents of interference. IOM will also utilize third-party monitoring to conduct warehouse verification, distribution, and post-monitoring activities. IOM through the IP will ensure the engagement of the IDPs in all the project stages.
