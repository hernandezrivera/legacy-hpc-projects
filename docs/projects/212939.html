Project Summary

Syria has been mired in a devastating conflict that has claimed hundreds of thousands of lives over more than a decade and caused its population immeasurable suffering. The war has left densely populated areas and others devastated and lifeless, particularly affecting vulnerable groups such as women and children. In light of the long absence of a peace framework that guarantees civil security, the humanitarian situation continues to deteriorate, with severe repercussions for the health sector. The destruction of infrastructure and the targeting of hospitals and healthcare centers have led to the deterioration of the health sector, affecting more than 70% of the Syrian population who are in desperate need of medical assistance.According to World Health Organization, more than half of the hospitals in the region are either closed or operating at low capacity. The mass emigration of healthcare workers, with nearly two-thirds of them leaving the country, is exacerbating the crisis. The remaining functioning health facilities face challenges such as lack of clean water, electricity and basic supplies. Al-Hasakah Governorate, an area severely affected by the conflict, had its share of this crisis .In recent months, it has faced external attacks, which exacerbated the already deteriorating situation. The attacks have left critical infrastructure either partially or completely out of service, with hospitals and healthcare centers bearing the brunt of the destruction. The targeting of the Suwaydiya station, which is responsible for supplying a large part of Al-Hasakah Governorate with electricity, led to the cessation of health services within medical facilities, which led to a significant deterioration in health care services.Access to basic health services remains a major challenge for the residents of the region, especially for individuals who face mobility challenges. The high costs associated with medical visits and admission to private hospitals - all of these reasons have led to a sharp decline in the general health status of individuals, and this was confirmed by the REACH organization in the winter of 2024.
This problem has had a major impact on the residents of the region, as they are witnessing widespread social and economic deterioration, leading to great psychological distress among the population.Estimates from HNO for Al-Hasakah Governorate drew a bleak picture, indicating that a large number of 829,023 people are in urgent need of health assistance. Against this background, civil society organizations are trying to address the multifaceted challenges facing societies. Recognizing the urgent needs in Al-Hasakah Governorate, Al-Khaboor Association has designed its activities in line with the 2024 Humanitarian Response Plan. Al-Khaboor aims to promote public health and community well-being by meeting a range of health care needs, including public health consultations, mental health services, and rehabilitation. physical, maternal and child health and ensuring their accessibility to all, with a commitment to alleviating suffering and strengthening resilience against this protracted crisis.
Overall objective: To improve and enhance the health conditions of local communities in Al-Hasakah Governorate.
Through this intervention, Al-Khaboor Association will establish and operate 5 mobile health clinics and 5 community health centers in Al-Hasakah Governorate. In addition, psychological support services will be integrated, ensuring a holistic approach to health care.
To ensure the provision of comprehensive healthcare, ALKhaboor Society will supply a range of essential medicines covering various medical conditions common in the region. These medications include, but are not limited to:Antibiotics: amoxicillin, , Analgesics and antipyretics: paracetamol. Antihypertensive drugs: amlodipine.Antidiabetics: metformin. Oral rehydration salts (ORS) to treat dehydration Anticonvulsants: phenytoin. Antacids: ranitidine.
