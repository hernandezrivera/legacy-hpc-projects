Project Summary

Improving the living conditions of 300 households affected by hurricane Matthew who were not able to rehabilitate their home through an aid provided by CARE. Their house was partially destroyed during the hurricane Matthew.

However, the beneficiaries were required to supply the wood for carpentry or additional materials if, however, the surface of the house was greater than the standard two bedrooms defined by CARE. At the end of the project all selected beneficiaries did not finalize the works because they were not able to provide their counterpart.
Households will be selected based on the following criteria: households whose house has been partially destroyed by hurricane Matthew who received shelter kit, household with 2 children under 5 years old; someone with reduced mobility; pregnant or nursing woman; female or teenage head of household; isolated heads of households, including a member who disappeared during the cyclone; loss of livelihood,

Needs

With regards to the Grand’Anse department – which is still in the process of recovering – it is worth reflecting back on what happened a couple of years ago. Hurricane Matthew, which made landfall in Haiti on October 4, 2016, had a devastating impact in the South and Grand Anse departments, which have been the most affected areas of the Southern peninsula by this category 4 hurricane. According to the Shelter and NFI Working Groups, Hurricane Matthew impacted an estimated 370,000 houses, of which 30,000 have been destroyed and 60,000 partially to seriously damaged. Mathew inflicted damage and losses in Haiti estimated at the equivalent of 22 percent of GDP (World Bank October 2017). According to the result of the Synthesis of the HNO analysis 333469 people are in need of urgent shelter assistance. The hurricane resulted in the destruction of 52% of the homes of the population affected by the disaster, causing households to move to temporary shelters or foster homes, which were also weakened. The loss of livelihoods during the disaster, lack of financial means and access to necessary materials, and adequate training to rebuild more safely did not allow a great number of affected households to rehabilitate their homes.Based upon the aforementioned elements, CARE proposes an intervention that will enable 300 vulnerable households in the department of Grand’Anse to rehabilitate their home that was destroyed during hurricane Matthew. Let us note that These households received a shelter kit containing materials for the rehabilitation of their homes from another project implemented by CARE until August 2018. The project's methodology was to support vulnerable households, identified by CARE, to rehabilitate their homes that were partially destroyed during Hurricane Matthew. CARE has provided shelter kits with ECHO funds containing: sheets, nails of different sizes, etc. to 1,000 households to undertake rehabilitation work. However, the beneficiaries were required to supply the wood for carpentry or additional materials if, however, the surface of the house was greater than the standard two bedrooms defined by CARE. At the end of the project all selected beneficiaries did not finalize the works because they were not able to provide their counterpart. With this intervention, CARE proposes to supply the missing materials to 300 households among the ECHO beneficiaries to enable them to rehabilitate their homes and thus have a house built according to the seismic and cyclonic standards. The materials to be distributed will be purchased on the basis of the pre-identified household needs in reference to the evaluation on the use of shelter kits already conducted by CARE among all beneficiaries. To contribute to the local economic development of the department, the acquisition of materials will be done using vendors located in the areas (CARE have used this approach during the response to Matthew). CARE will conduct awareness session on natural disaster risk and safe building methods for the 300 households. CARE will conduct refresher sessions in build back safer techniques for 100 technicians who have already been trained by CARE after Hurricane Matthew. Based on the result of assessment that will be conducted after the refreshment session, 50 technicians will be retained to conduct the work for the 300 households. CARE shelter team will supervise the work of the technician while household members will assist the technicians during the works. Technicians will receive a fee paid by the project for the number of days worked.CARE’s proposed intervention aligns perfectly with GoH sectoral programs and policies and is based on household identified needs. It will not only support the most vulnerable to meet their urgent needs; but will also contribute to strengthen and/or reinforce construction methods in the intervention areas.

Activities or outputs

The intervention will allow these 300 households to return to their homes under healthy and dignified conditions, CARE plans to distribute batches of materials to each beneficiary according to the intensity of the additional work to be done according to an estimate estimated on a case-by-case basis. To carry out the work, these beneficiaries will be supported by construction professionals (masons and carpenters) who have been trained by CARE in safe construction techniques to promote these techniques in these areas of intervention and reduce mitigating risks in these areas. the future. This action will be combined with technical support through the organization of training that will reduce the vulnerability of households to the future risks of natural disasters by rebuilding according to approved techniques and with materials complying with standards in risk areas, and to improve immediately the housing conditions of the beneficiaries.At the beginning of the project, CARE will organize community meetings to discuss with the community the overall strategy of the project and planned activities. CARE will present the beneficiary targeting process and selection criteria to the different communities. These meetings will be an opportunity to establish good communication between the project team and the community and to define a communication strategy for the future. The community will be part of the communication chain and will actively participate in the dissemination of information, the dissemination of key messages and the fulfillment of commitments made by the various project stakeholders. Indicators and targets:• Impact Indicator: % of disaster affected household in the communes targeted by the program that have receive assistance from CARE to address housing needs.
