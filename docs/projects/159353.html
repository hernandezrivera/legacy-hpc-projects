Project Summary

Millions of people in Ukraine, GCA and NGCA have been affected by the conflict. Within populations, some individuals and groups may be more vulnerable than others. It is necessary and a priority to identify these individuals or groups and to take measures that take into account their specific vulnerability. (Ukraine Thematic Update of Protection Group on Vulnerability, July 2018). In the area not controlled by the Government of Donetsk and Lugansk, there is no longer a program for the diagnostic and treatment of deaf children considered disabled under 7 years old. The AICM Ukraine Foundation proposes to help them with a program that includes the individual supply of hearing aids and medical diagnostic devices to the specialized service of the Donetsk Children's Clinic. Without this help, some of the children concerned may go into a state of prostration or even dementia for those who have other specific pathologies. This project aims to help thousands of young children who are located in all the separatist areas and who cannot travel to Ukraine, as most of them are in institutions. They must be diagnosed, equipped or treated for hearing loss. In the two Donetsk and Luhansk regions (NGCA), there are 1,058 children already registered with the requirement of hearing aids (information provided by the Donetsk de facto Ministry of Health and reported in the local statistics) and 3,500 awaiting the diagnosis. This project also includes prostheses for 48 partially deaf children after shelling (blast effect). Our on-site visit identified the following needs: the provision of detection equipment (which has been out of service for several years) at the only children's hospital (based in Donetsk) that can accommodate children from the uncontrolled Donetsk and Luhansk territories. The necessity of training and assistance for two local doctors by specialists sent by the Foundation for the duration of the project. And the help of one AICM local psychologist for children working with the Foundation since start of 2016. The ENT consultation center equipped with very basic equipment is where all the children of the NGCA come for consultations from villages and cities (recommended by pediatricians or family doctors). The coverage of the project is the totality of the NGCA districts. This project will not only cover the need for diagnostic equipment but, in accordance with specialist recommendations, AICM Ukraine will provide hearing aids on an individual basis and train a local maintenance technician. Indeed, hearing aids for young children require special care because there are often technical interventions. In addition, a hearing aid for a small child must be replaced by another (often reconditioned) every two/three years. There are three categories of hearing aids. Amplifiers (an external box around with a headphone), individual hearing aids and cochlear implants. Given that it is a humanitarian aid only the first 2 choices will be proposed because they are more economical. Once the diagnosis is confirmed, an individual request will be sent to the local representative of AICM who will ask a second opinion to our expert who directs the ENT Chair at the National Academy of Medicine of Ukraine. He will validate the choice of the type of prosthesis and the group deliveries will take place twice a month from Ukraine. This is our local psychologist, who is a specialist for disabled children who will do an assessment of the child before and during the adaptation (which will be done by the Doctors).
The diagnosis is always accompanied by psychological support of the child who must be prepared for a white period of incomprehension according to whether his deafness is old or not. Special care will be given to children with other congenital, infectious or trauma conditions.

1 - Needs

Since 2014, the territories not controlled by the Ukrainian Government are completely isolated and the health facilities are not supported by the Ukrainian Government (according to UNICEF 2016). 132 hospitals were partially or totally destroyed (according to Reach report 2019) and most of the medical equipment is not maintained (according to WHO July 2019). Disable Children deaf and/or autistic are not diagnosed and helped for more than 5 years, 3.500 of them are waiting for a diagnosis and 1058 for a possible adaptation of prosthesis. These children are mostly located in local institutions in all the NGCA territory. They can’t cross the border to receive such care. This specific equipment is not accessible from Ukraine to this territory except by the Humanitarian Organizations, and there is not any comparable project in any NGO since the start of the conflict except some punctual help provided by the ICRC.In the NGCA of Donetsk and Luhansk, there is no more program for the diagnostic and treatment for defects of hearing and deaf children (providing hearing aids, performing cochlear implant, surgery, etc.) because most of the specialists left the territory in 2014 and the equipment is not working since 2015. The possibilities of examining children especially before 7yo, with earing defect, mental disabilities (including autism) and those wounded during the shelling are at this moment extremely limited to simple control with an old audiometer. This exam does not give any information about the pathology and if it is curable. The diagnostic has to be confirmed before 5/7yo to preserve the capacity of rehabilitation, especially for the autistics. The same for children with congenital pathology of the hearing system.48 children wounded during the shelling are suffering from such defects and will be treated by this project. At present, 1058 children are registered in the office of a specialist in the Donetsk Children's Clinical Hospital (RCCH) covering the regions of Donetsk and Luhansk, including 1010 in urgent need of hearing prosthesis and 48 wounded children during the shelling. In addition, 3500 are waiting to be diagnosed.Currently, medical specialists are ready to be trained for the new equipment and to implement the standard of diagnostic. The structure exists but there are no more equipment and the financial possibility to acquire specific prostheses is very limited. The budget of the functioning and the salaries of this center are fully covered by the local authorities.
