Project Summary

"Following the signing of MoU with the Government of Bangladesh, the Rohingya refugee response on Bhasan Char will support and complement the work of the Government, in charge of the overall response, as well as Bangladesh NGOs, delivering critical assistance on the island. Ensuring the centrality of protection and gender mainstreaming, the response aims to provide protection and multi-sectorial assistance to the Rohingya refugees who voluntarily relocated to and reside on the island, in coordination with the Government of Bangladesh. The response on Bhasan Char presents a set of specific opportunities and challenges compared to the situation in the Cox’s Bazar camps, notably in ensuring the voluntary nature of the relocation and freedom of movement from and to the island on a needs basis, as well as the logistical considerations of establishing a new operation in a remote island location.
Bhasan Char’s unique context, combined with the significant infrastructural investment of the Government, allows for a leaner response with a lighter operational footprint. The coordination structure on Bhasan Char will be distinct from the existing coordination structure in Cox’s Bazar and remain agile and needs driven. UNHCR will lead the response on the island in collaboration with the Additional Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commissioner (ARRRC) as well as other Governmental representatives on the island and in Noakhali District. The response on Bhasan Char will be delivered through the work of the following eight Sectors: i) Protection, ii) Health and Nutrition, iii) Food Security, iv) Education, v) Non-Food Items, Site Management and Shelter, vi) Livelihoods and Self-Reliance/ Energy and Environment vii) WASH and viii) Common Logistics and ICT services."

1 - Identified Needs

The findings of multi-sectoral technical assessments conducted in November 2021 provide a comprehensive mapping of gaps and needs that inform the operational plan to ensure the human rights, basic needs and wellbeing of Rohingya refugees on Bhasan Char are upheld and provided for. Results show that Rohingya refugees living on the island have limited access to protection and legal services which can expose them to vulnerable siutation. Rohingya refugees reported limited access to healthcare on the island, derived from the limited capacities, unavailability of regular health professionals on the island and lack of medical supplies. Nutritional assessments showed precarious conditions, mainly in children, due in part to inconstant food service delivery, lack of resources and qualified personnel. The need for greater access to services is compounded by the remoteness of the island, vulnerability and risks to natural hazards, lack of coordination among service providers, limited access to information and essential non-food items, as well as the sense of idleness caused by the lack of education and livelihood opportunities.
