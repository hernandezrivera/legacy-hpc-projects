Project Summary

The roll out of the Myanmar Curriculum Pilot (MCP) in the Rohingya refugee camps is undisputedly a significant milestone in the goal to ensure quality education for all. The pilot also represents a timely opportunity to address the issues of adolescent & youth sexual and reproductive health and rights (SRHR), gender-based violence (GBV), gender equity, and their linkages through educational and skills development programmes in schools, clubs, as well as public awareness activities.
To this end, UNFPA proposes to deliver Generation Breakthrough (GB) - UNFPA flagship programme for in-school A&Y - in learning centres and community network schools to compliment the MCP, given the meaningful gaps in the MC as it pertains to the abovementioned topics. GB also entails implementing a contextualized version of the tested “Gender Equity Movement in Schools (GEMS)” curriculum across the intervention areas, targeting both girls and boys ages 11-18 years.
Through GB, A&Y will be able to access adequate and complete information through the delivery of GEMS in addition to counselling through a phone helpdesk and an interactive radio forum. GB will dismantle A&Y taboos around GBV and SRHR and will focus on respectful, equal, consensual, and healthy relationship building. GB will also focus on changing A&Y norms, values and behavior, while addressing those adults that have a great influence on their lives, thereby creating an enabling environment for positive change. Through effective SRHR and GBV information, awareness, skills and adequate A&Y friendly services, the programme will contribute to reduction of GBV, unwanted pregnancies, unsafe abortions, maternal mortality, and the spread of HIV and other STIs. GB will be for, with, and by A&Y and hence young people themselves will be engaged at every stage of the project. Through peer education and campaign activities A&Y will establish new norms for respectful, violence-free relationships, and future parenting.

1 - Identified Needs

Of the estimated 855,000 Rohingya refugees who are in need and targeted under the 2020 Joint Response Plan (JRP), approximately 38% are A&Y between the ages of 10 and 24. A particularly vulnerable target group, largely not prioritized, A&Y– have specific SRH needs as well as psychosocial support (PSS) needs, which must be addressed. These needs become even more imperative in the current protracted humanitarian situation, given the absence of a sustainable solution for Rohingya refugees, and where interventions must take into consideration the humanitarian-development-peace nexus, so as to ensure peaceful co-existence among community members. The 2020 JRP acknowledges that A&Y, face specific risks and their needs have not been sufficiently addressed in the response thus far. While the SRH, PSS, and GBV prevention and response interventions provided by UNFPA focuses on the needs of all relevant age groups, there is an increasing need to further these efforts and ensure the specific needs of A&Y refugees are given increased priority. Importantly, the particular vulnerabilities of A&Y girls and women, and the violence they have experienced has been recognized by many humanitarian actors. Similarly, adolescent and young boys show levels of distress due to memories of violence before their forced displacement, as a result of missing family, the lack of opportunities for schooling and the limited recreational activities available to them. Moreover, it is well-known that Rohingya boys and men were also targeted for conflict related sexual violence in Myanmar and adolescent boys are still vulnerable to different forms of sexual violence, including trafficking. The challenges experienced by A&Y in the Rohingya refugee camps have been further exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic. First, they have already been impacted by the closure of educational opportunities as well as restrictions on movement, which has disrupted their access to peers and educators. Prolonged closures of learning facilities and movement restrictions have led to increased levels of emotional distress and anxieties amongst A&Y, and in turn lead to higher incidences of violence in the home, unhealthy coping strategies, and suicidal ideation. In the likely event that a young persons parent or caregiver is infected, quarantined, or passes away, the resulting protection and PSS needs for that young person will be dire, particularly for adolescent girls. Moreover, in the camps, A&Y already face very high levels of domestic and intimate partner violence as well as child marriage. Due to the mandatory lockdown and its impacts on economic stability - as well as movement restrictions, people are confined, and protection systems are weakened - adolescent girls and young women are at even greater risk of GBV and sexual abuse and exploitation. Finally, in the refugee camps, not only are rates of GBV and child marriage exceedingly high, but A&Y are at greater risk of contracting COVID-19 due to their substandard living conditions. A&Y in the refugee camps also have limited access to technology, internet, and alternate forms of education and information, including on how to mitigate exposure to COVID-19. Finally, Cox's Bazar is geographically located in a disaster-prone area where monsoon, cyclone, floods, and landslides are frequently occurring. The readiness to address the challenges experienced by A&Y during emergencies is relatively low due to insufficient knowledge and skill, limited commodities, and policies related to A&Y during a disaster response. This reality reveals the necessity for SRHR and gender-responsive life skills education programming targeting A&Y as well as provisions to ensure that in the event of an emergency educational programming remains uninterrupted, in view of equipping young people with the requisite skills and knowledge to ensure their wellbeing as they transition to adulthood and leaving no one behind.
