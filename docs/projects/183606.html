Project Summary

The project meets strategic and cluster objectives:
Strategic objective 02: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL.
HEALTH AND NUTRITION CLUSTER OBJECTIVE 1: Ensure the availability of acceptable and quality essential healthcare services to vulnerable communities in oPt.
In general, this project will contribute to addressing the lifesaving needs for high risk babies at neonatal units in the Gaza and improving neonatal care management in order to contribute to reducing mortality, morbidity and long-term disability among newborns.
The project’s main two objectives are:
1- Saving neonatal lives by ensuring the availability of life saving items and essential infection control supplies at the neonatal units. MAP aims to achieve the project’s objectives in coordination with the Ministry of Health, UNICEF, WHO, UNFPA and other stakeholders.
2- Improving the skills and competencies of staff at the neonatal units on neonatal life support, neonatal safe transport, and infection control.

1 - Needs

MAP with MoH and other stakeholders are working to promote and support neonatal care services, as newborn babies remain vulnerable. In Gaza, “aggravating social determinants of health, particularly, affect the most vulnerable, impacting the health of mothers and babies. The period around childbirth is particularly critical for both mothers and newborns, hence childbirth care is lifesaving. The overburdened hospitals in Gaza are struggling to provide quality care for newborns and to cope with deteriorating health determinants” . In low-income countries, half of babies born before 32 weeks die due to the bad socio-economic status affecting health conditions, while in high income countries these babies survive (Lincetto and Banerjee, 2020). According to the WHO’s global health estimates, neonatal conditions were the fifth leading cause of death in 2019 and the biggest cause of disability-adjusted life years. The 2022 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) for the oPt indicates that there are 32,400 neonates in total with several health conditions. Of those it is estimated that half would be in need of transfer to nurseries and neonatal intensive care units for specialised life-saving treatment. The situation is exacerbated by the ongoing and protracted shortages of staff, drugs, disposables, salaries, infection control supplies and skills of those treating the babies. According to MAP’s assessment in 2021, there are still issues related to the capacity of NICUs in Gaza to address the needs of neonatal babies but mainly these could be summarized in: 1) lack of resources, 2) shortages in staff and skills, 3) the effects of the pandemic on the neonatal service. We are going to examine each one below more in details: Gaza Neonates amid COVID-19: Since March 2020, COVID-19 further impacted 80,000 women and 210,00 children including 25,000 neonatal babies in the oPt. The redirecting of funds towards the COVID-19 response threatens other vulnerable groups such as neonates. In a recent assessment interview with the Gaza Neonatal Network (GNN) chairman, he indicated that MAP was the only INGO that supported them with the surfactant medication; a lifesaving drug used for the serious respiratory issues of premature babies. In addition, the relocation of some neonatal services from one location to another due to COVID-19, such as moving the neonatal unit at EGH to Nasser, led to overcrowding of the unit. To cope with the pandemic, MoH needed to divert significant resources, including the manpower (doctors, nurses and midwives or infection control team leaders trained by MAP) from regular service delivery to response efforts in the quarantine centres. The coronavirus disease pandemic represents an additional challenge to address the needs of preterm newborns and their families and creates significant disruption of supply and demand of neonatal health services. Shortages in staff and Skills: Another major issue to address is the skills of staff at the NICUs. MoH have been suffering from high staff turnover and a rotational policy to fill the huge gaps of manpower; neonatal units are no exception. Therefore, it is important to continue training on Neonatal Life Support (NLS), neonatal safe transfer (NST) and NLS in-service to raise the skills of staff rotated through these units, and to activate the telemedicine and the virtual consultation as alternatives within the time of travel restrictions amid the Covid-19 pandemic.Added to this is MAP’s own evidence from the neonatal monthly monitoring data which shows that neonatal case fatality in 2020 reached 11.3% compared to 10% in 2019. So far the neonatal case fatality in 2021 reached 7.7% but there were huge spikes such as in June 2021 when it reached 14%. Incidence of neonatal sepsis deaths from total deaths reached 13.1% in 2021. All in which indicates the importance to continue supporting the infection control practices at the NICUs.Provision of essential neonatal medications, disposables, equipment, and infection control supplies: The MoH, GNN with the International Cooperation Department’s (ICD) collaborate with stakeholders to continuously assess the needs. However, gaps still exist, and the needs are huge. The estimated budget to cover the neonatal needs for 2021 in total was 4,610,000 USD and this is expected to be almost the same in 2022, according to the head of GNN. Recent MoH zero stock levels of drugs and disposables indicated that in September 2021 there was a shortage of 54% in MCH drugs specifically. For disposables, there are shortages of about 7% in ICU disposables specifically, which the NICUs are part of.

2 - Activities

• Procurement of life saving neonatal drugs and consumables to be used for 15,000 at high risk newborn babies/year including female and male babies admitted to neonatal ICU to benefit from the neonatal care project’s interventions. This will happen in coordination with MoH, UNICEF, WHO, UNFPA and all related parties. • Procurement of infection control supplies and printing educational materials (infection control booklets) to be used for 15,000 high risk newborn babies/year including female and male babies admitted to NICU and who will benefit from the neonatal care project’s interventions. This will happen coordination with MoH, UNFPA, WHO and UNICEF. • Procure necessary equipment that is urgently needed to address the over-crowding in NICUs. This will happen in coordination with MoH and NGOs. • Implement three Neonatal Life Support Trainings NLS and three Infection Control training courses for neonatal and obstetric department for 200 health professionals. 120 female and 80 male health professionals working at the neonatal and obstetric units of MoH will be targeted. These training will be coordinated with MOH different departments. • Implement on-job NLS training courses and procure the needed equipment for these courses. These trainings will be also coordinated with the above-mentioned parties. Please note that the usual way that MAP conducted these trainings was through medical mission, and that MAP was able to bring two medical missions in 2021 related to neonatal support. All the emergency services that MAP will support will contribute to the reduction of long term disabilities in neonates as these babies are endangered and subject to death or develop long-term disabilities. With regards to gender, MAP has designed the project activities to meet the different needs of women, men, boys and girls. The project will target male and female neonates but for the training, more emphasis will be given to female health practitioners as they form the majority of the workers at NICUs, plus, the local trainers’ cadre that MAP has invested in, who help the consultant in provision of all courses, are a mixture of both genders to address cultural sensitivities. As for partnership with local NGOs, while we do not have direct links under the neonatal project with any community partner; MAP has other partners involved in its MCH projects (with CFTA and Abdelshafi Association) focusing on women at reproductive age, MAP is working with these women to support prenatal and antenatal care which have direct impact on the neonatal morbidity and mortality. Please note that the focus of this project is about provision of life-saving items and equipment to the NICUs that are solely by MoH. There is only one neonatal unit in the NGO community which is considered as an intermediate unit. The babies received at this NGO unit, if extra care is needed, they will be transferred to one of the MoH NICUs eventually. Accountability:In terms of accountability to beneficiaries, please note that MAP has a dedicated team combined of in-country and over-seas staff dedicated to Beneficiary Feedback Mechanism and Accountability. We hold weekly meetings and discuss the BFM in all the projects implemented with community partners and the MoH. We have recently held meetings with MoH staff and agreed that it would be very important to have BFM tools in projects where we have direct interaction with beneficiaries. The tools that we agreed on are a mix of WhatsApp number, FGDs, handing mini leaflets with data about BFM and giving the beneficiaries a mini scoring tool to measure satisfaction of services provided. The tools were discussed and chosen carefully to make sure that we reach as many people as possible regardless of the literacy level and also to make sure that there are secure ways of reporting without the feel of being exposed. MAP has also a page on our website dedicated to feedback and complaints in both Arabic and English. The algorithm at the organisation has a triage system that makes sure that complaints are all handled in complete confidentiality but also ensuring that serious complaints would reach the highest levels of management in the organisation to examine and investigate.As for the MoH, as a partner and beneficiary, we make sure that they are involved with us in all aspects of the project from the selection of the lists to the technical committees and the examination of items once being received. We also make sure that we inform them of the donors (if any) and how to provide feedback to us or our donors at the start of each project.

3 - Indicators

The project will be monitored thoroughly via MAP’s staff including the Senior Programme Manager, the Emergency and Complex Hospital Care Manager, the programme officer, the programme assistant, the procurement department, and the finance department under the auspices of the Director of Gaza. PMEAL is a crucial part of our work and all our projects have logframes, M&E plans, implementation plans and detailed budgets. MAP conducts field visits to the NICUs, conducts interviews with relevant MoH staff, collects data and official records from CDS and also distribution lists to MoH, and has its own internal quarterly reports, financial reports, and end of phase reports. In addition, please note for this project in specific MAP collects monthly data from the 6 NICUs about neonatal mortality rates in general and those related to sepsis in specific to inform our decision making but also the MoH’s and the Gaza Neonatal Network (GNN). To add, MAP collects monthly data on Infection Control Prevention (IPC) compliance inside the NICUs which also helps us in planning proper interventions and workshops related to IPC. The last special M&E we have under this project is the fact that collect data on Retinopathy of prematurity (RoP) related cases. During the years RoP data has helped us indicate the levels of procurement needed to address the issue but also decide on new interventions related to treatment and training needs for MoH staff. It should be mentioned that for all training courses, we conduct pre and post tests to measure knowledge but also have satisfaction surveys. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

MAP has designed the project activities to meet the different needs of women, men, boys and girls. The project will target male and female neonates but for the training, more emphasis will be given to female health practitioners as they form the majority of the workers at NICUs, plus, the local trainers’ cadre that MAP has invested in, who help the consultant in provision of all courses, are a mixture of both genders to address cultural sensitivities. MAP applies strict rules to ensure that the items procured are what is needed by MoH. We provide testing as part of the contracts, and we reject any items that do not match the technical criteria ensuring that the medical items are of the most benefit to the beneficiaries.The provision of the items will make sure that availability (free donated items with no charge) and accessibility are guaranteed for the targeted population. In addition, we make sure that the quality of the items procured is top notch as we have many clauses in our contracts defending this right. As for acceptability, MAP always seeks feedback from the beneficiaries and the partner and will make sure that the intervention is tailored to the need and fills a critical gap. MAP will also make sure to coordinate the response with the MoH, health cluster, GNN, UNICEF, UNFPA, and other stakeholders in order to avoid duplication of services which in a way is considered as a harm being brought on the health system in general and the beneficiaries in specific.Safeguarding of children and vulnerable adults. MAP has developed an organisational algorithm for responding to Safeguarding concerns and region-specific algorithms for the initial investigation of such concerns. These documents were developed in conjunction with safeguarding experts and are considered fit for purpose and consistent with current sector guidance and the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS). They contain clear lines of responsibility and reporting and, where appropriate, confirm the timelines to be followed when responding to safeguarding concerns. All these documents are reviewed at least every two years. An incident report template is used to report any suspected breaches in MAP’s Code of Conduct or Safeguarding policy to MAP’s Safeguarding focal point.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

MAP is neutral and does not lean toward any group over the other in the context we live in. MAP is impartial, and we provide aid to those who need it regardless of any other consideration such as gender, age, religion, etc. MAP is an independent organisation and is not affected by commercial powers. MAP is humanitarian and provides aid to those vulnerable and suffering all the time. We deliver directly to the CDS which in turn distributes to all MoH facilities in need ensuring access to all services by all vulnerable groups regardless of their age or gender. MAP monitors the delivery at the facility level to ensure that items are made available for the beneficiaries. One of MAP’s aims of this intervention is to prevent long term disability resulting from poor services provided to neonates at NICUs. In addition, the BFM in place will make sure that any breaches to the above principles are to be addressed immediately by the organisation in order to avoid any partial and un-equal access to the services.
