Project Summary

The overarching objective of this project is to provide humanitarian assistance through provision of
hygiene kits to address the needs of the most vulnerable households in the protracted
Syrian crisis. The project will target vulnerable populations in Aleppo, combining
emergency response and cash for work schemes through the establishment of a sewing
workshop that produces facial masks to combat the COVID-19 pandemic in the area. In
addition, chosen households will also receive comprehensive hygiene kits to help combat
the pandemic and ensure dignity and health. Beneficiaries will also get information on how to reduce risk during the pandemic.

4 - Needs

After almost a decade of protracted crisis, the WASH needs in Syria remains large. According to the Syria HNO, based on WASH assessments, 53 per cent of the Syria population requires WASH assistance, with 28 per cent of these people considered in acute need (severity four and five). In terms of affordability and accessibility of WASH items, WASH assessment data indicates that hygiene supplies are available in most of the local markets. Nevertheless, in comparison to resident populations it is more difficult for IDPs and returnees, notably for female-headed households , to afford hygiene items with shampoo, washing powder and cleaning liquid reported most frequently as unaffordable with up to 20-25 per cent of households indicating not being able to afford such items. According to a recent survey done by Dorcas and partners, 61% of people surveyed in Aleppo cannot purchase personal hygiene items. In fact, the main reasons for not utilizing soap are that (1) it is too expensive and (2) it cannot be found within the marketplace. Good handwashing practices are found in 66% of Aleppo households, meaning that households wash often with soap and water. Regarding training, 56% in Aleppo have received a hygiene training in the last year. For this reason, distribution of hygiene kits as well as information on hygiene and COVID-19 protection are included in this proposal.

5 - Implementation of activities

Distribution of hygiene kits: Under this outcome, vulnerable individuals and households will be identified and assessed for selection from thecatchment population of the community centers in Aleppo based on pre-determinedvulnerability criteria such as women-headed households, people with disability, HHs with alarge number of dependents, people affected by conflict-induced displacement anddestruction in Syria. Hygienekits will be procured and distributed to vulnerable households and individuals. Post distribution monitoring (PDM) surveys will be carried out by partners to determine beneficiary satisfaction with and use of the provided items. Humanitarian assistance in the form ofhygiene kits will benefit 10,200 individuals in Syria. Dorcas will also distribute masks as part of the hygiene kits that fall under a separate cash for work scheme.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Dorcas mainstreams protection in all its activities. Dorcas prioritizes safety & dignity, and avoids causing harm to beneficiaries by preventing and minimizing as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the intervention conducted which can increase people’s vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. Dorcas makes sure that people have access to assistance and services without any barriers (e.g. discrimination). Dorcas pays special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services such as children, women, elderly and persons with specific needs.Dorcas has an already developed accountability system including PSEA which allow affected populations to address concerns and complaints. Within the community center, there are complaint boxes with posters to explain their purpose, and employees always explain to new beneficiaries how the process functions. In the community center, there is always a child safe place that is enclosed on all sides and has a specific entrance through which people can enter and exit. Dorcas is committed to beneficiary data protection in all phases of the project. All files will be kept in locked cabinet files, cases of protection will be coded, files and codes are kept in separate places. Dorcas is committed not to share names with anyone since it can put at risk all the mission inside Syria. Dorcas also commits not to share names with TPM unless with consent from the beneficiaries themselves. Specific to this project, Dorcas will ensure safety of beneficiaries is considered during kit distributions and that kits are appropriate to the needs of individual beneficiaries.
