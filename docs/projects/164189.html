Project Summary


The project would include activities that would ensure high number of displaced people with disabilities living in the community to be aware of the risks of war remnants and mine actions and are better linked with the medical services provided within the community.
Hurras will make sure that will be able to reach all affected people. Hurras will target 130 schools in Idleb and Aleppo including 15,000 children between 6-18years old including male and female,300,000 beneficiaries (male and female) 40,000 of them from the targeted children’s families and 270,000 beneficiaries including people with disabilities and their families. Moreover, Hurras will be distributing 20,000 magazines included awareness brochures about the mines and 180,000 posters will be distributed in the communities through the local councils and other community leaders. Furthermore, Hurras will reach out to 400 children between 0-18 years old who have physical disabilities due to being exposed to war remnants and mine actions.

4 - Needs

According to the 2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), One in two people in Syria live at risk of explosive hazards. Approximately one third of all populated communities in Syria are estimated to be contaminated . Based on current population estimates, 11.5 million men, women, boys, and girls live in the 2,562 communities reporting explosive hazard contamination in the last two years. These communities are spread throughout the country and correlate to areas that have seen a high intensity of hostilities over the past 12 months. Explosive hazard contamination can have severe socio-economic implications on families and communities, depriving them of livelihoods and sources of income, increasing vulnerabilities. It also prevents the safe delivery of humanitarian assistance, affects population movement and limits the scope for recovery efforts. High levels of past and ongoing hostilities have contributed to far reaching explosive hazard contamination across both rural and urban areas. Respondents in assessed communities reported that agricultural land, roads, private property were the most frequently contaminated locations, followed by schools, other public infrastructure and hospitals . As the scale of the explosive hazard threat is not yet fully known, prevention and mitigation measures to reduce the impact of the contamination on civilians need to be comprehensive and large-scale.Explosive hazard accidents can result in serious injury and death, leading to long-term physical and psychological trauma for survivors and their families. The number of casualties from explosive accidents has steadily increased the burden on the health sector, particularly with regards the provision of specialized services including trauma care, physical rehabilitation, and psychosocial support in the short and long-term. While needs are significant, available resources remain limited.

5 - Implementation of activities

In this project, Hurras will target 172 schools in Idleb and Aleppo including 15,000 children between 6-18years old including male and female,300,000 beneficiaries (male and female) 40,000 of them from the targeted children’s families and 270,000 beneficiaries including people with disabilities and their families. Moreover, Hurras will be distributing 20,000 magazines included awareness brochures about the mines and 180,000 posters will be distributed in the communities through the local councils and other community leaders. Furthermore, Hurras will reach 400 children between 0-18 years old who have physical disabilities due to being exposed to war remnants and mine actions. Hurras has had previous experience in humanitarian mine action projects whereas Hurras included outreach campaigns about protection against mines in its magazine (Tayara Warak) and distribute posters with the same topic in at least 200 schools in the targeted communities by Hurras.In this project, Hurras will implement the following activities in the targeted locations:1. Train 172 focal point from Hurras’s targeted schools and community leaders on Protection of mine actions and remnants of war. 2. Conduct follow-up meetings with the community leaders including the action points needed to achieve the output.3. Provide specialized services for children and youth affected by the war remnants and mine action, which include medication and rehabilitation related issues.4. Conduct awareness raising sessions in schools about protection of mines action and remnants of war.5. Print and distribute 200,000 copies of Tayara Warak magazine and posters on protection of mines and remnants of war. (Using Hurras’s posters and its previous experience)6. Conduct live interviews between beneficiaries and people with disabilities to explain their experience and mine effects

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: a safe environment allows the staff to implement the activities (low airstrike bombing),no delay in the provision of financial resources, engagement of local community with the committees, commitment of partner groups to Hurras SOP &availability of services providers or services required as part of case management plan.Risks: safeguarding incidents (Medium): HRS has a well-established safeguarding allegation system that is accessible by children, families and community members. Each activity is assigned a SGO and stakeholder are made aware of how to report incidents through multiple ways: online system, verbal communication with safeguarding staff and complaint boxes.Community acceptance (Medium): before engaging in any new community HRS outreach and community relation teams establish formal relationships with independent CP committees.The risk to personal safety of field staff (Medium): HRS has a duty of care policy and all staff are trained on safety measures including emergency locatorsSecurity incidents (Medium to High): All staff receive safety training and all facilities are screened and provided with first aid and fire extinguishers in addition to a selected safe room and evacuation plan. Poor staff capacity (Medium): HRS invests in 4 weeks training of staff when they join, and ongoing coaching and supervision processes are in place. HRs work closely with partners to identify the capacity building gaps and put in place training programs to fill the gaps.Poor evidence-based and knowledge management due to remote management (Low): in addition to M&E team, HRS uses staff from different offices and projects to cross-check its programming. A potential loss of money due to theft or airstrikes (Medium): HRS’s finance team limit the cash on hand to a certain ceiling to minimize the risk and rely on an established network of cash transfer offices.
