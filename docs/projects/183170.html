Project Summary

Medair’s health work has been operating since 2014, supporting both refugees and vulnerable Lebanese through health centres and community outreach. Whilst originally focussed within health centres within Social Development Centres as part of the Ministry of Social Affairs, since the beginning of 2021, Medair has expanded operations to the South / Nabatiye and working with an additional 15 health centres within the Ministry of Public Health network, particularly for routine vaccinations. Medair opened a COVID mass vaccination centre in Saida in June 2021. There is a growing focus on nutrition activities such as IYCF, which are detailed under the Nutrition sector of the ERP.
Health activities that are aligned under the health sector of the ERP include the provision of medication, PPE provision and IPC training for health centres, and integrated messaging through community health volunteers (CHVs). 54 CHVs focused on reaching Lebanese populations will be mobilised from pre-existing Medair networks, and while the remaining number 46+ (additional figures contingent on anticipated future funding) will be newly recruited (with additional CHVs as part of the LCRP). These Community Health Volunteers will reach all populations within their geographic areas of intervention (around the supported health centres).
Medair has seen a four-fold increase in the usage of medication in recent months as more Lebanese are dependent on medication in the public sector due to shortages within pharmacies. Medair therefore aims to provide more medication through supplying pharmacies in supported health centres – expanding the number of centres supported. Medication will be provided free of charge to all those accessing the centres. Medair acknowledges the challenges with procuring medicines in the current context given we do not hold an importation license, yet is actively participating in coordination conversations with other agencies to explore alternative options for procurement of imports as well as engaging with advocacy efforts at the humanitarian and governmental levels. Medair will also include a small cash-for-health component for vulnerable people needing to access medication from other locations, which has been piloted through a blockchain modality in the Bekaa over the past six months. Medair also has experience in cash-for-health programming within the Middle East region, in addition to cash programming within Lebanon.
Medair’s existing COVID programming already supports health centres with the provision of PPE and IPC training for staff and will be expanding this to a further 10 health centres. For more information on the centres, please see the list sent to the Health Sector.
Medair also has an active shelter team who cover water and sanitation components, in addition to expanding into alternative energy options. The health and shelter teams have recently been jointly assessing the health facilities to consider how to ensure the continuation of health services, including WASH and solar options – both fridges for cold chain, and for the whole centre to enable lights, equipment and the internet (to access PHENICS) to function. Medair is therefore well placed to provide integrated health, shelter and WASH solutions.
Medair is an active part of the health sector, and has close working relationships with both the Ministry of Public Health, the Ministry of Social Affairs and other key stakeholders listed in the ERP. Medair has programming operating across Lebanon, including in BML and the North/Akkar, although the majority of health programming under this ERP is anticipating to take place in Bekaa/Baalbek and the South/Nabatiye.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

O1: Medair will provide a range of quality and essential medicines (as per the WHO/MoPH agreed list) and medical supplies. This will be through pharmacies at health clinics, together with a cash-for-health component for vulnerable people needing to access medication from other locations. Medair also plans to provide 30 facilities with cold chain facilities for non-COVID vaccinations, through solar fridges/systems, which builds on existing activities to support routine immunisations. Medair is already coordinating with MoPH and UNICEF for this. Medair will also be training health care workers on the new ANC/PNC package.O2: No activities under this objective.O3: Whilst Medair is active on the vaccination side of the COVID-response and conducting IPC measures at Medair-supported clinics, none of the activities align under this ERP objective.O4: Medair will provide 27+ primary health care centres with PPE and complementary training (additional figures contingent on anticipated future funding). 100 outreach workers will aim to reach 100,000+ people with integrated and accessible health messaging. 45 frontline health care workers will be trained on safe identification and referral of GBV, with 4 who work particularly with migrants attending training on culturally sensitive approaches to migrants.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Medair inducts all staff, casuals and volunteers on PSEA and other protection risks, and follows a standard schedule of refresher training, including the use of UN online modules. Medair has an active Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) team, who conducts regular monitoring, including exit surveys at health facilities, assessments of health centres and post-implementation quantitative and qualitative monitoring to actively check if there is any cause for concern. Medair publicises the hotline through posters and distribution of hotline cards, with an established SOP and mechanism for the handling of complaints and feedback. Any complaints are investigated as appropriate, with the support of HR and Medair's Global Support Office as needed. Medair, as an implementing partner, underwent a PSEA assessment by UNHCR in 2021 and received good feedback. Protection, gender and social inclusion are considered in programming to ensure equitable access.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Medair's is fully committed to AAP, with an independent MEAL team who works with the project teams to ensure that the different aspects of AAP are implemented, with a dedicated Accountability Officer. This includes availability of information, through flyers and posters, including information about services available, costs and expectations of behaviour. It also includes participation of communities both in the design of projects, such as focus groups when developing projects, and ongoing monitoring and evaluation. The feedback system includes a hotline, with options of both whatsapp and mobile, with SOPs implemented for following up and responding to calls. Learning is integrated into projects through both informal communication, regular reports and lessons learned events. AAP is identified within the country strategy, with dedicated sessions in inductions and ongoing trainings.
