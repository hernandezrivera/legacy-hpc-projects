Project Summary

This one-year initiative aims to improve the welfare and protection by increasing resilience and empowering community members and survivors to make investments that will strengthen household and community capacity with knowledge, skills and services to reduce Human rights violation, vulnerability – efforts that will reduce the risk and impact of conflict and protection conditions on most vulnerable populations in Central Equatoria (kajokeji, Yei) Lakes (Rumbek centre) and Jonglei State (Bor South).
The target population will be IDPS, Returnees, and host community. The main purpose of the project is to address compounding effects of converging factors such as severe food insecurity /loss of livelihoods, economic decline, protection threats and lack of services which contributes and aggravates the prevalence of violence against children.
The approach TITI Foundation is using is Family Centered; The family centered approach will be at the focal point targeting different levels: the child, the members of the family, the community and the indigenous organizations and local governments that have the responsibilities with regard to those Child, women rights and protection. The family centered approach is also about comprehensive service delivery, i.e. a wide array of prevention, treatment and other support services.
The project will target 16667 beneficiaries in Kajo-Keji, Yei, Counties in Central Equatoria and Bor South in Jonglei, , Rumbek Centre in Lakes. The Project will increase access to gender-sensitive and age-appropriate services for 16667 beneficiaries where local leaders and community volunteers will serve as focal points for case management. The Team will develop and test a two-sided approach to case management– one that emphasizes both community- and facility-level referrals together with household level demand for services. The project envisions a case management approach that includes a ‘pull’ from service providers and a ‘push’ from parents/guardians and community members as they seek out services for their children due to a higher level of understanding of the benefits of each service.
The Team will meet with key stakeholders to validate the strategy and adopt/adapt materials from other programs to facilitate training and implementation of the case management approach. The project will reference the Inter-Agency Guidelines for case management and child protection, national SOPs and cluster strategy. The response is strategic to the attainment of Cluster objectives and align to activities, indicators, and outcomes of the HRP 2021.The project will work closely with GBV , FSL, Health ,S/NFI WASH, Education and Nutrition partners to ensure an integrated approach for a holistic service delivery that entrenches resilience and sustainability of the services rendered to the affected children and their care givers affected by multiple shocks.
The project activities will focus on mitigating child protection risks, preventing and responding to child protection cases through; CFS programming, capacity building of frontline service providers on CPiE, Case management including FTR services , Structured/non-structured PSS, community Outreach and dialogues, and safe referrals

1 - Needs

With reference to Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2021 54% of children account for the 8.3M people in need of humanitarian Assistance in South Sudan. The situation for children has deteriorated over 2020, with continued incidents of recruitment, abuse, exploitation and other grave violations. Over 26,848 children (12,506 girls and 14,342 boys) have been registered as unaccompanied, separated or missing as per recent UNICEF report 12th February 2021. According to the recently concluded IRNA in Kajo-Keji it indicated that constant displacement of population was mainly caused due to the high insecurity levels, presence of armed cattle keepers and food insecurity remain the key drivers of protection concerns.The situation has further been exacerbated by COVID 19. Measures adopted to curb the spread of COVID-19 like lockdowns and closure of schools have contributed to a rise in sexual violence incidents affecting children. Social structures and family bonds are breaking; children are separated from their families, and they have very limited access to basic services, which hinders their physical and mental developmentAdolescent girls are particularly vulnerable to multiple forms of violence and have limited support systems and access to information. The sub national conflicts have led to displacement, children being abandoned or encouraged to live alone. Reach Assessment of January 2021 in central Equatoria indicates that the Rebel Militia Group in Yei continue to cause insecurity ,displacement and fear. In total, it is estimated that 300 UASC children will need Comprehensive Case Management and additionally 80 children including OVC’s, children with disability and children with mental health disorders will need individual protection assistance in the greater yei area. Boys are at high risk of recruitment into into armed groups and girls are exposed to sexual abuse. More so the proximity of cantonment sites and the use of children to obtain food can amplify the risks, including eminent threats to their lives through recruitment into armed forces, abduction, rape and other assault. About 3600 caregivers and foster-care parents will also need training on appropriate and culturally acceptable child care practices. COVID-19 related restrictions on humanitarian organizations limited provision of protection services in 2020, including legal assistance, psychosocial support and case management. the CP response will focus on the most vulnerable children including children from displaced communities, children in hotspots, children associated with armed forces and groups, unaccompanied and separated children, children in detention, returnees and those living in host communities based on their needs or level of vulnerability. This will include the use of static team for MHPSS services and awareness raising to prevent protection risks, expansion of case management to cover child survivors of GBV, Reintegration services, integration with other sectors, door to door visits, situation and response monitoring and use of different communication models to promote resilience and reduce potential for negative coping strategies. The CPAoR will also role out the community strategy home care to prevent and mitigate sexual violence against girls, support local actors to take on leadership roles in CPAoR, and strengthen CP Systems including CPIMS+ roll out, social workforce strengthening, and cash based assistance and peacebuilding.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Titi Foundation has a comprehensive PSEA policy that all staff have signed on as part of our code of conduct and are trained on , including those located in the various field locations. Additionally, whenever services or projects are implemented, communities are provided with information on the PSEA policy, awareness on what PSEA is, and the process for them to report any incidents. Beneficiaries are encouraged to come forward with any concerns or information they have to any member of Titi Foundation, and are also provided with information on referral pathways for each community. Throughout the project basic PSEA trainings will be given to our targeted beneficiaries and community volunteers during our baseline surveys, focus group discussions, protection trainings and campaigns. titi will work in collaboration with other protection partners on the ground to ensure that our beneficiaries are included in ongoing PSEA trainings.Also IEC materials on PSEA will be sourced from the lead UNFPA and translated and distributed in project locations.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

All of Titi Foundations Local Monitoring Groups are inclusive of different population groups, to ensure that the staff are receiving feedback from even the most vulnerable members of a community. The establishment of the complaint desk with a focal person allows for people who are not able to utilize the suggestions box to also provide feedback. The Local Monitoring Groups also consult with the broader community, and are able to advocate on behalf of their most vulnerable members. Titi Foundation staff also consult with communities to identify vulnerable members to ensure that they have access to the program’s activities and services.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

To ensure effective records of feedback/ complaints from the beneficiaries for follow up titi teams will establish CRFMs such as the use of complaint forms, complaint desks, hotlines, complaint boxes and complaint committees based on the area of applicability. complaints and feedback will be registered and followed up with the communities on ongoing basis and will be used to adjust project activities. the records of complaints and feedback are kept in the kobo system and regularly analyzed by the MEAL teams in juba whose officers will track each case and support the program teams. the implementing program team under the supervision of the senior management if necessary will conduct regular review meetings and adjust the project implementation to changed circumstances or according to constructive feedback. where necessary titi will close the complaint and feedback cycle by giving answers to the beneficiaries on the raised complaints or make referral to other service providers.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Titi Foundation has established Local Monitoring Groups in each of the locations that it works in, to ensure that in addition to M&E mechanisms, community members are able to provide regular feedback on project assistance. Titi Foundation values and takes seriously all of the feedback it receives through these mechanisms, and uses it to inform the design of projects and activities where possible. This may include changing the location of activities to increase accessibility, assisting in the identification of referral services needed, or other mitigating measures to reduce risk or harm to beneficiaries, while also ensuring all projects are inclusive and accessible. Any complaints made against Titi Foundation’s staff or programming will also initiate an internal review and investigation, in alignment with the organizations policies and code of conduct.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Low risks • Risk of explosive hazard contamination in project target area Risks are low in the areas selected for this project. • Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources Titi Foundation maintains strong ties to community leaders and members in all of the locations that it operates in, reducing the risks of tensions around the distribution of services. The establishment of Local Monitoring Group, which are inclusive committees constituted of community members, also reduces the likelihood of this occurring. • Risk of unsafe and unethical management of data, especially disclosure of sensitive personal data on beneficiaries Titi Foundation has digitized its data collection procedures for M&E protocols to ensure both data privacy and quality. All staff are trained on ethical protocols for data management. • Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms The locations of the suggestion box and the desk for the focal point to receive complaints was decided in conjunction with communities, reducing the likelihood of this risk. • Risks that the project does not have sufficient internal mechanisms to combat and prevent child safeguarding and PSEA – Titi Foundation has a comprehensive PSEA policy which all staff are trained on in all locations. The organization’s management works closely with field-based staff to ensure that they have the resources and support needed to address any issues that may arise in this area. Communities are also provided information at the beginning of each project to ensure that they are familiar with the mechanisms within Titi Foundation that are available to support them if they raise any concerns or complaints. • Risks of women, girls and boys without and with disabilities exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance – Titi Foundation meets with community members before implementation to outline the plan and discuss any concerns that community members may have, including risks they may encounter when accessing services. For community members that may encounter barriers or difficulties in accessing humanitarian assistance, Titi Foundation works with the households and community members to identify safer alternatives, i.e. a household member can collect items on behalf of a person with disabilities, or Titi Foundation’s staff will deliver to their household while accompanied and monitored by community members. • Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.) Establishment of, and communication about, referral and protection services are communicated with each of Titi Foundation’s projects and services, to ensure that all community members have the information they need to access assistance. This is provided in all locations that the organization is working in. • Risks that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services Titi Foundation monitors the security context and any threats or risks posed to its beneficiaries in all of its locations. Services will be postponed when risks of violence to beneficiaries and staff are too high. In cases where alternative accommodations can be made, Titi Foundation will work to establish a secondary plan with community members.
