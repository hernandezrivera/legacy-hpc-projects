Project Summary

NGO Forum in partnership with NCA and AAR Japan, in close coordination with the DPHE, site management, CiCs, and UNO's, will respond to the WASH needs of both refugees and host community focusing in Teknaf and Ukhiya area including to reduce the risk of COVID-19 infection. The security context in the target area has recently deteriorated, with increasing tensions between host communities and refugees, exacerbated by major loss of livelihood and income due to the Covid 19 pandemic, as well as ongoing environmental deterioration and pressure on local resources, such as water and agricultural land. To enhance social cohesion and minimize conflict, the project will consult the refugees and host communities in order to prioritise activities that will integrate the WASH needs of the most vulnerable affected population. The Teknaf area suffers each year from water scarcity during the dry season and since the Master Water Network implementation has been hampered due to Covid19 in 2020, the proposed project will include contingency capacity for water trucking and rain water harvesting/storage to cover drinking-water needs or minimize drought impact. The project will focus on the COVID-19 related risk reduction through providing Hygine kit, awareness raising activities and disinfection of WASH facilities both in camp and host area of Ukhiya and Teknaf upazila based on strengthening hygiene promotion activities building on the good work done during Covid response. The proejct will maintain and improve water and sanitation infrastructures emphasizing durable solutions to the solid waste issue and services to support stakeholders in coping with natural disasters. The project will address Menstrual Hygiene Management gaps and explore the use of the voucher system in conducive market environment. The project will cover several camps and host areas; Camp: 5, 11, 25 and 27, Host: Rajapalong in Ukhiya and Hnila union in Teknaf.

1 - Identified Needs

According to the J-MSNA conducted in August 2020, the presence of the refugee communities has raised concerns over local environmental degradation, falling wages, and rising prices, exerting additional pressures on localities where public services and infrastructure were already lagging behind the national average. Furthermore, the Covid 19 pandemic and resulting govt control measures have exacerbated the access to essential services. Access to improved water source remains a challenge in Teknaf with about 26% of households reporting not having enough water to meet their domestic needs. Sanitation infrastructure maintenance has also seen some challenges with 14% of households reporting open defecation compared to last year. Even though 88% of the host communities indicate tubewells and handpumps as their main source of water, the quality of those sources can very often be questionable due to both operation and maintenance and lack of resources to ensure water quality. Solid waste disposal remains an area requiring improvement, with more reduction and reuse at the houosehold and community level, and more recycling infrastructure needed. Despite the good work done by WASH/Health/CwC agencies during the Covid19 response, with 98% of househlds reporting increased handwashing practices, there remains room for improving behavior change communication with Covid specific hygiene messaging, especially considering the near-total lack of Covid19 conscious behavior now commonly observed among both host and refugee communities. Gender and and all-inclusive WASH concerns are not always mainstreamed in WASH programs and MHM issues have seen gaps develop especially during the Covid19 response period. WASH actors have been playing one of the key roles to work against the spread of COVID-19 since March 2020. Although COVID-19 outbreak increased the handwashing practice in the camps (J-MSNA), stronger awareness raising is still needed to ensure that refugees and the host community are aware of COVID-19 prevention measures and the importance of mask wearing, and consistently wear the masks that has been distributed (ISCG SitRep, September 2020). According to the Rapid Gender Analysis, in the host community, provision of protective .and hygiene equipment to poorer household including female sex workers and transgender person is recommended. While interviewing the beneficiaries of WFS and CFS operated by NGO Forum and AAR Japan, the fears of using communal sanitation facilities including latrines and bathing cubicles during COVID-19 outbreak are widely shared. As sanitation facilities are shared in the community (18 persons per latrine and 38 persons per bathing cubicle), disinfection of those facilities is crucial to prevent the spread of COVID-19. Among more than 67,000 facilities, 46,122 facilities (ISCG SitRep, September 2020) are regularly disinfected.
