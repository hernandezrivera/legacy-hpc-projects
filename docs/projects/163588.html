Project Summary

Our project therefore seeks to:
(a) Assist those whose land certificates are missing or destroyed or have been burnt to legally repossess their real estate property there upon
(b) Prevent a civil strife that could result from land disputes after the crisis
(c) Prevent confusion and ease litigation over land and to forestall justice to the meritorious persons
(d) To promote rebuilding and the reconstruction of the villages after the crisis without being delayed by confusion and complicated land disputes resulting from the absence of the certificates
(e) Promote development in the villagers after the crisis through the development and selling up of businesses on the repossessed lands.

Besoins / Needs

Since the beginning of the Anglophone crisis in Cameroon more than three years ago, about 300 villages have been burnt including much property and documents and certificates such as marriage, birth, certificate and academic certificatesIn Anglophone Cameroon and in the north west region in particular land is the most important asset whether it belongs to individuals, families of communities. By the Cameron law, it is only through a land certificate that one’s ownership of land can be guaranteed. It cannot be over emphasized that land is the most important asset to the people of the affected regions. The destruction of this very important document that is proof of ownership of land means that litigation over land will be a major challenges especially when the present crisis is over.
