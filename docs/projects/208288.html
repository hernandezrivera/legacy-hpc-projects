Project Summary

The main goal of this project is to reduce Gender-Based Violence (GBV) in Wum Sun division, Menchum Division, Northwest Region where forced/early marriage especially amongst the Fulani is alarming, rape leading to teanage pregnancy appalling. The interventions and Needs assessment conducted revealed that 200 persons suffered from GBV in 2023, and more than 200 lacked civil documentation including birth certificates, ID cards, and marriage certificates. The project will ensure that the 5 communities (Wum, Befang, Bangwe, Aguili, Weh and Esu) are educated on sexual exploitation, abuse, and gender-based violence-related issues; Strengthen the capacities of Community-Based Organizations (CBOs) and structures such as health care workers to fight Gender-Based Violence (GBV); do effective referrals and provide comprehensive case management including conference case management and provide child-friendly spaces to children and women who are survivors of GBV. The project will utilize a community-centered approach to sensitize 16,000 persons, and provide comprehensive case management to 200 survivors of GBV including conference case management for pertinent cases where stakeholders such as the security, judiciary, and social affairs will brought to deliberate on the way forward. Furthermore, the project will ensure 4,000 children [and caregivers] access mental health or psychosocial support. The project therefore stands to mitigate the effects of GBV and ensure that women and girls who are survivors of GBV as well as persons living with disability benefit from comprehensive case management. The project also aims to support 200 children with birth certificates and 200 adults with ID cards and/or marriage and/or disability cards.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The war in the North West and South West regions of Cameroon has affected girls and women differently than men and boys. They all face specific risks. They also have vulnerabilities, needs, and coping mechanisms. The population of Wum Sub Division, Menchum Valley and Fungom in Menchum division as many others in the Northwest Region of Cameroon face a slump in economic activities. The poverty level of the people has increased resulting in a high dependency rate forcing young girls into early marriage and prostitution. A crime wave has also surged and all forms of violence against women have become commonplace. The shutdown of the majority of schools has increased the rate of gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse. These come to add to the existing laws, harmful cultural practices and customs of the people with male dominance where women are exempted from traditional law-making and decision-making. BIHAPH has offered psychosocial support to over 200 survivors of SGBV and sensitized over 4,000 inhabitants of Wum, Befang, Bangwe,Aguilui, Weh and Esu on the effects of GBV yet the unavailability of funds leaves the organization unable to do comprehensive case management, medical support and offer dignity kits to survivors, as a result of the armed conflict which has forced many women and children to flee their homes and seek accommodation in Wum central and live at the mercy of perpetrators. Survival of the fittest is a daily routine in Wum Befang, BANGWE, AGUILI, WEH and Esu where some of the girls as young as 13 have resulted in sex for petit cash as a means of survival. Interviews with queen mothers revealed that the majority of women and girls are exposed to sexual exploitation and abuse because they are idle. These communities are in dire need of assistance and BIHAPH has shown them a green light through its community-based counseling and peacebuilding activities. There has never been a time like this when rural communities in Enclave Menchum Division need more help than ever. Living conditions are deplorable, at least 2 in every household have suffered a form of SGBV either sexual exploitation, denial of opportunity, forced marriage, or rape. The men and boys alone cannot survive the families, they too have their own vulnerabilities. There is a need for women and girls such as women-led groups, and health personnel to be brought to the spotlight so that they can support BIHAPH lest we run the risk of a lost youthful age which will be catastrophic for Cameroon and the world at large. Furthermore, the issue of civil documentation is catastrophic given that the majority of the community members lost their houses to fire. BIHPAH referred 194 cases to a partner organization but only 14 were treated. BIHAHP also did a needs assessment among persons living with disability and found that they had specific vulnerabilities and needs Hygenic kits, food assistance and the need for an affordable community grinding Mill.
