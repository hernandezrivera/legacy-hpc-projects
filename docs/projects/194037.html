Project Summary

This project aligns with cluster objectives that aim provision of timely life-saving WASH assistance, to ensure crisis-affected populations access basic water services, adequate (basic or limited), protected and gender-sensitive sanitation services, and hygiene promotion and other environmental health interventions to reduce mortality and morbidity. Furthermore, it contributes to building resilience to recurrent shocks and improving vulnerable people’s access to basic WASH services, by enhancing the prevention and mitigation of protection risks and responding to protection needs, strengthening community resilience, and enhance risk mitigation measures. The project will target 255159 (58789 boys, 63,688 girls, 67,668 women, and 65,014 men) individuals in need living in 32 localities within 8 states including Blue Nile, North Darfur, North Kordofan, South Kordofan, Central Darfur, West Darfur, Sennar, Al Gazira, Khartoum, Gedaref and Red Sea states. The project will be focusing on provision of water supply at IDPs gathering points/camps, community and institutional level including water trucking in areas supply is disrupted , provision of essential WASH supplies & NFI (Soap, jeri-cans, aqua tabs and water purifications), establishment/ construction of emergency sanitation and handwashing facilities in the current hotspot locations construction, light rehabilitation and improvement for water sources, in addition to connection of water to schools, health facilities, and child friendly spaces. The project will contribute towards the elimination of open defecation within our operational areas through enhancing the capacity of local communities and mobilizing targeted communities to construct their own latrines. This in line with the construction of emergency latrines during emergencies for people affected by crises. The project will also contribute to solid and medical management efforts through provision of garbage and collection tools, support to the disposal of solid waste, construction of medical incinerators at health facilities to facilitate safe disposal of medical wastes. The project will focus on improving the hygiene situation and status within the targeted communities to mitigate and reduce the risk of diseases outbreaks as a result of poor WASH services through general hygiene campaigns, awareness sessions, home visits as well as strengthening the capacity of local community committees such as hygiene promotors, conduct training and establishing hygiene clubs at schools and child friendly spaces, this will be accompanied with provision of hygiene items such as soap to promote hand washing practices as mitigation measures to prevent disease outbreaks as well as supporting female with basic menstrual hygiene items to facilitate safe and dignified menstrual management for female at menstrual age. Moreover, the project will support families who have malnourished children with hygiene items including soap, jerry-cans and aqua tabs to address underlying causes of malnutrition. The project will create synergies, complement, and integrate with Health, Nutrition, NFIs, Shelter and Food security interventions within the targeted areas and worked with other sectors to address root causes of malnutrition and factors contributing to diseases outbreaks. The project will focus and enhance gender, protection, disability mainstreaming and management of environmental risks. Furthermore, the way that services are provided will seek not only to meet immediate life-saving and life-sustaining needs, but to build community resilience, contribute towards durable solutions, and strengthen the capacity of local and national actors, including the appropriate government authorities, to lead future service delivery. The project will be implemented in 12 months with estimated budget at $ 10,710,000

1 - Needs

The Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene situation (WASH) is deteriorating due to worsening economic crisis, political instability, non-functional or aging WASH infrastructure, a five-fold increase in operation and maintenance cost of water infrastructures, insufficient revenue collections, poor budget allocation by the Government, and limited external investments. Most affected WASH population groups are IDPs, returnees, and other vulnerable populations battling food insecurity and extreme poverty who cannot afford WASH services and practices. In addition, these population groups are mainly under the threat of WASH-related epidemics, severe acute malnutrition (SAM), the COVID-19 pandemic, and water resource-related conflicts. As per the HNO 2023, 10.9 million people (excluding Refugees) who fall under the above categories need humanitarian WASH assistance. The overall objective is to increase access to humanitarian WASH services for 4.8 million most in-need populations with a focus on building their resilience to WASH-related recurrent shocks with also emphasis on community empowerment and engagement of women, girls, and young people as enhanced enablers.5.6 million people need basic water services, of which 4.5 million IDPs, returnees, and other vulnerable populations will be targeted. 1.66 million people will be targeted with access to limited sanitation, in line with the response capacity of partners. The highest priority will be for the 1.2 million people (72%) who defecate in the open, cannot afford, and are more vulnerable to disease outbreaks. 4.8 million people with hygiene promotion interventions using risk communication and community engagement strategies focusing on handwashing with soap.WASH in schools was a challenge, where only 45.5% of the schools have access to safe water, while access to improved sanitation is 50.8%. Hand washing practice is about 56.5%, while in 57.1% of the schools there is no soap for proper hand washing. In 2018-2019 school year, the Ministry of Education conducted a National WASH in School Assessment, only 45.5% of the schools have access to safe water, while access to improved sanitation is 50.8%. Handwashing practice is about 56.5%, while in 57.1% of the schools there is no soap for proper hand washingBecause of the recent clashes and conflict between SAF, and RSF, lack of safe drinking water has been reported in all areas experiencing conflict, increasing the risk of water-borne diseases, including cholera and mosquito borne diseases (dengue fever and malaria) due to negative copying mechanisms such as drinking unsafe water, poor hygiene practices such as handwashing, clean food chain etc. Water quality treatment units, drilling rig and other equipment have also been destroyed, exposing people to untreated drinking water. Newly displaced people find themselves in crowded IDP camps experiencing shortages of water and latrines, further increasing health and GBV risks Increased pressure on the use of the already insufficient water and sanitation infrastructure has led to increased breakdown of the existing and functional water points and yet travel and movement restrictions have made it difficult to move spares and labor to repair the damaged water and sanitation facilities. Lack of basic hygiene and Sanitation supplies such as soap, MHM materials, handwashing facilities is highly impacting on the social wellbeing and the health of the affected communities. Long queues at water points due to the increased population in safe locations have led to scramble and violence at water points

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The climate crisis is an intergenerational child rights crisis, and the greatest threat to children's survival, learning and protection. Save the Children has an Environmental Sustainability and Climate Change (ESSCC) Policy and is committed to minimizing the negative environmental impact of our programmes and operations, contributing positively to environmental sustainability and climate change action, and implementing interventions which reduce vulnerability to climatic shocks.Save the Children will include environmental considerations into all interventions. In particular, the sourcing (local, national, international) of all supplies and materials, especially those used for construction and rehabilitation of infrastructure, will be carefully considered to select the option with the least negative environmental impact, considering both local dynamics (e.g., deforestation, brick manufacturing processes) and contributions to global carbon emissions caused by freight and production processes. All construction works will be done to limit negative environmental impact on the soil and its surroundings. The project will contribute to resilience and sustainable use of natural resources through involving and encouraging children and their communities to implement better practices which support and better environmental protection and a safe local environment. SC Sudan will also, communicate publicly about the impacts of climate change and environmental degradation in Sudan. Environment will be considered as an integral component for this project, and a number of factors will be essential for improving the humanitarian response such as coordination with different actors at the communities, prioritization and awareness raising amongst practitioners, and effective policy framework. The mitigation plans will include promoting innovative technologies; alternative energy sources such as solar panels for water pumps and lighting for safer use of latrines and showers; and ensuring high effectiveness of existing waste and medical disposal mechanisms as preventative measure against disease caused by insufficient or unsafe waste disposal through reducing packaging material and discouraging use of plastic bags, planning the locations of emergency waste disposal sites with local authorities to avoid contamination of water sources, disease vectors and odors, and using latrine construction materials which are more environmentally friendly such as using steel bars instead of bamboo wooden and sticks, and minimizing use of fi red bricks in construction through encouraging use of cement blocks instead.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Through all programming, Save the Children aims to increase safety, uphold dignity, and ensure equitable access to services, including through addressing the concerns of individuals and groups with specific needs. Programme design and implementation is informed by context analysis including local power and conflict dynamics. Save the Children will work closely with multiple stakeholders (community members, government partners, local NGOs) to avoid unintended negative effects on individuals or exacerbation of community tensions, and will seek programming which safeguards, protects, and capacitates vulnerable and marginalized individuals, and reduces tensions. SC Sudan Protection staff support the effective mainstreaming of protection across all programming including though training on referral pathways, conducting protection audits, and providing technical advice. A Gender Equality Specialist supports with improving the gender sensitivity of programmes, and all projects are assessed through gender equality marker. Through community engagement activities, training of community-based structures, and training of service providers, information will be shared on protection risks and appropriate prevention and reporting actions. The WASH project interventions will ensure protection mainstreaming during the design, implementation and monitoring phases of the project and will ensure maximum protection for beneficiaries specifically children, women, girls, people with disabilities and other marginalized entities. WASH interventions will priorities safety and dignity, and avoid doing harm through ensure the location of the facilities and the route is away from actual or potential threats such as violence; especially the risk or threat of gender-based violence (GBV), and attacks from armed groups, installing lights near to latrines as appropriate, infrastructure adaptations such as installing ramps and railings to latrines, proper locks from inside, separate and label the latrines“ male” and “female , also platforms and ramps at water points at appropriate height to ensure safe manual lifting to all. Because women and children are the most likely primary responsible for collecting HHs water, special consideration will be given to the design of WASH facilities such as providing shades during sun hours, schedule water collection times to limit overcrowding at the water points. Meaningful access will be ensured through discuss and consult with all representative samples of society (e.g., men, women, girls, boys, elderly, ethnic groups, persons with disabilities) that should have access to the service. Adapt the location to reduce the distance and to ensure that the most vulnerable/marginalized have access.
