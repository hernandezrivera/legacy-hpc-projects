Project Summary

Recent Inter-agency assessments in Jonglei and Central Equatoria indicate deficient key water supply infrastructure due to both the lack of maintenance and damage sustained during the active months of conflict, compounded by recent mass flooding in Pibor which disrupted livelihoods and displaced of over 14,348 households. Open defecation is widespread and risks exposure to water borne diseases; both communities reported an increase in cases of diarrhea and Acute Watery Diarrhoea. Untreated water from River Kengen, Kubal and Lothila including makeshift ponds has been attributed as the main cause; however, poor sanitation coverage including schools and lack of hand washing facilities, poor knowledge and practices on personal and environmental further fuel the situation.
Stop Poverty Communal Initiative (SPOCI), a national NGO, proposes to provide timely equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs/host communities and returnees; mitigate WASH-related GBV; WASH in Nutrition for famine prevention minimum package of interventions; and WASH in Health for outbreak control at wider community level. The project will target 15,000 beneficiaries comprised of 5000 girls, 5000 boys, 2500 men and 2500 women in Jonglei and Central Equatoria States. These will include elderly persons, pregnant and lactating women, people with disabilities drawn from the most vulnerable households. Among these will be beneficiaries at risk of gender based violence and sexual abuse, communities with poor access to nutrition and health, SAM/ MAM children or Ebola Virus Disease high risk zones in Central Equatoria. Inter-cluster synergies will be explored in targeted by close working with partners in nutrition, health, FSL, Protection.
Activities will focus on improving access to access to safe water supply, improved, secured and gender appropriate sanitation facilities , hygiene promotion messages, support children admitted for SAM treatment discharged with WASH/Hygiene kit from functional OTP/SC , provision of access to safe drinking/chlorinated water , provision of functional handwashing facilities by soap or 0.05% chlorine running water and populations are provided with a WASH minimum package of activities to mitigate WASH-related diseases.
SPOCI has been operational in proposed areas for many years and currently implementing SSHF and UNICEF funded WASH actions. SPOCI is a member of State level Cholera Preparedness Task
Force, National NGOs Steering Committee, member of the ICWG and WASH Cluster Accountability to
Affected Populations Technical Working Group. In addition, SPOCI has for the past two years supported over 30,000 vulnerable populations with WASH/Education interventions through UNICEF among other partners support in Pibor. Through these, the organization has fostered strong linkages with communities, state/county line ministries of water and irrigation. This is in addition to strong and competent team including staff redeployment to scale up interventions based on needs.
Activities will ensure delivering a WASH integrated package. These will be further integrated with other Health-related priorities, acknowledging that poor WASH services are a key driver to poor health, and ultimately resulting in high malnutrition. This is in addition to regular assessments to mitigate GBV risks including sexual exploitation and abuse.

Needs

The latest IPC September 2018 projections indicated an estimated 5.2 million people in the country facing unprecedented levels of food insecurity and malnutrition in the first quarter of 2019 and an estimated 5,712,000 people women, men and children will not have access to adequate WASH in 2019, a 7% increase compared to 2018. Recent SMART surveys conducted in Jonglei indicate 20.7 per cent GAM rates, way above the WHO emergency threshold of 15 per cent. Recent and renewed fighting, cattle related fighting incidents in Jonglei and Central Equatoria; coupled with perennial flooding in Jonglei, could potentially worsen the WASH and nutrition situation among vulnerable populations in 2019 (July 2018 Boma State Inter agencies Rapid Assessment). A continuation of localized conflict and ceasefire violations continue to negatively impact access to WASH services among others. Safe and consistent access to affected populations has significantly deteriorated in 2018 due to rising insecurity and attacks on aid workers. In addition, women and girls face increased risk of attacks including both physical and verbal harassment, assault and gender based and sexual violence when collecting water and using communal latrines, and access to menstrual hygiene products and appropriate and dignified washing locations remains their key need. WASH needs are also high among IDPs in non-camp settings and among their already stretched host communities (WASH Cluster Updates, 2018). Weakened government form key gaps and likely Ebola disease outbreaks especially via the various border entry points in Central Equatoria. Activities

• Provide timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected populations via rehabilitation of dysfunctional boreholes/ water points in safe locations• Increase knowledge among 15,000 beneficiaries on appropriate hygiene practices to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases. • Enable vulnerable and affected 15000 beneficiaries practice safe, sanitary and hygienic living through secure, dignified and gender appropriate. sanitation services via establishment and rehabilitation of latrine facilities. • Gender appropriate response starting from a risk assessment, for secure access, location and design of water points, toilets and showers • Provision of safe drinking water and nutritional centers (OTP/SC) from protected water point till the appetite test for SAM/MAM • Provision of WASH/Hygiene kits for the admitted SAM at OTP/SC till the household level • Provision of timely WASH minimum package of interventions on an ad-hoc basis, adapted to the specificities of the related-outbreaks, at an institution level as well as for the wider community • Settip up of WASH services, training of 300 health workers and pupils as ambassadors for appropriate hygiene practices within handwashing with soap at critical times at their household level • Carry out mapping and assessment of at least 100 dysfunctional handpumps in the areas with highest malnutrition rates (GAM)• Mobilize and train 200 hygiene promoters/ Health/Nutrition workers/volunteers trained on the WASH minimum package of activities
