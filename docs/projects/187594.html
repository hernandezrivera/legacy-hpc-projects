Project Summary

BONYAN has put providing water and sanitation facilities among the top priorities of its interventions in crisis. BO designed its 2022/23 WASH intervention based on the analysis of humanitarian needs in NW Syrian, need severity for WASH assistance, as well as, in line with SOs of HRP and WASH Cluster.
The project aims to restore functionality or keep water infrastructure at the minimum level of operation; and to carry out life-saving WASH interventions including, provision of water and sanitation facilities and services, and distribution of NFI kits, as well as improvement of hygienic behavior and practices of most vulnerable people.
The project is consisted of three main components targeting ~224,000 INDVs (91,000M/133,000F – 12,000PwD)
*Repairing and rehabilitation of water systems to enable greater quality control, improve
access to safe water, and minimize financial burden on already impoverished families as well as rehabilitation and extension of the sewerage networks targeting ~40,000 INDV (19,300M/20,700F – 2,000PwD)
Communities in NW Syria are overburden due to high IDP numbers resulting from continues displacement in addition to limited functionality and efficiency of water infrastructure and services. Therefore, BO will rehabilitate/expand 75Km of water network and 25 Km of sanitation network and 2 elevated tanks. Also, BO will provide operation support for 90 days to supply water to the most vulnerable people, in meanwhile, BO will be rehabilitated and support to sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems, will develop a tariff-based system management in coordination with relevant stakeholders (Mainly the Local Council in the project area). 140 latrines for PWDs along with water tanks will be established and will be rehabilitation WASH facilities in 25 schools.
*Distribution of essential WASH NFIs and conducting Hygiene promotion activities to improve hygienic behavior and practices targeting 2000 HHs (12,000 person 5,800M/6,200F – 1000PwD) of total, 65% are children under 18 years old and 25% are girls and women in reproductive age. In crisis, people tend to follow survival instinct which means prioritize avoiding tangible risks with less care to intangible risk such as dangerous impact of incorrect hygiene practices.
BO will conduct 6 Hygiene promotion campaigns along with distribution of the 2,000 WASH-NFIs to improve and encourage beneficiaries to strengthen already well-established hygienic behavior, knowledge, attitude, and practices among the most vulnerable people. The hygiene kits will be customized to address needed items for all gender and age groups (including toothpaste and toothbrush for children, and sanitary pads, feminine disinfectant ...)
*Improving access to emergency WASH facilities and services to reach SPHERE standards and cluster standards, both in terms of the quality of services and gender/protection considerations with no credible solution blooms in the horizon for the conflict in Syria, BO have set up an emergency plan for lifesaving intervention to provide water and sanitation facilities to potentially new displaced people through provision
1 elevated cement water tank with capacity of 150 cubic meters to improve access to clean and safe water for 20,000 INDV (9700M/10300F – 1000PwD) near overcrowded and HTR IDPs settlements
80 WASH unit that include 160 water tanks (2 cubic meters) and 160 latrine blocks (toilets and baths), as well as water trafficking. Desludging and solid waste management for 8,000 INDV (2560B, 2640G, 1500W, 1300M – 500PwD) for 90 days
BO will ensure that the support will be provided to the most vulnerable people through transparent process based on clear vulnerability criteria. Principles of Do No Harm and Accountability to Affected People are incorporated in the project design and will be ensured during the project timeline. Also, MEAL team of BO will conduct ongoing monitoring of project activities to verify achievement of indicators

4 - Needs

According to HNO2022 WASH assessments, the overall number of people in need of WASH assistance - 13.2 million.Shortage of electricity remains the root cause for water supply systems underperformance or cessation and is mostly related to the significant shortages of fuel for power plants (and water supply systems backup generators where they exist), and to the reduced Euphrates River flow that drastically diminished the hydroelectric potential of the dams.Reliability and efficiency of water systems have sharply decreased for the first time since 2016, with nearly 2 million fewer people using water networks as the only HH water supply modality, in comparison to mid-2020. This means up to 47% of the population is relying on often unsafe alternatives to piped water, to meet or complement their water needs (vis-à-vis 38% in mid-2020). Investment in sanitation infrastructure is critical, as at least 70% of sewage is discharged untreated and at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional. In NWS, wastewater from huge camp clusters is openly discharged, forming sewage rivers. Over 30% of garbage is inappropriately disposed or collected with low frequency, while the dumpsites often pose serious health, environmental and protection risks.Access to sufficient and affordable safe water, adequate sanitation, solid waste management and/or hygiene supplies will remain a challenge for more than 2 million people in IDP sites, highly dependent on continued humanitarian assistance.

5 - Implementation of activities

BO will coordinate with relevant stakeholders to conduct need assessment survey to collect up to date data about WASH needs of IDPs, Host Community and Returnees. BO will select beneficiaries following transparent prosses based on clear vulnerability/eligibility criteria to ensure provision of support to the most vulnerable affected population. Along with the implementation, MEAL team will carry out ongoing M&E activities to verify project indicators. CA1: Repair and rehabilitation of water systems 14,000 INDVs.CA2: Support to water quality assurance, operation, and maintenance of water supply systems 14,000 INDVs.CA3: Repair, rehabilitation and operational support to sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems targeting 5,000 INDVs.CA4: Distribution of essential WASH non-food items (NFI), hygiene promotion and messaging on COVID-19 targeting 12,000 INDVs.CA5: Improve access to humanitarian lifesaving/emergency WASH facilities and services, including strengthening Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) measures targeting 8,000 INDVs.CA9: Support to WASH facilities and services in schools and child friendly spaces in close coordination with Education and Protection sectors targeting 50 schools.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:- Project’s area is safe, secure, and accessible for both genders of staff and beneficiaries while implementing activities. - Procurement of items/ materials won’t affect the availability of commodities or prices. - Availability of clean and safe water sources for tracking water. - Local councils are cooperative with BO team. [*] Risk of theft of WASH rehabilitation/construction materials such as generators and construction materials (during delivery/storage/ after installation) by armed groups and used in construction activities. likelihood: Low Impact: High Mitigation: MoUs to be agreed with local authorities/councils to ensure local ownership of materials and rehabilitated/new facilities. promote community ownership. [*] Cash exacerbates existing tension in HHs and leads to domestic violence likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigation:Involve community in programme design (e.g., in determining frequency/timing/type of delivery), recognizing they may already have mitigation measures toward protecting their own asset. Also ensure coordination among cash implementing organizations, such that they apply best practice and share knowledge and experience.
