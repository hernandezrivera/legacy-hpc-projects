Project Summary

IOM estimates that approximately 15,000 people crossed the Kenya/Somalia and Ethiopia/Somalia borders in July 2021, of which approximately 50% were considered voluntary movements (for economic or family reasons) and 40% were considered forced (due to food insecurity, disaster, or conflict). This represents a 62% increase in movement from July 2020. Meanwhile, the Government of Kenya’s threatened closure of the Dadaab refugee camp in 2022 (home to 223,000 predominantly Somali refugees) is likely to accelerate the rate of returns to Somalia, as are the worsening drought conditions that are crippling livelihoods and reducing access to water in the Garissa and Mandera border districts of Kenya. Along the Ethiopian axis, worsening drought in Somalia has led to a 10% increase over 6 months in the population of refugee camps in the town of Dolo Ado, bordering Somalia’s Gedo region (CCCM Cluster Somalia, 2021).
Within Somalia, refugees and IDPs on the move due to conflict or food insecurity are forced to cross dangerous areas that they are unfamiliar with, with dangerous consequences. The security agencies in Jubbaland, bordering Kenya and Ethiopia, estimates that 20 people died due to roadside explosives and bombs since 2018. Furthermore, Al Shabaab’s practice of harvesting explosives from ERW to build their own Improvised Explosive Devices means that explosive remnants of war in the borderlands represent a further risk to security both for individuals who have the potential to be caught in their attacks and to the rule of law in an already-fragile state.
The consequences of this for the women, men, boys, and girls living in the country, for IDPs or returning refugees, for the communities who host them, and for local, regional, and national authorities attempting to promote both security and development, are dire. Women and children are also at risk due to their frequent movements to collect water and firewood, herd livestock, go to school, and play. Children represent 91% of the victims of explosive hazards in Somalia, and in 2019, 74% of victims were boys (HNO 2022), (AOAV 2020). IDPs are also especially at risk; as communities are displaced, their vulnerability to loss of protection increases.
A male IDP in Dhobley town, which hosts 60,000 IDPs just over the border with Kenya, reported that on their journey, they are “either coming from Al- Shabab controlled areas to Dhobley or from refugee camps to Dhobley. In both journeys, people pass through contaminated roads.” In Dollow, which hosts 67,000 IDPs on the Ethiopian border, 86% of IDPs report the presence of IEDs and ERWs in their community (OCHA 2021), and reported seeing “piles” of explosives collected by Al-Shabaab along their journey to the camp. In both camps, both IDPs and humanitarian actors reported no mechanism for reporting explosive hazards to the authorities; and the security forces themselves reported having no capacity to deal with them
This project will undertake to provide risk education, both directly through JUMAN, a local partner, and by training local NGOs, IDP community leaders, and NGOs to provide EORE to vulnerable IDPs and refugees, a clear reporting mechanism for hazards, and survey of hazardous areas in the surrounding areas of Dhobley and Dollow.

1. - Projects activities

Activity 1: Identify key community members in each location to inform the development of SA/EORE materials and ongoing activities. Activity 2: Undertake community-led development of appropriate SA/EORE messaging for 31,600 women, girls, boys, and men from Jubbaland border towns of Dhobley, Afmadow, Doolow, Belet Haawo, and Luuq locations. Activity 3: Provide training for key community members and community focal points on the delivery of tailored EORE sessions and reporting of ERW found by the community in each location on hard-to-reach areas for the CL staff.Activity 4: Develop gender-sensitive and inclusive community-based reporting mechanisms for identified ERW, tailored to each location.Activity 8: Train dedicated SEMA staff members on reporting and coordinating mechanisms, data collection, and information management.
