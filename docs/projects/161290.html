Project Summary

The aim of this project is to provide essential rehabilitation and mental health support to vulnerable and conflict-affected populations throughout South Sudan. It will adopt a static and also mobile-response modality, to ensure timely assessment, response and follow-up interventions in key areas of concern. Given the duality of the approach and flexibility afforded through the mobile response modality, the scope of the intervention is country-wide, but will specifically include areas such as Unity State: Rubkona, Koch Western Bahr-el Ghazal: Wau, Raja, and Central, Eastern and Western Equatoria: Juba, Yei, Morobo, Kajo Keji, Torit and Yambio. The project will target the most vulnerable and marginalized populations (including persons with disabilities, elderly, people with injuries, functional limitations and chronic diseases and those experiencing post-traumatic stress, psycho-social distress and associated mental health conditions) who are often excluded from wider humanitarian response efforts. Through specialized identification and assessment tools, as well as technical oversight, the project will ensure appropriate and timely access to rehabilitation services, assistive devices and short-term, mental health support interventions for highly vulnerable groups. Another central component of the projects approach is focused on capacity building and the maintenance of quality standards for physical therapy, rehabilitation and MHPSS services within the wider response effort in South Sudan. This is achieved through external coordination in technical working groups (DWG, MHPSS) and through capacity building activities through training or awareness on Rehabilitation and Mental health with community-based organizations, religious groups, NNGOs, INGOs, Health Workers and disabled peoples organizations involved in such activities. To promote sustainable care, HI also prioritizes responses and support to groups which align with the local context and take advantage of existing coping mechanisms or support structures. This project will directly contribute to the 2020 HRP and health cluster objective CO3 – Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled and mental health disorders. Activities such as individual and group therapies, basic counseling, cognitive therapy, self-care/self-help, psycho-education, including PFA and referrals will contribute to indicator 3.2 – number of persons receiving MHPSS services. Whilst physical and rehabilitation services including the provision of assistive devices, training to caregivers and family-members, device maintenance and disability awareness will contribute to indicator 3.3. – Number of persons with disabilities receiving rehabilitation services.

1 - Needs

The health cluster estimates that in 2020, over 3.2m people will require essential health services due to the protracted crisis in South Sudan. Conflict, displacement and instability have led to the deterioration of the health system across the country, and prevented access to essential care for a vast proportion of the population, particularly persons with disabilities. This project will specifically seek to address the gaps in the provision of mental health and functional rehabilitation services in South Sudan, and meet further demand through capacity building of healthcare staff and community groups on MHPSS and disability inclusion. At present limited knowledge of these areas prevents sustainable and context relevant interventions, and exacerbates marginalization and stigmatization in these areas so such training is critical to address the high needs. Based on WHO estimates, 22% of the total population or 2.4m people are living with depression, anxiety, post-traumatic stress disorder or schizophrenia and in need of mental health and psycho-social support services. Critical shortages in the mental health workforce and limited funding have meant both the government and humanitarian agencies struggle to adequately meet the needs presented. The irregular supply of psycho-tropic drugs, limited understanding and prevailing stigma related to mental health disorders also prevents people living with acute conditions from accessing appropriate services. Similarly, for persons with physical impairments, injuries and chronic conditions, the availability of functional rehabilitation services and assistive devices is limited, particularly in hard-to-reach areas. Despite the high rates of conflict related injury, and physical conditions linked to displacement, poor access to healthcare, nutrition and other basic services, the availability and coverage of functional rehabilitation services is severely limited. Whilst center-based care is available in key urban locations: Juba, Wau and Rumbek, there is a significant gap in outreach rehabilitation services to other areas. Similarly for mental health services – the continued neglect of hard-to-reach and conflict affected areas often leads to worsening of disorders among local populations. HI is currently the only organization in South Sudan providing integrated MHPSS and rehabilitation services through a mobile response modality – ensuring assessment, response and follow-up activities are conducted in communities most in need. Between Apr-Sep 2019, HIs flying team responded to critical needs in Wau, Raja, Bentiu, Yambio, Lobonok, Morobo and Yei. This translated into the provision of essential care and services to 1,944 beneficiaries (1,287 MHPSS and 657 rehabilitation) as well as the generation of comparable evidence on prominent needs and response strategies in each location. In HIs static sites in Yei, Torit and Yambio 4,591 people (2,029 men, 2,562 women) have also been supported through individual and group-based MHPSS services from Apr – Sep. The figures provided above highlight the huge demand for these services and the consideration for greater coverage and provision in key locations across South Sudan through this project.

2 - Activities

The proposed project aligns with, and directly contributes to the achievement of the South Sudan health cluster HRP strategic objectives for 2020, specifically CO3 to increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, persons with disabilities and mental health disorders. On a broader-level it supports key priorities within South Sudan’s National Disability and Inclusion Policy, including equal access to mental health and comprehensive rehabilitation services. The project also aligns with WHOs Global Disability Action Plan (2014-2021) by removing barriers to health, extending rehabilitation services and assistive device provision, and through the collection of internationally comparable data on disabilities in South Sudan. Project activities are designed to address the immediate and long-term mental health conditions, physical impairments and injuries directly correlated with, or exacerbated by the protracted crisis and instability in South Sudan. Under HC indicator 3.2 – number of people receiving MHPSS services, HI aims to support a total of 26667 beneficiaries, prioritizing persons with disabilities, mental healt needs. MHPSS interventions will be designated based on the response modality and context, including short-term low intensity psychological interventions such as brief cognitive therapy, self-care/help, psycho-education including psychological first-aid and referrals. In locations requiring longer term psycho-social support services, HI will conduct sensitization activities, individual and group counselling, recreational activities and set-up MHPSS peer support groups to promote understanding of mental health issues in communities, support self-healing capacities and develop positive coping strategies. The project's MHPSS component will also include a capacity building component to improve MHPSS provision and understanding among health workers throughout South Sudan. Under HC indicator 3.3 HI will conduct identification, individual assessments and provide physiotherapy, rehabilitation and occupational therapy services to 150 persons with disabilities, injuries or chronic illness throughout South Sudan. The provision of assistive devices will be a core element within the rehabilitation services, including sizing and assessments, device preparation, fitting / adjustment and training to the user, family members or carers on the use and maintenance of devices. As with MHPSS, the rehabilitation teams will also support capacity building of health workers, humanitarian partners and key stakeholders to promote sustainability in the response.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

In all locations, disputes over housing and land ownership are to be expected. Weak legislation and competing claims to land, in addition to the nature of conflict and displacement have complicated land rights claims, and led to expropriation of land and property on mass scale. To mitigate this effectively, HI teams will play a key role in monitoring HLP issues in communities – particularly in areas with high expected numbers of returns such as Yei, Morobo and Kajo Keji. Strong coordination with HLP partners in each location, as well as government ministries is required to mediate issues, but also to sensitize communities about how to manage disputes effectively. Priority will also be given to particularly vulnerable sub-sets of the IDP and refugee populations, such as single-female households, persons with disabilities and the elderly who may face greater challenges in addressing their property righIn all target locations there are high numbers of female IDPs living in insecure and unsafe settlements. In formal IDP sites such as Juba PoC sites, HI will continue to coordinate closely with GBV partners, camp management and health providers to ensure post-incident support to survivors, as well as to take necessary steps to address risks long term. Protection monitoring and reporting is critical to identify trends and prominent risks arising for female IDPs as well as to develop suitable solutions to address such risks. Community-based protection and MHPSS groups can also play a critical role in solutions development, as well to provide long-term support to survivors. Given that this project is intending to support marginalized and acutely vulnerable beneficiaries it is essential that needs-based selection criteria are followed. To mitigate against external interference in this process, HI will carry out detailed consultations with key stakeholders, and expressly outline its standardized vulnerability criteria for beneficiary and site selection.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

HI has a robust feedback and complaints mechanism for beneficiaries that combines both unsolicited communication channels such as suggestion boxes, text and phone-line as well as an anonymous reporting email service, with solicited forms such as monthly focus group discussions, interviews and post-intervention monitoring surveys. Detailed sensitization of communities on when, how and why to report is critical for the functioning of the system, as is HIs internal review, response and feedback protocols – designed to close the loop. To ensure the most vulnerable community members can report feedback and complaints, HI aligns communication channels to the context – using local languages, participatory methodologies and relying less on formal written feedback or extractive methods. HI will address communication and information barriers for persons with disabilities by utilizing accessible materials such as braille and visual formats, where required sign-language interpretation, sighted guides and physical accessibility will be accommodated to ensure maximum participation of persons with disabilities. Lastly, HI will work alongside disabled people’s organizations to mobilize and engage with persons with disabilities, and will take their advice on the adaptations required to ensure their meaningful participation in the process.
