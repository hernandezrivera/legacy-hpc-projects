Project Summary

This project aims to mitigate the COVID 19 outbreak impact on the integrated Sexual and Reproductive Health services (SRH) and ensure the life-saving care for the residents of the most vulnerable communities of Area C and East Jerusalem of the West Bank. The project will do this through three main areas:
1. Provision of health services that are needed due to the humanitarian crisis by a.) supporting MoH and PMRS to run primary health care level 2 mobile clinics including provision of SRH services for women, girls, young people including people with disabilities and b.) supporting emergency needs in fixed health facilities in the target areas, particularly H2.
2. Community outreach on essential SRH and COVID19 information, ie. danger signs in pregnancy, family planning, etc. COVID19 will be integrated into the information and community outreach in regards to how it impacts SRH.
3. Improved coordination of SRH humanitarian partners in close collaboration with MOH.

1 - Needs

The occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) is best described by the chronic political crises which has long been impacting the health care system in the country and has caused a severe crisis on the development and capacity of the system to grow and sustain essential services, especially for those living in Area C, H2, and East Jerusalem. In 2020, the COVID-19 outbreak put further strain on an already fragile healthcare system in the oPt. The sexual and reproductive healthcare (SRH) services including interventions targeting GBV survivors have been disrupted, particularly during the times of intense. Access to basic healthcare, including family planning, that prevents maternal and new-born morbidity and mortality has been severely disrupted in the whole country and in particular in Area C and H2 of the West Bank, including East Jerusalem. UNFPA conducted a rapid needs assessment in April 2020 on the impact of COVID 19 on the SRH services where it was recommended to increase access to SRH services, potentially through alternate modalities, such as home visits and mobile clinics, and by supporting the development and implementation of a revised MOH emergency action plan for SRH. About 173,000 Palestinians living in communities located in Area C, East Jerusalem, Hebron/H2 and the closed area behind the Barrier, continue suffering from limited or no access to primary healthcare and are therefore in need of mobile clinics services (HNO 2020). In July-August 2020, PCBS and the UNCT conducted an assessment on the impact of COVID19 on a variety of indicators and needs. The assessment found that although in general access to health services throughout COVID19 has been high, access to sexual and reproductive health (SRH) services has been significantly impacted. The results of the survey have not yet been made public by PCBS, however, the preliminary results have shown that among households seeking sexual and reproductive health services, such as antenatal care, many people were not able to access the required services, primarily due to facility closure, transportation and movement restrictions, and cost. It also identified the need for increased access to services for people living with disabilities. 9,000 households were surveyed. This indicates a strong need for mobile clinic services, especially for SRH care. Data can be provided on request, but cannot be included in the proposal, as the results are not public. Beside the alternative modalities of SRH service provision it is recommended to support the MoH PHC services care where they lack the ability to sustain the services due to the impact of the COVID 19, the disruption of the lockdown, and diversion of resources to COVID19. Even when services are available, there is a decrease in attendance among pregnant and lactating women due to; fear of being infected; movement restrictions; lack of public transportation; and loss of wages and inability to afford transportation to services (The Impact of COVID-19 on sexual and reproductive, including maternal healthin Palestine. April 17, 2020) In addition to the absence of a good and effective referral mechanisms between PHC services and the secondary level during emergencies, it is needed to establish SRH emergency committees to facilitate coordination and referrals and ensure that the situation in Hebron does not repeat itself either due to COVID19 or possible annexation. The pandemic of COVID 19 has clearly shown there is a huge need to improve the coordination among providers and to jointly establish unified referral pathways for emergency situations especially on the local level with aiming to link it with the national level as well. This will raise the quality and availability, and accessibility of health care services including the SRH services. a service directory developed during CO19 epidemic will be utilized as one of the tools to promote knowledge among communities on available services during emergencies.

2 - Activities

Activity 1. Sustain the MoH lifesaving SRH services, and support the delivery of mobile clinics of PMRS services in area C of the West Bank. To ensure sustaining the continuum of SRH lifesaving care services for women, girls, and youth by integrating the GBV, and youth services within the PHC of the MOH clinics in the marginalized areas of area C. Furthermore, the project will support PMRS to operate mobile clinics to support the same service delivery in the areas where MoH has restriction to attend and provide services there. The PMRS and MoH are both active members in the mobile clinic sub cluster where they will coordinate and collaborate to provide quality SRH services and facilitate referrals through establishment of unified and clear referral pathways. The MoH staff working in the PHC clinics and PMRS mobile staff teams will be trained on Infection Protection and Control (IPC), where needed, to further build their capacities in keeping the services and decrease COVID 19 morbidity among health care staff to be able to continue providing the services. In addition, provide PPE supplies for both staff MoH and PMRS mobile teams, the staff who haven’t been trained will be trained in detection and referral of GBV cases in the targeted. Trainings for health care providers in the targeted areas on MoH protocols on dealing with mothers with COVID 19. Provide necessary equipment and supplies mainly to ensure safe delivery at targeted locations and based on designated hospitals for delivery in those places. Furthermore, the project will support the procurement of family planning medications and supplies. The project will emphasize on the role of the midwifery in the continuum of the lifesaving SRH services through capacity building to local midwives to provide emergency support. This will be linked to Activity 3 to improve coordination and referral pathways among SRH stakeholders during emergencies. Furthermore, the project will include home visits and might support in home safe deliveries during the emergencies and movement restrictions due to COVID 19 or political upraising in the targeted areas. Home delivery is an absolute last resort, as women should deliver in facilities whenever possible. Technical support for MoH health care staff and the PMRS mobile teams will be provided by sensitization sessions and technical trainings to ensure that they have the ability to interact and provide quality SRH services for persons with disabilities. This will be done in partnership with organization of persons with disabilities (OPD) who will be consulted in the capacity building process and will implement the trainings and follow up on practices. • Activity 2. Community outreach and awareness raising. This will include dissemination of SRH service directory to all beneficiaries, and to develop accessible awareness messages on services available and hotlines to access SRH consultations in case of emergency in the targeted areas. Conduct educational sessions for women and pregnant women with coordination and collaboration with all SRH service providers in the targeted areas on the high risk pregnancies and post-natal care among other SRH issues. The project will support the community awareness sessions through selected grassroots CBOs for pregnant mothers and women on protections measures of COVID-19 and other SRH topics, such as family planning. • Activity 3. Improve coordination among stakeholders through the establishment of emergency committees on governmental level throughout the targeted areas. This will include creation of the committees which will have representatives from all service providers who are acting in the targeted area. These committees will be created based on a clear and agreed upon terms of reference to determine the overall objectives and function of the committee and the role of each member. These committees will be linked with the SRH Working Group where they will be supported technically to participate in the WG meetings during emergency situation to assess monitor and bridge any gaps with other service providers. The project will also support overall coordination of SRH through the SRH working group.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Due to the ongoing pandemic, it is crucial that the project integrates COVID-19 protection and prevention measures, such as PSEA, risk mitigation measures and adapts interventions. UNFPA will act upon the gained understanding with the priority to consider and uphold the rights and to not cause any further negative impact and harm. Safety and security will be guaranteed by upholding dignity and protection in the provision of services focusing on privacy, acceptance and person’s choices. Partners and other actors of the project will be trained and mentored to deliver non-judgmental, disability-sensitive and high-quality integrated PHC/SRH services ensuring confidentiality, privacy and respecting person’s choices about the care of those seeking help, including persons with disabilities. Safeguards are to be put in place in ensuring confidentiality to not be compromised when mobility is restricted. At the same time, the specialized personnel need to be supported in delivering services and upholding their right and dignity through being treated equitably and fairly. It is especially important to train on COVID-19 response guidelines both to protect the patient and the service provider. In relation to health facilities (both fixed and mobile), safety and protection will be reached by identifying and securing surrounding areas minimizing threats and vulnerabilities, especially following and implementing guidelines on COVID-19 IPC. Reasonable infrastructure adaptations will be made when needed e.g. ramps, railings, as well as information and communication means like braille sign language interpretation to partners’ health facilities in order to make services more inclusive for all.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project design takes into into consideration needs based on gender, age and vulnerabilities as well as the disabilities since the need for SRH and accessibility to services are different. Primarily, the project is ensuring that selected communities receive SRH services to improve their quality of life. This project is designed to provide equal and impartial accessibility without barriers and discrimination through all planned activities. Impartiality is upheld through striving for a large outreach and accessibility by coordination with other operating actors in combination with continuously conducting needs assessments. It is of importance to highlight and promote women, girls, and young people’s rights to health care and how/where to obtain it by comprehensive awareness and education sessions e.g. utilizing existing referral systems.The project is based on the needs of women, girls, young people, but take into consideration possible differences in urgent needs, faced risks and faced traumatic experiences, which are gender, age and disability -specific, vulnerabilities and connected to location of residency. It will primarily target identified vulnerable women, girls, young people for crucial SRH including considering the arrangement of peoples’ access and availability to services including adapting services and information (developing a specific alternative communication strategy in order to ensure reaching everyone, especially those at risk of exclusion). That is achieved by offering different alternatives in order to be able to widen the reach and met needs, through existing health clinics (fixed and mobile) and home visits when applicable and enhance services and interventions through other channels such as phone and virtual platforms. This will allow barriers to be tackled that may restrict freedom of movement and/or people from seeking help, for instance, making sure all persons with disabilities who are in need for SRH services are considered, and those who cannot access services due to for instance restrictions related to COVID-19, no access to internet or technological device, socio-cultural norms and restrictions by tackling stigma and misconceptions at all levels. Mobile clinics and virtual platforms will especially have the role to enable services and information and community-based support interventions to reach people in more vulnerable communities.
