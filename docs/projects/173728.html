Project Summary

Street Child will enhance the protection of 37,000 GBV survivors, particularly children, through strengthening the delivery of and access to quality GBV preventive and response services. This will include community awareness and engagement, mental health and psychosocial support services, GBV case management services, and livelihoods support services. The focus will be on children and adolescents, 70% of whom will be girls. Street Child will also seek to build the capacity of local actors to provide quality GBV prevention and response services, and will engage different community groups and stakeholder to build awareness of GBV risks and prevention strategies at local levels.
In Yobe, LGAs which will be targeted are Gujba and Gulani. In Adamawa, the targeted LGAs will be Mubi, Michika and Madagali. In Borno the targeted LGAs will be MMC, Jere, Gwoza, Damboa, Monguno, Biu, Mobbar, Dikwa and Konduga.

1 - Needs

The ongoing crisis in North East Nigeria is primarily a protection crisis, which has exposed civilians to violence, exploitation and abuse, including gender-based violence. Gender-based is widespread, and has been aggravated by displacement, limited access to services, disruption of livelihoods as well as harmful socio-cultural practices. An estimated 1.3 million people are in need of GBV support across the three worst-affected states of Borno, Yobe and Adamawa in 2020. Over 1 million of these are girls and women, and over 756,000 are children. These statistics show the particular vulnerability of female-headed households and adolescent girls, in particular, to sexual exploitation and abuse - including survival sex, early marriage and forced prostitution. Women and children who have been abducted by armed groups may have endured rape, sexual abuse or forced marriage, and often face high levels of stigmatisation after their release. Children associated with armed groups (CAAG) and unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) are therefore particularly vulnerable. Stigmatisation can also contribute to economic marginalisation, making it difficult for survivors to rebuilt their livelihoods and reintegrate fully into the community. Despite the particular vulnerability of women and girls, it is important to note that all population groups - girls, boys, women and men from host community, IDP and returnee communities are at-risk and in need of support. All forms of GBV, experienced by all types of survivors, are chronically under-reported due to stigmatisation and marginalisation as well as the lack of effective reporting and response mechanisms. Hence the importance of awareness creation and community engagement that encourages prevention and access for support for victims of GBV. There are significant gaps in the humanitarian response to GBV. There is a general lack of GBV services available, and where services are available the quality (including making of timely referrals and follow-up of referrals made) is often low. Coordination between different partners tends to be weak, and there is also a need to strengthen coordination at the LGA level by building the capacity of community actors and stakeholders as well as local partners. Borno State is the most affected; the GBV needs are categorised as catastrophic in the LGAs of Monguno, Mobbar, Damboa, Biu, Gwoza, Dikwa, Konduga, Jere and MMC. In Yobe, the most severe needs are in the LGAs of Gujba and Gulani. In Adamawa, the most severe needs are in the LGAs of Michika, Madagali, Mubi North and Mubi South.

2 - Activities

Street Child will provide immediate psychological first aid and material assistance, including dignity kits, to 15,000 children affected by GBV, including children associated with armed groups (CAAG) and unaccompanied and separated children (UASC). 15,000 survivors of GBV will also be provided with case management, including a tailored care plan and regular follow up. This will include mental health and psychosocial support by trained psychologists, and referral for other specialised services (such as medical and justice services) where appropriate. Survivors will also be supported with livelihoods support through a tailored package of vocational and business skills training, mentoring and cash grants to enable the survivors to access sustainable income-generating activities and promote their socio-economic reintegration. At the community level, Street Child will also build community-based protection and response mechanisms. This will include awareness raising activities and training of community-based committees on GBV prevention and response principles. Community-based GBV monitoring and support groups will be established as forums for peer support and mentoring, empowering community members to facilitate awareness-raising, prevention and response activities. These will also include establishing men's groups against GBV in order to engage men in combatting GBV and violence against women and challenging harmful gender attitudes. Through all activities, Street Child will seek to address the limited institutional capacity of national GBV partners and to improve the quality of service provision. Street Child is committed to localisation and works exclusively through local partners to implement project activities in addition to providing tailored mentoring and capacity building to strengthen technical and organisational capacity. The focus will be on building the capacity of local partners and stakeholders to respond to GBV in line with the inter-agency minimum standards for GBV in emergencies programming. Capacity building will also seek to improve coordination at the local level, including through the mapping of referral pathways, with the aim of improving the facilitation of timely care, intersectoral referrals and timely follow-up.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Street Child is committed to ensuring that protection is central to all of its activities and projects, acknowledging that prioritising protection, safety, dignity, accountability and minimising any unintended negative effects of our activities are crucial to meaningful, dignified access to support in the communities where we work. To achieve this in this proposed project, specific risks at all project stages have been identified and mitigation strategies integrated into the project design, clear protection reporting and response mechanisms have been included, and protection mainstreaming indicators have been included in the monitoring and evaluation framework. At the beneficiary selection stage, Street Child will use tested vulnerability assessment tools and clear selection process to ensure that beneficiaries are selected based on need and through predetermined and clearly communicated criteria of vulnerability. In order to ensure participation and minimise inter-group tensions, targets have been set to include different communities (IDP, host community and returnee) as well as both men and women. Community leaders and members will be informed of the selection process and criteria during the inception stages, and continuous sensitisation of the selection criteria will be used to ensure that possible intra- and inter-group tensions are minimised. Sensitisation on these topics through regular community visits and feedback sessions has proven successful during past projects, where dissatisfaction about not being included in the project has been reduced by clearly explaining the aims of the project and how beneficiaries were selected. As part of our commitment to accountability and protection, Street Child commits to work towards the elimination of exploitation and abuse in the communities where we works and in our engagement with vulnerable populations. Street Child has a clear safeguarding policy in place which applies to all personnel and affiliates, including implementing partners and community members who are affiliated to our work. All Street Child staff is trained on the safeguarding policy and is aware of their responsibilities. Similarly, all partner staff receive a training on Street Child’s policy and how to report incidents. This training is also given to all community members who are affiliated to the project – community leaders, PMC members, community committee members and community volunteers, for example. Sensitisation during activities through community forums and through the use of visuals such as posters also seeks to empower community members to better understand their rights, and to know how to report any concerns they may have. Finally, Street Child is an active participant of the PSEA working group in Borno, as an extra level of accountability, and follows the best practices and lessons learned shared through this working group.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

SC will utilise proven mechanisms for engaging stakeholders and beneficiaries throughout all stages of project planning, implementation and evaluation. A needs assessment will be conducted before implementation, and will include focus group discussions (FGDs) will different community groups - boys, girls, men and women. FGDs will also be held at baseline and endline to provide community members with the opportunity to give feedback and to understand community satisfaction with the project. A dedicated complaints mechanism (CRM) officer will be identified for all targeted communities. This person will be responsible for collecting feedback and complaints, and liaising between community members, partners and Street Child to share this feedback. The CRM officer will hold weekly help desk sessions in each community, where beneficiaries and community members can share feedback. A register of all complaints made, and responses to these complaints, will be kept. IEC materials will be strategically placed in the communities to educate all community members on the complaint mechanisms and processes. In addition, weekly meetings of community coalitions such as CBCPCs will be attended by either local partner or Street Child staff to collect feedback and share progress with regards to specific child protection concerns. Men and women will be equally represented in the CBCPCs. Anonymous feedback mechanisms will also be established in each community. Anonymous complaints boxes will be placed in each community, and will be checked weekly. Street Child will provide a toll-free line which can be called at any time by community members to provide feedback or report urgent concerns (such as safeguarding concerns). All feedback received through these diverse mechanisms will be fed into review sessions and management decisions, allowing activities to be adapted to respond to feedback where appropriate in line with accountability and evidence-based learning commitments.
