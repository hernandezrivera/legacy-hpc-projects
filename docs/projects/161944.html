Project Summary

The prioritized and calculated gender sensitive interventions as per needs of the affected communities are, to increase understanding of trafficking risks, understanding on sexual exploitation and abuse, understanding and capacity of local authorities, law enforcement agencies, legal structures, organic structure set up in camps, and service providers, to increase access to aid, legal and mental health psycho-social counselling assistance to victims and to create alternative and viable livelihood opportunities for victims as well as vulnerable communities. Light House is committed to, accountable to affected people(AAP) replicate 'do no harm' principles considering potential conflicts and misunderstanding among the refugee and host communities. LH and its partners are committed to complying with the code of conducts of sexual exploitation and abuse(PSEA). Considering the vulnerability, scale and gravity, the project targets both host and refugee communities. All the activities of protection will be implemented in close coordination with other sectors such as Education, Health, Food Security, Shelter etc. as per the sectorial guidance and by establishing reciprocal message dissemination mechanisms and exchanging the best practices.

Needs

As of September, 914,998 Rohingya refugees of whom 55% are women and girls are reported living in different camps in Cox’s Bazar since August 2017 who fled to escape targeted violence in Myanmar. The influx of refugees impacted negatively on the socio-economic conditions of already poverty stricken 336,000 host community. As Cox’s Bazar is one of the trafficking routes, thousands of people caught up in the refugee crisis are at high risk of trafficking in persons. The exodus of the Refugees in such a wretched condition carrying little or nothing in the flight created a new opportunity for traffickers to exploit some of the world’s most vulnerable people especially women and girls. Moreover, limited socio-economic and livelihood opportunities drive thousands of Bangladeshis to look for opportunities abroad many of whom believed to fall into the hands of traffickers ending up in forced labour, prostitution and other exploitative situations. Trafficking in persons also occurs internally within Bangladesh as well. In September, 2019 alone, law enforcers rescued 62 Rohingyas and held 5 traffickers in Cox’s Bazar. Since 2017, IOM supported 295 victims of trafficking. In partnership of its working partner MCCSS, in India, Light House repatriated and rehabilitated 41 trafficking victims so far by leveraging its well functional and tested mechanisms by closely working with Home Ministry Affair of GoB. Light House, and its implementing partner ISDE, LoCos after assessment and analysis do not underestimate the prevailing problems and have decided to intervene by replicating a justified multi-pronged and gender sensitive based approach as to prevention, protection and prosecution of counter human trafficking by strengthening comprehensive community based counter trafficking mechanisms in camps 1, 2,3, 4, 24,25,26 and host communities of Palongkhali, Nhilla and Teknaf Unions under Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazilas covering 228619 refugees and 148134 host communities based on its capacity, strengths and local presence in camps and host communities. The prioritized and calculated gender sensitive interventions as per needs of the affected communities are, to increase understanding of trafficking risks, understanding on sexual exploitation and abuse, understanding and capacity of local authorities, law enforcement agencies, legal structures, organic structure set up in camps, and service providers, to increase access to aid, legal and mental health psycho-social counselling assistance to victims and to create alternative and viable livelihood opportunities for victims as well as vulnerable communities.
