Project Summary

This project delivers a strategic and integrated set of interventions to restore the local economy in Northwest Syria. Idleb and Aleppo in the aftermath of protracted conflict further aggravated by the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic and deteriorating economic situation. The main objective is to create income-generating opportunities for the most vulnerable IDPs and host community members in the targeted areas. The project proposes an integrated livelihood and infrastructures rehabilitation intervention where the most vulnerable households (HHs) will be directly targeted to enhance their resilience and promote their access to employment and entrepreneurship opportunities. Besides, firsthand consultations, conducted specifically for this project with local stakeholders, have also informed the design of this proposal. By pursuing the referred main objective, the project aims to achieve the following outcomes:
1- Access to inclusive livelihoods and entrepreneurship support is enhanced through vocational training for 300 BNFs (40% females & 60% males), where unemployed people (females and males) receive livelihoods support through vocational training and micro-grants at Dana sub-district in 4 communities (Atma, Qah, Aqrabat, and Mashhad Rohin). 30 BNFs (12 women & 18 men) which equals 10% of the total BNFs (300) of this activity will be chosen from the disabled people who will be selected considering the Washington Group Short Set of Questions.
2- Essential socioeconomic services are restored through rehabilitating public parks and markets in Al-Bab, Azaz, and Jarablus communities, and providing cash for work against working in community campaigns.
For this purpose, a set of interlinked activities will be conducted in coordination with ERL and Protection clusters. Firstly, the project foresees the identification of 300 HHs (40% females & 60% males) with a specific focus on severe economic vulnerability to provide them with Vocational training. 100 of the trainees (40 females, 60 males) will receive microgrants support for start-up projects. Another 100 trainees will be directed to shops/workshops where they can work there. These shops/workshops will be supported to employ these people and develop their enterprises. Moreover, these activities will target both host communities and IDPs to enhance social cohesion in targeted communities as well as increase the coordination and harmonization among targeted people. Secondly, 6 essential socioeconomic infrastructures will be rehabilitated in Azaz, Al-Bab, and Jarablus communities, where a park and a market will be rehabilitated in each community. In addition, 450 unskilled workers (40% females & 60% males) of the most vulnerable HHs will receive short-term work opportunities through conditional cash-for-work for 2 months for conducting community campaigns like road cleaning, road afforestation, and sidewalk restoration and painting campaigns. Moreover, local councils, key people will be consulted regarding CFW intervention in order to apply to Do No Harm in the targeted communities regarding daily wages, number of working hours per day, and duration of the project, and to ensure project CFW activities in the targeted communities will not negatively affect the local labor market and will not draw workers away from local employment opportunities.

3 - Needs

Northwest Syria is facing significant challenges in its early recovery efforts following a decade of conflict. According to the HNO 2023 report, there are 3.6 million people in need of assistance in the region, including 2.9 million internally displaced persons (IDPs). One of the key priorities in early recovery is supporting the local economy and social cohesion through vocational training and rehabilitation of parks and markets.The conflict has disrupted traditional economic activities and led to high levels of unemployment, particularly among youth. Providing support for vocational training and rehabilitation of public spaces such as parks and markets can help to create employment opportunities and support the recovery of the local economy, as well as promote social cohesion and a sense of community in the region.In addition, it is also important to address the needs of IDPs and returning refugees. Many IDPs in northwest Syria have experienced trauma and have lost their homes, livelihoods, and access to basic services such as healthcare, education, and water. Providing support for housing, livelihoods, and basic services is critical to enable IDPs to rebuild their lives and communities in the region.Finally, supporting the rehabilitation of infrastructure, including parks and markets, is also essential for the early recovery of northwest Syria. Rebuilding these systems is necessary for improving the delivery of basic services and supporting the recovery of communities.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

ATAA PSEA policy developed in June 2022 and with official approvals from PSEA network and UNICEF PSEA focal point and delivered to all staff to be signed, on the other hand ATAA became low risk partner in SEA with UNICEF for 5 years after conducting PSEA assessment.ATAA adopted a core concept in all intervention to make PSEA and CSG as mandatory in all projects and activities, in this regard, all ATAA staff, volunteers and contractors will get orientation sessions about PSEA and CSG policies and sign both PSEA and CSG code of conducts.All project locations will include banners and complaint boxes with delivering messages from accountability teams regarding the complaints and feedback channels in addition to stand alone system regarding reporting and response of any PSEA case starting from receiving the report, to investigation process, and provide aid and support to the survivor till closure of the case after achieving the objectives of investigation.ATAA is PSEA network member participate in all regular network meetings, in addition to submit monthly reports to the network, in addition to conduct training for all staff about PSEA and target humanitarian workers in the intervention location to support the role of PSEA network.ATAA is operating PSEA stand-alone project focusing on raising the awareness of targeted communities (78 community) in NW Syria about PSEA and enhance the ability of BNFs to reach reporting channels of ATAA and PSEA network.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Poor knowledge delivered to trainees. Impact: Low qualified graduated trainees and lack of job opportunities. Mitigation: high experienced trainers will be hired to deliver high-quality information. Risk 2: Project items not available in the market to be provided. Impact: Beneficiaries will not be able to implement/develop their projects.Mitigation: availability of products in the market will be one of the criteria to select successful proposals to be funded with micro-grants. Risk 3: Fluctuation in rehabilitation general costs and raw materials. Impact: Poor rehabilitation quality.Mitigation: the technical assessment will be followed immediately by the determination of quantities, and this will be followed by the tendering process, therefore the tendering process will be based on updated prices. Risk 4: Delay in implementation activities. Impact: Rehabilitation works are delayed rainy season.Mitigation: Regular follow-up to the work plan, and good coordination with local communities and local councils, the rehabilitation works will be conducted in the dry season. Risk 5: High and critical markets volatilityImpact: Rehabilitation may not be handed over at the required quality.Mitigation: Market assessment will be conducted at the beginning of the project.
