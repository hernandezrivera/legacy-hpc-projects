Project Summary

In light of the context, there is need to relook at the issue of revenge killing from a critical perspective through a comprehensive assessment and inform a conceptual framework on how to use CwC approaches to strengthen community recognition of humanitarian work, and commitment to upholding the safety and security of aid workers, including by providing a feedback mechanism platform that is relevant, feasible and sustainable for both humanitarian actors and the local community. This will enable the community to voice their opinions, as well as to better appreciate and understand the impact of revenge killings on humanitarian action, thereby promoting mutual respect between the community and aid workers and helping to increase partners’ confidence and a safe environment for the provision of humanitarian services. A total of 30,000 beneficiaries mostly from host communities & local leaders will be targeted of which (14,700 Male & 15,300 female) as beneficiaries.

Needs

The issue of revenge killings is a deeply rooted cultural problem that has been the leading source of inter communal insecurity among the Nuer and Anyuak. This culture has brought adverse effects like multiple fatalities, disruptions to people’s way of life, led to displacements of entire sub clans and even under development due to the inability of educated persons to settle and work in their home areas. The current effect of the recent revenge killings in the recent times have threatened the humanitarian services as the targets of the revenge attackers has shifted to the local staff employed by various NGOs; the wave of these attacks have prompted the humanitarian actors to threaten pull out of the all the services if the revenge attacks continue. The problem is made worse by the fact that traditional justice systems have been seriously eroded, partly due to large scale conflicts in recent years and a weakened local authorities’ structure. The other driver has been cited as the presence of numerous arms in the hands of civilians following re arming of youths in the wake of the December 2013 civil war. The absence of an effective and efficient feedback mechanism that allows the community to understand and voice concerns about on‑going projects has meant that there is an absence of community recognition and meant that community members may not value and appreciate humanitarian interventions and understand how revenge killings affect the delivery of services. In light of the context, there is need to relook at the issue of revenge killing from a critical perspective through a comprehensive assessment and inform a conceptual framework on how to use CwC approaches to strengthen community recognition of humanitarian work, and commitment to upholding the safety and security of aid workers, including by providing a feedback mechanism platform that is relevant, feasible and sustainable for both humanitarian actors and the local community. This will enable the community to voice their opinions, as well as to better appreciate and understand the impact of revenge killings on humanitarian action, thereby promoting mutual respect between the community and aid workers and helping to increase partners’ confidence and a safe environment for the provision of humanitarian services.

Activities

Assessment of local capacity gaps in coordinating and supporting humanitarian activities. Assessment shall include constraints to meaningful participation in humanitarian interventions – with focus on the local authorities, capacity gaps in different sectoral groups and representatives of different vulnerable categories of community members.Capacity building of relevant stakeholders. The project will coordinate with sectoral agencies in the host communities to build capacity of leaders that play crucial coordinating role to enhance the work of humanitarian organisations. The stakeholders shall include county departments, local chiefs, civic leaders including representations of churches, women groups, other interest groups or organizations.The role of the stakeholders shall include; providing regular up date on beneficiary feedback to humanitarian actors, supporting efforts against hindrances or access limitations, coordinating information gathering in hard to reach locations, conducting social Mobilization and sensitizations – including use of local community radio/ pre-recorded messages to broadcasts ongoing humanitarian services; information sharing using different community media, among othersIn order to provide timely support in the event of emergencies, the project shall provide training on early warning & contingency planning to local resource persons. Identified persons from the different locations shall benefit from training sessions tailored to emergencies that are most prone in each location. This includes emergencies brought about by floods, impromptu and phased displacements, etc.Cross cutting capacity building sessions that include conflict sensitivity in different contextual situations shall be accorded during project implementation.Developing a feedback mechanism for humanitarian interventions: In 2018, UNKEA rolled out a successful pilot initiative on feedback mechanism in Longechuk county, Upper Nile state. It involved identifying a select team of community members who have no direct interface with the project either as beneficiaries or in the different project community structures. Their role included receiving complaints, observing project activities, meeting to discuss the issues arising and communicating to head office on all matters that arose on a quarterly basis. The outcome of this committee was instrumental, unearthing issues that had not been reported before. This pilot was premised on food security & livelihood intervention. Under this proposal, UNKEA plans to scale up this approach to cover multiple sectors that include health, protection, nutrition, WASH, and education. Specifically, UNKEA will;Form Pilot Humanitarian Partners Multi Sectoral Feedback Mechanism Platform in the two counties. This will include providing orientation to all identified participants in the two counties. A total of 16 committees, 14 at payam levels and 2 at county levels shall be established.During the pilot phase, UNKEA will review and provide technical support during the period the pilot for both payam and county level established structures.Documentation of Lessons and sharing with CwC WG partners for possible replication in other counties. Establishing & enhancing inter sectoral community committees. UNKEA shall work with different local teams to establish volunteer working committees that provide oversight in the delivery of humanitarian servicesRegular assessments that provide humanitarian actors understanding of the risk levels associated with the locations of assessed. Bulletins shall be published on a monthly basis to provide guidance to different actors on status in the project locations. UNKEA anticipates that these activities will increase impact and enable humanitarian agencies provide timely response even in the most hard to reach locations.
