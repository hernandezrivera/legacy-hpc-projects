Project Summary

An estimated 2.9 million people in Somalia are internally displaced as of 2021. The main drivers of displacement in the country relate to conflict and climate shocks such as drought and floods. As Somalia faces the prospect of three consecutive failed harvest seasons, many poor rural households have continued to relocate to main towns in search of livelihood opportunities as well as social and humanitarian support due to the lack of food and income sources in their villages.
Electoral violence, inter-clan conflict – driven by rivalries, land, property and resource control disputes will continue to displace and affect households. These dynamics are intensified by repeated climate shocks like droughts and floods, which further reduce the availability of water and pastures.
Humanitarian partners are also concerned about the recent displacement of thousands of people in Somaliland and Galmudug State. According to the UNHCR-led Protection and Return Monitoring Network (PRMN), since October 2, more than 3,300 of those affected have arrived in Gaalkacyo, of whom 986 have proceeded to South West State, Hirshabelle and Banadir. Thousands of people have also been displaced by the recent government offensives against Al Shabaab in Wisil, Amara and Bacadweyne areas as well as the recent Government forces and Ahlu Suna fights in Bohol village and Guriceel district. Key installations such as hospitals, schools and homes have been destroyed in the fight. Authorities have since appealed for urgent humanitarian assistance, particularly food, Water, Shelter and Health interventions. Partners including CPD have done a joint assessment to determine the exact numbers of affected people, needs and response. CPD is among agencies already responding to the emergencies with a one-month multi-purposes Unconditional Cash Support ($110) for 270HHs in Guriceel.
According to the JMCNA 2021, shelter is one of the top three priority needs of more than half of the entire population. An estimated 3.6 million people in Somalia need shelter and NFI assistance mainly due to poor shelter conditions, overcrowding and lack of space, poverty, and lack of security of tenure. Those in need of shelter and NFI assistance include displaced and non-displaced populations. The scale of Shelter and NFI needs in 2022 is expected to increase in comparison to the needs in 2021. Women and girls living in sub-standard and overcrowded shelters are particularly vulnerable and are unsafe in makeshift shelters without privacy and sufficient protection from weather and increases the risk of gender-based violence.
In line with the Shelter cluster strategy’s three main pillars: Shelter assistance, NFI assistance, and Provision of Community infrastructure, CPD plans to undertake the Provision of Shelter and NFI assistance to conflict and droughts-displaced persons in South Central Somalia (Galmudug State and Benadir Region) for up to 2,800HHs through mixed methods of in-kind and Cash Voucher Assistance support through contingency stocks and direct implementation respectively prioritizing IDPs as per the Shelter and NFI kits guidelines which will guide the appropriate processes for distribution and the key considerations to observe throughout the project cycle. The organization will target IDPs, host communities and refugee returnees. Newly displaced, evictees, IDPs affected by recent conflicts and offensives and IDPs living in sites without long term security of tenure and target areas with High Severity and People In Need will be prioritized.
To improve accountability CPD will monitor effectiveness of delivered assistance. Programe design will therefore take into account the specific needs of women and, where appropriate, women’s dignity kits will also be distributed. CPD will also establish complaint and feedback mechanisms for Shelter and NFI projects implemented and conduct and analyze PDM and use the findings to improve the quality of future projects and share lessons learned.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Supports or revitalizes local trade: Injections of cash or the use of commodity vouchers with local traders benefits the recovery of trade and the local economy. Cash-based support for livelihood rehabilitation can potentially also provide a link between relief and longer term economic recovery and may offer the opportunity for beneficiaries to increase income and create savings. Empowers the beneficiaries: The availability of cash gives households a sense of restored power over their immediate situation. In addition, there is evidence that receiving cash can at least temporarily empower women within the household. Access to financial structures such as bank accounts may empower beneficiaries, in particular women, in the longer-term as well. Ensures flexibility and choice: One size does not fit all! Using cash or vouchers recognises that beneficiaries are best placed to decide what their households need, and gives them the flexibility to choose according to their own diverse needs and priorities. Enhances dignity: In allowing beneficiaries to control what goods and services their households need, cash transfers are considered by many as more dignified than receiving in kind, and recognize beneficiaries as active participants in providing for the welfare of their families after a disaster. Reduces negative coping strategies: In the modern world, most households depend on cash to some extent. When aid is given in kind, households are often forced to seek cash in ways that may be detrimental to longer term livelihoods, for example selling productive assets, or giving children to the care of relatives. Evidence shows that cash transfers reduce the negative coping strategies used by beneficiaries. Has multiplier effects: By stimulating local markets, cash-based interventions may benefit more than the direct recipients of cash. Local traders and service providers, transport workers and suppliers and transporters of goods may all benefit from households having money to spend.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

As a first activity, CPD will carry out detailed needs and vulnerability assessments in identified and targeted communities. The project will use the Market Functionality Index (MFI) designed by WFP and is part of the new Business Process Model for cash-based transfers. It is made to support the ‘Market assessment and risk identification’ and 'Market situation monitoring' processes of cash-based assistance operations. The market assessments will tackled: 1) Assortment of essential goods,2) Availability3) Price4) Resilience of supply chains5) Market competition, 6) Infrastructure7) Services8) Food quality9) Access and protection. The project will also consider the use of the Somali shilling and its acceptance or not in some areas as well as the wide use of mobile money transfer by most of the population in Somalia.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

The project will ensure protection is mainstreamed by adhering to the following principles and promoting meaningful access, safety and dignity in the delivery of Shelter and NFI assistance by the project. The following elements will be taken into account in all activities: Risk of Exclusion and its mitigation: Ensuring that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable by Identifying and prioritising the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups. Applying a systematic outreach process in registering beneficiaries. Ensuring that the location, distance and access routes to sites are accessible to all members of the community, including the disabled and most vulnerable groups. Making infrastructure adaptions within the camps accessible to persons with disability. Establishing mechanisms to give priority to those who cannot stand in line for long periods such as persons with disability. The project will set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms e.g. place feedback surveys in NFI kits, feedback box at distribution sites, provide toll-free phone numbers in all locations e.t.c. Risk of interference from external stakeholders and its mitigation: This will be mitigated by avoiding community tensions, by providing the local/host community with access to camp services and assistance in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of non-food items. Providing clear information to distribution actors and affected population mostly women, girls and people living with disabilities explaining that beneficiaries do not have to pay or provide services/favours to anyone in exchange for receiving shelter or NFI assistance. Engaging the community and committee representatives to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response. Risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity in targeted project area and its mitigation; The project will take into account the land tenure arrangements of IDPs, including statutory/legislative and customary access rights to land and inheritance rights. It will also reach out to the land and property owners and local authorities to avoid eviction or disproportional rising in rent. The project will also obtain permission before using or building on any land or property, in writing, where possible.
