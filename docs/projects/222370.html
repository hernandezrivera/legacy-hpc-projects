Project Summary

Within Canal and Tonj East counties, heavy rains that commenced in July and September 2024 respectively, caused rise in water levels in the rivers, destruction of roads and continue to threaten many households leading to population displacement, destruction of farm produce and health care infrastructure.
COSEDA is seeking to respond to aforementioned challenges with a four-prong approach as follows; Improve equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations. Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases. Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms.
COSEDA, will seek to compliment HSTP by continuing to provide evidence based primary health care services to the people affected by flooding and disease outbreak in Canal and Tonj East Counties as per the MOH/WHO guidelines. COSEDA will operate the current 18 health facilities in Canal (6) and Tonj East (12) Counties. COSEDA proposes to add 1 mobile clinic and 1 CHD outreach team in each county in order to ensure quality delivery of services amid high number of patients in COSEDA supported static sites in Canal. In Canal county COSEDA will reach 59017(Women-21246, Girls-9443, Men-20066, Boys-8262) and in Tonj East county will target 191418(Women-68911, Girls-30627, Men-65082, Boys-26799) and 28644 persons with disability with lifesaving health services over the project period, Each Mobile Medical Clinic team per county will have 1 clinical officer, 2 enrolled nurses, 1 pharmacy technician, 1 lab technician, 1 registered midwife, 3 EPI vaccinators, 2 CHW, 1 drug dispenser, 1 Statistical data clerk, 1 community midwife, 2 guards and 3 cleaners. Additionally, CHD Outreach team per county will have 1 BHW supervisor, 20 BHW’s, 1 clinical officer, 2 enrolled nurses, 1 pharmacy technician, 2 Lab assistants/RDT takers, 1 registered midwife, 2 EPI vaccinators, 2 CHW, 2 drug dispenser, 1 Statistical data clerk, 1 community midwife, 2 guards and 2 cleaners. Both county teams will share canvas canopy tents, furniture, and various basic medical equipment.
Recently, Canal and Tonj East counties have witnessed increased incidence of immunization preventable diseases like measles and cholera. COSEDA will implement EPI within all its locations to ensure majority of the children less than 1 year are up to date with the South Sudan EPI schedule. COSEDA will procure medical equipment to cater for the ever-raising medical needs, utilize UNICEF funded speed boat and ambulance to ensure seamless referral of patients from static health facilities, mobile clinic and outreach sites to Malakal Teaching Hospital and Tonj Civil Hospital respectively. COSEDA will ensure proper waste management and within the outreach sites as per the IPC protocols by ensuring availability of color-coded bins, protective gears for staffs handling patients, required PPE for Cleaners, adequate hand washing facilities, functional incinerators and sufficient cleaning agents. To ensure adequate and proper storage of drugs, COSEDA will procure more pallets, metallic cabinets and shelves. For quality service provision within the COSEDA supported health facilities, COSEDA will conduct trainings for the new staff such as IPC training, BEmONC, IMNCI, case management of cholera, measles and bilharzia. To further build capacity and ensure available reference resources, COSEDA will print various protocols like IMNCI, South Sudan treatment guidelines, drugs formulary and job aids to support the clinical team in their quest to offer quality care. To improve response to disease outbreak, COSEDA proposes to set up 3 infectious diseases centers within Rumabuth in Tonj east, Canal and Kurwai.

1. Needs

In October, the humanitarian situation continued to deteriorate in South Sudan, driven by high food prices, a severe economic crisis, conflict, disease outbreaks and cross-border movements from Sudan and flooding. Measles and Cholera outbreak continued across the country, with an increasing number of mealses and Cholera cases reported in Tonj East and Canal Counties respectively. Most of the health facilities lack basic medical supplies, equipment and human resources to address the rising health needs within Tonj East and Canal counties. There's an urgent need for improved supply of medical supplies especially Trauma management supplies, basic examination equipment and class room tents to support Infectious Diseases Unit set up. For health partners to ensure quality drugs are dispensed, there’s need to set up facility-based drug storage facilities that have temperature control measures to ensure the received drugs are stored as per the WHO aware guidelines. Facility-based Medical Waste management is also a gap that needs to be addressed with setting up standard waste disposal site in each facility. Currently Tonj East county has only one local incinerator situated in Kacuat PHCU serving the entire county and Canal county has only 1 drum incinerator in Kurwai PHCC. The EPI services are on-going however, with significant drop out rates occasioned by displacement of population due to conflict, flooding and impassable roads due to floods and intercommunal conflict experienced in the two counties. There's need to conduct immunization campaigns to boost routine EPI activities. Patient referral is a challenge that needs urgent redress since the Tonj East community is depending on agency ambulance for referring emergency SRH and trauma cases to Tonj Civil Hospital in Tonj South. In Canal County the situation is worse with no speed boat hence emergency referral of SRH cases is a challenge.
