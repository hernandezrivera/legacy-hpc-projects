Project Summary

Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA) is one of the most food-insecure, conflict and flood-prone areas in South Sudan. The area is classified as Priority 1 for the HRP intersectoral prioritization. IPC projections predict that an alarming 80% of its population will be in IPC3 towards the peak of the lean season, with 88,000 people in IPC4 and 11,000 in IPC5. The area is particularly affected as a result of persistent seasonal flooding and the ongoing subnational conflict which often interrupts the food and seed systems of local communities, thereby compromising their food production potential and threatening their resilience and adaptive capacity.
The above factors are compounded by the currency, limited employment opportunities, particularly for youth and a general reduction in humanitarian funding which has led to the scaling down of humanitarian support for the population in need. This makes it extremely difficult for the population to recover from the shocks caused by these drivers without a mitigative response to facilitate recovery, resilience and adaptive capacity strengthening for local communities in the short and medium term.
Communities continue to face significantly high levels of food insecurity, and an increasing number of malnutrition cases. There is generally limited livelihood opportunities, forcing many families to resort to negative coping strategies.
Continuous cycles of flood/conflict-related displacement and general insecurity have made households lose their livelihoods and made them dependent on food and agricultural inputs from humanitarian agencies, while others practice cattle raiding and marrying off their underage girl children, as a coping mechanism.
Women and girls continue to bear the shocks of displacement arising from the conflict and the flooding.
To respond to the immediate needs of communities in GPAA, ZOA Dorcas proposes the Enhancing Climate and Food Security Resilience of Conflict and Flood-Affected Communities in GPAA (ECFR). The project will target 1,500 households equivalent to 9000 people in 3 locations of Pibor, Likuangole and Gumuruk.
The proposed response will contribute to mitigating the vulnerability of flood and conflict-affected communities in the GPAA by strengthening their resilience and adaptive capacities to cope with the adverse effects of conflict and climate as well as reducing their dependency on food and agricultural inputs from international aid agencies. The response is in line with HRP Strategic Objective 3 ‘Vulnerable people’s capacity to withstand the impact of shock is increased, and nexus opportunities are optimized’, and Cluster Objective 3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks and contextual changes”.
The proposed project seeks to deliver integrated interventions that are sensitive to both climate change and conflict vulnerabilities of affected households. It will strengthen the community's capacity to produce sufficient food, build their capacity to mitigate conflicts and strengthen people’s resilience to the adverse effects of climate change.
ZOA Dorcas has over 10 years of experience in GPAA working in the areas of FSL, WASH and Peacebuilding and is known as a trusted, reliable partner by the community and authorities. The proposed intervention builds on ZOA Dorcas’ existing track record in FSL and peacebuilding and projects in emergency food assistance and food systems resilience.

2. Project activities

The proposed response will work in support of cluster objective 3. The overall outcome is to increase community resilience, measured through the resilience capacity index, and reduction of post-harvest food losses. Specific outputs include 3.1 Improved livelihood productivity, output 3.2 Market Development and 3.3 Anticipatory actions implemented (including DRR and climate change adaptation activities).Output 1.1 Improved food security through direct support to farming households to produce sufficient food for home and market ((cluster output 3.1 – Improved livelihood productivity) Activity 11.1. Training on farm production and good agriconomic practices. ZOA Dorcas will use the FFS approach and support training of 400 farmers by trained OA Dorcas staff and agriculture extension workers (AEW). Training will follow the agricultural season and pay specific focus on -harvest handling practices like threshing, drying, sorting, grading and storage to reduce post-harvest losses while ensuring high-quality final products are available for sale. Activity 1.1.2 Distribution of seeds and tools to most farmers affected by flooding and conflict. Agriculture kits in form of sorghum, maize, cow peas, and tools. Activity 1.1.3 To improve storage at household level, the action will replicate innovative local granaries as well as explore other effective traditional storage structures improved household storage. The project will also ensure quality control at both farmers’ homesOutput 1.2 Market development (cluster output 3.2) Activity 1.2.1 Support Youths to set up IGAs (business skills) – 50 (25 men 25 women) Youths will be selected to benefit from income-generating activities and business skills training. The youth will also receive a start-up grant. To improve the entrepreneurial performance of young people through guided self-analysis, stimulating enterprising behaviour and the build-up of business competencies, the intervention will facilitate tailored business skills training for women and youth groups out of school 17 to 35 years targeting out of school. Activity 1.2.2 Establishment of VSLA groups and value chain development grants - 20 VSLA will be mobilized depending on the number of vulnerable people in each of the 3 payams targeting an average of 6 per community and provided with a kit constituting the money safe, saving books and training on saving and loans. Activity 1.2.3 Training and interventions to promote market access (Construction of local markets, Access roads, Value chain development). ZOA Dorcas will support 3 collecting market committees (CMC). This will aim to strengthen partnerships between farmers and market actors through capacity building to strengthen the technical and business skills necessary to produce good quality products able to consistently meet market demand. Output 1.3 Anticipatory actions implemented (in support of cluster output 3.3)Activity 3.3.1 Pilot the Use of local biomass briquettes & fuel-efficient stoves (Training targeting a total of 250 households in making briquettes, Distribution of fuel saving stoves). ZOA Dorcas will source, promote and distribute fuel saving stoves to vulnerable households in order to reduce pressure on the environment in the 3 locations.Activity 3.3.2 Setting up and training 20 community and county-level disaster risk reduction committees to lead the development of hazard information tools. Additionally, Community Managed Disaster Risk Reduction Committees (CMDRRCs) will be established as a local structure and strengthened to manage environmental care, land use planning and disaster preparedness and management/resilience mechanisms. Activity: 1.3.4 Use of conditional cash transfers to support the implementation of 16 CMDRR resilience and adaptation action plans Activity 1.3.5 Awareness raising on nutrition and cooking demonstrations, hygiene and sanitation – Project staff experienced in nutrition and hygiene promotion.
