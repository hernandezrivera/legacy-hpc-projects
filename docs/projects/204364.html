Project Summary

The impact of the fighting on the civilian population, particularly on the elderly, women and girls and people with disabilities, is devastating. Key infrastructure, such as power plants, the electricity grid, water stations, banking, health facilities and communications, have been severely damaged, making basic services unavailable with stocks and supplies rapidly reducing. There are reports of increased criminality by both combatants and armed criminal gangs, including forced evictions, looting, carjackings and sexual violence.
The project planning is taking into consideration that the situation likely be uneven throughout the country. A main factor to be considered in any scenario, however, is the impact of the conflict on the population of the densely populated capital Khartoum, the damage to key civilian and basic services infrastructure including health services as well as the large-scale displacement and lack of law and order on the roads. In this exceptional situation in response to these circumstances, UNDP Sudan is adjusting and preparing an emergency response prioritizing these emerging needs. This includes response to UXO threats, an emergency stabilization tailored to urban/densely populated settings.
UNDP has a proven methodology for UXO assessment and clearance.
Defining the Unexploded ordnance (UXO) as explosive devices that have been used in armed conflicts but have not detonated. They pose a serious threat to the lives and livelihoods of civilians, especially in post-conflict settings. UXO can contaminate land, water and infrastructure, hampering socio-economic development and peacebuilding efforts. The presence of UXOs inhibits the delivery of lifesaving assistance to people in need, hence, must be dealt with in order to provide the enabling environment for lifesaving activities to take place.
The United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) has developed a comprehensive methodology for UXO assessment and clearance, based on international standards and best practices. The methodology consists of four main steps:
1. Survey: This step involves collecting and analyzing data on the location, type and impact of UXO contamination. The survey can be done through various methods, such as desk research, interviews, field visits and technical surveys. The survey results in a prioritized list of UXO-affected areas that need clearance.
2. Clearance: This step involves removing or destroying UXO from the prioritized areas, using manual, mechanical or explosive techniques. The clearance is done by trained and accredited operators, following strict safety and quality procedures. The clearance results in a certificate of completion that confirms the area is free of UXO.
3. Verification: This step involves checking and validating the clearance results, using independent quality assurance and quality control mechanisms. The verification is done by external monitors or evaluators, who inspect the cleared areas and review the clearance documentation. The verification results in a confirmation of the clearance quality and effectiveness.
4. Handover: This step involves transferring the cleared areas to the national authorities or the intended beneficiaries, such as local communities, government agencies or development partners. The handover is done through a formal ceremony or a written agreement that acknowledges the completion of the clearance process and the ownership of the land.
The UNDP methodology for UXO assessment and clearance aims to ensure that UXO-contaminated areas are safely and efficiently cleared, enabling their productive use for development and peacebuilding purposes.
The project target will be primarily urban centers, that witnessed conflict and heavy destruction.

1 - Needs

The recent fighting between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces (RSF) has been concentrated in densely populated urban centres, with two-thirds of the fighting between SAF and RSF taking place in cities of over 100,000 people, limiting mobility between cities and between rural and urban areas, affecting access to agricultural land, supply routes and markets. Key infrastructure, such as power plants, the electricity grid, water stations, banking and communications infrastructure have been severely damaged, making basic services unavailable. Banks have closed, many small businesses in the urban areas have collapsed due to massive looting while others are on the verge of collapse, food prices have soared while cash circulation has been greatly limited due to the closure of banks. The health sector has been severely damaged, with over 16 hospitals directly hit and need urgent assistance – rehabilitation, equipment and essential medicines and other medical supplies. The impact of the fighting on the civilian population, particularly on the elderly, women and girls and people with disabilities, is devastating. The project planning is taking into consideration that the situation likely be uneven throughout the country. A main factor to be considered in any scenario, however, is the impact of the conflict on the population of the densely populated capital Khartoum, the damage to key civilian and basic services infrastructure including health services as well as the large-scale displacement and lack of law and order on the roads. In this exceptional situation in response to these circumstances, UNDP Sudan is adjusting and preparing an emergency response prioritizing these emerging needs. This includes response to UXO threats, and an emergency stabilization tailored to urban/densely populated settings.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

UXO clearance is the process of removing unexploded ordnance (UXO) from contaminated areas, such as former battlefields, minefields, or buildings and community infrastructure. UXO clearance has significant benefits for human security, development, and peacebuilding, as it reduces the risk of accidental explosions, injuries, and deaths, and enables the safe and productive use of land and natural resources. However, UXO clearance also has environmental impacts that need to be identified, assessed, and mitigated wherever possible. The environmental impacts of UXO clearance are: - Soil degradation: UXO detonation can create craters that displace fertile topsoil, increase erosion, and compact subsoil. This can affect soil quality, water retention, and vegetation growth. Soil degradation is more severe in dry, loose, and exposed soils. - Noise pollution: UXO detonation can generate loud noises that can disturb or harm wildlife and human populations. Noise pollution can affect animal behavior, communication, reproduction, and migration. Noise pollution can also cause stress, hearing loss, and sleep disturbance in humans.- Air pollution: UXO detonation can release toxic gases and particulate matter into the air that can affect air quality and human health. Air pollution can cause respiratory problems, cardiovascular diseases, cancer, and premature death. Air pollution can also contribute to climate change by increasing greenhouse gas emissions.- Water pollution: UXO detonation can contaminate surface water and groundwater with explosive residues, metals, and other chemicals that can affect water quality and aquatic ecosystems. Water pollution can cause harm to fish, plants, animals, and humans that depend on water for survival. Water pollution can also affect water availability and accessibility for domestic, agricultural, and industrial use.- Habitat loss: UXO clearance can involve the removal or alteration of vegetation, soil, rocks, or other natural features that provide habitat for wildlife. Habitat loss can reduce biodiversity, ecosystem services, and resilience. Habitat loss can also increase human-wildlife conflict and invasive species.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

UXO clearance is the process of detecting and removing unexploded ordnance (UXO) from areas affected by armed conflict or military activities. UXO clearance aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation by following several principles and guidelines. Protection mainstreaming is the integration of protection principles and standards into humanitarian action to prevent and mitigate harm, risk and violence for affected populations. Some of the protection principles and standards that UXO clearance follows are: - Do no harm: UXO clearance should avoid causing or exacerbating harm, risk or violence for affected populations, especially vulnerable groups such as women, children, elderly and persons with disabilities. UXO clearance should also respect the dignity, rights and capacities of affected populations and avoid creating dependency or undermining their coping mechanisms. - Participation and empowerment: UXO clearance should involve affected populations in the assessment, planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of UXO clearance activities. UXO clearance should also promote the empowerment of affected populations by enhancing their access to information, resources and services, and by supporting their participation in decision-making processes. - Accountability: UXO clearance should be accountable to affected populations by ensuring transparency, feedback and complaint mechanisms, and by adhering to quality standards and codes of conduct. UXO clearance should also be accountable to donors, partners and authorities by ensuring coordination, collaboration and reporting. - Non-discrimination: UXO clearance should ensure equal access and opportunity for all affected populations regardless of their age, gender, ethnicity, religion, disability or other status. UXO clearance should also address the specific needs and capacities of different groups and individuals within affected populations. By following these principles and standards, UXO clearance aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation, which contributes to the overall protection outcomes of humanitarian action.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The UXO clearance is a vital component of the humanitarian response to the conflict-affected areas. It aims to reduce the risk of harm and injury from unexploded ordnance (UXO) and to facilitate the safe return and recovery of displaced populations. The UXO clearance will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms in the following ways: - The UXO clearance will adhere to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, neutrality and independence, and respect the dignity and rights of the affected people.- The UXO clearance will coordinate with other humanitarian actors and relevant authorities to ensure a comprehensive and integrated response that addresses the needs and priorities of the affected people.- The UXO clearance will conduct regular assessments and consultations with the affected people and communities to identify their needs, preferences, capacities and risks related to UXO.- The UXO clearance will provide clear and timely information to the affected people and communities about the objectives, methods, progress and results of the UXO clearance activities, as well as the potential hazards and safety measures.- The UXO clearance will establish and maintain accessible, safe and confidential feedback mechanisms for the affected people and communities to express their views, concerns, complaints and suggestions regarding the UXO clearance activities.- The UXO clearance will respond to the feedback received from the affected people and communities in a timely, respectful and appropriate manner, and take corrective actions if needed.- The UXO clearance will monitor and evaluate the quality, effectiveness and impact of the UXO clearance activities, and use the feedback from the affected people and communities to inform decision-making and improve performance.- The UXO clearance will report on the achievements, challenges and lessons learned from the UXO clearance activities, and share good practices and recommendations with other humanitarian actors and relevant authorities. The UXO clearance will also ensure that its activities are in line with the inter-agency standards and guidelines on preventing and responding to sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) and other forms of abuse by humanitarian personnel. The UXO clearance will: - Adopt a zero-tolerance policy towards SEA and other forms of abuse by its staff, contractors, partners and beneficiaries, and communicate it clearly to all parties involved.- Conduct thorough background checks on its staff, contractors and partners, and provide them with mandatory training on SEA prevention and response, as well as codes of conduct and ethical standards.- Establish clear reporting procedures for SEA and other forms of abuse allegations or incidents involving its staff, contractors, partners or beneficiaries, and ensure that they are handled in a confidential, impartial and sensitive manner.
