Project Summary

The project will focus in providing direct and specialized support to vulnerable conflict-affected individuals; special focus will be given to woman and girls. The direct support is focused on providing GBV case management, psycho-social services and dignity kits.
The intervention will also enable the community (especially woman and girls) to access humanitarian assistance and services as well as establish long term solution as per needs identified in the GBV assessments. The data for needs assessment are collected though: FGD with community members and children; key informant interviews; information collected through community member conversations; Case Studies; observation by observed "event" such as social gathering and activities as well as experience based on previous projects that Al Mortaqa had run in different areas. Special focus will be given the establishment of GBV community centers where trained team will raise awareness, organize recreation activities, train community members and engage male and youth to promote equal rights for woman and girls. Woman and girls will also be provided with livelihood opportunities and access to services. Stakeholders and service providers will benefit from the project through training's, mentoring, establishing systems for case referrals and creating community based networks. Needs

Gender Based Violence is one of the main issues affecting woman and girls in Iraq. Access to services specialized to address Gender Based Violence don’t exist or is limited in the evaluated areas by Al Mortaqa. Harmful traditional practices prevent girls and women to access education and develop their skills. As a result, a very limited number of woman are employed in the country. Girls are also at risk of exclusion, violence, and harassment, which did lead to increasing incidents of girls being pulled out of school and forced into early marriage in an effort to preserve familial honor. Al Mortaqa approaches the Gender Based Violence by engaging directly with women, men, and children on identifying specific needs and considerations across all projects. In the community meetings that Al Mortaqa conducted the main reason mentioned as an issue is early marriage and domestic violence. Responses suggest that economic disadvantage as well as lack of education is increasingly associated with early marriage in the targeted communities. The child marriage negatively impacts well-being and psycho-social health as well as the educational opportunities of economically disadvantaged. Through the case management are identified high number of girls which is leading families to remove girls from school. Girls face higher levels of harassment in displaced places or in the way to school. Girls also highlighted (FGDs conducted by INGO) the poor state of classrooms, lack of proper hygiene facilities and safe drinking water as reasons for absence from school. High needs are among all community based stakeholder to have trainings and be prepared to deal with GBV. Case management referrals are needed to be established and need to be developed in the future. Community centers are missing in the targeted areas that can give a safe space for women and girls to gather, work and encourage each other. Also awareness in the community is needed on their rights. Through the interventions it is need to promote women and girl’s rights. Also by both improving access to community centers and services and working with communities to ensure that the women and girls rights are acknowledge and respected. The project will target fathers and male caregivers through awareness raising activities. Further consultations will be conducted with key stakeholders to identify any possible barriers and gender norms that the program can address. Another concern identified in the community is the early marriage for girls’ form 12-17 years old. Referring to Cluster to data by MCNA and our assessments we consider is critical and need to do intervention especially in Ninewa location of Sinjar (IDPs and Returnees) and Salah al-Din location of Shirqat (Returners). Where about 7% of this age group or at least per one family have marriage their child. Of course 99 % are refers to the early girl marriage

Protection Risk Analysis

The project not supported by the humanitarian organization might increase risk of violence and heighten of woman and girls. These targets can be forced to engage in negative basic needs like survival sex or forced marriage. Due to the consequences of GBV woman and girls might not consider unable and feel secured the referrals protection unit to resolve their problems. As lack of assistance and economic opportunities woman and girls offer herself to survival and take accessibility. Many IDPs woman and girl daylily are part of threaten of their physical saved security and ongoing harassment. For some case may be difficult accessing medical services that required appropriate medical treatment. Due to systematically sexual abused of woman and girls by ISIS during the war caused drama, caused of this target group loss of dignity and risk of relationship and also by their communities they feels shame, fear, discriminated and stigmatized. The less project time will required reintegration requires adequate follow-up care that cannot be addressed through one-time service provision. Culture behavior that existed between different communities might bring access prevention on the service of GBV response like children born by mother raped. Iraq is a men dominance society and the concern is that might be the less participation of females (woman and girls) in activities that will be conducted. Livelihoods options that are not controlled for families inside and outside the camps expose the IDPs to seriously risks especially to child labor, withdrawing children from school and also can increase selling their personal assets (girls might be pulled out of school to help the family at home when adults will be out working). Girls and woman might not be allowed and maybe be abused when they will more economic development options. During the projects implementation experiences of Al Mortaqa in many camps inform us that issues related to protection can happen due to heavy armed forces being present in the camps which are without major constrains. This can cause arbitrary arrest, pressure on humanitarian actors and harassment (including sexual harassment).
