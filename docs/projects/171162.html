Project Summary

The project aims to improve community access to adequate sanitation services as well as protecting community public health in Gaza City through extending sanitation pipelines and household connections, and promoting hygiene practices among 500 HHs.
The project will:
- Reduce the amount of contaminated wastewater disposed to open areas and aquifer
- Reduce the use of unreliable cesspits and septic tanks that have largely contaminated the coastal aquifer.
- Promote healthy hygiene practices amongst the local community to contain the transmission and exposure risk to COVID-19 in Gaza.
This 10-month project will reach its objectives through the following set of activities:
a) Extension of 4700 meters main sanitation UPVC gravity pipelines of diameters 200mm, 250mm and 300mm
b) Construction of 220 sewage household connections across four localities within Gaza City
c) Distribution of 500 hygiene kits to most vulnerable households in Gaza City, in coordination with WASH cluster
d) Dissemination of 3000 WASH related COVID-19 integrated awareness-raising materials in the targeted localities.
According to HA’s assessment, 31000 vulnerable individuals (8742 men, 8618 women, 6882 boys and 6758 girls, including 2108 persons with disabilities) in Gaza City will be targeted in this project from the following localities: Sheikh Ijlin, parts of Tal El Hawa, Zaitoun, and Tuffah (Daraj). These localities have been identified in close coordination with PWA, CMWU, and Gaza Municipality based on Cluster-agreed vulnerability criteria.
The project will have a tremendous positive impact at different levels. First, it will improve the quality of life of 220 of underserved households who have never had access to adequate sanitation in the past. The project targets highly populated under-served communities who have never had any sanitation services in the past 15 years. Second, it is also expected to put an end to the deteriorating environmental conditions and contamination of groundwater by stopping the frequent flooding of cesspits and septic tanks which are posing serious risks to the residents’ public health in the targeted areas.
The project has a strong exit strategy that will ensure long term sustainability where upon the completion of project activities, the project will be handed to the Municipality of Gaza and CMWU to sustain the network and ensure continuity of the sanitation service.
Proposed activities of this project are fully coordinated with key stakeholders including PWA, CMWU, MoLG, Municipality of Gaza, and WASH cluster. We will also coordinate closely with WASH Area Focal Point for Gaza and ensure info sharing/4W submission with the cluster.

1 - Needs

Nearly a quarter of Gaza residents live in areas without adequate sanitary sewage infrastructure, while existing infrastructure is in a state of disrepair as a result of shortage of the electricity supply, restrictions on the entry of the required materials including the spare parts, limited infrastructural operation and maintenance, and recent damage of water infrastructure. To cope with this, those who are not connected to adequate sanitation networks use unreliable cesspits and open drains to dispose of their wastewater.Cesspits and septic tanks in project area, especially in Tuffah (Daraj), Zaitoun and Sheikh Ijlin overflooding frequently. According to rapid assessment conducted in partnership with Gaza Municipality, nearly 45,000 vulnerable individuals (12690 men, 12510 women, 9990 boys and 9810 girls), including 3060 people with disability, are negatively affected by the lack of access to sanitation services. Children are more exposed to water-borne diseases as they spend most of their time playing in open areas and streets in these four localities. Raw sewage is still being disposed off into the environment through cesspits septic tanks that often get flooded without further treatment, which pose serious risks to the residents’ public health, especially among young children who play in the streets. This also causes contamination of the groundwater aquifer. Additionally, the residents living without proper access to sanitation have been suffering from bad odors and rodents, and have been having to spend substantial amounts of money on cesspit vacuuming. According to the Environment Quality Authority in Gaza, on August 19, 2020 around 63 per cent of Gaza’s beaches were contaminated due to the discharge of 117 million litres of raw or partially treated sewage into the Mediterranean.Human Appeal has conducted a field and rapid assessment in Sheikh Ijlin, Tal El Hawa, Zaitoun and Tuffah areas in close cooperation with Gaza Municipality, Ministry of Local Government and CMWU. We shockingly found out that more than %30 of residents of Gaza governorate are living without adequate access to sustainable sanitation services. Some families in Tuffah and Zaitoun reported having absolutely no access to any sanitation services in the past 15 years and they only rely on septic tanks. According to Municipality of Gaza, septic tanks in these localities are oversaturated which cause frequent flooding in open areas. Children are some of the most affected by wastewater flooding as they are seen playing in the streets and open areas all the time. Regarding Sheikh Ijlin community, Qatar Red Crescent is funding a similar sanitation project in this area and this proposed project targets some of the most vulnerable residents who remain under-served by interventions of the other actors in the community. Detailed maps of three out of four localities, within Gaza governorate, have been produced to demonstrate exactly where the project will take place. These maps can be accessed through this link: https://humanappealglobal-my.sharepoint.com/:f:/g/personal/azzam_saqqa_humanappeal_org_ps/EikVtVcCR9JJihk0pjiapRoB0QhOPwPqQBO9HkoLR9Bquw?e=aKPzUiThe nature of soil in Sheikh Ijlin is sandy which makes it quicker and much easier for wastewater to infiltrate into the aquifer. This imposes high risk on the aquifer, especially that 60% of the community relies on cesspits and septic tanks that keep frequently flooding into streets while residents of the community cannot afford the septic tank vacuuming cost due to the overall sharply deteriorating socio-economic situation in the Gaza Strip. Due to rapid urbanization in this community, cesspits and septic tanks are no longer sufficient to cope with the population growth in the community. This means that sewage is frequently flooded into streets and quickly infiltrates into aquifer due to characteristics of sand soil. This exposes residents of this community to serious public health risks, including but not limited to the possible outbreak of diseases and epidemics.According to the assessment, 30% of the community has been connected to sanitation networks as part of a $180,000 MDLF grant to Municipality of Gaza. Moreover, in October 2020, Qatar Red Crescent has funded a $300,000 USD project in cooperation with Gaza Municipality to extend sewer collection pipelines and household connections to almost 40% of the community. This proposed project will target some of the remaining parts of the residential areas of the community to ensure that more households in this community have adequate access to safe and sustainable sanitation services networks. The implementation of this activity in Sheikh Ijlin was not possible in the past given the topography of the land and the need to pump wastewater to reach the wastewater treatment plan. Now that Pumping Station No. 11 in the area is operating with dual electricity power lines which means it is working with increased capacity, the implementation of this activity has become possible and even a priority. This means that wastewater from this community will be pumped directly to the Sheikh Ijlin Wastewater Treatment Plant where 30,000m3 of wastewater will be treated in this facility. This wastewater facility can absorb this volume as an additional 30,000m3 of wastewater gets directly pumped from Gaza to Bureij biggest wastewater treatment plant.

2 - Activities

The project includes the following set of activities: a) Construction of 4700m of main sanitation gravity pipelines:This activity involves the construction of wastewater gravity system of high-quality durable sewer collection UPVC pipelines of different diameters ranging from 160mm to 300mm as well as the installations of concrete manholes, with different diameters and depths. This will be determined according to the topography and depth of the land. The length of the network will be 4,700 meters covering the following localities: • 950m in Tal El Hawa (map available)• 2230m in Zaitoun area, off street No. 25 and Samouni area (map available)• 850m in Sheikh Ijlin area (map available)• 670m in Tuffah (Daraj) areaSite Engineer and WASH specialists from Human Appeal, Gaza Municipality and CMWU will ensure a robust supervision during the design and implementation of the project. They will also guarantee the use of best durable materials. This will be demonstrated in the joint inspection committee approval prior to any delivery. This activity will reduce the negative impact on women, men, boys and girls from contact with polluted wastewater, and reduce the pollution of the environment and groundwater aquifer. Community feedback will be recorded and addressed during the implementation of the project to ensure the best interest of affected people is considered during project implementation. Detailed draft maps of these areas are available to visualize where exactly it will be implemented and to demonstrate complementarity with other actors in the same localities to avoid any duplication.b) Construction of 220 household sewage connections in Gaza CityThis activity aims to connect 220 HHs, from four localities across Gaza City, to the main sanitation network by installing manholes and sewer branches to connect these homes to permanent sanitation system. This includes the installation of households small to medium concrete manholes and UPVC gravity pipes to transport sewage to the main wastewater networks. Here is the distribution of household connections per locality: • 55 households in Tal El Hawa• 87 households in Zaitoun• 45 households in Sheikh Ijlin area • 33 households in Tuffah areaHuman Appeal will make sure to do no harm to any existing public or private infrastructure during the implementation of this project/activity through the coordination with MOG and other relevant authorities such as Telecommunication, electricity companies and traffic authority. If, god forbid, any accidents occur during digging or any other stage, we will make sure to fix everything and return everything as it was before the project. c) Distribution of 500 hygiene kits to most vulnerable households across Gaza in coordination with WASH ClusterAccess to essential hygiene products during an epidemic outbreak could mean life or death, especially among people with compromised immune systems. According to Ministry of Health, as of 28 October, 5,893 cases have been recorded in the Gaza Strip, including 651 cases in the project targeted areas (Tal El Hawa 73, Tuffah 340, Zaitoun 233 and Sheikh Ijlin 5). 3,000 individuals including 846 men, 834 women, 666 boys and 654 girls, from the 500 households across the four targeted localities in this project will be provided with a one-time household hygiene kit that is tailored to the context of COVID-19 outbreak. This kit will include hygiene products sufficient for a family of six members for at least one month. The design of the kits is tailored to the specific needs of women and men, boys and girls. The design of the hygiene kit is cross-checked with WASH Cluster Technical Guidance and matches the standardized hygiene kit content that was developed by the cluster. The hygiene kit will ensure that the basic needs of children, adults and women and girls are taken into consideration in the package. Hygiene kits will include the following items: • Hand sanitizer with alcohol solution of at least 70% alcohol• Soap bars, • Adult and baby shampoo• Antiseptic Liquid• Bleach • Disposable face masks• Disposable gloves • Tissues and wet wipes • Skin washing sponge/Shower loofah• Disinfection solution for cleaning shelter/house/toilets similar to Dettol (750 ml)• Detergent• dish washing sponge• toilet paper Each hygiene kit will include the instruction sheet in Arabic, in line with the WASH Cluster COVID-19 Technical Guidance. These kits will improve the health and hygiene standards of the targeted HHs.Beneficiary selection from the four localities will be based on COVID-19 vulnerability criteria agreed with the cluster and hygiene promotion working group. This could include vulnerable families who are home quarantined. d) Dissemination of WASH related COVID-19 integrated awareness-raising materials in Sheikh Ijlin communityWASH Cluster, UNICEF, WHO and other key actors in the WASH Cluster have produced valuable IEC materials to raise awareness on best hygiene practices in the light of COVID-19 outbreak. HA will use the already designed IEC materials in the WASH cluster and will get the technical inputs of the Hygiene Promotion Sub Working Group. Once the materials are approved and endorsed by everyone, we will produce 3,000 copies of the materials and disseminate them in public places, and share copies with every hygiene kit as well. We are expecting to reach at least 3,000 individuals. Human Appeal will partner with the Youth Empowerment Center (YEC) to carry out activities C &D. YEC is a not-for-profit, non-governmental, non-partisan, public and voluntary center, which was founded to lead youth work in Palestine (Gaza Strip). Furthermore, YEC has over a decade of progressive experience in designing and implementing successful and impactful hygiene promotion campaigns with multiple international partners. YEC is also an active member of the WASH Cluster, Hygiene Promotion and WASH in Health Working Groups.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Do No Harm analyses are always in the background of any assessment in order to ensure that our interventions do not have any unintended negative impact on anyone in the community. During the assessment all options have been explored to ensure that no disruption other services occur during the implementation of the project. For example, not to block roads or streets during the implementation of the project and if any road block is unavoidable to coordinate with local community on best alternatives. Personal safety and security of staff, community and all stakeholders involved is prioritized during all stages of the project. Human Appeal has the following set of policies and guidelines that are annually reviewed and updated and incorporated in our standard reporting formats: * Protection Policy and SOPs* Gender Policies and Gender Guidelines * Policy on elimination of gender-based violence in the workplace and in the community.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In principle, this project is being proposed in direct response to affected people's repeated complains of lack of access to adequate sanitation services and frequent flooding of cesspits and septic tanks. Human Appeal is committed to the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) and all staff have been equipped with knowledge and skills about adhering to the 9 commitments. Human Appeal believes that affected population are and should be the centre of all our work and that affected populations should be informed about projects and should be encouraged and even empowered to have meaningful participation in all aspects of project cycle starting from informing assessment, design, planning, implementation and through to monitoring and evaluation of projects and programmes that support them. Human Appeal has a policy and guidelines how to always improve our adherence to Core Humanitarian Standards. Human Appeal strives to Leave No One Behind in the projects by constantly improving our selection criteria, guidelines and ensuring that women, men, boys, and girls, with or without disability, old or young or of different forms of diversity has equal access to opportunities and support. Once the project starts, community members including, men, women, boys and girls will be invited to a town-hall meeting to discuss how the project will be implemented and listen to people's concerns and ideas to inform the delivery of the project.
