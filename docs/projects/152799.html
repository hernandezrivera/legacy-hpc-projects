Project Summary

At least 5.2 million people (IPC September, 2018) are estimated to be facing extreme food insecurity levels in January-March 2019, projected to significantly deteriorate further. This has been attributed to a protracted conflict with recent skirmishes and ceasefire violations in Western Bahr el Ghazal (OCHA, September 2018), further exacerbated by disrupted markets, poor access to seeds and tools, fall army worm invasion, reduced cereal harvests, negative terms of trade and a soaring inflation rate coupled with increasing bureaucratic impediments, livestock diseases and mortalities including Rift Valley Fever Outbreaks in Yirol (FAO, 2018 Updates).
PCO aims to work to establish a nexus between Humanitarian response and Resilience programming among vulnerable 7000 beneficiaries (2500 boys, 2500 girls, 1000 men and 1000 women) have improved food production to protect the erosion of their livelihoods.
These will be through implementation of crop, vegetable and fishing kits livelihood interventions and related trainings, prepositioned through FAO Cluster pipeline and animal health interventions – livestock treatment and vaccination campaigns coupled with relevant community animal health workers trainings.
PCO has been operational in Western Bahr el Ghazal, Lakes and Jonglei since 2006; with other ongoing programs. The organization has experienced technical teams and relevant office space, logistics, warehousing facilities and strong grass root networks that can be easily deployed, including staff redeployment to proposed areas for scale up interventions. The project will also ensure gender balance, accountability to affected populations, protection, HIV/AIDS control/ prevention, environmental conservation and youth engagements at all stages of implementation.

Needs

At least 5.2 million people (IPC September, 2018) are estimated to be facing extreme food insecurity levels in January-March 2019, projected to significantly deteriorate further. This has been attributed to a protracted conflict with recent skirmishes and ceasefire violations in Western Bahr el Ghazal (OCHA, September 2018), cattle raiding and inter-communal fighting in Lakes and Jonglei further exacerbated by disrupted markets, poor access to seeds and tools, fall army worm invasion, reduced cereal harvests, negative terms of trade and a soaring inflation rate coupled with increasing bureaucratic impediments, livestock diseases and mortalities including Rift Valley Fever Outbreaks in Yirol (FAO, 2018 Updates); low harvests, and increasing household expenditures on food (FSNMS, Round 2018). Increasing expenditure on food in market depended counties such as Wau, Rumbek despite reduced/ same monthly incomes have had an adverse impact on household purchasing power and consequent food insecurity (FSNMS, Round 20; HRP 2018). Crop production has been significantly disrupted by the conflict, compounded by fall army worm invasion with subsequent devastating effects on the crops (Fall Army Worm in Africa, FAO August 2017). These events have been happening despite the recent signed peace deal. Unprecedented food insecurity has been compounded by endemic livestock diseases and high mortalities with REACH 2018 Pibor County Food Security and Livelihoods Brief indicating over 10,000 cattle died in Gumuruk and its environs late 2017, cyclical waves of cattle raiding incidences in Jonglei and Lakes, RVF outbreak in Yirol East and with recent deadliest revenge attacks being reported in Boma (Boma Inter Agencies Rapid Assessment Report, July 2018) with subsequent displacement of more than 15,000 people (70 percent women and children), killings and abduction of 30 women and children (South Sudan Humanitarian Bulletin, Issue 5, May 2018). These events are further compounded by age-set fighting which continues to present a problem, with recent clashes reported between the Lango and Kurenen age-sets in Pibor. As needs are worsening, the there are increasing bureaucratic impediments with occasional fatal road ambushes being reported with looting of humanitarian aid and killings, further complicating aid deliveries. This is in addition to humanitarian workers facing increasing difficulty in accessing affected populations, with recently worse affected areas which suffered violent cattle raids such as Western Bahr el Ghazal among others, complicating efforts to reach the most vulnerable andcompounding existing needs. Peace Corps Organization (PCO), one of the leading national NGOs in the Country, proposes emergency livelihood support interventions involving crop, fishing and vegetable livelihood kits and animal health (treatment and vaccinations) which will play a vital role in provision of immediate, medium amd long term term access to food, incomes and protecting their livelihoods. The organization has competent and experienced technical teams and relevant office space in Pibor, logistics, warehousing facilities; and strong grass root networks that can be easily deployed, including staff redeployment to proposed areas for scale up interventions. The project will also ensure gender balance, accountability to affected populations, protection, advocacy against Gender Based Violence and Protection of vulnerable groups such as women/ girls against sexual exploitation/ abuse; ensure environmental conservation by promotion of environmentally friendly approaches; ensure participation of men, women, girls and boys in implementation.

Activities

1. Emergency food production: main season agriculture seeds & tool distribution; fish, vegetable & crop kits through prepositioning from FAO Cluster Pipeline and distribution of livelihood kits among 1,166 households. 2. Emergency food & livelihood support: fishing kits/ rapid response kits (fishing kits/ cow pea & three variety of fast maturing vegetable seed)3. Reducing pest and crop disease e.g. FAW surveillance, detection, prevention & eradication4. Livestock vaccination (40,000 livestock) ; disease prevention & treatment especially zoonotic e.g. RVF public health messaging targeting 1000 HHs 5. Training and equipping of 100 (60 male; 40 female) Community Animal Health Workers with assorted supplies and inputs. 6. Provision of solar powered cold chain facilities in the targeted locations – 2 solar fridges7. Capacity building and training in value chain addition, agronomic practices, post-harvest handling targeting 300 (200 male; 100 female) community resource persons.
