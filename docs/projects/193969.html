Project Summary

The purpose of this project is to improve access to safe water supply, sanitation and hygiene services and facilities for vulnerable populations, including IDPs, Returnees and Host Community in South Kordofan, Blue Nile, Khartoum, Central & West Darfur States in Sudan and reduce the spread of diseases by improving the communities access to hand washing facilities and supplies.
The project targets 529080 crisis-affected people (121090 women, 118500 men, 147200 girls, 142290 boys) , ( 106500 IDPS , 63800 Refugees , 37005 returnees and 321775 venerable resident ) in 9 localities of Central Darfur (Zalinge, Azum, Golo, Nertiti, Rokero, Bendesi, Mukjar, Wadi Salih & Umdukun), 7 localities in West Darfur (Genina, Kernik, Kulbus, Habila, Beida, Jabel-Moon & Sirba), 6 localities in Blue Nile (Kurmuk, Geissan, Bau, Wad Almahi, Tadamon & Damazine), 3 localities in Khartoum (Sharg Elneel , Um-bada , & Jebel Awlia) and 7 localities in South Kordofan (Abu Jubaiha, Abu kashola, Dilling, Gedir, Heiban, Tadamon, & Talodi).
The Interagency rapid needs assessment conducted in Central and West Darfur in May 2021 showed that continuous clashes between the armed forces of different tribes remains the main cause of displacement for more than 75,000 people and death of more than 200 innocent civilians in Krenik and Jebelmoon localities in West Darfur and displacement of many women and children from Jebel Marra areas in Central Darfur state. The displaced persons have lost all their valuables including residences, livestock and crops. The ethnic conflict in North and Western Ethiopia contributed to migration of around 15,500 refugees to Blue Nile. All the vulnerable people have poor access to clean water supply, sanitation and hygiene facilities. The inflation affected the purchasing power of poor families, accelerating the food insecurity and malnutrition levels increase the venerability of the affected people of the society - IDPs, refugees and hosting communities.
Firstly, this project aims to improve access to basic water services for crisis-affected populations. Under this objective, the interventions ensure drinking water from an improved source with a collection time of not more than 30 minutes roundtrip, including queuing. People targeted with basic water services will benefit from new installations and maintenance, operations, and rehabilitation of existing handpumps, water yards, protected wells, check-dams, and haffer.
Secondly so the project aims to ensure improved access to adequate, protected, and gender sensitive sanitation services for vulnerable populations. Interventions will ensure the installation, rehabilitation, and use of ventilated improved pit latrines (VIP), pit latrines with slabs. Household latrines (basic), latrines shared between two households (limited), communal latrines, CLTS as approached will be used where suitable to encourage the community to stop open deification, and to have their household latrine for family use.
Third the project will establish information dissemination system at the community level guided by trained community hygiene promotors, 1 promoter per 50 HHs to ensure effective awareness razing for behavior change. The CHPs will disseminate hygiene messages and monitor household hygiene practices. And, to ensure access to hygiene promotion services, water quality and environmental health interventions will be provided to the vulnerable populations. The messages will focus on handwashing with soap through household visits, campaigns, and media coverage. Half of the targeted population will be provided with soap and jerry cans. Handwashing facilities will reach people in communal setups. Women and girls who are part of the newly displaced population will receive personal dignity kits culturally appropriate menstrual hygiene supplies. 1 - Needs

Humanitarian needs in the two Darfur states (CD&WD), Blue Nile, South Kordofan and Khartoum state have grown over the past year due to the current economic crisis, as people who have already been receiving humanitarian assistance remain vulnerable due to the limited capacity to respond to and to recover from the crises. The Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) situation has deteriorated due to a worsening economic crisis, non-functional or aging WASH infrastructure, decreased revenue collections, poor budget allocation, and increased operation maintenance costs due to an increase in fuel prices in 2021. According to the HNO (2022), Sudan's protracted crisis continues to create water, hygiene, and sanitation needs for vulnerable people. The key drivers of WASH needs are the deepening economic crisis, conflicts, weak WASH services, poor knowledge, attitudes, and practices related to WASH, poor community governance of WASH infrastructure in rural areas, floods, climate change, inadequate and inconsistent availability of soap for personal hygiene, poor hygiene practices, open defecation due to absent and limited latrines, frequent breakdown of water supply infrastructure due to overuse, and erratic fuel supply. The data from the state MoH in Central Darfur indicates that diarrhea is still the major cause of mortality and morbidity of children, attributed mainly to poor access to clean water supply, hygiene, and sanitation. According to the HNO survey result (2023 ) 19% , 7% . 61% 12.5% of IDPs , Returnees , venerable resident are in need for safe water supply while 19% , 6%, 68 % and 7 % for improve sanitation which poses grave public health risks, including the transmission of water-borne diseases such as cholera, diarrhea, dysentery, hepatitis A and typhoid. There are also high WASH needs related to hygiene and water safety in our targeted areas. . Most of the schools and primary health care facilities have limited access to WASH facilities and services. Records showed 50% of the schools in the targeted localities have no gender segregated latrines which provide dignified service to boys and girls. Menstrual hygiene management is often not prioritized, negatively impacting girls' attendance and enrollment in schools. IDPs, returnees, and vulnerable residents will need continued WASH support. In many rural villages, these population groups walk over 30 mints to fetch water, and more during the dry season.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project takes the maximum care not to cause damage to the environment. For activities having an impact on the environment, the project design required mitigation measures, which will be implemented parallel to project activity implementation. The environmental markers guideline was reviewed to ensure that the project is designed in a manner that is sensitive to the environment, and that necessary mitigation measures were taken as per the guidelines. To ensure environmental compliance and adhere to no-do-harm principles, WR will provide training on integrated water resources management and create community awareness on the environment. The trained community members will further create awareness to the beneficiary communities on the efficient use of water resources and protection of the environment. Besides, the water management committees will be monitoring ground water for water projects supported by the project and WR, to avoid depletion of ground water and to take joint measures in enhancing the recharge of ground water. Emphasis has been given to installing solar energy instead of supporting diesel energy, because solar is environmentally friendly, cost effective, and efficient. In addition, the ferro cement screen household latrine is environmentally friendly technology which will ensure no trees are cut, rather that the technology will utilize sand mineral which is readily available in the area. The side slabs are thin and portable, and reusable in their lifetime as opposed to wooden superstructures. Additionally, this project will address the issue of environmental health by establishing solid waste management committees among the targeted villages. Furthermore, through FSL sector WR is supporting tree nurseries in the project proposed localities and the seedlings produced in the nurseries will be planted in the project operational areas.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

While providing WASH services and facilities, this project will consider girls' and women's security and safety. Their engagement will be critical while identifying water points and new water sources to save them travel too far to unsecured places to fetch water. Furthermore, this project will ensure that the communal and institutional latrines are gender-segregated, safe with latches on the doors, and easy to access in secure places. The project will link women and girls to income-generating women's groups to receive training on making sanitary pads and soap. Additionally, WRS will install and construct suitable facilities to suit the aged and disable population. WRS will uphold the five minimum commitments, including consulting or engaging the affected population in assessments, designs, implementation, monitoring, and inclusion of women and girls. Efforts will be made to strengthen feedback mechanisms and post distribution surveys.
