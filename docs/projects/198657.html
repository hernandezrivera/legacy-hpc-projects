Project Summary

The Project aims to provide immediate unconditional 12-month in kind and in Cash food assistance to the most vulnerable IDPs and local populations affected by crisis in the North-West and South-West regions. Access to some hard-to-reach communities is still challenging and risky, however, WFP has been able to penetrate some of these communities through its implementing partners to ensure that the vulnerable persons are identified and assisted. Beneficiary targeting is done through the WFP formal targeting process using the Household Economic Analysis which places the affected population, communities and their lived realities at the center of the decision-making at every stage of the programme cycle. The approach allows for better targeting of beneficiaries according to their degree of vulnerability and helps to provide precise information about the beneficiaries of actions undertaken, in terms of who benefits from what and when, and what effects the activities have had on the lives of households. Community leaders and/or members representing all different groups in the community together will actively be involved in identifying those eligible for assistance
In line with the results of the Comprehensive Emergency Food Security Assessment, and the Cadre Harmonise. WFP will provide food support to 320,000 targeted people affected by the crisis in the South West and North West regions. To save lives and protect the food security and nutrition status of these vulnerable people, WFP will provide immediate humanitarian food assistance to the targeted 320,000 IDPs and local vulnerable host populations (210,000 in the NW and 110,000 in the SW) in their various locations in urban, semi urban and rural communities. The modalities of assistance will be in-kind general food distributions and Multipurpose cash (MPC). Special attention will be paid to beneﬁciaries located in rural areas and diﬃcult to access settlements. The food basket and the daily ration are rations of 370 g rice, 120 g pulse, 40 g vegetable oil, 100 g super cereal and 5 g salt per person. To implement the Project, WFP will work through experienced Cooperating Partners that have a good mastery and knowledge of the culture and driving forces of the areas, for example; Caritas-Kumba, LUKMEF, COMINSUD , INTERSOS, CARITAS-Kumbo, SHUMAS and Plan International and AIRD as TPM partners.
During Project implementation, regular process monitoring of activities will be conducted during food distribution and cash out and monthly output data collected against performance indicators for reporting through WFP COMET system. During post distribution monitoring, emphasis will be on the role played by men, women, girls, and boys including decision-making on the use of the food assistance in the household. Nutrition, Gender and protection issues are taken into consideration at all the stages of the project elaboration, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. Gender and protection risk analysis will be carried out and the most vulnerable will be targeted, including female-headed households, pregnant and lactating women, persons with disabilities.
In line with WFP accountability to affected populations, targeted vulnerable IDPs and local populations will be closely involved in various stages of the project implementation including the targeting process, food distribution. They will be regularly informed on the project activities and plans. Through the complaints and feedback mechanisms they will freely express their views and concerns on the project implementation. In line with WFP accountability to affected populations, beneficiaries’ complaints are treated on a timely manner, and their views are considered for projects adjustment.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Since November 2016, the socio-political crisis in these regions has progressively translated into an arm confrontation. The escalation of tension and upsurge in hostilities between non-state armed groups (NSAG) and government defense and security forces have triggered humanitarian needs across the two regions, marked by high level of internal displacement. Assessments carried out by IOM reported that more than 1,300,000 people have been displaced in the North West and South West regions as of October 2021. The 2022 HRP report indicate 73k persons displaced from the NWSW to Nigeria as refugees, 400k returnees, and over 600k IDs within and out of the NWSW regions. The crisis continues to have a negative impact on livelihoods, trade, markets and access to land for the affected population. Farmers, small-scale ﬁshermen and their families ﬁnd themselves in an extremely vulnerable position due to the disruption of their activities. 8 out of 13 divisions in the NWSW regions remains in the phase if food insecurity with high prices of most staple food above average (Cadre Harmonise, October 2021). As far as the nutrition situation is concern, the overall GAM rate in the NWSW is at 6% average, with about 50% of HH practicing very poor IYCF practices and over 43% of children anaemic (the 2021 EFSA report). IDPs have had to resort to negative food-related coping strategies, these include; reducing the number of meals (to one meal a day) and /or the quantity of food consumed by adults in order to feed the children. Worrying trends include: • Up to 1.3 million people are food insecure of which 750,000 in North-West and 620,000 in South-West region (EFSA, January 2021); • Up to 57.5% in NW and 53.7% of households in SW have insuﬃcient food consumption (Cadre Harmonise, EFSA, October 2021); • Over 51.0% in NW and 70.2% in SW households apply potentially irreversible “crisis” or “emergency” coping strategies to cope with food insecurity and other basic needs (Cadre Harmonise, EFSA, October 2021); • Global acute malnutrition in SW and NW deteriorated to 7.1% and 5.1% respectively (Cadre Harmonise, EFSA, October 2021) compared to last results of January (EFSA 2021). The most vulnerable people in these regions highly need emergency humanitarian food assistance to save lives. The NWSW crisis that led to the displacement of thousands of people has created food insecurity as it is unsafe for people income generating activities such as farming and open shops or move for business. The food insecurity risks have impacted on those in desperate need for food thus leading to negative coping mechanisms. Assessments by humanitarian actors revealed that the high rate of displacement is due to to insecurity; armed clashes between NSAG and Government military forces. Also, there is high risks of enrolment in armed groups as a source of livelihood; increased kidnappings, killing, arbitrary arrest and disappearances. As far as gender and protection is concerned, the assessments indicated that sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) has been on the rise among other protection risks. Rape is being used as a weapon of war; younger girls are forced to engage into survival sex, (including with armed groups) leading to unwanted pregnancies and sexual and reproductive health problems, which need to be treated but are left unattended owing to ﬁnancial constraints. There are families separated and relatives killed, schools and school children are threatened, and civilians living in a „climate of terror‟ (HRP 2022). Freedom of movement is greatly restricted and becomes riskier by the day, and people who have ﬂed to the forests or bushes live precariously. Persons living with disabilities are subjected to targeted abusive treatment, torture, and killings by the conﬂicting parties in addition to the disproportionate impact of instability, sudden movements, and violence places on them (Protection Cluster Strategy, August 2021).
