Project Summary

This project aims to maintain and strengthen essential health services and systems for crisis affected populations particularly at the Ward level and rapidly respond to health emergencies by leveraging national capacities while progressively enabling local leadership and ownership. The goal is to gradually shift towards a more resilient approach in strengthening capacity of the health system while at the same time responding to acute health emergencies. This plans to achieve a sustainable transition away from the current substitutionary response and to strengthening systems and local ownership is consistent with the national health strategy which highlights the revitalization and strengthening of the disrupted health system as critical elements for access to better health care service delivery. In order to achieve the overall goal of maintaining essential health services and systems,WHO actions will contribute largely into three core areas:
1. Maintain access to essential health services via the one PHC per Ward government initiative
2. Establish a functional health information management system with the integration of platforms such as DHIS, EWARS/SORMAS, Health resource and partners mapping.
3. Facilitate local ownership by strengthening government leadership, coordination and accountability. Through this, the project will focus on maintaining and improving access and quality of health service delivery and strengthen health systems, strengthen health information management, including surveillance, prevention, and alert, strengthen national and partner capacity to promptly respond to and contain outbreaks and other health emergencies and ensuring effective local leadership, partner coordination and operations support in the States of Borno Yobe and Adamawa. It will focus on re-establishing functional, staffed and equipped health facilities to deliver health services to vulnerable people including host communities and returnees at ward level through a novel approach of “health services under one roof’’. WHO aims to support the availability of health workers, the establishment of a health financing mechanism and strengthening the HDN initiative with the overall objective to restore health systems and enable optimal delivery of health services across the BAY states. While making investments in strengthening the health system, WHO will also position as the provider of last resort in delivering life-saving essential health services in the event of new displacements and acute health emergencies. This will improve the quality of health data and foster peer-to-peer learning through effective monitoring & evaluation. State and partners technical and operational capacity for effective coordination and delivery of health services will be strengthened. The provision of essential health services target people in need (in IDP camps and host communities) is a core area of WHO’s support in health emergency response in NEN. This area essentially covers all service provision and support for outbreak response; In 2022, WHO will expand HTR package of services to include FP and NCD/mental health services, align reporting of health data with national HMIS platform with define indicators for MHPSS, GBV, and HTS. Advocate for utilization of strategic documents to guide service delivery. Community participation in planning and response to Public health emergencies will be supported and strengthened, support PHC services and advocate for implementation of rural incentive & minimum service package in line with governments policy to curb high attrition rate of health staff. Priority will be given to establish and strengthen health workforce and service delivery in current MHT (Mobile Health Teams) areas while strengthening local ownership and accountability through joint monitoring and supportive supervision.

1 - Needs

In the past 12 years, the north-east states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) have experienced unabating armed conflicts and terrorism leading to a humanitarian crisis situation which has displaced over two million people. WHO declared the humanitarian crisis in Northeast Nigeria a grade 3 emergency in August 2016 and leads the health sector response in collaboration with the State Ministries of Health; The conflict in NEN has led to a system-wide breakdown of infrastructure and public services across sectors. The operational environment is characterized by frequent security challenges, waves of new displacements, deepening food insecurity and malnutrition. There is also widespread protection concerns and human rights violations constricted humanitarian space and reduced access that hampers effective delivery of humanitarian assistance.Protection concerns are further heightened by the Borno State Government-led process of camp closures and relocations of IDPs in Maiduguri and environs. This process which started on the 19 November with the closure of Bakasi camp and in several locations where IDPs were returned or relocated, NSAGs attacked almost immediately after the returns took place, causing many of the newly returned IDPs to flee. This led to an outcry from the humanitarian community requesting the Government to adhere to principles and guidelines contained in the Kampala Convention and the Borno State Government Return Strategy. Overall, the potential impact of camp closures/returns/relocations on the welfare of IDPs is yet to be fully ascertained. WHO began its emergency operation in NEN in 2016. After a period of stabilization until November 2018, the situation deteriorated with new displacements and loss of access. A reduction in partners’ presence in all key Local Government Areas (LGAs) is part of the consequence despite increased risk of communicable diseases, GBV, mental health and SAM (Severe Acute Malnutrition) amidst disruption of health services and damaged health facilities. The closure of IDP (Internally Displaced Person) camps without a secured return to IDPs (Internal Displaced Persons)’ settlement areas of origin is likely to put further stress on host communities with unofficial IDP camps, especially in Maiduguri Metropolis and LGA (Local Government Areas) satellite towns. This can fuel anew ongoing needs.The above trends are further aggravated by the continued challenges and risks of COVID-19, cholera, measles and cVDPV2. Though over 3,039 cases of COVID-19 and 79 deaths have been reported in NEN, this doesn’t show the true picture of the situation due to challenges with laboratory testing, surveillance and access issues. Furthermore, it’s contributory humanitarian and economic impact imposes additional stresses on support systems in the BAY states and consequentially accentuates poverty, inequalities, and humanitarian needs across sectors. Improvement of global oil prices should benefit the Nigerian economy but do not trickle down to its population in need. In 2022, it is estimated that a total of approx. 5 Mio. people will need health care assistance. In the BAY states, current mortality rates appear to have worsened compared to records in same period last year. Based on samples of locations in each state,[1] in Borno,the crude mortality rate has risen from 0.22 deaths per 10,000 people per day in late 2020 to 0.31 deaths/10,000 people/day in late 2021. This is a 50% increase in one year. In Yobe, mortality rates almost doubled in all three sampled locations, from 0.17 deaths/10,000 people/day in 2020 to 0.32 deaths/10,000 people/day in 2021. In Adamawa, the rates doubled in both sampled locations, from 0.09 to 0.21. Mortality rates among newly-arrived IDPs is well above the emergency threshold for both crude mortality rate (2.39/10,000 persons/day, vs. threshold of 1.00) and under-5 mortality rate (3.58/10,000 children under-5/day, vs. threshold of 2.00).

2 - Activities

Following the grading of the NEN emergency and subsequent joint operation reviews, WHO’s support has increasingly transited from substitutive operations towards collaborations in achieving collective outcomes across the humanitarian development and peace nexus. In 2022, WHO will engage more in health system strengthening activities in LGAs where the security situation is stable. In areas with more IDPs presence, the humanitarian health response will continue and will incorporate actions to promote resilience of the system and accountability to the affected people. WHO will enhance efforts towards health system strengthening (HSS) for more durable solutions and effective utilization of resources, with dual purpose for ensuring resources for outbreak response. The priorities will be a mix of humanitarian and HSS needs to ensure access to quality health services sustainably with a focus on essential services at Primary Health Care at ward level. WHO will support the states in enhancing surveillance for VPDs (Vaccine Preventable Disease) within the IDSR Framework as well as monitoring adverse Event Following Immunization (AEFI). WHO’s role in providing leadership in HIM will involve supporting the transition from Public Health Information System for emergencies towards Health Information Management.this will involve complementing EWARS/IDSR with the State-led SORMAS (Surveillance Outbreak Response Management Analysis System) and strengthen MoH capacity for better monitoring and reporting of epidemic prone diseases.To prepare for outbreaks, WHO will support the SMoHs in developing response plans for epidemic-prone diseases and preposition medical supplies and commodities for case management and case-based surveillance. WHO will further train SMoH and partner HCWs in case management, IPC and mental health and psychosocial support in respond to an outbreak; WHO will also support the deployment of teams in areas of critical need as a provider of last resort to respond to outbreaks and provide supportive supervision for those teams, including for outbreaks of cVDPV2. WHO will support the SMoH in improving capacity of laboratory staff for diagnosis diseases, State and LGA Laboratories will be upgraded for diagnosis of selected priority epidemic prone diseases. WHO will support strengthening the technical and operational capacity of State-level actors and partners with a plan to establish an effective coordination by deploying assistant Health cluster coordinators in Yobe and Adamawa state. The project will expand HTR package of services to include FP and NCD/mental health services, align reporting of health data with national HMIS platform with define indicators for MHPSS, GBV, and HTS. Advocate for utilization of strategic documents to guide service delivery.WHO will strengthen local capacities to effectively communicate health risk and to address demand-side barriers to health service utilization. Vulnerable population groups will be proactively reached with risk communication tailored to specific needs of target beneficiaries.WHO will foster the implementation of IPC measures in health facilities at State and LGA levels. This essentially will involve strengthening capacity of SMoH and partners on Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) to respond to epidemic prone diseases. IPC/WASH supplies will be made available across the states to reduce infection transmission.
