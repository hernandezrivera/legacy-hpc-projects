Project Summary

Based on the analysis of an assessment carried out by H4BFin line with the Shelter Cluster guidance in August 2022, it was concluded that over 2000 HHs within Bui, Donga Mantung and Mezam need life S/NFIs (lifesaving shelter and vital household items). It is based on this analysis that H4BF will distribute S/NFI to 2000 vulnerable IDPs, non-displaced, and returnees (800 IDPs, 200 Returnees, and 1000 HH in host communities. The distribution targets 60% women and girls, 30% men, and 10% persons with disabilities) in Mezam, Bui, and Donga Mantung Division, Northwest region Cameroon. For each division, the targeted beneficiaries are as follows; Mezam: 400, Bui: 800, and 800 Donga Mantung. The modality for distribution will be in-kind and Cash transfer. This will be conducted via MTN Mobile Money by locally hired and trained agents and enumerators with the support of local authorities and community members. Prior to the distribution of the items, affected communities will be assessed to verify the level of S/NFI. Persons Living in Disabilities, Elderly persons, individuals with no community links, caregivers of orphaned children, child-headed HHs, women-headed HHs, and severely sick persons will be engaged in order to obtain specific S/NFIs needs facing them. A complaint mechanism will be put in place from the onset to ensure beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries can submit complaints and receive feedback on time. Information on CFM will be provided during the entire process of implementation. Due to displacement, IDPs and returnees returning home are finding their land or property occupied by those left behind. In some instances, the IDPs and the Returnees may require to be apportioned community land where they can settle. This if not handled well may trigger tension or conflict. H4BF will work closely with traditional representatives, landlords and other partners implementing shelter interventions in target locations, and Local landlords and authorities to address land and property issues that may arise during the intervention. To ensure adequate attention on HLP matters, H4BF will utilize IOM Housing, Land, and Property due diligence standard as a guide during the implementation process mitigate the risk that the construction of shelter contributes to increasing tensions and conflicts around land. The intervention is integrated and will seek to link activities and beneficiaries to nutrition, health, education, protection, FSL and WASH. To avoid duplication of activities, Project staffs will participate in S/NFIs meetings at the county, state and National level for information sharing. For durable solutions, community-based S/NFIs committees will be trained and supported on effective, sustainable, and equitable utilization of local resources to ensure community-based shelter solutions. Women and girls will receive training on alternative ways of cooking (energy-saving stoves) which include the distribution of solar lamps and green stoves to reduce the consumption of fuel products like kerosene lamps. This will reduce carbon emission and use of wood as fuel (lower cutting of trees). This action will also reduce the exposure of women and girls to protection risks like rape and sexual abuse as they will not have to travel over several miles into the forest to fetch wood. H4BF will conduct PDM using the Cluster’s PDM tool and Activities of this project will be reported monthly with use of 5W to the North West sub cluster and other concerned clusters, this will contribute to monthly sirep. Furthermore, H4BF has put in place measures to strengthen the collection, management and analysis of information to inform and adjust early warning and response. Also, it is anticipated that target locations will be accessible by road and air in order to deliver S/NFIs supplies on time to affected persons

1 - Besoins / Needs

1,813,757people need shelter and essential household items. Within the Northwest and Southwest regions, 885,211 people are in need as violations of human rights continue with the intentional burning of homes, overcrowded hosting shelters, and people living in inadequate makeshift shelters in the bush. (HNO Draft,2022). Key drivers of S/NFIs needs include latest, general and localized conflict, disruption of supply routes and markets, macroeconomic instability (hyperinflation), food insecurity that is leading families to spend the little they have on food than buying shelter or non-food items and time poverty. Some of the places that are likely to experience serious S/NFIs needs in 2023 include Mezam, Bui and Donga Mantung Division in the north west region of Cameroon. Other factors include inflow of displaced persons from IDPs host communities to their home of origin. The IDPs and Returnees are coming home to find shelters they left behind destroyed, looted or burnt as a result of conflict. Others find their original home occupied and have no means of generating income to establish new shelters. As a result, they are forced to sleep under trees, occupy abandoned structures and institutions, or share living and sleeping spaces with host community or relatives. In such circumstances, the affected individuals live in inhumane conditions, are exposed to elements of weather and protection risks. Women and girls sharing sleeping spaces with male counterparts or sleeping outside lack privacy and at risk of exposure to sexual gender-based violence. IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities in these divisions are currently exposed to severe flooding. Also, as a result of the flooding, malaria has increased due to elevated levels of mosquito breeding, lack of mosquito nets, misuse of mosquito nets (for fishing) as households which have depleted their food stock seek alternative sources of food and livelihood. Individuals or families facing crisis (IPC3) and emergency (IPC4) are most likely to utilize the little resources or money they have to buy food instead of shelter materials or other household items such as mosquito nets, cooking sets or clothes. Others are likely to sell the little items they have or exchange them for food. As a result of fighting, markets, supply routes, and livelihoods got disrupted. Vendors vacated insecure areas while transporters removed their vehicles from the road for fear of being attacked. Job opportunities shrunk as private, public and non-government organizations stopped or scaled down their operations. The cumulative impact such disruption has resulted in reduced production, collapse of markets, unemployed population that rely on aid and reducing purchasing power among populations. This would mean that essential shelter and non-food items are not found in rural markets, are too expensive or affected communities who rely on aid have no means of buying the items.
