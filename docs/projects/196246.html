Project Summary

YEDA will target 26,007 individuals with cash and in-kind modalities in Magwi County (6,581 ind), EES, Pibor County (4,362 ind), GPAA, Mundri West (703 ind), Yambio (3,479 ind) and Tambura Counties (10,881 ind), WES. Such response will focus mainly on the victims of conflict, while it will target people affected by flood in Pibor County.
The choice of modality is informed by YEDA’s previous experience, and the need to scale-up CVA as an avenue to recovery and resilience. All interventions will be carried out following Covid-19 preventive measures; social distancing, use of PPE and preventive messaging through IEC. YEDA will maintain static presence in Mundri West, Pibor and Yambio Centers, which will also allow delivering assistance to the targeted Counties via road. Magwi County will be targeted with mobile response.
Beneficiary targeting will be guided by the SNFI Cluster criteria of multiple vulnerabilities; PLWs, children <5, the elderly, PWN/PWDs (30%), high number of dependents, combined with multiple displacement, lack of ties with the local community/purchasing power, GBV and COVID-19 risks associated with SNFI needs.
The in-kind response will improve access to essential S/NFIs to 5,460 individuals (21% standard NFI kits, 25% light NFI kits, 54% NFI replacement) and to appropriate and safe shelter to 6,502 individuals (10% standard ES kits, 12% light ES kits, 78% shelter reconstruction and upgrade). Additionally, 8,842 beneficiaries in Pibor County will receive flood kits. For in-kind response, pipeline items will be requested upon approval of SNFI analysis report by the SNFI Cluster coordinators, in areas without access to local market/shelter material. The transportation from Juba to the field warehouse will be covered by the SNFI Cluster core pipeline, while YEDA will cover the last mile transportation costs.
The CVA will include the establishment of a multi-purpose cash fund for procurement of shelter material (poles/grass/tools) and NFIs from the local market, allowing 838 vulnerable families (5,028 individuals, 62% shelter upgrade, 38% NFI replacement) to repair their homes, and ensuring appropriate shelter, protection and dignity. Since in remote areas framing material is not accessible due to protection concerns, the project will include a small CFW component targeting 180 individuals for training in shelter construction by the YEDA local shelter expert, with secondary benefits for host communities (financial empowerment and conflict risk reduction). Wherever possible, shelter materials will be procured locally, using the same system. HLP and CCCM partners on mobile response will be engaged to minimize conflicts over land and shelters, as well as using an integrated response for camp-like settings in order to benefit groups of IDPs, following the HLP due diligence guidelines. Financial service providers will be mapped out in the project sites, and distribution facilitated by YEDA staff. Female heads of household and PWDs will be prioritized, to ensure financial empowerment of intended people and GBV risk reduction.
To measure the project success and take evidence-based decision on the course of action, continuous monitoring and end evaluation (third party monitoring) will be conducted jointly with community members/local leaders as part of a Response, Feedback and Complaint Mechanism.
Coordination with the SNFI cluster every two weeks, and with other humanitarian partners utilizing humanitarian hubs in Mundri, Pibor, Yambio and Magwi in order to take part to joint responses, will minimize duplication, and promote integrated response, gender and protection mainstreaming, GBV referrals, risk mitigation, and incorporation of HIV/AID and COVID-19 prevention messages. As WES SFP and Greater Mundri SiFP, YEDA will lead humanitarian interventions through the cluster methodology, lead humanitarian coordination and refer need/gaps to ICWG.

1 - Needs

South Sudan is among the top 10 world most vulnerable countries to the impacts of climate change, including droughts and flooding, which have indirect negative effects on peace, security and humanitarian aid delivery (International Humanitarian Summit, 2022).According to the HNO report 2022, a total of 8.9 million women, men, girls and boys are expected to be in need in 2022 including 328,610 refugees across all of South Sudan’s 78 counties. The need analysis at Country level conducted in 2021, revealed that three quarters of the South Sudanese people are in need of humanitarian assistance, 53% of which are children, 24% women, and 23% men. Mobility continues to serve as a critical coping mechanism and source of resilience for population grappling with multiple shocks related to conflict, natural disaster (floods) and diseases (HNO, 2022). IDPs, returnees and 5% host communities are the people who will need emergency shelter and NFI support in 2022 (HNO 2022). In 2022, approximately 2.4 million people in South Sudan continue to be impacted by the destruction of housing and loss of essential goods caused by ongoing flooding and conflict. In 23 counties, over 50% of the affected people requires emergency shelter support, particularly in areas with the highest concentration of displaced people, such as Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA). More so, about 875,012 IDPs returned to their area of origin after the signing of the peace agreement, and are unable to reach their property due to lack of resources for transport, destroyed infrastructures and properties (UN OCHA).As September 2022, extensive flooding, high levels of food insecurity, violence and disease outbreak continued to impact people across the country, including destruction of homes, infrastructures, health and education facilities, and livelihood sources. As the rain continues, the number of affected people is expected to increase, with Western Equatoria being one of the most affected (OCHA humanitarian snapshot). Affected people in CES, WES and GPAA urgently need access to emergency shelter and NFI materials to prevent further deterioration of their living conditions and of resilience against recurring hazards. For those living outside IDP camps, especially in areas of CES and WES, there are few natural resources available, depleted by natural and man-made disasters and often inaccessible to most vulnerable categories.Continued challenges related to transportation of materials and limited support for local markets in Western and Central Equatoria have left even areas with functional markets lacking essential shelter materials and NFIs, particularly challenging to those who have experienced multiple displacement.In GPAA, persistent flooding, endemic insecurity (involving the Dinka, Nuer, and Murle communities) and unstable markets have created a state of emergency. This contributed to the atypical corralling of communities into smaller spaces, further disrupting livelihoods, and putting greater stress on available resources (REACH situation overview, September 2022). Shelter remains one of the priority needs, as majority of IDPs and returnees are sleeping under the trees.At-risk groups include Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW), children under 5, the elderly and Persons with Special Needs/With Disabilities (PSN/PWDs), due to their higher vulnerability to environmental hazards related to lack of access to essential SNFIs, inability to collect shelter material, and higher vulnerabilities to protection and GBV risks, especially for women and girls. This justifies YEDA’s targeting criteria of most vulnerable households, considering such groups who cannot afford to access shelter materials and cannot afford to pay community members to collect it (labour rates are average $8/day for unskilled labour and $10 for skilled). Finally, findings from various FGDs revealed that the extent of damage on S/NFI differs, which will guide the cash voucher value.

2 - Project activities

YEDA will use cash and in-kind modalities in order to target the most vulnerable 26,007 individuals in WES, EES and GPAA with ES/NFI assistance. For in-kind assistance, activities will include;(1) S/NFI analysis, including protection risk analysis-involving at least 2 S/NFI partners, one GBV partner, and one health partner -and conduction of community consultations (total duration 2 weeks) and sensitization on disaster risk reduction. The analysis will also include analysis of the existing land rights systems in place and risk of conflict over HLP issues, through discussion with local authorities, host community leaders, HLP and S/NFI partners. (2) Community consultations and establishment of CFRM (3) Formation and training of Village Representation Committees (VRCs, including women/youth/PWD representatives, IDP and host community leaders, and sub-chiefs; 50% female). Training will be on their role and responsibilities during activity implementation, including beneficiary selection, distribution, CFRM, and mediation in cases of disputes, especially over land and property, using the appropriate channels.(4) Beneficiary registration and verification, including CFRM. (5) Request and transportation of loose items/kits from S/NFI cluster core pipeline (6) Local procurement of framing material (wooden poles, bamboo bundles) and roofing material (grass), where deemed the right approach(7) Secondary transportation and distribution of pipeline items and shelter material** (8) M&E and reporting: Rapid monitoring at the time of distribution, in order to record feedback and complaints from the beneficiaries concerning the distribution modalities and type/quality of items received, and PDM 4 weeks after distribution.***(9) Documentation and reporting to the cluster.For the cash modality, activities will include; (1) Community consultation including protection risk and context analysis, establishment of a CFRM and needs/market analysis, including Covid-19 preventive measures* and sensitization on disaster risk reduction.(2) Beneficiary identification (3) Market/Price monitoring – to update the prices due to currency fluctuation (4) Mapping of service providers for Cash-Based Intervention (cash voucher modality) (5) Community sensitization on cash modalities (vouchers, CFW) supported by community selected volunteers on source of funding, intended purpose, transfer value, CFMs(6) Provision of cash assistance (vouchers) through the selected service provider and then YEDA staff**(7) Training in shelter construction(8) Construction of transitional shelters (mixed modality) and provision of cash under the CFW modality (9) M&E; Rapid monitoring after cash distribution, M&E (after 1st installment) by the M&E staff through household visits and FGDs with beneficiaries and later after a month (final installments) a post distribution monitoring conducted to measure impact and effectiveness***(10) Documentation and reporting to the cluster (YEDA M&E data flow chart), exit activities (case studies, photos, documentaries). NOTES:* At all stages, the VRC members, volunteers and YEDA staff will all be provided with PPE (face masks) and IEC materials in local languages will be used for sensitization in all areas. Gathering of people will be limited to the essential and any activity that involves that will be conducted following the WHO prevention guidelines for Covid-19, including social distancing and provision of PPE for each head of household, as well as hand-washing stations**Each distribution will incorporate beneficiary sensitization on disaster risk reduction and Covid-19 prevention, as well asl PSEA, in collaboration with GBV/protection and health partners in the project locations.***M&E: YEDA may use third-party monitoring/evaluation, by involving a cluster partner, as well as an external consultant to measure effectiveness, efficiency and impact of the intervention, ensuring transparency and quality reporting

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Market functionality will be considered at all stages of the project cycle: preparedness, planning, design and implementation set-up, Distribution cycle and monitoring, exit and feedback.YEDA has put in place a contingency plan in order to be able to respond rapidly with targeted CVA in cases of crisis, since this kind of response can be implemented quickly on a large scale. Strategic placement of YEDA’s field offices in Yambio, Mundri and Pibor, as well as the preparation of guidelines and templates, and staff training on them, has been taken on during the preparedness phase of this project. The YEDA’s preparedness plan and guidelines are informed by its participation to the national Cash Working Group and the SNFI cluster working group, which together with information collected from field partners, allow us to monitor market functionality in different rural areas.At the planning phase, need and gap analysis will be complemented by a market analysis in all locations, to establish the best way of implementing CVA, if applicable. The response analysis report will inform the course of action by providing information on; beneficiary needs and preferences for assistance delivery, community and political acceptance of CVA, market conditions and operational conditions, including beneficiary access to the market, and supplier’s ability to restock and willingness to take part to the project with full awareness. Design and implementation set-up will be informed by the response analysis report, which inherently consider market functionality, including all actors involved in the market system where the CVA will be implemented, obstacles to access, protection risks and risk of harm to the local economy by introducing CVA, and related mitigation measures. Community consultations, including representatives of all vulnerable groups, are key at this stage to assure the best outcome of this intervention. Also at this stage, contracted partners and suppliers, such as financial service providers, will be required to establish all relevant operational processes and support mechanisms necessary for successful cash or voucher distribution, in order to ensure timely and efficient delivery. YEDA’s prior experience has shown that it is key that vendors and supplier undergo a creful vetting process and sign a MoU to ensure ability and commitment to provide quality items in a timely manner and at an agreed maximum cost. In this view, a risk analysis must also consider factors that might affect the cash transfer, for instance insecurity on the road and inflation which can cause the cost of items to increase or the exchange rate to be unfavourable. At the stage of distribution, rapid monitoring, monitoring of market prices and the established CFRM will allow a smooth distribution of cash or voucher transfers to beneficiaries and their subsequent exchange/utilisation by beneficiaries to purchase commodities and services. Due to this monitoring mechanisms, it will also be possible to make adjustments to the programme design and implementation processes if deemed necessary and appropriate, after consultation with the SNFI cluster coordinators and UN-OCHA.Finally, through the exit strategy developed during programme planning, YEDA will determine if the project will be handed over to local stakeholders, or formally closed. The exit strategy and outcome of the project will be communicated to the beneficiaries and target communities, and will involve restitution and closure of documentation and related contracts. Success stories and lessons learnt will be shared with all stakeholders, donors and cluster coordinators and will inform future programming.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

In South Sudan, CVA has been proved to be an appropriate form of assistance, because the following preconditions are met: cash is a familiar form of exchange for commodities and services within the community, and it is used to meet SNFI needs in areas with functional markets, and when accompanied by a careful planning informed by context analysis, cash assistance does not increase the level of risk or vulnerability faced by any recipient.For this specific project, CVA will be used in combination with in-kind assistance in areas where markets are functional, there are low risk of conflict and all target beneficiaries have access to the market. The latter includes absence of economic, social, cultural and physical barriers to access. In such areas, CVA can revitalize the local market, creating a multiplier effect that benefits the whole community, and can help restore dignity and coping abilities. It can also ensure more flexibility and rapidity in terms of aid delivery, and can often ensure the provision of higher quality items (for instance local shelter material) compared to the SNFI pipeline items.Evidence shows that in many contexts, cash transfers are more cost-efficient to deliver than in-kind aid as they require fewer staff and lower logistical costs. Additionally, multipurpose cash will give the maximum amount of flexibility to meet the beneficiary needs, as well as choice at a low cost – ensuring value for money. This has been proven by YEDA’s experience in implementing CVA in Mundri East, Mundri West, Yei and Nagero County, through a combination of Cash-for-Work for construction of shelters for vulnerable individuals by community members and cash vouchers for purchase of locally-procured shelter material. All these interventions empowered people by increasing their purchasing power, increasing their resilience to shocks, rehabilitating community assets such as access roads to the market, and providing them with essential skills (e.g. training in shelter construction) to be employable in the future, either by NGOs or within the community. Finally, careful project and market monitoring, also through the Complaint, Feedback and Response Mechanisms embedded in this project, will allow the YEDA team to ensure that the grants will be directed and used by the intended beneficiaries (e.g. selecting women as head of households/direct beneficiaries for cash delivery), that suppliers are able to meet the supply demands and to stock quality items in time.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Community consultations will be conducted at inception phase of the project, as integral part of the multi-sector analysis in the targeted project locations. They will not only include the community leaders, but also Focus Group Discussions with representatives of the vulnerable groups (women, youth, elderly and disable persons), in order to make sure that views, challenges and needs of the vulnerable groups are addressed. Also, in each distribution location YEDA will form a Village Representation Committee, including representatives of women/youth/PWD and other vulnerable groups, IDP and host community leaders, and sub-chiefs (50% female). The VRCs provide great insight for activity planning and project design, Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM), as well as context-specific information, changes, and early warning in case of conflict.The VRCs will work with YEDA in order to identification the most vulnerable households to be targeted by in-kind and/or cash-based interventions, based on the shared vulnerability criteria. The composition of the VRCs will ensure that each vulnerable and minority group is taken under consideration. The VRC will also be responsible for ensuring that IDPs, returnees and host communities are aware of the priorities for the selection of beneficiaries for distribution, and how vulnerable households have been verified. They will also assist the YEDA team in the distribution of emergency shelter material and NFIs (including a complaint desk), they will report to the YEDA staff on a regular basis about in case of any feedback and complaint about the intervention modalities (both in-kind and CVA), in order for the project team to be able to make due changes in the project activities, if need be. Finally, the VRCs will assist the YEDA team by mediating in case of discontent among the population in regards to the prioritization of the beneficiaries, quality of items, and any other complaint raised.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Addressing inequality and protection threats is key in this project. YEDA has trained and continuously reminds its staff on PSEA, including GBV referral pathways to ensure GBV risks are prevented and/or mitigated, and it is already in contact with the GBV FPs for any referrals. Under this project, any new staff, volunteers and local partners will be trained in gender integration and protection mainstreaming to better enable them apply a gender and protection lens (do no harm principle) throughout program activities. This also includes a zero tolerance to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA). Criteria for any activity implementation will consider 3 key components; gender appropriateness, safety and accessibility by all groups. Appropriate, safe and accessible solutions, including sites and schedules for distribution will be agreed upon in consultation with the elderly, PLW and PSN/PWDs, who normally face difficulties in accessing them due to distance and SGBV risks, and will be supported by community volunteers for shelter construction and transportation of items to their places.The selection criteria for the beneficiaries will prioritize gender and protections concerns to ensure persons with disability, pregnant and lactating women, female heads of households, and the elderly among other groups with protection concerns receive the assistance needed. Beneficiaries, as well as the community as a whole, will be encouraged to report any suspected behaviour/acts of SGBV in their community by humanitarian staff/volunteers, using the confidential CFRM that will be put in place, as well as the different channels (GBV/Protection referral pathways, or PSEA taskforce) for remedial. In order to prevent SEA, YEDA will mostly target female-headed households (widows, single mothers), providing items which are desperately needed to reduce the risk of exchanging sex. During the distributions, the beneficiaries will use separate queues by gender, in order to avoid harassment towards women, while the YEDA volunteers will handpick the most vulnerable people and assist them in receiving and transporting their items. YEDA will also work to ensure that the contents of aid are sensitive to any protection risks, monitored through community assessments, rapid monitoring and PDMs. YEDA is already in contact with the GBV FPs in WES, EES and GPAA, and referral pathways will be used to ensure PSEA risks are prevented and mitigated, and will collaborate with GBV/protection partners for joint activity implementation. A GBV/PSEA risk analysis will be undertaken on water, sanitation, hygiene, and ES/NFI item distributions to make sure that no harm is done and that a minimum of S/NFI and Protection sphere standards are implemented. This will also allow YEDA to take into account all gender perspectives, ensuring protection of all community members and mitigating risks that the project activities might pose to specific age and gender groups. All activities will include community sensitization on GBV and creation of Village Representation Committees (VRCs), with the aim to reduce dangerous behaviour and find common strategies to reduce inequalities and protection threats within the community.Finally, YEDA staff will ensure to inform beneficiaries that the assistance is free of any cost, and confidential complaints, feedback and protection mechanisms are in place to report any violation. Sensitization on GBV, in relation to creating a safe environment for women and girls, will be done with protection partners using gender-tailored messages.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The establishment of a Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) as an essential component of this project, through the establishment of complaint desks at every distribution center and of the Village Representation Committees will allow to adjust the interventions in the course of action. Such system will see key stakeholder act as a bridge between YEDA and the beneficiaries, in order to inform the organization about the best modalities for aid delivery, as well as issues, concerns and obstacles to the current intervention. For YEDA it is paramount to ensure effectiveness and efficiency in the aid delivery, and ensure that the interventions do no cause any harm to the local economy, social relations and fabric, and beneficiary wellbeing. To this effect, rapid monitoring will also allow to determine if the beneficiaries perceive the aid receive to be adequate, catering their main needs and ensuring that all communications are done appropriately, for instance using appropriate IEC, language and communication channels to reach a given aim. Such aim could include explaining purpose of response, targeting criteria and NGO capacity in order to manage expectation, enforce behavioural change for COVID-19 prevention, effective training in shelter construction, sensitization on the use of cash vouchers, NFI and shelter material distributed, explanation on the scope of the response and GBV referral pathways among others. Continuous project monitoring through the collaboration of the VRCs will allow us to determine if the modalities used are appropriate or need to be adjusted to that specific context or situation.Finally, the CFW and other interventions wherever possible will be conducted in two different phases, to enable close monitoring of the response and its appropriateness, and make adjustments where necessary. Rapid monitoring will be conducted after the first distribution, and PDM within 4 weeks, before the second instalment, to determine if the amount and delivery modality was appropriate, as well as the engagement of suppliers and ability to restock key items, if access to the market was maintained and protection risks minimized, and make adjustments if needed. The CFW beneficiaries will receive a first instalment after completion of a portion of their work in a satisfactory manner, or the successful completion of the shelter construction training, so that they will be encouraged to continue in order to receive the second half of the payment. This will also enable YEDA and the beneficiaries themselves to adjust any approach which was not working.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

To minimize the beneficiaries’ risk of being exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services, all activity locations and other key activity details will be agreed upon the members of the Village Representation Committees. For instance, the distribution locations, time and days will have to be favourable for all vulnerable categories to minimize their protection risks on the way. The VRCs include representatives of vulnerable categories, which will be able to advise appropriately on the matter, and local authorities that will negotiate access and ensure that no armed actors are present at the distribution site. The long-standing positive relationship of YEDA with the local authorities will also be helpful to this end. Additionally, there is often a risk of interference from local leaders and sometimes authorities in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries. This will be mitigated by leveraging the long-lasting positive relationship that YEDA has maintained with the local authorities during the years, sensitizing the local population on the need to maintain certain prioritization criteria for assistance and on the risk of conflict over resources and negative effects on social cohesion, and by creating project ownership by involving beneficiaries and affected communities at every stage of the project implementation (as local staff, volunteers, trainees, community representatives, etc.). Additionally, data triangulation and third-party monitoring, coupled with a carefully implemented CFRM, will guarantee to always target the intended beneficiaries of the project.Finally, the risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity in targeted project area will be mitigated by analysing the context beforehand, identifying land ownership and local mechanisms for settling land disputes, as well as previous incidents of conflict and tension over land. All the relevant local authorities and local stakeholders will be involved from the onset to handle eventual cases of disputes (e.g. returnees finding their land and/or shelters occupied) and communities will be sensitized on the matter. Additionally, in case of shelter material distribution and shelter construction, local authorities, HLP and CCCM partners on mobile response will be engaged to minimize conflicts over land and shelters, as well as using an integrated response for camp-like settings in order to benefit groups of IDPs, following the HLP due diligence guidelines.
