Project Summary

Through this project, WHO primarily aims to: Strengthen lifesaving preventive nutrition services for vulnerable population groups focusing on appropriate infant and young child feeding practices in emergency, micronutrient interventions and optimal maternal nutrition. (Lifesaving, protection and basic services).
Improve equitable access to quality lifesaving curative nutrition services through systematic identification, referral and treatment of acutely malnourished cases for boys and girls under five (Lifesaving and basic services/Resilience).
Background:
In GoS areas, WHO supports nutrition surveillance in 941 Heath centers across the country complementing ongoing efforts by UNICEF and its partners to detect malnutrition cases in community. In 2022, 2.4% of screened children in nutrition surveillance program were in acute malnutrition (22,792 patients); higher rates were noted in Daraa, Aleppo, Deir Ez-Zor and Rural Damascus.
Currently WHO supports the work of 761 health care workers delivering IYCF counseling and facility-based nutrition services which have so far provided more than 250.000 IYCF face to face counselling are presented in all Governorates.
For WHO and UNICEF and their partners, all global acute malnutrition detected cases without complications are referred to be treated in outpatient centers supported by UNICEF and WFP (22,792 cases on 2022) or to 20 stabilization centers in hospitals complications are present (1,659 cases on 2022) . During NS , in cases of complication according IMCI criteria , the SAM case is sent to be treated in Hospitals ( 20 stabilization centers are activated ) , and if there is no complications ) .
All disability cases with malnutrition are included in nutrition services, referred and treated as well in outpatient, or inpatient in cases of complications. Additionally, as part of the current cholera response, WHO’s teams are trained on nutrition management during cholera based on WHO and UNICEF guidelines.
The WHO Gaziantep field presence will continue leading SC TWG, provision of technical support and delivering case management of severe acute malnutrition with complications training, and provision of supplementary SAM-medical kits. In addition, SC/hospitals were included HRP under the WHO filling critical gaps of SHC as last resort funding
Capacity-building of health care workers on nutrition counselling, surveillance and referral contributes to the recovery of the health system which has suffered tremendous loss of human resources. Furthermore, by supporting early detection and intervention of GAM, children can receive the necessary interventions to prevent lasting effects on physical and cognitive development (such a stunting) thereby helping to support the success and capacity of the next generation.

3 - Needs

The nutrition surveillance system is now consisted of a network of up to 941 health centres, with more than 1m beneficiaries in 2021,further expansion is required to ensure more geographical coverage in rural areas In 2022, 20 stabilization centres for treatment of complicated SAM were functioning in 13 governorates, During the year, overall increases in demand were observed, particularly in NES and Aleppo. Notably, 1824 per months of global malnutrition cases were detected in 2022 compared with 1926 monthly in 2021 a¬nd 1620 per month in 2020. It also notes a decrease in the number of beneficiaries of the surveillance program in 2022 compared with 2021 (75,000 in 2022 Vs 83,000 in 2021 and 100,000 in 2020) due to the situation arising from the COVID-19. With rising fuel costs, beneficiary numbers could be expected to fall again as patients struggle to reach health facilities for routine care, including nutrition services. New equipment is needed to cover all 981 HC working in the nutrition surveillance program while additional items are needed to provide further support to NES Forum actors. The 20 stabilization centres are distributed across the country and cover most of the needs. The SCs will require continued support of nutrition therapeutic supplies, equipment and training of the human resources. To cover the needs of 1500 patients across the country F75, F100, and Resomal are needed particularly in Al Hassakeh (47% off all admissions ) and in Aleppo (18%).

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Spread of COVID-19 and/or cholera in the project facilities.Likelihood: medium, impact: highMitigation: COVID-19 mitigation procedure will be in place during the project and will include, the full implementation of IPC and triage protocols, the social distancing and hand hygiene practices, regulation of the patient flow to avoid crowding. For cholera prevention, WHO is working with its WASH team and WASH partners to improve WASH infrastructure and sanitation in health facilities.2. Staff and/or beneficiaries are subject to PSEA risk. Likelihood: low, impact: highMitigation: Raising awareness through messages about PSEA topics in addition to having a WHO acceptable complaint mechanism in place. Shortage of therapeutic supplies could lead to interrupted treatment, worsening of child despite seeking careLikelihood: medium, impact: highMitigation: Ensure timely procurement and distribution of needed supplies4. 8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

WHO has a strong PRSEAH policy which includes standards of behavior for organization’s staff. Additionally, WHO’s staff are required to complete a set of mandatory trainings on PRSEAH and WHO’s global integrity hotline is available 24/7. Implementing partners personnel and representatives, are also required to abide by WHO’s PSEAH policy as a condition of their contract. WCO is providing capacity building to humanitarian workers and implementing partners on PRSEAH). Victim’s assistance will be according to guidelines as well partners must adhere to WHO’s policy for zero tolerance for abusive conduct and commit to taking adequate action if faced with abusive conduct allegations, in the absence of which, contractual arrangements can be terminated or other action may be taken consistent with the terms of their legal relationship with WHO , and this will imitate on providing health services with a respectful way to APP. Health workers who trained will be chosen according to their roles in providing direct and indirect for agreed health services supported by WHO. All partners are obliged to have a compliant, reporting mechanism and referral services to support victims ( WHO will contract only partners who has a appropriate score in PSEA assessment conducted by ICN). Further GBV first line intervention training is integrated in all MHGAP workshops to allow examiners, HCW, and community workers to provide support to any case of GBV survivals.
