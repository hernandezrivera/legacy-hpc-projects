Project Summary

To address the high rate of malnutrition in Somalia, ARC will Providing equitable access to quality life-saving curative nutrition services through systematic identification, referral and treatment of acutely malnourished cases, support curative and preventive nutrition interventions to treat children under five (U5), pregnant and lactating women (PLW) with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM). Services include nutrition screening, targeted supplementary feeding programme (TSFP) and outpatient therapeutic programme (OTP). All interventions will be aligned with MoH, WHO and UNICEF guidelines/protocols and Somalia integrated management of acute malnutrition (IMAM) protocols through capacity building to the qualified staff and CHWs on IMAM, IYCF and CommCare COVID putting the COVID-19 protocols into consideration. ARC will improve access to life-saving preventive nutrition services with an integrated health response and protection mainstreaming for vulnerable population groups focusing on appropriate infant and young child feeding practices in emergency, provision of supplementary products, micronutrient interventions and optimal maternal nutrition
Nutrition services will be co-located at the MCHs to help ensure that children U5 a cross both genders (36,970 female and 34,126 female) and PLW presenting for health services are screened for vaccination, nutrition and likewise those presenting for nutrition services can be referred and easily access primary, maternal, and child health services, as needed. Considering all genders, all children U5 presenting at the MCH will be screened and if necessary, referred for admission to the TSFP or OTP per the IMAM admission criteria. SAM cases presenting with medical complications will be referred to the nearest stabilization center (SC). ARC is also planning to combine the nutrition and health nursing staff to foster more integration across health and nutrition activities at the facilities while undertaking protection mainstreaming. The linkage to WASH and health components will ensure a comprehensive approach to reduce morbidity and mortality associated with the compound effect of diseases resulting from poor sanitation conditions, lack of access to safe water, and malnutrition. To increase the impact, the nutrition project will be coordinated also with ARC WASH, Protection and Livelihood projects. ARC will undertake smart or IYCF survey for evidence-based intervention and Strengthen nutrition situation coordination, monitoring and surveillance, analysis and utilization of early warning information for timely coordinated response
Regularly updating the ONA and sharing 3W Matrix, ARC will closely coordinate intervention with the cluster, MoH and respond in locations where gaps in nutrition services have been identified. ARC will ensure the participation of the local communities, with special attention to participation of women, in decision making throughout the project cycle to strengthen community acceptance and ownership. Throughout the lifetime of the project, ARC will work with the MoH throughout the lifecycle of the project by sharing information and supporting joint support supervision to pave the way for a proper phasing out at the end of the project.

Mitigation Measures

ARC is committed to ensure that beneficiaries safely access services, devoid of harm, to promote meaningful access, accountability, and participation in the provision of nutrition assistance considering risks and barriers to access faced by persons with disabilities, maximizing the positive impacts of nutrition programs on people’s safety and dignity and support affected populations access and enjoy their rights. Establish proper information sharing protocol for the disabled in a manner they can understand and survivors of abuse to avoid exposing them to further trauma and that efforts are made to ensure confidentiality and wishes or comfort of the beneficiaries. ARC’s needs assessment tools and frameworks will be adapted to and strengthen understanding of disability inclusion ensuring that persons with disabilities are involved in any key informant interviews and focus group discussion. A diverse sample of persons with disabilities will be part of the focus group discussions including men and women, boys, and girls, of all ages and impairment types, so that a diverse insight into the risks and barriers can be understoodARC will keep beneficiaries and personal data records confidential and safe from unintended use for effective data protection measures. Clearly marked separate toilet and bathing facilities for males and females accounting for persons with disabilities and children will be provided. For impartial and equal access of the basic services for all, ARC will ensure proper documentation, collect and use data disaggregated by sex, age, disability and diversity to effectively inform programming and ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable. ARC requires all partners and contractors, to sign Sexual Harassment, Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) policy at the time of agreement, and all staff, suppliers and consultants at the time of offer letter, so that the contracts are not valid without a signed code of conduct.To strengthen participation and empowerment, ARC will involve all categories, age, or gender group, disabled and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to nutrition need. For purposes of transparency, ARC will set up accessible, confidential, and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment, and assist in detecting misconduct by creating complaint boxes at nutrition sites, phone lines, setting up mechanisms for submitting feedback that do not require the beneficiary exposing themselvesARC teams will respond to complaints, immediately documenting lessons learnt, organize awareness raising sessions so that people know how it works. ARC will utilize the real time beneficiary feedback system, Kuja Kuja as a tool that captures the ‘pulse’ of customer satisfaction with services and to allow service teams, technical teams, and support functions both at the local and global levels to understand real time issues being faced, see opportunities for immediate and strategic improvement to service delivery. Feedback mechanisms will be accessible to all persons including the disabled with support from the Kuj Kuja team members who are all times standby to provide necessary guidanceThrough the adoption of Do No Harm approaches, efforts will be made to ensure relationships with the project targeted population and local leaders are enhanced and programs implemented to improvement relationships, reduce tension, and promote peaceful pathways out of conflict situations. ARC believes that understanding the context in which it operates is paramount to ensuring that interventions are successful and avoids negative consequences. ARC will assess the context and conflict dynamics, especially the role of dividers and connectors to link the conflict analysis with program cycle helping ARC to re-plan, implement and monitor the intervention in a conflict sensitive fashion

Please provide further explanation

The proposed Nutrition interventions aim to support existing government health centers and primary health units to promote sustainability, strengthening of healthcare and nutrition system and the continuation of care, focusing on where the needs are highest in proposed locations. As a result of quality programming and proven results, ARC enjoy strong relationships with target communities, local and national authorities. ARC has consults routinely with the RMO, DMOH and MOH on the program design and benefits from its support. In addition, ARC hire staff from local areas to ensure they have a good knowledge of local needs and possess the contacts and community knowledge necessary for success. The interventions will be implemented in close coordination with MOH, UNICEF, UNFPA, WFP and WHO as the health cluster lead and other health actors in the area. District Medical office of health will be the counterpart which ARC will interact with most frequently as they will facilitate any coordination required at facility level as well as make sure the staff in facilities are well-managed. RMO will work with ARC on staffing, facilities selection and technical monitoring at the regional level. Quarterly to semi-annual joint-monitoring visit will be coordinated at regional level to ensure the project keep abreast of the progress. At the central level, the major role for the MoH is to guide the health intervention at a strategic level for all region making sure the overall direction of the intervention is in alignment of the health/nutrition strategy for Somalia and the approach adapted are technically sounds and contextually appropriate.
