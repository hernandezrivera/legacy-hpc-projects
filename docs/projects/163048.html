Project Summary

The El-Nino induced drought phenomenon has adversely affected the provision of safe water supplies increasing the risk of WASH related diseases among the vulnerable communities. According to RWIMS (July 2019), 28.8% of source of safe water in rural areas nationally are seasonal with only 12.74% providing reliable perennial yield. RWIMS reports that only 49.6% of water points especially hand pumps are fully functional.
The ZIMVAC Assessment report (June 2019), showed that 16% of households are travelling more than 1km to fetch water from the nearest main water source while 32 per cent are travelling more than 500m. This travel distance will increase as more water sources dry up thereby increasing the burden of fetching water on children, women and girls who are forced to travel longer distances in search of safe water forcing some to miss school.
The scarcity of water exposing women and girls to the risk of sexual exploitation and abuse and is forcing communities to compromise on safe sanitation and hygiene thereby increasing the risk of WASH related diseases.
In view of the above the project will restore access to sufficient water of appropriate quality and quantity to fulfil the basic needs of children and women and alleviate suffering among vulnerable communities in areas that were adversely affected by El-Nino induced drought and are prone to tropical diseases such as cholera and typhoid. High yielding water sources that have the potential of serving more people will be targeted to cater for gender, equity and social inclusion while promoting livelihoods. There will be capacity development of community level institutions for sustainability. The project will promote safe hygiene and sanitation practices, with a focus on participatory health and hygiene education in institutions (health facilities, schools) and communities with high malnutrition rates. This will include training of Environmental Health Technicians (EHTs), Village Health Workers (VHWs) and Community Health Workers (CHWs). Health and hygiene promotion messages will be crafted in consultation with communication for development taskforce for inclusivity covering hygiene behaviour change with regards to faecal oral route of transmission, child care and feeding, MHM, promoting construction of self-sponsored latrines and hygiene enabling facilities such as tippy taps, pot racks, group hand washing facilities and incinerators for disposal of sanitary ware in schools. Distribution of hygiene enabling NFIs will target the most vulnerable members of the community with emphasis on households with children affected by SAM and MAM

Plan fields

1 - Activities

• Rehabilitation/repair of 253 broken down water points(70 Chegutu and 183 Makonde) • Rehabilitation /development of 4 existing solar powered PWS; • Drilling and equipping of 20 new boreholes and development 4 community managed solar powered PWS from high yielding boreholes• Water quality testing for 253 repaired/rehabilitated water sources; • Health and hygiene promotion, safe and efficient use of water and protection of environment• Training of 25 EHTs, 150 school health teachers and 150 hygiene promoters in sanitation focused health and hygiene messaging including MHM • Awareness creation on prevention of sexual exploitation in humanitarian crisis• Promotion of affordable sanitation and hygiene technologies (self-sponsored uBVIPs and tippy taps),• Distribution of 3500 hygiene dignity kits to most vulnerable adolescent girls • Distribution of 3400 hygiene enabling kits including dignity kits targeting the most vulnerable care givers of severe and moderate acute malnourished children• Strengthening coordination and surveillance mechanisms with district and provincial rapid response teams
