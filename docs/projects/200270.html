Project Summary

The objective of the project is that 23,930 most vulnerable individuals in the South and Bekaa regions, are using safely managed drinking water and sanitation services whilst reducing health and environmental risks and improving water quality by increasing the proportion of wastewater that is safely treated. For this purpose, ACF will:
1. Aim at improving access to water by
a. conducting water source quality testing in the areas with confirmed or suspected cholera cases as part of the strategy to ensure safe access to clean water to the affected population,
b. ensuring the access to safe water for at least 14 days via Water Trucking (WT) services or point of use treatment systems, in accordance with the minimum Sphere standards of 35 litres/person/day,
c. working closely with public institutions to ensure water delivered through the public network is chlorinated and safe to drink.
d. coordinating with UNICEF to support Water Establishments and municipalities with the provision of chlorine to sterilize water and the reduce risk of waterborne diseases, wherever not already supported by UNICEF,
e. supporting public institutions, such as schools and primary health care (PHC) facilities to increase water safety delivered to students and patients,
f. providing solarization and/or other activities that contribute to a lower reliance on fossil fuel for water provision to schools and surrounding communities
g. providing emergency fuel to pumping stations connected to schools and surrounding communities to maintain water supply via networks.
2. Aim at improving access to sanitation by:
a. providing desludging services through already identified and validated suppliers to cholera affected or suspected households and its surrounding areas. The desludging services will only be used for decentralized systems that are not connected to the public wastewater systems, mainly in ITSs and Collective Shelters, but also in Lebanese communities using sceptic tanks (decentralized systems) as their wastewater disposal method,
b. disinfecting the main areas of the cholera hotspots (latrines, excreta and vomits, garbage dumping sites) and equipped with PPE through our WASH disinfection field teams, which are part of the Rapid Response Teams (RRT).
3. Enhance access to positive hygiene and WaSH behavior:
a. carrying out Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE) activities, by providing awareness sessions on the prevention and treatment of Cholera. The sessions will target households with confirmed or suspected cases, as well as surrounding households,
b. conducting community-based awareness sessions provided by ACF’s field officers will be complemented by the provision of a hygiene kit (HK) with items designed to help prevent and treat Cholera.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

ACF works to ensure gender and protection mainstreaming at every stage of its project through a compliance with ACF’s Gender Policy and Do No Harm approach. Needs and vulnerabilities of women, men, boys and girls have been assessed through a participatory approach to develop meaningful activities in line with the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, neutrality and independence. Additionally, ACF also ensures two-way communication with beneficiaries through its dedicated feedback and complaints mechanism, allowing complaints to be directly collected and addressed. ACF shall ensure that all its employees and personnel comply with the provisions of ST/SGB/2003/13 entitled “Special Measures for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Sexual Abuse”. ACF shall further ensure that none of its employees and personnel exposes any intended beneficiary, including children, to any form of discrimination, abuse or exploitation.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

ACF field staff engages with affected populations to clearly introduce them to program activities, expected impacts, and selection criteria in inception meetings planned in advance with local stakeholders and engagement and coaching of Community Mobilizers and Focal Points in diverse settings. The involved stakeholders, level of detail and level of engagement is project-specific, based on contextual factors such as access and acceptance in targeted locations. This engagement supports stakeholder trust and acceptance of project activities as well as providing a venue for stakeholder feedback on their relevance. ACF Lebanon is accountable to its beneficiaries, meaning it will acknowledge and act on all feedback and complaints from communities, local authorities, beneficiaries, contractors, service providers, and all other stakeholders throughout project implementation, especially through amplifying its Feedback and Complaint Mechanism (FCM).
