Project Summary

In this project TOD proposed to support 22 villages /communities in Rural Kassala locality. The activities will be CLTs activity delivered hygiene promotion, sanitation and water safety. Hygiene promotion is central to this approach and is defined here as the process of enabling people to increase control over their health and its determinants, and thereby improve their health. The three key areas of the program are information sharing, community mobilization and provision of essential materials
Community based participatory hygiene and sanitation promotion by trained community hygiene promoters in the community. Three volunteers will be trained on Participatory approaches like community dialog, focus group discussion and Participatory Hygiene and Sanitation Transformation (PHAST) to facilitate community meeting and conduct house to house visit to promote handwashing at critical time, safe water treatment and storage, construction and use of hygienic latrines. The trained volunteers will coordinate with traditional, religious and community leaders. Different IEC material will be distributed for the facilitation of the hygiene promotion activities. Also Important environmental hygiene practices target by hygiene promotors
• Keep animals away from areas where the food is prepared and served to the child, child play areas and water sources. Regularly clear compound of any animal or child faeces, at least daily.
• Control disease vectors such as flies, mosquitoes, cockroaches and rats by covering food, improving drainage and safely disposing of garbage and non-reusable materials into a waste receptacle or protected pit.
• Clean key surfaces. This may include cleaning latrines, basins and kitchen floors and surfaces with soap and water and possibly disinfecting after cleaning with a dilute bleach solution, if available.
• Provide safe areas that can be regularly cleaned where children can play. In addition hygiene promoters will be trained to test level of water contamination at house hold level using H2S (Hydrogen Sulfide) water testing kits with close supervision of NGO staff and distribute the Chlorine tablets (33 mg) for the household where water test show contamination. Combined flocculants and disinfectant (House hold level treatment) water treatment chemical or House Hold water treatment technology with combined effect of filtering and purifying water for drinking
Hygiene kit, house hold water purification tablets, jerry-can, and soap will be provided as incentive for family with good hygiene practice

Needs

The Eastern part of Sudan has been characterized by deprived socioeconomic status and limited access to basic services. Access to safe water and sanitary facilities are critical factors behind child morbidity. One of the poorest states in Sudan, Kassala suffers among the lowest health indicators in northern states along with the highest refugee population in the country.Water is a basic necessity for life. Unfortunately not all water is safe for human consumption. Water from contaminated sources causes numerous diseases and untimely death. The fact that human beings need water and cannot live without it forces them to use it even for drinking purpose from any contaminated source as the result many people suffer or die from water borne disease.Safe drinking water is a basic necessity for good health. Unsafe drinking water can be a significant determinant of diseases such as cholera, typhoid, and schistosomiasis. Drinking water can also be contaminated with chemical and physical contaminants with harmful effects on human health. In addition to preventing disease, improved access to drinking water may be particularly important for women and children, especially in rural areas, who bear the primary responsibility for carrying water. Inadequate disposal of human excreta and personal hygiene are associated with a range of diseases including diarrhoeal diseases, polio, and are important determinants of malnutrition such as stunting. Improved sanitation can reduce diarrhoeal disease by more than a third (Sudan MICS, 2014)The most recent S3M 2013 showed that, only 13.1% have access to improve source of drinking water and 13% have improved sanitation facility, also the Sudan MICS 2014 indicate that 43% of kassala population was drinking from unimproved sources of water from them 96.7% are used without treatment.People also had good knowledge of the signs and symptoms of diarrhea, but the causes were not always linked to the almost total lack of available latrines and hand-washing practices. Hand washing incidence was poor and often restricted to occasions prior to the preparation of food and following defecation. There was also improper disposal of children feces.
