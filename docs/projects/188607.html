Project Summary

The program’s overall objective is to strengthen social cohesion amongst conflict affected communities in North Syria and contribute to long-term peace and stability of Syria. Strengthening cohesive communities is vital to supporting the stability that is finally emerging in North Syria, and reinforcing post-conflict economic recovery. The combination of improving inter-communal relationships and reducing resource-based tensions will serve to prevent the escalation of some economic-based disputes into violence – for example between host and IDP communities over livelihood opportunities or social service provision – and reduce the ability of political actors to manipulate ethno-religious divisions and mobilise violence. To this end, and in close collaboration with local and neighborhood councils, Mercy Corps will implement community service projects to support local economic development and address resource-based tensions. Mercy Corps social cohesion approach is grounded on a sound context, conflict and conflict sensitivity analysis that supports the identification of viable entry points for strengthening social cohesion within and between communities on different sides of the conflict divide. Mercy Corps supports community members with the knowledge, skills and confidence they need to analyze opportunities and impediments to strengthening social cohesion; and to formulate and implement options for strengthening social cohesion at the community level. Through participatory methodology including community mobilization, dialogue, interest based negotiation and the use of sports and creative arts; Mercy Corps will encourage positive interactions between and among community members from across the conflict divide. Mercy Corps will also support conflicting communities to address drivers of conflict through the joint implementation of projects that address resource based conflict drivers. Mercy Corps community mobilization methodology multi-pronged approach to social cohesion Conflict sensitivity is at the heart of all social cohesion activities. The project will develop a conflict sensitivity strategy, guided by various conflict sensitivity analysis tools including the do no harm matrix and stakeholder analysis matrix, building off the findings of context and conflict analysis in the areas of implementation. Mercy Corps social cohesion project will ensure the meaningful participation of all sections of the community with an emphasis on women and youth (male and female).

4 - Needs

The conflict in Syria has contributed to the fragmentation of communities. Micro-level tensions and conflicts are prevalent, driven by unresolved historical and emerging grievances, population movements (displacements and returns), dissatisfaction with issues of governance, competition over control and access to resources, limited access to formal and informal dispute resolution and a deterioration in economic wellbeing amongst other drivers. Conflicts often manifest along identity lines including political, ethnic, displacement, and tribal identities. Displacement, gross human rights violations, forceful conscription, radicalization, tactics of war such as altering demographic composition of populations along ethnic lines and other impacts of the macro-level conflict in Syria damaged cohesive micro-level Syrian communities. Lack of cohesiveness in turn limits opportunities for transforming micro-level conflicts across the country and presents challenges for durable solutions. However, opportunities to build and strengthen social cohesion remain, evidenced by emerging micro-level initiatives by local and international organizations across Syria. Initiatives highlight the possibility of achieving gradual, incremental transformation for communities through carefully designed and context-appropriate interventions that facilitate constructive interactions amongst within and between identity groups – ultimately contributing to social cohesion.

(4) Community projects w/members and formal/informal leaders supported to participate collaboratively in project identification, design, implementation: (4.1) Form/strengthen community groups to plan, implement, oversee community projects; (4.2) Mobilize community to identify & prioritize projects; (4.3) Form project management committees; (4.4) Implement, monitor, launch community projects. 9 - Protection Risk Analysis

(1) Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers/partners: Mercy Corps monitoring and evaluation systems include accountability frameworks, and Mercy Corps has a beneficiary complaints mechanism. Mercy Corps is committed to the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and Child Safeguarding through appropriate policies (Code of Conduct) which are enforced through on-boarding and training to all staff, suppliers and partners. Distribution are carefully planned to ensure they are done at times and in places that are safe and accessible to women and girls, as well as the elderly and people with disabilities, and that any barriers to access as a result of gender norms or physical ability are taken into account when planning for the distribution. (2) Forceful conscription into armed forces: Adult men (above 18) are at risk of forceful conscription by armed forces and governing authority across Syria. This project will target youth and project activities could be exploited as an opportunity to forcefully conscript male youth. (3) Threat to physical safety and security of participants: The program could challenge harmful community cultures and norms. 10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Participant safeguarding is central to Mercy Corps’ values, and we uphold a zero- tolerance tolerance approach towards any form of sexual exploitation and abuse. In order to prevent safeguarding violations of any kind, all staff, casual labor and external contractors receive orientation and training, and are required to sign the Code of Conduct. The safeguarding training is comprehensive and is delivered to all Mercy Corps and partner staff. Where Mercy Corps and partner staff have higher levels of contact with participants, they will receive tailored capacity strengthening on Protection Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and will participate in quarterly events, which address PSEA concerns in target communities, and facilitate the roll-out of PSEA tools and preventative actions. This process is led by our WoS Safeguarding Manager and is supported by a network of focal points within Mercy Corps. Partner staff will have the opportunity to risk assess their own areas in terms of safeguarding and create proactive action plans. Community participants will be informed on what safeguarding is and how they can report any concerns through sensitisation work across programmes. In addition, external contractors and staff such as suppliers or Third Party Monitors (TPMs) are regularly trained by department focal points who have completed tailored Training of Trainer (ToT) safeguarding training.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

(1) Safety & security of participants and staff could be negatively affected: Conflict presents a constant threat. Mitigation includes ongoing context analysis including security and risk assessments to inform decisions on location, routes and timing of activities, ensuring decisions are in the best interest of the safety and security of participants and staff. Social and cultural norms of communities will be observed during implementation to ensure safety risks associated with real or perceived violation of norms are mitigated. Project staff will complete a mandatory safety & security training; participants will be sensitized to safety and security issues and how to recognize threats and take action to protect themselves. (2) Implementation delays due to COVID 19: Program implementation could experience significant delays due to the COVID-19 pandemic, occasioned by lockdowns and curfews. Mercy Corps will draw on experiences adapting program implementation over the last one year to ensure that as many activities as possible can be implemented. Simultaneously, opportunities for pursuing and strengthening remote program implementation capacities will be explored and harnessed. (3) Loss of access to target communities: Shifts in territorial control or major security incidents could temporarily or permanently impede access of Mercy Corps organization to project participants. Mercy Corps will continuously monitor the situation.
