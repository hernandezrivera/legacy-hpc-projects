Project Summary

Through the project WFP will provided unconditional food support to 70,000 Nigerian refugees in the Far North region including 3,000 contingency caseloads. In line with WFP Cameroon Country Strategic Plan (CSP 2022-2026), life-saving assistance will be provided Nigerian refugees in Minawao camp. The transfer modality of food assistance to the beneficiaries will be in-kind food through unconditional monthly food distributions to support refugees’ households’ access to adequate and nutritious food, therefore prevent them from adopting negative coping practices. The full ration of the in-kind distributions will provide 1, 095kcal energy per day per person for the beneficiaries who entirely rely on humanitarian food assistance The daily food rations will comprise 370g cereals, 120g pulses 40g vegetable, and 5g iodized salt. This assistance will be coupled to the nutrition prevention and treatment activities, WFP will provide support for children aged 6–59 months, this will include blanket supplementary feeding, counselling and support for moderate acute malnutrition treatment and improvement in adherence to treatment; mother and child health and nutrition programmes; and gender and nutrition- informed social and behavior change communication as part of an integrated nutrition package. The daily ration for the specialized nutritious foods per assisted child will be 100 g.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The Far North region of Cameroon is facing several complex crises, including the insurgency in the Lake Chad Basin, climatic extremes (drought and floods), and, more recently, several waves of inter-communal clashes over natural resources. These factors have plunged the region into a prolonged and growing humanitarian emergency and food insecurity. The number of people in need of food assistance has steadily increased over the years. According to the October 2022 Cadre Harmonisé (CH), the Far North region remains among the most affected region by food insecurity, representing 22 percent of the total acute food-insecure people in the country. The projections for June to August 2022 estimate that approximately 20 percent of the population within the region will be acutely food insecure, which represents nearly 1 million people out of the 3.2 million people estimated nationwide. Moreover, in this very region, more than 70 percent of the population cannot afford a healthy diet, according to the latest “Fill the Nutrient Gap” Report published in 2021 by the Government of Cameroon and WFP. The Join Assessment Mission conducted by WFP and UNHCR shown overall, almost half of Nigerian refugee households in Minawao settlement and in the host community of the Far North are highly vulnerable. Moreover, Against the background of high vulnerability levels, more than half of refugee households’ resort to negative livelihood coping strategies to meet essential needs.Since 2014, repeated incursions by armed groups into villages near the border with Nigeria have led to massive population displacement. As of June 2022, there were approximately 130,000 Nigerian refugees who fled the Boko Haram insurgency to Cameroon's Far North region, 60 percent of whom live in the Minawao camp, with the remaining living within host communities. In addition, there are an estimated 357,631 internally displaced persons (IDPs), and according to the results of the return intention survey conducted by IOM, 76 percent of them (IDPs and refugees) do not intend to go back to their place of origin because conditions are not conducive to return. This situation of insecurity is further exacerbated by climatic events. The Far North has experienced an increase in the frequency, intensity, and variability of weather shocks in recent years, including floods, droughts, and erratic rainfall. An observable aggravating factor is human-induced vegetation loss driven by the need for fuelwood and land for agricultural production. The loss of vegetation results in the inability to prevent the direct impact of raindrops on the soil, and the loss of roots decreases soil stability, destabilizing the soil structure and increasing vulnerability during floods.
