Project Summary

The Overall objective of the intervention is to Strengthen the access to livelihood by creating income generating opportunities and by improving access to production and market infrastructure to restore local economy recovery in Ariha and Azaz districts.
The project intends in its first outcome to strengthen Syrian families’ livelihoods, protect their assets and reduce their negative coping mechanisms to ensure they are better able to meet their essential needs and have access to activities which support their wellbeing. In its second outcome, the project aims to strengthen vulnerable women and youth’ livelihoods, provide them with hope for the future, reduce their negative coping mechanisms to ensure they are better able to play active role in rebuilding their communities. The first outcome will be achieved through enabling 70 Targeted households in Ariha and A’zaz districts to restore/establish livelihoods activities. Over 6 months the households will be provided with a combination of micro business cash grants, technical support, and in- kind food support. This will be done through a value chain approach, and enterprises supported are intended to be linked to priority value chains in the market based on the findings of market and area assessments that will be conducted in the beginning of the project.
The second outcome will be achieved by promoting the attractiveness of vocational education and training (VET) and ensuring its quality in Azaz, Aleppo. 180 young men and women will have access to business skills trainings. Additionally, The Action will create dignified work opportunities that include both formal employment and micro-enterprise streams. Over 9 months 140 disadvantaged young men and women will have the supportive environment and equal opportunities to access quality vocational training including well-designed apprenticeships systems that will be provided to 50 young men and women. Furthermore, business grants will be provided to 90 young men and women who completed their VET/ apprenticeships. To address any access issues, Fazaa is to offer transportation for trainees coming from surrounding villages and camps.
In each target area, Fazaa will work closely with local councils, community leaders, community-based organisations and other community representatives to target beneficiaries according to the relevant criteria for each intervention. Targeting approaches and criteria will differ based on the intervention type. The beneficiary targeting process and selection criteria will be clearly displayed at the community level to ensure transparency. Both host community and IDPs will be targeted for each result.
Fazaa’s approach in implementing development projects seeks to ensure the best possible quality both in terms the process and the outcome. Therefore, for each project, Fazaa will conduct a detailed process analysis and identify the gaps and potential weaknesses in each step. Furthermore, the process analysis will ensure and clarify how each step will be documented or verified taking into consideration all the necessary learning loops and impact assessment. The following link is an example of the process developed from a past businesses support project implemented by Fazaa: https://drive.google.com/file/d/0B6-QOtAlrAU2RFVmUmFIbXpxSEE/view?usp=sharing
Fazaa will develop regular monitoring tools to ensure prompt internal and external reporting and close follow up on the execution of the project plan. Fazaa focuses on measuring the impact of its activities both on the household and community levels. Therefore, baseline and endline assessments on the household levels will be conducted. Fazaa usually tries to measure the impact of livelihoods interventions on the food security level, coping strategies and child-protection issues.

Needs

As the Syrian crisis enters its eighth year, civilians continue to bear the brunt of a conflict marked by unparalleled suffering. Over half of the population have been forced from their homes, and many people have suffered multiple displacements. The scale, severity, and complexity of needs across Syria remain overwhelming. The UN states that 13.1 million people in Syria require humanitarian assistance. Of these, 5.6 million people are in acute need due to a convergence of vulnerabilities resulting from displacement, exposure to hostilities, and limited access to basic goods and services.At the same time, there has been a very significant decrease in net income due to higher production and marketing costs, and very constrained purchasing power as the index of food consumer prices increased by 800 percent between 2010 and 2018. As a result, 90% of households now spend over half of their income on food, against 25 percent before the crisis. In addition, only 25 percent of households still have access to income from any source, against 60 percent before the crisis.The lives and livelihoods of Syrians in Northwest Syria have been shattered and coping strategies have been exhausted, stretching their resourcefulness to its absolute limit. Access to essential services, including health care, safe water and education has been severely disrupted. Families are resorting to unsustainable and unsafe means of survival, including forced and/or early marriage, child labour, child recruitment, survival sex and temporary marriages, and joining extremist groups. Such survival practices reflect foremost on the wellbeing of vulnerable groups, particularly youth and women.Throughout much of the Syrian crisis, and more significantly in the past three years, northwest Syria (Idleb and Aleppo governorate) has served as a relocation point for civilians from across Syria in addition to fighters. Today Idlib hosts an estimated 2.5 million civilians including more than 1 million internally displaced from other regions of Syria while Aleppo hosts more than 870,000 IDPs with a percentage of IDPs to host community as high as 62% in Azaz district where more than 522,000 people live today. IDPs living in camps are of particular concern as they have extremely limited access to supplies and income generating activities which deepens their dependency on humanitarian deliveries, and adds negative social, psychological and ethical implications for their well-being. With only 9% of the assessed IDPs in Idleb and 24% of the assessed IDPs intending to return home, the majority of households remain undecided according to HNAP 2018 report on the intentions of IDPs in Syria. As the reported main push and pull factors that drive such decisions are the security and the economic situation, the need to empower those IDPs economic opportunities in work and livelihood seems more stressing than ever to avoid them decisions that could compromise their safety, pushed by their economic disadvantage.

Output I: Targeted households in Ariha and A’zaz restore/establish livelihoods activities through micro business grants and technical support that improve market functionality and strengthen value chainsThe Action will provide structured support to micro businesses enabling them to start trade and become more profitable and improve market functionality in the targeted areas. A market assessment will be conducted in target areas in the start-up phase and results will be used to inform the selection of key market actors of varying sizes to enable sustained economic activity within the value chains identified.70 micro, small and/or micro businesses and enterprises will be supported under this Action, benefiting approximately 350 people. Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Community tension arising from the beneficiary selection. Likelihood: 3, Impact: 4. Mitigation: maintaining transparency in the selection process through defining the criteria clearly in call for applications and coordination with local councils.Risk: Diversion of assets. Likelihood: 5, Impact: 4. Mitigation: Fazaa will provide in-kind food support for the beneficiaries during the first 4 months in order to mitigate the deprivation of capital assets. Contracts will be signed with the recipients and assets will be reportedRisk: security threats for the recipients of the grants as a result of increased visibility. Likelihood:1 , Impact: 5. Mitigation: The grants will be divided into two installments and the dates of payments will differ. The grants are delivered to the HH directly by Fazaa’s field team. Sexual abuse or harassment by humanitarian workers against women: Likelihood: 2, Impact: 5. Mitigation: All Fazaa’s field and project staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct. Fazaa takes awareness about protection risks seriously and include a training in its induction process. Complaints and feedback mechanism is in place.Risk: Exploitation by business owners against trainees in apprenticeships. Likelihood: 2, Impact: 5. Mitigation: All participants are to receive awareness sessions about their rights. MoU will be signed with each host business owner including code of conduct and child safeguard policy. Complaints and feedback mechanism in place.Regular, unannounced visits by Fazaa’s M&E is to be conducted.
