Project Summary

Oxfam will support the residence building in the crisis-affected agricultural communities through rebuilding their absorptive, adaptive and transformative capacities to address future risks, fragilities and uncertainties. While the project will help the communities to address their immediate and short-run needs, it will help them to recover their resilience pathways, in other words, building the links between emergency and development phases. The project will rely mainly on the existing systems, knowledge and capacities to make positive change that will foster positive transition. In full community participatory way, Oxfam will identify the critical risks and concerns and the main causes of vulnerabilities in the agricultural communities and the existing capacities and plans to address them immediately, in the short term and in the long term.
While Oxfam will target supporting 51900 most vulnerable HHs, by providing them with the needed assets like agricultural and livestock kits, it will also help enhancing the community’s capacity as a whole, this will be done through rehabilitation of irrigation system and bakeries by providing the needed assets and also by building the community mechanism to manage the system in a way that ensure women and most vulnerable’ access to water, advocating will different stakeholders to change the market environment to be more suitable for women assess and participation, rehabilitation of the irrigation channels through labour-intensive schemes when it is feasible.
Besides, the project will link the most vulnerable and willing families with the market systems in a sustainable way by supporting market driven income-generating activities (IGAs), including well-adapted vocational training. This will come after performing a through market and livelihood assessments in order to identify the existing opportunities and capacities in the local market systems.
The project will be implemented in full gender and climate sensitive manner; therefore gender aspects will be mainstreamed into all the project aspects in order to encourage women economic empowerment and participation in the communities decisions. Risks and vulnerabilities related to climate change will be prioritized and addressed.
The geographical targeting will be done after close coordination with other humanitarian actors in order to ensure the highest possible level of complementarity and avoid overlapping.
All the proposed activities will be in line with FSA Sector’s SO2/SO3 guidelines in terms of contents, conditions and frequency, Oxfam will communicate with the Sector any justified changes.

4 - Needs

According to the HNO 2021 FSA data sets, all the proposed sub-districts are ranked 3 or 4 on the FSA needs severity scale with total number of PiN 460,000 approximately(20000 in Aleppo,22200 in DZ &217,000 in Rural Damascus). Based on several assessments performed in Aleppo, RD &DZ, Oxfam found that farmers have lost their capacities to restart and sustain their agricultural activities due to: destruction of the irrigation systems, very high prices of farming inputs; livestock and fodder, high prices and shortage of fuel, suspension of the government subsidies programs due to lack or resources, insufficient access to lands which in many cases are controlled by military groups. Therefore, farmers have relied on several negative coping strategies that will contribute to worsen the agriculture sector more in the near future like,drilling of wells close to Euphrates the matter the cause salination, selling of their livestock, migrate to urban areas looking for livelihood opportunities& joining military groups.In a recent irrigation system rehabilitation project in Al Mayadeen Subdistrict of DZ, Oxfam found that the majority of farmers have restarted their farming activities in the first seasons after the work is done.The majority of the communities surveyed by Oxfam,farmers confirmed that supporting them to restore their agricultural business is more important that relief items& cash.The lack of bread in such communities is another important problem that increases the suffering of people and the risks of GBV&tensions between communities.Oxfam has performed several bakeries rehabilitation programs that contributed to increase the supply of subsidized bread in the local markets in a gendered sensitive manner.

5 - Implementation of activities

- Context analysis to identify the main causes of risks and vulnerabilities, the existing capacities and the potential pathways for resilience building- Vocational and skills building of the most vulnerable farmers and livestock breeders- Provision of agricultural kits (seeds, fertilizers, livestock, fodders ..) to the most vulnerable HHs through the appropriate modality - Post Distribution Monitoring to monitor the performance of the established businesses, their sales growth, existing problems and difficulties.- Support establishing micro and small businesses after ensuring enough linkages with the market system, special focus on women economic empowerment- Rehabilitation of existing irrigation systems and bakeries, supporting the communities to build gender sensitive water management sensitive - Post Distribution Monitoring to monitor the functionality of the rehabilitated irrigation systems and bakeries and whether women and PWDs have equal and dignified access to the irrigation water and produced bread.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: - Limited equipment and supplies available in the Syrian market due to instability, sanctions and transactions delays prevent the implementation of project activities. - The USD to SYP Exchange rate fluctuations could result in either big gain at end of project period with no time to appropriate the additional funds, or not enough funds to fulfil commitments and project objectives. - Potential of fraud and aid diversion, amongst Oxfam staff, suppliers and partners - COVID19 spread puts at risk the health and wellbeing of affected communities and of humanitarian workers delivering assistance. - Assumptions: - No interference by conflicting parties in the implementation putting at risk independence, and impartiality. the local authorities will allocate enough production inputs for the rehabilitated bakeries. the community will have the capacity to manage the work in the rehabilitated bakeries sufficiently. The social norms and traditions will enable women to participate in the communities’ decision-making process.Mitigation: Oxfam will perform rapid market assessment to check the availability of the required items and their prices. All Oxfam and partner’s staff will be inducted on Oxfam’s standards on safeguarding and accountability. Very clear accountability mechanism will be put in place prior to the implementation. Oxfam will go for NCEs to absorb the surpluses that it might have as a result to an increase in the exchange rate.
