Project Summary

Access to safe drinking water is essential to health, a basic human right, and a component of effective policies for health protection. Based on HNO, several factors have adversely impacted access to WASH services in Syria, including the Feb 2023 earthquake, conflict hostilities, economic decline, power shortages, and the impact of climate change. As a result of the conflict and earthquake, damage to WASH systems has restricted the population’s access to water and sanitation services in affected communities. Additionally, economic decline has impeded service providers' technical and financial capacities to operate and maintain water and sanitation systems adequately. This economic downturn has also affected households, limiting their ability to adopt coping mechanisms to maintain basic access to WASH services. Furthermore, frequent droughts, have negatively impacted the quality and quantity of freshwater in Syria. Together with critically bad sanitation conditions, poor hygiene practices and deterioration of water quality, the WASH conditions caused increased public health risks, including AWD/Cholera outbreak, leishmaniasis, and malnutrition, as well as an impact on the livelihood and protection of households. Additionally, WASH in health facilities (HF) is frequently substandard and in need of light rehab/intervention.
Although WHO continues to establish joint efforts to monitor drinking water quality improve this situation, access to safe drinking water remains limited among the vulnerable populations, and the acceleration of efforts to improve safe drinking water with multi-sectoral collaboration is needed. A joint WASH assessment in HFs identified 3 major gaps:
-health waste management
-environmental cleaning services and sanitation services
-water services and manual hygiene services

In 2024, WHO will focus on the following:
1. Surveillance of the safety and quality of drinking water by regularly measuring PH, ATP, residual chlorine of water samples from various water sources (e.g. water networks, ground wells, reservoirs, water tanks, jerry cans) and culture to confirm pathogens and provide technical & operational support. These activities will cover all governorates, with special focus in Rural Damascus, Aleppo (Rural), Al-Hasakeh and Deir-ez-Zor, by deploying WHO focal points to conduct testing in collaboration with Ministry of Health, Ministry of Water Resources, Ministry of Local Administration and Environment and WASH partners.
2. Procurement of diagnostics supplies & devices for water quality monitoring and provide related training.
3. Preposition and deliver water purification tablets to DoH, camps and camp-like settings to enable response to health alerts and detected contamination of drinking water by disinfecting reservoirs of HFs, identified facilities, and/or households with health risks.
4. Conduct light WASH rehabilitation of prioritized public health facilities.
5. Enhance medical waste management, which is key to preventing nosocomial infections
6. Carryout monitoring visits of rehabilitation projects and field activities.
WHO utilizes the health sector PRA to ensure protection of the most vulnerable within the delivery of its interventions. A dedicated PSEA focal point & committed have instituted a pillar-based approach to PSEA to ensure awareness AND response capacity within WHO and its implementing partners. These activities are further overseen by regional focal points. WHO also pursues and integrated approach by aligning GBV, PSEA and MHPSS to enable comprehensive response capacity. Finally, WHO has developed and regularly updates its risk matrix with specified mitigation measures. Business continuity plans are updated quarterly and WHO global has also rolled out readiness checklists to ensure offices are ready to respond to emergencies.
