Project Summary

Mehad aims to maintain the provision of comprehensive, high-quality healthcare services across 32 facilities in the four northeastern Syrian governorates throughout 2024. The goal is to ensure the operation of the facilities and strive to enhance stability and resilience among the population by enabling access to healthcare services.
These 32 facilities encompass Primary and Tertiary health services, as well as Central Incinerators, a Drug control laboratory and Medical training centers, catering to approximately 860000 beneficiaries annually. The health services are integrated with WASH, Protection, MHPSS and Case Management, as well as Nutrition services.
The facilities consist of 8 Basic Primary Health Care Centers (BPHCCs), 1 Comprehensive Primary Health Care Center (CPHCC), 1 Basic Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (BEmONC) unit, 3 Mobile Clinics, 4 Dialysis Units, 1 Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (CEmONC) unit, 2 Thalassemia Units, 2 Blood Banks, 2 Central Medical Labs, 1 Radiology Unit, 2 Medical Training Centers, 1 Drug Control lab and 4 Central incinerators.
Mehad has operated some of the facilities since 2018, while others are still under planning. Mehad covers salaries of 660 medical and non-medical staff and the monthly operating costs of the centers. A field coordination team in NES and Erbil, consisting of 75 staff members.
Mehad has a dedicated technical quality assurance team, comprising staff from quality control, health, and field staff from the MEAL departments. This team monitors the application of the Essential Health Service Package (EHSP) under primary and tertiary levels, with supervision, training, with coaching provided by the support team.
Additionally, Mehad has extensive experience in the field of IPC, and implemented a project to strengthen the health system that targeted three governorates in NES with activities such as capacity building, assessments, supervisory visits, and improving the waste management component at nearly 40 health facilities for several NGOs and local health authorities. Additionally, operating 3 medical waste incinerators for more than three years and managing them. Therefore, Mehad takes into consideration, the operation of central incinerators is a part of its strategic plan for the 2024 and 2025. In addition to strengthening the concepts of drug control and the central management of IPC in NES.
Within a wider healthcare project in northeastern Syria, a pivotal element involves introducing an advanced drug track and trace system. This initiative aims to improve pharmaceutical supply chains, implement global best practices, and guarantee access to high-quality medicines. It involves deploying technology for real-time medication monitoring, providing targeted training for medical organizations, and developing supportive policies. The ultimate goal is to eradicate counterfeit drugs and establish a sustainable pharmaceutical framework, resulting in a substantial enhancement of health service delivery and patient safety.
Notably, the locations of these 32 health facilities are strategically situated, with some multiple facilities stationed in the same building. The PHC centers offer preventive and curative consultations, dental services, and nutritional care. Community health programs are implemented through CHW teams conducting household visits, aligned with WHO protocols. Tertiary health care services include Hemodialysis, Thalassemia management, Blood transfusion services, and Radiology services. Mehad's medical training initiatives supported over 2,500 individuals in various topics, contributing to emergency management, resuscitation, echography, obstetrics and gynecology, and first aid.
Lastly, Mehad collaborated with the NES HWG to develop and approve EHSP. Mehad plans to monitor their implementation in coordination with the NES HWG and health partners.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Needs: The initiative is vital for sustaining Mehad HFs in NES. In 2024, 16.7 million people are assessed to need humanitarian assistance across Syria, up from 15.3 million in 2023. Of the 16.7 million people in need, 5.5 million are displaced, including over 2 million who live in last resort sites. After 12 years of hostilities economic crisis, people in Syria saw their situation worsen when a series of earthquakes hit northern Syria and Türkiye on 6 and 20 February 2023. The earthquakes uprooted hundreds of thousands of families, resulted in almost 5,900 deaths and more than 12,800 people injured in Syria, and severely damaged infrastructures weakened by the conflict. Most families lost their breadwinners due to death and injury, when the economic situation was dire, increasing the vulnerability of millions of people previously unable to meet their basic needs. Implementation: The project aims to enhance healthcare access in Northeast Syria through Mehad's sustained efforts across its 32 Health Facilities. This involves ensuring staff salaries, covering operational costs, procuring, and centrally storing medications. Monthly quality checks, field visits, and a robust data management system contribute to the project's success.Protection Risk Analysis: Mehad prioritizes gender equality and safeguard mechanisms for the social sustainability of its projects. Key initiatives include a comprehensive Code of Conduct for all teams, integrating gender principles and safeguards. In recruitment, efforts focus on gender-neutral job descriptions, inclusive interview committees, and achieving a balanced workforce with around 50% female employees. Health services emphasize a comfortable environment for women patients, ongoing GBV and gender sensitivity training, and comprehensive child safeguarding measures, particularly in pediatric health services. PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) Strategy: Mehad's HR and MEAL departments oversee the PSEA Code of Conduct, ensuring staff capacity-building and monitoring compliance. Training covers GBV and PSEA for all staff, maintaining a comfortable environment for women patients with sufficient female medical staff. GBV services are provided at various levels, including community awareness, PHC-level identification and referral, and CMR case management. MEHAD enforces a zero-tolerance policy for suspected SEA cases, with compliance procedures and investigations supervised by the HR team and Senior Management. The 2024 strategy integrates specific GBV-related services and activities in all MEHAD health facilities. Risks and Mitigations: The project confronts potential challenges such as security-related curfews, protest demonstrations, and border closures with Kurdistan Region of Iraq. Mitigation strategies include closely monitoring security situations, exempting health centers from curfews, and training staff for diverse scenarios. Online meetings and local implementation teams.
