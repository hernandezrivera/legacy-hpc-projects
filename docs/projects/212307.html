Project Summary

In the early hours of 6 February, multiple earthquakes, the strongest being of 7.7 magnitudes on the Richter scale struck southern Türkiye and NWS. Communities in at least 35 sub-districts in NWS have been affected, impacting an estimated 3,04 million people. The earthquake hit during harsh winter conditions with freezing temperatures at a time when communities already have been under severe stress, compounded by over ten years of hostilities and protracted displacements. Humanitarian needs were already immense before the earthquakes, with an estimated 4.1 million people in need of assistance at the end of 2022.Infrastructure and WASH systems that were poor before the earthquake also became unusable in some areas after the earthquake. WASH systems in Syria have suffered from the high capacity operation, limited or no maintenance, demolitions, displacement of technical staff and poor water resources management, with large numbers of IDPs overloading the system. WASH infrastructures and service providers are therefore in need of significant investment, repair and operational support. The project intervention will improve access to basic WASH facilities through the identification of critical gaps and providing WASH assistance in particular areas with a high number of IDPs and areas with anticipated significant inward displacement.
TRC is planning to respond to three main GAP with this project;
(1) Water Supply: Access to WASH services will be facilitated by increasing the basic living facilities of those in need, especially in the identified areas.
(2) Operational support to sanitation/sewage: After the earthquake, the spread of common diseases that will arise especially due to the increase in the population in the region will be prevented. It will improve access to humanitarian life-saving/emergency WASH facilities and services, including strengthening Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) measures.
(3) Distribution of Hygiene Kits with supported Hygiene Promotions: A sustainability of hygiene in the region will be ensured. In this way, the health sector, which is associated with WASH activities, will also be supported indirectly.
Safeguarding and protection principles, including the Do No Harm approach, will guide the TRC team throughout all the phases of the project cycle. TRC is fully committed to the SPHERE standards and humanitarian principles based on which TRC’s internal policies and procedures have been created to guide project implementation.
Accountability to the affected population will be realized through strengthening community-based complaint mechanisms, improved participatory assessments and enhanced outreach through the TRC Field team.
TRC will implement this project in close coordination with various stakeholders on the ground for an effective response, including the Local Councils and relevant UNOCHA Clusters.
