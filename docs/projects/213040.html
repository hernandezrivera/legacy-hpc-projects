Project Summary

Syria continues to face a multitude of challenges that make it one of the world’s most complex humanitarian and protection emergencies. After twelve years of crisis, most
Syrians live in a context of continuing humanitarian decline with 15.3 million people requiring humanitarian assistance, including 2.1 million IDPs living in last-resort IDP sites, with needs that keep increasing across all sectors. The country still has the largest number of internally displaced people in the world: 6.8 million people, and the largest number of people in need since the beginning of the crisis. Humanitarian and economic
indicators continue to deteriorate, basic services are 2023 Humanitarian Needs Overview assessments (HNO), According to key findings of "REACH Initiative" on Humanitarian Situation Overview report in Syria (HSOS) - Deir ez-Zor, it clearly mentioned consistent barriers to accessing basic Goods and services in northeast Syria (NES). Reported barriers for services and basic goods included difficulties in reaching health facilities and
markets and increasing disruptions of water and electricity networks. To meet their basic needs, families reportedly had to travel to nearby communities or pay for alternative services. People of Deir ez-Zor require humanitarian assistance, some of which are in acute need due to a convergence of vulnerabilities resulting from displacement, exposure to hostilities and limited access to basic goods and services The framework for the
Protection and assistance provided to IDPs, returnees and affected populations is the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) We especially mention the of protection The activities of Syrian Al-Yamama and its partners are aimed at supporting the Government's efforts to reduce vulnerabilities, with attention to the most vulnerable groups.
