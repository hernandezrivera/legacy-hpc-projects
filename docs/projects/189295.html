Project Summary

The year 2022 has seen more people in need of humanitarian assistance estimated at 8.9M South Sudan than last last. This is due to the devastating effects of last year's flooding, continued skirmishes of insecurity from armed groups and communal conflicts arising from tribal clashes due to cattle ruslting and economical difficulities arising from the effects of Covid-19 pandemic . Similarly, HealthNet TPO expects widened gaps in delivering essential health care services across its current project areas since the number of health facilities it has been supporting with funding from its donor- HPF, will be reduced to 56 from 91 due to funding gaps from the donors pooling funds into the HPF. From April 2022, this reduction will leave very many communities vulnerable especially in their quest to accessing essential maternal child health, sexual and reproductive health care services and mental health and psychosocial support services. In order to meet the health needs of these vulnerable communities, HNTPO proposes a 3 -way approach to increase access to essential health care services including curative and preventive services in the communities through mobile clinics, providing basic mental health and psychosocial care support and clinical management of victims of sexual violence and rape witll be managed from. Additonally, HealthNet TPO (HNTPO) intends to support CEmONC services in Gok Machar PHCC of Aweil North to serve Aweil North and Aweil West County Communities since the two counties do not have a county hospial or a CEmONC center in either of them. These interventions will contribute to achieving the over-all South Sudan health cluster 2022's objective of decreasing excess morbidity and mortality rates from illnesses, such as malaria, diarrhea, acute respiratory infections, and vaccine preventable illnesses, such as measles, among 50,000 (inclussive of 3,075 people with disability) people in need comprising of 8500 children under 5 years of age, 15,500 children 5-17 years of age, 21,500 adults of 18-60 years of age and 4,500 elderly people beyond 60 years of age people in need, in the targeted communities of Terekeka, Aweil North, Aweil West and Raja counties of South Sudan by end of 2022.

1 - Needs

At present, the health systems in South Sudan is weak, mostly hard to reach and insecure communities hardly access basic live-saving services. Perennial flooding, cattle rustling and communal conflicts have has displaced communities and perished their livelihoods, thus worsening the situation by causing massive deaths, injury and disability as a consequence of increased susceptibility to hunger, diseases, trauma and mental illnesses due to inadequate accessibility to health, nutrition, WASH and mental health care services. In HealthNet TPO’s current areas of operation (Terekeka, Raja, Aweil North and Aweil West Counties), only the Health Pooled Fund (HPF) supported Health Facilities are functional but are also not able to provide a complete package of services as required by the BPHNS guidelines of South Sudan due to funding gaps. A total of 61 health facilities from Raja, Terekeka, Aweil North and Aweil West counties which are not supported under HPF 3 funding have ceased functioning due to lack of supplies, skilled staff and limited general support from the government. The number of unsupported health facilities will increase in year 4 (April 2022- March 2023) to 83 as HPF will drop support to additional 22 health facilities in the above-mentioned locations. These facilities are mostly located in distant and extremely hard to reach locations. Communities in the catchment of the unsupported and non-functional facilities are isolated and experience frequent tribal fighting and extreme weather conditions (floods and drought). The situation is compounded by high morbidity levels with many treatable illnesses observed among children under 5-years-old, mostly malaria, respiratory infections and diarrhea and malnutrition.In addition, the few accessible health facilities have inadequate health workers presently in facilities, some communities are located in extremely hard to reach and insecure areas with no health facilities and many communities often do not feel safe to trek long distances and hours to access the health services from the existing health facilities thus affecting health seeking behaviors and put many vulnerable populations especially women, children, disabled and the elderly at high risk of morbidity and mortality. Further, young girls and women often are victims of rape while trekking the long, insecure roads given the fluidity of security in these areas. To respond to these needs in a holistic approach, HNTPO recommend a 4-way approach to increase access to essential primary health care services with emphasis on maternal child, SGBV/CMR and MHPSS services. These approaches include;1. Establish mobile clinic teams in Terekeka, Aweil North and Aweil West counties to extend services to the communities that are distant and hard to reach, and fill gaps created by HPF dropping support to the health facilities in those communities, in addition to strengthening referral pathways in the isolated communities for continuum of care2. Establish a Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (CEmONC) center in Gok-Machar PHCC (Aweil North County) by equipping the operating theatre and recruiting skilled personnel for the Centre. 3. Establish a unit for Case Management of Rape Survivors in Raja, Terekeka, Aweil North and Aweil West Counties. 4. Establish a unit for delivery of Minimum Service Package (MSP) in Mental Health and Psychosocial Support Services in Raja Hospital, Terekeka, Aweil North and Aweil West Counties.Below are the reference for assessed needs:- DHIS-2 (Ministry of Health) South Sudan- Humanitarian Data Exchange (humdata.org) South Sudan- Global Health Observatory WHO South Sudan (who.int)- UNICEF Child-Related SDG Progress Assessment for South Sudan - UNICEF DATA- Step up, don’t scale down | UNICEF South Sudan report - Situation reports | UNICEF South Sudan- South Sudan: Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022 Humanitarian Response

2 - Project activities

This intervention will contribute to reduction of excess morbidity and mortality in the targeted vulnerable communities, thereby complementing what is being done in health facilities in the communities through HPF support. Activities will broadly fall into the priority activities of the sector and they will be a combination of curative consultations, preventive interventions, emergency response interventions which will be implemented in the targeted communities. Specifically, provision of life-saving essential healthcare services including management of non-communicable diseases through mobile healthcare services, procurement of essential medical kits and training of health workers. Epidemic disease surveillance, prevention and control including outbreak detection, investigation and response. Community health service provision linking with facility-based services using initiatives such as Boma Health Initiatives and ICCM. IPC in health facilities through regular assessment using score cards, provision of supplies and protocols and continuous monitoring of WASH in health facilities enhancing referral pathways between the communities and static health facilities for critical conditions. The project will specifically: 1). 3 Mobile clinics will be established so as to reach hardly inaccessible communities especially those displaced by floods, in Payams where health facilities are non-functional. An integrated services package including antenatal, postnatal, immunization, curative consultations and growth monitoring, nutrition screening and health awareness sessions will be provided in the mobile clinics. Linkages will be established with the nearest PHCCs and hospitals where severe cases will be referred for further management and appropriate care provided. Women in labour presenting in the mobile clinics will be referred to functioning PHCCs for skilled birth services, and a CEmONC Centre supported in Gok Machar PHCC will provide emergency C-Sections and blood transfusions to communities in Aweil North and West Counties. These services will be provided at no cost to all age groups. 2). Immunisation services for children will be provided in the targeted communities to ensure that every child is reached. All antigens in the Expanded program on Immunisation according to South Sudan guideline will be available and provided as per the agreed schedule. Cold-chains established in the fixed sites will provide the mobile clinics teams with the vaccines in cool boxes and vaccines carriers. At the community level, Boma Health Workers will be helpful in defaulter tracing and referral to the nearest mobile clinic points and immunisation centres3). Using the essential medicines list of South Sudan, a list of essential commodities including psychotropics will be procured for health care services provided through the proposed service points. Procurement of the medical commodities will follow the procurement policies of the donor in consideration with organisational policies. Mechanisms for rational use will be instituted and stock closely monitored to ensure that stock ruptures are not minimised during the project period. 4). Medical and paramedical staff in mobile clinics and facilities within the targeted communities will be trained and refresher trained on various health topics including, Clinical management of rape and mh-GAP in MHPSS. Beyond trainings, mentorship, coaching and supportive supervisions will further be used to enhance staff capacity. 5). During mobile clinics, health education will be provided on daily basis on various topics. Other health awareness sessions and campaigns will be organised to promote health and prevent diseases. 6). Surveillance mechanisms will also be instituted so to monitor the trend of epidemic prone diseases. Friendly and private spaces will be created within the 3 PHCCs and Hospitals to provide care counselling support for victims of sexual violence and MHPSS care

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

In addition to the above consultative methods to collect beneficiaries’ feedback, other feedback mechanisms such as suggestion boxes will also be used. These feedbacks will be analysed to assess quality, satisfaction and evaluate if the project is not putting more harm on the beneficiaries and their communities. The information collected will continuously be analysed to idnditify gaps, level of satisfaction to quality of range of services provided which subsequently will be used for decision making that involves where necessary, the beneficiary communities esepcially with regards to the discussions on how they would want the project to be redesigned so it can be specific to addressing the pressingtheir needs or how it can be re-strategised to improve its quality to the users with sepecific attention to gender, age and disability status needs. Continuous analysis of the feedbacks will ensure that the project stays current and evolves according to the ever-changing demands and humanitarian needs of vulnerable communities and therefore aligning it to reality.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

HealthNet TPO in its programs ensures that there is promotion of gender equality and social inclusion by designing programs that are responsive to the specific needs of the vulnerable groups including women, girls, children, elderly and people with disabilities. In addition, recruitments are done with gender equality in mind and projects strive to encourage women participation in implementation of activities. Data on project activities will be collected and disaggregated by sex, age and disability status. In ensuring feedback from the most vulnerable groups, no discrimination policy will be at every service point and equal presentation from the vulnerable groups during consultative, management committee and review meetings at mobile clinic sites will be expected. Similarly, review Meetings and community-based activities will be open to organisations representing women, PWD, and other vulnerable groups. Mobile clinic sites will further be established close to beneficiaries thus minimizing access constraints faced by who is not in condition to freely move because of physical, social and economic dependance, as women and PWD offering these people the opportunity to provide their feedback and inputs. Suggestion boxes will be fixed in place places where women and PWD feel free to provide their feedbacks without fear. An anonymous patient exit survey will be administered in a way that makes it accessible to all kind of patients independently on their education background and without requiring time wasting and further movements.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

HNTPO has in place integrity policies including the PSEA policy. Information PSEA within the organisation starts with ensuring that its staff and stakeholders that do business with the organization such as suppliers and contractors are provided with the policy and they sign to ensuring that they observe and adhere to the guidelines provided for, in the policy of PSEA. The staff then act as champions in promoting PSEA and raising awareness in the communities about PSEA and how to report in case it happens. Continuously awareness is done to the beneficiaries who use services at service points. IEC materials are further used to drive the point on PSEA. The integration of PSEA information in this framework will be central to ensure the beneficiaries are protected from exploitation

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Information about the project will be provided to the communities through existing community structures including local leaderships and during consultative meetings with beneficiary communities through cultural acceptable modalities. The same mechanism will be used to get feedback and complaints from the beneficiary communities. HNTPO through its present projects has already maintained interface with communities and their representatives such as Health management Committees, chiefs at all levels in the counties, organized groups and community based organisations, who are normally invited to take part in routine program review meetings. Across project sites, client satisfaction and quality of services is also assessed through administering randomized patient exit interviews without discrimination to sex, age and disability status without and analysing the results. Additionally, HNTPO also supports a network of Boma Health workers through the Boman health initiative (BHI) program of the government and this network can be used to disseminate information about the project. User group meetings and dialogues will also be organised so that feedbacks can be provided to the communities. Other means of disseminating information to communities will be through heatlh education and awareness sessions at mobile clinic sites, health facilities, schools, churches and public gatherings.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Essential health cares including integrated Mobile clinic service provision will be close to communities, free of charge. Community representatives and mobiliser will provide authorities with information on where people with special needs can be accessed and how they should be protected. Right based approach will inform capacity building/training activities for health staff and authorities, for all patients being attended, independently on their belonging. Vulnerable communities will be involved in the design implementation and monitoring of the project so to promote acceptance of the project and ownership. Regular feedback through suggestion boxes and meetings will be organized to understand if there are any grievances to the services and service provisions so that remedies can be derived while involving the communities. Mechanisms for community involvement in health system are in place but members are mainly men, as per traditional habits. HNTPO will promote gender balanced composition of health committees through community wide sensitization. Women inclusion will create a virtuous circle of increasing awareness on the importance of all groups contributing to health system running. Further, health authorities and staff will be advised and assisted in taking advantage of each contact with beneficiaries to ask and get inputs on activities/support responsiveness and acceptability. For instance, patient exit survey will be administered at the same place/during the same moment people are getting services, without discrimination. Further, services increase the number of contacts between staff and beneficiaries and expand the area these beneficiaries belong to and therefore the possibility for them to offer their feedback will be enhanced. HNTPO has worked for many years with targeted Counties health authorities, providing technical assistance and mentorship for the in delivery of health care services. This constitute a solid ground to prevent external interference in the identification of project beneficiary communities and mobile clinic sites, which will take place through a widely conducted field needs assessment, with the direct involvement of local health authorities and community representatives. The project is likely to gather beneficiary information such as age, sex, marital status, medical conditions, health services received on different intervals in the life of the project. Based on the Data Protection Impact Assessment, HNTPO’s risk mitigation plan on data protection will minimize any risks related to data processing.

12.1. Does your project contribute to any of the three Collective Outcomes, i.e. Improved Food security; Enhanced support for prevention and response to needs of survivors of Gender-Based Violence; and Flood displacement?

Yes

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

The project will support essential integrated health services, promoting them as entry point for protection ones, especially for women and children and people with disabilities. The project will enhance accessibility to health services by establishing mobile clinic points as much closer to beneficiaries as possible while maintain a reliable referral mechanism, overcoming those geographical/economical/social barriers which constrains vulnerable people and groups and access to services. The project also intends to provide clinical management of rape to survivors of rape besides providing psychosocial support to the victims and clinical management for mental health diseases in the targeted communities. Staff in selected PHCCs and hospitals will be trained on clinical management of rape guideline and also on mhGAP for non- psychiatric health professionals working in the health facilities and they will be provided with the necessary tools including drugs to provide services. HNTPO and health authorities will ensure awareness raising an as integral part of the part of the project while coordinating and cooperating with actors in the protection sector and stakeholders, therefore, creating and effective management platform for cases including effective referral pathways

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The project will contribute to minimizing displacement by guaranteeing the provision of essential health care services in the areas affected by due to flooding emergencies if the situation can still allow people to live in the flooded areas. By taking services to communities affected by floods, affected people will not leave their communities for others due to lack of access to essential health care services. Thus, making people to further develop resilience mechanisms.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

This process is entirely on health that it does not directly address food security, but, it will contribute to minimizing the immediate effects in terms of malnutrition and disease and its complication. At service points, growth monitoring and nutrition screening using MUAC will be conducted on children 6-59 months of age and for pregnant and lactating women during EPI, antenatal and postnatal care services such that those identified with malnutrition from screening will be referred for malnutrition treatment centers (SAM and MAM) within the targeted communities. This way, an integrated approach in programming will be effective since health services will be the entry point to nutrition services for malnourished children and PLWs, who will be timely identified and transferred to receive the due care. Further, health providers will include nutrition related topics into the health education sessions program, contributing to malnutrition prevention. Clinial management of rape for survivors of rape will be provided in the targeted communities inadditonal to proviidng psychosocial support for victims and those undergoing stressful moments as a result of being affected by floods.
