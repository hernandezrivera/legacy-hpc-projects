Project Summary

The project will be implemented in partnership with Anando and DSK. Anando will manage WASH services in specific areas within the camps, covering blocks A, B, and C of Camp 24, subblocks A4, A5, B6, and B7 of Camp 25, as well as blocks A and C of Camp 27. ANANDO will take over full responsibility for block A camp 27 from TDH. Meanwhile, DSK will be responsible for the operation and maintenance of the RO plant and will oversee WASH facilities in blocks D, E, and F of Camp 24. Together, the partners aim to sustain and enhance essential WASH infrastructure across Rohingya refugee camps 24, 25, and 27 in Teknaf, Cox’s Bazar. This will involve regular operation, maintenance, and targeted upgrades to core facilities, including surface water treatment plants, advanced reverse osmosis units, plastic recycling plants, and material recovery facilities to support sustainable waste management. The project has the provision of emergency water trucking if required. Essential sanitation infrastructure, such as latrines, bathing cubicles, community kitchens, and handwashing stations, will also be prioritized. A comprehensive needs assessment will guide the project's approach to upgrading and, where necessary, constructing new WASH facilities to meet the growing population's evolving needs. Gender sensitivity is integral to the project’s strategy, with hygiene promotion efforts tailored specifically for women and adolescent girls, including menstrual hygiene management (MHM) support. To encourage sustainable hygiene behaviors, the project will employ innovative, behavior-focused initiatives like the RANAS model and the “Mum's Magic Hands” campaign. The project emphasizes the active participation and empowerment of female and adolescent girls within user groups for WASH facilities, ensuring their needs and perspectives are represented. All facility designs will undergo community consultation to address the specific needs of persons with disabilities, the elderly, lactating mothers, and young children. Gender-segregated latrines and bathing cubicles will be installed to uphold dignity and privacy. Community engagement will also be central to promoting sustainable waste management practices. Through collective responsibility initiatives, the project will introduce efficient desludging methods to improve as well as maintain sanitary conditions and establish recycling mechanisms to minimize environmental impact. Camp cleaning campaigns and observances of key events—such as World Water Day, World Toilet Day, and Menstrual Hygiene Day—will foster a culture of shared responsibility for health and hygiene. About 26% of the total budget will be allocated for volunteer incentives, adhering to the guidelines for volunteer allowances and incentives. The project will adhere strictly to sector guidelines and standards, integrating cross-cutting issues such as protection, gender sensitivity, accountability to affected populations, and humanitarian principles. To ensure safe and high-quality programming, benchmarks will be established within a robust MEAL framework, including the development of monitoring tools, functional and non-functional assessments, and NEAT+, KAP surveys. Collaboration with key stakeholders will be vital to the project's success. The project team will coordinate closely with the WASH sector, cross-sector focal points, area focal agencies, other WASH actors, camp administration, and relevant stakeholders to align efforts and share resources. Project staff will actively participate in sectoral meetings across WASH platforms, with a focus on capacity building through targeted training sessions and workshops facilitated by experienced resource persons. This holistic approach aims to foster a healthier, more resilient camp environment that respects health, dignity, and sustainable practices in WASH services.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The humanitarian needs in Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh, are especially acute compared to other regions in the country. According to the latest joint registration by the Government of Bangladesh (GoB) and UNHCR, there are now over 981,000 Rohingya individuals residing in 33 camps in the Ukhia and Teknaf areas of Cox’s Bazar district and Bhasan Char. Since arriving to the Teknaf camps Rohingya refugees face water scarcity due to ground water declining, leading to increase public health risk. Access to clean water and proper sanitation facilities is severely limited, increasing the risk of disease outbreaks. Cox’s Bazar camp areas are prone to different natural disasters like flash flood, cyclones and landslide. Also, in the recent times fire outbreaks caused heavy damage to refugee lives and properties. Women, girls, and children are more vulnerable and exposed to different protection risks while accessing services and resulting in limited mobility, and rights to practice. The needs assessment reveals significant gaps in WASH services, emphasizing the urgent requirement for gender-sensitive, inclusive facilities and infrastructure in the targeted camps 24, 25, and 27. Still, concern is raised by women, they feel a lack of privacy and security for using latrines and bathing cubicles, especially for women and girls, who face heightened risks of harassment and violence, particularly at night. There is a critical need for gender-segregated WASH facilities with enhanced safety features, such as solar streetlights and strategically located latrines and bathing cubicles. Menstrual hygiene management (MHM) resources are insufficient, necessitating the distribution of dignity kits to meet the needs of women and adolescent girls. safe water crisis leads to impacting safe water access, and semi-permanent sanitation facilities require frequent maintenance to ensure functionality and meet community demand with a downward trend of funding. Waste management practices through engaging the community are limited, contributing to environmental contamination, while fecal sludge disposal remains unsustainable without designated advanced treatment facilities. Still, there is a gap in upgrading WASH infrastructure, improving hygiene practices through behavior-focused initiatives, and ensuring that community feedback mechanisms inform ongoing improvements. The host community of Nilla Union needs safe drinking water and a proper sanitation system findings come from JMSNA.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project, as a humanitarian agency, is dedicated to delivering high-quality outcomes aligned with its goals and budget, guided by humanitarian principles and prioritizing support to the most vulnerable. Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) will be a cornerstone of the project, ensuring the highest benefit to both Rohingya refugees and host communities by centering their needs and perspectives at every stage. To support this, a comprehensive Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) will be established and actively maintained, ensuring that quality, accountability, sustainability, and transparency are embedded throughout the Project Cycle Management phases—planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation. This mechanism will empower affected communities to provide meaningful feedback, fostering continuous improvement and responsiveness, and ensuring that the project fulfills its ethical responsibility with a clear focus on inclusive, transparent, and people-centered outcomes.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

As implementing partners, ANANDO and DSK are fully committed to integrating their Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) guidelines at every stage of project implementation. This commitment ensures the highest standards of moral and ethical conduct, promoting a safe, respectful environment for all beneficiaries and staff. To reinforce these standards, WHH, as the leading organization, will conduct regular oversight and follow-up to ensure PSEA practices are firmly rooted in field operations. This collaboration will embed a culture of accountability, vigilance, and zero tolerance toward any form of exploitation or abuse. Through training, awareness-raising, and clear reporting channels, all staff and stakeholders will be empowered to uphold these essential protections, ensuring PSEA principles are not only policies but also integral to daily operations. This approach safeguards the dignity and rights of all community members and reinforces trust between the project teams and those they serve.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The project will integrate climate action as a core element in addressing Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), Disaster Risk Management (DRM), and pressing environmental challenges within the assigned Rohingya refugee camps in Teknaf. To promote climate adaptation and mitigation, the project will establish a warehouse at camp level, prepositioning the stock, staff capacity on DRR in WASH, construct disaster-resilient WASH infrastructure including Material Recovery Facility (MRF) and a plastic recycling plant, improving water network, contributing to waste reduction and sustainable resource use. Additionally, climate-responsive initiatives will include toilet desludging services, tie downing, site protection of WASH facilities to ensure safe sanitation practices and regular hygiene promotion sessions to build awareness around climate resilience. These combined efforts aim to reduce environmental impact, promote healthier living conditions, and empower the community with practical tools for adapting to and mitigating climate change effects.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

The project has established a comprehensive Emergency Preparedness and Response Plan (ERP) to ensure preparedness and effective action in crises. Implementing partner staff from DSK and ANANDO are active members of the camp-level Disaster Management Committee (DMC), engaging proactively and ready to respond immediately in any emergency. At the organizational level, both implementing partners have an Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) Committee. In the event of a triggered emergency, this committee is automatically activated to coordinate and execute all necessary actions, ensuring swift and organized responses that prioritize the safety and needs of affected communities.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

The project’s WASH initiatives prioritize inclusivity, engaging beneficiaries across different age groups, including youth. Youth and adolescent groups will play an active role throughout the project, contributing to various activities and enhancing project outcomes. Male youth will be involved in regular operations and maintenance, construction tasks, and community mobilization efforts, helping to sustain and expand WASH services. Female youth will primarily focus on leading hygiene promotion sessions, with a particular emphasis on menstrual hygiene management (MHM), and will work to raise awareness across different user groups, including the WASH Management Committee. This targeted involvement empowers young people, especially female youth, to be key contributors in fostering a healthier, more informed community.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

The project has a plan to connect with the BMZ-funded project within the same area of this project in 2025 to enhance the resilience of Rohingya refugees by providing livelihood opportunities and transferable skills development across various trades, strengthening the CSOs, including WASH management committee, DMC, Community protection group and increasing social cohesion for peacebuilding. This collaboration aims to equip refugees with practical skills they can utilize upon their eventual return to Myanmar, fostering long-term self-reliance and adaptability. The development linkage will ensure in terms of humanitarian, development, and peace nexus approach.
