Project Summary

The current situation of WASH in communities affected by the crisis in the South West region is deplorable as over 530.000 people were displaced from the SWR and NWR. According to the Chief of Service of Water for the SW region, almost 80% of streams/rivers are polluted by surface runoff. However, affected populations that depend on these water sources receive less than half the amount of adequate aqua tabs for purification. Fako and meme divisions which are host to some 340,000 IDPs is challenged with poor WASH services and facilities. According to the WASH cluster, Only a total number of 34 WASH activities were implemented in the NWSW reaching 11,247. This leaves over 489,000 un met needs in 2019
(4,235), water supply (4,027 persons), WASH NFI kit distribution (2,565), sanitation (418) and
WASH training (2). The key constraints continue to be limited access to funding. Women and girls are more vulnerable. Young girls of school going age go without basic hygiene and sanitation, with them facing serious challenge on menstrual hygiene and water points are not regularly clean. The recent cholera outbreak in the South West region is an indication of the urgent need to steup WASH services in the two regions. With the absence of clean water, girls of school going age are challenged with menstrual hygiene management (https://www.journalducameroun.com/en/cameroon-cholera-outbreak-kills-two-in-south-west-region/) is an indication of poor WASH services and facilities . The practice of open defecation in the divisions makes the people especially women and girls vulnerable to water-borne diseases. There is therefore an urgent need for humanitarian intervention in this division and region as the cholera outbreak is liable to affect more women and girls as they use water more for washing, cleaning and drinking. The need for safe and affordable WASH services is therefore of utmost priority and necessity. This project intervention is aimed at protecting people(women and girls as direct beneficiaries) from water,sanitation and hygiene related diseases
Our current Project targets 45,000 IDPs (70%) and Host communities(30%) with WASH services desegregated into sex and disability

Besoins / Needs

As per the MSNA assessment conducted by IOM in 2019, 489,000 IDPs and Host communities are in urgent need of WASH service in the North West and South West Regions. On the basis of the above, our project is aimed at using a community based approach and people centered strategy to reach out to some 45,000 IDPs and Host communities with WASH services in 2020. our focus will be on access to clean drinking water, awareness raising on hygiene and sanitation in communities and public places including schools and health centres
