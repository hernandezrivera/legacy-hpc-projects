Project Summary

To improve the effectiveness and efficiency of humanitarian health interventions by strengthening coordination and emergency preparedness through better information management, leading the health humanitarian response and dissemination of relevant and quality information to sector partners. The goal is to ensure that the highest number of people have access to lifesaving healthcare across the oPt, either through direct service delivery and/or advocacy towards duty bearers.

Needs

The health sector has faced a number of acute emergency events against the background of an extremely fragile health system, which has affected resulted in the loss of lives and an increased burden of disease. For most of 2018, as a result of the mass demonstrations, hospitals and frontline health workers were faced with a weekly influx of trauma casualties. There was also a large increase in attacks on health workers and facilities, in Gaza; meanwhile in the West Bank access for ambulances, and other essential services, including mobile health clinics, continue to face multiple barriers in delivering basic healthcare. As the humanitarian situation deteriorates, the ability for the health sector to maintain its preparedness and resilience diminishes. A rapid analysis by the Health Cluster towards the end of 2018, showed that only 5 partners had the capacity or the resources to preposition lifesaving supplies. Furthermore, within the public health sector, basic disposables and medical supplies remained at low supply, leaving doctors, nurses and paramedics no choice but to improvise. In the West Bank, the closure of UNRWA’s mobile health clinics serving some of the most vulnerable communities in Area C, continues to jeopardise the health status of vulnerable groups, particularly pregnant mothers, children under 5 suffering from malnutrition, and the elderly. Not only does the closure of these clinics impact health, but it also closes the entry points for those who are victims of gender-based violence and domestic abuse. As health partners across the oPt upscale their response, the funding stream has not matched the growing humanitarian needs. However, there is a continued effort amongst partners to be more effective and efficient through enhanced coordination and enhanced information. Within this context, the Health Cluster has played a critical role; in 2018, the Health Cluster established an emergency response plan to the mass demonstrations, in addition to establishing the Gaza Trauma Working Group, Mobile Health Clinics Working Group and supporting the deployment of national and international emergency medical teams. Through support to over 30 active partners working through the recent emergency, the Health Cluster was able to support over 20,000 frontline emergency consultations, over 40,000 rehabilitation sessions, and over 1,300 early reconstructive surgery in response to the mass demonstrations. In Area C, the Health Cluster enhanced the coordination of 18 mobile health teams across 10 partners ensuring that over 332,442 people have access to basic primary health care. However, as the situation continues to deteriorate the need to sustain and upgrade coordination and information services for health is paramount.

Activities

The Health Cluster will aim to enhance the response of over 60 partner agencies. Key activities such as the monthly meetings, 4Ws, monitoring of health indicators, and setting of minimum standards for health, will continue to take place. In addition to this, the Health Cluster will focus its activities on key areas that help drive an operational response, particularly through inter-sectorial coordination and linkages. The activities are split into oPt, Gaza, West Bank. Across the oPt, the Health Cluster will focus its activities on: 1. Development of emergency preparedness capacity for health partners. This would include systematic mapping, training on emergency preparedness, in addition to joint emergency response interventions. 2. Situational Reports issued routinely. 3. Development of a Health Cluster Contingency Plan to ensure a timely response to emergencies, whether it is conflict-related or outbreak prevention. 4. Health Cluster will work closely with the partners and the MoH to hand-over health service to the local authority. One example of where this is already taking place is the mobile health clinics. 5. Strategic advisory group: The Health Cluster will establish a Strategic Advisory group consisting of health partners, in order to strengthen linkages with other clusters. 6. The Health Cluster will work jointly with the Protection Cluster, WHO and the ICRC to promote monitoring for attacks against healthcare, including barriers to accessing healthcare. This will enhance the protection of healthcare staff as well as the beneficiaries. 9. The Health Cluster will conduct health needs assessments, by mainstreaming geographical analysis using GIS, focusing on key areas, such as trauma needs, Area C and East Jerusalem. 10. Resource Mobilisation activities will take place, primarily through quarterly briefings targeting donors; alongside disseminating information to highlight the impact of shortages in funding. 11. Gender and disability mainstreaming will take place through a dedicated gender and disability focal point in order to support partners to improve gender/ disability reporting. 12. Enhance community engagement in health through the active participation of beneficiaries during monthly project visits. In addition to the above, in Gaza, the Health Cluster will focus its activities on the following areas:1. The Gaza Trauma Working Group will continue its efforts to establish key standards for trauma care across the trauma pathway, for all key players including ICRC and MSF. The Trauma Group will enhance the: Patient Allocation Tool (PAT), EMTs calendar, and monitoring of emergency medical teams. 2. In 2018, the Health Cluster piloted the HeRAMS, an approach to monthly monitoring of health facilities and resources availability. The aim is to expand the HeRAMS as a monitoring tool that also encompasses NGO facilities. The Health Cluster will work closely with the WASH cluster to share data and key indicators (electricity etc). In addition to the activities listed under oPt, in the West Bank, the Health Cluster will also focus its activities on the following key areas: 1. ‘Mobile Health Clinics in Area C’ Working Group will continue the regular meetings. In close coordination with the MoH, the Group will establish a monitoring tool. This monitoring tool will capture key issues, such as the prevalence of children with malnutrition. 2. The Health Cluster will also conduct a joint assessment with key players on understanding the barriers to healthcare in East Jerusalem. This study will focus on identifying areas where local NGOs, supported by the UN and donor community, can provide essential health care.
