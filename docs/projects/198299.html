Project Summary

The Norwegian Refugee Council through its Information Counselling and Legal Assistance (ICLA) and General Protection programming seek to address needs of IDPs, host communities, returnees and other vulnerable individuals by increasing access to protection services, basic essential services and securing Housing Land and Property (HLP) rights through improved security of tenure to conflict affected population in Northeast Nigeria, Nigeria.
NRC being the HLP Sub-Sector co-lead will coordinate and strengthen HLP services and structures for the target communities through series of coordination meetings, community engagement, initiatives and events. In order to achieve that, logistical support will be provided to dispute resolution centres/structures and the land ministry to facilitate their work. The project intends to enhance capacity of local authorities and community leaders on HLP and collaborative dispute resolution so they can uphold basic human rights of IDPs, returnees and host communities. Furthermore, the absence of civil administration in hard-to-reach LGAs pose a challenge in terms of accessibility to basic essential services especially for the physically disabled and other vulnerable groups. Non State Armed groups (NSAG) in north-east Nigeria continue intensely across villages, communities, and towns and this has led to more displacement. Camp closure and return plan by the Borno State Government has potentially exert pressure on existing facilities and structures both at return locations as well as areas of integration which had led to reduced access to essential services especially for IDPs and returnees. Cases of evictions have been reported, and it is likely that the trend will continue into 2023, with the most vulnerable households at risk of eviction and other related HLP right violations.

NRC’s Protection from Violence team aims to contribute to increasing the rights and capacity of the conflict affected populations to prevent protection risks and threats. Specialized protection activities will be provided to improve the well-being and strengthen their resilience, protection, and dignity of the conflict-affected population in BAY States. The NRC protection project will target vulnerable individuals from displaced and host communities to benefit from the specialized protection services that respond to their needs. This project will contribute to Protection Sector’s response objective to; a) to mitigate and respond to the harm caused to persons who have suffered violence, coercion, exploitation, serious neglect, or discrimination, and to restore their capacity to live safe and dignified lives, and b) to strengthen targeted protection services to address the needs and vulnerability of persons at heightened risks of violence, exploitation, serious neglect, or discrimination; enhance their capacities and reduce the risk of resorting to negative coping mechanisms.

1 - Needs

The protracted nature of the conflict, coupled with Borno State Government-led returns, flooding and high cost of living, has exacerbated the displacement of vulnerable conflict affected population in the BAY States. Secondary displacement, destruction of HLP assets, forced evictions, and disputes between original owners and secondary occupants which are the major drivers of HLP needs. These issues expose the most vulnerable to significant protection risks that threaten their dignity, physical well-being, health and living standards. Approximately 1,05 million people are in need of HLP support across the BAY states, out of which 280,118 people are targeted by the HLP AoR in 2023, including 150,513 women, 164,358 children and 13,680 Persons with Disabilities (HNO 2023). Moreover, challenges of access to HLP rights for the affected population groups revolve around lack of access to land / farmland, evictions, living in poor housing, congestion and conflicts over land, among others. Some locations of return, relocation and integration lack the basic essential services, housing facilities, farmland and the affected communities are unable to claim and exercise their rights, especially in garrison towns in Borno State. Evictions are severe, with female-headed households having difficulty in accessing and paying for rental accommodation, especially more in Borno than Adamawa and Yobe States. Eviction cases continue to grow, the cases identified can only be viewed as proportion of the cases, as many remain undocumented and unresolved. The challenges are generally compounded by lack of awareness, illiteracy and restrictive religious and cultural practices that affect different members of the community. The traditional dispute resolution structures in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe are existent but require support for capacity in order to adjudicate justice in an efficient, fair and transparent manner. The ongoing conflict in Northeast Nigeria continue to put people at risk as the situation remains fragile, leading to increased protections concerns and weakened rule of law especially for the vulnerable population. There is lack of basic services being provided which leads affected population to resort to negative coping mechanisms. The Protection Sector assesses an increase in general protection, CP, GBV, and HLP needs across 2023. According to the PSNE 2023 Response Plan, the general protection under the protection sector response aims to target 365,000 people with protection needs with the highest severity ranking as per the Humanitarian Needs Overview. The NRC protection responses will contribute to reaching the target population and will focus on the vulnerable and at risk due to conflict related displacement. NRC will address the protection risks and threats faced by people affected by displacement (IDPs), host communities and returnees) in both camp and non-camp settings. The overall objective of NRC Protection activities is to contribute in the improvement of safety, dignity and protection of rights of conflict-affected populations in BAY States. -

2 - Activities

NRC's ICLA will establish local coordination points through local partners most especially in the areas of return/relocation. Through the HLP Sub-Sector it will strengthen the capacity of the existing dispute resolution mechanism (both formal & informal) in material and technical capacity to be able to respond to HLP disputes arising in the places of return and relocation. It will carry out provision of information/awareness on HLP rights and legal identity, provision of counselling services on HLP and legal identity. It will support beneficiaries with processing of HLP documentation including certificates of occupancy for returnees and IDPs with land allocation to secure their land title and prevent future dispute on those land. Special consideration shall be given to those benefiting from permanent shelter construction by Shelter partners as this will assist in the achievement of durable solution. Also, through the sub-sector, it will continue close monitoring of informal settlements and any likely additional to mitigate against surprising eviction incidence. ICLA team will strengthen the capacity of line ministries and legal identity structures through provision of material and logistical support to enhance its capacity to be able to response to issues and it will propose to establish and donate a dispute resolution centre to the line ministries. Through the protection activities, the conflict-affected communities in Nigeria are aimed to be protected from violence, abuse, coercion and deprivation and are physically and psychologically safer. Strong advocacy and coordination will also be NRC’s commitment in North East Nigeria. NRC’s protection objective to contribute in the improvement of safety, dignity and protection of rights of conflict-affected populations will be achieved through standalone and integrated protection responses such as community-based protection approach, direct service provision, establishing protection information management systems for evidence-based advocacy use and development of self-protection strategies aiming to prevent the violence in conflict affected communities. In 2023, the NRC Protection team will provide case management services, individual protection assistance, internal and external referrals, capacity building of key stakeholders through training on protection topics. It will also carry out protection monitoring and establish or strengthen the existing community-based protection structures. NRC will provide case management services. Based on individual assessment, NRC may provide the following basic psychological first aid, tailored ad-hoc assistance to meet the specific needs or referral to other NRC internal core competencies or to external partners providing services in the target area. NRC will also provide individual protection assistance (IPA). IPA support is a one-time off assistance and should be directly related to a specifically assessed protection needs of the individual, with a clear protection outcome. NRC will establish or strengthen community-based protection networks who will be provided with various capacity building training to strengthen their structures or committees and mainstream protection especially on understanding protection risk, vulnerability and identification of persons with specific needs. Other relevant stakeholders in the community will also be trained about protection. NRC will carry out protection monitoring and assess the situation and respond to immediate needs of the affected population. NRC will adopt the harmonized protection monitoring developed by the Protection Sector Technical Working Group on Protection Monitoring which NRC is an active member.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

NRC will adhere to the core guiding protection mainstreaming standards throughout the project. NRC will ensure that the proposed intervention ensures the safety and dignity of target populations, as well as avoids causing harm. NRC will ensure that all demographic groups can be part of the community engagement and governance process, to ensure a better representation. The participation of women, youth, older persons, and persons with disabilities will be highly encouraged and supported. NRC will thereafter work in close coordination with community representatives to identify needs and conduct referrals to appropriate services. NRC will also mainstream protection and GBV in the site improvement and maintenance activities, by ensuring that specific groups of the population have an appropriate access to communal services. Infrastructures and services will be improved to integrate accessibility and inclusion facilities. NRC has developed a Safe and Inclusive Programming (SIP) Action Plan and will deliver training to project staff, to increase its staff capacity to ensure SIP in all the phases of its programming. NRC will ensure that any community-based structures that it work with are trained on safeguarding and basic protection principles, as part of the induction process.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

NRC takes guidance from the Core Humanitarian Standards definition of accountability and understands AAP as a process of using power responsibly, taking account of, and being held accountable by the people we seek to assist. As with other NRC projects, a Complaints and Feedback Response Mechanism (CFRM) will ensure that beneficiaries and communities have the opportunity to register feedback and complaints. This mechanism includes complaints and suggestion boxes at project sites. NRC will ensure that these sites and CFRM are accessible for people with disabilities. NRC’s CFRM mechanism will also include complaint and feedback hotlines, email, and face-to-face sessions between staff and beneficiaries. NRC uses this feedback to resolve any issues that may arise, and adjust programming plans to better suit the local context. A dedicated team, which is responsible for ensuring multiple channels, delivers this CFRM and tools for complaints and feedback are available for the affected population. The M&E team, as well as technical program staff will manage complaints related to NRC services. CFRM staff reporting directly to senior management provides additional accountability toward targeted communities and ensure feedback and complaints are discussed on a senior level within NRC.NRC encourages face-to-face meetings with senior managers for all site visits, as well as ensuring a practical ratio of female field programming staff. Beneficiaries will therefore have the opportunity to express their grievances, feedback and report challenges with accessing the assistance directly. NRC’s M&E department will carry out post-distribution monitoring (PDM) to measure the progress of activities towards achieving the project objectives.
