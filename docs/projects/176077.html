Project Summary

Two years after the signing of the revitalized peace agreement, its implementation has not reduced the humanitarian needs of the South Sudanese people. South Sudan remained a protection crisis in 2020. Lack of durable peace and limited investment in basic services are holding people back from stability and sustainable development. In 2020, communities were hit hard by the triple shock of intensified conflict and sub-national violence, a second consecutive year of major flooding, and the impacts of COVID-19. Some 1.6 million people remained internally displaced and another 2.2 million as refugees in the region. Insecurity, lack of basic services, and unresolved housing, land and property issues prevented people from returning home in large numbers.
In the 2021 HRP, ARC plans to continue with on-going life-saving interventions to affected populations by responding to WASH needs identified through data collection and analysis in the course of implementation of the ongoing intervention and periodic assessments conducted jointly by UNOCHA and other partners. ARC is already implementing an integrated intervention among its sectors of WASH, GBV and Nutrition and will enhance and strengthen this approach. The current ARC WASH activities are targeting locations where ARC is implementing both GBV and Nutrition or either of the two by co-creating technical/ infrastructure solutions with the people from the communities, human-centered and interactive approaches in hygiene and sanitation promotion such as the PHAST, Wash’em and Super Momas; and gender balanced-community lead operation and maintenance practices. Being human centered and design through co-creation cultivate the radical transparency atmosphere as statement of Accountability to Affected Persons. In this HRP plan, ARC Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) sector will provide services to men, women, boys and girls IDPs, returnees and host communities in Kajo-Keji in Central Equatorial State, Budi and Kapoeta South in Eastern Equatorial State and Aweil West and Aweil Center in Northern Bar Ghazal. Besides ARC having presence in the selected areas, Kajo-Keji county where ARC has its major humanitarian response is rated as Level 5 in the WASH severity mapping and IPC 3 in Food and Nutrition April-July 2021 projection. Here, ARC has funding support from BHA/USAID, UNHCR, WFP and UNICEF for GBV, WASH, general protection and Nutrition .Services target IDPs, returnees and host community. Similar responses are on-going in selected counties of Budi and Kapoeta South in Eastern Equatoria for GBV and WASH. In Northern Bar Ghazal, ARC is operating five nutrition centers in Aweil West and one women center in Aweil Center with extensive outreach activities in the outlying villages. WASH has strengthened integration by working with women and adolescent girls on operation and maintenance of WASH facilities and promoting hygiene at the centers.
The WASH sector is responsible development, operation and maintenance of WASH facilities in these centers.

1 - Needs

Protracted conflict in South Sudan has contributed to an extended humanitarian crisis, which has been exacerbated by unusually high levels of flooding and the COVID-19 pandemic. Although conflict has reduced between parties to the Revitalized-Agreement on the Resolution of Conflict in the Republic of South Sudan (R-ARCSS), violence has continued in many pockets of the country, threatening to reverse gains made at the national level since the peace deal was signed in September 2018. Conflict with non-signatory groups in the Equatoria Region has continued causing displacements, loss of livelihoods and in instances loss of life. People’s physical and mental wellbeing, living standards and coping mechanisms is expected to further deteriorate in 2021. Some 8.3 million people in South Sudan are estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2021. These include 8,000,000 South Sudanese women, men, girls and boys and 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers. This is an 800,000 increase in absolute numbers from the 7.5 million people in need in 2020.In preparing its 2021 HRP, ARC will base its response on needs identified and outlined in documents including 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), IOM South Sudan Crisis Response Plan 2020 – 2021, OCHA Humanitarian snapshots, assessments by REACH, multi-agency assessments, periodical publications and bulletins. Also informing ARC HRP development will be key cluster players like WASH Cluster technical working groups. It is reported in OCHA November, 2020 Humanitarian snapshot that fighting between armed groups in Kajo-Keji, Central Equatoria, displaced over 9,000 civilians with the reports of threats against and abduction of civilians. The escalation of hostilities between armed groups in Kajo-Keji has impacted heavily on the lives of over 12,000 IDPs who were sheltered in Korijo IDPs sites half of whom have now crossed over to Uganda. The rest are reported to have taken refuge among their kinsmen within the host community. Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2021 places Kapoeta South under extreme in the overall severity on humanitarian condition in South Sudan .Food insecurity weakens people’s health and nutritional status and exposes them to greater protection and health risks. Similarly, lack of access to clean water increases the likelihood of waterborne diseases and malnutrition. Aweil West County is one of the 5 counties in the Northern Bar El Ghazal (NBeG). As reported by RRC and County Agricultural Department, since September 2020, an estimated 2,422 households have been affected by flooding in areas along the River Lol, with households displaced to higher ground to escape the rising waters; women and children are most affected. Between 8th and 9th October 2020, Humanitarian partners from five different organizations, (Welt Hunger Hilfe, ARC, Concern, SPEDP and HEALTHNET TPO), the RRC and County Agricultural Department launched Inter-agency Rapid Needs Assessment (IRNA) mission in 14 villages across seven of the most effected Payams. Budi County: Budi County is where ARC has been providing comprehensive support to the County Water Department for the past two years. ARC provides hand pump repair kits, undertakes major rehabilitations and support CWD pump mechanics. REACH in its Humanitarian Situation Monitoring Central and Eastern Equatoria States - South Sudan April - September 2020 reported functioning boreholes % remained stable in EES between March and September 2020 (87% and 86%), except for Budi County where it increased from 47% in March to 64% in September. Nevertheless, the proportion of settlements reportedly using unprotected water sources as the main source of drinking water increased between April (10%) and September (36%) in Budi County. A reason for this may be the limited access to protected water due to distance, bad roads and fear of encountering cattle raiders.

Kajo-Keji County: Before the conflict, ARC was providing a range of humanitarian services to over 12,000 IDPs in Korijo. ARC is working with the RRC in Kajo-Keji Central and ROSS in Liwolo Payam to extend GBV outreach services to families that have settled in these areas after fleeing Korijo IDP sites. 7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

ARC works to minimize the risks to vulnerable populations in its water and sanitation programs by developing an in-depth understanding of the community dynamics in relation to individual and group interests and sensitivities such as ethnicity, religious beliefs, economic status etc., in order to ensure equitable access for all. Through its WASH technical designs, ARC strives to reduce the risks from exposure to GBV, create safe and friendly environments for women and children, and make the facilities accessible to individuals with special needs. ARC adheres to the set of guidelines that are an intrinsic part of our program design paradigm. These include the IASC/ Global Protection Cluster Guidelines for Integrating Gender-Based Violence Interventions in Humanitarian Action, WASH Thematic Area Guide (TAG), and the Handicap International guidelines on design of interventions for people with disability. Illustrative actions ARC takes to ensure protection and gender are mainstreamed in WASH programming include: • Separate communal latrines for women and men constructed at suitable locations where health teams will provide their services regularly. • Positioning of latrines at visible locations in the village to discourage GBV perpetrators to commit act of violence in clear view of the community, while at the same time, ensuring dignified access to women, adolescent girls and other users through installation of partition screens between latrines used by men/ boys and by women/ girls, or positioning the block for women/ girls separately from the block for men/ boys where more suitable. • Solar lights and strong door locking system at each latrine cubicle. • ARC communal latrine design includes positioning of baskets inside the latrine cubicles/ blocks designated for women and girls to dispose sanitary items in dignity, with clean water jerry cans positioned inside

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

ARC will be keen on the gender considerations and the unique needs for people with disabilities so that the feedback mechanisms are sensitive and responsive to their special needs. ARC is fully aware that people with disabilities are the most knowledgeable about their own needs and are the best source of expertise on what works best for them. The impact of this project is likely to affect men, women, boys and girls differently and efforts will be made to ensure that their needs, priorities, suggestions and capacities are considered during project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation to avoid perpetuating gender inequalities and promote equality

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

ARC stands committed to collecting gender specific data on vulnerable populations. Our data collection tools are designed and developed in consultation with affected community members to capture robust data that can be disaggregated with respect to sex, age, ethnicity, female-headed households and geographic area when applicable. ARC uses a mixed methods approach to data collection that allows triangulation to ensure validity. All data collected by ARC on its project beneficiaries is Sex and Age-Disaggregated (SADD) to ensure that ARC’s responses appropriately and effectively meet the needs of vulnerable affected women, men, boys and girls. Data is collected on a routine basis in consultation with community members using the following approaches: Key informant interviews, Separate Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) for men and women, Post distribution monitoring 2 weeks after distribution activities are completed, with a focus on hygiene kit, waste management and hygiene promotional material distributions, Direct observation, Survey and Feedback and complaint mechanisms. To ensure personal information is documented and stored in an ethical manner, ARC will introduce a secure GBV Information Management Service (GBVIMS) and continue to utilize established case management tools. ARC will also analyze GBV data to monitor trends and to inform GBV programming design.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

ARC uses beneficiary feedback mechanism as a monitoring tool to gauge beneficiary satisfaction with our services, where possible modify the project objectives and activities to best meet the needs of the beneficiary, to ensure maximum reach and also track any malpractices among people responsible for resource management. The affected people have the possibility to contact the field staff on daily basis to describe their needs. Further they can talk with specific Sector leaders in the camps, who are in contact with Camp management and ARC field staff. ARC conducts continuous Monitoring where the beneficiaries can express critics and concerns about their satisfaction with the project implementation. Further anonymous Mechanisms, such as Feedback Boxes on central places in the camps and communities are used. ARC is eager to improve any feedback mechanisms, so the beneficiaries are able to articulate their needs independent from aid organizations. ARC developed Feedback Mechanisms in all areas of operations to allow community members to voice their concerns about project design, implementation and evaluation.
