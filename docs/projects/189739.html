Project Summary

This project is designed to address the development problems in Tonji North county of Warrap state associated to lack of clean water access and low level awareness of the people on improved methods of sanitation and hygiene practices. It will specifically targets communities in the payams of Manalor , Marial-Lou ,Rualbet ,Pagol, Awul and Kiirik. The total number of population inhabited in this county is 81,240 (County RRC, 2022). The 2021 IPC nationwide report classified the county at the level of five as a result of communal conflict and the devastating impact of seasonal flood. The situation resulted in displacement of people from six payams and also various infrastructures were damaged including boreholes, houses, roads and institutions. The people displaced were hosted by neighboring communities which had a significant impact on the livelihood and various types of infrastructures. Hence, this project is aimed at mitigating the challenges related to safe water access in the selected six payams of the county. Besides, it will endeavor to enhance knowledge and technical skills of the target households on improved practices of sanitation and hygiene. A total of 28,200 people will be reached with various types of WASH interventions along with the FSL activities that Dorcas and its partners are implementing in the area. This sector includes crop production, horticulture, income generation, animal husbandry, disaster risk reduction and humanitarian aid. The integration of different sectors is expected to bring tangible and sustainable outcome among the target households. Major undertakings of the project will be new drilling of boreholes, Rehabilitation and repairs of boreholes, hygiene and sanitation promotion campaigns, training of water management committee (WMCs), training of Community hygiene promoters (CHVs), training of hand pump mechanics, supply of bicycle for hand pump mechanics, provision of repairs tool kits for boreholes, provision of dignity kits for adolescent girls, conduct water testing for chemical and bacteriological tests, and setting up of hand washing stations in public markets. Furthermore, the project will increase the awareness of the community on sanitation through the application of CLTS approach which will be triggered in each village. Dorcas has applying this method in Wau and Jur River counties and successful results were achieved by teaching and encouraging people to construct latrines at household level using locally available materials.

1 - Needs

Dorcas South Sudan has been implementing WASH project in Warrap State since 2015 focusing on improving water access, sanitation and Hygiene practices in Akon North and again in Tonj since 2020. The organization has undertaken the following activities in the state in the elapsed years: - Drilling of new hand pumps - Repairs and rehabilitation of broken/non-fictional hand pumps- Hygiene and sanitation promotion through CLTS ,CHAST & PHAST approaches - Formation and trainings of volunteers (WMCs, CHVs and VSCs). - Training of hand pump mechanics and equipping them with tools and bicycles.- Massive COVID-19 campaigns at public places and distribution of personal protective equipment - Formation and training of schools hygiene clubs- distribution of dignity kits to girls in school In Tonj North County where this project will be implemented there are a number of broken boreholes ,lack of sanitation facilities ,poor hygiene practices ,lack of clean drinking water that may put the population at risks of contracting diseases related to WASH and COVID-19 that needs interventions to reduce the negative impact in the communities in of six Payams of Tonj North County. The county inhabited a total of 81,240 people in nine Payams. The majority of the people lack access to safe and sufficient drinking water imperative for the healthy life. The activities implemented through this project is expected to reduce prevalence of water borne diseases and mitigate risks of targeted households particularly women and children. Tonj North County was classified under IPC 5 for 2020-2021 due to intermittent communal conflicts that displaced significant number of people to neighboring Payams and other Counties. Out of nine Payams of Tonj North six Payams were affected badly and displaced families seeking safety in the payams including Manalor , Marial-Lou ,Rualbet ,Pagol, Awul and Kiirik. The existing hand pumps that were serving the communities were destroyed or broken down by the disaster beginning from 15th of May2020 to January 2022. In February 2022 the government of Warrap managed to reconciled the communities of Tonj North in which some communities are returning to their original homes but lack of clean drinking water has becaome the major impediment. At Manlor Payam there are 1,408 IDPs that have been hosted by the communities and resettled in Mathiang village for the last season of 2021. 500 HHs hosted by Pankuot Boma and other 900 IDPs are hosted at the church in Warrap Town of Awul Payam. The total IDPs from three Payams is 2,808 HHs and Gogrial East County hosted 2,600 HHs by December 2021. Tonj North County consist of 9 Payams which included the followings (Pagol ,Rualbet, Aliek, Awul, Manlor, Akop, Alebek, Mariallou and Kirik Payams). Out of the 9 Payams , 6 areas were affected badly by the conflict which includes the following Payams (Pagol, Rualbet, Awul, Manlor, Mariallou and Kirik) Dorcas is planning to support vulnerable people with WASH interventions to alleviate the communities from suffering and provide clean drinking water. According to inter-sectoral Clusters Coordination Group (ICCG) report of 2020-2021 about 75% of South Sudan population practices open defecations. Particularly in Tonj North the practice of open defecation is 90% which indicates the high need of intervention. The general WASH problems in the selected locations are: 1. Lack of cleaning water and people walk far distance to fetching. 2. Existing hand pumps are non-functional 3. Poor hygiene and sanitation practices which put children and women at risk. 4. Lack of sanitation facilities at household level 5. Limited knowledge on the water resources managements7. Lack of safe drinking water facilities 8. Lack of tools for the hand pump mechanics to conducted minor repairs and rehabilitation of boreholes in the communities.

2 - Project activities

In the prioritized project location various activities will be undertaken to achieve the intended objectives. The intervention will target those households that require access to basic WASH services. These includes both water and improved sanitation & Hygiene awareness. In the project area as a result of shortage of safe water as the majority of the people fetch water from unprotected sources. Moreover, there is a lack of knowledge on improved hygiene and sanitation practices. Therefore, the project will target those community members deprived of clean water access by bringing permanent solution by developing new water facilities and repair/rehabilitation of non-functional water sources. The interventions will strive to make sure the sustainable use of the facilities by establishing and capacitating management committees in collaboration with government experts. As Dorcas has been encouraging and training the community on sustainability, significant changes have been observed in different intervention locations where households began to contribute money on regular basis. The is being collected by selected water management committee members in the objective of maintaining boreholes that are broken or stopped functioning. Therefore, in the same approach the project will be undertaken in the objective of ensure long term use of the clear water sources. In connection to improving hygiene practices the same target groups will be reached through various approaches to enhance communities awareness and skills. This will be done through organizing and training of village Community Hygiene Promoters.The awareness creation will be conducted through facilitating campaigns and door to door education on sanitation and hygiene. These approach are expected to reduce morbidity and mortality rate and level of severity in the county. Major activities that the project will undertake includes:1. Drilling of 12 new boreholes2. Rehabilitation and repairs of boreholes of 35 boreholes3. Hygiene and sanitation promotion campaigns (six campaigns) 4. Training of water management committee (WMCs) 470 members 5. Training of Community hygiene promoters (CHVs) 282 members 6. Training of hand pump mechanics (25 members) 7. Supply of bicycle for hand pump mechanics (6 bicycles) 8. Provision of repairs tool kits for boreholes (5 kits) 9. Provision of dignity kits for adolescent girls in schools (600 kits) 10. Conduct water testing for chemical and bacteriological tests (47 villages)11. Setting up of hand washing stations in public markets (6 stations)12. Conduct geophysical survey at 12 sites 13. Training of village sanitation committees (72 members)14. Provision of digging tools (to 18 villages, 10 set per village)15. Provision of hand washing kit (500 households)

3 - Project indicators

Apart from the regular monitoring of project activities the project will conducted PDM at the end of the project period to see the outcome the interventions. The following are the indicators that will be measured during evaluation period. 1. % Providing essential supplies and facilities to reduce the high risk of hygienic practice 2. % of the population at risk from vector borne disease understand the modes of the transmission and possible methods of preventive measures. 3. # of population that have access to clean drinking water recommended for human consumption 4. # of community members capacity built and trained to manage and operate water resources efficiently 5. % of water tests meeting minimum water quality standard 6. # of girls of menstruating age provided with access to appropriate materials for menstrual hygiene management. 7. % of population that have access to sanitation facilities at household level.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The beneficiaries feedback will be collected periodically through various ways and the program team will discuss on each of the points forwarded from community members and provide timely response to the beneficiaries. In relation to feedback that require adaptation possible efforts will be done in consultation with stakeholders and the Donor to incorporate in the project undertakings.  Complaints and feedbacks of the community will be collected through different channels like suggestion boxes (which are currently installed in all JR locations), telephone (directly to AAP officer), counter book (managed by village AAP committee), quarterly meeting with AAP committees and also through project staff.  Feedbacks and complaints from each location will be report to AAP officer for analysis which will be done jointly with AAP committee. The committee will decide on the response, adjustment to be taken and follow up its implementation. The AAP committee will meet twice a week and share the minute of the discussion to the partners.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Dorcas has inclusion officer who is responsible to plan, monitor and evaluate the integration of the project with respect to people with disabilities.Furthermore the project will work with community based organization like Organization for People with Disabilities to respond to the need of people with impairments. In addition to the methods of feedback collection mentioned above the inclusion officer will play a key role in ensuring comments are gathered and considered for further improvements.In the intervention areas Dorcas's experience has shown that there are significant number of people with various type of disabilities. Hence, the organization has been working to reach this groups of people as they are vulnerable and impacted by different kinds of crisis. High probability to disaster risk, negative attitude, illiteracy, dependency on others, low participation in community affairs, lack of skills, and low income are the major challenges of individual with disabilities. In the last couple of years Dorcas has given attention to people with impairments and revised the beneficiary registration template to include information related to this group. Furthermore, community leader including project management committees were oriented about inclusion which helped them to give priority to the group in setting beneficiary selection criteria and involve them in the assessment, implementation and monitoring of project activities. These all efforts will be done in this project. DORCAS will ensure that beneficiary registration process is inclusive. This will be included in the registration form right from the designing. The project management committees and the staff will be trained on Disability Inclusion so that, the selections templates are designed to capture data on the beneficiaries in terms of their needs, status, health conditions, locations, age and mobility abilities. This will assist the staffs to collect the relevant data and ensure that during the implementation process all the registered beneficiaries including those with disabilities have received equal support from the project. Secondly, the PMC will be informed to make sure that PWDs are all given the first priority when they conduct beneficiaries, selection. In the registration forms, there is a column for PWDs which will be used to track number of beneficiaries with disabilities. During beneficiaries’ verification, the general community meeting shall be held and the participants will be asked to confirm if anyone with disability have been missed out and why not included. It is clearly understood that, in every disaster such as conflicts, floods and draughts, people with disabilities suffered more because of their inabilities to move far or to listen or to see the magnitude the disaster. They are often left behind by relatives who cannot afford to take them along to long distances. As such it has often been emphasized by the consortium to make sure the people with disabilities are either supported by the projects or provided jobs if they have they the required qualifications. It has become and internal policy of the Dorcas to make sure all the vulnerable groups are represented in the project as well as in the communities structures established by the project. Additional methods used to ensure that PLWD are included in the response are as follows:- During the initial assessment households with PLWD’s were identified, though not yet registered for the response (this happens after the response starts and after the reassessment and inception meetings).- In the inception meetings, DORCAS will discuss the selection criteria with community representatives, consisting of women, men, youth, PLWD and other groups.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Project information is provided through kickoff meetings that will be carried out with community representatives. Besides, the project will establish village level project management committees (PMCs) which will involve in the project from the inception period. PMCs disseminate information related to the project to the beneficiaries in addition to staff. A PMC constitute 50% women so as to ensure sufficient representation and bring forward the needs of women and for the purpose of capacity building. PMCs will be selected by the project beneficiaries and attempt will be made to represent all sect of the community groups to achieve the intended objectives of the project. Accountability “is about using power responsibly. It involves taking account of the needs, concerns, capacities and disposition of affected parties, and explaining the meaning of, and reasons for, actions and decisions. Accountability is therefore also about the right to be heard and the duty to respond” according to Humanitarian Accountability Partnership International definition. To strengthen accountability for the affected population and to guarantee their full participation in project implementation, a feedback and complaint mechanism (FCM) will be formed. The feedback and complaints mechanism will provide safe environment conducive for project participants/community to present their feedbacks or complaints during the implementation period. The FCM investigation team will be form and mainly will receive and analyze the feedback or complaints and report the finding to both the participants or complainants and Programme Manager. Secured suggestion boxes, community help desk, phone feedback (hotline), and field monitoring visits shall be the main channels for gathering feedbacks or complaints from project participants/community. The choice for these FCM channels is based on their simplicity to use by the participants or community to provide feedback or complaints. The feedback and complaints procedures shall involve the reception of the feedback or complaints from the complainants, its registration by the receiver preferably MEAL officer, acknowledgment of the receipt of the feedback or complaints to complainants, investigation and finding, Reporting (communication of the findings to the complainants), and appeal if the complainant is not satisfied with the findings and resolution and appeal reporting. In a nutshell, the feedback and complaints mechanisms should be designed focusing on beneficiaries or communities’ participation to enable their full involvement during project implementation.In general:The project stakeholders will be informed about the project using the following approachesI. Project launching at the state level: There will be a one-day workshop all the directorates and line ministries to different sectors of SSJR to discuss the key content of the project including but not limited to objectives, implementation strategies, roles and responsibilities, planned activities, overall monitoring aspects, challenges and lessons learned among others. II. The inception meetings at will also be conducted in all the areas of our intervention at the community/Payam level with project stakeholders including community representatives, village chiefs, PMCs, CAC committee and local authorities to inform them about the project particularly the activities planned for each location, target groups, beneficiary selection criteria and expected roles of stakeholders. III. Information to crisis affected people will be shared through Project Management Committees, Community Accountability Committees, Village Chiefs, Community Volunteers, and project staff.
