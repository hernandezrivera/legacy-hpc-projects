Project Summary

This project aims to ensure the respect of the fundamental rights of the people affected by the crisis in the South West region and ameliorate access to humanitarian assistance without discrimination. The intervention objective is to improve access to humanitarian assistance for p internally displaced persons, host communities and returnees through a mobile and community-based protection monitoring system including sharing information on the protection situation and humanitarian needs with actors and conduct of Advocacy to address specific protection issues. In order to ensure safety, dignity and non-discriminated access to humanitarian assistance for displaced persons, the intervention led to strengthening capacities of humanitarian actors and other services providers on protection and humanitarian principles

Needs

Access to better protection, especially for people who are victims of violence, abuse and non-respect of their basic rights in South West region is necessary; Indeed, according to the needs assessment conducted by IRC in September 2018 and other humanitarian actors as presented in the humanitarian emergency plan 2018, the crisis in the South West region has highlighted many needs related to protection, human rights and access to humanitarian assistance. The proliferation of armed group’s increases insecurity in the affected areas, limiting access to assistance for affected people whose humanitarian needs are not sufficiently communicated and shared for all localities where people are displaced. In addition, right’s abuses such as arbitrary arrests, kidnappings, reprisals and economic exploitation of the population have highlighted the need for robust advocacy for the respect of international humanitarian law and greater access to beneficiaries are fundamental to effective humanitarian action. In this context, safety and dignified access, without discrimination is an essential to ensure recondition for the affected populations to benefit fully from the assistance and services provided. Access to many basic services is limited by such fact as school closures, arbitrary arrests, psychosocial trauma from exposure to different forms of violence, violence against women and girls, including physical violence and sexual exploitation, the weak capacity of protection actors to promote and protect IDP rights requires strong action such as capacity building to remove these barriers.
