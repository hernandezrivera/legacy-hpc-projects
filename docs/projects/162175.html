Project Summary

CARE/PMRS will respond to this situation with a comprehensive intervention that combines elements of direct service delivery to the identified vulnerable communities/groups in Gaza Strip in response to the continuous crisis and the need for urgent emergency response as highlighted by the HNC, with special focus on women and children, in particular children with disabilities and with chronic diseases who are more vulnerable than others. Persons with disability (PWD) in Gaza Strip (6–7 % of Gaza population) are one of the most vulnerable and often the most disadvantaged of all groups in society from the poorer segments of society, no sufficient facilities to take care of their needs comprehensively. The proposed project will address critical issues and priorities in Health cluster, and links with the health cluster HRP strategic objectives objective 1: “Ensure the availability, accessibility, acceptability and quality of essential lifesaving health services to the most vulnerable communities in Gaza and West Bank/EJ, including through health system strengthening, preparedness and community resilience building”. The project was designed in full coordination and cooperation with Health & Nutrition Cluster and it goes in line with MoH and humanitarian needs.
The project activities will respond to the different humanitarian needs of the most vulnerable groups (vulnerable children with Disabilities & children with chronic diseases), to improve their health status and improve access to health services. This project will improve access to healthcare services with a focus on persons with disabilities and children with chronic disease (type I diabetes Mellitus) through provision of comprehensive rehabilitative healthcare services, including physiotherapy, occupational therapy and psycho-social support, treatment and regular medical check-ups including diagnosis and treatment, home adaptations, and provision assistive devices, diapers, hygiene kits and medications.
the project was designed in full coordination and cooperation with Health & Nutrition Cluster and it goesin line with MoH needs.
The project will target the marginalized communities who are subject to a high risk of destructions, restrictions and incursions by Israeli forces. The targeted locations are Beit Hanoun, Beit Lahia , Jabalia and Gaza communities

1 - Needs

According to HNO 2020, an estimated 1.3 million people are in need of humanitarian health interventions across the oPt, of whom two out of every three are in Gaza. Within Gaza, almost 20% of the population requires humanitarian interventions for reproductive, maternal, neonatal and child health services including nutrition; one out of six of Gaza’s adult population requires treatment for non-communicable diseases (NCDs); 10% suffer from severe mental health disorders; and 20% of pregnant women in the poorest communities are undernourished. Meanwhile, the health system is struggling to cope with the ongoing influx of conflict-related trauma casualties each week, as part of the Gaza Mass Demonstration. However, infrastructure, human resources, and the referral system remains only partially functional after 13 years of blockade, inadequate to cope with the growing needs. The health system is already overburdened by the protracted emergency situation. The critical situation Gaza people are living under, specially people living in vulnerable communities has resulted from the continuance incursions of Israeli military which lead to deterioration of the general situation in Gaza including the health services and others. Furthermore, the blockade in Gaza is preventing vital food and supplies reaching families in Gaza communities. These restrictions culminate in the loss of livelihoods, and are worsening poverty for many Palestinian family’s basic needs and Medical equipment are in short supply. One of the most frequent chronic diseases in childhood is Diabetes Mellitus Type I. Caring for children and adolescents suffering from the disease is a challenge. In Gaza Strip, this challenge is multi-folds due to the ongoing obstacles facing the health sector and the population. Long years of Gaza Strip’s blockade have caused the health system to decay and left people in severe poverty and unable to provide for their families. Families of children with type I diabetes cannot provide their children with the health care they need to prevent complications. These services include routine check-ups, blood tests, visits to specialized doctors, medications and awareness, among others. The proposed action aims at improving access to healthcare services in Gaza and rehabilitation services for children with disabilities and children with chronic diseases. The activities will be implemented in partnership with Palestinian Medical Relief Society “PMRS” through the provision of medical health care and rehabilitation services as highlighted by the HNC HNO 2020, with special focus on children with disabilities “PWD” and children with chronic diseases who are most vulnerable communities in Gaza. PWD still the most vulnerable group in the GS as they need special care while Problems faced by people with disabilities are not only the results of their individual impairment, but also due to the negative attitude and beliefs existing in the community including: facing many challenges on daily basis when accessing services, buildings, private and public spaces due to unfriendly environment and infrastructure, transportation, financial constraints, social stigma and attitudes. More than 70 % (PNGO Report –Sept 2019) of disabled do not receive appropriate care and are denied access to and participation in rehabilitation and health care, mainstream education, employment, and social participation. They face many challenges to access services due to physical, financial, cultural and attitudinal barriers. PMRS data shows that people with more severe disability has less chance to access education, employment, health care and social participation. In fact, most people with disability and their families struggle to meet the basic needs as assistive devices, medical disposables, medications...etc.

2 - Activities

The project was designed in coordination with other health actors like HNC in order to avoid duplication, maximize the number of beneficiaries those in need in the most effective and equitable manner. CARE has fully participated in the HRP process and are aware of needs and key actor’s responses. Accordingly, we will be working to achieve the following results/ activities: Result #1: children with disabilities in vulnerable communities in Gaza have improved their access to essential health care services. Activity #1: Comprehensive Rehabilitation Health Care Services Provide comprehensive rehabilitative healthcare services, regular medical check-ups comprising diagnosis and treatment of children with disabilities in the northern governorates, physiotherapy, occupational therapy, and psycho-social support. Result #2: 40 people with disabilities “PWD” homes have been adapted and improved their health status. Activity #2: Improving the home environment through infrastructure adaptations" Improve the quality of life of the poorest physically disabled children (40) through infrastructure home adaptations in order to support them in bathing, dressing...in bathing, dressing, eating, transferring, using the toilet, or getting around the home and/or instrumental activities of daily living (IADLs) i.e., meal preparation, shopping, finances, housework, using the telephone, or managing medications. Additionally, the provision of assistive devices, diapers and hygiene kits to increase their quality of life and independence. Result #3: children with chronic diseases in particular type I diabetes Mellitus have improved the quality of their healthcare services Activity # 3: Improving the Wellbeing of Children with Type I Diabetes Mellitus in Gaza communities The proposed intervention will help 120 children (60 boys – 60 girls) with diabetes mellitus Type I registered in PMRS chronic disease centre with control and treatment of the disease by providing them with the needed medications, blood glucose measuring devices strips for self-care practices and needed laboratory tests, as well as check-ups by Physicians and endocrinologist.
