Project Summary

The Action will contribute towards durable solutions for IDPs and conflict-affected people through the provision of legal assistance, capacity improvement of local self-government entities, and advocacy. The project will improve access to legal assistance and legal remedies in Donetsk Oblast GCA for people living within 5 km of the contact line and in NGCA. Direct legal aid provision will take place in the buffer zone. The Action will provide legal aid at the three entry-exit checkpoints (EECPs), namely Marynka, Novotroitske and Mayorsk) in partnership with the Free Secondary Legal aid Centre (FSLAC). This will be a continuation of legal aid activities which have been carried out since 2018 and 2019, respectively, in partnership with FSLAC. Moreover, the Action will deliver multi-sectoral services in cooperation with the healthcare actors.
The Action will also include community-based legal aid provision in two buffer zone settlements in a partnership with Caritas Mariupol as an implementing partner. The community-based legal aid provision will build upon the valuable experiences of similar community-based interventions implemented with local self-governments which are currently carried out in five communities in Donetsk oblast.
Advocacy will be an integral part of the project. DRC-DDG will retain its specialized focus on legal analysis with the monthly Legal Alerts being the primary output along with other ad-hoc legal analysis for advocacy purposes. At the national level, DRC-DDG will primarily contribute to the collective advocacy efforts under the umbrella of the HCT, the HCT Protection Strategy and Advocacy WG, HLP TWG, and Mine Action Sub-Cluster and its working group on legislation.
All legal aid components will function in an integrated manner and provide the full spectrum of DRC-DDG’s standard legal aid services, i.e. one-on-one counselling, information dissemination, civil documentation and administrative assistance, in-court assistance, local self-government training and advocacy.

1 - Needs

The action was designed to address HNO 2021 key humanitarian needs including access to documentation and HLP rights, access to social benefits and pensions, and access to services in isolated settlements. In January 2020, an assessment by DRC-DDG in partnership with Caritas Mariupol revealed that:- 72% of people surveyed did not receive free healthcare services from state medical institutions since the conflict has started;- 62% did not receive any medicines free of charge under the state program “affordable medicines”;- 22% of respondents lost access to different state social payments, including pensions;- 42% need privatization certificate, while 22% of people surveyed require assistance with ownership documents for their residential property;- 14% of agro landowners have tax debt issues;- 41% of respondents aged 18-49, as well as 45% of those aged 50-59, need documentation assistance as their photo in the national passport has expired.This indicates that for this population, with a high proportion of elderly and persons with disabilities, the protection needs revolve largely around obstacles to meaningful access to essential services, entitlements and rights, with health, social benefits and personal property as key concerns equally relevant for men and women. It leaves already vulnerable categories of people exposed to further risks of harmful coping mechanisms, exploitation and threats in the physical environment. According to 2020 REACH Multi-Sector Needs Assessment of NGCA, between 11 and 12 % of assessed households in NGCA reported a need for legal assistance. The report also states that around 670,000 people crossing EECPs are residents of the NGCA crossing into the GCA for temporary trips, often to access financial and administrative services, particularly related to receiving pensions and government payments, and the issuing of documents. This means that between 15% and 20% of the NGCA population exits NGCA on a monthly basis to access services in GCA. In June 2020, DRC-DDG Legal Team undertook a brief qualitative survey to assess the impact of COVID-19 on the legal aid needs of the conflict-affected people. Findings of the assessment reflect early stage opinions of the assessed communities. Participants of the FGD fear that much larger volumes and types of legal aid needs will start to surface once the EECPs are fully operational and NGCA residents start to approach the state authorities for their benefit and entitlements. According to the assessment findings, the legal aid challenges in the post-COVID-19 period will be evident both in the volume of the demand and the complexity of the cases. Civil documentation is likely to be the main legal aid volume driver with nearly six months of the backlog created in NGCA with regards to death and birth registration, issuance of new passport, and renewal of existing passport.
