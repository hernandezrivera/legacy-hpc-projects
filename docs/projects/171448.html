Project Summary

Agricultural livelihoods are crucial to improving the food security of vulnerable communities. The resources for repair and improvements of agricultural businesses are scarce, land quality has deteriorated, and water resources depletion and water salinity levels are affecting production. Farmers lack sufficient knowledge of modern agricultural techniques and environmental risks connected to excessive use of chemical pesticides and fertilizers. Practices are negatively affecting farmers’ health, soil, and water. Limitations of water for irrigation are a main concern for vulnerable farmers near ARAs. The chronic energy deficit has placed additional pressure on farmers who already experience an increase in agricultural input costs. This has indirectly affected small-business people including farmers who have to make additional marketing efforts and explore new markets to sell their products.
The project is designed to be in line with FSS HRP 2021 Top Humanitarian Priorities. The project will directly enhance income generation and food security for 500 HHs in nine most vulnerable agricultural communities North Gaza and eastern Khan Younis and Rafah through rehabilitation of agriculture assets, and improving access to irrigation for small farmers in the target areas. The Key activities include Rehabilitation of 700 dunums of farming land for 350 small-farmers (280 M, 70F), the work will include levelling work, installation of irrigation system, and provision of seeds, seedlings such as squash, Okra, melon, corn and spinach, eggplant, pepper onion and garlic etc. .) and fertilizer. It will also include rehabilitation of 150 dunums of greenhouses (GH) for 150 small farmers (30 F ,120M), work includes Nylon sheets and anti-virus net and irrigation dripping system. Moreover, three agricultural wells will be equipped with solar energy units to improve access to irrigation for 90 farmers. Rehabilitation of open lands and greenhouses will be mainly through labor with limited use of machinery in farms where the land topography needs.
The proposed project will build on SCI previous agricultural interventions which are being implemented with the same partners in eastern Khan Younis and north Gaza (2017-2020).The project will be integrated with current SC nutrition and protection interventions in eastern areas of Gaza strip.

1 - Needs

The ongoing Israeli blockade on Gaza, electricity crisis and the instable political and security situation continue to prevent access to livelihood opportunities and limit the free movement of people, goods, and services. In Gaza Strip, the real GDP contracted by 4.8% and it is insufficient to reduce unemployment and boost per capita income. World Bank estimates that in 2020, the Palestinian economy would shrink by 2.5-7% with an estimated deficit of $1.5 B. PCBS expect that the GDP for 2020 to decline by 14% compared to 2019. According to HNO 2021, the unemployment rate during the second quarter of 2020 reached 49.1 per cent, a historical high, up from 45.5 in the previous quarter. According to Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS), the number of the unemployed in the local market is expected to increase drastically in the coming period due to expected economic recession following the declaration of State of Emergency COVID-19 in March 2020. According to HNO 2021, the economic crisis, prior to the outbreak of COVID-19, left nearly one third of the population – 32.7 percent, or 1.6 million people without access to nutritious food. Food insecurity is high among women – 32 percent of families headed by women are food insecure – particularly in the Gaza Strip. The public sector salary crisis and restriction of movement, due to COVID19 crisis, have affected households’ income stability and weakened their purchasing power which in turn has affected the local market, farmers and small businesses. Agricultural livelihoods are crucial to improving the food security of vulnerable communities. However, the increased costs of production inputs and poor productivity pose obstacles to any improvement in food security in Gaza Strip. Border security tensions add new challenges to farmers who have limited access to their lands to cultivate. Farmers cannot afford the cost of repairing and improving their farms. Their production is also affected by the deteriorating land quality, water resources depletion and water salinity. The electricity outage leads to shortage of water for irrigation and irregular irrigation, which negatively affects the agriculture production. In August 2020, SC partner Bayader conducted three FGDs with 35 farmers (9 F, 26 M) in Khuzaa, Alshuka (Rafah and Wadi Salqa to identify the risk and needs of farmers close to ARA). The results of FGDs showed that the farmers whose farms are close to or within in ARA identified support with agricultural inputs and rehabilitation of their farms to restore their ability for normal production as their urgent needs. The participants stated that most of small farmers need to improve their knowledge in managing farms and mitigaigate the potential risks. It should be noted that the finding of our assessments revealed that there is a need for agricultural inputs and rehabilitation of open farms in ARA, however, it will be challenging to implement greenhouses interventions. To have better understanding of specific challenges of farmers in the target areas, SC partner, Bayader, conducted 3 FGD in the beginning of October 2020 in eastern Khan Younis and eastern Rafah attended by with 31 farmers (8 F, 23 M). All focus groups held by Bayader were held taking into consideration the COVID-19 restrictions in terms of using open space and physical distancing and utilizing masks, temperature check and disinfection. The design of the project was based data was analyzed based on SCI previous and current projects and the challenges identified by the Farmers highlighted as the following: The negative effects of the blockade on agriculture is reflected in the limitation of exports and imports and increasing cost of farming inputs. In addition, the limitation and instability of market due to decrease purchasing power of the consumers contributed to a sharp decrease in the price of their products in the local market, resulting with huge losses to farmers’ income and putting HHs under the risk of food insecurity. •Covid-19 pandemic also affected negatively the value chain of the agriculture products; the farmers are subject to heavy losses due movement restrictions causing the farming input price to increase. Farmers reported an increase in the price of inputs, shortages of fertilizers, and limited availability of workers due to movement restrictions. •The farmers reported that the weather conditions caused huge damages to their crops resulting from heavy rains and winds in the winter season and extreme heat waves in the summer. This has caused losses in production where 80% of farmers reported significant decrease in their income. •The farmers within whose farms are close to the Access Restricted Area (ARA) have special concerns due to the security situation in this area, poor infrastructure and access to their farms. SCI and partners updated a socio-economic assessment in North Gaza and eastern Khan Younis, with a sample of 280 poor household’s farmers (HHs), whose main economic activity is farming, to measure the change on their income and the effect on their food security and coping mechanisms. The assessment was conducted via phone calls through other livelihood ongoing projects. The assessment has shown that the average household income has fallen to NIS 580 per month. Food insecure families spend approximately half of their income on food. Other essentials are deprioritized, and female-headed households experience even worse food insecurity and livelihood situation. SCI identified the needs based on the partners’ assessment conducted in SCI previous project which indicated that there is a bigger need in the south compared to northern Gaza.

2 - Activities

The project is designed to be in line with FSS HRP 2021 Top Humanitarian Priorities. The project will directly enhance income generation and food security for 500 HHs in 9 most vulnerable agricultural communities through rehabilitation of agriculture assets and improving access to irrigation for small farmers whose farms are located in areas close to ARA (minimum distance of 300 meters). Most of the work of leveling will be implemented using manual labor, while utilizing machinery where possible and allowed by the security restrictions. The proposed project will build on SCI previous agricultural interventions which are being implemented with the same partners in eastern Khan Younis and north Gaza (2017-2020). The project will be integrated with current SCI nutrition and protection interventions in eastern areas of Gaza strip. The project partners will coordinate with MoA and FSS to ensure the quality of work and the integration with other actors to increase the project impact and sustainability. SC and the partners will select the farmers based on vulnerability criteria. SC will implement the project activities in North Gaza in partnership with BLDA and in partnership with Bayder in eastern Rafah and khan Yunis The Key activities are:1. The project partners will publicize the activities in the target areas and invite farmers to submit their applications to benefit from project. The partners will screen the applications and provide SC with the list of potential beneficiaries, SC team will conduct technical assessment to assess the needs of each nominated farm, SC team will prepare the bill of quantities of the rehabilitation works needed.2.Rehabilitation of 700 dunums of farming land (North: 310, Eastern Khan Yunis: 300, and Eastern Rafah: 90) for 350 small-scale farmers , with an average of 2 dunums of open farm per farmer (70F;280M), the work will include levelling work, installation of irrigation system, and provision of seeds, seedlings and fertilizer. The partners will implement the rehabilitation work under supervision of SC3.Rehabilitation of 150 dunums of greenhouses (GH) for 150 farmers (North:50, Eastern Khan Yunis:50, Eastern Rafah:50), with an average of 1 dunum of greenhouse per farmer (30M;120M), including Nylon sheets and protection-insect net, wood and gutters. The partner will implement the rehabilitation work under supervision of SC4.Three agricultural wells , in Abasan, Fukhari and Qararah, will be equipped with solar energy units total of 90 kW to improve access to irrigation for 90 farmers in eastern Khan Yunis in close coordination with MoA and FAO and SC will install the new solar energy pumping system directly by SC. Beneficiaries will be selected based on SCI’s social data and poverty assessment. Household outreach visits will take place to identify beneficiaries for participation. Group consultations will ensure community participation in beneficiaries’ selection and identification of needs. Although traditionally most farmers are male, but attention will be paid to targeting farms headed by women or those that employ women. SCI has developed this intervention in collaboration with the target intervention to ensure meeting local needs and priorities among most vulnerable communities. SCI will coordinate with project partners and health, WASH and protection internal SCI staff to ensure integration of the project activities and selection of beneficiaries. SC will also conduct technical assessment to identify the needs of the targeted farms and to develop BoQs for rehabilitation work and implementation plans. SCI will also coordinate with MoA and FSS with the selection of beneficiaries to avoid overlapping and update the intervention plans based on the input from the different stakeholders. SCI’s partners have been selected based on previous joint project implementation experience with these partners successfully implementing various donor funded projects of a similar budget value (as proposed here). They are current operational partners of SCI with well-established systems and adherence to SCI’s partnership framework and principles including having passed a comprehensive partner assessment and various donor audits. The intervention will be integrated with SCI’s current livelihood, protection and nutrition interventions to increase the impact and enhance sustainability, building on its existing projects and extended partnership with the different stakeholders towards comprehensive approach for strengthening coping mechanisms for the beneficiaries. The intervention will be built on the understanding of gender-specific needs and expectations as well as socio-economic factors and cultural sensitivities.SCI will implement the three proposed activities through partnership with two local partners BLDA and Bayader. These partners are well-established in the proposed target locations, and their strong expertise and experience with SCI in implementing similar interventions. Both partners also have a long track record of sound implementation and organizational capacity in delivering similar projects. Given that they have access to these communities, they will support SCI in the selection of the target groups, facilitate SCI access to these communities, and ensure that implementation of activities is up to quality benchmarks.Through this project, SCI will aim to increase the partners’ capacity and experience, along with administrative guidance provided by SCI on the general project implementation through hands-on practical approach to their learning. As part of the project, SCI will train partners in transparent procedures and participatory and conflict-sensitive/do no harm methodologies for the selection of beneficiaries, and guide them towards quality implementation through frequent field monitoring visits. The partners will gain experience in set-up of innovative energy alternatives and sustainable farming.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

SC will follow a conflict-sensitive approach to ensure timely identification and mitigation of potential negative risks and consequences. SC will conduct strong community engagement to ensure regular exchange of information, consultation on priorities and preferences and transparency for preventing tensions. Beneficiary selection will be triangulated by field visits, cluster consultation, and local stakeholder information. SC will maintain its independence and impartiality. Staff will not engage in discussions on sensitive political and community issues while working with beneficiaries. SC will continuously monitor the development of the COVID-19 fragile context and risks. It will analyze the potential interaction between the planned activity, the changing context and potential implications for the relations between stakeholders and communities. SC`s Social Tensions Tracking and Mapping system (STTM) will locate geographic areas with communities vulnerable to social tensions. SC`s strong MEAL approach will identify any emerging harmful trends and activities and introduce course correction to programming as required. SC will strictly enforce its Child Safeguarding policies and processes including support to partners as needed.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

SCI considers it imperative that the project is inclusive of individuals living with physical and mental disabilities, especially given that current services outreach of both male and female people with disabilities tends to be limited. The project will prioritize for example PwDs with means to enhance their economic independence and productivity. The project gender sensitivity will be integrated and considered as a minimum standard. The project will be open to both women and men according to the vulnerability criteria, with the option to create women only working groups to facilitate their participation. The selection criteria will focus on female headed HHS. SCI, together with project partners, will check during the project visits the problems that women are facing and that the project activities are designed through gender and child-safeguarding (CSG) lens to prevent any harm by project activities and/or abuse by the family members. Both Men and women will be included in sessions with different interests and fields related mainly to enhancing their capacity in agricultural production. Youth, including female entrepreneurs and people with disabilities (PwDs), will be provided with employment opportunities through increased employment generation projects and improved training.The Gender Advisor will ensure gender mainstreaming in the interventions. SCI’s Field Officer will supervise the implementation in cooperation with the partners to ensure equal access to the project services. Inclusion and equity are dealt with by SCI at two levels; the first through streamlining in existing activities and indicators as part of SC policy to reach the most marginalized, and the other through promoting specific interventions to serve the children with disabilities in communities to assist in their access to food security services.

Project staff are required to attend PSEA training.The protection from sexual exploitation and abuse of children is dealt with under our Child Safeguarding policy. This policy is specifically concerned with the Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) of adults. This includes direct or indirect beneficiaries of our programming and adults in the wider communities in which we work. For anyone under 18, refer to the Child Safeguarding Policy. For issues in relation to staff refer to the Global Anti - Harassment policy which covers sexual harassment, SEA and bullying of SCI staff. The PSEA policy contains essential principles and rules that reflect the behaviours and standards expected from employees, trustees, volunteers, interns, trainees, consultants, partners, and other representatives working for, or contracted on behalf of SCI in any capacity and covers our international development and humanitarian work.SCI is committed to reducing the opportunities for abuse and exploitation to happen through effective prevention, ensuring that survivors are not exposed to further harm through good reporting and responses, and ensuring that survivors receive appropriate care. Effective prevention and providing safe responses requires all staff, volunteers, interns, partners and other representatives to be clear about their individual accountabilities and responsibilities. It also requires all senior staff and managers to ensure that their working environments minimise the opportunities for sexual exploitation and abuse, and to ensure that reporting and response do not expose survivors or reporters to any further harm. Sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) is a gross breach of both standards of aid, and of the rights of those in need. The PSEA policy applies 24 hours a day, 7 days a week and 365 days a year. Any breach of the policy will be taken extremely seriously and responded to. Breaches of the policy may be grounds for termination of engagement with SCI. SCI takes the position that early reporting of any concern supports both prevention of further abuse and safe response through the hotline available 24/7 (1800-724-726). There must be increased awareness about reporting, including the importance of reporting of any and all concerns, however apparently minor. SCI’s commitment to Zero Tolerance of sexual exploitation and abuse means all programme design and planning must include a gender power analysis, which also looks at the roles and structure of SCI staff, volunteers, interns and partners and a safeguarding risk assessment SC has a PSEA Focal Point at the country level and the field sites, who is responsible for driving awareness of the policy and on how to report, inviting, receiving and collating all causes for concern. When these collected pieces of information generate a picture of a site, or a programme, or a particular person, the PSEA Focal Point must escalate the matter to trigger further inquiry and/ or investigation.
