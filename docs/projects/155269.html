Project Summary

Given its international experience in disaster risk reduction, resilience and women’s empowerment, Mercy Corps seeks to implement a project that addresses women’s needs and perspectives in local contingency planning in order to pave the way for humanitarian response that will adequately address vulnerabilities and capitalize on positive gender dynamics in target communities. By partnering with women’s organizations that are involved in local humanitarian and development efforts, Mercy Corps will provide them with tools needed to better integrate women’s needs into their planning, as well as better link their planning to department-level DRR plans.
The “Fanm yo pare” project aims to build capacity of women’s organizations to support women’s empowerment and strengthen the resilience of women to better face natural disasters. The project's activities will be developed and adapted to meet the needs of women in the west (Cabaret and Arcahaie) and the Nippes departments. The project will integrate 750 women and men into gender-focused DRR and humanitarian response trainings, workshops, and working sessions, and also integrate 1250 women and men into VSLAs, which will contribute to gender equity and social cohesion in the target communities. This multi-platform approach will build understanding of gender and protection among civil society at large, and will integrate women, girls, boys and men's needs into contingency planning, which has the potential to improve humanitarian response for the larger community, estimated at about 10,500 direct and indirect beneficiaries.
Outcomes:
1. Women's organizations have the capacity to ensure gender-responsive disaster-risk reduction planning and action, and emergency response at the community level.

Needs

Haiti is the poorest country in the Americas with a growth rate estimated at 2.7% in 2014 and is located on the path of tropical natural disasters and particularly exposed to hurricanes, tropical storms and droughts. The country’s ability to adapt and cope to those climate hazards is currently very limited and the effects are exacerbated by the country’s inadequate infrastructure and fragility of already overexploited natural resources. For example, some parts of the country are still recovering from the impacts of the category 4 hurricane which devastated the south and coastal areas in 2016 and had come on the heels of a severe drought. Thus, two years after the hurricane, many families continue to struggle to recover and lack resilience should another shock occur. In addition, the country is currently facing critical and chronic food and nutritional insecurity, with more than 30% of the Haitian population acutely food insecure and 131,000 children malnourished. We know that women and men are not affected by crises and shocks in the same way. For example, women who are pregnant or breastfeeding are particularly vulnerable to malnutrition during times of food scarcity, which can have long term consequences for the baby as well. During rapid-onset emergency response, women are often more vulnerable to exploitation or exclusion. Assistance that does take into consideration the specific impacts and needs of women and girls risks at best, not being relevant, and worst creating harm for the beneficiary. In fact, women are not often at the table where decisions are being made, or facilitating the distribution of aid. Since the Hurricane Matthew response in 2016, Mercy Corps has conducted a series of gender analyses of both the response itself (Sud, Nippes, Grande Anse, Ouest), as well as how other crises are impacting genders differently, including the food insecurity crisis in the North. From these field assessments, we have learned that too often emergency response planning and implementation do not sufficiently account for gender-based needs, capacities, and impacts. For example, in Mercy Corps’ post-Mathew gender analysis in Maniche and Cavaillon (South) as well as Beaumont (Grand’Anse), we found that local and international response actors underestimated the value of women's participation in activity design (for example, in the case of a cash for work program, the absence of beneficiaries in the design process resulted in an intervention that created a double burden for the women), and men were reluctant to integrate women's organizations in the processes of risk and disaster management. Moreover, during times of crisis, cases of gender-based violence and exploitation are more likely and yet they are ignored or not sufficiently addressed due to the lack of local institutions able to deal with these situations. Mercy Corps believes that when Haitian women and gender-focused associations are participating in disaster preparedness and emergency response, communities will have the potential to generate better-adapted responses and reduce the risk of exploitation of vulnerable populations- particularly women and girls. We believe that disaster risk reduction activities must be conducted through a protection and empowerment lens in order to pave the way for responses that will adequately address vulnerabilities and capitalize on positive gender dynamics in target communities.

Activities or outputs

1. Women's organizations have the capacity to ensure gender-responsive disaster-risk reduction planning and action, and emergency response at the community level. 1.1: Conduct mapping of community organizations / structuresMercy Corps will conduct community meetings with local stakeholders including elected local leaders, the Direction de la Protection Civile (DPC) as well as existing women's organizations, structures, and networks to inform them about the objectives of the project. In order to identify local actors who will benefit from the program, Mercy Corps will use a snowball referral methodology to develop a database of women's organizations. This methodology will allow Mercy Corps to start with well-structured and better-known organizations to develop knowledge about less formal and less structured organizations. During this process, a database will be created with all the organizations listed by the department to create departmental networks of women's organizations and associations. 1.2: Conduct workshop on gender and DRR and build operational plans with departmental and municipal officials.To ensure that relevant actors in DRR consider socio-cultural aspects related to gender, Mercy Corps will conduct participatory workshops with key actors in DRR as well as groups of women (civil society organizations, local associations) at a communal and departmental level, dealing with the topics of disaster risk assessment, early warning, information management, as well as awareness-raising and training. These workshops will ensure that DRR policies, decisions and programs are gender-sensitive and include women in the development of contingency and operational plans for long-term risk reduction. Participants will be asked to identify the roles that women could play in an integrated system of prevention, warning and response and to establish an action plan for integrating women into current systems implemented by the National System of Risk and Disaster Management (SNGRD) at the level of departments and municipalities, according to the following aspects: ● Capacity assessment of at least 15 women's institutions involved in preparation and response.● Development of a gender-sensitive strategy and action plan for COUDs, town halls and DPCCs responsible for the systemic coordination of early warning.● Engagement and involvement of women's non-governmental organizations to contribute to capacity building.● Identify the best mechanisms for women's integrated communication in preparation, alert and responses. 1.3. Build capacity of 15 women’s associations and organizations to participate in humanitarian response. Once integrated into planning around disaster risk reduction, Mercy Corps will train women’s groups on key principles of humanitarian response, including foundations of project design and implementation, management competencies, M&E, and cash-based programming, as well as humanitarian principles and accountability. 1.4: Creation of 50 women savings and loans groups in collaboration with women's community organizations.Mercy Corps will select women in collaboration with CBOs to receive training on the VSLA (Village Savings and Loan Associations) group methodology. The trained women will serve as VSLA group trainers in the communities and serve as technical support for created groups of 25 women each. In addition, VSLA groups will have training on gender and life skills that include conflict management, leadership, and psychological first aid. Beyond specific trainings, participation of women in VSLA groups will allow them to improve their leadership skills, their planning capacity and increase their ability to share their perspectives with local women organizations. As a result of the work done in these groups, women participants will be able to provide support to one another and participate as actors in the preparation of community contingency plans.
