Project Summary

The project aims to recover and expand the economic situation of the returnees, Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and host community business owners in the Aleppo governorate in Northern Syria, specifically targeting Atareb sub-district as hot spots for high number of returnees and IDPs.
These early recovery efforts will be achieved by, firstly, conducting a needs assessment on understanding the factors of production (Labor, Capital, Input and Technology) for Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) which will lead to development of customized business development plans for local SMEs; and secondly, by providing a combination of business development services in the form of consultancy and seed grants based on the needs of the local businesses to revive and enhance the production capacities of local businesses in the targeted locations Ex: supporting the commuting sector, engine maintenance, and Support maintenance of water pumping. To enhance social cohesion and reduce competition for resources, the project will target 100 businesses, supporting 30% IDPs and 70% host community enterprises, including 15 female business owners.
The project will be closely monitor through an array of instruments specifically developed for the purpose of the project.

Needs

WATAN conducted an assessment focusing on Atareb sub-district in May 2018 by analysing the economic conditions and labour market opportunities from this area. The coverage was 95% of the territory. Main conclusions have revealed that the civic administration is still being rebuilt and the population still generally lacks access to identification documents. Available Public services include: Waste collection; Sewage system and Paved Roads (though many still require rehabilitation). The private sector has stepped in to cover other public services not being provided by the authorities. These services refer to Electricity supply, Water (delivered using tankers), Transportation services and Internet provision.The local economy consists of 520 service providers in the sub-district. The providers are 20 plumbers, 50 barbershops, 300 groceries and 150 clothing shops. Additionally, the assessment noted that there are 40 manufacturing businesses (including sewing workshops, electric generators and chicken altar) and 150 businesses engaged in agricultural activities.The relief agencies working in the livelihoods sectors are Ihsan, IYD, LDSPS and WATAN.

Protection Risk Analysis

Gender inclusion and capacity building approachGender equity is a key component to this intervention and is to be mainstreamed across all activities.Organizationally, WATAN has a Gender Policy, which outlines its commitment to addressing practical gender needs, through ensuring there are no gendered inequalities in access to services such as water, education and food, and strategic needs with the aim of a reduction in gendered power inequalities. Programmatically, WATAN remains committed to ensure that gender differences are assessed and used to inform programming decisions via the assessment of gender-disaggregated data gathered through surveys and gender disaggregated focus group discussions. WATAN recognizes the differing needs of all sectors of the community and seeks to answer these needs in effective and appropriate ways. Practical steps employed:Staff selection – Employ women at management and field level to cover strategic and current needs. WATAN offers support to women to join the team by providing safe activities which are not endangering their position both from security and culturally point of view.Beneficiaries selection – Within the project, a caseload of 15 businesses headed by women will be prioritise for support purpose. Furthermore, WATAN will encourage business owners to open their job vacancies for women also. The data are processed on gender base.Activities delivery – The activities are tailored on gender subjects considering the women and men needs. Special emphasis is placed on the risks and protection measures for each of the two genders that differ across regions/locations.Monitoring and post-monitoring – A special attention is given to the importance and impact of the assistance received. Women/girls are going to constitute a percentage proportion of the sample to measure the influence of aid and any potential harming effects. A special attention is given to monitoring if the assistance is matching women and girls’ needs. Capacity building approachThe whole project contains a dimension of capacity building as emphasis the development of SMEs through personalised packages. The support covers both financial and technical side and it will be delivered through specialist.Capacity building is also offered to the staff through the specific programme that WATAN runs for increase the competencies and skills of its employees.
