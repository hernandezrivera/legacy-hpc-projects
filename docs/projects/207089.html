Project Summary

At this moment, MENTOR Initiative is considered by the health services of the province of Cabo Delgado as one of the most reliable and effective partners in the response to cholera outbreaks in the last year (districts of Chiure, Mueda, Montepuez and Metuge - available to cover more provinces and districts if needed.), due to the quick response capacity and logistical reach and prolonged in time, both in terms of direct assistance to the population (prevention) and logistical supply to health units and CTC's, Health sistem partnership and capacity building of the provincial health services. Being able to organize an effective response in less than 48 hours, with prepositioning elements in various strategic warehouses, and logistical support for distribution and transport of people and items.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

According to the current experience in Cabo Delgado and the response and intervention of MENTOR in partnership with DPS in response to cholera outbreaks in 3 of the districts, MENTOR has made an EOI to continue responding to these outbreaks in other districts or provinces. The partnership MENTOR/DPS aims to facilitate collaborative efforts for cholera response interventions in any Mozambique province (if neede) with declared outbreaks of Cholera or Acute Watery Diarrhea. MENTOR is able to establish and deploy a diverse team covering Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH), Health, and Social and Behavior Change (SBC) to identified outbreak hotspots within 48 hours of the mechanism trigger. The intervention's duration is adaptable, contingent on the specific context. This includes initiatives to bolster government and humanitarian cluster capacities for WASH, Health, and Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE) at the national, provincial, and district levels in cholera-hotspot areas.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Access to high quality healthcare is critical to reduce deaths from tropical diseases and improve health in humanitarian and post-crisis contexts. MENTOR’s work in this area focuses on these key areas: • Training of health workers and community health workers. MENTOR has extensive experience in Mozambique and abroad to train health personnel in different health sectors: vector borne-diseases including malaria, dengue and others; communicable diseases such as diarrhoea, cholera, acute respiratory diseases; neglected tropical diseases; Integrated Management of Childhood Illness; Nutrition; etc. • Support and supervision of community health workers and health workers. MENTOR supports the district and provincial authorities in conducting supervision of the health personnel. These supervisions follow MoH guidelines and ensures that the quality of care is sustained. • Supply chain support. MENTOR has been supporting the different health authorities and health centres in ensuring the coordination and communication flow in order the community health workers are not short of diagnosis and treatment kits in Cabo Delgado. Internationally, MENTOR has been providing logistic support to authorities in different contexts such as CAR, South Sudan, Ukraine, in terms of drugs and material provision. Water, sanitation and hygiene is integral to our work tackling vector-borne and neglected tropical diseases. • By addressing the faecal-oral route of transmission and reducing breeding sites for multiple disease vectors, healthier environments are created to help prevent the spread of diseases. To improve access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene practices we focus on:• Infrastructure development - MENTOR works with local authorities and other agencies to improve water supply systems, build or repair sanitation facilities, and establish safe and sustainable hygiene infrastructure.• Urban WASH - In urban areas, addressing water supply and storage alongside sanitation and waste management challenges require approaches that consider population density, limited space and varying socio-economic conditions. • Tippy Taps - are simple, low-cost handwashing devices that promote proper hand hygiene, especially in settings with limited resources. They are an innovative and sustainable solution to encourage handwashing. • Community-led total sanitation empowers and engages communities to take ownership of their sanitation and hygiene solutions. Through a sense of ownership and shared responsibility, community-led total sanitation create lasting changes in sanitation behaviours, reduces open defecation and contributes to overall improvements in health.• Waste management is essential for maintaining a clean and healthy environment and to reduce mosquito breeding sites. Waste management strategies are implemented that promote safe disposal practices and removal of waste, in partnership with local stakeholders. Activities focus on raising awareness, training local health workers, and establishing waste collection and disposal systems.• Cholera response: MENTOR has been supporting the provision of WASH practices and interventions in Cabo Delgado with the coordination of DPS, SPS and other partners. Social Behaviour Change Communication (SBCC) strategies promote behaviour change, encourage the use of preventive measures.• Hygiene promotion campaigns have been conducted in several countries including Mozambique. Currently a network of 200 community activists are conducting SBCC activities door-to-door and in community gatherings to sensitise the population on hygiene practices and prevention of diarrhoea. Last year more than 600,000 people were reached during these campaigns in Cabo Delgado.• SBCC campaigns have also been used for vector control activities, nutrition programmes, neglected tropical diseases activities. All of these campaigns had a focus on involving the community to increase acceptability and ownership.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

An initial risk assessment will be conducted in order to identify the potential risk that the programme may encounter or cause. Follow-up assessments and meetings with representative beneficiaries will occur throughout the programme to understand if life and condition has changed in the population and if any kind of harm is being caused. MENTOR coordinators will be trained to monitor these risks and identify potential harms. The findings of the risk assessment will be used to design specific action plans to mitigate the risks identified. All MENTOR staff will be trained on PSEA and they will be committed to respect the Code of Conduct. Specifically, for the different activities the practical points that we will implement are: - Implementation of a green line available to denounce any kind of harm. - Implementation of suggestion boxes across the sites of implementation.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

MENTOR will include women and elderly people within the disaggregation of the data collection indicators. This will ensure that specific groups are reached. MENTOR will include in the trainings of staff and short-term activists PSEA and points about elderly population and people living with disabilities. When implementing the activities, the activists and MENTOR staff will need to map and target people that may encounter difficulties in accessing assistance. Supervision of the activities will also focus on this matter. In all activities proposed, MENTOR will look for gender parity across all human resources - if they are hired by MENTOR. MENTOR will also work together with representatives of different groups of the communities including women groups and community leaders. These meetings will serve to communicate and collect feedback from the activities at the planning phase as well as in the evaluation phase in line with accountability of affected population.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

People living with disabilities may have social and physical barriers to access assistance. In the different activities of these project, the community leaders, health technicians and all WASH FIT group members will be trained to map individuals with difficult access to assistance and will be encouraged to give attention to this. Durind on job visits MENTOR will monitor the activities in order to include mapping of people living with disabilities.
