Project Summary

For this HRP cycle, our NGO aims to address urgent and lifesaving nutrition needs for pregnant and lactating women, adolescents, and youth (10-24 years) are included. and children under 5 years, the most vulnerable group, in Northwest Syria. Nutrition activities throughout 2023 will continue scaling up lifesaving curative and preventive nutrition interventions to the mothers and children living in Azaz sub-district (Al Rayyan Camp and Suran town), Jarablus sub-district (Zoghra Camp), Al Bab sub-district (Bazagha town) and Afrin sub-district (Afrin city) in Aleppo governorate, and in Dana sub-district (Atmeh Camp Cluster), Qah sub-district (Al Karameh Camp Cluster), Maaret Tamasrin sub-district (Kafr - Kafrehmul town), and Idleb sub-district in Idleb governorate. Nutrition activities will continue prioritizing the following responses:
-Strengthen adherence to operational guidance for response adaptations in the context of COVID-19 and Cholera to mitigate risk of infection including roll out of the Family MUAC Approach (FMA) to increase early acute malnutrition cases detection and refer them for treatment.
-Quality improvement in the treatment of acute malnutrition
-Prevention of micronutrient deficiencies using different modalities: supplementation through distribution of micronutrient powder and tablets targeting children aged 6 to 59 months and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) in collaboration with the health sector, diet diversification awareness, and fortification.
-Protect, promote, and support recommended maternal, infant and young child nutrition practices in emergencies (MIYCN-E) including breastfeeding/ complementary feeding and Social Behavior Change SBC via counseling or education. In tandem, Mother to Mother Support Group sessions MTMSG will be established with new mothers (PLWs) and caregivers with the aim of providing a space for them to be supported in caring for their children's nutrition needs. They will be trained on specific nutrition topics and be able to share this knowledge with other mothers and caregivers in their communities.
-Prevention of chronic malnutrition and maternal malnutrition through adequate focus on the first 1,000 days of the child’s life including the pregnancy period.
-Capacity building of partners on key nutrition interventions (community management of acute malnutrition, promotion of appropriate maternal, infant and young child nutrition and caring practices and age-appropriate micronutrient supplementation).
-Strengthening collaboration between Nutrition, Health, and Protection activities on implementing an integrated response that addresses the immediate and underlying causes of acute and chronic malnutrition.
- Conducting Family MUAC approach among the mothers and caregivers to include them more in preventive nutrition activities and early detection of malnutrition cases.
from other hand, in case of supplies procurement, the organization will receive the nutrition supplies (for preventive and treatment) on quarterly basis from UNICEF through PAC organization as a donation.

3 - Needs

The prevalence of malnutrition is a significant public health challenge in Syria. The main groups affected by malnutrition are infants, young children under five, and pregnant and lactating women (PLW). The different forms of malnutrition include wasting, severe acute malnutrition (SAM), moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) as well as chronic malnutrition or stunting (low height for age), and micronutrient deficiency (especially iron-deficiency anemia which occurs in young children, PLWs, and girls of reproductive age. Based on SMART Survey report which conducted in NWS (including our targets areas) (Jul 2022), the overall GAM rate was 5 % while in June 2021 SMART survey in NWS the GAM rate by WHZ was 2.5%, SAM was 1.7 %, Stunting rate was 22.3% which is slightly more than the last survey conducted in April 2019 (19.4%), and the PLW MAM rate was 12.4%. The rate of exclusive breastfeeding in infants 0-5 months was 56.25% (29% in 2021). Only 8.09% of children 6 – 23 months had consumed five or more of the eight defined food groups, from other hand, only 4.62% of children 6 – 23 months consumed a minimum acceptable diet MAD during the previous day.and based on the HNO 2023 PIN & severity scales, the severity stunting in the targeted locations as following: Afrin: 4, Al Bab: 4, A'zaz: 4, Suran: 4, Dana: 5, Maaret Tamsrin: 4, Idleb: 4 and Jarablus: 5.while the severity of MDD and MAD among the children is 5 for all locations.

4 - Implementation of activities

our organization's nutrition team will support facility-based and community-based (MIYCN) activities through providing consultations on MIYCN practices in two supported facilities, eight primary health care facilities, and two maternity and child hospital. Additionally, doctors and nurses will provide micronutrient supplementation for the prevention of micronutrient deficiencies for women and children through Community-Based Management of Acute Malnutrition through four supported CMAM clinics. Our organization will also support facility-based and community-based screening for acute malnutrition through Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) screening for PLW and retain Z-Score Charts for children aged 5 years and under with detection of the edema intensity. CMAM clinics will provide treatment for MAM cases among children 6-59 months and PLW through TSFP. and OTP for SAM cases among children 6-59 months. the nutrition team will conduct Family MUAC Approach and Mother to Mother support group sessions for both PLWs and caregivers.#of CHWs in all locations: 49the supervisor will conduct regular supervisory visits at least once a week using nutrition checklist to ensure high quality services are provided and ensures the appropriate collection of data from the field: each beneficiary has a card, which includes assessment data, actions taken and general information. Once the card is filled by CHW, they enter the data into the data collection tool, which is an online form used by team.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Mitigation and Prevention Measures:• Our organization has prepared a safeguarding action plan to promote and make sure safeguarding measures are in place. • A safeguarding policy is being distributed for all staff to read and then sign acknowledgement letters.• Our organization delivers MAST training including SEA training for new hires and an annual refresher for all staff.• Our organization has safeguarding focal points in the fields for the staff to revert to in case of any concern, question, or complaint.• Our organization has safeguarding posters placed in all our facilities, along with the several complaint hotlines for staff and beneficiaries' use.• Our Protection Team delivers training sessions for all staff on PSEA, safeguarding and the referral pathways.• Our Protection Team conducts a safeguarding risk assessment for all facilities.• Our organization has the CBRFM in place, taking consideration of community awareness.• Safeguarding measures are implemented in the recruitment processes to ensure suitable candidates are hired within our organization.
