Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in Zimbabwe has been further worsened by the current COVID19 pandemic. Approximately, 56.2% or around 5.5 million rural people are likely to be food insecure at the peak of the hunger season from January to March 2021. The prospect of significant deterioration in the health and nutritional status of the communities cannot be ruled out unless support to these communities is strengthened and maintained.
In response to this humanitarian situation, Save the Children proposes to implement lifesaving nutrition interventions in 5 Districts across the 5 provinces thus Binga, Beitbridge, Kariba, Mbire, and Matabo districts. The project will target the most vulnerable population thus the children under the age of five, pregnant and lactating women. The key area of focus includes strengthening the capacity of health workers and community volunteers (VHWs) to provide timely community based nutrition response. These includes supporting caregivers to screen their children and improve the referral, reporting and treatment of children with acute malnutrition. The project will also support the formulation and proper implementation of community based IYCF activities that includes counselling of caregivers of children 6-23 on IYCF by VHW
The project aims to save the lives of under-five children with acute malnutrition through improved multi-sectoral coordination and integrated emergency response in 5 drought affected districts of Zimbabwe. This will be realized through strengthening mass screening for early case identification of moderate and severe acute malnutrition, and behaviour change communication for improved infant feeding and child caring practices. In addition, this nutrition emergency response will build capacity of health workers and community volunteers on life saving nutrition interventions like IMAM and cIYCF as well as strengthening counselling of caregivers of children 6-23 on IYCF. The project will also strengthen linkages with health, HIV care and treatment, WASH and Social protection.

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

SC will engage with the communities to gather and share evidence to inform programme design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. SC will support monitoring by the community to contribute to better accountability and to engage them for improved ownership. A toll-free number will be promoted and posters that raise awareness will be produced and distributed. Feedback meetings will also be held with children as well as adults in the targeted areas to strengthen accountability and engage the targeted communities. Complaints and Feedback Mechanism: SC will set up help desks staffed by men and women at key activities, use a toll free hotline and ensure all programme staff are trained on how to receive complaints and feedback using KoBo Toolbox – allowing staff to instantly document and submit a complaint/feedback to our online database through their phone. This builds on existing CFM approaches SC has been using in other responses and development programmes across Zimbabwe. Additional CFM channels, including child-friendly ones, will be identified through an accountability assessment, to understand affected children and communities’ preferences around information sharing and CFM channels, especially of people with specific vulnerabilities. Complaints and feedback will be analysed, key findings will be reported through a regular accountability report for decision-making, and the feedback loop will be closed on individual and/or community level. CFM data analysis will focus among others on understanding which vulnerable groups are able to access our channels, to ensure they are appropriate and accessible.
