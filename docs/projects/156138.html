Project Summary

Close to eight years of crisis which has severely stretched the capacity and resilience of people living in Syria to cope with shocks and stresses brought about by the crisis. This project comes to meet the needs on ground in line with the FS Cluster second objective of Supporting self reliance of affected households by protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income generating opportunities to save and sustain lives. The project will consist of two main interventions. First, distribution of agriculture inputs which will contribute to having Kitchen-garden in their homes. Second, Provision of training that will help them use the agriculture inputs correctly in addition to use and how to benefit from the corps. The project can reduce food insecurity and improve the ability of 300 beneficiaries and their communities to meet the concerns of food security, nutrition, and health in one hand. In the other hand, this project extends beyond the food security and offer a way to generate a small income rapidly. A small investment in seeds and a small area with little number in laboring can provide a return from the sale of vegetables within six to eight weeks.
The project activities will be conducted in Bennsh and Teftnaz. Share Foundation will be responsible for implementing the selection process transparently in consultation with community members. The information gathered will triangulate lists provided by local authorities through key informant interviews and random household visits. The selected families will be both IDPs 30% and host population 70%, including 60%women and 40% men, so that the social tensions among the two groups are minimized and.
Once procured “The seeds and tools from Idleb”, the items will be transported directly to beneficiaries. Door-to-door distributions will be utilized to both avoid placing undue burden on intended beneficiaries and prevent attracting unwelcome attention. Small trucks will be hired to transport the agriculture tools and kits (and related items). The households selected for agriculture kits will receive a kit tailored to their needs and available space of their kitchen garden. For example, some households will have 100m2 other will have 150m2. These beneficiaries will receive agriculture extension support through the experts that Share Foundation plans to hire locally for refresher training and new methods and practices.The main intervention of this project is to improve food security of the most vulnerable households through assets for training (AFT) and cash for training (CFT). By providing families with productive assets, technical training and income generating opportunities. The main activities include the provision of agricultural inputs for Summer seasons -Home gardening- including seeds, fertilizers and agriculture tools for families who have access to home garden with minimum 100m² to maximum 500m² and water sources.
The second, is to provide a technical capacity for the beneficiaries in the proposed areas of intervention. The main activities include the delivery of specified training and consultative support through the farming process. Training will cover many aspects such as:
• Training on the use of agricultural tools.
• Training on the use of agricultural residues.
• Taking advantage of the project's outcomes in regards to the dried and stored agricultural products.
Furthermore, Share Foundation will distribute cash, that is linked with the above-mentioned activities, so that the selected beneficiaries will have cash during 5 months starting from the beginning of the project. The distribution period was determined based on the agricultural calendar of the cultivated crops, and that means beneficiaries will be supported with cash till they have the crops and sell them and can be able to sustain without any more support.

Needs

Due to deteriorating economic environment and food insecurity stemming from massive displacements, agriculture and livelihood interventions are critical to increase self-reliance, strengthen resilience and early recovery, especially in increasingly stable areas for both acute and at risk of food insecurity groups. According to HNO 2019 An estimated 6.5 million Syrians are food insecure and an additional 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity. Moreover, agriculture was the main source of employment for 47 per cent of the population. However, With an 83 - 89 percent people living below poverty lines , the relationship between poverty and food security is becoming inseparable as $16 billion in losses and damages of the GDP attributable to agriculture sector alone. In response to the above-mentioned situation, Share Foundation team inside Syria started collecting the data since July 2018, targeting the two villages, Bennsh and Teftnaz. which was analyzed and resulted that the top priorities in the target areas that are in need for this kind of intervention. Population in the targeted areas about 12,400, where female compose the vast majority of the population with 57 percent.After analyzing and studying the information we obtained we found out the following needs:Families in the targeted locations are in need for agricultural inputs, many of them have a space in their home which can be cultivated as kitchen gardening. Access for water is available their and both locations are famous of their vegetable crops and are strongly needed in the local market.Beside that some questions were also asked to indicate the extent of women's participation in this type of project. After the rapid needs assessment process, and according to local councils at least 40% women in targeted communities were interested in agricultural work but due to the high prices of cultivation requirements they can’t offered that.

Implementation of activities

The project activities which will be conducted after developing the project management plan, will be conducted by Share Foundation Field staff: 1- A base-line assessment will be conducted in the targeted locations to assess the exact needs and beneficiaries . 2- Signing MOU with the Local Councils. 3- Select the project team , the gender criteria will be taken into consideration. 4- Train the selected field team officers on basics of direct dealing with beneficiaries and on mechanisms and procedures of the program. 5- Procurement of the agriculture inputs. 6- Provision of the technical capacity building for the beneficiaries 7- Continuous follow-up of the beneficiaries. 9- Continuous Monitoring, Evaluation and Learning. 10- Reporting

Protection Risk Analysis

•Ensure locations and times meet the needs of women and adolescent girls who have family-related responsibilities•Support local organizations, community groups and businesses to provide adolescent girls and other at-risk groups opportunities to connect with each other in a safe space, share resources and skills, and communicate about important livelihoods issues.• Consult with participants to identify potential safety risks related to livelihoods activities, and support participants in managing and making empowered choices about these risks. •Situate livelihoods activities in safe locations and schedule them during times of the day/week that minimize the risk of GBV. •Support communities’ proposed solutions for mitigating these risky situations.
