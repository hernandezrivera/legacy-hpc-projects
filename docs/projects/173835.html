Project Summary

The proposed project aims to enhance food security of vulnerable conflict-affected households in the North West & South West regions and aligne with CLUSTER OBJECTIVES AND INDICATORS (HRP 2021):
1. (COB1) Save the lives of food insecure populations through inclusive, coordinated and integrated assistance.
2. (COB2) Improve food security (access and use) of refugees, internally displaced persons, returnees and vulnerable local populations (men and women).
3. (COB3) Ensure the availability of quality information on food security and vulnerability for better humanitarian planning and strengthening the humanitarian-development nexus.
The overall objective of the project is to ensure continued and regular access to adequate and nutritious general food assistance for the vulnerable IDPs, returnees, refugees and host communities in proposed target areas of the North West & South West Regions of Cameroon.
INTERSOS will work specifically through general food assistance (GFA) to meet the basic food needs of 27,000 vulnerable IDPs and local population from crisis affected households from Boyo Division through the timely distribution of overall 1,780.24 Mt. in-kind food from January to June 2021, on a monthly basis. The proposed project will complement the ongoing intervention carried out in partnership with WFP, whose funds keep decreasing, making it difficult to implement activities in an effective way.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The North West & South West regions of Cameroon are affected by a serious crisis since 2016 and over 1.6 M people are affected by food insecurity (IPC Phase 5). Children continue to be among the worst affected by the ongoing armed conflict in the NWSW regions. According to the EFSA March 2019, it is estimated that the number of children between 6-59 months with Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) is currently up to 21,557 and keeps growing, in a context where even health facilities are facing closures.According to UNOCHA, the humanitarian situation in NWSW continues to deteriorate with serious protection incidents. Since January 2020, there has been an upsurge in violence especially in the NW region affecting mostly women and children (399 protection incidents were registered only in the month of February 2020), including excessive violence against civilians. Displacement of civilians continues to increase due to the destruction of houses and villages, leading people to leave all they own to find refuge in the bushes or in the closest urban areas. Ten thousand (10,000) new persons were reported to have been displaced in the NWSW as they flee February 2020 confrontations between the warring parties. UNOCHA reports so far (February 2020) 679,000 IDPs originating from these two regions. Humanitarian access to persons in need throughout NWSW continues to be a challenge due to rainy season conditions, roadblocks and insecurity. In particular, violence against civilians in NWSW regions continues to have a serious impact on livelihoods and generate acute humanitarian needs. Entire families are forced to flee, losing all their sources of income and livelihoods, thus creating additional pressure to the already scarce resources and services of the urban areas in which they resettle. Or, for those hiding in the bushes, keeping them at the dependence of humanitarian assistance.The rainy session has made access to goods and services even more difficult for both returnees, IDPs and the local population, while several enterprises and cooperatives have been shut down due to the conflict, disrupting the supply system and the functioning of market. The restrictive measures to face COVID-19, from lockdowns to borders closure, have further exacerbated the food insecurity of the conflict-affected populations of the NWSW regions.The situation in the North West and South-West regions, which started as a political crisis, is marked by violent clashes and has led to a complex humanitarian emergency with 2.3 million people in need in early 2020. In comparison, in early 2018, 160,000 people were in need but 1.3 million people were estimated to need humanitarian assistance in early 2019. This significant increase can be explained by several factors. Firstly, sectors have increased their capacity to assess the needs of the population in the North West and South-West regions.Secondly, further deterioration in the security situation and attacks on populations and their properties has triggered more displacement and an increase in needs. Killings, arbitrary arrests and sexual violence, including women and children, is taking its toll on local communities. While at the end of 2018, 530,000 people were estimated to have been displaced due to the crisis, needs assessments carried out in 2019 indicated displacement of more than 720,000 people – an increase of 30%. The number of people displaced within the two regions remained at around 450,000 between the assessments carried out in July 2018 and August 2019. However, the number of people displaced to other regions of Cameroon increased almost three-fold from 80,000 to 220,000 persons displaced to the Adamawa, Littoral, West, and Central regions. Including the people in need of urgent humanitarian assistance within the North West and South West (1.7 million), assessments have shown that 600,000 IDPs and host community members need assistance in the Littoral, West and Central regions.
