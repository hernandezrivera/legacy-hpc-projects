Project Summary

CARE plans to implement and integrated lifesaving integrated health and nutrition services to IDPs and disaster affected populations targeting 58,500 children U5 years (boys and girls) and pregnant and lactating women living in Bari, Sool , Sanag,Bari and Lower Jubba regions covering 10 districts (Bosaso, Badhan, Lasqoray, Ceelafweyn, Erigavo, Ainabo, Dhahar, Lascanod, Talex and Kismayo). project will complement ongoing interventions by CARE responding to the effect of floods, locust and COVID 19 pandemic.
This The Project will be implemented through support to static Health facilities and mobile services for hard to reach populations to improve access to essential health care. CARE will work with relevant ministry of health authorities to strengthen local capacity and sustainability of project. CARE will provide equitable access to integrated health and nutrition services at both health facilities and through mobile teams. The health team will be comprised of two qualified nurses, two midwives, an auxiliary nurse, a registrar and a community health worker (CHW). This team will provide preventative and curative health services including outpatient consultations (especially for sexual and reproductive health,common childhood illnesses including diarrhea, acute respiratory infections, influenza, malaria, measles, and pneumonia). The team will refer complex or severe cases to the nearest district hospital where the patient will receive free medical care, as well as basic laboratory testing for malaria, common infections, etc. The mobile teams will provide the same services through an outreach modality working in different villages each day with weekly follow-up schedules. Nurses will use the Somalia treatment guidelines as well as the IMCI guidelines translated into the local language to diagnose and treat patients according to the country protocol. CARE will pay the incentive of one medical doctor at each district hospital so that all referred patients including survivors of Gender Based Violence (GBV) can assess free medical inpatient care as well as emergency obstetric care. In addition to routine health services, MCH clinics will also act as a hub for the storage of vaccines to be administered by the mobile teams. Medical supplies for mobile teams will likewise be stored at the MCH clinics. CARE will also support workers at these front-line health care facilities to gather health data from the population and report to the MoH using the MoH’s Communicable Disease Surveillance and Response Early Warning and Response Network (EWARNS). CARE will also support health management information systems by submitting monthly district level health reports to the MoH’s DHS 2 tool. CARE will use standard infection prevention and control procedures (IPC) as outlined by WHO . In all HF CARE will; Encourage Health workers to have a high level of clinical suspicion; establish a well-equipped triage station at the entrance to the facility, supported by trained staff; institute the use of screening questionnaires according to the updated COVID-19 case definition. This measures will enable contact tracing and follow up. In addition, CARE will put visible signs in public points within HF reminding patients with symptoms of COVID-19 to alert the Health workers and also install hand washing stations to be used by all patients before coming for services. CARE will also support the transportation costs of the referral in order to make the referral pathway effective. Through linkages and support from CARE's existing counsellors will support GBV cases will referred. CARE will compliment this project with existing funding from OFDA,FFP, DFID on health and nutrition and address gaps not covered by the project. CARE’s activities will benefit both women and girls, as well as boys and men, to ensure that their unique needs are covered. Age, sex, and vulnerability criteria disaggregated data will be used to track inclusion, promote accountability
