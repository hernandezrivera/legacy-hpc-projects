Project Summary

This project intend to ensure 6, 000 Target beneﬁciaries are provided sustainable reintegration services through Psycho-social Support, Economic Skill and livelihood Activities, Case Management, Monitoring and reporting of Incidences of Grave Child right Violations and child’s right awareness raising on grave violations to children, adolescents boys and girls formerly associated with armed groups and case management of children living with various forms of disabilities. 4,000 conflict-affected girls, boys, women and men and young people affected by grave child rights violations will benefit from information on grave child rights violations, 2,000 children will benefit from Reintegration services and among which 100 Children living with disabilities will benefit from case management services and other child protection specialized services.

1 - Needs

Based on its past and ongoing work, an effective reintegration programme for children formerly associated with armed forces or armed groups, Children living with disabilities, Prevention and response to GBV are the priority areas for WINN intervention in the state. Under a previous UNICEF-funded 12 months project, and 6months NHF funded project, and Save the children 2years casemanagement for UASC and CBM livelihood empowerment for persons living with disabilities, WINN identiﬁed and trained 115 MRM Volunteers from 6 LGAs of Bayo, Kwaya Kusar, Konduga, Mafa, Gwoza and Dikwa to document proﬁles of CAAG, unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) and other vulnerable groups including orphans. Despite the progress made in previous projects, some gaps and needs remained due to high inﬂux of people on daily basis from the remote villages after military clearance and operations. There is an urgent need to strengthen the system of community-based system of identiﬁcation and acceptance of CAAG and Children with Disabilities, and children released and escaped from JAS, and provide them with case management, psycho-social support and sustainable Socio-economic reintegration services linked to their individual needs and services availability, aimed at reducing their vulnerability to other grave violations of their rights. Also, to address violations that already occurred to them. WINN will work in collaboration with existing/established child protection partners/committees in project locations. WINN will use CPIMS and CPIMS+ tools developed by CPSWG currently being used by CPSS Members. This will ensure standardized approach to data collection and information management for CAAG, and Otherwise vulnerable children including child at risk etc. Identiﬁcation needs to be strengthened by ensuring that case workers/MRM Interviewers apply the deﬁnitions CAAG and Disabilities within the local context and culture and account for varying levels of vulnerability. This understanding includes the formal and informal association of children with armed groups including the CJTF and local vigilante groups and also those rescued, released and escaped from armed groups.

2 - Activities

Through this project, WINN with the support of Donors and other stakeholders proposes to implement a broad-based programme using seven main strategies. These include:1. Building the capacity of MRM and Case Workers, MRM Informant/Interviews to strengthen the monitoring, veriﬁcation, prevention and reporting of incidents of grave violations against children; 2. Building the capacities of community actors on Disabilities inclusive in development and case management, 3. Building the capacities of community actors on MRM and CAAG programming; iv)Putting in place community-based mechanisms to prevent marginalization of Children with disabilities, the recruitment and use of children by armed groups; 4. Providing Psycho-social support and short-term reinsertion packages to Both Children with Disabilities (CwD), Boys and Girls and Caregivers released, escaped, surrendered from armed groups. 5. Providing Reinsertion kits to children and caregivers released from administrative custody/military detention; 6. Providing economic reintegration based on age and gender sensitivities for CAAG and other highly vulnerable children including children in CJTF as well as the caregivers. In order to actualize these strategies, WINN will ensure an effective coordination and partnerships arrangement are in place with all relevant State actors on Disabilities in Development (DID), MRM, CAAG and Child protection programmes.
