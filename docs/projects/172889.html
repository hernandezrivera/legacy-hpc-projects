Project Summary

Zimbabwe experienced floods in the previous 2 years (i.e. 2019 and 2020) during January to March, which saw the destruction of infrastructure (latrines, water points, urban water reticulation system and homes), production land and vegetation including loss of human lives in Chipinge (March 2019), Chimanimani (March 2019), parts of Masvingo (March 2019), Hwange (January 2020) and Binga (February 2020). As eastern and northen Zimbabwe struggled with the aftermath of floods, other parts of Zimbabwe (Gokwe-south, Gokwe-town, UMP, and Ruwa) were facing a very different crisis: too little water. The 2020 WASH severity rating indicates that UMP, Gokwe-South and Ruwa Local Board have a severity of 3 whereas Gokwe-Town has a severity of 4. In recent years, there was inadequate basic sanitation and safe water for drinking in the four districts coupled with poor service delivery especially in small towns. The aim of this intervention is to counter the negative effects of potential flooding, associated epidemics and drought during the year 2021. The project will target communities and Institutions (Schools and Health facilities) and ensure that 75,331 children (boys and girls), men & women are reached with access to adequate and quality safe water; sanitation, health and hygiene messages and NFIs in, Gokwe-South, Gokwe-Town, Ruwa Local Board and Uzumba Maramba Pfungwe (UMP).

Improved access to adequate and potable water supplies will be achieved through; I. Rehabilitation/repair of water points (boreholes, wells and springs). [Ruwa Local Board, UMP, Gokwe South and Gokwe Town] II. Installation/extension of solar powered water reticulation systems. [Ruwa Local Board, UMP, Gokwe South and Gokwe Town] III. Rehabilitation/repair of urban water reticulation systems and headworks construction. [Gokwe Town, Ruwa Local Board] IV. Nutrition gardens will be set up around water points to enable Communities and Health facilities to provide nutritious food for their supplementary feeding programs. [UMP and Gokwe South] Improved access to hygiene enabling toolkits/NFIs will be achieved through; I. Distribution of WASH hygiene kits (NFI and MHM kits) to vulnerable community households and Health facilities. 8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

I. Suggestion/complaints boxes will be set up to capture the views of the affected people inorder to address barriers and challenges being faced.II. Community based management concept will be used to make sure that communities participate in all the stages of the project, from consultation, inception, implementation to post monitoring of the project.III. Community engagements, reviews and surveys will be conducted inorder to tailor make the project in the context of the actual needs of the affected people and not impose technologies or humanitarian assistance on them.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

I. Formal Environmental Impact Assessments will not be conducted but the project will use existing data on all impacts caused by natural disasters. Environmental Management Agency (EMA) is a member of the District Water and Sanitation Sub Committee (DWSSC) and has the mandate to advice DWSSC on possible negative environmental impacts and mitigation measures. The project will have sensitization and planning meetings at sub-district level where consultations with the communities will come up with possible negative environmental impacts and mitigation measures thereof.II. All environmental impacts that the project can cause will be mapped out and the mitigation measures thereof put in place.III. Tapping of underground water for household and irrigation (nutrition gardens) use will be controlled using the Zinwa standards and technical support recommendations to avoid over utilization of underground reserves.IV. Waste from all construction works will be disposed of at designated local Authority dumping/disposal sites.V. The project will consider the up- and down-stream impactsof water use and sanitation, as well as its cumulative impact on a watershed to prevent the effects of underground water contamination.VI. Goods with the minimum possible packaging, especially containers that can be reused or recycled will be purchased.
