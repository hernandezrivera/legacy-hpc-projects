1. Needs

Humanitarian situation has deteriorated in South Sudan after the outbreak of civil war in the neighboring Sudan in April 2023. The United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian affairs estimates that 9.4 million people which is 76% of the total population, will need assistance in 2023 through 2024. This is far greater than the number projected in 2023 and previous years. The drivers to this situation stems from cumulative and compounding effects of years of conflicts, sub national violence among communities, food insecurity, climate disasters and public health difficulties. The worse affected are the border areas receiving unprecedented influx of returnees and refugees from Sudan. According to IPC projection for the lean season April – July, 2023, 7.8 million were estimated to face crisis or worse food insecurity with Ayod, Duk, Twic East, Aweil East and Aweil North classified in phase 4 while Bor South was in IPC 3. According to IRNA conducted by partners in Northern Bahr el Ghazal in March prior to the Sudanese conflict in April, there were recorded 1,256 households or 7,536 individuals returning to Mangok payam of Aweil East county of Northern Bahr el Ghazal from Sudan or displaced elsewhere. By October, 317,993 refugees, asylum seekers and returnees have been registered to have crossed into South Sudan with 9,463 in Aweil (October OCHA). The vulnerable people comprised of women, children, and the elderly are exposed to bad weather and protection challenges, including limited water supply, shelter, sanitation services (open defecation) and lack of food etc. While provision of critical services to the affected population is constrained by poor infrastructure, lack of roads, flooding and funding constraints to respond to the needs of both new arrivals and receiving communities. There is a growing shortage of access to clean drinking water, and many recent immigrants depend on unclean or riverine water sources, which raises the risk of illness and increases the exposure to waterborne infections. The need for sufficient WASH support is highlighted by the fact that the current rainy season increases the danger of waterborne illnesses in transit areas (USAID August, 2023). According to the HRP 2023/24, Aweil East and Ayod are priority one counties while Aweil North is a priority two county and Bor South being a priority 3. GBI has been providing humanitarian responses since 2019 in Aweil East, Aweil North, Twic East, and Duk among other areas and will therefore, use its networks, experiences and establishment to provide assistance to the vulnerable communities affected by conflict and natural disaster through 2023/24. GBI will implement integrated programming with nutrition through provision of WASH services (WASH minimum package to the caregivers of Acutely Malnourished Children and provision of clean water) at nutrition centres, PSEA by conducting risk assessment before rehabilitation of boreholes while FSL will be integrated through provision of water for kitchen gardening activities at nutrition centers.

2. Project activities

Activities: 1. SAM children and their caretakers as well as Pregnant and Lactating women will be targeted at nutrition facilities with WASH services (minimum package for famine prevention comprising of Aqua taps, Soaps, collapsible jerry cans and buckets) to reduce disease transmission at the household level. 2. Nutrition facilities are also targeted with a basic water and sanitation package to reduce the risk of HAIs transmission due to poor WASH conditions. 3. Provision of WASH NFIs, hygiene promotion, emergency sanitation, and water supply to immediately break down disease transmission cycles. 4. Construction of quality sustainable infrastructures such as flood-resilient facilities, robust supply systems, and self-sustained maintenance mechanisms through enhanced community engagement and ownership. Activities recommended under this approach include repair/rehabilitation of water points, extension/upgrading of existing water supply systems, and upgrading water points with flood-resilient approaches for those located in flooded areas. Indicators: 1. 400 SAM children/caretakers provided with standard kits paired with hygiene promotion 2). Number of latrines constructed/rehabilitated in nutrition facilities (10 nutrition facilities with improved access to sanitation). 3). Nutrition of facilities with improved access to safe water (10 boreholes constructed/rehabilitated in nutrition facilities). 4). 3000 people reached through household water treatment. 5). Number of people having access to an improved water source (45,000 people reached with safe water other than household water treatment). 6). 30,000 people with access to sanitation facilities. 7). 30,000 people reached with hygiene promotion and soap. 8). 15,000 women and girls with improved access to water and/or sanitation after a safety audit. 9). 20 water points rehabilitated/constructed with a flood-resilient design. 10). 15 latrine stances rehabilitated/constructed with a flood-resilient design and also with disability inclusion.
