Project Summary

A Local to Global Protection (L2GP) study into protection and self-protection from 2014 confirmed many flaws in current internationally led responses and documented how externally driven interventions in some cases resulted in failure rather than strengthening community resilience. As a reaction towards these experiences, DCA/NCA has been working with EJ-YMCA in the West Bank (since late 2015) and MA’AN in Gaza (since 2019) adopting the survivor and community-led response (SCLR) approach, where 35 communities have been reached (22 in WB and 13 in Gaza) since then. This includes the use of community cash grants as a direct means for these communities to identify and implement their own resilience and protection responses. In summary, the findings suggest that SCLR that has been found to be effective in enabling communities to identify and address protection and resilience through their own initiatives and in accordance with their own priorities. Such approach has been also found to foster the community resilience and spur a sense of dignity along with a revival of an otherwise faltering culture of volunteerism. The SCLR empowers women and youth at individual, family, and community levels as well as being a useful tool for communities to hold local authorities accountable and push them to deliver on things like essential services.
The proposed project will build on this and reinforce the targeted communities’ resilience with its definition as “The ability of a system, locality or household to resist, absorb, accommodate and recover from the effects of hazards and shocks in a timely and efficient manner through the preservation and restoration of its essential basic structures and functions”.
The proposed project will target six vulnerable communities (3 in the West Bank and 3 in Gaza). DCA/NCA and its partners in WB (EJ-YMCA) and Gaza (MAAN Development Centre) will reform the existing community protection groups (PGs) to make sure they represent all societal groups of the community including women, youth and PwD’s. The PG members will be trained to identify the risks, vulnerabilities, existing assets and resources and will prioritize and develop community action plans accordingly. The protection groups will use the community cash grant to implement their action plan towards addressing the community level protection threats and needs. The planned targeted communities in Gaza are: Al Farata area (Biet Hanoun), Abasan Al Kabira (Khanyounis) and Al Naser area (Rafah), while the targeted communities in the West Bank are: Kisan, Mansheye and Fqeqes (Area C).
The project objective is: To empower vulnerable and marginalised communities in the WB and Gaza Strip (including women and youth) to plan and implement inclusive, accountable and collective efforts to better withstand chronic and intermittent shocks and mitigate protection threats.
This is in line with the HRP objective #2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL, and the protection cluster objective # 2: Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence. The intervention will be coordinated with protection cluster and other relevant actors in the targeted communities.

1 - Needs

Significant humanitarian and protection challenges prevail, including a continuing need for protection measures in the targeted communities in both WB and Gaza– who are experiencing or are at risk of, conflict, violence, displacement, denial of access to livelihoods, entrenched levels of food insecurity, high levels of poverty and unemployment, and declining ability to cope with the prolonged nature of the humanitarian crisis. In the West Bank: Village councils in the targeted communities lack ability to provide basic services to its constituents due to both shortage of resources and continuous pressure from the occupation. Targeted communities are in area C of the West Bank in which Israeli occupation forces are increasingly threatening the presence of these communities by seizing further lands, exploiting natural resources, and facilitating the sharp increase of illegal settlers in the area. Israeli authorities’ policies and practices applied have created a coercive environment, which constantly pressure Palestinians to leave their communities. The number of demolitions orders and actual demolitions of houses, schools, livelihoods is increasing, service infrastructure is mainly denied, restrictions on farming and grazing land is growing accompanied with lack of any enforcement responding to violence from illegal settlers.The communities targeted in the West Bank ( Kisan, Mansheye and Fqeqes) suffer from many major issues mainly lack of reliable representation, land confiscations, increase number of demolitions, increase in settler populations and settler violence, refusal of Israeli army to announce master plans , psychological problems suffered by the community members, as well as gender discrimination. Rural Palestinian women have not been able to participate on equal terms to realize their economic, political, cultural and social rights. The communities also suffer from lack of major life necessities such as proper roads, proper sewage systems, high unemployment, incomplete education services, inconsistent public transportation, lack of basic health care and social services, and unreliable civil policing.In Gaza: The targeted communities in Gaza witness high levels of aid dependency with deteriorated public infrastructure and poor access to basic services. The communities in Gaza are constantly threatened by impending disaster and challenged by the deteriorating conditions for social development. Devastating military conflicts put civilians in danger. Environmental degradation increases as the trapped, urbanized population grows and puts increasing stress on limited resources such as water. These natural and manmade threats combined with a tenuous economic and social development situation prove the breeding ground for disaster.The three vulnerable communities in Gaza face 1) health risks due to deteriorated WASH situation (i.e., sewage flooding, cesspit tanks and bad management of solid waste) in addition to the bad water quality, 2) poor infrastructure with unsafe/risky and poor conditions of sandy roads that hinder safe movement of people especially during night-time and winter season. This also hinders the access of children to schools, 3) bad socioeconomic situation with high rates of food insecurity, 4) gender discrimination including depriving women from inheritance 5) health risks due to delay responding to health needs during acute emergencies (i.e., first aid), 6), GBV risks (i.e., due to inadequate WCs inside schools/lack of privacy, poor access to GBV services, lack of community knowledge) and psycho-social Support (PSS) needs. 7) Lack of safe spaces for children.Worthy mentioning that further in-depth assessment for community needs and protection threats (considering the needs and vulnerabilities of different groups in the community including women, children, elderlies and PwD’s) will be conducted by the PGs during implementation using participatory need assessment tools to identify, update and analyse the community vulnerabilities and priorities that will inform the design of the community initiatives. The assessment tools to be used are the Action Learning in Crisis (PALC) in Gaza and Participatory Vulnerability and Capacity Assessment (PVCA) in the West Bank. Gender-specific characteristics will be considered in the targeted locations (e.g., level of access to and control over resources, gender division of labour, etc.)

2 - Activities

Formulation, mobilization and building capacities of PGs inside the targeted communities:• PGs to be reformed through community elections to ensure that all community factions and groups are well-represented. PGs will act as the voice of the community in dialogue with the duty bearers. They lead the response, liaison with stakeholders, defend the rights of the community, and ensure the fair and accountable utilization of resources. A total of six PGs (three in Gaza and three in WB) will include 60 members, 50% are females.• The capacity of PGs will be strengthened to enable them carry out their role in their communities and to oversee the implementation of gender-sensitive, culturally responsive and participatory actions. PGs will receive trainings on PLAC (Gaza communities), PVCA (WB communities), accountability, monitoring, networking, fundraising and CHS. • The volunteers of the PGs, together with their communities, will conduct PALC assessment (in Gaza communities) and PVCA (in WB communities), as part of the process to identify the community needs and vulnerabilities that should be addressed through the community initiatives. • Community feedback and information sessions will be conducted by PGs to announce findings of the assessments and agree on the identified vulnerabilities and needs. • Based on the assessment findings, the PGs will develop action plans that will inform the process of designing the community initiatives (6 community action plans to be developed). Community Cash grants and Implementation of community initiatives:• PGs to design their community initiatives and apply for the community cash grants• The applications/ for community grants will be reviewed by a committee that encompasses representatives from the PGs, CBOs, community leaders and partners. • Capacity building training will be conducted on cash grant management skills (procurement, grant management, planning, finance, etc.) for the PGs.• The community grants will be paid to the PGs over two (2) equal instalments. The average amount of the community cash grant is 5,500 USD. The PGs will be encouraged to seek contributions (financial or in-kind) from the targeted communities and other stakeholders such as the municipalities and the private sector. • A total of 12 community grants will be paid to the PGs (six for Gaza and six in WB – two grants per community) to implement the community initiatives in coordination and cooperation with the CBOs, municipalities, and other relevant stakeholders.• A total of 12 community initiatives to be implemented in both WB and Gaza, where a total of 3,000 community members are expected to benefit from the implemented initiatives (810 Boys, 810 girls, 710 women, 670 men – including 119 PwD’s) The PGs in the targeted communities will work in close coordination and cooperation with the CBOs inside their communities. The CBOs will be involved in monitoring the implementation of the project activities and help the PGs addressing the difficulties and challenges they face during their work. Furthermore, the PGs will collaborate closely with the municipalities in the targeted areas. The project contributes to improve resilience, alleviate suffering, and enhance preparedness mechanisms of the most vulnerable communities. While responding to the current deteriorating humanitarian situation, the intervention support communities in developing and implementing sustainable strategies to anticipate and respond to threats in the long run, building on their own resources and strengths. The project approach strengthens the capacity of the communities to self-mobilise, self-organise, anticipate, design, plan and implement their own projects and initiatives based on their own collective analyses of opportunities, threats, and priorities. This approach is all about the transfer of power, ownership and leadership from donors and INGOs to local communities. The local partners develop strong relation with the communities they work with and keep following up with the Protection Groups and other community members to assist them when necessary, according to their priorities. The project team will support the communities to enhance networking and advocacy initiatives with duty bearers (governmental, non-governmental institutions, community leaders and decision makers, etc.) that in turn enhances the sustainability. DCA/NCA will conduct a PSEA session to its and partners’ staff at the beginning of the project and will ensure that a plan is put in place on how the partners will raise awareness about PSEA among the targeted communities. PSEA will be addressed through the capacity building training for PGs. DCA/NCA and its partners will develop and disseminate awareness raising materials including messages about PSEA and the available complaints mechanisms and their entry points.Activities will be ensured to be accessible to both women and men (e.g. when organizing training sessions, adapt tools/ methods to suit participants’ level of literacy, ensure that everyone is available at the planned times and locations of meetings or training sessions, and ensure that women’s participation would not lead to them being harmed; plan support measures so that women can participate actively and safely in the management of the project and in decision making), use sensitive language to gain support of the community members and leaders to avoid social conflicts.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

Providing community cash grants will enable the PGs to address multisectoral community needs and protection threats in efficient way. A critical underlying aspect of the mechanism is to seek to transfer decision-making power and agency to affected people and community groups to enable them to better respond to their own needs and priorities. Providing the cash grants directly to the PGs will 1) enhance PGs capacity and networking with the market actors “PGs will be introduced to the best practices doing procurement and enhance the self -learning”, 2) enhance ownership and motivation of PGs and other community members to actively participate in the process 3) enhance cost efficiency where the PGs can get lower prices to implement the initiatives, through their connections with the suppliers/vendors/labors in their communities. Consequently, this will ensure the sustainability for the PGs roles in their communities after the end of the project. This will be accompanied by enhancing the local accountability measures (PGs who are doing procurements to be accountable for their communities by sharing information and receiving feedback through different channels).

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

The core element of the SCLR approach is the inclusive and collective community participation in needs identifications, response design and implementation of the initiatives. The methodology stresses the importance of the collective work of the communities and the inclusiveness of all community sectors and groups. Facebook pages that will be developed by PGs, mosques, schools etc. are spaces were communities use to inform and encourage participation and feedback. The vulnerabilities and needs of targeted communities will be identified using the participatory approaches (PVCA and PALC) with direct involvement of the communities and considering the different needs of vulnerable groups. After mobilizing a set of volunteers from the community who represent all societal groups (Protection Groups), the partners will support them to conduct the PVCA (Participatory Vulnerability Capacity Assessment) and PALC (Participatory Action Learning in Crisis). These tools are developed to enable community groups to develop the outlines of their needs by analysing and evaluating the vulnerabilities and risks in the community area as well as their strengths, capacities, and available resources. Based on the results, the community action plans will be developed with the involvement of the various community groups. The PG members will announce for a public meeting where all community members are invited to join and vote for top priorities to be addressed. During the implementation of the initiatives, the PGs will be the focal points for their communities, to receive and respond to feedback raised by community members. PGs will discuss the received feedback among the PG members and with the partner organizations to agree on the proper response and take corrective measures as relevant. The feedback will be documented and analyzed to feed into the decision-making process and inform possible corrections during implementation that ensures a better response to the needs.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The project adopts gender-sensitive and culturally responsive approach to implement the project activities which will highly contribute to address the social and cultural risks. DCA/NCA and partners fully recognize and understand differences, norms, taboos, and stigmas surrounding social and cultural back draughts of the right holders. All safety and security measures will be considered when implementing the project activities to prevent incidences and reduce safety and security risks. DCA/NCA and partners will keep close monitoring for the security situation and will advise the donor and partners in case any emergency indicators arise. DCA/NCA is a Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) certified agency(www.hqai.org) and works continuously to improve the quality and effectiveness of the assistance it provides to the affected population. Internal conflicts and family feuds that might take place can lead to communities being divided into different neighbourhoods and/or families where members of the conflicted parties would not cooperate with others. DCA/NCA and its partners, EJ-YMCA and MAAN, realize the sensitive nature of such internal conflicts. Therefore, the project coordinators usually emphasize the importance of inclusivity in our approach and the benefit of all community members working together collectively. Despite the fact that the mobilizing of the community might take more time than anticipated, project coordinators use several mechanisms to help get all neighbourhoods and families to meet together such as requesting the help of a respected figure in the community and/or conducting a small meeting which includes the heads of each family/neighbourhood and finding ways to work together. From previous experience, project mobilizers have been successful and with time, good relations started between the different families in the targeted communities. Usually, women protection group members play a vital role in alleviating the tension or preventing the escalation of any conflict. Also, the EJ-YMCA and MAAN ensures to train all target communities on the CHS principles, on, the sharing of information, participation, and complaints.The project’s methodology aims to create the space necessary for the communities to challenge the restrictions addressing their needs and priorities. The project does this by enabling the communities to access and enhance control over resources and assets. The project targets systems and structures to facilitate such access to resources. It also provides them the safe platforms to raise their voices, advocate for their rights and mobilize the available resources to meet the needs of their communities. PSEA mainstreaming: DCA/NCA implements a code of conduct that is signed by all its staff that reflects among others the values and standards regarding PSEA. DCA/NCA ensures that the agreement with implementing partners consists of an article that requires its partner to have an internal staff code of conduct in place that reflects as a minimum the values and standards of partners regarding PSEA. • DCA/NCA will ensure that the target groups and communities have an accessible, a confidential and a safe complaint handling mechanisms during the implementation of the project, where the sensitive complaints are dealt with and acted upon with confidentiality (either by using DCA’/NCA’s already functioning complaints mechanism: me.complaints@dca.dkand/ or using the partners complaints mechanism channels: MAAN Website: www.maancomplaints.com Hotline: 1800900901, EJ-YMCA: Mobile: +970562610061, Email: complaints@ej-ymca.org.• DCA/NCA will conduct a PSEA training to its and partners’ staff and for the PGs and will ensure that a plan is put in place on how to raise awareness about PSEA among the targeted communities.• DCA/NCA’s PSEA local focal point is an active member in the oPt-HCT-PSEA Network. Accordingly, DCA/NCA will continuously update its partners with relevant and needed PSEA information.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

How does the project enable equal and impartial access to assistance and services and the targeting of vulnerable groups and people with specific needs? The equal and impartial access to the project services will be ensured through the participatory and inclusive approach the project adopts. The PGs will include representatives from different vulnerable groups including women and people with disabilities. The project relies on the localization and shifting power to people, where it provides them with the safe platforms to raise their voices, advocate for their rights and mobilize the available resources to meet the needs and respond to risks of their communities,Activities will be ensured to be accessible to both women and men (e.g. when organizing training sessions, adapt tools/ methods to suit participants’ level of literacy, ensure that everyone is available at the planned times and locations of meetings or training sessions, and ensure that women’s participation would not lead to them being harmed; plan support measures so that women can participate actively and safely in the management of the project and in decision making), use sensitive language to gain support of the community members and leaders to avoid social conflicts.The local partners and DCA/NCA staff in Gaza have experience working under conditions that lead to restricted humanitarian access and adjust timing and approach as required. DCA/NCA has developed an Emergency Preparedness and Response Plan for assuring access. Networking with CBOs and NGOs in the targeted areas, as well as empowering women leaders within safe spaces, will guarantee access of vulnerable groups to project services. DCA\NCA access approach is consistent with the humanitarian core principles (impartiality, neutrality, independence, and humanity) in accordance with IHL. Partners apply: 1) Humanity in addressing the human suffering of most vulnerable people; 2) Neutrality so that humanitarian aid must not favour any political side; 3) Impartiality in providing services to women and girls based on need and without discrimination; and 4) Independence in advancing the autonomy of humanitarian project objectives from political, economic, or other objectives.DCA\NCA with its partners monitor access and discrimination through disaggregated indicators by age, gender, and location or specific group (e.g., persons with disabilities, elderly). Furthermore, to manage security risks and access, DCA/NCA employs a security focal point who monitors the security situation daily, exchanges information with other international organizations and the UN and updates staff. Standard security procedures are in place, which all staff are aware of and follow. DCA/NCA has an extensive network of both humanitarian and development partners, as well as actively participates in the humanitarian cluster system, which facilitates information sharing regarding recent security incidents and forecasting of events. DCA/NCA is connected and follows UNDSS security updates, which is shared with its local partners, who also follow their local networks for security updates. Additionally, daily assessment for the security situation on the ground in coordination with relevant organizations, partner CBO’s and local committees in the area will take place to ensure the safety and security of staff.
