Project Summary

Based on multisectoral needs assessments carried out in the Far North, the North-West, South-West, Littoral and West regions in 2022, coupled with the monitoring of access to water, hygiene and sanitation services, it is estimated that 1.9 million of people (51% of female and 49% of male) in Cameroon will need humanitarian assistance in water, sanitation and hygiene. These includes 0.6 million of Internal displaced population and 0.4 million of returnees.
This project will ensure children and families have access to WASH services and adopt good hygiene practices and essential WASH services. Specific activities will include distribution of individual hygiene kits, family WASH kits including water purification supplies (aqua-tabs) and jerry cans for water storage. Distribution of hygiene kits will be accompanied with hygiene promotion, including guidance on the correct use of WASH infrastructure that has been set-up for the emergency (if the affected population usually practice open defecation). There will be a focus on the rehabilitation of Community Water points/small water supply systems. Water management Committees (WMC) will be revitalized/ trained to ensure water infrastructure is maintained. The programme will involve the construction of family/household latrines through the provision of latrine digging kits and squatting slabs. The affected population will be encouraged to use locally available materials to construct family pit latrines. Only families without latrines will be selected. WASH Committees will be formed in each of the target areas, and as community volunteers, their main role will be to guide people on the operation and management of their latrines. Each family will be responsible for cleaning, maintenance and decommissioning of its latrine. The affected population will also be trained on how to treat and reuse squatting slabs once the pits are full. Displaced and vulnerable individuals will be provided with adequate knowledge and means to ensure basic personal hygiene practices, and to maintain existing WASH infrastructure. Hygiene promotion activities will be implemented in line with the recommended WHO infection prevention and control measures. For effective and efficient implementation of the Hygiene promotion activities under the rapid response modality, hygiene promotion messages will disseminate. One of the main objectives of the programme will be to ensure access to safe drinking water for vulnerable people (mainly IDPs and host communities). This will mean prioritizing communities hosting IDPs who do not have access to adequate safe water, rehabilitating non-functioning water points serving communities and in the vicinity of health centers, markets or schools and targeting non-functioning water points in returnee areas, including in key institutions such as health facilities and child-friendly spaces. The project location is in the NW region, in Bui, Donga Mantung, and Mezam Division). The project is expected to reach 9000 people before the end of 2023

1 - Besoins / Needs

Cameroon remains a lower-middle-income country with insufficient basic social services such as health, education, water and sanitation. This situation is exacerbated by internally displacement of population, and poverty. Insecurity resulted from a socio-political crisis in Southwest and Northwest regions, population moved from their localities to new area with or without water and sanitation facilities to seek for security, save their life and hope for wellbeing. As result, part of Cameroon population is still in need of humanitarian assistance in the water, sanitation and hygiene sector. Based on multisectoral needs assessments carried out in the Far North, the North-West, South-West, Littoral and West regions in 2022, coupled with the monitoring of access to water, hygiene and sanitation services, it is estimated that 1.9 million of people (51% of female and 49% of male) in Cameroon will need humanitarian assistance in water, sanitation and hygiene. These includes 0.6 million of Internal displaced population and 0.4 million of returneesThe displacement of population due to insecurity, the insufficient investment of Cameroon Government in basic social services and the insufficient response provided by Humanitarian actor in the Northwest and South west regions continue to exacerbate the water and sanitation need of affected population including in the neighboring regions (Littoral and West regions). The last MSNA surveys conducted reveals that 55 per cent of assessed village collect water from an unimproved water point. In some division of Northwest (Menchum and Ngo-ketunjia) and Southwest region (Ndian and Manyu), at least 75% of assessed localities collect water from an unimproved water point. The three main challenges relative to water are the insufficient of improved water point, the long distance (due to insufficient of safe drinking water point) and the lack of containers to collect and store water. Concerning sanitation, although open defecation is low (2.4 per cent of assessed villages), utilization of pit latrine without slap is predominant (78.8 per cent) among assessed villages. This represents a high risk of contamination of surface and groundwater used by population in concerned localities.
