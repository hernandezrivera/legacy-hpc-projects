Project Summary

In this project IOM will ensure that the most vulnerable population affected by conflict and/or natural disaster with specific needs have timely access to emergency shelter and non-food items. IOM's plan is to distribute 3,000 emergency shelters and 9,000 NFI kits to vulnerable populations in all Darfur states as well as South Kordofan and West Kordofan. Surveys and assessment will be conducted to prioritize the most vulnerable individuals and households.
Before conducting the assessments, IOM will coordinate with the EFI sector and other partners to avoid duplication of effort. As mentioned in the needs there has been a high number households registered in these areas who are in need of support and/or interventions including emergency shelter and NFIs. IOM will also provide training for beneficiaries on proper distribution and construction techniques. IOM will ensure through the distributions that 55% of the selected beneficiary population will be females. IOM will take all the feedback from the beneficiaries following the completion of the post-distribution monitoring mission and collect this information to be used to inform future planning e.g. kit item selection, types of techniques and training provided.

Needs

According to IOM DTM data, IOM has registered new caseloads during 2018. In Central Darfur, 7420 IDP households were registered, in South Darfur, 3225 IDP households were registered and in South Kordofan 2223 IDP households. IOM has registered 1446 returnee households in the Um Dukhun locale in 2018. Up to the end of 2018, a total of 59096 individuals were registered in Golo, of which 54,594 individuals are returnees and 4502 are IDPs. 63 % of the registered caseload are under 18 years old, of which 30 % are under 5 years. All the registered returnees were IDP returnees. Up until the end of 2018 , a total of 35218 IDPs were registered in Nertiti. Up until the same period 6015 IDPs and 73180 returnees were registered in Rokoro. As the general situation in Darfur remains unpredictable and displacement camps/sites continue to receive new arrivals, it is crucial for IOM as one of the first partners to be deployed to displacement sites to establish preparedness to support the sector in the provision of emergency shelter assistance, especially in the stocking of improved emergency shelter assistance for the most vulnerable groups. Additionally, to mitigate the risk of prolonged displacement as a result of destroyed or torched villages, it is important to provide IDPs with sheltering materials that can be easily transported to areas of return and can additionally be used to support in the reconstruction of permanent shelters (e.g. bamboo and wooden poles which can be used as roofing support structure).

Activities

1. Conduct needs assessments to identify and prioritize the most vulnerable households within each community Throughout consultation with the community, including gender sensitive groupings. 2. Durable construction techniques training, ensuring that constructed structures will be capable of withstanding disaster related risks among others. Training will also include promoting alternatives to firewood such as fuel-efficient cooking and livelihood options to preserve environment.3. Train distribution committees in target camps and communities of high displacement and return on identification of PSN, and on gender considerations related to the participation of women in the distribution process and the roles played by males in promoting women’s safety. 4. Train beneficiaries on Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and construction of safe and durable emergency shelters to support community early recovery response and facilitate their own livelihoods; raise awareness on environmental impacts, mitigation and use of environmentally friendly alternative materials. IOM will also add conditional cash transfer/ cash for work approach as an initial form of awarding the beneficiaries for their efforts, stimulating self-reliance. 5. Purchase, preposition, and provide improved emergency shelters for 3,000 HHs of the most vulnerable protracted IDPs and returnees based on assessed needs and PSN criteria utilizing environmental-friendly material and locally acceptable shelters. 6. Purchase and provide ES/NFI assistance to benefit 9,000 crisis and disaster affected IDPs and vulnerable mobile households, prioritizing female, child and elderly headed households. 7. Conduct post distribution and monitoring (PDM) in areas where ES/NFI and improved emergency shelters have been distributed. The PDM will include household to household interviews on the usage of distributed materials, beneficiary satisfaction, and complaints on the quality and quantity of received materials.
