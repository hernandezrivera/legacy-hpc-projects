The country has experienced multiple emergencies, including disease outbreaks, floods, and conflict-related displacement. The impacts of these factors on the health system are further worsened by the Sudan conflict which has resulted in an influx of over 810,000 refugees and returnees (UNCHR -October 2024) into already vulnerable communities with very weak health systems. The project seeks to decrease excess morbidity and mortality rates from illnesses such as malaria, diarrhea, acute respiratory infections, and vaccine-preventable illnesses like measles.
The project will reach 22,570 individuals (4,850 girls under 5 years, 5,597 boys under 5 years, 5,486 females over 5 years, 6,637 males over 5 years) at a cost of 50,000 USD.

1. Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan remains dire, characterized by a protracted humanitarian crisis, widespread displacement, food insecurity, and limited access to basic services, including healthcare (OCHA 2023). The country has experienced multiple emergencies, including disease outbreaks, floods, and conflict-related displacement. In 2024, an estimated 9 million people need humanitarian assistance. Out of these, 6.3 million need health services. Key health indicators lag behind global targets, with a maternal mortality ratio of 295 per 100,000 live births in 2017 (WHO, 2024) and an under-5 mortality rate ranging from 30 to 112 per 1,000 live births across different states (WHO, 2024). The impacts of these factors on the health system are further worsened by the Sudan conflict which has resulted in an influx of over 810,000 refugees and returnees (UNCHR -October 2024) into already vulnerable communities with very weak health systems among which include Panyikang and Baliet Counties.In upper Nile state an estimated 895,000 people (56.9 percent of the state’s population) are classified in IPC Phase 3 or above, of which 10,000 people are in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). IPC projections of December 2024 to March 2025 puts Baliet County in IPC 3 (Crisis) while Panyikang in IPC phase 4(Emergency). Projections of April to July 2025 puts all the two targeted locations in IPC 4(Emergency) highlighting severe food insecurity and a critical humanitarian need.The ongoing flooding in Panyikang County has displaced over 11000 individuals leaving them in dire need of humanitarian assistance including health services. The flooding further blocked off the already weak health facilities disrupting access to essential health services and increasing vulnerabilities. (Panyikang IRNA assessment, August 2024). Displaced population face increased risks of waterborne diseases such as cholera and diarrhea as well as malaria The floods have also compounded logistical challenges in reaching the affected populations. In this County patients move long distances to access health services, lack of referral services and home deliveries. This puts the vulnerable children, women, elderly and people living with disability at more risk of mortality worsened by protection concerns against women and girls. ACRA remains the only organization providing health services for the Panyikang county under the HSTP project. ACRA needs assessment conducted in September 2024, indicated there is still a substantial health services gap in Panyikang County that has led to low health indicators the survey showed majority of communities drink water from the river, 80% lack mosquito nets, 65% of mothers has home deliveries and challenges included unavailability of some health services in remote areas, limited referral options, poor drug storage facilities and shortage of essential medicine. In January 2023 Baliet County received influx of over 9000 returnees from Jonglei state due to communal conflict that occurred between October and December 2022. (Protection cluster Upper Nile State – Flash report Feb 2023). The large influx of returnees and displaced people which continues to date in these locations is significantly straining the already fragile health systems and limited resources in these areas. This influx has overwhelmed existing health facilities, which were already struggling with inadequate infrastructure, a shortage of medical supplies, and a limited number of trained health workers. Additionally, Baliets geographic isolation, lack of transportation, poor referral system, lack of drug storage in health facility and insecurity further hinder access to essential services. The proposed project is seeks to deliver integrated health services to increase and expand equitable coverage and access, especially for hard-to-reach populations

2. Project activities

Agency for Child relief Aid (ACRA) will undertake the following activities. Activity. 1. Conduct regular assessments of health facilities on IPC to ensure identified gaps are addressed. ACRA will provide IPC materials like handwashing stations PPEs etc to 3 health facilities and 3 mobile sites. We will ensure training of health workers on IPC. Activity 2. Strengthen routine immunization services through fixed and mobile outreach teams to ensure PENTA3 coverage of children under one years. This will include community mobilisation and use of boma health teams reaching 980 (480F, 500M). Activity 3. Provide rehabilitation services at the health facilities and outreach services to 750 individuals in need. Activity 4. Provide mental health services to 760 (418M, 342F) individuals by integrating mental health consultations into primary health care, training health workers and ensuring referrals. Activity 5. Provide outpatient consultations at static and mobile outreaches especially in hard-to-reach areas. ACRA will ensure use of MOH treatment guidelines during this activity reaching 17750 (9763M, 7987F). ACRA will distribute insect treated mosquito nets and community awareness on malaria prevention to reduce on malaria mortality and morbidity. 6. Provide BEMONC services. ACRA will train skilled birth attendants and equip the health facilities with BEMONC equipment and supplies. Community awareness on the importance of skilled deliveries will be created through the project. The project will conduct 260 skilled deliveries. Activity 7. Establish clinical management of rape services in health facilities and provide training to health workers on survivor centered care. ACRA will raise awareness about the availability of the services. Activity 8. Provide MHPSS services to 1350 (670M, 680F) and offer psychosocial support through community-based sessions, group counselling and individual counselling. Train health workers on MHPSS. Activity 9. Strengthen disease surveillance system and train health workers on IDSR. Activity 10. Conduct reactive vaccination campaigns in areas with measles outbreaks. Activity 11. Preposition and distribute interagency emergency health kits to 3 supported health facilities and outreach sites. This will ensure timely replenishment of essential medical supplies. Activity 12. Conduct awareness sessions to mitigate protection risks (GBV) among vulnerable populations accessing health services. Activity 13. Establish feedback mechanisms at health facilities and outreach centres to ensure community concerns are addressed promptly. Activity 14. Conduct monthly health awareness sessions focusing on key health topics. Activity 15. Conduct monthly coordination meetings with County health department to plan and review activities. Activity 16. Conduct community consultative meeting to identify health needs to improve on service delivery.
