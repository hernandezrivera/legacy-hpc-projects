Project Summary

RI aims to provide prevention, empowerment, and response GBV services and child protection (CP) prevention and response services through a standalone and integrated approach with RI health services in the targeted area. RI will target the most vulnerable women, girls, and boys including people with disability, widowed women and children living in widows camps, women and children headed households, adolescent girls, adolescent boys, and older women. RI will utilize a community-based approach to enhance access to these groups through outreach and engagement with community committees. RI will support 4 WGSSs in Teftanaz subdistrict in Idleb, and Azaz subdistrict in Aleppo. RI will strengthen its integrated model of health and protection services to prevent and respond to GBV, building the capacity of all staff (GBV caseworkers, PSS workers, and outreach teams) through provision of training by RI technical team, utilizing materials endorsed by the capacity building initiative for GBV and GBV sub-cluster (SC) including GBV case management (CM) training. Supervision mechanisms including assessing attitudes, knowledge, and skills and providing staff care through in-person and online supervision and follow up, will ensure quality of service. RI will use its supported health facility in Teftanaz - AlNour Maternity and Children Hospital - as an entry point, linking survivors to specialized services provided in the WGSS. RI will coordinate with other actors through the referral pathway developed by GBV SC, and RI will regularly engage with the localized service mapping in the target areas to ensure safe referral and avoid duplication.
In line with the GBV Minimum Standards in Emergencies, the services provided within RI WGSSs are GBV response including CM, psychological first aid, individual and group counseling, and referral to clinical management of rape, mental health, and non GBV services based on the needs and informed consent of the survivors and women and girls at risk in addition to prevention services. RI will support the establishment of women’s committees to foster the participation of women in decision-making structures as well as build on community protective mechanisms already in place. RI will work on capacity building for all staff, including health workers, to be aware of GBV concepts and deal with GBV survivors upon disclosure.
RI will also provide CP services including outreach activities, awareness-raising on CP concepts, structured PSS for boys and girls including children with disabilities, parenting skills, and providing CP case management for children with, and at risk of, child protection risks. RI will ensure engagement of the community through establishing and training CP committees from the community to be responsible for raising awareness on CP concerns in the targeted areas, as well as identification and referral of children at risk to response services provided at the three child friendly spaces (CFS) that will be supported in Azaz.
All activities will be implemented while adhering to strict COVID 19 prevention measures. Hotline service will be used additionally to provide online information sharing, psychological first aid, and referral.
The GBV services will be monitored, measured, and evaluated with recommendations to improve the services delivered in the targeted areas according to the GBV MEAL toolkits developed by the GBV SC, focusing on the quality of services benefiting from the supervision mechanisms and monitoring tools including the survivor satisfaction survey. CP services will follow a MEAL approach including monitoring, evaluation, accountability, and learning which is supported by technical expertise in gender, age & disability, child protection and consistent and accurate flow of information and data in relation to gender, age, and disability.

3 - Needs

According to VOICES FROM SYRIA 2022, the common violence mentioned by women and girls in NWS was IPV and family violence, digital/online violence, restrictions of rights and movements, denial of education - linked to early marriage and child labor, deprivation of inheritance especially widows, sexual exploitation and harassment and early marriage. Women and girls with disability are most commonly subjected to violence. Widowed women are at increased risk for forced re-marriage and forced abandonment of children, movement restrictions by family members, and social and community violence (stigma, shaming). According to HNO 2023, CP risks were grave violations against children, neglect of persons with disabilities, and already children with disabilities being denied access to basic rights, and persisting violence including physical, emotional, sexual, and domestic violence. Psychosocial distress, family separation, and child labor. By September 2022, 1,895 grave violations were verified by the MRM in Syria. Of those, 1,291 children were cases of recruitment and use of children (69 girls and 1,222 boys). Ninety-six percent of children live in communities where child labor is reported. Another 84 % of children live in communities where child marriage is an issue for girls (15 -17 years) and 52 % of people live in communities where family separation is reported. Psychosocial distress among children is reported in 28 percent of surveyed households (2 % higher than in 2022).

4 - Implementation of activities

The project will support 4 WGSSs and 1 GBV team in a hospital, in addition to 3 CFSs.GBV response services for survivors and women and girls at risk will include case management, focused PSS, referrals, and PFA. GBV prevention includes outreach, dignity kits distribution, and PFA - especially in emergency response in coordination with protection cluster, awareness-raising utilizing the GBV SC toolkit, and vocational training after consultation with women and girls. RI will conduct safety audits to services and facilities, risk mapping, contextualize participatory GBV key messages, integrated awareness-raising for men and boys as well as psychoeducation to address behavioral problems to enhance their engagement in the GBV programming. CP services will include CP case management and CP community-based approach, which will be provided by trained and supervised CP staff. All supplies will be provided to the CFS including the PSS toolkits for children and adolescent boys and girls. Child protection networks (CPNs) will be established and maintained based on parent volunteers, selected in the awareness-raising sessions. Supervision will be provided remotely and physically to CP staff to ensure quality service provision in line with the CP Minimum Standards. Parenting sessions and structured PSS will be conducted in the CFS and on the community level.Transportation will be provided for women girls and boys to access the services in WGSSs and CFSs.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

First risk: Lack of quality services and referral expose survivors to harm, enhancing the risk of stigmatization, reprisal, and further trauma in addition to the possibility of breaching confidentiality, putting at risk SGBV survivors due to lack of capacity on information management of service providers. Likelihood: High; Impact: High. RI will implement the following mitigation measures: 1. Case management with survivors to be conducted by trained personnel only; 2. Service mapping includes only those that can provide survivor-centered services; 3. Mainstream messaging on the risk of the available legal systems which is likely to cause harm to survivors; 4. Self-care strategies as part of programmers and organization HR policy. Second Risk: Risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls, and boys (and their caregivers) through the provision of specialized CP services if service providers are not adequately trained/services are of low quality. Likelihood: High; Impact: High. RI will mitigate by 1. Ensuring training packages are in line with child protection minimum standards and considering remote management modality; 2. Services should not be provided by any staff who have not received and demonstrated proficiency in training modules; 3. Include a practice element to training modules that can be overseen through remote mentorship arrangements; 4. Service mappings should only include services that are determined to provide safe and quality services.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The project aims to provide life skills, skills development, and vocational training for women and adolescent girls in addition to linking them with livelihood opportunities. However, livelihood opportunities may increase risks of exploitation by service providers or by another actor in the community and RI will work on mitigation measures through the following: 1. Consult women and men, boys and girls in the design of the program. 2. Ensure participants know services were to disclose if violence occurs 3. Raise awareness on PSEA and other types of exploitation and where to report 4. Establish good relations with local authorities, clarify humanitarian principles, and stick to them. 5. Ensure the compliant and feedback mechanisms are accessible for all women and girls. Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information is linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific person/groups. Likelihood: medium. Impact: High. RI will mitigate the risk by 1. Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and who services are for. 2. Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA. 3. Provide awareness-raising sessions on PSEA for boys, girls, and parents 4. Provide child-friendly feedback and complaints mechanisms.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: The CFSs and WGSSs are located in relatively safe areas. Risk: Armed Clashes, arrest and detention, shelling/artillery, VBIEDs/IEDs. Mitigation: staff to contact the security focal point and alert all staff to be prepared for immediate evacuation/hibernation if possible. Activate an emergency plan. Assumption: Communities will engage in CP program activities. The risk is that existing traditional practices and community structures conflict with child protection principles (e.g. child rights messaging is perceived as a threat). RI will mitigate the risk by 1. Training some staff as key resource persons to act as trainers/ facilitators. 2. Regular engagement/communication with communities through the CPNs and community leaders. Assumption: WGSSs will be operated smoothly in the targeted communities especially in the widow's camps. Risk: Resistance or backlash from sections of the community and community leaders on WGSS support in widows’ camps. Mitigation: RI will use their existing access through health services in the area to strengthen community engagement from the onset of the project, from the assessment phase, and throughout all project cycles including meetings with religious and community leaders and engagement with the community and local authorities and clear communication on project objectives to ensure the ability to establish the WGSSs. RI will target all surrounding camps not only the widow’s camps to avoid any stigmatization for the project clients.
