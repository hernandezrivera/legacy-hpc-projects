Project Summary

This project contributes to attainment of strategic Objective 2 by addressing protection issues regarding Housing, Land and Property (HLP) rights including illicit occupation of HLP, limited knowledge of legal protection of HLP rights of IDPs, HLP based communal conflict, limited provision of legal information, counseling and lack of sustainable and innovative community based HLP dispute resolution mechanisms.
Therefore, this project seeks to prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and community resilience to prevent and address housing land and property conflicts associated with displacement. These will be achieved through; (1) Raising awareness on HLP and the law, on spot monitoring and response to assist Returnees, IDPs and Host Community reclaim their land and properties, (2) provision of free legal aid and (3) strengthening and establishment of innovative and sustainable community based alternative dispute resolution mechanism. Due to the complex nature of HLP issues and limited information on HLP issues in the project locations, SSLS intends to implement these interventions in a period of at least twelve (12) months in order to empower communities to take proactive role in addressing HLP issues in a sustainable manner.
Acknowledging that the protection cluster has a number of HLP partners, SSLS will closely coordinate with the protection partners and regularly share information about plans for implementation of the activities and progress report to avoid duplication of activities.

1 - Needs

In South Sudan, housing, land and property (HLP) issues are both a consequence and a cause of conflict. Widespread destruction and looting of houses and properties, degradation of housing due to displacement, secondary occupation, and forced eviction, loss of documentation and title deeds, and disputes over customary land tenure are all features of the humanitarian crisis in South Sudan. Awareness of HLP rights is often low among local authorities and affected populations. Displaced and returnee populations, in particular women and child-headed households, also lack legal assistance to access the limited courts or alternative dispute resolution mechanisms to claim their rights. HLP issues constitute a major challenge to creating conditions conducive to pursuing durable solutions for displaced populations. In a context of increasing land-related disputes, and anticipation of returns, relocation, and local reintegration of IDPs and refugees, HLP issues will be a major priority need to be addressed in South Sudan in 2019. Awareness of HLP rights is often low among local authorities and affected populations, in particular women and child-headed households. These HLP issues are more prevalent in urban centers compared to peri-urban areas and this problem has been aggravated by lack of legal information, assistance and litigation, limited access to courts, alternative dispute resolution or grievance management mechanisms where people may claim their rights. Based on the SSLS assessment, Magwi and Wau have high priority needs for HLP programming. However, Juba and Bor South also fall under high priority needs for HLP programming because of massive destruction, displacement which make these locations high areas of potential return. HLP issues constitute a major challenge to creating conducive conditions for return and reintegration of displaced populations. As a result of signing of the R-ARCSS, HLP needs are expected to increase during return, reintegration and resettlement of displaced persons in the project locations particularly during the fourth coming dry season. One of the key issues affecting the IDPs is the limited access to legal information, counseling and litigation. Most of the IDPs don’t understand their rights, to land, housing and property, their documents destroyed and their houses occupied. Available protection, monitoring and response mechanisms with regards to land, housing and property is lacking in most of protection programing. Strengthening HLP Legal frameworks provides an opportunity to enhance the protections afforded to women, vulnerable groups, and communities, promote transparent land governance, expedite the peaceful resolution of HLP-related disputes, and remove barriers and obstacles that currently impede the displaced population’s access to justice.Therefore, this project seeks to improve and strengthen protecting housing, land and property right of displaced person, returnees and host communities in the project locations. These will be achieved through, legal awareness, counseling and litigation, free legal aid and innovative and sustainable alternative dispute resolution mechanism.

2 - Activities

The project activities will include; (1) Raising awareness on HLP and the law, on spot monitoring and response to assist Returnees, IDPs and Host Community reclaim their land and properties, (2) provision of free legal aid and (3) strengthening and establishment of innovative and sustainable community based alternative dispute resolution mechanism. Due to the complex nature of HLP issues and limited information on HLP issues in the project locations, SSLS intends to implement these interventions in a period of at least twelve (12) months in order to empower communities to take proactive role in addressing HLP issues in a sustainable manner. Activities description 1.0 Free legal services provided to at least 4000 spontaneous returnees, IDPS host communities including women and girls in Wau, Juba Bor South and Magwi 1.1 Establish 2 new Legal Aid clinics (LACs) in Bor and Magwi and sustain operation of the three already established clinics in Wau, Magwi, Bor South and Juba: The SSLS will establish two Static Legal Aid Clinic (LAC) in Wau, Magwi, Bor South and Juba. The Legal Aid Clinics will be staffed with one Legal Aid Attorney, Two Legal Aid Officers and two volunteers from the CMG. The Legal Aid Clinics will provide legal information, counseling and litigation support on HLP cases, SGBV cases, family and family law cases. The LAC will run throughout the life of the project. The SSLS will rent an office in each of the project location. Such location will be situated with close proximity to the target beneficiaries for easy access. The office will require furniture, generator, computers and Printer to aid smooth running of the static legal aid clinics. This will be a walk-in clinic where beneficiaries freely and easily access. The clinics will operate will work from Monday through to Friday however for urgent cases, the legal aid will be reachable through hotline office phone call. The Legal aid officers will be responsible for the day to day running of the legal aid offices providing legal advice, counseling and referral. The Legal aid officers will also be responsible for managing and monitoring the CMGs. The Legal Aid Officer will be 100% of her/his time to the legal aid operations. The Legal Aid Attorney will be responsible for handling complex HLP cases that may require litigation or mediation. The Legal Attorney will provide technical advice to the CMGs in handling HLP cases. The Legal Aid Attorney will dedicate 100% of her/his time to the legal aid operations handling complex cases before courts and providing technical support in Alternative Dispute Resolution. 1.2 Mobile Legal Aid conducted SSLS will conduct regular mobile legal aid outreach to provide on spot legal advice, information, counseling and referrals to target beneficiaries in the project areas. Due the scattered population in the project locations as the result of the conflict, SSLS will conduct monthly mobile legal aid Outreach to specific identified and accessible locations. The mobile legal aid days will be conducted by the Legal Aid Officers and the CMGs. Each mobile legal aid outreach sessions will last for the whole day where the Legal Aid Officers will separately address clients’ cases. The each Mobile Legal Aid Outreach will target amount 1500 persons include men, women, boys and girls. The selected category of beneficiaries will include IDPs, refugees’ returnees and host communities. This base on the statistic provided by the shelter cluster, Reach and SSLS HLP assessments. In Wau and Yei alone over 35,000 housing, land and property are destroyed and occupied by secondary occupants. In Bor over 50,000 HLP are also occupied by secondary occupants. The SSLS will coordinate with the CMGs to resolve complex cases that cannot be resolved by the CMGs. The CMGs will refer cases that prove too complex for them or requires litigation to the Legal Aid Clinic in order for the Legal Aid Officers and the Senior Legal Aid attorney to handle.
