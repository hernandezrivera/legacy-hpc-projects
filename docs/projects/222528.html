Project Summary

Through this project, WFP will provide cash and food support to 50,000 vulnerable people (mostly refugees) in the Far North region, in line with its Country Strategic Plan for Cameroon (CSP 2022-2026). Targeting will be refined in parallel with physical checks to ensure that only households in extreme food insecurity will be assisted.
The modality for transferring food aid to beneficiaries will be mostly cash (CBT) through unconditional monthly cash distributions to support vulnerable households' access to adequate and nutritious food, thus preventing them from adopting negative coping practices. The monthly cash transfer ration is 9,000 FCFA per person to beneficiaries who are entirely dependent on humanitarian food aid. A further 40% of beneficiaries will be assisted in kind via monthly food distributions. Mechanisms will be put in place to assess the evolution of household needs to ensure a gradual approach from one form of assistance to another. During project implementation, regular monitoring activities will be carried out during food distribution, and monthly data will be collected against performance indicators for reporting through WFP's COMET system. In line with WFP's responsibility, affected populations, targeted vulnerable IDPs and local populations will be closely involved in the various stages of the project, including the targeting process. The people concerned will be regularly informed of project activities and plans. Through complaints and feedback mechanisms, they will freely express their opinions and concerns about project implementation. In line with WFP's responsibility to the affected populations, beneficiaries' complaints are treated with respect, and their opinions are taken into account in project adjustments. The WFP, in collaboration with its partners, will carry out assessments to identify and update the list of the most vulnerable displaced persons and local populations to be taken into account in projects on the basis of vulnerability criteria.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

For almost a decade, the Far North region has been experiencing growing humanitarian crises, mainly linked to the Lake Chad Basin crisis caused by Boko Haram armed groups. People affected by the crisis are struggling to survive in difficult conditions in villages or small towns, putting enormous pressure on already limited community resources and the local economy. According to the report published by OCHA and UNHCR (January 2024), 769,479 people have been forcibly displaced in the Far North region. Logone-et-Chari, Mayo Tsanaga and Mayo Sava are the most affected divisions, with over 400,000 people on the move. While most of this mobile population is made up of internally displaced people (59%), the remainder is made up of returnees (26%) and out-of-camp refugees (15%). The arrival of displaced people in food-insecure areas has severely affected host communities. Attacks by Boko Haram have disrupted economic, agricultural, and pastoral activities in the Far North region, resulting in the impoverishment of household livelihoods and assets. As a result, existing chronic vulnerability and structural food insecurity have been exacerbated. The region is predominantly arid and regularly hit by drought and seasonal flooding. The most recent floods in October 2024 (OCHA, 2024) affected some 459,102 people, destroyed 85,253 hectares of cultivated land and lost 5,510 head of livestock. According to the March 2024 “Harmonized Framework” produced by the government and its partners in the 10 regions, the Far North region has around 654,605 people in phase 3 of the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) due to flooding and pest damage to crops, as well as attacks by Boko Haram. 06 divisions in the Far North are in the crisis phase of food insecurity. The World Food Programme (WFP) and its cooperating partners will support the Government of Cameroon in strengthening food security in the Far North region.Main activities include:- Food distribution: WFP will distribute food to vulnerable populations affected by food insecurity.- Cash transfer: Cash transfers will be made to enable beneficiaries to purchase food and other essential goods.These activities will be implemented over a 12-month period and will cover around 50,000 people from vulnerable households in the Far North region. The WFP already has a strong presence in Cameroon, with initiatives such as cash transfer programs for vulnerable people.In addition, the WFP is working closely with the government of Cameroon to strengthen food security and community resilience. These efforts are crucial to meeting the needs of vulnerable populations in the Far North region.
