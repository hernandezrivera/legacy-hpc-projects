Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan remains dire, exacerbated by years of conflict, displacement, and fragile infrastructure. The onset of the Sudan conflict has worsened this crisis, leading to a significant influx of South Sudanese returnees. Many of these returnees, previously displaced within Sudan, are arriving in a country grappling with deep-seated challenges, including food insecurity, inadequate infrastructure, and limited livelihood opportunities.
This intervention seeks to address the immediate needs of these returnees and vulnerable host communities by providing essential food assistance while supporting their reintegration and long-term recovery. Specifically, the project aims to diversify livelihoods and build resilience through integrated support activities, ensuring that communities have sustainable means to cope with ongoing challenges.
To ensure a targeted and effective response, the intervention aligns with the Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) Cluster's objectives and is informed by the most recent IPC data to identify and prioritize the most affected regions. The project will focus on areas like Upper Nile, Unity, and Central Equatoria States, where the influx of returnees has placed severe pressure on local resources and infrastructure.
Context and Rationale
2.1 Humanitarian Situation in South Sudan
South Sudan continues to be one of the most fragile states globally, with over 4 million people displaced due to conflict, environmental challenges, and systemic poverty. The escalation of violence in Sudan has led to a wave of South Sudanese nationals returning home, with over 300,000 returnees recorded as of mid-2024. These returnees are primarily settling in Upper Nile, Unity, and Central Equatoria States, including regions such as Maban, Malakal, and Renk in Upper Nile, and Robkona, Leer, and Panyijar in Unity State.
These areas already face high levels of food insecurity and limited infrastructure. According to the most recent IPC data, parts of Upper Nile and Unity States are classified in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency), underscoring the urgent need for assistance to prevent famine-like conditions.
2.2 Challenges Faced by Returnees
Returnees face a myriad of challenges, including lack of food, shelter, and healthcare. Overcrowded camps like Gorom Refugee Camp in Juba County and overstretched host communities are struggling to accommodate the new arrivals. This has led to increased competition for scarce resources, escalating food insecurity, malnutrition, and social tensions. Livelihood opportunities are minimal, further compounding the vulnerability of returnees and host communities alike.
Objectives of the Intervention
The project has three main objectives:
Provide Immediate Food Assistance
Deliver life-saving food aid to returnees and host communities, targeting areas with the highest food insecurity. Activities will include general food distributions (GFD) and targeted assistance to vulnerable groups such as children, the elderly, and persons with disabilities.
Improve Nutritional Status
Prevent malnutrition and related health risks through the provision of nutritionally balanced and fortified food, prioritizing children under five, pregnant women, and lactating mothers.
Diversify Livelihoods and Support Reintegration
Support the reintegration of returnees by promoting livelihood diversification activities, such as vocational training, small-scale farming, and income-generating initiatives. These activities aim to reduce dependency on aid and strengthen community resilience.
Conclusion
This intervention provides a holistic approach to address the immediate and long-term needs of returnees and host communities in South Sudan. By combining food assistance with livelihood support and ensuring alignment with recent IPC findings, the project aims to alleviate suffering, foster resilience, and contribute to sustainable recovery in the most affected regions.

1. Needs

This intervention aims to address the urgent food insecurity and various vulnerabilities faced by returnees and vulnerable populations in South Sudan, exacerbated by both the ongoing crisis in Sudan and internal displacement. Food Insecurity and Nutritional Gaps: Immediate Food Insecurity: Many returnees and internally displaced persons (IDPs) face severe hunger due to disrupted livelihoods and lack of resources, often arriving in South Sudan with minimal belongings.Nutritional Deficiencies: Vulnerable groups, such as children, pregnant women, and the elderly, are at high risk of malnutrition, stunting, and micronutrient deficiencies. Nutritionally balanced, fortified food is urgently needed to prevent further deterioration of health. Shelter and Livelihood Support: Overcrowded Camps: Many returnees are living in overcrowded camps like Gorom Refugee Camp, where inadequate shelter, sanitation, and water facilities increase health risks.Loss of Livelihoods: Many returnees lack access to income-generating opportunities, land for farming, or livestock, leaving them highly dependent on humanitarian aid. Strained Host Communities: Pressure on Local Resources: Host communities are already struggling with food insecurity and poverty, and the influx of returnees has stretched local resources, increasing the risk of tensions and conflicts.Increased Vulnerability: The additional strain on host communities’ resources exacerbates vulnerabilities for both returnees and local populations, requiring support for both groups to prevent worsening poverty and insecurity. Health and Sanitation: Limited Healthcare Access: Returnees, particularly in camps or with host families, face limited access to healthcare services, which, combined with poor infrastructure, increases health risks.WASH Needs: Lack of clean water and sanitation facilities in displacement areas poses a serious health threat, particularly in overcrowded camps, making the provision of safe water and sanitation crucial. Long-Term Reintegration Support: Need for Reintegration Support: While immediate food aid is critical, returnees also require long-term support for livelihood recovery, such as access to seeds, tools, and agricultural training, to reduce dependency and help rebuild their self-sufficiency. Protection Needs: Protection Risks: Vulnerable groups, particularly women, children, and the elderly, are at heightened risk of exploitation, abuse, and neglect in displacement settings. Protection concerns such as gender-based violence (GBV) and child protection must be addressed alongside food and shelter assistance to ensure safety.ConclusionThe key needs for this intervention are food security, nutrition, shelter, livelihoods, health services, sanitation, and long-term reintegration support. These needs are intensified by the ongoing crisis in Sudan and internal displacement, leaving both returnees and host communities vulnerable. Addressing these immediate and long-term needs through targeted assistance will contribute to the stabilization and recovery of affected populations in South Sudan.

2. Project activities

This intervention focuses on addressing the immediate and long-term needs of returnees and vulnerable populations in South Sudan, particularly in light of the food insecurity and other challenges caused by displacement. Key activities include: Provision of Immediate Food AssistanceGeneral Food Distribution (GFD): Monthly food rations (cereals, pulses, oil, salt, and fortified foods) provided to returnees, IDPs, and vulnerable host communities to address urgent food insecurity.Targeted Food Assistance: Special food distributions for highly vulnerable groups, such as malnourished children, pregnant women, and the elderly, to prevent severe malnutrition. Nutritional SupportProvision of Fortified Foods: Delivery of micronutrient-enriched foods (e.g., RUTF) to vulnerable populations to prevent malnutrition, especially among children, pregnant women, and the elderly.Nutrition Education: Awareness sessions on healthy eating practices, hygiene, and malnutrition prevention for caregivers of young children. Shelter and Non-Food Item (NFI) SupportShelter Assistance: Provision of temporary shelter materials to returnees, especially in overcrowded camps like Gorom Refugee Camp, to improve living conditions.Non-Food Item (NFI) Distribution: Distribution of essential items such as cooking utensils, hygiene kits, mosquito nets, and clothing to support basic living standards. Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) SupportProvision of Clean Water: Installation of water points and distribution of clean water to camps and host communities to prevent waterborne diseases.Sanitation Facilities: Construction and maintenance of latrines, shower facilities, and waste disposal systems to improve sanitation.Hygiene Promotion: Distribution of hygiene kits and conducting hygiene awareness campaigns to improve sanitation and reduce disease transmission. Livelihood Support and ReintegrationAgricultural Support: Provision of seeds, tools, and training to returnees and host communities to support livelihood recovery and reduce dependence on humanitarian aid.Livelihood Skills Training: Vocational training for youth and women (e.g., tailoring, carpentry) to build income-generating skills and promote resilience. Health and Medical SupportBasic Healthcare Services: Support for health clinics, providing medical supplies and offering essential health services (vaccinations, maternal health, etc.).Nutrition Screening and Treatment: Screening for malnutrition and offering therapeutic feeding for severely malnourished children. Protection and Gender-Based Violence (GBV) PreventionProtection Monitoring: Ongoing monitoring of protection risks, particularly for women, children, and vulnerable groups, to address exploitation and abuse.GBV Prevention and Response: Establishing safe spaces for women and children, providing GBV awareness campaigns, and supporting survivors with medical care and psychosocial services.Child Protection: Offering services like family tracing, reunification, and psychosocial support to protect children from abuse and exploitation. Coordination and Community EngagementCommunity-Based Approach: Engaging local communities and leaders in the planning, implementation, and monitoring of activities to ensure culturally appropriate interventions.Feedback Mechanisms: Establishing mechanisms (e.g., community meetings, suggestion boxes) for beneficiaries to voice concerns and improve transparency. Monitoring and EvaluationRegular Monitoring: Ongoing monitoring of all activities to ensure that assistance reaches intended beneficiaries and is delivered effectively.Impact Evaluation: Periodic assessments to evaluate the impact on food security, health, livelihoods, and community stability. ConclusionThese activities will provide immediate relief to the most vulnerable populations while supporting long-term recovery, resilience, and self-sufficiency, ultimately contributing to the stabilization of communities in South Sudan.
