Project Summary

This intervention aims at supporting newly displaced households through one-off MPC grants to help them addressing their survival needs. Cash recipients will be selected based on socio-economic vulnerability criteria such as lack of income, lack of household manpower, children-headed households, women headed
households, lack of adequate shelter.
The cash grant will amount to $120 per household in line with December 2019 value of
the survival minimum expenditure basket (SMEB) which represent the minimum items
required to support a six person household for a month. This intervention will target 7,000 newly displaced households in Idlib (Abul Thohur -Maaret Tamsrin- Al Mara - Harim - Ariha - al Qoz) and Aleppo ( Azaz - Aghtrin) governorate.

4 - Needs

According to the UN and implementing partners, since the beginning of December 2019, 586,000 individuals have been displaced by the Government of Syria’s military offensive, predominantly into northern Idleb governorate. Hostilities in northwest Syria continue to impact heavily on civilians who have endured nine years of crisis, violence, multiple displacements and economic downturn. The humanitarian situation, particularly in the Idleb de-escalation area, continues to deteriorate as a result of the hostilities that escalated in late 2019. The majority of those newly displaced have stayed within the Idleb area, moving to either urban centres such as Idleb city or Ariha or to areas that already have significant IDP populations such as Dana, Maaret Tamsrin and Atareb. Some 45,000 of newly displaced individuals chose to move to areas such Azaz in northern Aleppo governorate to seek safety and to reach services. Most of these households fled their homes within the space of hours without any belongings and are in urgent need of support to avoid depletion of assets and adoption of negative coping strategies. According to OCHA and CCCM cluster data, the most urgent needs of recently displaced individuals are non-food items, shelter, food, and cash assistance. The humanitarian community acknowledges that when when markets are functioning, safely accessible to beneficiaries and transfer modalities exist, cash assistance provides the opportunity to support recipients in meeting their survival needs in an effective and efficient way. Besides, beneficiaries preferred this method of assistance because they have more control on their decision and they can prioritize their own needs. WV will assess the market functionality and safety before starting the cash distribution in the targeted locations.
