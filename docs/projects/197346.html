Project Summary

INTERSOS intends to tackle identified problems/unmet needs through a comprehensive protection response (GP, CP, GBV, MA) targeting the most vulnerable population in areas with high severity conditions (3-4 as per Protection Sector Severity & PIN HNO 2023) in Hama, Idleb, Rural Damascus and Deir-ez-Zor. INTERSOS will integrate this response with education (HSYR23-EDU-196630-1), health (HSYR23-HEA-197010-1) and ERL (HSYR23-ERY-196628-1) interventions. This action aims to enhance the prevention, mitigation of protection risks and respond to protection needs through supporting the protective environment in Syria (HRP Strategic Objective 2). It will contribute to improving psycho-social wellbeing and self-reliance capacities of conflict-affected populations through stand-alone and integrated protection interventions. Protection activities will be implemented through a static-mobile approach and include: a. targeted protection at individual-group level for the most vulnerable children and adults: case management (CP, PwSN), in kind assistance (medical assistive devices, dignity/education/winter kits, transportation to access services), safe identification/referrals, structured and unstructured PSS activities for children and adults; b. work at community level to build on pre-existing strengths and self-protection mechanisms: establishment/support of community groups, community-led initiatives, prevention/empowerment activities for women/girls, awareness raising, MRE, training and capacity building on key protection topics.
Integrated actions include CP in EiE activities are conducted directly in schools to enhance the protective environment for children through training of education personnel & PTAs: school-based structured and unstructured PSS and ICM; referral to specialized services; reduction of barriers to access education and school-dropouts’ rates in addition to targeted intervention for OOSC. Also, protection is integrated with health, training health workers and volunteers about key protection topics as well as safe identification and referral, ensuring referral pathways between protection and health teams, and providing medical in-kind assistance to vulnerable cases identified through ICM. Lastly, vulnerable individuals identified during ICM are referred to livelihood services to mitigate risks/vulnerabilities, empower them and enhance their self-reliance, and reduce their dependence on humanitarian aid.
Protection and age, gender and diversity (AGD) considerations are mainstreamed throughout all interventions aiming at avoiding causing harm, ensuring meaningful access, accountability, equal participation/empowerment of all affected individuals. INTERSOS implements protection programming under different modalities with SARC (implementing partner), MoE (protection activities in schools, sub-MoU signed in June 2022) and MoSAL (direct implementation, sub-MoU signed in August 2022). In November 2022, INTERSOS was included in the roster of potential UNHCR implementing partners for health and protection in the target governorates.

3 - Needs

INTERSOS RMNA (May-June 2022) in Hama, Idleb and RD highlighted widespread lack of legal documentation, explosive ordnance contamination, GBV and CP related concerns including physical and emotional abuse, violence and neglect. In parallel, limited access to basic services (electricity, fuel, healthcare, school system) along with the depletion of households assets and resources due to the deteriorating socio-economic situation have led to the adoption of negative coping mechanisms and heightened protection risks. Child labour, early marriage and child separation are reported across locations, school-drop-out rates and child labour are cited as recurrent practice, leading to increase landmines and ERW related injuries. Early marriage is a prevalent form of gender based violence overwhelmingly affecting adolescent girls. Psycho-social distress and unaddressed trauma are reportedly affecting communities at large. MHPSS needs rank the highest with women and girls representing the main part of those in need of psychological support and a significant number of boys in need of structured psychosocial support. In addition, access to specialized mental health services is limited due to shortage of qualified mental health professionals. Specialized services for elderly and PwDs are also highly limited. PwDs and their families continue facing increased economic hurdles, physical and environmental barriers limiting their access to services, and exacerbating stigma and social isolation.

4 - Implementation of activities

Protection activities are conducted through mobile/static approaches at community and individual level. Protection programming includes stand-alone, integrated protection, protection mainstreaming. INTERSOS implements protection activities in partnership with SARC and directly under the framework of sub-MoUs with line ministries (MoE and MoSAL). Activities include: capacity building on core protection subjects (protection principles, GBV, CP, safe identification/referral) for SARC volunteers and MoE/MoSAL staff, community members/groups, education personnel, PTAs, health workers; awareness raising on core protection topics including legal awareness (through SARC) and mine risk education; establishment of community groups and support of community-led initiatives; prevention/empowerment activities for women/girls; unstructured/structured PSS for children and adults, caregivers programs; CP/PWSN Case Management for individuals at risk; in kind assistance (assistive devices for PWDs, dignity/education/hygiene/winter kits). INTERSOS is part of the IA Referral Task Team and will contribute to strengthen referral mechanisms to specialized services. A M&E plan is developed with quantitative/qualitative indicators to measure achievement rate/impact of each and every intervention. Needs assessment/community consultations (through FGDs & KII, PDM) are regularly conducted to foster communities active participation.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

R: INTERSOS/implementing partners (IP) staff, suppliers (etc.) have prior conviction for SEA related offenses; IP, staff member, suppliers, do not comply/accept PSEA policy; IP does not have a strong PSEA system in place; SEA perpetrated by staff/service providers; PSEA allegations are not timely/effectively addressed; survivors have limited access to safe assistance/remedies; lack of PSEA reporting mechanisms; communities not aware of PSEA risks/reporting channels. MM: INTERSOS Global PSEA/CP Policy applies to all personnel in addition to a country level Strategy to ensure SEA risks/mitigation/procedures are tailored to the context. INTERSOS staff must sign a PSEA/CP Policy Declaration, attend a PSEA/CP Policy induction and one refresher training per year. 1 PSEA Referent and 4 PSEA Focal Points (2 in Damascus, 2 in Hama) are appointed to receive complaints/reports about alleged acts of SEA and follow up in compliance with relevant guiding principles, ensuring safety of survivors. Complaints are referred to the in-country PSEA Referent and are subject to investigation. An assessment about IP commitment and capacity to prevent/respond to SEA is conducted; efforts are made with IP to harmonize PSEA strategies/reporting procedures to the best extent possible. Training is offered to partners to strengthen the identification of SEA risks/adoption of related SEA mitigation measures; when possible awareness raising on PSEA risks/reporting channels is conducted at community level.
