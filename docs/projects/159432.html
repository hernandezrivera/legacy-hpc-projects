Project Summary

The project aims to enhance safe access to inclusive early education which was extreme negatively affected by the current situation in the Gaza Strip.
The project will target 15 KGs in south Gaza (East Khanyounis, West Khanyounis and Rafah) and will work through a set of activities toward:
- Enhance appropriate and safe learning environment for pre-schoolers.
- Strengthen emergency preparedness capacity of KGs based on UNICEF Emergency Preparedness Guidance-Note 2016
- Improve the psychosocial situation and trauma response capacities of vulnerable children.
The project will last for 24 Month targeting 15 KGs in south Gaza and it will be implemented in a partnership with 3 local partners.

1 - Needs

The The situation of Gaza Strip characterizes a complex protracted crisis that is not traceable to a single acute shock but rather is a constant state of high vulnerability driven by a combination of inter-related factorsThe economic situation in Gaza has deteriorated since the salaries cut for the PA employees and Gaza government in ability to pay its’ employees except 40% of their salaries causing deteriorating of the economic situation and increasing the rates of poverty; as an impact the Education system was one of the most affected educational operate In 2018 the Protection cluster estimated that in Gaza 295,913 children and 190,279 caregivers would be in need of psychosocial support. The series of intense exchanges of fire in recent months had devastating PSS consequences on the population.Based on Reality of kindergartens in South Gaza needs assessment conducted by EducAid in October 2017 and updated in Sep.2019 2018 targeting 45 KGs in the southern area of Gaza Strip the results showed a significant decrease in the number of enrolled children in KGs compared to the last 2 years: the decrease reached around 20%; 68% of which is related to girls’ enrollment. Especially those enrolled in KG1, the assessment showed 74% decrease of the enrollment of CWD, in addition 80% pay less than 50% of the fee, 18% paid nothing The assessment also highlighted as an impact of the financial crisis significant absence of educational resources for KGs, high demotivation among teachers and education personnel; affecting negatively teachers’ performance and their ability to improve students’ learning outcomes. In addition to the significant decrease in parents’ and community involvement in supporting development of their children, in ability to fulfill their children educational needs like uniform and materials, worrying indicators of potential increase in the dropout rates, increase in the negative coping mechanisms among teachers and parents as an impact of the financial crisis. In light of the above and according to the recent study implemented by IRPAL which mapped Gaza Strip most vulnerable localities in terms of, Education, Protection, WASH, Shelter and Economic vulnerability, it was found that Rafah and KahnYounis are the highest in the overall vulnerability 68% and 65% respectively• Reality of kindergartens in South Gaza needs assessment findings were presented in the Education cluster in 2017

2 - Activities

.1.1 Identification of children at risk (dropout) agree with KG Exempt them from fees compensated with what the project will provide the KG poorest children2.1.2 Conduct winterization activities focus in providing children in need with winter clothes, shoes and bags in order to allow them to attend KG with high protection during winter.Project’s staff in collaboration with the KG principals, and KG committee will identify girls and boys to be targeted in 2.1.1 and 2.12 based on specific criteria will be developed by project steering committee2.1.3 Provision of educational materials: in addition, the project will support the KGs with educational needed materials to put into practice teachers’ trainings skills2.2 Provide essential and basic emergency rehabilitation to KGs to ensure that school environments are safe for students.2.2.1 Provision of upgrading activities to targeted KGs based on KGs needs including rehabilitation, furnishing and outside toys.In order to allow the adoption of adequate inclusion practices and their proper implementation, KGs will benefit from structural/non-structural rehabilitation works including WASH facilities to upgrade the KG premises and facilitate with a special focus on interventions promoting the accessibility of KGs for CWD. KGs will also be supported through the purchase of furniture and outside toys responding to the main needs identified during the initial assessment.2.3 Supporting conflict affected KG children with disabilities (hearing and visual) with assistive devices.EducAid will support CWD (hearing and visual) enrolled in the KGs with needed assistive devices. The children will be identified through hearing and visual screening all KGs children.3.1 Support the most vulnerable kgs in Gaza in responding to emergencies through structured preparedness packages and post emergency responses based on UNECIF Emergency Properness guidance-Note 20163.1.1 establishment of an emergency committee (composed by 2 KG staff, 2 parents and 2 community members) in each KG to provide KG with support during emergency situation. And train the committees on the National emergency plan in Education sector.3.1.2 Trainings of KG principals, MOE representative and KGs’ emergency committee members on Risk analysis, scenario definitions, preparedness action, child protection principles and EIE to support the establishment /improvement of a child friendly and protective environment, definition of child friendly safety practices and contingency plan in case of emergency3.2.1 ToT training will be provided by educational experts from CEIS (Italian–Swiss educational center) to experienced educators who will then train all KG teachers, Kg principals and specialized educators on child protection principals and psychosocial support of children in emergency situations through the Diamond Kite Project (DKP) approach.3.2.2 Realization of coaching activities for structuring psychosocial support interventions through the approach of Diamond Kite Project within Kgs .3.2.3 Conduct psycho social support for children and cwd through recreational activities led by experienced animators, girls and boys will be supported in expressing their emotions and discovering their abilities and potentialities. The recreational activities will be carried out based on Play Bus methodology which developed by EducAid and implemented through several interventions in Gaza the main was funded by OCHA.3.2.4: Awareness raising for parents who will be subjected to 12 sessions based on COPE methodology.3.4.5 Organization of drama activities in kindergarten: the activity will involve mothers, teachers and children of targeted kindergartens.Through the recreational and parents activities the animators, and educators will identify a group of children and parents who are in need for more PS support and refer them based on the referral pathway.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

EducAid adopts the participatory approach and promotes community based management. The policies of EducAid are obviously supporting the bottom-up framework where beneficiaries are actively involved in decision making, providing feedback and reporting any kind of complaints. EducAid is always encouraging the open-door policy and constructive dialogue with the targeted groups. The confidentiality is always reserved all over the way. The beneficiaries of this project are effectively engaged in the decision-making regime through: - Project Design: the beneficiaries were an integral part in constructing the project and drawing the project plan. The beneficiaries were also joined up in the need assessment process. This project comes up as a reflection to their needs and aspiration. They take a part in identifying the objectives and shape of the project. - Project implementation: sharing responsibility through the steering committee. The feedback will be used to guide and/or correct any deviation in the project activities. - Project Evaluation: EducAid is highly interested in continuous improvement and deliver the outputs in an optimal way. Whilst the project approaches to wrap up, the target group, both direct and indirect, will be invited to a workshop entitled “Feedback, Challenges and Lessons Learned (FCLL)”. This activity is a part of EducAid’s commitments towards the communities and it is explicitly incorporated in their objectives and approach in being close to the beneficiaries, backing up the continuous improvement, avoiding challenges of sustainability, supporting the psychological ownership and joining the targeted population in making decisions especially the decisive ones.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

EducAid and partners build the project with high level of beneficiaries’ participation to guarantee an intervention responding to the real needs and insure the respect of human rights.As a prior step of the design process, a detailed and comprehensive context analysis for the targeted environments was conducted in order to define the vulnerability, local capacity, understand threats, and identify needs. This step was to assess in priorities safety and dignity for individuals and communities. The community participation is highly adopted along the project lifetime and beyond. A committee will be established that consists from KGs staff, children parents and community representatives in order to ensure avoiding doing any kind of harm due to misunderstanding or lack of communication with targeted communities. The project staff and volunteers are going to participate in a training about protection mainstreaming before entering the filed in order to strengthen their capacities while serving beneficiaries. There is an adopted complaint mechanism to encourage individuals to share their complaints in a safe and confidentiality manner.Furthermore, the project focuses on children with disabilities, so the staff are specialized and trained well to deal with CWD making sure that there is no harm or extra threats or vulnerability factors will be added during the implementation of project activities.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project is designed following a methodology of ensuring equal and impartial accessibility to aid without any discrimination or abuse. The project activities and time frame are ensuring the accessibility for beneficiaries mainly the distribution points and playgrounds in addition to workshops/training spaces taking to consideration the time of implementation and locations that appropriate and suitable for all. The project is designed in abases of good communication channels with beneficiaries mainly to share information since they have the right to be informed about their rights, to participate in the decision making as well. In addition, the project steering committee will develop a set of criteria to identify the right beneficiaries for each service of the project, these criteria will be shared with project committee through project communication channels. The project also will do mapping for existing available services and will participate in the existing referral system. Also, the project will advocate for the existing needs that cannot contribute to full fill it.
