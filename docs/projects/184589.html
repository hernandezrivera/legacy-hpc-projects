Project Summary

Somalia continues to experience or face a dire humanitarian situation contributed to
natural shocks such as drought, protracted conflicts, political instability, COVID-19, and
severe seasonal flooding. These factors largely affected at least an estimated 2.9 million people
internally. The displaced persons are in need of humanitarian assistance. The majority of
displaced people have self-settled in over 2,400 sub-standard and unplanned IDP sites in
urban, semi-urban, and rural areas across the country. UN reports indicate that Hirshabelle
region experienced severe flooding in 2020 that affected nearly 1 million people and
displaced about 400,000 people leaving them without shelter and claiming the life of at least 24
people in Beledweyne and Jowhar districts. Nearly 40% of the people in Jowhar, a smaller
centre of about 20,000 residents, have been displaced from their homes, according to the
Somali government's Ministry of Humanitarian Affairs and Disaster Management in
Hirshabelle state. According to the reports from UNOCHA, the areas are still recovering
from the effect of last year's flash flood.
According to the government of Somalia report May 2021, heavy GU rain-affected
over 45000 people in the Jowhar district in Middle Shabelle. Amoiko village is located
the southwestern corner of Jowhar district an estimated distance of 5Km from town hosting
300 households that were affected by GU floods which destroyed infrastructures and property
of the people living in that area.
The assessment conducted by FAO on food security and agricultural livelihood in Somalia
in January and February 2021, focusing on impacts of COVID-19, FAO interviewed 2,720
household across 17 regions, including 160 agricultural households in the regions. It
indicates Somalia experienced a resurgence of COVID-19, straining one of the world most
fragile health systems which affected agricultural productivity and livelihood of rural
population resulting in acute food insecurity.
The recent fight between the Galmudug state army, Somali federal forces, and Ahlu sunnah
wal jama'a (ASWJ) worsened the situation of IDPs in Beledweyne and Jowhar in
Hirashabelle State. Since these regions neighbor the affected region, they received a
a huge volume of Internally displaced persons (IDPs) from Guricel due to. As a result, tension and insecurity in the area worsened the humanitarian situations of the regions that were
already struggling with main shocks such as COVID-19, drought, conflicts and seasonal
flooding.
The IDPs in the Hirshabelle regions and Somalia at large are increasing due to evictions,
floods, and drought and inter-clan conflicts. Fighting between federal forces and AS has
displaced rural and urban communities. As of August 2021, 92,042 individuals have been
evicted from their settlements stemming from a total of 2,304 incidences of forceful
eviction perpetrated by landowners (NRC, Evictions Information Portal 2021) and Somalia
CCCM cluster sites verification. The majority of IDPs have settled in informal and
unplanned settlements where the conditions are very poor, and forced eviction remains a
common threat.
DAN organization will strengthen site maintenance, governance, camp management and
camp coordination (CCCM) system in IDP camps in Jowhar and Beledweyne districts targeting
an estimated population of 3900 households (23400 individuals). The sites are managed
through capacity building aspects, community engagement and creating effective
community support structures. DAN purposes to establish/revive trained, comprehensive and
inclusive CCCM committees in each of the targeted areas to support the project
implementation. DAN plays an important role in improving the coordination and
monitoring of service provision at the site level and avoiding duplication or gaps through
establishing CCCM sub-cluster in Jowhar and Beledweyne and attending the
CCCM cluster meetings.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

There is no sufficiency internal mechanism to combat the risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure or contamination by explosive. The risk of exposing displaced boys and girls to violence caused by living in highly insecure settlements will be safeguarded in compliance with the PSEA by the DAN organization. In the project activities implementation process, DAN is guided by the PSEA and its child safeguarding policies through emergency preparedness and capacity-building measures. Dan will participate in the cluster activities, including assessing needs, developing plans, joint monitoring, and developing policies and procedures through working groups to reduce the risks that marginalized groups such as persons living with disabilities, women, boys and girls face. They do not participate in consultation or decision making processes for the projects or are often excluded from humanitarian services. DAN will recruit 3 focal points from the persons with disabilities, minority, and women and children to take part in the projects implementation, monitoring and reporting of the progress of the project. This will help in identifying the gaps, challenges, needs and opportunities in the implementation of responses. It will give the minority an avenue to participate in the decision making processes. DAN will also create a platform to reduce or monitor overlap and duplication of resources. DAN promotes community communication and consultation on equitable distribution of resources among the beneficiaries, which helps information sharing and effective service provision. DAN will establish a help desk and complaints feedback mechanism and site monitoring in the targets IDP camps to effectively monitor resources and activities.
