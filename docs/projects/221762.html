Project Summary

The education crisis in South Sudan has intensified in 2025, driven by conflict, climate change, economic instability, and sociocultural barriers. An estimated 3.9 million people, primarily children, urgently need education services, with 3.1 million children out of school. Systemic challenges, including non-functional schools due to violence, looting, and flooding, alongside inadequate infrastructure and untrained teachers, hinder access to quality education. Over 230 schools have been destroyed by flooding in the past four years, forcing children to travel long distances and increasing dropout rates. Vulnerable groups, such as girls and children with disabilities, face additional barriers, including cultural norms, lack of inclusive facilities, and limited protection mechanisms. Despite these challenges, education remains critical for resilience and recovery.
Save the Children’s 2025 Humanitarian Response Plan Education Project, "Ensuring Safe, Inclusive, and Quality Education for Vulnerable Children, Out-of-School Youth, and Adolescents in South Sudan," addresses these challenges. Aligned with South Sudan Education Cluster (SSEC) objectives, it focuses on: (1) providing emergency-affected children with access to safe, inclusive education services; (2) improving teaching quality to support retention and progression; and (3) strengthening crisis management for accountable and timely education responses. The project targets 23,025 beneficiaries, including 22,000 children (11,000 boys and 11,000 girls, with a focus on children with disabilities) and 1,025 (513 Male & 512 Female) teachers and PTA members, and will be implemented in Abyei, Maban, Renk, Akobo, and Bor South. The project will target IDPs, returnees and host community members.
The project will rehabilitate and construct 35 Temporary Learning Spaces (TLS) and mobilize families to enroll 22,000 children, prioritizing marginalized groups. To support girls, 15,000 dignity kits will be distributed to promote gender equity and reduce absenteeism. Teachers and PTA members will be trained in Education in Emergencies (EiE), psychosocial support (MHPSS), and inclusive pedagogy to improve learning outcomes. Additional support will include 100 education kits and socio-emotional learning (SEL) programs to enhance teaching quality and foster child development.
Resilience efforts include constructing 35 gender-segregated WASH facilities, promoting hygiene, and implementing Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) plans to prepare schools for emergencies. Climate-resilient infrastructure will be piloted to ensure education continuity during floods and other crises. Community engagement will involve establishing child help desks in 55 schools, conducting 10 Back-to-Learning campaigns targeting out-of-school children, and forming protection committees to address barriers such as early marriage and gender-based violence. Parent-Teacher Associations (PTAs) and School Management Committees (SMCs) will be trained in governance and disaster preparedness to foster sustainability.
A robust Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) framework will ensure transparency and improvement. Real-time data tracking and feedback mechanisms, such as hotlines and suggestion boxes, will ensure the voices of affected populations are heard. Key cross-cutting themes include conflict sensitivity, gender inclusion, safeguarding, and sustainability, ensuring interventions address immediate needs and establish a foundation for long-term recovery.
The project aims to increase access to safe, inclusive education for 22,000 children, enhance teaching quality for 900 educators, build school resilience to withstand crises, and empower communities to sustain education services. Through its holistic approach, the project addresses South Sudan’s urgent education challenges while fostering sustainable development and peacebuilding, ensuring every child has the opportunity to learn and thrive.

1. Needs

The education landscape in South Sudan remains critical in 2025, shaped by conflict, economic instability, climate change, and sociocultural barriers. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2024, 3.9 million people—98% of whom are children—urgently require education services. This marks a sharp rise from previous years, driven by declining enrollment rates, displacement, and systemic challenges (UNICEF, 2024). Approximately 3.1 million children remain out of school, underscoring the need for coordinated interventions to restore access to education and improve learning outcomes. Conflict and insecurity continue to disrupt education, particularly in areas such as Akobo, Nyirol, Bor South, Abyei Administrative Area, Kapoeta East, and Maban. The Education Needs Assessment (ENA) 2023 indicates that 12% of schools in these regions are non-functional, up from 8% in 2021. Factors like recurrent flooding, looting, and violence near schools have forced closures and increased dropout rates. Among operational schools, 34% of headteachers reported missed instructional days due to insecurity and unpaid teacher incentives (Global Education Cluster, 2023). These challenges highlight the fragile nature of education delivery in conflict zones. Accessibility remains a significant challenge, particularly for children with disabilities (CwDs). The ENA 2023 reports that 42% of surveyed schools lack facilities accessible to CwDs. Seasonal challenges, such as damaged infrastructure during rainy seasons, further restrict access. Gender disparities persist, with girls disproportionately affected by early marriage, teenage pregnancy (33% among girls aged 15–19), and sociocultural norms, all contributing to high dropout rates (Save the Children, 2024). Addressing these barriers is critical to advancing gender equity and ensuring girls’ education. Climate change exacerbates the education crisis. Recurrent flooding has displaced thousands, destroyed infrastructure, and rendered over 230 schools non-functional in the last four years (World Bank, 2023). Children in flood-prone areas face long commutes to operational schools, disrupting learning and increasing risks like child labor and recruitment into armed groups. The lack of climate-resilient infrastructure further threatens the continuity of education during emergencies. Systemic gaps in teacher capacity and retention continue to undermine the education sector. Many teachers work without pay or proper training, leaving schools ill-equipped to deliver quality education (UNESCO, 2024). Less than 25% of schools report functional child protection referral mechanisms, leaving children vulnerable to violence, abuse, and exploitation. Adolescents in rural and underserved areas face limited access to Accelerated Learning Programs (ALPs) and vocational training opportunities, hindering their ability to reintegrate into education or secure livelihoods. Despite these challenges, education remains a lifeline for South Sudanese children, offering pathways to resilience, poverty reduction, and peacebuilding. The 2025 HRP emphasizes rebuilding schools, strengthening teacher training, and promoting equitable access to education. Integration with sectors such as child protection, WASH, and health is vital to address the holistic needs of children and communities. The 2025 HRP underscores collaboration between the Ministry of General Education and Instruction (MoGE&I), humanitarian actors, and local communities to achieve sustainable outcomes. By focusing on access, quality, and resilience, the education sector aims to support South Sudan’s recovery and lay the foundation for long-term development. Targeted investments, strategic partnerships, and innovative approaches are essential to addressing the evolving needs of South Sudan’s children.

2. Project activities

Activities for the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) 2025: 1. Access to Educationo Enroll 22,000 children (11,000 boys and 11,000 girls) in pre-primary, primary, and secondary schools/learning centers, prioritizing refugees, returnees, and vulnerable host community children.o Establish community enrollment task forces to increase advocacy for education access in underserved areas.2. Teacher Mapping and Capacity Buildingo Develop and roll out a teacher retention plan, including financial and psychosocial support mechanisms.3. Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS)o Train 150 teachers (75 men and 75 women) on MHPSS approaches to support the mental health recovery of learners and educators affected by emergencies.4. Back-to-Learning Campaignso Conduct 10 comprehensive Back-to-Learning campaigns, targeting out-of-school children and adolescents in hard-to-reach areas of 10 counties.5. Provision of Educational Supplieso Provide 100 education kits, including age-appropriate materials and digital learning tools, to learners in schools and learning centers.6. Child Protection and Safe Spaceso Establish and operationalize child help desks in 55 schools to address child protection concerns and link learners to psychosocial services.7. Teacher Training and Developmento Train 900 teachers (450 men and 450 women) on EiE pedagogical skills, child-centered methodologies, and inclusive education practices to improve learning outcomes.8. Learning Spaceso Establish and rehabilitate 35 temporary learning structures, incorporating durable materials to ensure long-term use during recurring emergencies.9. WASH in Schoolso Construct and rehabilitate 35 gender-segregated WASH facilities in schools and learning spaces, ensuring accessibility for children with disabilities.o Integrate menstrual hygiene management (MHM) facilities and awareness sessions into WASH programs.10. Sanitation and Hygieneo Provide and distribute sanitation and hygiene materials to 150 schools, focusing on sustained hygiene promotion through training and awareness campaigns.o Distribute 15,000 dignity kits to adolescent girls, ensuring comprehensive hygiene education and support.11. Remedial Education and Catch-Up Classeso Support catch-up classes in 55 schools and learning centers, with additional learning support for children with disabilities and other vulnerable groups.12. Recreational and Psychosocial Support Materialso Provide 6,000 emergency-affected children (3,200 boys and 2,800 girls) with quality recreational and psychosocial support materials to promote healing and resilience.13. Community Engagement and Protectiono Establish and strengthen child protection and GBV-focused community committees in targeted counties to ensure child safety and well-being in emergencies.14. Capacity Building for Governanceo Train 125 school management committees (SMCs), parent-teacher associations (PTAs), and education authorities (63 men and 62 women) on school governance, psychosocial support, disaster risk reduction (DRR), and inclusive education practices.15. Disaster Preparedness in Schoolso Develop and implement school-level DRR plans in collaboration with local communities, focusing on mitigating the impact of recurring emergencies.
