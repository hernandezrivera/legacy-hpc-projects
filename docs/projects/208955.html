Project Summary

The proposed WASH project seeks to deliver quality life saving and life sustaining, inclusive and gender
responsive interventions to 265800 crisis affected populations in need, across Aljazeera,
East and South Darfur, Blue Nile and South Kordofan States. This will be achieved through
the following thematic areas;
• Provide crisis-affected and vulnerable people access to emergency water services.
• Provide and enable crisis-affected and vulnerable people access to emergency
sanitation services
• Provide and enable crisis-affected and vulnerable people access to hygiene services
• Provide outbreaks affected and ‘at-risk’ population access to minimum WASH for Health
package
The response targets three localities in Aljazeera state (Madani El Kobra, El Hasaheesa
and El Kamleen), five localities in East Darfur (Eldaen, Bahar Alarab, Abukarinka, Shariya
and Yassin) five localities in Blue Nile (Baw, Geisan, Damazine, Al Tadamon and Rosaries), Abukershola, Abujubehia, Abbasiya, Kadugli, Rashad and Delling localities in South kordofan and five localities in South
Darfur (Beliel, Nyala North, Nyala South, Mershing, and Buram,)

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection will be mainstreamed into planned activities. The project will ensure thateach activity will not expose vulnerable groups including women and children toprotection risks. Community consultation will be conducted throughout all the projectcycle. Community feedback and complaint mechanism will be set up. The feedbackmechanism will ensure anonymity and confidentiality to protect the reporting person.In cases of GBV or child abuse cases, support including referral to other serviceproviders will be provided and the survivor will be linked to protection agencies(Government entities or other organizations dealing with protection issues) to preventexposure from further harm. To possible extent, community discussions will be heldseparately between women and men to create safe space for everybody to participateequally. Discussions for children will always be held separately from the ones foradults. The project will ensure inclusion of most vulnerable groups (like minoritygroups, PLWD, among others) within the target communities in close coordination andcollaboration with key stakeholders. Selection of villages will be done in consultationwith line ministries, HAC and other relevant agencies such as UNFPA, department ofwelfare in the Ministry of social welfare to ensure needs of people with special needsare taken care of. The safety of beneficiaries, other project stakeholders and WVstaffs is not negotiable and project will ensure implementation activities will not putbeneficiaries, WV staff or project stakeholders on security risks. Activities for childrenwill be done during the day with the present of caregivers or other responsible adultsfrom the communities. While the project will prioritize female and child-headedfamilies to participate in project activities or benefit from the assistance, it will alsoensure such inclusion does not create another type of exclusion. Discussion will beheld to inform communities on beneficiaries’ selection criteria. The discussion willinvolve different groups within the communities including women and children. Toadhere to Humanitarian Accountability Standard, feedback mechanism will be set upusing suggestion boxes or other means preferred by the communities. Program staffwill use current safeguarding and accountability standards and policies. Orientation tonew staff will include Code of Conduct and safeguarding principle to prevent abuse and/orexploitation of the beneficiaries. Sanitation facilities in schools will ensure gendersegregation, be located in well lit safely accessible spaces especially for girls and bedesigned and constructed to offer safe and private dignified access to boys and girls.Care givers will be educated to ensure that children only carry age appropriatejerricans when they have to help in fetching water

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

Community feedback will be fundamental in informing continuous improvement in programming. Where the feedback is positive, WV will continue with the practices and even enhance them to optimize impact of proposed interventions to program participants. Where improvement is needed, WV will take prompt corrective action, develop measures to mitigate against recurrence and give feedback to the aggrieved communities. If complain is of a safeguarding nature, the issue will be processed according to internal policies and humanitarian standards, ensuring that the victim is protected from further risks as justice is sought, the required confidentiality is maintained and the prescribed reporting and resolution timelines are observed.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

The project will root all its interventions on all relevant national and international humanitarian standards including the principle of “Do No Harm” conflict sensitivity, and meaningful participation of all segments of society for informed decision making. Complaint Response Mechanisms and Monitoring, Evaluation Accountability and Learning (MEAL) tools will be used to gather both affirming and constructive feedback from project participants. World Vision will identify affected peoples’ preferred methods for providing feedback, considering the specific needs of women, girls, boys and men in order to determine the most appropriate, accessible and safe mechanisms for all beneficiaries. Possible feedback mechanisms will include a feedback hotline (beneficiaries can flash the number and will be called back), community meetings, help desk, community feedback point persons (both female and male) and anonymous comment boxes. Feedback will be collected and sorted into these groupings: 1) positive feedback 2) request for assistance 3) minor dissatisfaction 4) major dissatisfaction 5) sexual exploitation (escalated immediately to the National Director) and actioned accordingly. Feedback is used to determine where adaptations and improvements should be made to the project design. . In addition to regular consultationas part of ongoing interventions, IDP , host communities and government leaderswere informed of this project and involved in selecting beneficiaries and prioritizinginterventions. At the project onset, kickoff meetings at the village level will befacilitated by project staff to further publicize the program and communicateopportunities for participation. Feedback mechanisms: The program will institutefeedback mechanisms such as ‘suggestion boxes’; quarterly feedback forums and‘hotlines’ where feasible. The ‘hotline’ approach has been piloted successfully inDarfur. The project staff will be tasked with mobilizing communities – particularlywomen – to participate and will be responsible for investigating factors that hamperparticipation. These factors will have come through normal feedback mechanismsand / or through or discovered through interactions. The project staff will worktowards ensuring that beneficiaries are aware of their rights and entitlements,ensuring that have timely access to relevant and clear information and they aresatisfied with the opportunities they have to influence the response. The programstaff will consult with beneficiary communities’ on the design, implementation andmonitoring of complaints-handling processes. Program staff Welcome and acceptcomplaints, and communicate how the mechanism can be accessed and the scope ofissues it can address. The complaints will be managed in a timely, fair and appropriatemanner that prioritizes the safety of the complainant and those affected at all stages
