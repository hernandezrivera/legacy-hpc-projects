Project Summary

The action integrates multiple programmatic sectors. The LFS response will provide life-saving multipurpose cash and support livelihoods to foster household self-reliance. NRC will engage with the private sector to provide opportunities to improve market-based approaches for both safety nets and livelihoods activities. Communities will be consulted on the most suitable livelihood activities to be supported by the project. Efforts towards an integrated approach by targeting the same communities with food security, livelihoods and interventions from other sectors will be done to strengthen the holistic approach.
The legal protection action will aim to address the limited access to legal identity including civil documentation for the most vulnerable refugees and support the Ethiopian emergency response by facilitating access to essential services and promoting social cohesion through collaborative dispute resolution. The project will provide information, counselling and legal assistance, integrated capacity building and collaborative dispute resolution to achieve this. The project will also work to strengthen capacities of civil registries and local authorities.
The WASH response will ensure that the most vulnerable refugees have access to safe drinking water and sanitation through household level interventions, support water safety plans from the distribution points up to the point of use and provide family shared emergency latrines and vector control interventions. The response will further upgrade/ expand small-scale water infrastructures for both host community and refugees, increase the water supply, improve the water quality which will consequently reduce conflicts over scarce natural resources. Market-based approach will be utilised to effectively meet people’s WASH needs.
The shelter action will undertake the provision of adequate shelters and NFI for refugees, rehabilitation/repairs of existing shelters to ensure minimum protection against the elements, and replenish and/or issue essential NFI kits for the most vulnerable groups. The response will additional support the establishment of communal infrastructures to facilitate social cohesion and integration with host communities. Household-level energy interventions will mostly focus on solar solutions. The action and precise response modalities will be informed and driven by a market systems analysis.
The EiE intervention and support will focus on enabling refugee and vulnerable host community boys and girls of school age to gain access to timely, safe and quality education through addressing multiple barriers preventing their education. Structural upgrades, rehabilitation of schools, WASH facilities and distribution of essential hygiene supplies and teaching and learning materials will be a key component.
The action will establish a Digital Community Hub (DCH) which will serve as a two-way communication platform with people of concern. Information distributed through the platform will include life-saving and life improving awareness-raising messages as well as information on how to access services. Similarly, people of concern will be able to ask questions, raise emergency alerts, communicate their needs and obtain referrals for services, and voice complaints or feedback via the hub.
The project will assist the most vulnerable refugee households to meet their basic needs through multi-purpose cash. The project plans to support these new arrivals with half the value of the MEB for a period of 6months. A market assessment will be undertaken before the proposed cash intervention.
The response will target the most vulnerable refugees living in camps, camp-like, out-of-camp and urban settings in White Nile and Gedaref and those in formal or informal settlements in South Kordofan. Beneficiary targeting, within refugee communities and their nearby hosting communities, will be done by applying socio-economic vulnerability criteria.

1 - Needs

Refugees face dire conditions and are often unable to meet their basic needs, while returnees suffer from lack of access to basic services and livelihood opportunities. in addition to this,Sudan is receiving Ethiopian and Eritrean refugees fleeing Tigray Region. The economic downturn, currency depreciation as well as high inflation creates a difficult environment for refugee’s who continue to face challenges with accessing work permits in Sudan. The available livelihood opportunities in the informal sector are uncertain and accompanied by high risks of exploitation, sexual harassment and abuse. The drastic increase in prices of food and non-food commodities is expected to continue impacting the most vulnerable households affected by displacement due to conflict and flooding. Use of consumption coping strategies will compromise the nutritional status of most households. Refugees encounter several barriers to accessing legal identity and civil documents as a result of delayed registration procedures and limited access to service providers. Inability to access legal documents gives rise to various protection concerns including; limited access to essential services, livelihood and employment opportunities and risk of statelessness for refugees in protracted displacement which prevents durable solutions. Refugees out of camps face additional challenges resulting in heightened protection risks. For new arrivals access to humanitarian services is a priority. Disputes over resources frequently occur either between individuals or with the host communities. Such disputes can be resolved through collaborative dispute resolution. Lack of land tenure is often a source of conflict and tension among populations and could undermine humanitarian interventions. Only 74% of the population has access to drinking water, 39% to limited sanitation services, and 23% to basic hygiene services. 0.9m refugees and 291,000 returnees are in dire need of WASH support. 60% of the PIN in the WASH sector are boys and girls below 17 years. Access to WASH in schools and primary health centres is limited. Approximately 30% of schools do not have any functional latrine and fewer than 50% of schoolchildren have access to soap and water for handwashing. This negatively impacts children’s attendance and enrolment, especially for girls. Recent peace talks in Juba are expected to contribute towards durable solutions. The return process will require substantial WASH long-term solutions. Refugees, returnees and host communities, are in need of shelter and NFI assistance. These items help restore dignity and protection against the elements, mitigating health risks and providing enhanced privacy and security to those in need. Annual replenishment of key items to the most vulnerable is required. On the environmental and energy component, fuel for cooking is a major issue. Women often face protection incidents when collecting firewood from the camp/settlement surroundings. Majority of refugee sites don't have electrical/lighting system. In many locations of intervention, influx of refugees has increased the burden for already struggling host community schools by worsening overcrowding and increasing the already high teacher pupil ratios. In other cases, most of these settlements lack decent schools or the host community schools are located too far from the refugee camps/settlements for children to safely access them. Financial challenges facing most refugee families affect their ability to afford costs associated with education hence failing or struggling to provide basic scholastic materials to them. In light of the Covid-19 pandemic, communicating with communities (CwC) has become increasingly difficult with frequent lockdowns and limitations on traditional face-to-face communication modalities. Risk communication platforms are urgently needed which can reach and are accessible to vulnerable women, men, youth, and persons with special needs.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Safety net activities through food distribution, cash vouchers or a mix of the two will be implemented. Market based programming will be used to identify relevant modalities. Awareness on the use of cash and household gender dynamics will be conducted before and after distributions. Provision of agricultural inputs and strengthening of extension services will be undertaken for targeted households. The project will promote the use of supply markets for agricultural inputs and services. The feasibility of income generation activities will be assessed before providing financial/in-kind support to beneficiaries for start-up of household income generation activities. Post distribution monitoring and establishment of a complaints and feedback mechanism will be done. ICLA team will provide information on access to emergency assistance, essential services, the importance of legal identity, civil documents, and the procedural requirements associated with securing these rights and services. Legal counselling and legal assistance will be provided for the most vulnerable populations. Integrated capacity building for local authorities, community leaders and humanitarian partners will be provided through; trainings, workshops and provision of technical or material support . Collaborative dispute resolution support will be given for potential conflict over resources. Lastly, ICLA will mainstream HLP across all core competencies through the development of due diligence guidelines. WASH intervention will; upgrade and/or maintain the water supply system, provide water storage and treatment facilities and training and rehabilitate or provide adequate sanitation facilities. The response will further provide; training on the use and maintenance of sanitation facilities and environmental hygiene and conduct hygiene promotion campaigns. Community base approaches and cash and markets modalities will be prioritized. Household-level emergency shelter and essential NFI assistance will be delivered including the repair/sealing-off shelter kits and NFIs. Assistance will be delivered through the most appropriate transfer modality – preferably cash/voucher modalities. Household-level light rehabilitation works will be undertaken to ensure climatic protection, safety, security, privacy and dignity. Provision of transitional or permanent upgrades/repair of basic and social infrastructure will be undertaken to improve social cohesion among displaced and hosting population. Communities will additionally be supported through the upgrade/repair of productive assets that support recovery and access to livelihoods. The response will strongly integrate with ICLA to strengthen security of tenure and improve access to services. Rehabilitation/ construction of school infrastructure (including WASH facilities and TLSs in camps) will be undertaken for host community schools supporting refugee children. Basic school supplies for students, teachers and schools will be provided. Key stakeholders such as teachers, PTAs in the targeted schools will be trained on various relevant packages as per the Education Sector leadership and MoE. Awareness on COVID-19 prevention and general child protection perspectives in education will be incorporated. Qualified teachers will be engaged to support teaching in the additional classrooms established. Accelerated learning programs and catch up classes will be provided for refugee children. Unconditional unrestricted Cash Assistance with be provided to help beneficiaries meet their immediate life-saving basic needs through a market based approach. The use of in-kind assistance will be maintained for contexts where CVA is not feasible. Market assessment will be undertaken before any cash response. The Digital Community Engagement and Assistance project will; establish and run multi-agency digital communication with communities’ platform including a complaint and feedback mechanism as well as a service referral mechanism.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Assessments in South Kordofan, Khartoum and White Nile states, conducted by NRC indicated that communities already depend on natural resources for energy, income (charcoal and firewood), wild fruits, farming and grazing of the livestock. The LFS project plans to engage targeted groups in food production, mainly agricultural activities. These activities if unchecked may have negative impacts on the environment and continue to affect climate change. The project will promote climate smart agricultural practices through its collaboration with the extension staff from the State Ministry of Production and Economic Resources. Food/cash for assets interventions in partnership with the World Food Program (WFP), will incorporate activities that contribute to environmental protection and conservation. By analysing the baseline practices that have an impact on the environment through the various assessments, the project has understood practices that require mitigation measures to reduce their impact. The project will work with women, men, girls and boys, local leadership and authorities, engaging them in the identification of the problems and designing the mitigation challenges. NRC held initial meetings with the Reducing Emissions from Deforestation and Forest Degradation (REDD+) unit under the Forest National Cooperation (FNC) which is part of the federal ministry of Agriculture and Natural Resources. The meetings aimed at identifying areas of collaboration to promote community-based forest management initiatives. The project will additionally provide inputs for community mitigation measures such as reforestation, gully reclamation, and planting of fruit trees after consultations with the target communities and authorities. Solar powered systems will be prioritized for water facilities, energy elements both at household and communal levels and infrastructure in general (schools). The project will additionally explore environmentally friendly cooking and fuel options.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

NRC practices safe programming across all its projects through training of project staff to be able to engage with communities in identifying protection issues for the different gender and age groups. Awareness raising within the communities is carried out to enable them to understand protection issues that can affect the different people by gender, age and displacement status and reporting mechanisms. Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) training is mandatory for all NRC staff and this is cascaded to casual labourers, incentive staff and project management committees. Basic do no harm principle and accountability to affected populations will be upheld to ensure all aspects of the project take into consideration the needs of the elderly, the sick, people with disabilities, women, youth, men and children. The protection environment is not sufficiently safe or stable. The project has therefore taken into consideration risk analysis and mitigation measures in order to reduce the impact. Target communities were involved in identification of protection and security risks in their locations and this analysis has been captured in NRC's area and country strategies. These risk assessments remain living documents as they are continuously updated during specific periods of the year and when certain events take place in an area. Before implementation the project will undertake a gender sensitive assessment that will consider different information needs and communication channel capacities of men, women, boys, and girls, and of specific groups that may be vulnerable, marginalised, or discriminated against. The project design will prioritise participation of vulnerable groups such as women and persons with disability ensuring that they are able to access services and assistance and remove any barriers that may prevent full and effective participation. The project itself aims to ensure the voices of persons of concern are heard and responded to, and that information reaches those most in need. The project will set up complaints and feedback mechanisms in collaboration with the target groups in order to enable them report on issues including PSEA and other protection concerns. Two-way communication will be established between NRC and the target communities using diverse methods in order to capture issues from all groups. The project will additionally be be compliant with humanitarian standards (Sphere).

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

NRC conducts needs assessment before designing any intervention in order to engage and capture target beneficiaries’ needs. The project will conduct community mobilization and sensitization to inform the target communities about the assistance that will be provided. Feedback on how target beneficiaries prefer to receive the assistance will be recorded and used to inform the modalities and response strategies. The project has a complaints and feedback handling mechanism that include different ways of collecting information from target community members including people living with disabilities, those who are illiterate, women and girls. The mechanism provides for anonymous complaints and feedback to be captured and uses different media and methods that the affected can choose to use. The feedback mechanism will allow the project to adapt project assistance based on information received. Monitoring and evaluation activities will measure if beneficiaries felt well-informed and respected during project delivery and their overall satisfaction with the project. The evaluation will reflect how the project tracked and responded to beneficiary complaints and how the feedback shaped the project delivery.NRC will establish a mass communication platform using sms text and voice messages. The platform will allow two-way communication with target beneficiaries. NRC has a dedicated Monitoring & Evaluation team that will support the development and maintenance of a contextually appropriate feedback and complaint mechanism where beneficiaries will be able to report any irregularities or needs that may arise throughout the implementation period. NRC will engage community processes, leaders, members and structures in all activities to increase access, acceptance and ownership of the project. NRC will co-chair and participate in the AAP WG and its task teams, which willcollaborate closely with the PSEA network. As such, the digital community hub will beused to regularly collect feedback from people of concern which will be shared withthe AAP WG. Additionally, NRC will participate in the mapping of existing complaintand feedback mechanisms and support the development of guidelines which can beused by partners in the establishment of their own mechanisms.
