Project Name

Post-earthquake debris management and recycling project to facilitate local level recovery and rehabilitation in quake affected NW Syria.

Start Date

01/01/2024

End Date

31/12/2024

Project Summary

the project aims to achieve the following objectives:
· Remove 80,000 m3 of debris in areas in need of this service by establishing an “emergency debris alert system” for removal activities in close coordination with the ERL cluster and communities to cover all locations in Idleb and Aleppo governorates.
· Recycle 250,000 m3 of debris into (re)construction materials for the rehabilitation of roads and other community infrastructure (ensuring the recycled debris meets requisite technical specifications), thereby reducing costs and the need to extract more quarry materials.
· Use of the recycled debris to gravel 40 km of roads thus supporting improved access to the most needed facilities and/or agricultural areas, and rehabilitation of 28,000 m2 of other infrastructures in need such as public yards, walkways, and camps foundations using recycled debris, and, Using recycled rubble in concrete to create shops.
· Increase purchasing power of 600 local men and women most affected people by the earthquake, through the establishment of a CfW scheme for debris removal.
. address urgent needs for access and flood prevention by rehabilitating a damaged section of the main roads (11.5 km) in Idlep and Aleppo Governorats. It also includes expanding the WaSH main sewage network and improving rainwater drainage infrastructure
. Distributing cash grants to beneficiaries of the market built using concrete produced using recycled debris.
This intervention will support the communities residing in both Idleb and Aleppo governorates, by responding to alerts for debris removal from different locations, while ensuring best environmental practices in their communities through recycled debris and the use of recycled debris in repairing roads and community infrastructure.
Risks might be considered during the project cycle:
Safety Hazards: Debris sorting and processing can involve hazards like sharp objects, dust, and hazardous materials.
Market Fluctuations: The value of recycled materials can fluctuate, impacting project profitability.
Public Perception: Concerns about noise, dust, and potential environmental impacts from the recycling process.
Beside other assomptions:
Local authorities will grant permits for debris collection and processing facilities.
There is sufficient demand for recycled materials from the construction industry or other sectors.
The project has access to necessary equipment for debris sorting, crushing, and transportation.
Weather conditions will allow for safe and consistent debris management activities.
Mitigation Measures:
Safety: Implement comprehensive safety protocols, provide personal protective equipment (PPE) for workers, conduct regular safety training.
Logistics: Develop a detailed logistics plan outlining collection routes, processing procedures, and wast.
PSEA Integration:
Community Engagement:
Describe how you will involve the affected community in decision-making throughout the project. This could involve community meetings, focus groups, and grievance redressal mechanisms.
Emphasize how you will identify and address the specific needs and vulnerabilities of women, children, elderly, and disabled individuals within the community.
Equitable Labor Practices:
Outline your plan for ensuring fair wages and working conditions for all laborers involved in debris management and recycling activities.
Commit to following anti-discrimination practices in recruitment and employment.
Protection Points:
Child Protection:
Develop a Child Protection Policy that outlines measures to prevent child labor and exploitation at debris sites.
Train workers to identify and report any potential cases of child endangerment.
Gender-Based Violence (GBV) Prevention:
Integrate GBV risk mitigation strategies throughout the project cycle. This could involve ensuring well-lit work areas, buddy systems, and providing confidential reporting channels for GBV incidents.
