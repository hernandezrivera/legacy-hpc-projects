Project Summary

The deadly fighting between Sudan Armed Forces (SAF) and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces (RSF) which erupted on April 15, has further exacerbated the already-precarious situation in Sudan, putting millions of lives and livelihoods in danger. So far 843,130 people have been internally displaced as of 12 May and 220,652 new refugees and returnees have crossed to neighboring countries. The situation has compromised the food and livelihood security of the most vulnerable and threaten to worsen the conflict dynamics. Apart from growing criminality and insecurity in conflict-affected areas, unprecedented spike in acute food insecurity has been induced by previous dry spells and erratic rains, high price inflation for food, fuel and other commodities, floods, and persistent disease outbreaks. Combined, the above are resulting in record numbers of people in need of humanitarian assistance for the third year in a row.
According to FAO, production of staple crops during the 2022/2023 cropping season was above average. However, very high food prices emanating from the increasing cost of production has been responsible for worsening food insecurity. The conflict has caused tremendous human suffering and exacerbated pre-existing humanitarian needs amongst the civilian population in Sudan, including urban areas previously not or only marginally covered by the HRP. Before the conflict erupted in April 2023, 16.2 million people were food insecure across Sudan according to the Comprehensive Food Security and Vulnerability Assessment, representing about 34 percent of the population. In contrast, according to the revised HRP, it is estimated that the number of the food insecure will jump to 19.1 million people in the next three to six months if no urgent actions are taken. About 40 per cent of the population in phase 2 of the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) could slide into phase 3. It is foreseen that if the conflict continues, local food basket is projected to increase by a further 25 per cent in the next three-six months.
The risk of missing the current planting season is a matter of concern. Failure of the agriculture season will increase food insecurity and further exacerbate the conflict dynamics. Whilst insecurity has constrained pastoralists, agro-pastoralists, and farmers from pursuing their activities, some of the locations remain relatively safe, thus presenting an important window of opportunity to provide critical agriculture assistance.
This project will respond to critical life saving and life sustaining food security and livelihoods needs of over 60,000 people (31,350 women, 20,100 men, 5,700 girls and 2,850 boys). These include 25,000 vulnerable host community members: 22,000 IDPs and 13,000 returnees, vulnerable host community members and impacting other groups of concern including refugees. The overall objective of the project is to save lives by preventing failure of the agriculture season and protect the food and livelihood security of both displaced population and vulnerable host community member across 13 States in Sudan. It will focus on emergency agricultural support, livestock Support, livelihood cash support using Cash for Work modality.

1 - Needs

The already fragile situation in Sudan is being exacerbated by the deadly fighting between Sudan Armed Forces (SAF) and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces (RSF) which erupted on April 15, putting millions of lives and livelihoods in danger amid deepening hardship and human suffering. So far 843,130 people are internally displaced as of 12 May as 220,652 new refugees and returnees escape into neighboring countries. The situation has compromised the food and livelihood security of the most vulnerable and threaten to worsen the conflict dynamics. Apart from growing criminality and insecurity in conflict-affected areas, unprecedented spike in acute food insecurity was due to dry spells and erratic rains, high price inflation for food, fuel and other commodities, floods and persistent disease outbreaks are resulting in these record numbers of people in need of humanitarian assistance for the third year in a row. According to FAO, production of staple crops during the 2022/2023 cropping season was above average. However, the very high food prices emanating from the increasing cost of production is responsible for worsening food insecurity. The above has eroded livelihoods, damaged farms, and triggered widespread unemployment, especially among the already-vulnerable groups. The conflict has caused tremendous human suffering and exacerbated significant pre-existing humanitarian needs amongst the civilian population in Sudan, also affecting urban areas previously not or only marginally covered by the HRP. Before the conflict erupted in April 2023, 16.2 million people were food insecure across Sudan according to the Comprehensive Food Security and Vulnerability Assessment, representing about 34 per cent of the population. The conflict has caused loss of employment and economic opportunities in the affected areas and the disruption of supply chains and markets, commodity prices are now skyrocketing beyond the purchasing power of the most vulnerable. In the rural areas the number of the food insecure will increase significantly as they enter the hunger gap period. According to the revised 2023 HRP, it is estimated that the number of the food insecure will jump to 19.1 million people in the next three to six months if no urgent actions are taken. It is estimated that 40 per cent of the population in phase 2 of the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) could slide into phase 3. It is projected that if the conflict continues, local food basket is expected to increase by a further 25 per cent in the next three-six months. The risk of missing the current planting season is a matter of concern. Failure of the agriculture season will increase food insecurity and further exacerbate the conflict dynamics. Whilst insecurity has constrained pastoralists, agro-pastoralists, and farmers from pursuing their activities, some of the locations remain relatively safe, thus presenting an important window of opportunity to provide critical agriculture assistance. The on-going grave conditions affects women and girls disproportionately. UNDP and their partner field monitoring indicates that in some communities, girls and their mothers are always required to eat last after the men and boys have eaten the best part of the household meal. This puts women and girls at risk of food and nutrition insecurity. In other communities, it was discovered that women and girls do the cultivation, but men take over the harvests and sell most of the farm produce, living little behind for women and girls to feed on. In other areas, it was reported that women don’t own land for cultivation, they have to access land in the name of their male family members for them to cultivate. In such cases, women are required to compensate the landowners with certain percentage of their harvest. This, again, puts women at risk of food and nutrition insecurity.

2 - Activities/Outputs

This project will contribute to the Strategic Objective 1 of the of Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) Sector. It will focus on the following components and activities: 1. Agricultural Support (Seeds, Tools): UNDP will work in partnership with 13 implementing partners (national and international NGOs) in 13 States to provide emergency agriculture assistance to 23,000 vulnerable persons in protecting agriculture production by ensuring access to high quality seeds and improved tools on timely basis. This will also include irrigation kits, gardening kits etc. to enable them to restore and stabilize their food production. UNDP will support seed distribution using a combination of direct distribution, seed vouchers and seed fairs where appropriate. Working with FAO, the Ministry of Agriculture and Natural Resources, UNDP will provide extension services benefiting over 23,000 persons. 2. Livestock Support (distribution and treatment): UNDP will work with FAO to support livestock restocking to some 17,000 most vulnerable persons, especially women including vaccination to ensure the improved livestock health. UNDP will focus on identifying, procuring and distribution of high-quality adapted breeds and provide vaccination and treatment services through community livestock health workers in partnership with FAO. 3. Livelihood Cash/Voucher Support: The project support the food security of vulnerable people by improving their purchasing power and to enable them increase agricultural production, protect essential assets and creating emergency emergency employment through cash transfers (Cash for Work) benefiting over 17,000 people. They may also include community and group farms; the provision of essential labor for vulnerable households to enable them to cultivate in the current farming season and rehabilitation of dams, irrigation canals and other live-saving infrastructure. Implementing Partners:UNDP will implement this project with 13 different implementing partners including national and International NGOs. UNDP currently has maintain a roster of 42 pre-qualified implementing partners, covering all the 13 States, who are selected and engage on rapid basis soon as funds become available.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

This Project will mainstream environmental protection in all activities to ensure that production and utilization of food and the pathways to household livelihood restoration have no impact on the environment. UNDP will promote climate-smart and self-restoring agricultural methods that has minimum impact on the environment through provision of solar pumps and high quality extension services and farmer field schools. UNDP will also apply a conflict sensitive approach to planning and implementing the project, ensuring the expansion of agriculture production and other livelihood opportunities do not create and or exacerbate existing natural resource-based conflicts.

6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The intervention will mainstream protection concerns in food security and livelihood activities in order to address, mitigate and reduce risk to particular vulnerable groups especially women. UNDP will prioritize safety, dignity of vulnerable groups and avoid causing harm. UNDP will also ensure a transparent selection criteria and participatory processes, promote equitable and impartial access for all groups, enhance accountability. UNDP will also specially target and promote the participation and empowerment of the most vulnerable groups especially the Youth and women and take steps to address structural obstacles to their meaningful participation. The project will contribute to Sudan’s protection environment by conducting awareness raising trainings and campaigns on civil, social and economic rights of IDPs and refugees to increase self-reliance and opportunities for solutions. Focus will be on awareness and sensitization initiatives on basic rights with local authorities, host communities and service providers to reduce social tension and promote social cohesion. Capacity building trainings of community institutions that will be established to management the project such community management committees (CMCs), beneficiaries and key community members will include topics with focus on human rights, refugee rights, gender equity, SGBV, rights of people living with disabilities among others. The principle of equity and or “leaving no one behind”, targeting the most vulnerable and most in need will be incorporated in all stages of project implementation. Targeting criteria will be used throughout to ensure the most vulnerable are targeted.Project will also ensure to share information on protection issues that the project will come across with humanitarian and protection partners working in the state. The project will also ensure stronger coordination with humanitarian actors, especially UNHCR, WFP, IOM, etc, to avoid missed opportunities for integrating protection information in refugee profiling for project assistance. The project will seek for cooperation with NGOs, civil society and community-based organizations on any protection issues that will be countered during project implementation. FSL activities will carry out a “Do No Harm” analysis by understanding the impacts of assistance on the socio-political schisms that cause, or have the potential to cause, destruction or violence between groups. The Do-no-harm analysis will help to understand the complexity of the conflict environments in all project locations so that we can see how FSL decisions we make affect intergroup relationships. Every society has groups with different interests, leading to divisions in communities, hence, FSL activities will try to identify such groups as well as what divides those groups including sources like economic relations, geography, demography, politics or religion. This will also help us to think of different ways of FSL activity implementation.
