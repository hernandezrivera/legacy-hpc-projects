Project Summary

An increasing number of IDPs are continuing to explore options for return and relocation. Especially at the outset, people may return temporarily or seasonally to farm and access assistance and livelihoods. However, a highly militarized environment, and the frequent cattle raiding and revenge killings among the different clans and tribes exacerbate the risk of GBV in South Sudan. Similarly, displaced women and girls in the targeted locations are at risk of being raped, beaten or killed as they fetch water, firewood and other essential goods and services they need for domestic purposes. Furthermore, lack of recognition of GBV as violation of fundamental human rights perpetuates a culture of impunity throughout the country. Gender-based violence (GBV) cases, including sexual violence, are under-reported by survivors due to stigma, shame, low confidence in the rule of law to ensure justice for perpetrators, limited service availability, and reliance on informal justice structures which due to cultural norms can be an impediment to resolving some of the cultural issues around GBV.
INTERSOS will therefore implement GBV prevention and response activities targeting 35,895 beneficiaries in Yei, Laniya, Morobo and Kajo-Keji counties in Yei River State, and in Mayendit County, Unity State in the following payams: Madol 1, Pabuong, and Malkuer. In its response activities, INTERSOS will provide to all survivors of GBV a safe and confidential environment in GBV corners in Primary Health Care Centers/Units (PHCC/PHCU) and Women and Girls Friendly Spaces (WGFS). In the latter, survivors will be able to easily access psychosocial support services in form of peer-to-peer support, individual case management and recreational activities. All activities will focus on overcoming trauma and psychosocial distress by strengthening individual coping mechanisms, resilience and social network. Cases will be identified through the Community Mobilizers and community-based protection networks and will be referred to trained and skilled GBV Caseworkers. In order to comprehensively address GBV cases, INTERSOS will both conduct/update a service mapping to set up/strengthen the referral pathway as well as provide in-kind support to survivors to ensure their basic needs are covered in the cases in which referrals might not feasible. To ensure uptake of services, INTERSOS will conduct community awareness about available GBV services. Awareness raising sessions, together with community dialogues, will also play a preventive role by encouraging discussions with the community around social norms/behaviors and gender.

1 - Needs

Yei River State (YRS) remains largely underserved with an IDP population of 97,826, thereof 29,331 arrived in the last one year. Yei, the largest county hosts the second biggest IDP population in South Sudan . Fluid security situation outside Yei Town has prevented establishment of static presence of GBV actors, hindering the population’s access to GBV services. Interagency assessments in Morobo, Laniya and Kajo-Keji indicate that populations are in dire need, with no access to basic services. Even within Yei, there is limited capacity, which, together with the continuous influxes of IDPs, results in insufficient services. According to IOM reports, last September there were 674 HHs (1,699 individuals) displaced in YRS. Limited funds had immense effects on GBV in Emergencies programming including suspension or ending of partnerships with key GBV partners. FGDs and KIIs with IDPs on the ground performed by Yei Protection cluster and INTERSOS revealed numerous protection violations. In addition, proximity to Ebola prone zones remains worrisome as IDP’s face secondary and tertiary displacements. Yei has been identified a priority state by the GBV sub-cluster and Protection cluster. INTERSOS is now finalizing a 3-months GBV emergency response in Yei County and Mugwo County. Over the first two months of intervention, INTERSOS registered 56 cases on GBVIMS, including 21 of sexual violence, showing a considerable increase from the trend observed over the previous months. Such increase can be explained by the success in reaching communities that had never been accessed by GBV partners before as well as the effectiveness of the latest referral pathway that INTERSOS updated and disseminated. Mayendit County in Unity State is remote with limited access to basic services, such as health, education and WASH. According to an assessment by the education cluster, only 29% of girls are enrolled in school in Malkuer payam, representing a 53% drop from last year. Annual floods, resulting in temporary shelter and long unsafe distances to access sanitation facilities further exacerbate GBV risks. Lack of awareness of general protection, Child Protection and GBV is one of the major challenges. Traditional beliefs limit women’s access to income generating activities, rendering them fully dependent on men. In addition, the stigma associated with GBV and the fact that traditional structures do not consider GBV a violation, hinders survivors’ access to services, leading to underreporting. Besides the gaps in GBV services, the three payams targeted (Madol 1, Pabuong, and Malkuer) are also hotspots, with multiple fights reported.NTERSOS recently implemented an SSHF-funded GBV project in Mayendit in which pathways were created for reporting GBV cases in the community and two Women and Girls Friendly Spaces were established in Paboung and Madol 1 payams, providing a safe space for case management, PSS and skills development. On GBVIMS, 138 cases (21% related to girls) were registered over the six months’ period thanks to the creation of a safe and trustful environment for seeking assistance. Lack of GBV actors after INTERSOS’ intervention has resulted in discontinuation of services. Addressing these urgent needs requires protection-specific response and provision of accessible confidential, survivor-centered PSS services to address GBV and to ensure it is appropriately addressed across all sectors. Although the full magnitude of GBV in South Sudan is unclear, studies indicate that 65% of women and girls have experienced physical and/or sexual violence in their lifetime, 51% have suffered intimate partner violence and 33% of women have experienced sexual violence from a non-partner, mainly during attacks or raids. Perpetration of GBV with impunity continues in most parts of the country. Targeting GBV through the provision of life-saving services will reduce the most fatal consequences for survivors, while also improving their wellbeing.

2 - Activities

Rapid protection assessments and protection monitoring. Service mapping and establishment of a referral pathway was already established in the previous phase. INTERSOS will conduct assessments, through different community dialogue, safety audit and focus group discussions with men, women, boys, and girls and home visit to determine the factors that drive GBV to enable proper planning of GBV interventions in each of the target areas. INTERSOS will deploy Protection Monitoring Officers, and Community Mobilizers who will work with community-based protection mechanisms to monitor the situation and refer cases to INTERSOS trained GBV Case workers. Comprehensive Case Management Service provision with in-kind support. Case management (CM) services will be provided in safe spaces, which will consist of both Women and Girls Friendly Spaces (WGFS) and GBV corners in PHCC/PHCU and target 345 GBV survivors. INTERSOS will provide in-kind support to survivors to support their recovery and cover potential gaps in the referral pathway. INTERSOS will provide 450 dignity kits and other goods and services (e.g. food, NFIs, transportation) on a need basis, including to women and girls at risk to avoid stigma.Case management capacity building. INTERSOS will deliver case management training to 100 local protection and health actors to build/strengthen local capacity. INTERSOS will prioritize protection partners included in the referral pathways previously updated as part of the interventions in Yei and Mayendit. Women and Girls Friendly Spaces (WGFS). INTERSOS will establish WGFS to offer a safe and confidential environment where 1,300 women and girls will access psychosocial support services in form of peer-to-peer support, individual case management, recreational and IGA activities. Two facilitators will be hired locally by WFGS and trained on PSS, case management, and facilitation skills. The WGFS offers flexibility to roll out standardized activities to boost involvement and contextualize them to specific PSS needs of beneficiaries. All activities will focus on overcoming trauma and psychosocial distress by strengthening their individual coping mechanism, resilience and social network with the objective of will serve to link the women from the community and create a sense of protections among themselves. Individual/Group Counselling. Trained case workers will conduct PSS sessions with individuals identified to be in need during the assessments and the protection monitoring activities. PSS activities may be conducted both on an individual level and in small group sessions, respecting confidentiality and do no harm principles. PSS capacity building. INTERSOS will also deliver PSS training sessions to 75 local protection and health actors to build/strengthen local capacity. Prevention and mitigation of GBV risks through IGA. INTERSOS will support the recovery and economic empowerment of 140 women through IGA, incl. fuel efficient stoves, and livelihood for protective purposes. A selected group of women interested in starting their own business will attend regular skills training, e.g. on building fuel efficient stoves, and be assisted in developing a business plan. Selection will be on the basis of their vulnerability, e.g. women-headed households, GBV survivors, etc.GBV Awareness raising and prevention messaging. INTERSOS will conduct awareness campaigns targeting 23,255 women, girls, men and boys to inform about existing services, de-stigmatize help-seeking behavior as well as to address specific problems affecting women and girls such as early marriage. These campaigns will also engage the male population to ensure effectiveness of preventive work, and peer dissemination of messaging. Discussions around gender and social norms will be facilitated in regular workshops, including influential community leaders, men and boys to reflect on masculinity and the reasons behind violence towards women, gender, sexuality, and substance abuse.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes. INTERSOS has been serving displaced populations in the areas of intervention during 2019 so their perspectives and challenges are well understood and considered into the programme design.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

All relevant governmental and CS stakeholders have been involved in the need assessment and strategy identification steps. INTERSOS strives to incorporate participation of stakeholders in all interventions that it conducts. Throughout the assessment, and implementation, it will involve stakeholders, meaningfully respecting their decision-making abilities regarding needs. Arrangements with local authorities and leaders will facilitate the involvement of stakeholders at all stages of intervention (assessment, planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation). INTERSOS team will always involve local authorities and community leaders to share design and objectives of the interventions.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The intervention is aligned with Protection Cluster and GBV sub-cluster strategy within the framework of HNO 2020. In particular, the project aims at reducing the most fatal consequences of GBV, ensuring safe, equitable and dignified access to critical basic services to cater for the population's basic needs and enabling vulnerable people to recover from crisis, and build resilience through psychosocial and in-kind support and income-generating activities to support their coping capacities and livelihoods.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

According to the evolution of the context and with the deep knowledge gained by INTERSOS in the specific areas, the context results to be relatively stable. However, the project foresees mitigation measures in order to cope with any kind of fluidity and change of the social, political and accessibility situation.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes. INTERSOS work will focus on strengthening community structures and building local capacity through case management and PSS training to ensure sustainability. Particularly CBPN will be mobilized and introduce in the community to be contacted for necessary assistance. These messages will motivate at-risk individuals and survivors of GBV to report and get the necessary assistance.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Complaint and feedback mechanisms will be envisaged to provide multiple parallel channels (written/verbal, anonymous, etc.) for reporting. INTERSOS will also closely work with Protection Monitors and the community to identify vulnerable community members and ensure they have access to services and are considered for feedback.
