Project Summary

This project aims to provide lifesaving Services through integration between Nutrition and health by applying Nutrition Program (outpatients and inpatient services) in Nutrition Services facility which provide services at the community level to IDPs and host population (children U5 and pregnant and lactating women PLWs) And facility level. Nutrition services will provide by the Health staff (community health workers, Nutrition Technicians, doctors, and Nurses) Nutrition services include IYCF practices strategy and screening for malnutrition and Referring malnutrition cases (inpatient and outpatient) to health facilities and treating cases as needed. CHW Teams who will do MUAC screening at the community level and Nutrition technician will do MUAC screening at health facility level to all children (boys and girls )6_ 59 months and pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) .and by using Z-score also. Also, CHWs and Nutrition technicians will provide IYCF practices counseling to all caregiver of children (0 _ 59) months and PLWs in order to raise the awareness of beneficiaries in IYCF Practices including breastfeeding and supplementary feeding Nutrition supplies (Micronutrients, and plumpy doz will be distributed to children 6 _59 months and also those materials and Iron folic acid will be distributed to PLWs to prevent malnutrition, anemia and another micronutrients deficiency diseases. Counseling and screening services will be provided during community health workers visits camps and houses periodically. So operating costs for MUAC screening and transferring of the teams must be secured. Community health workers will do screening and Referral of malnutrition cases to Nutrition facility and Nutrition technician will do screening by z-score and send patients to doctor to be treated by applying CMAM (community management of acute malnutrition) program. Then community health workers will follow up outpatient cases by weekly visits to insure continuity of treatment and awareness raising caregivers about IYCF practices. UOSSM is a partner of UNICEF in field of Nutrition and works under the umbrella of the Nutrition cluster in coordination with other Nutrition partners these Nutrition center will receive malnutrition cases that were referred form Nutrition cluster partners to be treated.
Inpatient facility will be located in Sarmada and community health workers will provide services in Harem sub district and referrer malnutrition cases to health facility.
Nutrition program services target children under five years of age and pregnant and lactating women. Age of pregnant and lactating women is more 18 years to 24 years and this age group is about 35% of adolescents (10-24) years of age and they will receive services without any discrimination .This age group is involved in assessing need assessment through questionnaires. As well as adolescents will be involved in evaluating the services provided during the project period through the beneficiary satisfaction surveys. Focused discussion groups include adolescents Their suggestions are respected and used to solve problems and improve the quality of services provided .In addition to involved them in evaluating services after the project will finish.

Needs

According to HNO 2019 4.734,890 girls and boys under the age of five years and pregnant and lactating women are at risk of under nutrition and in need of comprehensive curative and preventive nutrition services to continue during 2019. Around 91,811 children under the age of five years are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition during 2019, out of which 30,821 girls and boys are at risk of death due to severe malnutrition if not treated appropriately. Without appropriate care, acute malnourished children are eleven times more likely to die than well-nourished children. 865,295 girls and boys under the age of five years suffer from micro nutrient deficiencies. It is estimated that 1,596,413 Million mothers will require maternal nutrition services and skilled support for optimal infant feeding and caring practices.Global Acute Malnutrition among boys and girls under the age of 5 years remains within acceptable international benchmarks in most of the assessed areas during 2018.The severity scale ranking for nutrition shifted from areas that were deemed mostly ‘critical’ and ‘severe’ in 2018 to mostly ‘severe’ and ‘major problem’ for 2019. Nutrition needs have increased in 2019, and therefore the response warrants an increase in coverage of nutrition services, in addition to flexible life-saving service modalities that address both immediate emergency nutrition needs of displaced populations and the medium to longer term needs of the community, returnees, and people living in newly accessible areas. SMART surveys and nutrition surveillance in sub-districts throughout Syria in 2018 generally show that global acute malnutrition (GAM) is mostly at acceptable levels. However, in Eastern Ghouta in Rural Damascus, the GAM rate did reach emergency thresholds at 11.9% following besiegement. In areas with newly displaced populations such as in Idlib, acute malnutrition of children 6-59 months of age rapidly increased in a space of 6 months (from January 1.5% to June 4%), and hard to reach areas such as Ar-Raqqa also nearly tripled (4.6% to 11.9%) in 2018. Pregnant and lactating mothers are particularly vulnerable due to their specific nutritional needs and health risks. Reporting data on nutrition screening in 2018 confirmed that moderate acute malnutrition rates of PLWs increased by 134 percent. That is, 10,064 pregnant and lactating women were malnourished in July 2018 compared to 4,297 in July 2017. Lack of access to health services and negative coping mechanisms such as meal reduction are some of the causes linked to deteriorating maternal nutrition. In addition to data that indicates ‘High’ stunting levels in Lajat, Dar’a in 2017 (27.5%) and in the sub-district of Jarablous, Aleppo in 2018 (26.7%) , stunting rates confirmed to be ‘Very High’ in Tel Abiyad (32.4%) and Eastern Ghouta (36%) in 2018, as indicated by SMART surveys and using the 2018 WHO-UNICEF stunting thresholds. The increase in chronic malnutrition potentially reflects long term inadequate dietary intake, including inadequate micronutrient intake, as well as repeated infections in younger children, poor feeding practices due to low rates of exclusive breastfeeding in the first 6 months of life, and inadequate complementary feeding in terms of diversity and frequency. A smart survey was conducted in Idleb by PAC team in coordination with UNICEF and Nutrition cluster .survey was conducted in October 2017 and the Results was: The prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM), defined as Weight-for-height Z scores (WHZ) <‐2 and/or edema was 2.1% (1.0- 4.1 95% CI),and the prevalence of severe acute malnutrition (SAM), defined as WHZ <‐3 and/or edema, was 0.3% (0.1- 1.4 95% CI), with no cases of edema. The prevalence of global acute malnutrition (GAM) in children 6–59 months, defined as MUAC <125 mm was 1.9% (0.8 – 4.6 95% C.I), and the prevalence of severe acute malnutrition (SAM), defined as MUAC < 115 mm was 0.3 % (0.1 – 1.5 95% C.I), No cases of edema had been found.

Implementation of activities

Comprehensive preventive and curative Nutrition Services will be providing to outpatient and inpatients through health staff (doctors-Nurses-Community health workers-Nutrition technicians) at Community and health facility levels.•Strengthening life-saving and preventive Nutrition services for vulnerable population groups focusing on safe and appropriate IYCF practices in emergency contexts and beyond,Nutrition supplies (plumpy doz-micronutrients- high energy biscuits-Iron folic tab) interventions, and optimal maternal nutrition.Infant and young child feeding interventions will be provided in the community,health facilities, and local health system structures in close collaboration and coordination with the health sector and reproductive health sub-cluster, food security and child protection sectors.•Improve equitable access to high quality,life-saving, curative nutrition services through systematic identification,referral, and treatment in nutrition facility of acutely malnourished cases for boys and girls under five and PLW.The response modality will be informed according to context and will be adjustable and flexible to serve the needs of the target groups. Provision of management of acute malnutrition will be ensured at the health facility and community level and in integration with infant and young child feeding services and primary health care services.•Receive referrals of malnutrition from other nutrition partners and treatment.The Nutrition program activities priority groups are children under five and PLW,regardless of their status or geographic location due to their general vulnerability to Nutrition deficiencies and their immature or compromised immunity.However,children under five and PLW living in harsh conditions such as those in hard-to-reach areas,IDP sites,overburdened communities,areas affected by a high intensity of hostilities, and women and children“on the move”are particularly vulnerable.These groups will be further prioritized by the Nutrition sector with the aim of providing minimum life-saving Nutrition services jointly as part of,where possible, an inter-linked and complementary multi-sector response.The project aims to improve life-saving byexpanding interventions in malnutrition SAM and GAM through referral and treatment in a specialized nutrition services facility and initiative to receive referrals from other nutrition partner will control acute malnutrition cases.Also,specific focus of integrated IYCF practices,especially primary healthcare,reproductive health at the community and facility level.The capacity of stakeholders such as health practitioners,nutrition staff,and community workers will be strengthened to facilitate critical one to one IYCF counseling support, group sessions, and community outreach activities.Preventive nutrition actions will focus on promoting child growth and development in the first 1000 days which will include maternal nutritional support during pregnancy and lactation.Micronutrient deficiency prevention will be promoted through service platforms such as micronutrients supplementation within health facilities, during accelerated campaigns,and improving vitamin A coverage for children 6-59months in coordination with immunization teams and routine distribution whenever feasible.The Nutrition sector will also prioritize the provision of specialized nutritious food to children 6-59 months among the populations in hard-to-reach areas,IDP last resort sites,overburdened communities or areas affected by a high intensity of hostilities as well as recently displaced IDP children or returnees.Quality,life-saving,curative activities will focus on community-and facility-based screening for acute malnutrition among girls and boys under five and PLW, followed by referral for treatment of acutely malnourished cases(outpatient and inpatient)with a focus on program quality performance indicators.Treatment of acute malnutrition will be integrated with the infant and young child feeding

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Increase risks of GBV while receiving nutrition services Likelihood: low. Impact: High.Mitigating Measures:1.Prepare separated reception utilities for men and women2.Availability of women in the medical staff (Nutrition technicians &community health workers) to provide privacy while the provision of service3.Separated rooms for men and women while staying in the health facility for recovery and monitoring Risk: Increase risks of Sexual Exploitation from staff while receiving nutrition services. Likelihood: low. Impact: High. Mitigating Measures:1.Providing a simple and clear compliant mechanism2.All staff commits to sign a code of conduct3.Providing PSEA training for all staff Risk: Increased risk of inter-community tension due to providing nutrition services to specific members of the community. Likelihood: low. Impact: Medium.Mitigating Measures:1.Providing service to all people in need regardless of any other criteria2- community raise awareness about nutrition services standers especially formula distribution. Risk: Difficulty of reaching health facilities/services for PWDs. Likelihood: low. Impact: High.Mitigating Measures:1.Providing facilitation utilities that make it easier for PWDs to reach out for the services2.Providing comfortable chairs and latrines in place of healthcare service for PWDs3.Implementing all the global standards for dealing with PWDs Risk: Possible inconsistency with local traditions and culture. Likelihood: medium. Impact: High.Mitigating Measures:Availability of women in project staff at all levels where contact with beneficiaries are presented.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: - Health facilities in safe areas are not vulnerable to targeting - Exchange rates are fixed and do not affect the purchase of materials according to the appropriate quantity and quality- Nutrition Supplies is available in time Risks- Targeting the health facilities of the project because of the war- Great changes in Exchange Rates - Nutrition supplies is not available Mitigating measures - Prepare an emergency plan so that we provide service to beneficiaries through mobile clinics - The budget includes a margin covering the expected change in the exchange rate- Secure inventory in the warehouse- Referral inpatient cases to be treated in other health facility.
