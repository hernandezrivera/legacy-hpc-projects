Project Summary

UNICEF will provide support to ensure quality, equitable and gender-sensitive maternal, newborn, child and adolescent health services, sexual and reproductive health (SRH) services including counselling and referral for GBV and support to pregnant and lactating women inclusive of children with HIV, with attention to people with disabilities. The project targets a vulnerable population of 357,414 people across the Rohingya refugee and host communities. To respond to the basic health and nutrition needs of Rohingya and host community women and children, UNICEF will continue providing harmonized health services through the 13 health facilities in the camps (5 PHCs, and 8 HPs) and 3 Diarrhoea Treatment Centres (DTCs) – two of which are hibernated) as well as strengthening government health systems and facilities in adjacent host communities in Ukhiya and Teknaf (2 union sub-centres and 16 community clinics). A special focus will continue to support the continuation of well-functioning Newborn Stabilization Unit (NSU) with Kangaroo Mother Care in Ukhiya andTeknaf UHCs, Chokoriya Upazila Health Complexes. Support will continue to the special newborn care unit (SCANU) at the District Hospital as a referral center with focus on providing comprehensive primary health care services through the Essential Services Packages (ESP) as per Government’s Guideline and SOPs including maternal, newborn care, immunization, integrated management of childhood illnesses, nutrition, acute watery diarrhoea prevention and management, prevention of mother-to-child transmission of HIV services and community engagement. UNICEF will also strengthen the Health Management Information System (HMIS/DHIS2) through technical support with 01 HMIS Consultant (based in Civil Surgeon Office) and capacity building of services providers on DHIS2 in coordination with all health sector partners including government counterparts, and coordinate with team at National level in Dhaka to address technical issue in the system. UNICEF has developed the Emergency Response Plan to provide support during the emergency/disasters.

1 - Identified Needs

The influx of Rohingya refugees from northern parts of Myanmar Rakhine State into Bangladesh restarted on 25 August 2017. The Fully Vaccination Coverage (FVC) in Rakhain State was very much low. As a result, the migrated children with their family remain vulnerable and prone to disease outbreak. UNICEF is primarily focusing on preventive Health services in close cooperation with the Government and other partners such as WHO as high number of partners are working in curative side of Health. This aims to provide emphasis on routine immunizations and mass campaign on immunizations. UNICEF and WHO supported routine immunization through government to reach 30,960 children (male: 15,472; female: 15,488) under 1 year of age. 130 vaccinators (male: 89, female: 43) work in 816 outreach sites in the camps and in 58 fixed sites (through NGOs). Health facility utilization is within the normal range of 2-4 consultations but access to 24/7 services remain a challenge. Adherence to minimum standard of services and staffing is a concern and quality of services need to be improved. Scaling up Prevention of Mother to Child Transmission service in the selected health facilities in the camps, in two upazila health complexes and district hospital. To ensure quality Newborn Care, UNICEF has established NSU in the UHCs of Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazila Health Complexes. UNICEF also supporting to make functioning of KMC Corners in those UHCs. Referral linkages, especially at night required to be strengthen and more support needed for secondary care in field hospital and upazila health complex to reduce burden in District Hospital. Maternal and neonatal death surveillance need to be expanded to help identify and address avoidable mortality triggering factors. There are unmet needs to address vulnerable population particularly newborn and their mothers as over 70 percent of deliveries occur at home without trained assistance and antenatal care is infrequent and remains sub-standard. Children under 5 of age are at risk for common childhood illnesses such as pneumonia, diarrhea and malnutrition, some of which stem from difficulties ensuring exclusive breastfeeding for children under six months of age. There is inadequate access to information on services particularly for women and adolescent girls and boys, with men predominantly holding the decision-making power in Rohingya households. GBV and domestic violence affect women and girls disproportionately. UNICEF and its partners ensured the full involvement of female as community health workers (CHWs) - currently 85 per cent - who are better able to engage with mothers and families as well as on GBV issues under SRHR activities. The household visits increase the possibility for women and girls to access care and information since their movement sometimes limited. However, community engagement will involve not only the women and female caregivers but also men and male caregivers. This will continue to be vital for improving health seeking behavior and linking communities with the health care system. Community outreach activities need to be harmonized, cross linked with different working groups and monitored so it can be improved. Health information and management systems does exist, however, government platform DHIS2 needs to be strengthened adjusting the technicalities of interoperability with other existing systems. Acute watery diarrhoea and other infectious diseases are need to be prevented and treated, and consider the risk in the overcrowded makeshift settlement and host communities, it needs robust surveillance systems.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1.1. Implementing routine immunization for children < 2 yr of age & pregnant women, & SIAs activities for Rohingya and host population in Cox's Bazar. Support 4 EPI sites in Cox's Bazar District EPI store, Ukhiya, Teknaf and Balukhali Sub Centre. HR will be provided to Balukhali sub centre to ensure the optimal functionality Activity 1.2. Providing quality & equitable MNCAH services, SRH services & counselling & referral for GBV & support to pregnant women & children with HIV, with attention to people with disabilities. Activities include 5 PHCs & 8 HPs ; SCANU at DH and NSUs Ukhiya and Teknaf UHCs and Newborn Care Corners in health facilities ; strengthen newborn and child health services (IMNCI/N) both at HFs & community levels. Support capacity building of the health service provider and conduct training on ETAT, sick newborn management, and KMC, EMEN Clinical Standard, 5-S QI and PDCA. Some effort had been made toward DFHF. Coordination on MPDSR extended to cover Rohingya communities in camp in addition to host population. Partners: PHD & RTMI.Activity 1.3. Strengthening PMTCT services for Rohingya and host communities (includes consensus building, awareness and education on HIV/STI/PMTCT, ANC HIV testing, initiate ART for identified positive pregnant women, ensure safe delivery, prophylaxis and ART for infants, ART adherence follow up mother and children through outreach workers, and referral for Early Diagnosis of infants ). Support to Cox's Bazar District Hospital, Ukhiya UHC, and 17 facilities in the camps with collaboration with other UN Agencies (UNHCR and IOM) & Save the Children. Activity 2.1 Preventing and managing AWD outbreak including management of 3 DTCs - 1 in Leda and 2 hibernated - (activities include capacity building, procurement of medicines and supplies, surveillance, hygiene promotion and OCV effectiveness study. Strong collaboration with WASH and C4D will be maintained. Activity will be implemented by icddr,b. Activity 2.2. Preparing monsoon and cyclone contingency plan (procurement and preposition of supplies including tents for temporary health facilities, capacity building of community health workers, health mobile teams, rapid needs assessment, assessing and reinforcing health facilities and assisting vulnerable population.Activity 2.3 Establishing and maintaining the SARI-ITC in Teknaf with collaboration with icddr,b, including capacity building/training for IPC and identifying and managing COVID-19; and risk communication to improve understanding of COVID-19 among the refugees and host communities, improve health seeking behaviour in relation to COVID related illness, addressing rumour and misconception among the communities.Activity 2.4 in relation to risk communication and awareness creation to ensure rapid uptake of COVID-19 vaccines among target population refugees and host communities. The activities including managing supply and logistic as part of cold chain strengthening and communication.Activity 3.1 Strengthening community health mechanisms to enhance community based MNCAH services including health promotion, and community outreach services. Community Health Workers are part of primary health care services in order to improve health seeking behavior and linking communities with health care system. Capacity of multi-purpose volunteers in community platforms and systems (including CHWs) for continuing dialogues with community for raising health awareness and community accountability through mobilizing families, caregivers involving male households, adolescent boys and girls, pregnant mothers and children. Activity 4.1 Strengthening HMIS, specifically DHIS 2 including MNCH services, Immunization, Community Health and NCD data tracking, data analysis and evidence-generation to inform strategic policy decisions and guidance. Strengthening e-tracker systemsActivity 4.2 strengthening coordination mechanism at district & field level. Improved coordination mechanism
