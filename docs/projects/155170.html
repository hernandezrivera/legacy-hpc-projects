Needs

In 2019, the SMS agencies will continue to support the CiC & RRRC in the management of sites to ensure that organizations and programs are efficiently coordinated and to avoid gaps and duplications. Key areas that will need strengthening in the coming months is the consolidation of Communication with Communities effort as well as the implementation of the new community representation system (guidelines currently under revision by RRRC). The expected appointment of teams for each CiC in the course of 2019 will require dedicated capacity building and mentoring to ensure that these teams understand well their role & responsibilities while adopting “do no harm” approach. Similarly, continuous effort to build the capacity of SMS agencies and especially national partners will be required as the efforts for localization continue. Continuous and coordinated information management and mapping capacity will be required to ensure the response is properly tailored to meet the needs of the affected populations and support the required prioritization of competing priorities. In the continuity of the efforts pre- and during the monsoon / cyclone season, Disaster Risks Reduction (DRR) efforts will need to continue to mitigate risks and ensure preparedness. Streamlining the roles and responsibilities of the various groups and volunteers involved in preparedness and response will be key to ensure effectiveness and efficiency. Holistic macro-level development strategies and detailed micro-plans will be developed in tandem and used to improve access across the camps and ensure an equitable distribution of services, while simultaneously decongest the mostly densely inhabited areas and create well-planned communities. In parallel to this, we will seek to improve the natural environment though landscape restoration and reforestation. Integrating neighboring host communities to these plans will be important to ensure they benefit from planned infrastructure and that the negative impact of refugee settlements on host communities are minimized. Coordination with a large variety of stakeholders will be required such as technical and local authorities (e.g. LGED) or Development donors (such as the World Bank and the Asian Development Bank) that will fund large infrastructure projects in and around camps. SMS agencies’ knowledge of the camps, existing relationships with the communities and technical capacities will be needed to support these actors. Both small scale site improvement and large infrastructure will need to continue to ensure equal access to services though the construction of pathways, bridges, staircases and roads. The construction and maintenance of drainages as well as slope stabilization will be required to mitigate risks. The installation of solar street lights will need to be scaled up as there is still very limited lighting at night.

Activities or outputs

1. CCCM Sector Coordination: a) Deploy and maintain a coordination team, with a strong focus on macro-planning and disaster risks reduction - 2. Site Management Support (SMS): a) Coordination with a focus on the rationalization of partners at camp-level, b) Monitoring of services, gaps and duplications, c) Roll-out of the community representation model, c) Establishment of community participation mechanisms to reduce the vulnerability of the refugees, foster positive interaction with the host community and ascertain that the assistance meet existing needs, d) Strengthen complaints and feedback mechanisms and CwC, e) Decongestion of the highly populated areas through reorganization of spaces, increasing the usable space and relocation, f) Provide SM support training and on-the-job mentoring to both SMS staff and authorities - 3. DRR activities will include both preparedness and mitigation measures while building on the resilience of the displaced communities and neighboring host communities: a) Community risks mapping, camp wise risk assessment and risk informed disaster Management Plans, b) Develop hazard specific disaster management plans and simulations, c) Targeted relocations from dangerous areas to reduce exposure to natural hazards, d) Mapping of CPP capacity, needs and gaps, e) Assessment of the Fire Safety & Civil Defense capacity for intervention in the camps, equipment and trainings for SUV, CPP, f) Tree planting and landscape restoration, g) Use of bio-engineering for slope stabilization - 4. Site Improvement: Small Scale Rehabilitation: a) Improvement in living conditions for refugees and host populations through neighborhood-level quick impact projects implemented through CfW. Projects identified and prioritized by the affected population and include construction of secondary roads, pedestrian pathways, water crossings, drainage, slope protection, landscape restoration - 5. SD - Large Scale Infrastructure: a) Work towards more durable solutions to promote safe and dignified living conditions for refugees, b) Facilitate decongestion of the most densely populated camps, and enhance access to services via extended road and bridge networks, c) Support the work planned by the WB and the ADB and relevant authorities (such as LGED) and ensure the maintenance of IOM previously built infrastructure - 6. Community Lighting: a) In coordination with the EEWG, support the regular mapping of existing solar street lights in sites and continue the installation of solar street lights, ensuring the maintenance through the training of maintenance committees - 7. Redeveloping existing settlements: a) Redevelop the decongested areas of the camps to create well planned, safe and accessible settlements, b) Work with the SM teams and CiCs to create Camp-level plans to identify key locations for redevelopment and/or installing new civic infrastructure - 8. SMEP: a) Clearing and strengthening primary drainage channels, b) Maintaining vehicular access via maintenance, emergency repair and rehabilitation, c) Materials supply, fabrication, and pre-positioning for rapid response, d) Road construction, e) Landscaping and slope protection - 9. Needs and Population Monitoring: a) 6 rounds of NPM SA covering all locations hosting refugees, b) Regular updates of site profiles with key indicators of multisectoral needs, c) Provision of updated imagery of displacement sites, d) IM and GIS support to the definition and adjustment of site boundaries, e) Surveys to fill information gaps regarding host community relations and perceptions, f) Ad hoc rapid needs assessment - Gender & Age: IOM will work to ensure the inclusion of women and girls in its programming and implemented dedicated programming when relevant to address the specific needs of women, girls, boys and men - AAP: Participation will be a key component of the SMS programs to enable affected populations to play an active role in the decision-making processes that affect them.
