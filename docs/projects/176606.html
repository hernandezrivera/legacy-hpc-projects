Project Summary

CMC's GBV and GP Intervention aims at provision of critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated/mainstreamed approach to address the priority needs of IPC 4&5 affected population with PIN severity level 3&4 including vulnerable women, men, girls and boys and inclusion of elderly, people with disability and people living with HIV/AIDs in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas and Prevent, mitigate and respond to GBV risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience to achieve cluster’s objectives (GBV) PO1&2 (CO1.1.3, 1.1.2, 2.2) .
CMC aims at Prevailing social norms, gender roles, national convict and other hazards like COVID-19, floods, a highly militarized environment, and the frequent cattle raiding and revenge killings among the different clans and tribes exacerbate the risk of GBV in South Sudan. Protection concerns such as generalized violence and insecurity, access to food, water and firewood, expose women to a high risk of GBV. There is, therefore, an urgent need to enhance GBV prevention efforts with additional strategies to mitigate risks, such as fuel strategies and protective accompaniments. Furthermore, lack of recognition of GBV as violation of fundamental human rights continues a culture of impunity and other injustices throughout the country. Therefore, notwithstanding the vitalized peace agreement, it is important to continue to raise awareness, reduce prevalent stigmas in the communities and increase identification and documentation of GBV cases as well as providing survivor’s access to psychosocial support and other services for their recovery and healing.
CMC will provide psychosocial support services, women and girls friendly spaces, CBP preparedness, Cash base intervention and protection risk mitigation. Individual/ comprehensive case management and recreational activities. Identification of cases through the community engagement groups, local authorities, community-based protection networks and will be referred to trained skilled GBV Caseworkers. Women empowerment of through income generating activities that support their livelihood through mainstreaming GBV implementation alongside other sectors response. A selected group will be train on business skills on starting their own business in developing a business plan. In order to confront social norms that drive GBV, focusing on engaging men and boys through EMAP strategy in its programming to become change agents for shifting gender roles and ensuring gender gaps are covered through gender responsiveness. The discussion around gender and social norms will be facilitated in regular workshops, including inertial community leaders to react on masculinity and the reasons behind their violence + − Leaflet | © OpenStreetMap towards women, gender, sexuality, and substance abuse. CMC protection team will conduct awareness campaigns addressing specific problems affecting girls such as child and forced marriage and the low inclusion of girls in school. CMC will play an important role in strengthening coordination among Humanitarian actors present.
CMC will strengthen coordination with the GBV Working group, as well as coordinating strengthening of the GBV referral pathways and information sharing at national level and joint activities with Partners to prevent duplication and incorporation of GBV Sub-cluster Strategy. To effectively respond to GBV, CMC will collaborate with strategic partners to contribute to the provision of the basic package of multi-sectoral GBV services at community level and build capacity of service providers and communities to deliver quality GBV services in line with best practices and minimum standards for humanitarian settings. Strengthening GBV prevention and risk mitigation across its interventions through mainstreaming GBV in Education, WASH, FSL, legal/justice and Health sectors to further ensure active community engagement throughout the implementation

1 - Needs

According to the South Sudan HNO 2021, Gender-based violence is pervasive in South Sudan. As a result of structural gender inequality and unequal gender power relationships, with women and girls and especially those who are internally displaced or living near cantonment sites, or with a disability continue to suffer from GBV. Flooding, armed conflict in pockets of areas, sub-national violence and COVID-19 are escalating the risk of GBV including Conflict Related Sexual Violence (CRSV) and sexual exploitation and abuse. The condition also compounded reduced access to sexual and reproductive health services for women and girls. Measures adopted to curb the spread of COVID-19 like lockdowns have also contributed to a rise in GBV incidents. In 2021, 2 million people are estimated to be affected by GBV attributed to COVID pandemic and subsequent limit access to services. The Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) ‘Key IPC Findings indicates: December 2020 to March 2021, an estimated 5.82 million people (48.3% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 11,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Pibor County of Pibor Administrative Area. During this period, an estimated 1.79 million people are likely to face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Between December 2020 and March 2021, the food security situation is expected to seasonally improve with an estimated 190,000 people (29.4% of the population) likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 153,000 people will likely be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, and 37,000 people will likely be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Similarly during this period, Raga and Wau counties are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, whereas Jur River County is classified in Stressed (IPC Phase 2) acute food insecurity. A key driver of the improvement in the food security situation is the availability of harvests. Although market prices will likely remain high across the state, and some COVID-related restrictions will remain in place, trade routes from Sudan and from other countries via Juba will become more accessible thus increasing availability of food commodities in the markets. Based on nationwide October 2020 FSMNS+ assessment, the risk of GBV is identified high as 49% of surveyed households expressed concern on GBV and 34% of households travel more than 30 minutes to fetch water. Similarly, 49% of affected women and girls who are in need of services have no access to GBV services and only 12% of the community have awareness on available GBV services. With this trend women and girls living in Aweil South and Aweil West are likely to be trapped in a web of transactional sex in desperation to provide food for their children. Based on the 2020 GBV IMS data analysis, 29% of GBV survivors in need of safe house did not obtain the service because of lack of safe houses. Reach assessments of January 2021 reported an increase in Intimate partner violence in Yei county and rape cases perpetuated by men in uniform. IRNA assessment for October 2020 indicated massive abduction, revenge killings and forced early marriage of women and girls attributed to subnational violence among Pibor and Akobo communities .Increased risk of GBV coupled with gaps in response services necessitates an urgent need for strengthened primary GBV prevention, integration of GBV risk mitigation in other sectors, and scaling up GBV response services. Provision of safe housing, dignity kits and livelihood support continue to be a high priority. The continuous conflict dynamics and natural disasters perpetuated women’s vulnerability and exposure to GBV. 2 - Project activities

CMC will work closely with strategic partners to avail comprehensive basic package of multi-sectoral GBV services at community level and build capacity of service providers and communities to deliver quality GBV services in line with best practices and minimum standards for humanitarian settings. CMC protection team additionally, will undertake activities in line with IASC guidelines for GBV programming, Cluster Strategy and MOH/WHO COVID guidelines. 1. Provision of appropriate GBV case management services including coordinated care and support for child and adult survivors 2. Establish and / or strengthen GBV referral systems and linking GBV survivors to available response services and support. 3. Provision of survivor centered psychosocial support focused on healing, empowerment and recovery including referrals for MHPSS 2. 4. Implement Women and Girls Friendly Space (WGFS) programming to provide services, information and activities that promote healing, well-being and empowerment` 5.Implement community engagement and awareness raising activities on GBV risk factors, mitigation measures and on how survivors can access support and services in line with the “do no harm” principle 6. Engage men and boys for accountable practices (EMAP) to transform harmful social norms that perpetuate gender inequality, and to promote the health and safety of women and girls 9. Conduct safety audits and protection risk analysis with multi sector partners ( WASH, Health, Nutrition, FSL Education ) 10. Conduct training of community/frontline workers on comprehensive case management 3. Conduct training of community/frontline workers on PSS/ PFA and community Based PSS 11. Conduct referrals of women, men, girls and boys supported to legal aid and legal counselling services in close coordination with general protection partners including law society of south Sudan. 12. Conduct awareness campaigns targeting women, men, girls and boys on GBV prevention and risk mitigation strategies including PSEA in coordination with FSL and WASH partners 13. Distribute dignity kits and hygiene kits targeting women of reproductive age and adolescent girls in collaboration with menstrual health and hygiene partners and WASH partners. 14. 3 - Project indicators

M&E plan will be developed for the project to determine the type of data that need to be collected, frequency of data collection, tools used for data collections, responsible person and track the progress against key results and output indicators and the information captured in different databases. CMC’ M&E team will develop standard cluster approved data collection approaches participatory in joint needs assessments the cluster’s M&E working groups. Periodic data analysis and interpretation through weekly reporting to the donor and the 5Ws reporting for the cluster will be establish at diverse levels of project implementation and shared with relevant stakeholders to inform about the project progress. A mix of quantitative, qualitative, participatory and observatory data collection methods will be engaged for collecting data in relation to the key indicators and the findings to be incorporated into the CMC M&E reports. Data collection and analysis will be done separately for women, men, children and the elderly. CMC will utilize sex and age disaggregated data throughout the action. Baseline survey will be conducted to establish pre-project benchmarks for key project level indicators and will be used to measure the project progress towards the end of the project. Quantitative data collection methods will be used for this purpose. Post intervention monitoring (PIM) and Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) will be conducted in all target areas at the end of the interventions and/or after 6 to 8 weeks of each distribution to assess the short-term impact of the interventions and perception of people on the quality of items distributed, convenience of distribution location, safety of people traveling to and going back from distribution locations, etc. and inform subsequent distributions. Households Interviews, Focus Group Discussions (FGD), Key informant interviews (KII) and observations will be the major methodologies during PIM/PDM. All survey data collection will be supported through mobile-based systems. Some of indicators used will be: # of women, men, girls and boys provided with individual, group, specialized PSS and other related services (e.g. recreational activities, livelihood services, parenting skills) #of women, men, boys and girls with protection concerns supported through case management and referral services, # of women, men, boys and girls with protection concerns supported through referral services, # of women and girls who accessed services in Women and Girls Friendly Space (disaggregated by age and type of service), # of women, men, girls and boys supported with legal counselling, # vulnerable women and girls supported with cash and voucher assistance and other livelihood an economic support, # of women, men, girls and boys reached through GBV awareness raising activities, # of women, men, girls and boys reached through GBV prevention initiatives (disaggregated by type e.g. SASA, EMAP etc), # of men, women, girls and boys receivingservices in safe house/shelters, # of staff, community/frontline workers trained on GBV guiding principles and prevention and response, CMC will collect qualitative data about assistance, satisfaction, changes resulting from the project. CMC conducts periodic Lessons Learnt reviews which fuels the process of constant improvement. All reports will be shared between relevant stakeholders, other partners and clusters. . In order to protect the health of the community and staff and to curtail community transmission of COVID-19, these surveys will be conducted depending on the COVID-19 situation in South Sudan
