Project Summary

With this project, UOSSM seeks to contribute to achieving the following:
Increase access to life-saving and life-sustaining coordinated, equitable humanitarian health services for those most vulnerable and in need.
Strengthen health sector capacity to prepare for, detect and deliver timely response to disease outbreaks.
Strengthen health system capacity to support continuity of care, strengthen community resilience, and respond to IDP movements and changes in context.
This project will contribute to strengthening the health care and wellbeing for conflict-affected communities in NS by improving access and ensure the sustainability and quality of health services targeting Syrians including the hosts and IDPs with more focus on the most vulnerable people including women, children, and the elderly as well as the PWDs by supporting 28 HFs (4 hospitals,4 specialized centers, 5 CCTCs, and 15 PHCs) in Idleb and Aleppo. As such, this project will mitigate the economic burdens that patients face in securing medicines, especially for patients with chronic diseases, children, and pregnant women. The intervention will also contribute to response to the COVID-19 by providing the HFs with PPE and early detecting of the suspected cases and refer them to PCR Labs and through CCTCs.
The above-mentioned HFs are equipped and staffed but have a lack of medicine mainly, the HFs located in areas with high priority according to health cluster and the TWGs, where distributed in densely populated and accessible locations, most of the people are IDPs with a very low level of medical care and bad economic situation.
All of the HFs were included within the central referral network as the capacity of the HFs gives them an important role in the referral system, and the HFs have close coordination with IPC central desk as well as HFs commit to sharing the health data through HIS, 4Ws monthly.
Supporting and upgrading capacity of the HFs is very important in light of their strategic locations in addition to the size and variety of health services provided through these HFs that include:
PHC services: management of childhood illness, management of NCDs and CDs, providing RH services, providing dental services, applying IPC measures, vaccination services, lab tests, and bandage services).
Hospital services: general surgical, obstetrics, RH services, internal medicine, laparoscopic services, urinary surgeries, orthopaedic, and ophthalmic services their related surgeries.
Specialized centers services diagnostic, treatment, and blood transfusion services for thalassemia patients, specialized radiography services, dialysis services, and providing patients with blood products and blood transfusions for them as well as providing the surrounding hospitals with blood units though the blood bank.
CCTCS: most prioritized centres recommended by the COVID-19 task force. The centres are ongoing and funded by OCHA until end of Aug 2021, CCTCs provide services for treating mild and moderate cases of COVID-19, as they are considered the largest percentage of confirmed cases, ensuring that the severe cases in which the morbidity is high will be referred to the case management hospitals and ICU.

5 - Implementation of activities

Provide essential PHC and STHC services, including trauma care, MH, nutrition, surgeries, Specialized health services, BEmONC, CEmONC, and treatment of mild and moderate cases of COVID19 and creating service mapping of the different humanitarian actors, and coordinating with health partners in the area to create referral pathways linked with the PHCs.Provide health facilities with essential medicines and medical supplies, by preparing the list of essential medical supplies according to the EHSP purchasing process, delivering the supplies to HFs. Strengthen the capacity of health care providers and CHWs, by conducting training need assessment, providing training packages, conducting supervision visits to evaluate the improvement in the capacity of the trained staff.Increase access to health services by supporting 28 HFs and rehabilitating them to be more suitable for beneficiaries and creating areas that secure the privacy of patients and prevent sexual exploitation and to ensure easy access for PWDs, and create effective referral pathways.Coordinate the humanitarian health response through participating in health sector coordination meetings held across response hubsM&E activities: including the M&E team in the designing process and activate their role by conducting regular visits , preparing the satisfaction reports ,highlighting the needs and feedbacks of beneficiaries, insuring the reporting(4Ws, HeRAMS, EWARS/EWARN, and the surveillance system of attacks on healthcare).

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1-Risk-based violence, gender, and sexual exploitation as a result of overcrowding and shared toilets as well as the exploitation of staff to patients in exchange for service delivery: possibility: high, effect: highmitigating measures: Establishing separated toilets for men and women-Ensuring that the waiting areas are sufficient for patients –and activating the appointment by phone-Allocating the guard to regulate the flow of patients –Establishing an easy-to-reach complaints mechanism (complaints box, WhatsApp number, exit interviews). Where UOSSM adopted this mechanism in the ongoing PHCs and benefited from the feedback of beneficiaries in redesigning the project to meet the beneficiaries' needs and that shows the engaging of beneficiaries in the in-project designs-Training all employees on the policy of preventing harassment in the workplace and including this policy in their contracts 2- Neglecting the most vulnerable (children, women, the elderly, and the disabled): possibility: moderate, effect: highmitigating measures: -Employing female staff to ensure that services are being provided to women–Ensuring that the logistics are suitable for elderly and disabled people (e.g wheelchairs)- Activating the role of M&E team to evaluate the satisfaction of patients 3- Discrimination in service delivery: possibility: moderate, effect:high mitigating measures: - A comprehensive services mapping of the area - Activating the role of the CHWs to communicate with the population in the targeted communities and to identify the main needs - Selecting accessible locations of HFs. Monitoring the risks: Post-monitoring/client feedback forms are used. Resources Required: Monitoring staff.Acceptable Level of Risks: Yes

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Risk of doing SEA by not PSEA trained staff utilizing the authority (Provision of services):Likelihood: high Impact: high Mitigation: 1. UOSSM developed PSEA code of conduct in line with the PSEA network. All the recruited staff should read and sign the PSEA code of conduct.2. Recruited staff should attend PSEA training provides by UOSSM’s PSEA Focal Point.3. Establish a PSEA reporting mechanism for beneficiaries to repot any misconduct by staff. we will include (phone numbers, e-mails, verbal reporting and complaints’ box) in any awareness materials.4. Distribute awareness images inside the centers in addition to use the child satisfaction forms (expressive images and tacit questions) to make sure that no abuse has been done. Parents will also be encouraged to register any complaints expressed by children UOSSM has PSEA code of conduct it’s included on to set forth the expectations of conduct and mutual respect in regard to sexual exploitation and abuse and the process of complaint if these expectations are not met or violated.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: UOSSM will coordinate closely with the health clusters, as well as local authorities and humanitarian actors inside Syria throughout the proposed intervention, informing the cluster if and when adjustments are needed to the intervention. Location remains accessible for the team and the beneficiaries. Inflation and currency exchange rates remain within an acceptable range that does not affect planned deliverable quality and quantity. Local qualified staff required for project activities are available and adequate.Risk: The conflict prevents beneficiaries and team access to facilities. The close of borders prevents the shipments of supplies and medications to access facilities. Rates of increased infection cases of Covid-19 are much higher than expectations and greater than capabilities  The spread of Covid 19 infection among the project staff significantly Lack of specialists and qualified staffmitigation: Develop a strong risk management strategy, (preparing an emergency plan /plan B/ according to the expected scenarios in conflicted areas. Capacity building plan for the staff. Enhance the coordination with the health partner and the active participation in the TWG to fill up the gaps of medical supplies. Expanding the project and adding other isolation centers (UOSSM has the capacity to implement if the fund is provided). Implement strict recommendations on quarantine, isolation, and provision of IPC means and PPEs Conducting an enhancement training course (the proper use of personal protective equipment).
