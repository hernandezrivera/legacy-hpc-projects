Project Summary

This project aims to respond to cholera outbreaks in NWS by providing multi-sectorial (integrated Health and wash) lifesaving interventions for the most vulnerable in NWS.
1. Mercy-USA will establish a cholera community treatment center integrated with the treatment of malnutrition cases for children who have cholera.
2. Also, community mobilization, early identification of cholera cases, and referral will be made through our current RRTs.
3. Our current fixed nutrition centers will provide rapid access to oral rehydration solutions for patients with suspected cholera in our operation areas. This will be done in close coordination with WHO to ensure we have enough supplies to distribute.
4. Mercy-USA will choose two small camps (500 populations) to provide them with the basic needs of WASH activities; this will include water trucking, solid waste management, desludging septic tanks, and hygiene items.

3 - Needs

The outbreak of Cholera highlights critical deficiencies in safe water supplies in Northeast Syria. Water treatment plants face occasional gaps in chlorine for water treatment, piping systems are in poor condition, water quality surveillance is not taking place, and private tankers are unregulated and deliver raw water directly from the source. All these elements have contributed to the outbreak, as well as to peaks of other water borne diseases that occur every year. The cause of the cholera outbreak is the use of infected drinking water from the Euphrates River, likely as the result of a lack of water treatment (chlorination), as reflected in the geographical distribution of the infections, outlined in the map below. It should also be noted that in Northeast Syria, all wastewater from communal sewage systems is released into the environment untreated. For communities located along the Euphrates riverbank, sewage is discharged directly into the river, which is the main water source for between 800,00 - 1,200,000 people. On September, WHO and EWARN confirmed the first cholera case in NWS in Jarablus. Where Mercy-USA is operating, there are no cholera confirmed cases yet. This concept note is a preparedness plan for intervention of cholera outbreak in NWS.

4 - Implementation of activities

The two selected IDP camps (500 individuals) will receive water tracking (Each person will receive 40 L/Day of safe water as per the latest WASH cluster recommendations).Mercy-USA will ensure the water quality by chlorinating the water at the source (WHO standards 0.2 - 0.5 mg/L ) , in addition to water quality testing in the main tank and at the household level.Also, desludging of the septic tanks in the targeted camps will done regularly and that will help maintain a healthy environment, especially in those crowded locations, this makes them safe from all water-borne diseases.Moreover, this project will cover the solid waste management activities on daily basis in 3 camps which includes distribution of 6 (100 L) solid waste container across targeted camps. The collected waste will be dumped in a formal dumping site in the targeted sub-district.Mercy-USA will contract with suppliers, one for providing clean chlorinated water to the targeted camps, one for desludging the septic tanks and one for collecting the waste.WASH and hygiene messages will be provided also to those camps.Mercy-USA will provide Cash based vouchers (CVA). $10 will be distributed to 100 HH for six months to receive hygiene items to maintain the level of personal hygiene among IDPs living in those camps. Hygiene kits contents were designed to meet the need of the IDPs and the WASH cluster standards.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

MUSA maintains its accountability toward the targeted community members to ensure that the provided services have no harmful effect on beneficiaries. Transparency is one of many essential elements that the MUSA aims towards, by providing communication mechanisms and enabling communities to play an active role in the decisions that will impact their lives by establishing clear guidelines and practices on participation and ensuring that the most marginalized and at-risk are represented and have influence. MUSA engages communities in the decision-making process by conducting focus groups discussions with beneficiaries during the early stages of the projects to actively seek the different points of view that communities have to offer in order to improve practices in programming, ensuring that feedback and complaints mechanisms are streamlined, and ensure that the communities are not negatively affected by the provided services. awareness raising is carried out during the implementation of each activity regarding the services provided, targeted locations by the project, and available communication channels, in addition to distributing banners, posters, and complaints within the targeted communities.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Mercy-USA maintains a ZERO TOLERANCE policy towards SEA in accordance with internationally-established protocols and standards for SEA protection.
