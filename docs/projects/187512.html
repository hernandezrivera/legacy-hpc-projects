Project Summary

This project is designed to build on gains achieved from providing improved integrated PHC services in supported vulnerable communities in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states. These states are share borders with Cameroon, Chad and Niger respectively and are the most affected in the 11-year armed conflict that has seen over 2.1 million persons internally displaced with over 1 million refugees in the neighboring countries. The majority of IDPs in Borno are in host communities in camp-like settlements and around 15% in IDP camps characterized by congestion and other specific challenges and vulnerabilities with an already suboptimal functioning health service, poor and inadequate shelter and poor hygiene conditions.
Escalating clashes between armed actors in this protracted complex crisis is a major driver of displacements towards garrison settlements especially in Borno state.
Intercommunal conflicts are also major cause of displacements especially in Adamawa state. The effects of these stressors are made manifest in the health indices (U5MR, NMR,MMR, SAM rates) and other socioeconomic indices (literacy rates, food security index, etc). The BAY states are also prone to natural disasters such as droughts and flooding and consequent environmental degradation coupled with poor water and waste management, the possibility of disease outbreaks such as Cholera is very high as experienced in 2021.
The BAY states suffer from a greatly debilitated health system which is currently being strengthened through close collaboration between humanitarian actors and government with significant gains over the past two years. It is therefore critical that the COVID-19 outbreak readiness and response strategies, plan and operations are strengthened and extended to all persons regardless of status and ensures inclusiveness as well as protecting and sustaining the progresses made in health systems strengthening (HSS)
through a multisectoral approach.
UNICEF in partnership with the state government agencies (SPHCDAs and SPHCMBs) and the State Ministries of Health of the BAY states will continue to provide integrated primary health care to locations with high and moderate humanitarian needs and continue to support HSS in the rest of the states. We will focus on how to reach people in need including returnees, support the states to implement interventions that will enable transition from emergency phase to development in some LGAs, partner with INGOs that are present on the ground and provide strategic technical assistance in building capacity for resilience at the community level. We will review existing supplies and focus on the critical component. We will leverage resources through convergence with other sections within UNICEF and other sectors at the state, LGAs and community levels.
The following objectives will be met through the project:
1. Support the provision of basic essential quality health care services to vulnerable populations.
2. Support the timely disease outbreak response through active participation in EOC activities.
3. Support improved access to health care strengthening health system recovery, enhance resilience, and promote humanitarian-development linkages.
4. Support mitigation of the risk and contain the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic and decrease morbidity and mortality in IDP camps and host communities.

1 - Needs

Now in its twelfth year, the armed conflict has significantly disrupted the health care services delivery system in the three states, and the existing health facilities are overwhelmed. The health care delivery system in Borno previously relied on a fragile network of LGA-level health facilities comprising of General hospitals, PHCs, health posts and dispensaries; but because of the conflict about 497 of the 755 health facilities (66%) were destroyed, looted or damaged (Health Resource Availability Mapping System (HeRAMS), February 2018. Nigeria has one of the largest stocks of human resources for health (HRH) in Africa but, like the other 57 HRH crisis countries, has densities of nurses, midwives and doctors that are still too low to effectively deliver essential health services(1.95 per 1,000) (WHO, 2020). The low densities are more pronounced in NE where most of the health workers have moved to safe havens in other parts of the country or state capitals or city centers, where there is relative safety or better security. This has resulted in health facilities having inadequate staff and this is further compounded by maldistribution of key technical staff resulting in an inappropriate mix of skill as well as insufficient supplies and equipment to meet the health needs of the population. In addition, the overcrowded living conditions in some IDP camps where there is limited access to safe drinking water and sanitation significantly increases the risks of preventable disease outbreaks. Before the conflict, the BAY states were characterized by extremely poor health indicators, which were significantly worse than the national-level indicators. While the national maternal mortality ratio (MMR) was 576 per 100,000 livebirths, in the 3 states MMR was as high as 1500 – 2000 per 100,000 live births. Under-five mortality (U5MR) was also above the national average of 126/1,000 live births with over160/1,000 in the 3 states (DHS 2013).These trends are reversing with improved high impact health interventions in the Northeastern states especially in Borno. U5MR in Borno stands at 86 per 1000 live births compared to a national 132 deaths per 1000 live births(NDHS 2018). Sustaining this achievement will require strengthening and scaling up on-going interventions especially maternal, newborn and child health services at PHC level and to also ensure an even distribution of trained health personnel. The results of this protracted conflict have led to increased displacement of populations and many trained health personnel fled their duty stations. This has left many of the IDPs without quality health services delivery. While humanitarian efforts have been deployed to ensure that challenges such as lack or damaged health facilities, insufficient supplies and equipment, inadequate and maldistribution of human resource for health are effectively tackled, overcrowded living conditions in IDP camps with limited access to safe drinking water and poor sanitation significantly increases the risks of preventable disease outbreaks like Cholera, Lassa fever etc. The HNO had predicted that 7.9 million people will be expected to need humanitarian assistance in 2021. COVID-19 pandemic continues to add an increased burden to the already fragile and overburdened healthcare system. Persistent challenges with the health system include the following: Poor health infrastructure, insufficient and inadequate HRH, low budgetary appropriation for health, weak medical supply chain, weak surveillance and disease outbreak response mechanisms and a weak referral system to and from secondary/tertiary health facilities.

2 - Activities

To adequately address the gaps mentioned above in locations with high humanitarian needs (locations categorized as category 4 severity in the HRP/HNO 2021 document),UNICEF, through its implementing partners will continue to ensure availability of essential drugs and supplies, improve on the existing logistics capacity to deliver essential commodities to end users at the last mile. Improve the technical capacity of skilled health workers and support their deployment to locations where they are most needed. UNICEF will do this by supporting training and provision of technical assistance in supported health facilities and IDP camps, undertake limited rehabilitation of damaged health facilities and construction of semi-permanent health facilities in worst affected LGAs according to the Government priority of one PHC per strategy. UNICEF shall in partnership with other health sector actors, participate in ensuring that a robust referral system is supported by improving pre-referral treatment in PHCs and the revitalization of ambulances, through providing financial support to state-owned ambulances for maintenance and fueling, to meet the demands of vulnerable populations, especially in IDP camps. UNICEF will also work closely with the health sector to ensure that adequate disease outbreak response activities are well coordinated. UNICEF will continue to provide support for data generation- through production of data tools, transmission- using ODK and third copy documents and analysis using the nationals standard platforms for Health Information management which serves to collate data for both routine services and surveillance activities- EWARS, SORMAS and DHIS2.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

UNICEF will align its support with government policies and partner with SPHCDAs and SMoHs to provide sustained, equitable access to essential, high-quality health services responsive to people’s needs without financial hardship, thereby protecting poor and underserved people from illness, death, and extreme poverty. UNICEF major focus of investment will be to align with government policy of one PHC per ward as contained in the State Strategic Health Development Plan 2018-2022, which basically is to strengthen them to be able to respond to the needs of the population.Through its health strategy, UNICEF support for health delivery is envisaged in a three-pronged approach:1)Emergency PHC services: this include; support to the six building blocks of PHC in highly prioritized most affected and in need Local Government Areas (LGA) in Borno and Yobe states with emergency PHC services2) Early recovery and system strengthening: this support is targeted at medium prioritized selected LGAs and the support includes all the above interventions except the support for human resource deployment at health facilities level – major area of UNICEF HSS support3) Strategic planning needs-based support: this support includes only the provision of technical guidance and supplies and logistics to SPHCDA to ensure continuity and service provision on a need based.UNICEF will invest in HSS to promote state ownership and sustainability, scale up solutions, and promote greater efficiencies in service delivery. Under this Vision, UNICEF’s strategic outcomes to achieve the overarching goal: financial protection, essential services, population coverage, community-based programming, decentralize health governance and responsiveness. To achieve these strategic outcomes, HSS activities will be aligned with the six core health systems functions: human resources for health; health finance; health governance; health information; medical products, vaccines, and technologies, service delivery and community-based programming. These functions – also referred to as the six building blocks – comprise an established framework for guiding HSS programming. Country realities have shown that health systems constraints can involve several interconnected functions, and this vision emphasizes our work to improve multiple functions at the same time while collaborating with other inter-governmental sectors relating to WaSH, Protection and Education.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The project will ensure that beneficiaries are not harmed and that it is designed to prevent/minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of accessing health interventions. In addition, WHO treatment standards will be followed to ensure interventions achieve the desired effect and do not cause any harm. There will be no discrimination and anyone within the targeted areas can access the services. Patients seeking treatment will be treated with respect, dignity and confidentiality shall always be kept.The impact of the project will be maximized through the incorporation of protection principles into the delivery of the project and ensure that activities target the most vulnerable, enhance safety, dignity, and promote and protect the rights of the beneficiaries.Health workers and communities sensitization on GBV and PSEA activities and feedbacks in all locations.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

A) Project design and planning phase:Religious and community leaders will be engaged for a meeting by the Government Authorities to be informed about the setting up of health facilities for their populations. They will be informed that all the services will be free of charge and encourage them to ensure their people utilize the services. B)Project implementation phaseDuring implementation, the community volunteer structure will be used to conduct community sensitization about early health seeking behaviors among the vulnerable communities. In addition, the religious leaders will be engaged to ensure that their communities have equitable access to health services as quick as possible. C) Project monitoring and evaluation:Religious and community leaders will take part in project monitoring, ensuring that the project benefits the target population. Monthly meetings will be conducted between these leaders and the health facilities team to discuss service utilization, attitudes of health worker toward the patients and other relevant aspects that needs to be addressed (e.g. environment of the health facilities, WASH facilities, etc.). The performance of the community volunteer work and the messages sent will be discussed as well. At all stages, any complaints from patients will be reported to the individual/s in-charge of the health facilities or the head/s of the community leaders who will have daily contact with the health team.The U-Report platform will encourage and ensure that beneficiaries of the UNICEF health interventions are provided with a feedback mechanism which is accessible to the community at all times (24 hours, 7 days a week).
