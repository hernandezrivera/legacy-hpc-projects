Project Summary

Severe flooding and drought have adversely impacted livelihoods and increased protection concerns, particularly for women and girls, limiting access to basic public services such as protection, education, health, shelter, water, sanitation, hygiene, and food. Children are often disproportionately affected during times of crisis, exposing them to grave violations such as family separation, sexual abuse, and recruitment , exploitation and neglect.
A total population of 2,123 HH (roughly 16,884 individuals) in Gokjok Pigi Canal, Jonglei State, is in dire need. In the IOM DTM-Melut population count exercise, 15,347 individuals (2,600 households) were reported to be residing at the site, with 56% being women and girls. Over 90% reported arriving between November 2022 and January 2023, with December 2022 accounting for 72.9% of reported arrivals. The OCHA Sudan crisis situational report for August 2023 reported that 243,940 persons (56,661 households) have been registered at border crossings into South Sudan since the fighting in Sudan erupted on April 15, 2023. About 97% of new arrivals enter through the Joda/Renk border. Approximately 3,000 and 4,000 returnees were stranded in Paloch in dire conditions, including separated and unaccompanied children. Most internally displaced persons (IDPs) reported fleeing from Fashoda County (50%) and Manyo County (46.9%), while over 500 IDPs reported being displaced from Melut, Panyikang, and Maban. They continue to live in deplorable conditions and urgently need onward transportation to their destinations.
War Child Holland is responding to the influx of returnees and refugees in South Sudan, with over 110,000 arriving since the conflict in April 2023. The Upper Nile region has received over 71,000 arrivals. To address these challenges, War Child Holland is implementing a project to address the needs of marginalized people through multi-pronged approaches to child protection and community-based mental health and psychosocial support (MPHSS) without compromising gender and disability mainstreaming services. The project will build relationships between War Child Holland, government ministries, humanitarian agencies, and related working groups. It will support at-risk or most vulnerable children through comprehensive case management activities and referrals. Advocacy and community dialogue will be used to address specific child protection and psychological issues among children at the community and national and state levels. The project will enhance community-based child protection, mental health, and psychosocial support mechanisms through peer-to-peer support groups, ongoing psychosocial support sessions, and psychoeducation on stress reduction and positive coping strategies and case management services.

1. Needs

War Child Holland prioritized the severely affected counties of Melut, Fashoda, Malakal, and Panyikang (Upper Nile State), Bor South, and Canal Pigi (Jonglei) for this project. The above locations are occupied by IDPs and returnees from Sudan with high life-threatening needs. For instance, there are currently 6,000 returnees living in Bulakat Reception Centre in Malakal town and surrounding areas/schools with limited access to basic needs, intensified congestion with limited access to privacy, and lifesaving services awaiting to be transported to respective states. Looking at protection cluster indicator disaggregation, about 4,168 unaccompanied or separated children need Family Tracing and Re-unification (FTR) services and alternative care support, and around 33,368 need case management, including cash support, in Upper Nile. Given the fact that issues of child recovery and integration into the community are common in Jonglei for CAAFAG, local structures need to be strengthened to provide quality alternative care support to abducted (CAAFAG) and unaccompanied and separated children (UASC). 78% of IDPS and returnees’ children are living in single-headed households with no source of income while experiencing insecurity, hence threatening the lives and movements of their children, e.g., to school and safe spaces. 70% of adolescent girls and women who are in the IDP camps and host communities fear going to the bush to collect firewood and materials needed to build shelter due to insecurity. Women and girls also fear being attacked while fetching water at the riverside, especially in the late evening. While on the move, IDPs reported cases of extortion, rape, and abduction by unidentified groups. Insecurity continues to threaten the lives of children and their families. It is reported that 42.3% of IDPs are being forcibly displaced, mainly due to insecurity, living shocks, and traumatic experiences in their minds. WCH will engage these individuals (children) in wellbeing, play, and learning activities via CFS. To address increasing child protection and humanitarian needs in Upper Nile and Jonglei, War Child Holland will provide child protection, psychosocial support, case management, and cash assistance to the IDPS, returnees, and host communities. War Child will use evidence-based, informed, community-led approaches and multi-level strategies to respond to the needs, e.g., Team UP, PM+, parenting without violence, children's wellbeing sessions, advocacy, and community dialogue. Adolescent girls and boys will be reached with protection and prevention strategies to reduce issues of sexual exploitation and abuse. 80% of returnees and IDP children, including those with disabilities living in the most vulnerable families and at risk of further abuse, will be supported with cash initiatives to cover their basic needs and generate livelihood incomes. 60% of adolescent girls and boys at risk of sexual abuse should be engaged in life skills and PSS interventions, including referral pathways and reporting. The project will increase awareness among parents, teachers, and children about the importance of education while increasing support for children by providing scholastic materials to increase enrollment in schools.

2. Project activities

• Strengthen the capacity of CBCPNs in target counties through training and follow up • Support life skills and PSS sessions for children and young people through CFS, schools and and community spaces.•Provide case management support to children and caregivers with heightened CP/MHPSS needs and concerns. The CP/PSS officers, case supervisors, and case workers will be responsible for identifying and assessing individuals with heightened child protection needs or risk in the IDP camps, returnees, and host communities. After verifying the concerns, the case worker will then develop and implement an individualized case plan with the beneficiary. Information on each beneficiary receiving case management services, including referrals, will be updated daily in the project's case management forms (CPISM+) to ensure timely follow-up on all cases while safeguarding the beneficiary's information through strict adherence to War Child Holland data protection protocols.• Provide family tracing and re-unification services to unaccompanied and separated children. Community-based child protection networks will be trained to support the identification of UASC. WCH will work with other agencies, such as government institutions, SCI, UNICEF, IRC, and other CP agencies, to coordinate the tracing and reunification of UASC while observing case management principles and guidelines. UASC will be provided with integration packages and intensive follow-up until the child is fully integrated with no other threats emerging.• Facilitate parenting skill sessions for foster parents and caregivers of vulnerable children• Conduct service mapping to update referral pathways to facilitate individual referrals of children and caregivers at risk. • Facilitate identification and referral of at-risk IDPs, returnees and new arrivals, children, parents, and caregivers, including persons with specific needs, to access different services.• Conduct awareness campaigns. The project will use radio talk shows, jingle messages, outreach programs, and postures during big event days such as the DAC, WMHD, WID, and DAD, among others, to pass on key messages about the situation of children and call for action.• Strengthen the capacity of frontline staff, government, volunteers, and local structures to withstand impacted shocks and provide safe and protective services. Training of war child staff, volunteers, duty bearers, local structures, and service providers on child protection/MHPSS and case management, including disability inclusion components.• Train duty bearers on social norm change approaches and identification and referral . Monitor and report on activity progress
