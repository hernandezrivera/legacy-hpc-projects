Project Summary


This project will support the monitoring of population movements across Non-Government-controlled areas in northwest Syria to make available reliable and verified data and information for the humanitarian community, in order to address new or changing demographics and needs in an efficient and coordinated manner. An online monitoring dashboard will provide humanitarian actors with the latest information per community level, regular/monthly overviews and trend analysis will be shared with Cluster members, and ad-hoc emergency updates will be produced and shared when required. Technical guidance will be provided to Cluster members to support with the collective gathering of data.

4 - Needs

Due to the lack of access to areas in northern Syria and the remote management of the cross-border operation, obtaining comprehensive and accurate data on populations and displacements is a challenge for humanitarian actors. In 2019, CCCM cluster’s displacement tracking activities concentrated on the north-west of the country, which witnessed nearly one million population movements between January-October 2019, with many of these movements indicating the presence of IDPs that had been displaced multiple times. On average, the cluster tracked over 100,000 population movements on a monthly basis, an increase from 96,000 movements per month reported in the last HNO. The main causes of these displacements in the north-west were renewed hostilities in Idleb, Aleppo and Hama governorates (particularly in May and December 2019). The CCCM Cluster has developed an efficient inter-agency-based system to collect data in a coordinated manner, triangulate, and when possible, verify the data. UNHCR partner ACTED/REACH will use Rapid Displacement Monitoring (RDM) system to collect near real time snapshot about current location displaced people in order to produce categorized (age, gender, vulnerability and shelter type) number of IDPs within 24 hrs and identify their top three needs on community levels. In addition, the Cluster also relied on specialized organizations to provide data and information on population movements to ensure sufficient geographical coverage and technical capacity to collect and disseminate this information in a coherent and rapid manner for the humanitarian response. Through its partner ACTED/REACH, UNHCR aims to continue providing such dedicated support to the CCCM Cluster under the IDP Situation Monitoring Initiative (ISMI).

5 - Implementation of activities

Objective 4: disseminating operational information on movements of IDPs on a timely basisOutput 4.1: track and share IDPs movements and analyses displacements trendsOutput indicator 4.1.1: # of updates provided to CCCM Cluster members, Clusters, and other operational actors on sudden displacements (target: 24) The main objective of this project is to obtain and disseminate timely, accurate and comprehensive data on displacements and population movements in northern Syria, support CCCM members’ capacity to collect quality data, and support the CCCM Cluster in providing quality reports and analyses to humanitarian actors. Building upon the existing system and strengths of the CCCM’s population movement monitoring in northern Syria, UNHCR, as the Cluster lead agency, will continue to contribute to the technical expertise, geographical coverage, and frequency in this monitoring exercise. The scope of data collection, using a ‘baseline’ is to compare tracking efforts and enabling more frequent, consistent and uniform approaches to population movement monitoring across northern Syria. The modalities for presentation and dissemination of information includes maps, factsheets, reports, trend analyses, and rapid assessment profiles. These are adapted to the type of assessments, e.g. baseline situation overview, monthly overviews, ad-hoc updates, incident reports, snapshot, thematic assessments and trends analysis. Reports analyse a number of topics including displacement patterns, trends, intentions of movement and triggers, in addition to numbers and locations of IDPs. During periods of rapid displacement, regular updates on population movements are provided every two to four days, presented in the form of rapid assessment profiles. This information will support humanitarian actors in their programming and (geographical) priority setting. Overall, this intervention furthermore will contribute to empowering the local NGOs and local actors’ capacity.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Do-No-Harm is an integral principle of the humanitarian response in (northwest) Syria. UNHCR, as a lead agency of the CCCM Cluster, aimes to strike the fine balance between ensuring that dignified life-saving assistance is provided to IDPs in sites while also making sure that these sites remain a last resort option and do not contribute to any push/pull factor or create aid dependency. Any assistance provided to IDPs in sites is upon consultation and agreement with the CCCM Cluster. There are some specific risks related to the implementation of this project, namely: - Risks of physical and sexual exploitation and abuse. UNHCR ensures that partners have a code of conduct (CoC) in place, supports developing/reviewing PSEA prevention materials, organizes internal and external CoC/PSEA sessions, and follows-up on PSEA on a regular basis. PSEA focal points are identified in the operation whom individuals can contact and/or report misconduct by humanitarian staff.- Risk that displaced people seek refuge in areas where there is no monitoring and/or assistance being provided. UNHCR and its partner REACH established almost complete geographical coverage on population movement monitoring in Non-Government-controlled areas in northwest Syria.- Risk of monitoring/assessment fatigue among beneficiaries and Cluster members. The purposes of monitoring and assessments are clarified to beneficiaries without raising expectations. The tools and questionnaires are developed to be as light as possible.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

To address a variety of risks which might affect the implementation of activities, UNHCR and its partners have several procedures and tools in place to identify and mitigate risks (e.g. corporate risk register, risk-based monitoring and control plans, risk assessment and management plan) as well as procedures in to guide their programme, finance, procurement, logistics, and human resource management, and conduct third-party monitoring and audits to confirm assistance and services have reached beneficiaries without major discrepancies.Firstly, the security and safety of humanitarian staff is at risk, and partners are supported to develop and implement Safety & Security and Duty of Care policies. Partners can suspend activities when this is required by the local conditions and should take preventive measures to ensure the safety of their staff.Secondly, access to beneficiaries is regularly disrupted due to developments beyond the control of the humanitarian community. UNHCR works with local NGOs who have presence on the ground and are aware of the local context. Sharing information on incidents is done at the inter-agency level to promote collective action and messaging.More specific examples of risks and mitigating measures are:- Risk of irregular procurement processes. Mitigation measures: bulk procurement of core-relief/non-food items is done in Turkey or internationally and goods are transported to northern Syria.- Risk of aid diversion. Mitigation measures: partners are supported with capacity-building initiatives, closely monitored and verification is implemented through independent third party monitoring arrangment.- Risk of interference in humanitarian assistance by armed groups. Mitigation measures: open communication lines and relationship are maintained with partners on local developments and specific incidents, and the inter-agency coordination is informed about (major) incidents to ensure unified and effective messaging to AOGs.
