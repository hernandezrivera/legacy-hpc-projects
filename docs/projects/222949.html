Project Summary

As of 8 November 2024, South Sudan is experiencing unprecedented floods, affecting over 1.4 million people nationwide, with Jonglei, Western Bahr el Ghazal, and Unity states being the hardest hit. Damaged and impassable roads have hindered access to affected communities. The crisis spans 43 counties and the Abyei Administrative Area, with Jonglei and Northern Bahr el Ghazal states comprising over 51% of the affected population. More than 379,000 individuals are displaced, and large areas of the country have been submerged year-round, with sudden floods impacting new areas. The heavy rains have rendered 15 major supply routes impassable in the greater Upper Nile and Bahr el Ghazal regions, forcing humanitarian actors to rely on costly and unreliable air and river transport.
The severe flooding, coupled with recurrent violence, weak governance, persistent poverty, and a lack of basic infrastructure and services, has created a complex humanitarian crisis that hinders the nation's progress. South Sudan’s health system, heavily reliant on international aid, is overwhelmed by staffing and resource shortages. Vulnerable groups—including women, children, the elderly, and persons with disabilities—face limited access to healthcare, heightened risks of illness, and increased mortality rates. Food insecurity, disease prevalence, and poor hygiene have driven acute malnutrition, and the situation is expected to worsen due to continued flooding, conflict, refugee influxes, and deteriorating economic conditions.
In Aweil Centre and Aweil Town Municipality, the flooding that began in August 2024 has severely impacted over 16,070 households (96,420 individuals). Of these, 50% face severe disruption, 30% moderate impacts, and 20% minimal but significant effects. Lowland areas are particularly affected, with 7,104 feddans of farmland submerged, 5,413 livestock displaced, and 691 homes destroyed. This has devastated livelihoods, exacerbating the crisis for affected households.
The floods have disrupted hygiene practices, resulting in widespread open defecation and escalating public health risks. Communities are increasingly vulnerable to diseases such as malaria, pneumonia, diarrhea, cholera, skin rashes, and bilharzia. Snake bites have become a critical issue, with 63 cases reported, including four fatalities, one being an 8-year-old child. The absence of antivenom in health facilities leaves those affected without essential care.
Health services are severely compromised, with seven facilities rendered inaccessible. Many villages lack basic health infrastructure, and the nearest operational facilities are 25–30 kilometers away, forcing residents to undertake arduous journeys. These facilities are critically understaffed and lack essential supplies, leaving them unable to meet rising demands.
Female-headed households face disproportionate challenges due to limited livelihood opportunities and systemic exclusion from traditional male-dominated courts, where their concerns remain unaddressed. Vulnerable groups, including the elderly and children, are at heightened risk of respiratory illnesses from cold exposure. Women lack access to maternity care, leading to reported cases of newborn deaths. Additionally, the absence of Expanded Program on Immunization (EPI) services has left many unprotected against preventable diseases.
Action Against Hunger (ACF) and the International Rescue Committee (IRC) are providing limited health services in select areas, including Akoon and Maulaidit, which support Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and flood-affected populations. IRC also supplies medical provisions to targeted Primary Health Care Centers (PHCCs) and Units (PHCUs). However, funding constraints limit the reach and impact of these efforts.

1. Needs

The recent floods in Aweil Centre and Aweil South Counties, have severely disrupted health services and exacerbated public health challenges, leaving vulnerable populations without access to essential care. A multi-agency conducted an initial assessment in Aweil Center County between 17Th to 24Th August 2024, and reported Aweil Centre and Aweil town municipality, as the most affected areas with the impact of the floods rated as “catastrophic”. Over 16,070 households (96,420 individuals) were affected. 50% of the population were facing severe disruption, another 30% experiencing moderate impacts, and the remaining 20% suffering minimal but still significant consequences. The floods, which began in August 2024, proved particularly destructive in the lowland areas, where 7,104 feddans of farmland were submerged, 5,413 livestock displaced, and 691 homes reduced to rubbles. The extensive damage to crops and livestock threatens the primary sources of income and sustenance for the affected households, deepening the crisis. 15 snakebite incidents with no fatalities reported. 326 homes were damaged, forcing household members to sleep in single tukuls, increasing the risk of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) for women and girls. 2,026 learners (1,155 boys and 871 girls) from 32 schools were also affected by the flooding either due to inaccessibility of the schools or displacement of their families forcing them to temporarily dropout from the school. Seven health facilities are either inaccessible or dilapidated, lacking basic infrastructure, equipment, and supplies. Health workers face critical shortages of personal protective equipment (PPE), laboratory reagents, and patient monitoring tools, impairing their capacity to manage disease outbreaks and provide life-saving care. Inadequate hygiene and sanitation facilities at health centers increase the risk of waterborne diseases, while the absence of safe drinking water further compounds public health risks. Maternal and child health services have been critically undermined due to inaccessibility, lack of skilled staff trained in Basic Emergency Obstetric Care (BEmOC) and limited access to immunization programs. Female survivors of gender-based violence (GBV) remain underserved due to insufficient clinical management expertise and inadequate awareness campaigns. Additionally, health workers require updated training to address prevalent diseases and malnutrition effectively. The situation is further worsened by logistical constraints, with poorly maintained ambulances and vehicles hindering patient referrals and outreach efforts. Mental health and psychosocial support services remain scarce, leaving flood-affected communities with unaddressed trauma and stress. The lack of functional health facility committees also hampers community engagement in health governance and recovery initiatives. Comprehensive interventions are urgently needed to rehabilitate and equip health facilities, enhance hygiene and sanitation, strengthen health worker capacities, improve maternal and child health services, and address the psychosocial needs of affected populations. These efforts are essential to restore health services, build resilience, and ensure equitable access to care for the flood-affected communities in Aweil Centre.In addition, the vulnerability pf these populations demand for protection measures in order to remain safe and regain their dignity. These services should include child protection and GBV case management services to affected population, awareness raising on gender equality and GBV awareness, provision of life-saving information to the community including psychosocial support services (PSS) to distressed children and women and girls, establishment of community protection networks and referral pathways mechanisms. Our SEA team leader will train staff and help improve mainstreaming of protection in our project and ensure that these activities are reported.
