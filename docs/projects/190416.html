Project Summary

The core idea motivating this project has been drawn from the fact that millions of children in rural and urban areas of Tigrai are still deprived of their basic rights to quality education for over two years because of COVID 19, swarms of desert locust in an unprecedented number, and the ominous war the central government waged over Tigrai and its people. The latter has seriously disrupted the entire lives of the over eight million people of Tigrai. Despite the remarkable progress in the past decades, Tigrai has now fallen under siege almost the whole society is found in between life and death, looking for ultra-urgent food assistance.
Due to absence of education, the future of many poor and disadvantaged children remains undecided. The issue of acquiring quality education in rural and urban areas has been compounded over the last two years not only with manifold problems like quality teachers, lack of materials, hard communication, parents’ unawareness, etc. but also with nutritional deficiency of the children. Families are unable to afford to send their children to school, or their children may go to school without food, especially morning meal that affects their concentration to and comprehension of lessons at school
Hence, school feeding in Tigrai is a critical and timely intervention because of the existing serious hiccups of food insecurity and worst livelihood situation of the region, aggravated by the devastating war. Primary schools in Tigrai are open according to the regional government’s recent official announcement thereof in which the government calls on the international community and stakeholders for their organized but urgent supports of various kinds. However, the existing dire situation doesn’t allow parents to feed and send off their children to school. If children have to get back to school, they need to get nutritious food to gain focus on their education. Thus, the launching of school feeding program is of paramount importance for the over 1 million children to get access to general education through establishing child health and nutrition programs predominantly feeding in food insecure contexts for children from the impoverished and war-battered areas.
Therefore, is a project initiative by TDA to co-work with your good organization that aims to improve food security of the poor school children with a general objective of improving educational performance of students by increasing children enrolment and retention (i.e. attendance/completion) and improve children’s nutritional status through school feeding program. The project has also specific objectives of, increasing school enrolment, attendance and retention rate of the school aged children in the project areas, improve academic performance of students by addressing short-term hanger and improve micronutrient status, reduce student dropouts and save children from involving in child labour and other harmful activities, prevent the students and their families from migrating in search of food and seasonal employment and enhance awareness among school authorities, local communities and children of the importance of improved diets on learning and education.
The project has planned to serve food that include, but not limited to, oat, rice, bread, biscuit, tea, and other nutritional food in the morning as a breakfast meal that is usually considered to positively affect their concentration to and comprehension of lessons at school.
This project is believed to enhance the nutritional status of primary school students, their enrollment and continuous attendance/completion and thereby ultimately achieve excellent educational performance. 163 districts are the target area of this project. Total direct beneficiary students will be 65,263 and indirect beneficiaries will be 133,189.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

The project will be implemented by TDA’s project coordination team together with relevant stakeholders at regional, woreda, kebele, school and community level. Regional stakeholders include Bureau of Education, Bureau of Health, Bureau of Water Resources, Bureau of Agriculture and Rural Development, Bureau of Labour and Social Affairs, REST and Bureau of Finance and Economic Development and their structures at woreda level. At woreda and Tabia level the Woreda and Tabia Administration will be highly involved. At school and community level school administration, teachers’ representatives, students’ representatives and community representatives will be involved. To administer the school feeding program transparent institutional administration as well as procurement arrangements will be established. The administration in Tigrai is highly decentralized from region to Woredas then to Tabias. Hence the management of school feeding program will be decentralized likewise in which the woreda administration together with relevant stakeholders like the woreda office of education, woreda health office and community, students’ as well as teachers’ representatives from the beneficiary schools will play a pivotal role in the administration and management of the project. The level of decentralized administration is believed to help use the capacity of the government at different levels to manage the program using existing staff, infrastructure, and accountability systems. This will also help to easily mobilize different types of resources available from each locality (cash or in-kind) and identify key implementing partners, especially those who can help in the school feeding program. With the full and active involvement of relevant stakeholders, this school feeding project is designed to address issues and challenges like environmental concerns related to cooking and fuel utilization, corrupt practices in procurement and contracting and waste management and disposal. In this regard, based on the potential of each locality economically sound and environment friendly cooking energy source will be sought for each school. Procurement modality to secure food and non-food items for the school feeding program will be through relevant institutions at woreda level. Hence rules of regulation of procurement of the regional government will apply to the school feeding program. TDA believes that local procurement is a means to achieve sustainable school feeding programs and, at the same time, to use the marketing activities as a stimulus for the local agricultural economy. As such, local purchase of food for school feeding is seen as a force multiplier, benefiting children and the local economy at the same time. To keep the environment of the school and surrounding community from pollution proper waste management and disposal system will applied in consultation with the respective woreda health offices.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

The school communities will be involved in the management of the project. They will have a voice and opportunity to influence relevant decisions affecting the implementation of the project. It involves giving the school communities the power to influence on their priorities, and needs. For this purpose TDA will share information timely on the project to the school communities and stakeholders and take participation as tool to encourage discussion and decision making. TDA will seek and collect feedback from the beneficiaries of the SFP and other stakeholders in which any complaints are properly handled and addressed by setting clear, transparent procedures.Any complaints and feedbacks will entertained through setting proper mechanisms that are effective, accessible and confidential to all ages and gender. It is therefore important for TDA to establish robust feedback mechanism and follow appropriate procedures in addressing them. Hence, TDA together with relevant stakeholders will set feedback desks in each school where any grievances are submitted and followed for timely and effective response and correction.
