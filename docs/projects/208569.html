Project Summary

Nkafamiya Rescue Mission will enhance the protection of 20,000 GBV survivors, particularly children, through strengthening the delivery of and access to quality GBV preventive and response services. This holistic protection package will cover community awareness, MHPSS, GBV case management, and livelihood support. The project will focus on child and adolescent survivors (60% girls) and aims to provide a package of support activities to beneficiaries that facilitates psychosocial recovery and reintegration.
In addition to direct service provision, the support package will be localized with capacity building provided for community structures to replicate GBV prevention and response services. It will also implement awareness activities by engaging community stakeholder groups, building knowledge of GBV risks and prevention-response strategies at the local level.
Within the scope of this project, the following LGAs have been identified for intervention:
Adamawa LGAs: Fufore, Guyuk, Shelleng, Demsa
Borno LGAs: MMC, Biu, Ngala, Damboa, Shani
Yobe LGAs: Gujba, Gulani

1 - Needs

The ongoing crisis in northeast Nigeria is primarily a protection crisis, which has exposed civilians to violence, exploitation and abuse, including gender-based violence. Gender-based violence is widespread, and has been aggravated by displacement, limited access to services, disruption of livelihoods as well as harmful socio-cultural practices. The 2021 Nigeria HRP identifies 1.5 million people in need of GBV assistance in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States with the 81% being women (24%) and girls (57%). In the northeast, female-headed households and adolescent girls are at heightened vulnerability to sexual exploitation and abuse – for instance survival or transactional sex, early marriage and childbirth, and forced prostitution. Women and girls have also been abducted and forcibly recruited by armed groups and subjected to forced marriages and rape. Women and children formerly associated with armed groups are furthermore exposed to social stigmatisation upon leaving armed groups, which further heightens vulnerability to GBV concerns as these individuals struggle to rebuild livelihoods and reintegrate into the community. Unaccompanied and street-connected children (UASC), who often are without social protection mechanisms, are also at heightened risk. Despite the particular vulnerability of women and girls, it is important to note that all population groups - girls, boys, women and men from host community, IDP and returnee communities are at-risk and in need of support. All forms of GBV, experienced by all types of survivors, are chronically under-reported due to stigmatisation and marginalisation as well as the lack of effective reporting and response mechanisms. Hence the importance of awareness creation and community engagement that encourages prevention and access for support for victims of GBV. There are significant gaps in the humanitarian response to GBV. There is a general lack of GBV services available, and where services are available the quality (including making of timely referrals and follow-up of referrals made) is often low.An estimated 1.3 million individuals need GBV prevention and response services across the BAY states in 2022 (of whom 46% are IDPs, 23% returnees, and 31% host communities; 82% are women or girls). The majority (63%) of those in need are in Borno State, 22% in Adamawa and 15% in Yobe. The GBV sub-sector uses the Protection Sector severity rankings and the severity analysis for the GBV Area of Responsibility (AoR) to calculate the number of people targeted in each state and LGA. Based on these analyses, the GBV sub-sector and its partners plan to target 1,030,000 people (46% IDPs, 23% returnees, 31% host communities; overall 82% are women or girls) with GBV prevention and response interventions in 51 LGAs (25 in Borno, 15 in Yobe and 12 in Adamawa) with the severest needs, as informed by the JIAF and other analyses from the GBV Information Management System. The majority (63%) of those to be targeted are in Borno State, 22% are in Adamawa and 15% in Yobe. As highlighted by the GBV Information Management System data, GBV continues to be a major protection concern for women and girls in north-east Nigeria and thus the reason why they remain the main target. Men and boys and other minority groups will also be targeted.

2 - Activities

15,000 survivors of GBV will also be provided with case management, including a tailored care plan and regular follow up. This will include mental health and psychosocial support by trained psychologists, and referral for other specialised services (such as medical and justice services) where appropriate. Survivors will also be supported with livelihoods support through a tailored package of vocational and business skills training, mentoring and cash grants to enable the survivors to access sustainable income-generating activities and promote their socio-economic reintegration. At the community level, Nkafamiya Rescue Mission will also build community-based protection and response mechanisms. This will include awareness raising activities and training of community-based committees on GBV prevention and response principles. Community-based GBV monitoring and support groups will be established as forums for peer support and mentoring, empowering community members to facilitate awareness-raising, prevention and response activities. These will also include establishing men's groups against GBV in order to engage men in combatting GBV and violence against women and challenging harmful gender attitudes.

The prioritisation of protection, safety, dignity, accountability and minimising unintended negative impacts ensure that the communities where Nkafamiya Rescue Mission works can access meaningful, dignified support. Nkafamiya Rescue Mission is committed to centring and mainstreaming protection throughout its activities and projects, and has pre-emptively identified potential protection risks within the project as well as taking active steps to integrate mitigation and management strategies. Protection reporting, response and prevention mechanisms are integrated into the design, and protection mainstreaming indicators are included within the monitoring and evaluation framework
