Project Summary

In the context of deplorable humanitarian situation in Somalia, AVORD is submitting this response plan to improve the living conditions of 17,000 most vulnerable IDPs and host communities in different regions of Somalia. The plan involves a number of WASH activities including construction and rehabilitation of water infrastructure including a borehole and shallow wells, provision of water to communities in humanitarian crisis through vouchers, and construction of sanitation facilities where demonstrated needs exist. WASH committees will be established and trained on a range of topics including community mobilization and sensitization on safe sanitation and hygiene practices. AVORD will also undertake 10 integrated hygiene promotion campaigns involving door-to-door sensitization, installation of posters and distribution of hygiene promotion leaflets. The campaigns will focus, among other themes, disaster risk reduction, environmental sanitation, proper hand-washing with soap and menstrual hygiene and will be conducted following distribution of WASH kits containing basic hygiene items such as female sanitary towels and soap. The process will be further complemented by emergency environmental cleaning and management through drainage and/or removal of stagnant water ponds and garbage piles. Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), environment, gender and protection issues will be mainstreamed throughout the project cycle. On DRR, WASH facilities will be designed and constructed while considering existing disaster risk and vulnerabilities e.g. building of flood proof latrines, enhancing communities' disaster risk awareness and mitigation as part of hygiene promotion campaigns. Strategies that AVORD will adapt to promote protection levels for target beneficiaries include establishing of WASH facilities in central locations that are not more than 500 meters from settlements and provision of adequate lighting, undertaking distributions in safe and accessible locations and in daylight hours, ensuring that the hygiene kit size is appropriate for the beneficiaries to be able to carry home with no additional physical or financial burden and without being dependent on others to carry it for them and building WASH facilities in a way that can preserve the safety and dignity of users, among other strategies. AVORD will also work in coordination with protection actors to put in place measures to prevent and respond to physical/sexual violence arising from use of or lack of WASH facilities. The design of activities will always be informed by context-specific need and protection analysis and will be completed in close coordination with target communities to promote ownership and ensure sustainability. The effectiveness and quality of the aid package will always be evaluated through monitoring exercises, field visits, and other evaluation methods thus ensuring that beneficiaries access their services safely and in a dignified manner and that anticipated outputs are realized. Elaborate feedback and complain mechanisms will be setup to ensure accountability to the effected population.

Project Activities

Repetitive natural disasters and conflict continue to sustain humanitarian crisis in Somalia. In addition to vulnerable host communities, the country is home to one of the largest Internally Displaced People (IDPs) in the world. According to OCHA HNO, there was an estimated 2.6 million IDPs in Somalia by end of 2018. Thenumber continues to grow due to conflict, forced evictions and natural disasters. Data from JMCNA that was carried out in 48 districts indicates that 47% of assessed households have insufficient water for household purposes, as measured against the SPHERE standards of 15 litres per person per day; 41% rely on unprotected water sources that are more susceptible to contamination which can increase the risk of transmission of waterborne diseases. Only 13% of assessed households across Somalia reported that they treat their drinking water and over 52% have indicated that they wash their hands with water only. Open defecation is practiced by as many as 39% of the population due to lack of access to sanitation facilities and awareness. This is one of key factors driving cholera outbreaks. In this context, AVORD seeks to improve the living conditions of 17,000 vulnerable DPs and host communities in Somalia (2,830 households) by undertaking the following activities: 1) Enhance access to water and promote resilience among affected population by constructing 1 borehole, 10 shallow wells while rehabilitating and protecting 50 additional shallow wells and 5 boreholes, with full compliance to WASH Cluster guidelines. The process will engage target communities in cash for work schemes to have the ripple effect of enhancing food security among the target populations. Construction/rehabilitation of water infrastructure will be complemented with the establishment of small scale water supply systems including pumping, powering (solar/hybrid), elevated water tanks and distribution systems. 2) Emergency water supply for 500 IDP and host community households in humanitarian crisis. Where market conditions allow, AVORD will employ a cash-based approach enabling beneficiaries to use vouchers to source water from local vendors. Each household will receive 15 liters of water per day for survival during emergencies. Water containers will also be provided to each household for appropriate storage and transport as needed. Household water treatment will also be conducted.3) Cleaning campaigns involving drainage and/or removal of stagnant water ponds and garbage piles. In tandem with these campaigns, AVORD will provide hygiene kits and solid waste management tools to maximize impact and efficiency of the response.4) Construction of 1,000 dislodgeable latrines with handwashing facilities. Women and girls will be involved in the technical design of latrines to address their unique needs for privacy and protection. Separate latrine blocks will be constructed for men & women with entrance on opposite sides and clearly marked signs in the Somali language. Consultations will be made with key local stakeholders to ensure that latrines are built in sites where eviction is unlikely. Latrines will be lockable from the inside with adequate lighting to further enhance protection levels for IDP woman and girls. 5) AVORD will also undertake 10 integrated hygiene promotion campaigns involving door to-door sensitization, installation of posters and distribution of hygiene promotion leaflets. The campaigns will focus, among other themes, disaster risk reduction, environmental sanitation, proper hand-washing with soap and menstrual hygiene and will be conducted following distribution of WASH kits containing basic hygiene items 6) Establish and train 10 water management committees in sites where WASH infrastructure was established.

Mitigation Measures

Mitigation measures to exclusion and marginalization based on status (2a): 1) Undertake direct identification and registration of beneficiaries without use of middle men or gate keepers. 2) Develop and enforce an elaborate selection criteria with priority accorded to most vulnerable households including those from marginalized and minority groups. 3) Adapt the highest levels of transparency in beneficiary selection and provide target communities with with timely information on the selection criteria, organisational procedures and processes that affect them. 4) Establish Joint project implementation committees involving different fabrics of target communitiesthus creating a platform where marginalized communities can share their grievances, if any. 5) Operationalize a project-level hotline where target beneficiaries can share views and/or concerns. Mitigation measures for exclusion and marginalization based on gender and age: 1) Collect and use data dis-aggregated by sex, age and diversity to inform beneficiary selection and maintain gender parity. 2) Ensure that activities are designed in a manner that address the unique needs of women, men, boys, girls and those with special needs. 3) Establish Joint project implementation committees involving youth and women groups from target communities thus creating a platform where youth and women can share their grievances, if any. 4) Undertake direct identification and registration of beneficiaries without use of middle men or gatekeepers to ensure inclusivity regardless of age and gender. 5) Develop and enforce an elaborate selection criteria with priority accorded to most vulnerable groups such as women, boys and girls. Mitigation measures for exposure of beneficiaries to violence:1) Continuously assess local dynamics and make plans accordingly. 2) Ensure that access routes to distribution points and services are safe with enough space fororderly and safe distribution. 3) Undertake program activities during daylight hours, ensuring ample time for beneficiaries to go back home before nightfall. 4) Adapt and observe the Do-No-Harm principles at all times to prevent unintended negativeconsequences. 5) Provide maximum protection to beneficiary data and household information to prevent misuse and exposure of target persons to risk.

Please provide further explanation

This proposal is humanitarian in nature focusing on provision of life-saving WASH assistance to IDPs and host communities in humanitarian crisis. However, it also contributes to building the resilience of targeted communities by rehabilitating and constructing water supply systems including shallow wells and boreholes in strategic drought affected locations and/or cholera hotspots which contribute to overall improvement in living conditions and accessing livelihoods hence leaving beneficiaries more resilient to future shocks. Sustainable sanitation facilities will promote reintegration of protracted IDPs. To maximize impact and sustain project outcome, AVORD will seek integration with other clusters providing response in areas where proposed activities will be implemented. Details pertaining to project activities will be shared through relevant clusters and 4W reporting matrix to avoid duplication of effort. Local communities will play a key role in different phases of the project to create sense of ownership which will contribute to sustaining of project outcomes. AVORD will strive to build local capacities thus ensuring that various community leaders and committees are able to sustainably maintain resources following project completion and handover. Disaster risk reduction and environmental sanitation will be mainstreamed in project cycle to ensure that proposedinterventions build communities' resilience and coping mechanisms to future shocks. The Do-No-Harm principles will be adapted through continuous contextual analysis and exploring how various activities interact with target communities to avoid unintended negative consequences.
