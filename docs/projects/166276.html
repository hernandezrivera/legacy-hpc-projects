Project Summary

In response to the COVID-19 pandemic, DRC proposes a 9-month WASH project with the aim of reducing the spread of the virus in the targeted camps. The proposed activities are aligned with the WASH sector and JRP and will complement existing DRC programming in Site Management and Development, Livelihoods and Protection in camps 8E and 8W. Up to 65,819 individuals residing in the mentioned camps will be targeted by the project.
50 portable hand-washing stations with soap will be installed in public spaces such as entrance into markets, mosques etc. to in order to promote hand and social hygiene which are key elements in the prevention of the spread of the virus. The stations will be connected to existing water supply lines and where there are no water lines, DRC will install water tanks which will be filled on a regular basis to ensure the stations have adequate water.
Sewage/drainage systems will be cleared to prevent accumulation of waste which could aid as transmission route. DRC will clean 5,000 square meters of drainage in the two camps to ensure liquid waste flow is not interrupted and the waste drains into the intended locations in the camp where it can be better managed. Latrines will be rehabilitated and disinfected, in addition ramps will be constructed in order to enable People with Disabilities, and children and older persons have access to these facilities.
Lastly, DRC teams will distribute 1,000 hygiene kits and carry out hygiene promotion. In order to reinforce positive behavior, hygiene promotion plays a big role and DRC will use this opportunity to disseminate behavior change communication on COVID-19 transmission and also encourage the targeted population to use the designated WASH facilities.
Through implementation of the proposed activities, DRC will contribute to the reduction in the spread of COVID-19 virus as well as facilitate adequate access to WASH services to all camp residents.

Needs

Since the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic and the reporting of the first case in Bangladesh, 80,971 people (DGHS) are in home and institutional quarantine, with 29,749 being in Dhaka while 13,364 are in other districts. The virus has spread to 60 out of 65 districts with only 1 testing facility being available in Cox’s Bazar. According to the update NGO platform published on 26th April 2020, 5 cases have been reported in Cox’s Bazar with 67 and 722 people currently in institutional and home quarantines respectively. No cases have been confirmed in the Rohingya camps yet with 451 persons (WHO week 16 Epidemiological highlights) being in institutional quarantines. In the camps, where currently over 855,000 Rohingya’s (UNHCR camp population data) reside in 34 overcrowded camps, the risk of the virus spreading is high and could overwhelm the existing systems if proper measures are not put in place early. Despite the government’s efforts to minimize the spread by introducing preventive measures such as limiting access to the camps by only allowing essential activities to be carried out; data and trends from other locations with confirmed cases indicate that this measures are not adequate and should be complemented with other WHO recommended measures to increase the chances of controlling the spread of the virus successfully. As reported in the WASH COVID-19 response plan (ISCG) the virus is mostly spread through respiratory droplets and direct contact. Evidence-based research indicates that provision of safe water, sanitation, hygienic living and environmental conditions are essential to protect human health during infectious disease outbreaks. To date, there is no clear evidence on whether the virus: can be persistence in sewage and untreated drinking water or transmitted via faecal-oral route. Other Corona viruses can survive in non-chlorinated water for two hence assumptions should be made that the COVID-19 can survive in similar conditions. Therefore, lack of adequate WASH facilities and clean and safe drinking water increases the potential transmission routes for the COVID-19 virus which will have grave consequences if measures are not put in place to prevent transmission especially in the camps. Many humanitarian actors have been providing services in the camps and all the gains made are now at risk hence urgent COVID-19 response activities are required to ensure the virus do not increase the burden on the Rohingya refugees who are already vulnerable to different shocks.
