Project Summary

The social crisis in the Anglophone Regions that began in late 2016 has eroded many years of social and economic development efforts, increased levels of risk and violence, breakdown in social infrastructures like schools, health, water, and sanitary infrastructure pushing the populations to a situation of poor hygienic practices. In the North-West and South-West, almost all 366,000 IDPs were displaced due to the violence. The multi-sectoral needs assessment carried out in February and March 2022 in the two regions found that access to drinking water, food, health and education were the priority needs of IDPs and returnees. The female population accounts for 48% of the total displaced population, children 44% (51% girls and 49% boys), and older people for 12%.
Threats to outbreak of diseases related to poor sanitation continue to be a call for concern. In most localities there is poor access to basic sanitation and safe drinking water to the masses of displaced persons and the host communities that is exposing the affected population to an increase morbidity and mortality. This situation can be put under control if water points are constructed or rehabilitated, latrines constructed, sensitization carried out on hygiene promotion and basic WASH materials made available to IDPs and Vulnerable host community members.
This project aims to:
-Improve sustainable access to basic sanitation and safe drinking water to vulnerable people in high concentration localities.
-Reduce the risk of poor hygiene-related morbidity and mortality of the affected population in the crisis.
This will be achieved through:
-Construction as well as rehabilitation of non-functional water points in affected localities,
-Construction of community latrines with showers to serve the masses thereby guaranteeing 100% confinement of feaces matter,
- Engaging in hygiene and sanitation promotion and supporting individual households with financial and technical support to enable them to construct their household latrines.
This project plans to:
Make available 10 water points with functional user committees, put at the use of communities 5 public gender disaggregated latrines with showers, support 40 households realize family latrines and reach out to 50,000 people with behaviour change communication on safe hygiene and sanitation practices amongst vulnerable people.
Accountability to beneficiaries will be ensured through education and sensitization throughout the project, involvement in scheduling of activities, as well as through beneficiary complaints and feedback mechanisms that will be put in place. Accountability to the partner will be through regular sharing of reports.
The project plans to address the following needs through its activities: Gender-sensitive latrines with showers: Water points: 5 water points (boreholes, water taps, small water networks) will be constructed and 5 non-functional water points rehabilitated in IDP sites and vulnerable host communities to give at least 10,000 people access to a safe water source (3,500 men and 6,500 women). WASH and Menstrual hygiene kits: 5,000 WASH and Menstrual Hygiene kits distributed to vulnerable households in the target communities. Water user committees: 10 water user committees will be established/ revamped in crisis-affected localities where the water points will be constructed or rehabilitated. Safe hygiene and sanitation practices: 3,000 persons will be sensitized on safe hygiene and sanitation practices including COVID 19 preventive measures and each receive soap.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The availability of WASH facilities in the South West and North West Region has been a serious call for concern since the start of the crisis in 2016. Due to this effect for the past 7 years now, most of these communities are suffering from the effect of poor water supply, poor latrines and women and girls having insufficient menstrual hygiene kits in their various communities. • The Humanitarian needs overview (HNO) 2023 for Cameroon estimates that 1.1 million people in need of humanitarian assistance in water, sanitation and hygiene are affected by the NWSW crisis. This represents 61 per cent of the people in need of humanitarian WASH assistance in the country.• The 2023 MSNA round 8 shows that access to safe drinking water remains the first top priority in the NW and SW regions.• An assessment conducted in 2022 by the ministry of water resources and energy (MINEE) and the WASH Cluster shows that 51 per cent of water points assessed in the SW region are non-functional. This situation seems similar in the NW region according to WASH partners.• Regarding sanitation, although open defecation is low (3 per cent) according to the MSNA round 8, 81 per cent of assessed villages utilized pit latrines without slab.• More floods and landslides hit NWSW regions as the rainy season returns.• The crisis has resulted in a system-wide breakdown of services and infrastructure in the most affected areas.• OCHA 2022 report states that Partners reached 283,006 people through different interventions including hygiene promotion accounting with 85 per cent of people reached and the distribution of key wash supplies accounting to about 10.81 per cent of the beneficiaries. Only 3.31 per cent of beneficiaries were provided safe drinking water and basic sanitation, as a result of multiple challenges at the funding and implementation levels. Most people reached are from the host population (62 per cent), IDP (25 per cent) and returnees (13 per cent).
