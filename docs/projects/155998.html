Project Summary

This proposed intervention will target conflict-affected men, women, boys and girls in northwest Syria, Idleb and Aleppo governorates, in order to provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelter & NFI assistance and strengthen their resilience and protection from harsh winter conditions, which will promote protection and meet immediate and urgent needs of IDPs and hosting communities through hosting them in sustainably rehabilitated housing units for at least one year.
This project aims at rehabilitation of 1200 partially damaged houses at an average cost of $865 per house and six collective shelters hosting IDPs in sub-districts of Ariha, Marrat al-Nu’man, Jebel Saman, Al-Bab, Mare', Teftnaz and Badama in order to provide shelter for hosting 1300 households for at least one year (390 HHs of hosting communities and 910 of IDPs).
This project will also aim at providing life-saving and life-sustaining assistance through the distribution of 500 shelter kits, 2500 new arrival kits, 2500 kitchen kits and 6000 clothes kits to IDPs in Afrin, Harim, Kafr Takharim, Atareb Heish, and Daret Azza.
Moreover, the project will cover the needs of 2500 HHs (750 host communities HHs and 1750 IDP HHs), through the distribution of heating fuel for three months (120 liters per family each month) with 500 of most recently-arrived IDP households covering their needs of heating stoves by receiving one diesel stove for each family in al-Bab, Mare’ and Maaret Tamsrin.
The project aims to assist 76800 people, roughly divided as 13900 men, 17500 women, 22700 boys and 22700 girls; where female beneficiaries are no less than 52%.
Needs assessment was conducted to help select implementation locations. Housing, Land and Property (HLP) rights of both shelter beneficiaries and house owners will be considered throughout all stages of shelter repair interventions.
There will be continuous cooperation between SDI and other cluster members and actors on the ground to ensure geographical separation and avoid overlapping/duplication regarding beneficiaries.
Rehabilitation activities will allow direct beneficiaries’ access to dignified and durable shelters which will contribute to saving and sustaining lives providing better living conditions and a healthier living environment for beneficiaries. Moreover, the project will support the targeted communities and contribute to the resilience and cohesion and protection of communities, including the creation of job opportunities for both IDPs and local communities and enhancing the prevention and mitigation of protection risks, and respond to protection needs through supporting the protective environment in Syria, by promoting international law, International Humanitarian Law, (human rights law) HRL and through quality principled assistance.

Needs

According to the Draft HNO2019 report, shelter situation in Syria continues to worsen, in terms of the available shelter capacity, the adequacy of shelter conditions, the ability of people to repair their own damaged shelter and the affordability of rent. The increase in shelter related need is particularly acute in more densely populated areas. There is significant overcrowding in Idleb and north-west Aleppo. 4.7 million people are in need of shelter support in Syria due to inadequate conditions in existing shelters and lack of available, adequate and affordable shelters. It is likely that these needs will further increase in 2019. 4.4 million people require NFI assistance. In some areas, such as the northwest, is in fact deteriorating amidst the increase in people in need. Needs remain substantial, in terms of humanitarian life-saving needs, supplementary and seasonal assistance. The acute humanitarian situation in the northwest is driven by the high proportion of IDPs, overcrowding and the lack of shelter capacity, high rental prices and unaffordable items. HLP issues remain a challenge on many fronts. Many displaced people do not have sufficient documentation. Populations in areas which experience cold conditions during winter continue to need seasonal support. The need for clothing, hygiene items and heating fuel were also among the most reported NFI needs across the country.”In reference to the same report, Jabal Saman, al-Bab, Maarrat Tamarin, Badama, Harim, Kafr Takharim, Maarat al-Nu’man and Heish were reported with severe shelter needs, while Afrin Daret Azzeh and Atareb were reported to have severe shelter adequacy problems. As for NFI severity, Harim, Maarrat Tamarin and Heish were reported as critical, while Kafr Takharim, Taftanz and Badama was reported as severe, while Afrin and Ariha were reported as having major problem in addition to that they host big number of IDPs and PIN. Moreover, in the end of 2018 SDI has conducted its own rapid SNFI assessment in several communities in northwest Syria, SDI collected data via contacting local councils and interviewing key informants using Kobo questionnaire aimed at learning the population, IDPs number, and identifying needs by targeted questions on the need for shelter and NFIs and the situation of the IDPs and residents in general in addition to HLP issues. It was found that among the areas that are in needs of shelter interventions are Maarat al-Nu’man (with 1600 partially damaged houses), Mare’(450), Ariha(674), Taftanz (250) Jebel Saman (5 public building with IDPs), Badama (10 public buildings). HLP documents are available for at least 50-75% of house owners in all assessed areas. The economic situation is bad especially for IDPs who are usually unable to afford the rent of decent houses or repair the ones they live in. They suffer from cold and lack of privacy (due to not having locks, door or windows) or use primitive ways to seal them (like using blankets) which has bad protection and psychological effects, especially on women and girls and badly affects the health of all residents especially elderly, disabled and ill people. Many women and adolescent girls may find themselves forced to GBV related situations to stay in shelter, or push their young female children to early marriages to grant a safe place to live in. Men, women, boy are girls in the sub-districts of al-Bab, Mare’ and Maarrat Tamarin are also affected by their extremely cold weather snow falls especially those who suffer from disability, severe illness, elderly people, small boys and girls under six and pregnant and lactating women, as they are among the most affected categories who need protection from cold weather due to their vulnerability and need for warmth to avoid heath concerns.Further more detailed needs assessment will be conducted in target areas before implementation to ensure update information and filling gaps in coordination with SNFI cluster.

Implementation of activities

SDI will engage with local community leaders and stakeholders in order to inform them of the project goals and increase awareness to the project activities and collect suggested beneficiary lists from multiple sources and hold meetings and/or focus groups with prominent figures, older persons, women and community-based organizations in order to involve them in designing activities and coordinate beneficiary selection process to reach the most vulnerable persons and ensure impartiality. SDI will review beneficiary lists, pay visits to a random sample of beneficiaries to verify they meet the following criteria: Beneficiaries affected by conflict and are in need of assistance. Priority will be given female, child, disabled, and elderly-headed households, then families with children below 5, elderly, severely ill or disabled members; with special attention for those recently displaced or living overcrowded, damaged shelters or tents.In activities, 2.1.1 Rehabilitation of damaged houses and 1.4.1 Rehabilitation of collective centres, SDI will also announce the project and open a registration office to receive applications directly from house owners and beneficiaries. The technical staff, will conduct a technical field inspection of all shelter units to verify that they are safe and repairable. SDI will conduct community HLP verification to all units (100%). Experienced technical staff of engineers will examine shelters and prepare BOQs and scope of work needed in each unit. An agreement will be signed with all owners that shows their approval to the rehabilitation works and to host IDPs for a year.Local suitable contractors will be contracted in a transparent and competitive bidding manner to ensure impartially and rule out fraud to undertake the required restoration works. SDI's technical team of engineers, will closely supervise the works of contractors to ensure quality rehabilitation works. After rehabilitation of shelter units, beneficiaries will be hosted in those houses after signing shelter agreements.In activities 1.1.1 Provision of non-fuel core and essential NFIs, 1.2.1 Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs including heating fuel and 1.3.3 Distribution/installation of emergency shelter materials/kits, All items will be procured within a transparent and competitive manner according to SDI’s policy and procedures and will be stored in the local warehouses with high levels of safety and security in safe locations guarded 24/7 and provided with functional firefighting equipment. In order to ensure accountability and quality assurance at the warehouse, SDI's field staff will document the receipt of the items at the warehouse using receipt notes that include a manual count of items and inspection of items to check for any damaged items and those inconsistent with the required specifications. All items will be distributed according to the needs and to cluster standards. Distribution points and times will be agreed on with local authorities and/or community leaders, clearly advertised, efficiently run, and will consider the protection of the beneficiaries. Materials could be delivered to houses/places of beneficiaries who cannot come to distribution centers. In all activities, female staff will be recruited to ensure more protection for girls and women. Complaint mechanism is in place to receive feedback, complaint numbers and email will be well-advertised at work places; brochures of safety and security practice will distributed to beneficiaries. We will take photos pre, post repair works and during all other activities of the project.An M&E plan is in place including HLP community verification, pre-rehabilitation monitoring, PIM and focus groups to ensure quality implementation and project compliance with the adopted plan, indicators and outputs. Progress reports, M&E reports will be prepared in addition to a final report that sums up all achievements, financial info, impacts and lesson learned.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk1 Increased risk of inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across membersLikelihood: highImpact: mediumMitigation: The project will use the clusters recommended minimum standards and specifications. Risk2. Tension between host communities, IDPs and returnees.Likelihood: highImpact: highMitigation: beneficiary selection be done through a transparent process based on validated needs. Moreover, the project will target about 30-35% of vulnerable host community in each activity. SDI will establish clear well-advertised feedback and complaint mechanism. Local councils and other stakeholders will be involved in beneficiary selection process. Risk3. Abuse of Authority by distribution actors (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion).Likelihood: highImpact: highMitigation: All staff of SDI are required to read and signed the Code of Conduct. A clear well-advertised feedback and complaint mechanism is in place, through WhatsApp and email well displayed on banners in each distribution and work location; through M&E staff who will be present in all distribution centers. Risk4. Risk of physical harm at distribution points (security, safety). Likelihood: MediumImpact: highMitigation: Beneficiaries will be divided into small groups, distribution date and time with be coordinated with local authorities and informed to each beneficiary group before distribution inorder to avoid crowdedness, long rows, long waiting times or disorder in distribution places. Distribution will complete in time for beneficiaries to reach their homes during daylight. Items will be brought to the doorsteps of the very vulnerable HHs who cannot come to the distribution centers Risk5. Risk of exclusion from shelter assistance due to lack of civil status document/ID.Likelihood: highImpact: highMitigation: SDI will work closely with communities, community leaders and authorities to identify alternative way of identification. Risk6. Eviction from shelter due to lack of approval from property owner and/or exposing beneficiaries at risk due to lack of HLP documentation for the shelter rehabilitatedLikelihood: highImpact: highMitigation: SDI will not proceed with the shelter rehabilitation unless with the consensus of the owner, proof of registered HLP ownership, verified ownership claims or in accordance to HLP Due Diligence Guidelines.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption 1: Access to targeted areas remains possible and safe.Risk1: Access to one (or more) of targeted areas becomes hindered or insecure.Likelihood: medium. Effect: medium. Mitigation: SDI will advise staff on alternative routes to mitigate the risk. If the security situation changes in a certain area, we can wait or postpone activities for a few days or change targeted areas to another accessible area in need of support. Assumption 2: Skilled and unskilled workers can be hired from the local market to complete rehabilitation works.Risk: It might be difficult to find enough qualified technicians and engineers to implement the rehabilitation works,Likelihood: low.Effect: low.Mitigation: contract and recruit high-quality workers from SDI roster or hire from a wider geographic range if needed. Assumption3: Rehabilitation items and materials are available to be procured locally.Risk 3: It might become difficult to locally secure one (or more) of the needed rehabilitation items/services of good quality or qualified workshops to implement the rehabilitation works.Likelihood: medium.Effect: high.Mitigation3: Social Development International (SDI) through previous experience was able to directly purchase the materials from larger markets within Syria and transport them to the project sites. Moreover, SDI will seek to contract high-capacity trusted vendors who can deliver the required rehabilitation works in Syria effectively. Assumption 4: HLP documents are available when implementing the activity of shelter rehabilitation.Risk4: Lack of formal property documentation because of war, sudden displacement and property transactions in areas under opposition control. Thus, some people do not have documents or can't have them registered through the formal state process.Likelihood: high.Effect: low.Mitigation4: Hold awareness meeting for the field staff and local authorities about inter-linkages between HLP rights and shelter and the importance of HLP verification in shelter intervention. Carry out HLP ownership community verification and ensure ownership of houses in accordance with HLP SNFI cluster guidelines. HLP documents will be very well considered by integrating HLP rights verification and monitoring into all shelter activities. Risk5: Warehouses might be exposed to dangers such as robbery and fire.Likelihood: low.Effect: highRisk mitigation3: Social Development International (SDI) usually takes into consideration safety and security measures in choosing warehouse locations, regular checks on firefighting equipment and 24/7 active guards. Risk6. Fire hazard during or after distribution of fuel.Risk mitigation: Ensure diesel is of good quality that is not too inflammable according to profession standards. Moreover, fire extinguishers will be present in distribution places and vehicles. Brochures about usage of stoves safety instruction and fire hazard will be distributed to all beneficiaries.
