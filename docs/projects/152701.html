Project Summary

Despite the recently signed revitalized peace agreement in late 2018 and the promise of better times to come, a lethal combination of prolonged dry spells, floods, conflict, poor access to inputs, low productivity, pests such Fall Army Worm invasion, livestock diseases and subsequent high mortalities, poor infrastructure, dysfunctional markets, poor terms of trade and poor trade flows, declining economic opportunities and rising inflation have pushed food insecurity situation in South Sudan to the highest levels since 2010, with at least 5.2 million people projected to be extremely food insecure in the first quarter of 2019 (IPC, September 2018 Update; FEWSNET-SS Food Security Outlook, 2018).
Throughout the crisis, many vulnerable host and displaced families, especially women, widowed women, child headed households, the elderly, boys and girls and children solely depend on agriculture (crop and livestock production) and fishing among other coping strategies. Fishing resources (projected to deliver immediate/short term benefits) and vegetable production (medium term benefits) are projected to still play a very key role in securing household food and nutrition security in 2019 especially among children (boys and girls) under 5 years and pregnant women (FSL Cluster Strategy, 2018). In locations with IPC 4 and 5, VSF Suisse will deploy limited unconditional cash grants to selected worst off households, to enable them access essential foods from the market, which should result into improved food and nutrition outcomes of the Households within a short period and inject cash in the nascent markets. Cash-based transfer interventions will be guided by information gathered from its own assessment and emergency market assessments (EMMA) conducted by other partners. The cash transfer value will be based on the prevailing cost of food basket (linked to the calories required per person per Household in a day).
Many displaced families, especially women, the elderly, boys and girls and children camped with livestock in the safe havens for easy access to livestock and fisheries derived food (meat, milk and fish) (FAO Conflict and Livestock in South Sudan, February 2016).
VSF Suisse proposes to target 30,000 beneficiaries (men, women, girls and boys – includes elderly and people with special needs) with a multi-sectoral approach to promote food and crop production, reduction in post-harvest losses and safe guarding animal health, restoration and support of fishing activities, focus on restoring and strengthening livelihoods (IPC 4 and IPC 3) alongside more multi sector integrated actions (e.g. WASH, Nutrition & Health), advocacy for women’s needs, voice and rights, capacity building and skills transfers, livestock vaccinations and treatments, cash transfers where possible to empower beneficiaries and increase efficiency and informed assistance through robust assessment and post distribution monitoring.
Through inter-cluster synergies, quality programming, cost efficiency in addition to mainstreaming cross-cutting programmatic approaches that include application of Accountability to Affected Populations principles, protection including Gender Based Violence/ Sexual exploitation mitigation measures, Do No Harm, conflict context analysis, gender equity, HIV/AIDS awareness and prevention, environmental conservation and sustainability.

Needs

Despite the recently signed revitalized peace agreement, a lethal combination of prolonged dry spells, floods, protracted conflict and continued ceasefire violations, poor access to inputs, low productivity, pests such Fall Army Worm (FAW) invasion, livestock diseases and subsequent high mortalities, poor infrastructure, dysfunctional markets, poor terms of trade and poor trade flows, declining economic opportunities and rising inflation have led to unprecedented food insecurity situation in South Sudan, with at least 5.2 million people projected to be extremely food insecure in the first quarter of 2019 (IPC, September 2018 Update). According to the latest September 2018 IPC update, an estimated 5.2 million (49% of the total population) people are in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity between January and March 2019, with 36,000 in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). Among these, catastrophe is expected in Pibor, Leer and Mayendit Counties of former Unity state. Levels of acute malnutrition are expected to deteriorate further in the first quarter of 2019 with an early onset of the lean season as most households are expected to deplete food stocks from own production. 2018 FAO/ WFP Crop and Food Security Assessment Report indicate flooding and inundation of crop fields and villages in some parts of Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Unity and Jonglei significantly damaged crops and livelihoods. In addition, endemic presence of common pests, FAW outbreaks, has caused mild to average damage on maize and sorghum crops. These have overally contributed to a 7.5% reduction in net cereal production, 14% percent below the average of the previous five years. These coupled with cyclical waves of cattle related fighting/ raiding across NBEG, Unity and Jonglei, endemic livestock diseases and recent outbreaks of Rift Valley Fever in Yirol and suspect cases in Panyijar have led to significant high mortality rates have and subsequent erosion of the livestock sector contribution (VSF Suisse Disease Outbreak Report,s 2018). These have consequently negatively contributed to poor nutritional status among the populations with recent SMART surveys conducted indicating Global Acute Malnutrition (WHZ) rates GAM (WHZ) 15.0 – 29.9%) in Unity, Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal among others. Due to inadequate veterinary services, livestock diseases threaten to erode livestock contribution to food security and coping strategies among agro-pastoralists in 2019. Throughout the crisis, livestock assets, crops, vegetables and fisheries resources have provided a critical safety net to the conflict affected communities, including the displaced populations in hard to reach areas (2018 FAO/ WFP Crop and Food Security Assessment Report ; FAO Conflict and Livestock in South Sudan).In locations with IPC 4 and 5, VSF-S will deploy limited unconditional cash grants/vouchers to selected worst off households, to enable them access essential foods from the market. VSF Suisse has been operational in the proposed states for over two decades with competent and experienced staff that will be rapidly deployed to respond to the needs. The organization currently has on-going FAO and OFDA funded food security and livelihoods project in Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Unity/ Jonglei respectively. VSF Suisse has built very good rapport and excellent community goodwill, local state ministries linkages, strong local networks and coordination with the FSL Cluster. VSF Suisse will ensure a multi-sectoral approach through inter-cluster synergies, quality programming, cost efficiency in addition to mainstreaming cross-cutting programmatic approaches that include application of Accountability to Affected Populations principles, protection including Gender Based Violence/ Sexual exploitation mitigation measures, Do No Harm, conflict context analysis, gender equity, HIV/AIDS awareness and prevention, environmental conservation and sustainability.

Activities

• Distribution of crops, fishing kits and vegetable kits among 30,000 beneficiaries (5000 HHs)• Livestock vaccination and treatment services: • Refresher training for 230 CAHWs (20 % female, 70 % male) • New Training for 150 new CAHWs (50 female 100 male) • Train 12 new stock persons/AHAs (4 male, 6 female) • Refresh 25 existing SP/AHAs on cold chain management and monitoring and CAAP • Conduct 11 sessions of Participatory Disease Search sessions for emerging livestock diseases • Vaccinate 700,000 livestock heads against endemic livestock diseases • Treat 70,000 livestock heads against endemic livestock diseases • Install and maintain 20 solar fridges for vaccine handling at the field • Procure and distribute 2000 new CAHW kits • Conduct 11 monitoring sessions • Support 2000 HHs with seed fairs using locally sourced seed; seed security & multiplication strengthening in NBEG • Unconditional cash transfers/ Voucher system for seed procurement targeting 2000 HHs• Support value chain addition e.g. through milk & fish preservation & marketing among 2000 HHs• Training & capacity building: both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production (farmer field schools, demonstration plots), income generation (business skills), vocational training etc. among 2000 HHs

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

VSF Suisse will adopt a consultative project implementation approach; by information sharing and including equitable representation of male and female, youth, boys and girls, women and men (including disabled and elderly); local community leaders, youth and women representatives among other relevant authorities in decision making as well and also ensuring their full participation in project planning, selection, verification, implementation, monitoring/evaluation. VSF Suisse field teams will also conduct weekly/monthly project management meetings to review and consider any issues emerging that may impact on the project implementation, in addition to mainstreaming of cross cutting issues such as gender, protection, HIV/AIDS and accountability etc. Activities including livelihood kits distributions and animal health interventions will be conducted in presence of beneficiary trustees such as local community leaders, RRC and youth/women representatives and facilitated by representatives of specific beneficiary groups; including inviting the Cluster in implementation and monitoring. Beneficiary distribution lists will be developed, verified and endorsed by beneficiary representatives and filed for records / references. Men, women, boys and girls are consulted jointly and separately during project review meetings and postdistribution monitoring exercises. Activities will be implemented in a timely and consultative manner; to avoid any risks attributed to congregation of beneficiaries, especially during livelihood kits distributions in addition to evaluating any potential risks more so among women and girls using a 'Do no Harm Approach and protection. Sufficient information on the project, including sharing relevant documents with the local leadership, local State/County ministries will be shared to enable them understand and influence key decisions in the project including putting in place a feedback and complaints desk at activity sites and appropriate contacts. VSF Suisse will also engage Cluster leads, communities, local leaders FAO in ensuring that the most vulnerable populations are targeted and reached. An active Complaints and Feedback desks shall be established at various distribution/ training with results analyzed to inform a better response.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project take into consideration protection from sexual exploitation and abuse which is enshrined in the organization’s code of conduct that addresses prevention of: sexual exploitation and abuse by staff; sexual activities by staff with children (under 18 years); staff exchange of money, goods and/ or services for sexual favours; sexual relationships by staff with beneficiaries; exploitation of fellow workers within the organization or counterpart agencies; and maintenance of an environment that strictly prevents sexual exploitation and abuse. Any violation of the above is regarded as gross misconduct which appropriate actions taken against the perpetrators.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

VSF Suisse will ensure effective and rapid implementation of the project directly in close consultations, collaboration and involvement of beneficiary communities and counterparts and in partnership with key relevant stakeholder and partners such as FAO, FSL Cluster and agriculture and animal resources/ livestock and fisheries ministries. VSF Suisse will hold consultative meetings with community leaders, local authorities including the state/county agriculture ministries, FAO State leads, Cluster leads as well as community members to introduce the project, discuss and agree onroles and responsibilities; including beneficiary selection criteria, implementation as well as coordination modalities. This will be in addition to ensuring coordinated response with counterpart agencies implementing Nutrition, Health and WASH programming to ensure a holistic approach and achieve better impacts of the project. As a principle, VSF Suisse will consult and ensure active participation of men, women, boys and girls including youths, elderly and disabled will be encouraged to ensure that interestsof various beneficiary groups are taken into account during planning and project implementation and monitoring. Existing agricultural extension workers, community animal health workers and key resource persons will be mobilized, trained and deployed to to support implementation.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

VSF Suisse will conduct routine gender and protection risk assessments and risk monitoring to understand and adapt to the evolving protection concerns during implementation. This is in addition to the project prioritizing beneficiary safety and dignity in consideration with the principles of ‘Do no harm’; non-discrimination, equitable, gender segregated and meaningful access to proposed services/facilities and cater for specific needs of girls, women, youth and men including the elderly and disabled. Communities will also be engaged among other relevant stakeholders, including awareness and training on basic human rights while implementing various activities.For instance, the primary beneficiaries for this project are identified as host and IDP communities. This is designed to deliver services in a non-discriminatory manner for all those who are most vulnerable, and to ensure any tensions between host communities and IDPs over access to facilities and services is avoided.The bulk of the program activities are planned to be delivered in the dry season, to ensure communities are not distracted from the core farming activities with potential to harm their productive livelihood engagements.Beneficiary information dissemination on specific support packages by the field teams will be done, in addition to instituting a “feedback and complaint mechanism”. Contacts of key project teams, senior management and counterpart independent agencies such as FAO and RRC will be shared to ensure accountability.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

VSF Suisse does regular context analysis and protection assessment including evaluations on possible impediments and beneficiary access constraints. Ways of mitigating these including advocacy with key stakeholders is done to ensure unimpeded access to targeted beneficiaries.In addition, VSF Suisse has been operating in the proposed areas for over 20 years with established local networks. This is in addition to the organization having locally recruited staff, having good rapport and working relations with local state ministries, FAO etc., that would be rapidly engaged, mobilized and facilitate access the proposed areas.Close collaboration and coordination with other counterpart agencies such as ICRC, UNIDO, Nile Hope, ICRC, FAO will be involved to fast and efficient access to most vulnerable beneficiaries.VSF Suisse will also liaise very closely with UNMISS regular patrols to access hard to reach areas and will be in a position to secure space for persons to deploy or for supplies to be delivered, especially in the dry season. VSF Suisse has also tried successfully the use of human porters and water/ canoe transport to deliver supplies.

VSF Suisse through consultations, context analysis, regular protection assessments, coordination and participation of key stakeholders in implementation will ensure the project addresses inequalities and protection threats. Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Activities will be implemented during safe periods and early hours of the day. Centralized implementation sites including distribution centres will be established to avoid the risk of beneficiaries walking for long distances to access services, further exposing them to harm. Inequalities such as people with special needs including elderly will as well be prioritized with services provided at their residences including prioritization and involvement. Marginaized groups will as well be prioritized. Child/women/widowed/elderly/disabled headed and those with pregnant and lactating mothers will be prioritized and identified as primary beneficiaries to ensure their dignity and safety is guaranteed.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project will ensure measurement of access to beneficiaries during the initial preliminary assessments, planning and consultative sessions with beneficiary representatives among other key stakeholders. This will be based on time and distance taken to access the services/ support packages whilst taking into account the various vulnerabilities including access constraints, with meaningful mitigation measures put in place to ensure unhindered and easily access to the services/ support.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

The project will carry out regular risk and protection assessments to take into account potential hazards and protection concerns which are likely to be faced by beneficiaries. Considerations on gender, specifically women/ girls at risk of sexual and gender based violence, elderly and children who may be at risk of violence and abduction (children) including recruitment of children into armed forces/ groups. This will be done in consultations with key focal points including local leadership and line ministries and formulate mitigation measures. Focused group discussions, including observations, interviews and consultations will be used to assess potential threats and mitigation measures put in place, which will be regularly shared with key stakeholders.

Interviews and consultations with beneficiaries including local leadership and line ministries and counterpart agencies will be done including analysis of results and corrective measures and recommendations done. The project will aim at prioritizing beneficiary safety and dignity in consideration with the principles of ‘Do no harm’; non-discrimination, equitable, gender segregated and meaningful access to proposed services/facilities and cater for specific needs of girls, women, youth and men including the elderly and disabled.
