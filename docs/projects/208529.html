Project Summary

The project aims at Increasing protection of children, especially Unaccompanied Minors, Separated, orphaned, abused/neglected children in Borno and Adamawa states.
UNHCR intervention will strengthen the child protection environment by; -
1. Strengthening community-based child protection systems/structures.
2. Promoting participation and psychosocial wellbeing of children and their caregivers through CFS activities.
3. Providing individual case management, psychosocial support, and material assistance to children at heightened risk and children living with disabilities) and alternative care providers.
4. Conducting awareness raising/sensitization and advocacy to promulgate key children’s rights and protection messages to communities and duty bearers.
5. Increasing communities’ knowledge on feedback and reporting mechanisms.

1 - Needs

Since the beginning of the ongoing crisis in 2009, more than 2.1 million men, women and children have been forced to flee their homes in North-East Nigeria. The crisis coupled with soaring inflation and climate change, has compounded the vulnerabilities of the displaced men, women, girls, and boys resulting in massive unmet needs. According to UNHCR, 363,000 people have sought refuge in neighbouring Cameroon (121,000), Chad (21,000), and Niger (221,000) as of 30 September 2023. In the BAY states, a total of 8.3 million people are in need of protection and humanitarian assistance according to the Northeast 2023 Nigeria Humanitarian Needs Overview. This includes 2.0 million IDPs (in and out of camp), 1.5 million returnees, and 4.8 million host community members. Of the total persons in need, 22% are women and 59% are children. The percentage of persons with disabilities is 8% and is expected to be higher in locations with conflict. In Adamawa, as part of UNHCR’s ongoing level two (L2) biometric registration exercise of Cameroonian asylum seekers, as of 13 November there are 2,462 households of 7,656 individual asylum seekers/refugees in Adamawa State. Among these individuals, according to the L2 registration as of 13 November, 4,661 among the registered asylum seekers/refugees are minors. As registration is ongoing, the number of asylum seekers/refugees is anticipated to be over 35,000 individuals by the end of 2024. There are also asylum seekers from Niger republic in Geidam, Yobe State that are yet to be registered. Amid this situation, children and their caregivers face heightened protection risks which has resulted to increase in female and child-headed households, unaccompanied/separated children, adolescent boys, and girls among other vulnerable populations. Recovery efforts are largely hampered by continued cycle of displacement induced by incessant NSAG attacks and clearance operations by the military. There is lack of basic infrastructures, limited availability of basic services, and limited presence of the relevant national actors and key humanitarian partners in some locations, all of which exacerbate protection risks for the most vulnerable populations (especially children and women). The community based- child protection systems are weak or broken and the capacity of duty bearers to respond to child protection concerns is weak, thus complicating the process of recovery. Limited access to education, dilapidated education infrastructure, abductions, lack of basic needs (among other needs) discourages affected children from enrolling or staying in school. High numbers of unaccompanied and separated children, orphans and child-headed households have critical protection needs and many children have had to resort to hawking/begging as means of survival. There has been an increase of instances of attacks, harassment, abduction and GBV when women and adolescent girls venture outside sites' security perimeter to search for firewood and food. There is increased vulnerability to sexual violence, sale, and exchange of sex for food or money and forced/early marriages for adolescent girls due to pre-dominant gender inequality, culture and religious norms in the north east, lack of sanitary materials and basic necessities. There is a critical need to continue to scale up efforts to prevent, refer and respond to cases with identified child protection risks with effective child protection interventions. Strengthened engagement, collaboration, and coordination with all stakeholders to find durable solutions for children is highly required.

2 - Activities

A wide range of activities will be implemented under 5 main axes of intervention namely Strengthened community-based Child protection systems and structures; Promotion of participation and psychosocial well-being of children; providing individual case management, psychosocial support, and material assistance to children at heightened risk and children living with disabilities) and alternative care providers; conducting awareness raising/sensitization and advocacy to promulgate key children’s rights and protection messages to communities and duty bearers; as well as increasing communities’ knowledge on feedback and reporting mechanisms. UNHCR efforts will be geared towards identification of children with protection risks and incidents and ensure individual case management, timely and appropriate reporting, and referral for comprehensive protection response with other service providers within the support system and referral pathway. To ensure community-based child protection structures are strengthened, the following activities will be implemented:• Training, coaching/mentoring of Community-based structures on child protection, children’s rights, and related issues.• Conducting regular meetings with community based-child protection structures• Identifying and strengthening the capacity of alternative care arrangements (parents, foster caregivers etc.) on child protection and positive parenting skills.To promote community Participation and Psychosocial Counselling of children the following activities will be implemented:• Providing CFS (learning, recreational & PSS) activities • Conducting kid’s clubs’ activities • Conducting individual and group psychosocial support counselling sessions for children and their caregivers, in school, community and Child friendly spaces.• Conducting community sensitization and awareness on child protection and children’s right.• Commemorate international events (16DOA, IDGC, DAC, WCD).• Ensuring individual case management of 2,200 UASC orphaned and other vulnerable children - including BIA/BID as appropriate. • 200 adolescent boys, girls and vulnerable caregivers undergo life skills building activities at the CFS. To promulgate key children’s rights and protection messages to communities and duty bearers, awareness raising/sensitization and advocacy will be conducted. To increase communities’ knowledge on feedback and reporting mechanisms, UNHCR will intensify awareness creation on the existing complaints and feedback mechanisms - including toll-free/hotline, confidential email, complaint boxes, and reports to UNHCR or partner staff or community volunteers.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

Adhering to the UN triple nexus of humanitarian, development, and peace considerations, UNHCR is committed to collaborating with partners to provide durable solutions to internal displacement. This involves addressing immediate needs while empowering affected populations to enhance their capacity to tackle emerging challenges. The focus is on managing existing and potential tensions within the local context, ensuring more sustainable and effective outcomes. The strategy emphasizes context-based programming, actively engaging local communities in the development and implementation of projects to yield tangible and lasting results aligned with the specific needs of the affected populations. The approach is rooted in the centrality of protection, with a key focus on long-term durable solutions, including livelihoods. UNHCR's involvement in joint risk analysis incorporates conflict sensitivity, disability inclusion, and gender-sensitivity, fostering a shared understanding of the risks, needs, and vulnerabilities of the affected population for informed planning and programming.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The key protection principles underpinning the provision of humanitarian assistance informed the design of this project and as such will be observed during the project implementation. UNHCR will adopt a rights-based approach in the planning, implementation and evaluation during the entirety of the project. To ensure comprehensive protection mainstreaming across this project, the following measures will be taken for each of the protection principles: • Prioritize safety and dignity and avoid doing harm during the project implementation, UNHCR will closely monitor the security situation of the area of intervention, and mobile teams will use safe and authorized roads to access people in need. • To ensure meaningful and equal access, in the identification of beneficiaries, men, women, boys and girls will be prioritized based on need alone. Particularly vulnerable groups such as female- and child-headed households, but also elderly and persons with disability will be prioritized. The composition of the assessment teams will reflect gender balance, to facilitate contact with women and girls. • Additional measures will be taken to reach persons with disabilities and others extremely vulnerable. This can be the case of disabled persons unable to reach the protection desks. • In order to promote accountability, participation and empowerment of beneficiaries, beneficiaries’ participation to the project will be promoted by involving, to the widest extent possible, men, women, boys and girls of different ages in the community-based structures. • Establish measures aimed at preventing and responding to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, bearing in mind the UN zero tolerance policy. • All along project implementation, UNHCR will monitor protection, age, and gender aspects and will address any arising issues. To ensure a comprehensive response, all the findings will be shared with the Protection Sector as well as other relevant Sectors, with whom UNHCR will maintain close coordination.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

UNHCR will strengthen community-based complaint/feedback mechanisms, which will allow men, women, boys and girls to express any complaint; beneficiaries will be informed of the existence of such mechanisms, and it will be ensured that they are comfortable in accessing it. Child friendly complaint boxes will be introduced in child friendly areas as well as in educational structures. The existing tollfree number and email will be vulgarized and peer to peer approach will be established to support reporting of complaint. Focus group discussions with people from different age, gender and diverse origin will be conducted regularly to collect feedback from affected population on available services and as well as protection risks. UNHCR will ensure that committees of women, men, boys and girls are in place in order to participate fully in the implementation of activities. Through these, UNHCR will ensure that issues of concern to individuals, as well as, to the community are brought to its attention as well as to the attention of other partners, for intervention. UNHCR will leverage on its active membership of the Taskforce on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and its Age Gender Diversity mainstreaming policy too. UNHCR and partners will also ensure that staff involved in the project comply with its code of conduct and the SG’s special measures for protection from sexual exploitation and abuse.
