Project Summary

Since 2015, The North West and South West Regions of Cameroon are currently facing a complex humanitarian situation which has continued to be marked by violent clashes, with some 3 million people affected and over 705,800 internally displaced.
According to OCHA Cameroon Situational Report of December 2022 at least 567,673 people need shelter in the NW, including 264,213 returnees, 225,516 IDPs, and 77,944 non-displaced people. In the SW, more than 317,538 people need shelter, including 150,758 returnees, 132,915 IDPs, and 33,865 non-displaced (OCHA, 2022; https://reports.unocha.org/en/country/cameroon/).
Numerous health centres have been closed intensifying the deteriorating health conditions for the over 5.2 million people living in the Anglophone regions. 80 percent of schools have been shut down and over 75 percent of businesses owned by women, men and boys have been destroyed due to this crisis, leading to a high crime wave in the community. There is a large number of women and girls having huge potentials for development if institutionally and operationally empowered to effectively play their role.
As stated above, continuation of the conflict brings with it not only loss of lives, shelter, unavailable key support services such as health in this community, but also cuts off the people from livelihood activities. A harmonized analysis carried out in November 2020 estimates a total number of 1 million people who are food insecure due to limited resources. The current crisis affecting the NWSW has seriously affected the economic livelihood of over 10,000 members of our community especially women and girls including the elderly who barely struggling to provide for the needs of their families.
According to a statement made by the UN Secretary-General, Ban Ki-moon in launching its 2008 campaign, Unite to End Violence
against Women, reported that “at least one out of every three women is likely to be beaten, coerced into sex or otherwise abused in her lifetime.” The stigma associated with gender-based violence and mental health concerns can stop women from sharing their experiences, reporting the incidents, and accessing support. Many women say the fear of not being believed by their friends, family, or authorities keeps them from disclosing their experiences, and losing custody of their children is another concern that may keep women from disclosing their experiences.
Despite the effort done by UN women, UNFPA and the host of other stakeholders like NGOs, CSOs, and IGOs, gender inequality has continued to negate women’s values in society. This has inevitably made women and girls more vulnerable to shocks and stresses, including gender-based violence. Thus, to effectively mitigate GBV and its drastic effects on the mental well-being of its survivors, it is important to take into consideration that each GBV survivor is marginalized in a unique way. Thus we intern to carefully and detailly put in place measures to remedy them, strengthened critical support for women experiencing mental health concerns, both to prevent and address violence, including access to long-term counselling, childcare supports, better legal assistance, employment opportunities, create awareness workshops and seminars to men violating the rights of women and girl, create available report stations/follow-up commissions put in all remote areas wherein victims of such violations could report and be taken care of through both physical and psychological check-ups until these survivors fully recover and we will create close collaboration with other stakeholders NGOs, CSOs, and IGOs in taking necessary measures to not only protect them but equally train them in a trade that will eventually yield them revenue.

1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Women and girls have experienced GBV incidents four times more than males. Emotional abuse denied access to resources and domestic violence constituted the vast majority of GBV incidents. Sixty-three per cent of all the GBV incidents were perpetrated by the intimate partners, brothers, fathers and/or mothers. Objective#4 Results: A nationwide movement for the elimination of all forms of violence and discrimination against marginalized women/girls is established and working at different levels to monitor, report, advocate and respond to all cases or threat of violence against marginalized women and girls in CameroonActivity 4.1: design, print and distribute 20,000 informative, education and communication materials in both national languages Activity 4.2: organize 50 TV and 100 Radio live and/or prerecorded programs and awareness raising and activist recruitment messages to prevent and respond to GBVActivity 4.3: organize major campaigns and commemoration of key national and international days on women rights including but not limited to 16 days of activismActivity 4.4: establish an online and/or cable TV/radio channels for sustained and adapted content development and broadcasting local languages for towards comprehensive awareness on women rights, the prevention and response to GBV
