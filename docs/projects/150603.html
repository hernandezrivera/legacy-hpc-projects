Project Summary

The overarching goal of this project is to better equip communities, prioritizing the most vulnerable IDPs, with the tools and systems they need to manage and respond to the needs of displacement in urban setting continuing over the next year. This project is under the lead of NRC in close coordination with IDPs WG to support local authorities and communities in this regard, linking municipal forums to the MoSD and humanitarian actors’ community outreach structure. The project will have two main pillars:
1. Increasing local authorities’ emergency risk management knowledge, including in settings of urban displacement
2. Building municipal forums capacities and awareness of humanitarian disaster response mechanisms in Gaza.
The project will build the capacities of 750 beneficiaries including vulnerable households, municipal forums members, CBOs members, MoSD & municipalities’ staff involved in community risk mitigation measures and emergency preparedness. In addition,, 200 HH in DES surroundings will be provided by multi-purpose cash to restore their NFIs and purchase hygiene items, in order to be able to accommodate IDPs families in case of any displacement to their areas.

Needs

Following the closure of the Collective Centers (CCs) in June 2015, the IDPs caseload is fully scattered in urban and out of camp settings. In such displacement settings, there are no clear roles and responsibilities defined amongst humanitarian actors and emergency responders. Gaps in communication and field coordination mechanisms were identified alongside the lack of information from and representation of the vulnerable and displaced population. NRC has invested in filling these gaps through the introduction of Urban Displacement and Outside Camps “UDOC” approach in the Gaza Strip. UDOC is a component of the Camp Management (CM) Core Competency aiming to facilitate the access of the affected communities to protection and basic services. The UDOC team established representation and field coordination structures, municipal forums, and commenced the development of the Urban Displacement Response guidelines and tools. UDOC’s Municipal forums are representation structures established through the UDOC intervention. These forums include representatives encompassing youth, women, community leaders, local CBOs, the municipality, and IDPs themselves. Municipal forums have agreed to and endorsed TORs from the municipality with whom they are working. Municipal forums act as an area based coordination structure that supports the two way communication between the affected population and all other stakeholders involved in the humanitarian services. Linking Municipal forums with clusters focal points in different governorates will reflect communities’ needs and priorities in clusters coordinated efforts to respond to these needs and priorities. Municipal forums can also support in services mapping in their areas. The latest humanitarian services’ mapping conducted by NRC in Rafah during September 2018 emphasized the lack of knowledge amongst affected communities about emergency services and mitigation measures. This reality also was also emphasized in Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) conducted in June 2017 with communities at risk of displacement, whereas 67% of the participants in those FDGs stated that the information they hold about how to act in emergency not helpful enough. Furthermore, the latest IDP vulnerability assessment, published in June 2018 by NRC, revealed increased risks of GBV amongst displaced families as stated by 49% of the assessed families. In addition, a similar FGD from September 2018 clearly articulated that vulnerable households are depleting their coping mechanisms by selling furniture and NFI items. If this trend continues, the host community’s ability to receive and accommodate IDPs will be jeopardized. It’s necessary to accumulate more awareness and capacities before any emergency strikes so that communities can locally cope and respond to emergency impacts .Therefore, community representative structures (municipal forums, CBOs & committees including women & youth) in the displacement prone and receiving areas need to be strengthened through the proposed trainings and cash support to be able to accommodate IDP families and provide adequate referrals. Building the capacities of municipalities and local communities to respond to humanitarian needs during and post emergencies whether inside collective centers or in urban settings is a clear need. Communities and local authorities need to better understand humanitarian disaster response mechanisms in Gaza including how to reach the displaced population, services and capacities of the DES and how they can support displaced population in host communities. In additions, vulnerable families in DES surroundings should restore their NFIs assets prior to large emergencies because market functionality and availability of NFIs is not guaranteed then. If this knowledge is acquired and NFIs assets are restored, communities will be better placed to accommodate the influx of IDPs to perceived safe DES zones.

Activities

NRC is currently leading the development of tools and guidelines needed to respond to urban displacement, in close cooperation with MoSD and OCHA. To build on this expertise, NRC proposes to expand coverage through providing tailored training on theUrban Displacement Caseload Management to the staff of the big 5 municipalities in the Gaza Strip and MoSD staff at governorate level. Afterwards, the trained staff of the big five municipalities and MoSD will be linked with municipal forums, neighbourhood committees, CBOs and community groups at different areas. These links will be established through a series of workshops attended by the staff and community representatives. These links will enable municipalities and MoSD staff to easily access information about the needs of IDPs in urban settings as they can contact community focal points in certain areas. The focus will be given to areas adjacent to the currently identified DESs as these neighbourhoods will be seen as safe refuge by the affected population and will potentially receive a higher proportion of IDPs. Furthermore, due to deteriorating socio-economic conditions in the Gaza Strip, it is expected that host communities would be overwhelmed if they receive IDP families on their premises.Therefore, this intervention will prepare host communities with the tools to provide adequate support to IDPs during emergencies. Though multi-purpose and restricted cash assistance that would be provided to purchase NFI and hygiene kits, families will be able to restore their resilience and ability to host IDPs. This cash assistance will be given based on detailed assessment of the needed items per household, and cash will be distributed in consecutive three batches, one multi-purpose and two restricted installments to beneficiaries restricted to agreed upon NFI items through cash vouchers. Such a mechanism would increase communities’ preparedness before an emergency and support the local market. Cash and vouchers will be given before the potential emergency in order to ensure that the market is equipped with the necessary NFI items. This system is seen as a preparedness for response mechanism; cash and vouchers distributed after an emergency has taken place as a response mechanism is not preferred in this scenario as it may require a standardized NFI kit model and would not suit the needs of each family to purchase the NFIs they deem most necessary. In addition, the market will likely be healthier before an emergency allowing a wider selection and guarantee of certain items. NRC is proposing to provide municipal forums and community groups with trainings on the humanitarian disaster response mechanisms in Gaza, including clusters’ coordinated services so that they can identify vulnerable cases and link them to service providers and/or cluster members. These trainings will also jointly target MoSD staff at governorate level in order to link them with their communities and CBOs. Activities of knowledge sharing and capacity building will create a roster of community groups that can be easily accessed by the humanitarian actors to seek information about the affected population, or to convey certain messages to the affected communities. UDOC will share this roster with clusters’ members based on their geographical area of work. Trainings will be followed up by awareness sessions to the host communities, where the 200 vulnerable households in the potential host communities will be prioritized for multi-purpose and restricted cash assistance. NRC’s staff will provide coaching and mentoring to ensure that these capacities are utilized and sustained. Additionally, NRC will support the big five municipalities to design and print guidelines and maps illustrating safe shelters and procedures during emergencies. This will be done under IDPs WG supervision and in collaboration with the shelter cluster.
