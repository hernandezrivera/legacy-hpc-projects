Project Summary

An already impoverished Palestine refugee population in Gaza faces increased vulnerabilities due to on-going blockade with lengthy closures of the border crossings at Erez and Rafah as well as unpredictable commercial crossing at Kerem Shalom and access to the sea. It is further compounded with the loss of income and increase in prices due to the global COVID-19 pandemic with the further economic restrictions that followed, and the recurrent escalation of hostilities. UNRWA continues to provide in-kind food assistance to more than 1 million Palestine refugees in the Gaza Strip, covering a part of daily caloric needs, yet the stagnated economy continues to be a burden for the majority of the refugees. The population has already been facing years of humanitarian crisis, and many people are relying on daily labour and casual work to secure their minimum requirements when and if available. The families with high dependency ratios, including those with children, older persons and headed by females or persons with disability, are particularly at risk, as they are more likely than others to suffer from poverty or other forms of deprivation. Through this project, UNRWA will provide one-off multi-purpose cash assistance of US$40 per individual to more than 180,000 vulnerable and cash-strapped Palestine refugees identified through multi-dimensional vulnerability criteria including female-headed households, households headed by older person or an adolescent, and persons with disability.
The project is directly addressing the HRP Strategic Outcome 3: the ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued.

1 - Needs

Recurring rounds of violence and over 14 years of blockade imposed on the Gaza Strip remain the root cause of widespread food insecurity in Gaza. Restricted access to land, water, production inputs and markets constrain productive capacities and limit economic opportunities (HNO 2022). According to the Palestine Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS), the overall unemployment rate in Gaza reached 44.7 per cent during the second quarter of 2021. These trends have a particularly severe impact on vulnerable groups including women and young people. Unemployment rate is especially high among women with 64.0 per cent (PCBS, Q2-2021), facing both lack of opportunities and social barriers to employment in Gaza. Young people between the ages of 15 and 29 are also significantly affected with average unemployment rate of 63.8 per cent (PCBS, Q2-2021). Despite being highly educated, young people in Gaza face the prospect of unemployment, remaining dependent on already strained families and isolated from the outside world, rather than creating an independent future for themselves. The poverty rate increased from 38.8 per cent in 2011 to 53 per cent in 2017 (SEFSec Survey, 2018), and is expected to have increased to 56.4 per cent as a result of the May hostilities (World Bank, 2021: https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2021/07/06/the-rebuilding-of-gaza-amid-dire-conditions-damage-losses-and-needs). According to MSNA findings conducted in July 2021, 53 per cent of surveyed households in Gaza reported having received the assistance of some type since the beginning of the escalation, while ninety-four per cent reported wanting to receive assistance in the future (HNO 2022). Same study showed that cash transfers were the most preferred form of assistance (by 77 percent), followed by food in-kind (by 36 percent). In 2020, UNRWA started providing a one-off unconditional cash assistance to the most vulnerable Palestine refugees to mitigate additional socio-economic hardship due to COVID-19 and continued to provide assistance in 2021 to supplement UNRWA’s in-kind food assistance with a focus on the most vulnerable families.

2 - Activities

UNRWA will provide unconditional one-off cash assistance to the cash-strapped Palestine refugees in Gaza suffering from worsened socio-economic situation. The assistance will be provided to the most vulnerable families identified through UNRWA’s multi-dimensional vulnerability criteria, including female-headed households, adolescent-headed households, households with persons with disabilities, households with older persons, etc. The status of the listed beneficiaries will be further verified by UNRWA Relief and Social workers. UNRWA’s Finance Department will arrange the bank transfer based on the verified list of beneficiaries. SMS will be sent to the targeted beneficiaries when the transfer is completed. A phone number will be included in SMS for beneficiaries to contact in case of any issues or concerns. UNRWA aims to provide support to 45,000 vulnerable refugees per quarter with an annual target of 180,000 Palestine refugees. While the area is not a part of the selection criteria, estimated number of beneficiaries per area are: 31,103 in the North Area, 49,956 in Gaza, 834,690 in the Middle Area, 34,463 in Khan Younis, and 28,788 in Rafah. UNRWA has a well-established monitoring mechanism with dedicated Monitoring and Evaluation team who regularly conducts post-distribution monitoring for food distribution. In this project, the M&E team will conduct post distribution monitoring survey and assessment through home visits if the situation allows, otherwise the monitoring will be done remotely. As a member of PSEA network, UNRWA commits to all PSEA mandatory activities across its programmes, and the interventions included in this proposal are not exception. UNRWA adopts a safe complaint channel(s) for beneficiaries based on consultations with the beneficiaries and accessible to different groups. The PSEA focal points are trained to receive complaints in multitude of ways, including a 24/7 WhatsApp number available through text or phone for SEA complaints, and a centralized confidential online/telephone/email complaints mechanism to respond to specific confidential complaints are in place. To ensure that the beneficiaries are aware of PSEA mechanism, PSEA awareness raising material will be disseminated to all project recipients. UNRWA regularly engage with HCT oPt PSEA network, and project staff will engage with UNRWA’s representative to this body via training and through project monitoring.

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

The Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) Unit, in coordination with the other implementing units, prepares internal Post Distribution Monitoring reports based on the survey. The reports are sent to the Agency’s management for information, evaluation and corrective action as required. The collection of beneficiaries’ feedbacks through both UNRWA M&E and RSSP units contributes to continuously improve the cash distribution mechanism at different levels.

9.1a - Specify the mechanisms for feedback and/or complaints

a - Generic email,b - Project hotline,c - Complaint box,d - Satisfaction survey,e - Field visit,f - Other

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :

In normal circumstances, anyone can access to closest Area RSSP office if there are any concerns. Furthermore, telephone hotlines and help lines are established in support of the relief operation to maximize efficiency and to minimize movement and in-person transactions. The dedicated staff responsible for handling beneficiaries’ concerns and inquiries provide needed information and assistance. UNRWA also runs its own portal where beneficiaries are encouraged to raise any general query with regards to any issue including the quality of services delivered. Post Distribution Monitoring survey will be conducted to assess beneficiaries’ access to, use of, and satisfaction with cash assistance provided.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

UNRWA has a multitude of options to receive feedback and ensure accountability to the affected population, including in-person reception in all UNRWA installations throughout the Gaza Strip, a targeted online and telephone complaints portal (nominal and anonymous), a 24/7 WhatsApp number available through text or phone for SEA complaints; a centralized (Amman) confidential online/telephone/email complaints mechanism to respond to specific confidential complaints. Direct (in person) and indirect (press, social media, etc) outreach is further undertaken to disseminate the information on the above mechanisms.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

UNRWA has a specific obligation to mainstream protection across its services and operations, as well as made a clear commitment to protect and uphold Palestinian refugees’ rights. The pandemic is resulting in an increase in the protection threats facing groups with multiple overlapping vulnerabilities, which includes those at risk of domestic and intimate partner violence and the older persons. The risk of sexual exploitation and abuse may have also increased, as evidenced in other crisis situations. The Agency is ensuring that protection principles are mainstreamed and integrated in the response, and that special attention is paid to the needs of the most vulnerable. Initiatives to raise awareness on and prevent gender-based violence (GBV) have been launched across all fields. Communication with communities will be supported through dedicated hotlines to provide information on available services. The Agency has also reviewed guidance from the Inter-Agency Standing Committee on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) in the context of COVID-19, which will inform the response.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

This project is targeting the most vulnerable Palestine refugees in the prolonged socio-economic situation regardless of the targets’ area of residences in the Gaza Strip. The cash assistance will be transferred to the banks, and the beneficiaries will be able to collect it from the nearest bank for safe access avoiding unnecessary movement. The cash support reduces the burdens of poverty while relieving strained families and enhancing their sense of dignity and self-reliance. Agency’s multi-dimensional eligibility criteria system is created to identify the targeted beneficiaries who are most vulnerable. This project ensures gender equality in its planning, operations and monitoring.
