Project Summary

"This project is designed to support the objectives of the Shelter-CCCM Sector by providing equitable shelter, non-food items (NFI), and camp management services to the population living in Cox’s Bazar camps. By addressing physical and protection risks alongside climate adaptation efforts, the project focuses on improving access to safe, sustainable energy and building resilience within the affected communities.
The project prioritizes the development of camp management skills through the Capacity Sharing Initiative (CSI) and continued empowerment of the refugee volunteers. This modular training equips camp management personnel with the necessary skills to meet the operational requirements of the camps. In collaboration with the Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commissioner (RRRC), Camp-in-Charges (CiCs), and other partners, UNHCR supports adherence to camp management standards and coordination of all actors, as reflected in the ISNA 2024 findings that highlighted good satisfaction level on site management services. Given the temporary nature of camp infrastructure, the project focuses on maintaining critical structures such as pathways, bridges, and drainage systems to ensure safety and accessibility. Additionally, shelter enhancements, such as piloting two-story shelters, are included to improve space utilization and structural integrity. The distribution of essential materials like bamboo, tarpaulins, ropes, and tie-down kits will strengthen shelters, particularly during high-wind monsoon seasons. Emergency shelter and NFI stocks will be strategically pre-positioned, based on assessments such as the Inter-Sectoral Needs Assessment (ISNA), to enable timely and targeted response to emergencies. Furthermore, as climate-related risks such as fires and extreme weather events become more frequent, the project focuses on improving shelter resilience by using fire- and weather-resistant materials that enhance safety and reduce environmental impact. Through MICRO and MESO settlement planning, the project ensures the integration of sustainable practices and multisectoral approaches to strengthen resilience.
The project emphasizes a strong commitment to disaster preparedness, ensuring that personnel, equipment, and essential supplies are readily available for rapid mobilization during crises. Disaster Risk Management (DRM) includes continuous staff training, simulation exercises, and the strategic prepositioning of emergency supplies, improving the ability to respond quickly and effectively. Additionally, resource optimization is achieved through improved LPG refill cycles and the promotion of pressure cookers, which reduce environmental impact and enhance cost-effectiveness through data-driven decisions.
The project encourages active community participation by implementing feedback mechanisms, providing mobile services, and supporting marginalized groups such as persons with disabilities, youth, women, and the elderly. Cash-for-work initiatives and support for community-led projects help build ownership and participation. Joint efforts between refugee and host communities are promoted, supported by the Common Feedback Platform, which ensures inclusive and transparent service delivery, fostering peaceful coexistence and responsiveness to community needs."

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

UNHCR and its partners conduct regular, sector-specific assessments to guide response strategies within their Area of Responsibility (AoR). Despite progress in addressing shelter and non-food item (NFI) needs and improving living conditions, significant gaps remain due to operational challenges and funding reduction. Settlement planning has become critical in light of funding reductions, climate change impacts, and challenging conditions in the camps, such as limited access to services and risks of landslides, floods, and fires. Given the high risk of fires and extreme weather, exploring fire- and weather-resistant materials for shelters is increasingly necessary. Currently, shelters are primarily constructed from bamboo and tarpaulin, materials highly vulnerable to fire and extreme weather conditions. Currently, only 13.5% of the 92,332 households in UNHCR-managed camps (approximately 12,537 shelters) have been upgraded with improved materials, such as metal footing, treated bamboo, and reinforced pillars. Consequently, 86.5% of households still require higher-quality shelter assistance. UNHCR aims to support 50% of these households (46,166 shelters) with regular repairs and plans to construct 4,000 additional improved shelters across the camps. An emergency stock of 18,000 shelters will be maintained to support households affected by unforeseen incidents. UNHCR's shelter approach will also include more community engagement including the involvement of more women in the construction process for example in the production process of materials. Approximately 65,000 individuals residing in high-risk areas (landslides and flooding) in Ukhiya require protection. To build climate resilience, UNHCR will implement urgent repairs on 182 km of slope protection and construct an additional 132 km of durable infrastructure, subject to government approval. Where feasible, nature-based solutions will be prioritized, supplemented by civil engineering for larger projects. Flood mitigation efforts include the maintenance of 297 km of drainage and the construction of an additional 82 km. UNHCR will continue implementing essential site development interventions, coordinating with government officials, partners, and refugees to prioritize the repair and maintenance of critical infrastructure. Continued support is vital for the Government (RRRC, CiCs) to strengthen camp administration and management capacities, including enhanced coordination for comprehensive service provision at the camp level. The ongoing distribution of NFIs, including solar lamps and LPG, and training on energy-efficient cooking methods remain essential, as does regular maintenance of primary and secondary drains. Pre-positioning of shelter and NFI materials will improve rapid response capabilities in emergencies. Other key priorities also include Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), environmental restoration, and watershed management. Community-led initiatives for slope stabilization and nature-based solutions will foster environmental rehabilitation, biodiversity protection, and long-term resilience. Finally, UNHCR’s response strategy emphasizes inclusive engagement, accountability, and empowerment, ensuring that community members are involved in decision-making, have access to feedback mechanisms, and that marginalized groups are targeted for assistance.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Protection will be mainstreamed across all Shelter-CCCM interventions, ensuring that response efforts prioritize saving lives, enhancing safety and security, alleviating suffering, and preserving dignity for women, girls, boys, and men, especially those in marginalized groups such as the elderly, persons with disabilities, and minority groups. UNHCR and its partners will apply gender-sensitive quantitative data analysis to monitor beneficiary satisfaction, participation, acceptance, knowledge, and practices. UNHCR’s response will maximize protection by focusing on the most vulnerable groups facing protection risks. Additionally, activities such as Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), provision of safe cooking energy, reforestation, and climate action will support the overall protection and sustainability of affected populations.

2.3 - Age & Disability

This project aligns with the JRP Guidance Note on age and disability by prioritizing accessibility, resilience, inclusive shelter solutions, and meaningful community engagement across all activities. UNHCR integrates infrastructure and support mechanisms to ensure equitable access for all, particularly children, elderly individuals, and persons with disabilities, recognizing their equal right to site management services. Accessible infrastructure is prioritized in all 16 camps, including pathway maintenance and the development of new access routes to support safe service access, especially for those with mobility challenges. By reinforcing slopes, enhancing drainage, and applying a mix of engineering and nature-based solutions, the project reduces risks for older persons and those with disabilities. Shelter upgrades, including resilient materials and double-story designs, enhance accessibility and flexibility for those with unique needs. UNHCR’s advocacy for durable, eco-friendly materials supports lasting inclusivity, while active community engagement allows individuals with disabilities to influence planning. This inclusive approach strengthens resilience and ensures that the needs of persons with disabilities are central to all project outcomes, creating a safe, accessible environment for all camp residents.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

This proposal aligns with the shelter, NFI, and Camp Coordination and Camp Management needs identified by Rohingya refugees in the 2024 Inter-Sector Needs Assessment (ISNA). The project will ensure that beneficiary selection processes and criteria are communicated clearly through regular engagement in the Rohingya language. Inclusion will be reinforced by involving community groups across age, gender, and diversity (AGD) categories and by enhancing mobile Complaints and Feedback Mechanisms (CFM) to reach marginalized groups, including persons with disabilities, youth, women, girls, and the elderly, in coordination with Camp in Charge officers (CiCs). To foster meaningful participation, community groups—such as men, women, older persons, neighborhood groups, youth, community leaders, Female Religious Teachers (FRTs), and community volunteers—will be actively involved in all phases of the project cycle, including work planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation. Any project adjustments due to funding changes, CFM feedback, or research findings will be communicated to refugees in advance through audio, video, and simplified pictorial formats in the Rohingya language.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Provision of Shelter- CCCM services in addressing specific needs of Rohingya refugees is a fundamental principle of protection and gender-based violence (GBV) risk mitigation. Assessment of the needs of the various communities, strengthened communication with the commuity through various community structures including camp committee, thematic and neighborhood groups, will focus on gender-based needs to determine appropriate refferal and response in coordination with various service providers. Assistance to persons with special needs and risks associated with gender, age and diversity will be assessed through participative and inclusive risk mapping focused on the provision of basic services, as well as undertaking safety audits across all sectors. UNHCR will ensure ongoing information sharing between site management, site planning and development, shelter, protection and GBV field staff in the targeted areas, and ensure appropriate training of the partners and community volunteers on gender and age equality and GBV mainstreaming.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

In response to the increasing frequency of seasonal disasters in Bangladesh, UNHCR integrates climate adaptation strategies with disaster preparedness and infrastructure improvements to ensure long-term resilience. The project also includes climate adaptation efforts like slope protection, shelter relocation, and watershed rehabilitation to mitigate the risks of landslides and floods. Nature-based solutions aligned with the natural environment reduce environmental impact and enhance the resilience of both refugee and host communities. Projects like habitat restoration, disaster risk reduction, and biodiversity conservation will support a sustainable approach to camp management.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Given the high vulnerability of the refugee camps in Cox's Bazar to climate-related disasters, UNHCR is part of Core Team of the EPR WG and has incorporated emergency planning, aligned to the Multi-Hazard Response Plan -2022 and relevant SoPs, to address potential crises such as cyclones, flooding, fires, Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD), cholera outbreaks, dengue, and the shelter, NFI and site development needs of potential new arrivals.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

UNHCR, and its partners actively involves youth through a dedicated youth group representing all blocks within the camps under UNHCR’s management. Guided by a jointly developed Terms of Reference, this group supports community communication and leads initiatives in emergency preparedness, including responses to monsoons, cyclones, and fires. Youth members receive targeted training to build skills for preparedness and resilience and participate in neighborhood meetings led by volunteers to raise awareness and address camp issues. Monthly meetings focus on emerging concerns, underscoring a strong commitment to youth engagement and capacity-building in community resilience and preparedness.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

The construction of improved shelters and repair support ensures that refugees have safe and secure homes, vital for their well-being. Site development activities, including building access roads, pathways, bamboo bridges, stairs, ramps, drainage systems, and protection walls, create a more organized and livable environment. These interventions address immediate needs while reducing disaster risks and enhancing community resilience. Camp coordination and management are essential for the effective delivery of services such as NFI distribution, healthcare, and education. Involving refugees in these activities fosters community participation, leadership, and self-reliance. As a result, shelter improvements, site development, and efficient camp management play a key role in enhancing the resilience and self-sufficiency of Rohingya refugees in Cox's Bazar camps.
