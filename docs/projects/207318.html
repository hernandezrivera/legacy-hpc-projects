Project Summary

For more than seven years now, NW and SW regions have been the center of violence that has resulted in the displacement of over 630,000 individuals (UNICEF, July 2023). The conflict in the NW is highly characterized by violent clashes between government forces and Non-State Armed groups (NSAGs) and inter-communal violence, that leads to civilians’ killings, kidnapping for ransom, arbitrary arrests, destruction of properties and the disruption of markets food and nutrition services due to violence. The conflict has aggravated the humanitarian situation with needs ranging from immediate food, protection, shelter/NFI, water and sanitation as well as access to health and education. Forced displacements have been either as a result of destruction of property or threats to life. In some areas, whole villages have been razed down and livelihoods destroyed. Seven years into the crisis, some displaced persons and household are seeing the need to return to their communities and to start afresh. Shelter constitutes a major asset that can enhance the quality of life for households returning to their communities. There is thus a need to support affected persons who are willing to return to reestablish their basic shelter that can enable them settle.
COMINSUD plans to work in line with the HRP 2023 SHELTER / NFI SECTOR LOGICAL FRAMEWORK SNFI OBJ 01: Provide life-saving shelter and non-food item (NFI) support focusing its intervention to contribute to objective 3.1: Increase in adequate housing stock available to targeted crisis-affected household, including integrated IDPs, returnees and not displaced people including host communities
The project activities will address : Support to sustainably construct/repair/rehabilitate housing and related community/public infrastructure and facilities that meet environmental criteria. Activity indicators: # of HHs assisted by constructed/repaired/rehabilitated house/apartment
The following performance indicators are envisioned # of HHs assisted by constructed/repaired/rehabilitated infrastructure and facilities
In implementing this action COMINSUD will be placing a special emphasis on women and girls especially as existing gender roles and power dynamics most often discriminate against them, and create significant obstacles to their ability to access safe housing. This action proposed by COMINSUD aims to meet the needs of the targeted especially returnees affected by the crisis effectively and equitably. This will be achieved by ensuring that in the identification of the 500 planned beneficiaries, consideration will be given to reach out to at least 50% women headed households, 2% youth headed households, 4% consideration to households headed by aging persons and 2 % to households headed by persons living with a disability. The local committees set up for the purpose of this action shall equally reflect equal participation of men, women, youths, older persons and persons with a disability.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

In 2022 information gathered showed Returnees who return to find their homes damaged or destroyed: The OCHA MSNA estimates that 53% of villages are partially damaged in the Northwest and 31% of villages are partially destroyed in the Southwest. This same source using a key informant methodology estimates that 27,115 returnee households have had their homes partially damaged, with over 21,000 of those households being located in the Northwest Region. Beyond these numbers, the Plan household estimates that 58% of returnees in the Northwest region and 40% of returnees in the Southwest region have reported damage to their homes. Housing is an essential aspect of durable solutions and houses that are not able to protect people from the elements puts them at risk for displacement. In addition to those who have already returned to their area of origin, 20% of IDPs in the Northwest and 31% of IDPs in the Southwest region have the intention of returning to their area of origin in the next year. In addition to this data from the Plan Household assessment, Dedi, a national NGO based in the West region found that 25% of IDPs displaced from the Northwest and Southwest regions to the West had the intention to return between one month to over a year.There has been a recorded increase in the number of people returning home both from situations of internal displacement elsewhere in the two regions or from the West or Littoral regions. Returnees are often returning to homes that are damaged. New estimated figures from the OCHA Multi-sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) conclude that there are now an estimated 366,300 IDPs, with 234,100 IDPs in the Northwest region and 132,300 IDPs in the Southwest region. The MSNA also identified an estimated increase in returnees with, 371,900 returnees, with 238,600 returnees in the Northwest region and 133,600 in the Southwest region. The HNO identifies support for shelter repairs especially for returnees as one of its priorities.To this end, COMINSUD plans to implement activities in the NW that will contribute to improve shelter for affected returnee households. In 2023 the Shelter cluster targeted: 53,244 HHs out of the total 238,600 returnees in the NW (2023 Humanitarian Response Plan). COMINSUD’s target in its 2024 intervention is 500 household comprising both sexes (250 females and 250 men) to assist them in shelter repairs.
