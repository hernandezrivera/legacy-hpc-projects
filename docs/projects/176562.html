Project Summary

The project goal is to address identified critical protection concerns and gaps through service delivery (identification and assessment, targeted protection assistance, comprehensive case management (including individualized care plans, accompanied referrals, and advocacy), capacity building and strengthening of community-based protection mechanisms through protection monitoring and increased focus on integrated general protection-MHPSS activities for the most vulnerable population in South Sudan.

1 - Needs

Across South Sudan, people with disabilities are disproportionately affected by the crises with limited access to services and material assistance. In addition, the country has a high prevalence rate of post-traumatic stress disorder among the population – given the linkages between protection and mental health needs, an integrated response is required to support the most vulnerable in target locations. HI’s participatory protection monitoring activities have identiﬁed substantial protection needs among the population of people with speciﬁc needs (PSNs), including but not limited to persons with disabilities. These common protection risks include: lack of early identiﬁcation of persons with disabilities among mainstream humanitarian service providers; low levels of participation of PSNs in camp coordination/leadership structures; theft, bribery, coercion, and physical violence targeting PSNs at higher rates; inaccessibility of humanitarian services, such as activity sites that lack physical accommodation for people with mobility impairments and food distribution agencies not allowing for prioritized distribution to people at risk; lack of livelihood opportunities and insuﬃcient material assistance—particularly for new arrivals —forcing women and girls to seek dangerous work in the informal economy; and low levels of social acceptance of disability and mental health issues, leading to discrimination even extending to service providers. As such, there is need for on-going protection monitoring and assessment, comprehensive case management support to individuals with heightened protection risks/needs, psychosocial support particularly aimed at fostering social connections and community cohesion, as well as diverse forms of awareness raising on mental health, disability, services and referral pathways.

2 - Project activities

Activities are designed to mitigate the risks and address the immediate and long-term protection and mental health needs of acutely vulnerable populations (with and without disabilities), whilst mainstreaming inclusion in the wider protection response.The project will provide direct MHPSS and protection services to identified at risk persons including individual and group based MHPSS counselling, protection monitoring and referral of protection cases to specialized services.To ensure large coverage, inclusive access to services and sustainability of the approach, HI will also carry out capacity-building activities, and especially:*Capacity building and strengthening of OPDs, * Capacity-building of community leaders and focal points on Disability Inclusion & MHPSS* Capacity building of humanitarian actors and strengthening of cluster system on disability inclusion* Capacity-building and strengthening of duty-bearers and key stakeholders on Disability Inclusion & MHPSS to strengthen systems.In addition, awareness raising activities will be carried out on Disability Inclusion and MHPSS To complete the approach, HI is aiming at promoting the implementation of the National Disability & Inclusion Action Plan as well as the ratification of the UNCRPD through active advocacy.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

HI has a robust feedback and complaints mechanism for beneciaries that combines both unsolicited communication channels such as suggestion boxes, text and phoneline as well as an anonymous reporting email service, with solicited forms such as monthly focus group discussions, interviews and postintervention monitoring surveys. Detailed sensitization of communities on when, how and why to report is critical for the functioning of the system, as is HIs internal review, response and feedback protocols – designed to close the loop. To ensure the most vulnerable community members can access feedback and complaints mechanisms, HI will include considerations such as the use of local languages, reasonable accommodation for persons with disabilities (i.e. accessible venues, sign-language interpretation, sighted guides, accessible information materials), as well as genderspecic mobilization and messaging to minimize any barriers these key groups encounter. Moreover, HI is proposing many entry-points for feedbacks/complaints to reach all: 1. In-person oce visit to FCMS Desk 2. HI community & group meetings 3. Complaint/Feedback Box 4. Toll-free telephone Hotline and SMS 5. HI email address 6. Beneciary Satisfaction Surveys

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Any complaint related to HI programming and areas of operation that is nonsensitive is categorized as an operational complaint. This includes but not limited to complaints such as: • dissatisfaction with the provided goods and services • quality and quantity of the distributed items • failure to provide timely assistance or request for additional assistance • issues related to elilgibility, registration, or distribution • lack of general project information • non-serious, yet impolite or irresponsible behaviour of HI staff or partner staff Operational or a programme relevant complaints are critical for the FCMS focal point as they need to track them until they are resolved and closed. For operational complaints, the procedure is as follows: 1. Operational complaint is forwarded as email to the relevant project/program manager, copying area manager or Senior Project Liaison Ocer (PLO) and where appropriate technical specialist of respective sector (with MEAL Manager in cc) on the same day of receiving the complaint 2. Complainant receives a written receipt (see External Acknowledgement Form) in their preferred modality: paper copy, email, or SMS text message within max. 7 calendar days 3. Relevant Program staff reviews the complaint, brings forward recommendations for action, and shares these with Complaints Focal Point and/or MEAL Manager. Some complaints may require Country Director oversight or consultation. 4. Complaint Desk providesfeedback to the complainant in his/her preferred modality: paper copy, email, or SMS text message, and informs about the appeals procedure within max. 21 calendar days The complainant has the right to appeal the feedback provided by HI within a period of maximum 30 calendar days. Appeals shall be dealt with on a case-by-case basis unless case-type precedence has been established. Complaints may require immediate action, for example an issue that poses a security threat to beneciaries, HI staff, HI resources/assets or HI reputation should immediately be acted upon. In such a scenario, the complaint focal point needs to contact the relevant person, either by phone or by visiting them (If they are in the same oce) such as area manager, safety advisor or his/her replacement and country director, deputy country directors etc. These managers should decide the future course of action by assessing the severity and the nature of the complaint. Following this, the complaint focal point should write an email containing all the details about the complaint provided by the complainant to ensure the formal submission along with adding this to FCMS centralised data sheet. All non-programmatic HI related complaints should be forwarded to the relevant staff head of the department by copying their immediate supervisor. The relevance of the complaint will be determined by the nature of the complaint as explained above. Operational complaints will be processed and delegated internally by the complaints and response focal person by forwarding the complaints and response log to the Area Manager. If the complaint entails a senior member of staff or is about the Area Manager/PLO, the complaint will be sent directly to the CD in Juba. If the complainst is about the CD, the complaints will be sent to the HQ via email : alert@hi.org However for most other programmatic complaints, the Area Manager/PLO will review the complaints listed and refer them by email to the relevant department and person For urgent action, the feedback will directly be communicated for immediate reaction. It will be included in the project action plan as soon as possible to ensure the problem will be solved timely. Regarding project/program related feedback - the feedback will be taken into account through usual coordination mechanism, including coordination meetings (including operations and support services), to ensure the project adapt to the feedback.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

8.10. Given that certain target locations particularly away from urban centers are heavily contaminated with cluster munitions, ERW and UXOs it is critical that due diligence and coordination with mine-action partners is completed before conducting outreach activities in rural areas. The presence of explosive hazards in communities would heavily impact the delivery of activities, but also factor into risk awareness and livelihood approaches. 8.9 In all locations disputes over housing and land are expected, particularly given the nature of land rights and legislation South Sudan. The nature of conﬂict and displacement has also complicated claims to land, and led to expropriation of displaced populations property. To mitigate this effectively, HI will monitor HLP issues in communities. Strong coordination with HLP partners, as well as government is required to mediate issues effectively and mediate disputes. 8.2 In target locations there are high numbers of female IDPs living in insecure and unsafe settlements. HI will coordinate closely with GBV partners, camp management (if available), UNHCR and health providers to ensure post-incident support to survivors, as well as to develop solutions to address risks with protection partners. 8.8 Given that the project is intending to support vulnerable beneﬁciaries it is essential that needs-based selection criteria are adhered to. To mitigate external interference, HI will utilize standardized vulnerability and targeting criteria, and consult all local stakeholders on this process to avoid any bias in the process. Furthermore HI will work closely with community-based organisations and representatives of marginalized groups to ensure we reach the intended beneﬁciaries.
