Project Summary

Through involvement and participation of the targeted community, this intervention seeks to deliver increased and improved access to life-saving water, sanitation and hygiene services to the vulnerable populations in NBeG, Unity, Jonglei and WES areas of South Sudan in a manner that is responsive to their protection needs, especially from gender based violence (GBV) against women and girls as well as enhancing their nutrition. The populations in this area have been prioritized as the communities among the most affected by conflict, malnutrition and at risk of gender based violence among other challenges experienced in the country. To achieve the objectives of this intervention, IAS proposes to address the needs in a threefold approach, namely; To re-establish and improve access to water, sanitation and hygiene infrastructure for 120,000 vulnerable people by rehabilitation of non-functional water points at households, community and schools; improve the population's health and coping capacity against threats such as preventable water related diseases and gender based violence through increased knowledge of appropriate hygiene and sanitation practices delivered through community training and hygiene promotion activities; and introduction of nutritious vegetables and their effective farming methods to supplement the beneficiaries’ diet. The intervention will target IDPs, host communities, and under-served communities, in line with CERF life-saving criteria.
IAS South Sudan is guided by the Humanitarian Code of Conduct, adheres to the 4 core humanitarian principles of humanity, neutrality, independence and equality. Furthermore, IAS South Sudan is working towards the application of the Core Humanitarian Standards and is guided by the Sphere minimum standards.

Needs

The planned intervention will be partly a continuation of the ongoing humanitarian response efforts in increasing access to WASH services, but also enhanced to respond to the evolving needs of the affected populations in nutrition, as well as ensuring that measures are mainstreamed in the WASH services towards protection against gender-based violence (GBV). The activities will target vulnerable populations that remained unreached in the 2018 response. This will also enable a better consolidation of gains made in the 2018 response towards increased resilience of the communities, thus providing a good foundation for subsequent development interventions. The extended history of armed conflict in the country, environmental and climatic shocks such as increased flooding and dry spells have continued to negatively impact local populations from many fronts. These coupled with frequent epidemics as well as the worsening economic hardship has led to a protracted humanitarian crisis among the local populations in areas such as the ones targeted by this response. Lack of access to clean water and the weak health system have further contributed to high morbidity and deaths from preventable diseases such as diarrhoea. While sceptical on the recent peace agreement signed between the main belligerents, most South Sudanese are anxious to see the outcome and hope that there will indeed be implementation that brings lasting peace.The main needs to be addressed by this intervention among the targeted communities are prioritized as those under;• Lack of access to clean water safely and within 30 minutes distance.• Risk of outbreaks of diseases associated with poor sanitation• Poor diets leading to malnutrition• Increased gender-based violence

Activities

The activities under this intervention will include;• Construction of new water points• Rehabilitation of non-functional water points• Conversion of high yielding boreholes to solar powered mini water yards• Training of water user committees and hygiene promotion• Distribution of menstrual hygiene kits• Distribution of WASH NFIs• Construction of sanitation facilities

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project will mainly aim to increase access to safe WASH services to the targeted population. The distance between the water points and the beneficiaries will be considered and expected to meet the threshold of less than 30 minutes walk.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Upon consultation with the beneficiaries including women and girls who are mostly the water fetchers, the project will design its services to include protection and safety features. These specific features will be considered during monitoring.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Privacy and dignity of the WASH services users will be an important feature of the project. This will also be considered in the design, implementation and monitoring stages.
