Project Summary

The main aim of this project is to contribute to coordinated efforts to prevent the spread of nCovid19 virus among most vulnerable men, women, girls, and boys among Rohingyas in camps and affected host communities. The first is to support sustaining and enhanced JRP 2020 objectives and critical lifesaving activities while mitigating gender and protection risks in the COVID pandemic context through enabling operational capacity, back up reinforcement, and approach modifications. Secondly, it will cover additional WASH needs created by Covid19 in camp and host communities such as the provision of additional water and distribution points, COVID handwashing stations, and sustaining trained and oriented community-based volunteers and workforce to maintain WASH services and facilities. Protection and limiting exposure to the virus for frontline workers and volunteers through training & orientation, rotating facing communities, provision of PPEs as appropriate to the job.
In addition to sustaining and enhancing JRP 2020 critical WASH – water supply, sanitation/SWM/FSM, and HP & CE which will cover by AFAs - following are the Covid specific broad lines of activities Oxfam is proposing
• Modify/adapt- based on on-going consultations with women, men, girls, and boys:- water points for social distancing, provision of disinfection of non-pipe water (handpumps).
• Provide soap, hygiene items, disinfectants both in camps and Host’s communities according to different needs of women, men, girls, and boys
• Organise disinfection of WASH facilities and common spaces frequented by women, men, girls and boys
• Increase coverage and operationalization of hand-washing stations both in camps and host’s communities with consumables and equal male-female attendants as appropriate
• Strengthen hygiene promotion focusing on Covid-19 incorporating safety and social distancing coupled with behavior change in redistributing the role of care work in the households
• Capacity building and protection (PPE) of staff, equal numbers of male and female community volunteers to work in Covid context to ensure services & interventions benefit women, men, girls and boys equally
• Back up capacity by enrolling and orienting 2nd line volunteers and care & maintenance workers for lockdown context, with an equal gender ratio
• Trained and equip WASH rapid response team(s) to respond to emergencies in 24 hours that ma includes WASH services installation for health facilities, quarantine area, isolation outside redline, based on consultations with women, men, girls, and boys.

Needs

Women and girls with their roles in the family have more burden in stay at home protocols and reduced and risky access to WASH facilities. As risks for COVID increase, increased burden on care work, caregivers of children and ill persons at home, and as primary water collectors in crowded water points put them at risk, where social distancing is challenging for persons with disabilities. Access to WASH facilities is likely to add risks to all sex and age groups in context of reduced humanitarian access. Men and boys on the other hand as more likely to have mobility in markets and in mosques are at-risk for contracting the virus. Such trends are likely to exacerbate as health services are stretched, and care burden will fall on women and girls at the household level. Women and girls are highly likely to be at an increased risk for sexual abuse and exploitation, IPV, and other forms of domestic violence. Deep-seated socio-cultural gender norms have conflated with beliefs surrounding the COVID-19 disease, which put the blame on women and their behaviors. Susceptibility to the disease is a punishment, and piety as prevention. This is likely to exacerbate the stigma already experienced based on gender, age, sex, and disability. Oxfam’s internal gender analysis finds that the current design, delivery and reach of COVID-19 messages are not adapted and don’t equitably reach all sex groups and the most vulnerable among them. For instance, in both Rohingya and host communities, women were reported to know less about COVID than men – a result of disproportionate gender access, gaps in message delivery and socio-cultural norms. Unequal access to information is likewise experienced by elderly, persons with medical conditions, and with disability. Being excluded from community and formal decision making, women & girls have less opportunity to be meaningfully engaged by humanitarian actors, despite having capacity to do so (some informal and formal leaders, self-organized groups & networks) – and is seen to be worse given telecommunication blockage and reduction of humanitarian workers in camps. 54% of the refugees have access to chlorinated water supply through small water networks completed by huge numbers of handpump operated shallow and deep tube-wells. Access to adequate sanitation remain a challenge in the camps mostly due to the lack of space, maintenance and improvements of sanitation facilities including latrines. Due to gaps in fecal sludge treatment facilities in Camp 22, some latrines may become unusable. Bathing facilities (40 persons per shower), fecal sludge management units and solid waste management remain critical activities as Covid-19 survives in sludge and wastewater requiring extra-care for treatment.Hygiene promotion need to be strengthened to increase awareness of the refugees, and content and delivery of awareness messages have to be tailor-fit to needs on gender, age and disability. Distribution of hygiene kits including specific items needed by women and girls and vulnerable, soaps and aqua-tabs will be increased to make sure of full and regular coverage in the camps. Finally, WASH needs to respond to increasing demand for new facilities and increased water quantity in the health centers and isolation facilities upon request by the Government or Health sector. Specific hygiene kits adapted based on consultations women and girls, men and boys in isolation wards are provided by WASH partners. Faecal sludge treatment capacity is currently 50% after phasing out emergency lime-based treatment and challenges constructing appropriate systems under JRP in COVID context. Half of the latrine may become unusable if urgently not restarted in COVID context. Water points in JRP not designed for social distancing and crowd control ar challenging for women and girls as primary water collectors. Displacement/relocation, boat survivors, quarantine space- will require WASH services to adapt to the different needs by gender.
