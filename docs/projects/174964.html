Project Summary

The barriers that prevent people living with disabilities from living a normal life with respect to access and service delivery is an aged old problem that has for so long kept people with disabilities from living secured and dignified lives especially within low and middle-income countries. According to March 2020 statistics (by Disabled World) about 10% of the world’s population lives with disabilities. That is about 1 in every 10 persons, however, women and girls with disabilities constitute 1 in 5 women globally, and up to three quarters of persons with disabilities in low- and middle-income countries. Despite their prevalence in the population, they face multiple forms of discrimination in all areas of life such as access to services and resources, social protection, and social contribution. About 80% of women and girls in Cameroon living with disabilities are marginalized especially in rural communities and urban centrals. The socio-political crisis which has hit Cameroon for the past 4 years has further contributed in bringing untold suffering to these persons especially poor women and girls living with disabilities. Those of which are IDPs and from ethnic minorities suffer even more hardships and unequal treatment. Gender disabilities and structural inequalities which characterized societies during the crisis are being exacerbated by the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic that hit Cameroon in May 2020. People with disabilities in hard-to-reach areas of the crisis zones lack access to proper information on the COVID-19 pandemic as a result of predominant use of non-disability-sensitive information mechanisms; and thus, are ill-equipped to protect themselves from the disease. For them, the everyday barriers to COVID-19 information and service accessibility is life threatening in the midst of the ongoing socio-political crises. Women and girls with disabilities face even higher risk of infection from COVID-19 (given that stigmatization and other societal ills prevent them from advancing in education and thus limiting their understanding of COVID-19 information even that which is available to them) and deserve the right to access relevant information that can contribute to their well being and those of their loved ones.
In view of these gaps, this project (with a goal to provide lifesaving aid to persons living with disabilities in the South West region) sets out to simplify, develop and distribute COVID-19 information in disability friendly formats and popular local languages as well as distribute COVID-19 kits to some 500 people living with disabilities and enhance the capacity of five (5)health facilities and workers within the project area in dealing with people living with disabilities within the context of the COVID-19 pandemic.
To realize the full extent of this project, the following activities are in perspective
Act #1: To simplify, develop and distribute COVID-19 information in disability friendly formats and popular local languages within the project area.
Act #2: To provide COVID-19 kits to 500 persons living with disabilities within the project area
Act #3: To enhance the capacity of 5 medical facilities and 200 health workers within the project areas in dealing with people living with disabilities within the context of the COVID-19 pandemic.

Plan fields

1 - Besoins / Needs

Provision of COVID-19 Information to People living with disabilities: Since the outbreak of the COVID-19 pandemic within the national territory of Cameroon, spread mitigative strategies, as well as safety mechanisms have been tailored to suit the majority of the population who are without visible disabilities. This is evident in the numerous distribution of COVID-19 information via posters and social media uploads which are totally negatively biased towards the minority group made up of people with disabilities. The result is poor information and misinformation via uncensored sources among people with disabilities, this clearly highlights the high risks faced by people with disabilities. This increases their chances of getting in contact with the virus and later on act as vectors of their own to the entire Civil Society Organization
