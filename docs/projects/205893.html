Project Summary

Sudan Evangelical Mission (SEM) will implement these proposed activities in Ibba, Maridi, Mundri East, Mundri West, and Jur River Counties of South Sudan. We shall reach a total of 10,650 (1,850 boys, 2525 girls, 2500 men, 3,775 women) beneficiaries from internally displaced persons, returnees, and host communities in all the target locations.
The goal of this project is to fulfill the HRP strategic objective of vulnerable crises affected people have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable and dignified access to life-saving assistance to meet their needs. We shall achieve our goal through implementing activities under cluster objective PC1 – ensuring the mobile or static provision of critical protection assistance and services; PC 2 - ensuring that human rights are upheld and promoted for the persons of concern by strengthening coordination, and advancing centrality of protection; and PC 3 – to promote nexus to systems building, and the provision of long-term development assistance.
We shall increase access to GBV preventive and responsive services by conducting awareness-raising and prevention messaging on GBV targeting boys, girls, men, and women to pass key messages on gender-based violence, available services, and prevention/ harm reduction. The community awareness-raising sessions will enable durable solutions in the community by engaging community members in dialogues that promote respect for human rights and challenging cultural beliefs and practices that are used to justify gender violence and inequality. The communities will commit themselves to not tolerate GBV and for survivors to receive support.
Compliance with Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS) will ensure accountability to our beneficiaries by creating an avenue for Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) between this project and our target community. Our field-based case workers will be at the frontline of service delivery and day-to-day activity implementation and assisted by field supervisors who will be reporting to the project officers who share monthly reports on the progress of outputs and results with the project management team. The project officer will be monitoring the progress on each indicator on a quarterly basis to determine progress against them.
Dignity kits distributed by this project will provide a key entry point for understanding risks facing women and girls in communities for the community-based protection structures to take action to improve the safety of women and girls in the community. Girls with disabilities in schools those out of school, and women with disabilities will also be among the selected beneficiaries and ultimately benefit through improved well-being, hygiene needs, and health.
Mapping and updating the GBV referral pathways will provide frontline persons responding to GBV with a one-stop guide that includes all services presently being offered in their locations of operation. It will also promote the use of a survivor-centered approach that ensures survivors are active participants in defining their needs and deciding what options best meet their needs with the aim of improving the quality and timeliness of care received by GBV survivors.
Providing access to income-generating activities will be an essential way for crisis-affected women to cope with and recover from shocks as well as to provide safe alternatives for generating income. Women with disabilities and parents of children with disabilities will be among the selected beneficiaries.
This project will therefore achieve the outcome of having strengthened protection systems that give access to improved and equitable prevention and response services; build resilience in vulnerable boys, girls, men, and women by protecting them from stress and enhancing their mental health; and avail more income, increase well-being, reduce vulnerability, and improve food security among women and girls through livelihood activities.

1. Needs

Findings from an intersectoral needs assessment published in October 2023 in Wau Around 33.7 percent of the survey respondents reported that they needed and tried to access protection services. Around 19.7 percent of respondents were unsuccessful and 14 percent were successful in accessing protection services. Around 23.3 percent reported that protection services were unavailable, however, 47.5 percent reported that MHPSS services are available, in addition to services relating to rape treatment and treatment of physical injuries (37.6%), legal aid services (18%) and livelihoods services for women and girls (14.6%). Due to the insecure environment, 2.2 percent of the survey respondents reported that household members had to restrict their movement. The main reported reasons respondents were restricting their movement due to insecurity include sexual violence and death (50% each), followed by the presence of unexploded ordnance (25%), lack of identification documents (25%), and kidnapping (12.5%). Other findings from an inter-agency report on floods assessment in Mundri East and Mundri West Counties published in September 2022 revealed that there was poor menstrual management among the women and girls in the reproductive age groups, increased pressure on female-headed families due to limited access to food commodities and basic services and women and girls being at high risk of sexual abuse and exploitation due to sleeping in open spaces.Through this project providing a timely, and comprehensive case management process to boys, girls, women, and men including those with disabilities will help address the needs mentioned above. We shall put the rights of each survivor at the forefront of all actions and ensure that each of them is treated with dignity and respect. Service providers be oriented on disability inclusion for them to be responsive to the needs of persons with disabilities. The awareness will reduce the barriers faced by persons with disabilities in accessing mainstream services. Adopting this approach promotes their recovery, and reduces the risk of further harm. Therefore, we shall establish a relationship with the survivor that promotes their emotional and physical safety, builds trust, and helps them to restore some control over their life.This project will address the above needs by providing PSS activities that are quality and contextually appropriate for SGBV survivors, helping them heal, regain daily function, and re-integrate within their communities.We shall also avail opportunities for income generation to the women and girls to increase their access to livelihood needs mentioned above. We shall provide basic livelihood skills like baking, embroidery, and basketry and will avail an opportunity where 600 women will generate income to care for their families and build their resilience to economic hardships. We shall make use of our experience of promoting inclusion by selecting women with disabilities and parents of children with disabilities to be among the beneficiaries.According to the South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2023, inter-sectoral assessments conducted by humanitarian partners in flood-affected areas in Greater Equatoria, and Northern Bahr el Ghazal states indicated a lack of protection assistance, including dignity kits for displaced women and girls of reproductive age. We shall give additional training to women and girls with disabilities on menstrual hygiene management (MHM). This project will respond to the specific needs identified in the inter-sectoral assessments by the provision of dignity kits to help 1,350 (675 girls, 675 women) beneficiaries maintain proper hygiene to promote protection, physical and psychosocial well-being, mobility, and hygiene for women and girls at risk.

2. Project activities

This project will address cluster objective PC1 – ensuring mobile or static provision of critical protection assistance and services, including specialized response services, tailored to the specific needs, considering age, gender, and disability by conducting the following activities:1) Provision of the timely, and comprehensive case management process to 1200 (300 boys, 300 girls, 300 men, and 300 women) beneficiaries that will meet their needs from the point they were identified and assessed (# women, men, boys, and girls supported with specialized GBV response services (GBV case management, referrals, legal aid, safe houses & WGFS programming).2) Providing individualized and group psychosocial support and recreational services targeting 1,600 (300 boys, 300 girls, 500 women, and 500 men) beneficiaries according to their individual needs. We shall also have recreational activities targeting adolescent girls, boys, and women to connect socially with each other and create a platform where peer-to-peer support will be reinvigorated (# women, men, boys, and girls supported with individual and group Psychosocial support including reactional activities).3) Provision of dignity kits to help 1,350 (675 girls, 675 women) beneficiaries maintain proper hygiene to promote protection, physical and psychosocial wellbeing, mobility, and hygiene for women and girls at risk (# of women and adolescent girls who received dignity kits).4) Conducting mapping of service providers and updating 5 referral pathways and dissemination to communities to safely link survivors and those at risk of GBV to qualified, supportive, and competent services that they may need as a result of the violence experienced (# counties with functional GBV referral pathway).We shall address PC 2 - ensuring that human rights are upheld and promoted for the persons of concern by strengthening coordination, and advancing centrality of protection among humanitarian through conducting the following activities:5) Conducting awareness-raising and prevention messaging on GBV targeting 5,000 people (1,250 girls, 1,250 boys 1,250men, and 1,250 women) to pass key messages on gender-based violence, available services, and prevention/ harm reduction (# awareness-raising sessions conducted with women, men, boys, and girls on GBV risks, available response services and timely reporting).6) Provision of basic livelihood skills like baking, embroidery, and basketry will avail an opportunity where 600 women will generate income to care for their families and build their resilience to economic hardships (# of women, girls supported with GBV risk mitigation interventions (Income generating activities, fuel-efficient stoves, intergradation of GBV by other clusters and humanitarian actors).We shall address PC 3 – to promote nexus to development and solutions as the transition between the delivery of emergency humanitarian assistance, systems building, and the provision of long-term development assistance to meet peoples’ needs by conducting the following activities:7) Conducting training to 450 (225 women, 225 men) GBV humanitarian workers with a focus on changing their knowledge attitudes, and beliefs on gender, GBV integration (# of non-GBV humanitarian workers trained on GBV integration in their sectoral response).8) Strengthening 5 community-based protection structures and mechanisms that will work towards a common goal of preventing and responding to gender-based violence in their communities through the action plans that they will implement to build a protective, enabling, integrating, and peaceful environment (# of community-based protection structures established or enhanced).
