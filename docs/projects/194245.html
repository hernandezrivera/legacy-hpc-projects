Project Summary

Nile Hope (NH) is a national non-governmental Organization founded in 2004. It operates in South Sudan in the states of; Upper Nile, Unity Jonglei, Western Bahr el Ghazal and Warrap. The organization has its headquarters in Akobo County, Jonglei State and as well as satellite coordination offices in Juba and Nairobi. Nile Hope is an important player in the Education Sector, SS Education Cluster, strategic advisory group (SAG), NEC, Inter-Agency for Education in Emergencies (INEE) Network and NEAR Global network and continues to participate in critical forums and policy gatherings to generate such tools as the South Sudan Minimum Standards for Education in Emergencies (INEE). The education needs continue to be many and diverse therefore; the project is aimed to improve equitable access to quality, safe and protective and inclusive education services to 12,386 desegregated into 11,566 (6400 B, 5166 G) children and youth comprising of Host, IDPs, Returnee and including persons with disabilities in Akobo, Nasir, Uror and Pibor Counties of Jonglei and Upper Nile States of South Sudan. The project will equally support 360 teachers (260 M & 100 F and 460 PTA (260 M & 200 F). The program will seek to Improve equitable access to quality, safe and protective and inclusive education services among the crisis affected children and youths as an efforts to promote sustainable and equitable quality education with enrollment and attendance to learning, reduce risks of cognitive underdevelopment and malnutrition, and provide access to safe and protective learning environments. The response is aims at enrolling and retaining children in Pre-primary, Primary and secondary schools/learning centers, establish new TLS and rehabilitates new safe and protective learning spaces and damaged classrooms with gender segregated WASH facilities, water points (using locally available materials) for girls, boys and teachers; Enroll children in Pre-primary, Primary and secondary schools/learning centers, distribute teaching and learning, essential education supplies such as school¬-in-a ¬box kits, ECD kits, recreation kits, movable blackboards (procured from the Core-Pipeline); Distribute or provide sanitation and hygiene materials (hand washing soap, sanitizers, masks, etc.) and PPE for students and teachers; Provide emergency-affected children quality education recreational materials; Train teachers on EiE pedagogical skills and child-centers methodologies to improve teaching and learning process for emergency-affected children; Train and update teachers and learners on PSS, CP, and referral mechanisms including CP/GBV, health and nutrition; Provide life-skills and basic vocational training to youth and adolescents; Train SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management, governance, psycho social support (PSS) and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR)/ climate change; this will ensure proper management and maintenance of school infrastructure in their respective schools. Sustainably engage the communities through social mobilizations and awareness raising on the importance of education with emphasis on education of girls and children with special needs including pastoral communities. The project equally hopes to enhance psycho social development of learners besides improving hygiene and sanitation. The project will use its existing M&E system to monitor, provide reports including 5 WS, feedback, document, and share evident lessons.

1 - Needs

Education is a basic human right and as such, all children, regardless of gender or disability are entitled to access it. Education empowers people to live healthier and sustainable lives. It is also crucial for fostering tolerance between people and contributes to more peaceful societies, which is very relevant to the South Sudanese context where target communities have been exposed to protracted conflict, widespread flooding, deepening food insecurity, inflation, high food prices, and lack of access to basic services. In response to the above, Akobo and Uror falls under IPC phase 4 mean while Pibor is under IPC phase 5 (South Sudan: IPC Results October 2020 - July 2021). The insecurity in and around schools were the major reasons for learners absenteeism and drop-out in schools (Education Need Assessment (ENA), 2021). School functionality has also been severely impacted by flooding in 2021 and was mentioned as impediment for learner's access to school in the qualitative component of the FSNMS+ 2021. Jonglei, Unity and Upper Nile have been the worst impacted by the floods, and officials in these states reported in the ENA that over 190 schools closed due to flooding. Prior to COVID-19 school closures, about 2.2 million children were already out of school. The school closures further impacted the education of over 2.7 million additional children. In May 2021, after 14 months of closure due to COVID-19, schools in South Sudan re-opened. The current estimated number of out of school children is 2.8 million. Through this project, Nile Hope will deliver a holistic program that will improve equitable access to inclusive, quality education and create a conducive and safe learning environment for both boys and girls and children with disabilities (CWD) age 3 to 17 years). For decades, those affected by humanitarian emergencies in South Sudan, especially girls and young women, have been deprived of education (UNESCO, 2020).The project will facilitate establishment of new Temporary Learning Space and rehabilitates new safe and protective learning spaces and damaged classrooms with gender segregated WASH facilities, water points (using locally available materials) for girls, boys and teachers; Enroll children in Pre-primary, Primary and secondary schools/learning centers, distribute teaching and learning, essential education supplies such as school-in-a box kits, ECD kits, recreation kits, movable blackboards (procured from the Core-Pipeline); Distribute or provide sanitation and hygiene materials (hand washing soap, sanitizers, masks, etc.) and PPE for students and teachers; Train teachers on EiE pedagogical skills and child-centers methodologies to improve teaching and learning process for emergency-affected children; Train and update teachers and learners on PSS, CP, and referral mechanisms including CP/GBV, health and nutrition; Provide life-skills and basic vocational training to youth and adolescents; Train SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management, governance, psycho social support (PSS) and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR)/ climate change; this will ensure proper management and maintenance of school infrastructure in their respective schools. Sustainably engage the communities through social mobilizations and awareness raising on the importance of education with emphasis on education of girls and children with special needs including pastoralist communities. Enrolling more out of school early childhood education (ECE), primary and secondary level. Parent teacher associations (PTAs), school management committees (SMCs) and other stakeholders will be actively engaged to participate in these mobilizations and school-based campaigns and among others.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will enable affected populations in all categories including individuals living with physical impairments to play an active role in the decision-making processes that affect them through the establishment of clear guidelines and practices to engage them appropriately and ensure that the most marginalized and affected are represented and have influence.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will create a platform of engagement with the stakeholders and community leaders through meetings, Involve them in the planning and decision making on matters that promote the project. Establish held desk in school to enable the community share their views on how the project is impacting them.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

In order to ensure that the project eliminates the issues of sexual exploitation, the project will be guided by the organization code of conduct that stipulates on the guidelines and measures of how to deter and handle sexual exploitation cases and abuse. The project will employs the zero tolerance policy states: treating beneficiaries with dignity and compassionately is a matter of priority, there will be no act or behavior exploitation and abuse shall be condoned. Training will be conducted with teachers, PTA and SMC members, and other parties engaged in the project, on all issues relating to protection. Close collaboration and coordination will be carried out with protection teams in issues relating to protection, and all partners, participants, and children (girls and boys) will be made aware of the various organizational channels through which they can report or otherwise claim their grievances. Moreover, a key component of this project will be to highlight referral services and pathways, including the capacity development of formal, semi-formal, and non-formal teaching and learning institutions and spaces, as well as developing the capacity within the individual roles of teachers and other school personnel. This project will work in collaboration, consultation, and coordination with other partners in the area to consolidate workable and effective referral pathways for the survivors to access services without hindrance, as they are needed.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project will actively seek the views of affected populations to improve policy and practice in programming, ensuring that feedback and complaints mechanisms are streamlined, appropriate and robust enough to deal with (communicate, receive, process, respond to and learn from) complaints about breaches in policy and stakeholder dissatisfaction. For example, a complaints and feedback suggestion boxes will be placed at the school and in strategic public places identified and agreed by the community. Weekly, the project team will open and collect any feedback and complaints deposited in the suggestion boxes. A log of all feedback received will be kept and at the end of every month analysis of the log sheet is done to inform project modifications.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

Reduced flood related displacement in Akobo County and Pibor Administrative area by 30% in 2025. The project will achieve this through continuous sensitization and creating awareness on the best practices for flood mitigation, establishment of school infrastructures in areas safe for school going children as well as children with disabilities.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

Nile hope will coordinate efforts to sensitize school children including boys and girls and engage local community members and local leaders to ensure safety in and out of schools to mitigate GBV risks. The project team will proactively work with learners in primary and secondary schools as well as community members, PTAs, SMCs, community leaders, and women’s groups to identify potential risks to provide context-based solutions. Nile hope team and Counties Education Departments will work together to give support and guide the schools authority through these aspects to raise and ensure that school safety planning is given acceptable attention and an account for the needs of children with disabilities, girls, and learners trekking further distances from home to school on a daily basis as well as other vulnerable children. Sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and GBV risk mitigation will also be given additional emphasis through safety audits and protection concerns in schools. The local authorities and traditional leaders will be involved in this project to better understand and exercise their role(s) and enforce local laws where applicable.
