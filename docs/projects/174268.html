Project Summary

Plan International Nigeria will implement a 12-month project focusing on improving access to quality curative nutrition services and strengthening capacity for the management of severe acute malnutrition in Stabilization Centers (SC) and in Outpatient Therapeutic program (OTP). This will be done through screening & referral of treated Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) cases from SC to OTP, treatment of Complicated SAM cases in SC, treatment of uncomplicated SAM case in OTP, management of moderate acute malnutrition inTSFP and promoting Optimal IYCF-E (Infant and Young Child Feeding in Emergencies) in the SCs for Kaga, Konduga, Mafa, Hawul, and Chibok Local Government Areas (LGAs) in Borno state and Bade, Nguru, Yusufari, Jakusko and Bursari LGA of Yobe state. The project proposes to provide treatment of complicated SAM cases for a total of 2069 children under five-years of age in 6 Stabilization Centers (SC), 26, 625 children with uncomplicated SA, at OTP and promoting IYCF for PLW through care group model. The proposed activities will strengthen the management of acute malnutrition through CMAM approach by strengthening existing SC and OTP sites for IDP/host community and providing Optimal IYCF Promotion, providing technical and logistical support, as well as strengthening the supportive supervision in the LGAs. The project will focus on supporting the most vulnerable population groups, specifically children under five years of age and caretakers of SAM children and other vulnerable groups. Plan International will integrate management of acute malnutrition cases into existing health services in each general hospitals and primary health clinics and health center using SPHCDA and hospital staff in all targeted LGAs.
At project start-up, Plan International Nigeria will prioritize identifying Complicated and uncomplicated SAM cases to facilitate access to life-saving services for children under five years of age and to enable management of these cases in collaboration with State Primary Health Care and Development Agency (SPHCDA) and hospital board staff. We will strengthen the capacity of the government service providers and mothers/care takers to prevent malnutrition and respond to the treatment of SAM cases and we will work closely with government and partners working in each LGAs.
The project will strengthen accountability to beneficiaries through the establishment and strengthening of a community and facility -based complaints and feedback mechanism that will allow women, girls, men, and boys to safely report concerns of the emergency nutrition response in their respective Hospital.

1 - Needs

The October 2020 Joint Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance conducted in BAY States found the Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) and SAM rates in Borno state were at 10% and 1.5% respectively. GAM and SAM rates in Yobe were similarly at 12.3% and 1.6% respectively. In the same survey, in central Borno (Mafa, Konduga and Kaga) GAM and SAM rates by weight-for-height (WFH) survey depict 10.7% and 1.6% respectively. Furthermore, in southern Borno LGAs (Chibok, and Hawul) assessed GAM and SAM rates by a WFH survey were at 8.8% and 1.1% respectively. In central Yobe, (Bade, Jakusko, and Bursari LGA) the GAM and SAM rates by a WFH survey results were at 14.7% and 2.6% respectively and in Northern Yobe (Nguru, Yunusari, and Yusufari LGA) the GAM & SAM rates by Wfj Z score survey depicts 15.8% and 2.4% respectively. The IPC acute malnutrition analysis issued in February 2020 showed that central Yobe (including Bade) are classified as IPC 3(Serious) and require scale up of treatment. Another survey conducted by Save the Children in July 2019 in central Borno (Kaga and Konduga), found that GAM and SAM rates were at 21.1% and 5.5% respectively. The overall acute malnutrition rate in Borno and Yobe states is alarming, with the GAM rates reaching emergency levels which needs immediate humanitarian assistance and scale up of treatment. This situation is further aggravated by outbreak of disease during rainy seasons like malaria, AWD and cholera, extreme flooding and COVID-19 pandemic, poor ANC, delivery, PNC (maternal) nutrition care for women and children as well as low level of sanitation and health care, food insecurity, low rate of exclusive breast feeding, poor dietary diversity, and protection concerns related to new displacement following ongoing military operations.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Plan International promotes gender equality as a central part of our vision of change. Plan’s Global Policy on Gender Equality and Inclusion requires that all activities undertaken are based on an understanding of the underlying gender-issues and barriers that impede women and girls specifically in benefiting from project activities. Gender equality will be fully integrated throughout the project, starting from targeted inclusive implementation processes to staff recruitment and the participation of both men and women in project structures and evaluation processes. Women and men will be given equal chances during project staff recruitment, with women highly encouraged to apply for positions. In addition, because of their important role in successful community mobilization, the project will deploy as many female workers as possible. While all program staff are accountable for project gender equality and safe programming goals, a Gender and psychosocial support Officer will work closely with staff members to ensure gender and child protection standards integration into interventions.Training/orienting all project staff, partners and stakeholders on the gender-related barriers, child protection and building their accountability on safeguarding standards to address issues and concerns on risk of harm will also take place. In collaboration with the government, awareness/capacity building of health and child protection personnel participating in the project will be raised around the effects of gender inequality, power dynamics harmful traditional practices that have a direct impact on feeding practices and nutrition of children, especially girls as well as the differential experiences and effects of child protection and safeguarding issues on girls and boys through training. Activities proposed are specifically designed to better meet the needs of children of different age and gender groups. Project includes estimated sex and age disaggregated data for the target groups based on the findings of the needs assessment. Plan International Nigeria follows the principle of “Do No Harm” which will be considered in all of its activities, which requires the organization to take specific steps and measures to protect and reduce the risk of harm from all intervention processes and activities , such as participatory beneficiary selection, involvement of community members throughout the implementation, informing them about the intervention’s aims and activities, informed consent collection, conducting a risk assessment prior to engagement , as well as ensuring the existence of a functioning and responsive feedback and complaint mechanism at community level that allows timely action to address issues of concern that may cause or put beneficiaries at risk of harm, reputational risk or create conflict in the beneficiary communities.The Emergency Nutrition Project in Nigeria illustrates that it is vital that beneficiaries have a strong sense of ownership of the project and that the ability of men and women to assess their own situation including safety and their ability to take actions to improve their own and their children’s nutritional status. Moreover, identifying gender-differentiated opportunities and constraints for improving nutrition during the design phase of a project often leads to better interventions. In line with this, and building on our ongoing experience, sensitization on gender, child protection and safeguarding concerns is included in any training and awareness raising activity, especially during the inception workshop. Other sensitization measures include the mandatory onboarding signature of the Safeguarding Policy and Code of Conduct by Plan International Nigeria staff and associates, and the gender responsive safeguarding risk assessments conducted prior to engagement to mitigate all concerns of risk of harm

PLAN is a Humanitarian Accountability Partnership certified agency, and has ensured the involvement of beneficiaries in the assessment to identify the needs of target population and in designing the project. PLAN Nigeria will ensure its accountability to the beneficiaries by ensuring that they understand the procedures, decision-making processes and financial flows of the projects. PLAN will sign MOU with the concerned government partner organizations and facilitate launching workshop with the participation of local governments, community and beneficiary representatives for them to better understand project objectives, procedures as well as their roles and responsibilities in the course of project implementation. During execution of project activities, coordination with other humanitarian actors will be emphasized to extend the number of services. The team will also explain the beneficiary selection criteria, which are informed by the national protocol admission and discharge criteria for CMAM activities. It is mandatory to ensure that all beneficiaries are well informed about treatment and service provided in CMAM.
