Project Summary

The project aims to:
1. To ensure the provision of quality, lifesaving nutrition services for acutely malnourished children (boys and girls 6 - 59 months of age) and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) among highly vulnerable communities
2. Improve equitable access to integrated program interventions for the prevention of under nutrition among affected communities
The project targets children aged 6–59 months and Pregnant and Lactating Women and Girls with specialized nutritious foods and social and behaviour change messages aimed at the prevention and treatment of acute malnutrition across selected states and localities in Sudan.
A comprehensive package of food and non-food-based interventions are provided taking into account nutritional vulnerabilities.
The proposed response from WFP is in line with the Nutrition Sector Strategy and with the Multi-Year Humanitarian Response Strategy of Sudan. Through this project, WFP will be responding to the nutritional needs of targeted beneficiaries under Outcomes identified by nutrition sector.
With a particular focus on Do No Harm and Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) principles, WFP Sudan strives to enhance protection mainstreaming through the following priority areas: 1) Training and capacity building of key WFP and Cooperating Partners (CP); 2) protection risk analysis focused on safety, dignity and integrity of beneficiaries; 3) integration of protection indicators into program tools, notably Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) tools; 4) design, implementation and adjustment of programs in line with identified protection concerns particularly for those disabled and with special needs; and 5) strengthened partnerships and coordination on protection issues.

1 - Needs

COVID-19 is an unprecedented global health crisis, with particularly severe impacts on the lives of the most vulnerable, lockdown and quarantine strategies to mitigate the spread of the virus and the concurrent economic impact could double hunger (acute food insecurity) in Sudan and worldwide and drive up multiple forms of malnutrition, including child wasting, stunting, obesity, and the hidden hunger of micronutrient deficiency. Political unrest during 2019 and 2020 has negatively affected smooth implementation of many humanitarian response including nutrition which consequently reduce the access to nutrition programmes by beneficiaries to get services. Acute and chronic malnutrition are widespread and pervasive and constitute a significant public health problem. Over 2 million children under 5 suffer stunting annually, and 2 million wasting. There are several causes of undernutrition, including limited access to food, marginal and unstable livelihoods, poor quality of health services. Sudan Ministry of Health conducted national nutrition survey in 2018 (S3M II) which indicated slight reduction has been noted in the main nutritional indicators in the S3M II (2018) when compared to S3M I (2013) but the nutrition situation remains poor: The Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate remains concerning at 13.60%, out of which 10.78% are Moderately Acutely Malnourished (MAM) and 2.72% are Severely Acutely Malnourished (SAM). The prevalence of chronic malnutrition is 36.35%, meaning that one in every 3 Sudanese children do not grow to their full cognitive and physical potential. WFP uses the GAM prevalence’s at state and/or locality levels as entry points for the targeting approach for the nutrition interventions, mainly the life-saving Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programme (TSFP) and emergency nutrition response.

2 - Activities/Outputs

WFP activities are aligning with nutrition sector strategy outlines which composed of active case finding, screening and referrals of MAM& SAM cases to address their needs at nutrition respective services. 1. Provide preventive and curative nutrition activities to children aged 6–59 months and PLWG: Immediately following a shock and until relevant indicators stabilize, WFP will conduct emergency blanket supplementary feeding for all PLWG and children aged 6-59 months in affected populations, complementing general food distribution. 2. Provide curative and preventive nutrition activities to children aged 6–59 months and PLWG and capacity strengthening to national and state health institutions.3. Food-based prevention of malnutrition targeting the first 1,000 days of life to prevent stunting and wasting, consisting of nutrition rations for at-risk PLWG and children from 6-23 months, accompanied by context-specific nutrition-sensitive interventions4. Home fortification interventions through the distribution of micro nutrient powders free of charge to children 6-59 month 5. Social and Behavior Change Communication (SBCC) as an integral part of all prevention and treatment activities
