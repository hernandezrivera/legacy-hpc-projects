Project Summary

Through this project, YOPCODI seeks to improve the resilience of Children including children living with disabilities affected by emergencies through providing continuous equitable access to child protection services in Yunusari, Geidam, Bade, Gujba, Yusufari, Fune and Nangere. YOPCODI will implement the project through 3 result areas.
Result area 1:
Children at risk (boys and girls) including adolescents, children with disabilities access preventive and comprehensive quality response services in a timely manner, this includes provision of both access to quality to effective case management of MHPSS for children affected by emergencies, including children with disabilities through structured age-specific, creative and recreational manner including life skill education interventions for adolescents.
Result area 2:
Parents, caregivers, children, and communities promote positive social norms that protect children and women from violence, abuse, neglect, exploitation, and harmful practices through awareness creation, caregiver’s economic empowerment and positive parenting session to caregivers including caregivers of children living with disabilities.
Result area 3:
To improve sustainability, YOPCODI will enhance the capacities of child protection partners and social welfare workforce to deliver quality prevention and response services through a strengthened child protection system at community, LGA level, and state level.

More so, YOPCODI will engage government and community stakeholders to have an all-inclusive monitoring of project activities and also workshops will be organized to identify and discuss solutions to problems identified and develop action plans for efficient implementation.

1 - Needs

According to the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview (2022 HNO) 8.4 million people are in need of humanitarian support, the Protection Sector assesses that 4,572,018 People in Need (PiN) in North-East Nigeria require protection interventions, among them 409,030 IDPs in camps, 516,956 IDPs outside of camps, 973,163 Returnees, and 2,672,879 Host Community members. Protection needs differ by groups, the most affected from among the 4,5M are 1,432,169 girls, 1,253,701 boys, 1,001,345 women, and 884,803 men. This has increased needs for 2023 as compared to 2022 (4.3 million) is due to the following factors: secondary displacement of IDPs due to the government enforced “returns”/relocations into unsafe areas not allowing for self-sufficiency (movements, livelihoods, services etc.) To hasten and add, approximately 2.1 million people need child protection services due to the absence of child protection systems and community-based safety networks, of which 1.3 million children need child protection services, The child protection area of responsibility aims to reach 1.5m PiN (1,050,000 children 53% Girls and 450,000 caregivers 53% women. 13 years of war in the North-East Nigeria has deprived children of a normal childhood, exposing them to violence, abuse, neglect, exploitation, and conscription into NSAGs. The most vulnerable include children in IDPs camps, secondary displaced children from closed camps, returnee children, Children Associated with Armed Groups, unaccompanied/separated children, and children with disabilities. Girls are exposed to grave violations of children’s rights such as limited access to education, child marriage, sexual gender-based violence, emotional/ physical violence, and trafficking. Boys face the risk of forceful recruitment/abduction/re-recruitment by NSAGs and trafficking. Parents and caretakers have been exposed to stress /violence and need child protection services. Based on the Child protection Area of responsibility strategy 2022-24, that is flexible and caters for both humanitarian and development context given the different levels of humanitarian situation across BAY states will enable this project to bridge the humanitarian and development nexus while setting up a robust child protection system and fostering the localization agenda as part of the grand bargain where the Child Protection Area of Responsibility in Northeast Nigeria is quite a head. The concept seeks to build on this strategic direction to ensure comprehensive prevention and response services linked to the humanitarian country team centrality of protection strategy, the humanitarian response plan 2023 and the Child protection Area of Responsibility strategic priorities in the following Local Government areas of Damaturu, Gujba, Geidam and Yunusari LGAs in Yobe State that have been identified with the highest severity levels of needs by HNO 2023.

2 - Activities

This proposed project is designed to respond to the most critical child protection needs and gaps identified in Yunusari, Geidam, Bade, Gujba, Yusufari, Fune and Nangere. This is proposed to be delivered through the provision of psychosocial support through structured and mobile recreational facilities in child friendly spaces, provision of psychosocial support services, counselling, and positive parenting to caregivers, reinforcement of community-based child protection mechanism. 8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Understanding that women, men, boys, and girls are affected differently by disasters and displacement, the project will prioritise gender equity and the promotion of women's rights in all our humanitarian responses. YOPCODI policies recognize that SGBV is a violation of human rights and seeks to mainstream work to reduce SGBV and protection into all programs. YOPCODI has an internal gender policy, a SGBV policy and has minimum standards for gender in emergencies as a tool to measure consistency in promoting gender equality through interventions. The Organization trains its programme personnel in gender mainstreaming and has GBV and PSEA focal points that work closely together to mainstream gender into programmes and projects. All activities will incorporate considerations for gender and different needs of women, men, boys, and girls as well as people with disabilities.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

YOPCODI will work within the framework of accountability designed by the AAP/CE working group that enforces provision of information, involvement of the target community/persons in decision making process related to the humanitarian response and setting up of complaint mechanism and feedback loop during the implementation of the project. Therefore, complaints and feedbacks generated through such channels will be used to make necessary adjustments to program delivery. At inception of the project, a meeting with state and LGA level stakeholders will be held in the community for easy attendance by the beneficiaries including special interest groups such as persons with disability, women and girls to explain to the beneficiary groups and government partners the project deliverables, their roles and expected results of the project, and the change the project aims to support. To ensure that the principal beneficiaries’ (Children) views are captured, YOPCODI will promote child participation in the appropriate layers of the project, to give them a sense of belonging and encouragement. This will create continuous communication with communities to inform them of about the programme (at inception), gather and respond to feedback on monthly basis and disseminate assessment findings, lessons and data (baseline). The dissemination will be followed with explanation on what the resultsmeant and brainstorming sessions on what the representatives felt could be done to improve the situation in their communities. YOPCODI will adopt community participatory approaches in planning, implementation and monitoring of the project. Examples of approaches that will be used in ensuring accountability to the affected populations include age, gender and culturally appropriate consultative forums with stakeholders at the state and community levels on key aspects of the project, community mobilization and sensitization including feedback from and to stakeholders in the affected communities.
