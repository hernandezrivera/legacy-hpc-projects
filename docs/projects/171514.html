Project Summary

With this action, ACTED and ARIJ propose to cover outstanding WASH needs in the identified 42 most vulnerable communities in 4 governorates, in complementarity with other ongoing and planned WASH interventions including under HF funding, as follows:
- Hebron: Umm Zaitonah, Al Ganoub, Jurat al Khiel, Umm Qussa, Al Abaidiya, Umm al Kheir, Al Rifayah, Al Samiya, Hiware, Umm al Butm, Osaileh, Wedadie,Khirbet Bir al Idd, Khirbet Tawil ash Shih, H2, Hitta, At Taybe, al Baqa’a
- Bethlehem: Artas, Abu Nujam
- Jerusalem: Al Khalayleh, Al Khalayleh Bedouins, Tublas, Mikhmas Bedouins, Abu Shusheh Bedouins, Al Baqa'a, An Nabi Samwil Bedouins, Abu George Road Bedouins Nkhaila, South al Jib Bedouins, South Bir Nabala Bedouins, North Beit Hanina al Balad Bedouins
- Ramallah: Kobar bedouins, Ein al Qilt, Al Mu'arrajat Center, Ras al Tin, Ein Samiya, Wadi as Seeq, Ein ar Rashash, Khallet al Maghara, Maghayir ad Deir, Deir Abu Masha'al Bedouins, Ein Ayoub Bedouins
Activities will include:
- rehabilitation and provision of key materials to ensure the maintenance of 8 water sources, 1 water network, and 8 schools,
- support to 180 most vulnerable households with the self-repair of their WASH facilities at household level through cash assistance.
- In addition, ACTED will respond to outstanding WASH needs in terms of demolitions by rehabilitating up to 15 WASH community infrastructures in affected communities, based on needs arising in 2021

1 - Needs

The intervention is informed by ACTED’s active participation in the WASH Vulnerability Assessment led by WASH Cluster in July/August 2020. The assessment supported the identification of areas of acute WASH vulnerability and served as a key reference for resource allocation and mobilization. Out of 734 surveyed communities, ACTED led the assessment in 414. ACTED consulted with WASH stakeholders to cross-check covered/remaining needs in identified most vulnerable communities with ongoing/planned WASH interventions to avoid duplication and exploit synergies. ACTED coordinated with PWA, OCHA field offices (South, North and Middle), WASH cluster geographical focal points and WBPC. The proposed intervention is coordinated with ACTED’s other planned WASH interventions in 2020 and 2021. In September 2020, ACTED conducted a rapid need assessment including field visits and KIIs with heads of villages/representatives in 59 communities identified as the most vulnerable in terms of WASH, not yet targeted by any interventions, and located in governorates most affected by COVID-19 since June 2020, to better understand gaps in assistance. ACTED teams visited HHs and interviewed female members to assess their perceptions/specific needs. ACTED also conducted a desk review for updated/reliable data on the status of COVID-19 within eligible communities, most notably drawing upon data from the Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS). ACTED and ARIJ will target 42 communities, representing 50,687 individuals, in the Ramallah, Jerusalem, Hebron and Bethlehem governorates. The assessment found that these communities mainly rely on the same water sources for water consumption, fully or in part on water trucking, with over 200 water trucks needing maintenance. Over 300 HHs need rehabilitation of their WASH facilities, including over half of these HHs who do not have their own latrine. In al Khalayleh Bedouins community, there is a dire need for water network installation and extension as the community is not currently connected to water networks. 5 communities in Hebron governorate also showed an urgent need for WASH rehabilitation within their schools. While demolition trends continue to increase, needs arising from incidents targeting WASH infrastructure at community level (i.e. water networks, cisterns, wells) remain largely unaddressed. Since 2019, out of 85 incidents recorded, targeting WASH infrastructure, over 40% (36 incidents) remain not responded to. Vulnerable groups are most affected by poorly maintained water systems, especially during the COVID-19 crisis. Women are on the frontline of the crisis: they are more exposed (54% of cases in oPt), and its impact. Due to traditional roles, women are in charge of fetching water for the HHs’ consumption. Women have also been the first impacted by loss of employment, due to their predominance in the informal sector and their traditional child-rearing responsibilities that enforced them to stay home to take care of children after school closures. Women and children reported lack of dignity and safety due to poorly maintained HH-level WASH facilities. Elderly people are more affected, with more critical exposure and death ratio. The elderly and PWDs are further isolated due to movement restrictions/closures preventing their carers from reaching them and limiting their access to water points. As a result, ACTED and ARIJ propose to cover outstanding WASH needs in these 42 vulnerable communities through rehabilitation and provision of key materials to ensure the maintenance of 8 water sources, 1 water network, 8 schools, and by supporting the 180 most vulnerable households with the self-repair of their WASH facilities at household level through cash assistance. In addition, ACTED will respond to outstanding WASH needs in terms of demolitions by rehabilitating up to 15 WASH community infrastructures in affected communities, based on needs arising in 2021.

2 - Activities

Preparatory activity:ACTED and ARIJ will hold meetings with local authorities of each community to present the project and ensure the local authorities' support. ACTED and ARIJ will then conduct information sessions in target communities, to explain the project's aim and activities to community members. Ahead of each technical assessment, ACTED and ARIJ will hold gender- and age-sensitive focus group discussions (with men, women, children, elderly people, and people with disabilities) to ensure differentiated needs are taken into account while designing rehabilitation works. Activity 1. Rehabilitation of water sources and water networksACTED and ARIJ engineers will conduct detailed technical assessments of all water sources and trucks within targeted communities. The assessment will facilitate the selection of 8 water sources with the highest rehabilitation needs, to be targeted for maintenance. ACTED and ARIJ engineers will develop Bills of Quantities (BOQs) for the 8 water sources, outlining maintenance needs and action plans to redress them. ACTED and ARIJ will launch a transparent tender process to select contractors to proceed with the maintenance works. It is expected that more than one contractor will be selected to ensure cost-efficiency and timeliness of interventions. ACTED and ARIJ will conduct regular follow-up and monitoring visits to ensure works are completed as agreed and respecting WASH and SPHERE standards, as well as provide corrective actions wherever needed. Costs of the rehabilitation will be cost-shared with communities to ensure ownership. For the one water network installation/extension, ACTED engineers will conduct technical field visits and develop BOQs with support of PWA engineers and community representatives. ACTED will launch a tender process to select contractors to proceed to the works and conduct regular follow-up/monitoring visits on-site to ensure works are completed as per BOQs and in respect of international standards. Activity 2. Rehabilitation of WASH facilities in schoolsACTED/ARIJ Engineers will conduct a technical assessment in the 8 schools, in collaboration with MoE engineers, and will develop a detailed plan for the needed rehabilitation works to be carried out in 3 schools and WASH items (water tanks, safe drinking stations) to be provided in 5 schools. During the technical assessment, the project team will ensure that specific needs of people with disabilities (PWD), as well as age- and gender-sensitive concerns, are integrated into the BoQs. ACTED/ARIJ will launch a tender process to select contractors to proceed with the works, and will perform regular monitoring visits. ACTED/ARIJ will provide cleaning staff in the 5 schools with operation and maintenance training to ensure sustainability of the action. Activity 3. Rehabilitation of WASH facilities at household levelACTED/ARIJ will conduct information sessions in the target communities to explain to community members how to fill in the application forms, which will be distributed in public spaces such as mosques, markets and local councils. ACTED and ARIJ will make sure to support vulnerable households in filling the assessment (i.e. illiterate individuals). ACTED/ARIJ will identify the 180 most vulnerable households in target communities through transparent application processes and socio-economic/protection vulnerability assessments. Once the 180 households are selected, ACTED/ARIJ will develop tailored BOQs with each household (taking into account differentiated needs per household), to establish rehabilitation needs and costs. At least 1 male and 1 female member of each household will participate in the development of BOQs to ensure that all specific needs are taken into account during rehabilitation works. Agreements will be signed with each household. A self-repair approach will be used with the provision of restricted cash in 3 instalments (based on BOQ but expected to be around 800 USD). Based on previous experience, beneficiaries, especially women, prefer cash provision as it gives them more flexibility and ensures their dignity. ACTED/ARIJ engineers will monitor progress and address technical issues through regular visits. To confirm use of funds, assess beneficiary satisfaction and formulate recommendations, ACTED will conduct a Post-Distribution Monitoring. Activity 4. Emergency response to man-made and natural disasters to WASH infrastructureUpon reception of the OCHA trigger, ACTED will conduct post-incident assessments (community-level structure tool) within 2 days. In cases where community WASH infrastructure is demolished/confiscated, ACTED engineers will conduct an additional technical assessment to develop BOQs. The emergency responses will be provided following a competitive tender and regular follow-up from ACTED field staff to ensure quality completion of the needed works. Based on trends, ACTED is expecting to cover the rehabilitation of 5 water networks and 10 sources/cisterns/wells following demolition incidents in 2021 or to cover needs arising from pending incidents from 2020. Activity 5. Creation and capacity-building of Community-Based WASH Committees (CBWC)In each cluster of target communities, ACTED/ARIJ will establish CBWCs to ensure solid engagement and ownership at community level and to guarantee better respect of the safety and privacy of each individual, especially the most vulnerable groups (female headed HHs, disabled, elderly). CBWCs will be composed of at least 7 members (including at least 40% of women), and will be trained on topics including protection mainstreaming; WASH minimum standards, ACTED's CRM. These CBWCs will participate in the selection process for rehabilitations at household-level to ensure transparency in the process and community engagement. They will monitor the implementation and act as a liaison between Implementing partners and community members in case of any feedback, complaint or challenge.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

The project will be underpinned by ACTED’s programmatic approach of meaningful community engagement and participation of the population, in line with the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Five Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP), namely (1) leadership/governance; (2) transparency; (3) feedback and complaints; (4) participation; (5) design, monitoring and evaluation. More specifically, AAP will be ensured throughout the intervention by taking account of, giving account to, and being held to account by the affected population as follows. ACTED and ARIJ will be held to account by the beneficiaries in line with Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) principles, by using ACTED’s Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM). The CRM is overseen by ACTED’s AME Unit, which is independent from the delivery of activities. Beneficiaries can access ACTED’s CRM through a dedicated phone line, whatsapp, email address and complaints boxes as well as directly to ACTED staff during field visits. Through this CRM, beneficiaries can raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on ACTED and co-applicants’ work in a secure and confidential way, and receive a response in a timely manner. ACTED’s AMEU is in charge of handling this mechanism to provide timely response or adequate action to address any concerns. Having a well-designed and well-managed mechanism for handling beneficiary complaints will improve the quality of the action, enhance the trust and confidence of beneficiaries and identify areas of the project which need to be improved, and ensure that co-applicants learn from the feedback provided through this process

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

In all programming, ACTED ensures respect and promotion of the rights of all individuals and communities involved, in accordance with the relevant bodies of law (international human rights law and international humanitarian law). In order to take account of affected populations, the action will also take strong consideration of the needs and interests of beneficiary communities, particularly in regard to designing activities with their safety and dignity in mind. As part of protection mainstreaming, ACTED analyzes protection risks; anticipates possible consequences that may arise; and ensures that issues can be reported/referrals can be made to appropriate agencies/host government structures if required. In particular, ACTED and ARIJ will undertake regular visits to the communities and beneficiary HHs to ensure that protection concerns are incorporated throughout the intervention, thus enhancing safety, security and privacy for women, children and other vulnerable groups. ACTED is firmly committed to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality, and strives to ensure the ‘Do No Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities do not inadvertently worsen tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance. To this end, all staff will be made aware of and in adherence to the Do No Harm principle as outlined in ACTED’S Code of Conduct and related policies including Child Protection; Anti-Fraud, Bribery & Corruption; Anti- Terrorism; and Policy Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Under this framework, ACTED has developed mechanisms that can contribute to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse such as: signing of the code of conduct by the staff in order to clarify unacceptable behavior and in particular sexual exploitation and abuse; information to the beneficiaries of existing remedies (Complaints and Response Mechanisms); implementation of training and awareness sessions in order to train and empower the teams and identify risk factors on an ongoing basis. Finally, all activities will be conducted in strict adherence of COVID-19 mitigation measures including social distancing and personal protective equipment (PPE) to minimise the risk of COVID-19 transmission among beneficiaries, staff and stakeholder alike.ACTED developed strong global tools for cash programming, ensuring transparency/accountability within all implementation stages, including beneficiary selection with clear criteria; distribution; and M&E. ACTED is currently implementing several projects through cash-based programming during the COVID-19 crisis and has acquired lessons learned and clear SOPs to proceed in a safe manner during the crisis

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In order to maximize the reach of the intervention, the project will adopt a deeply integrated protection approach (IPA) throughout all stages of the project cycle. The action fully acknowledges that different groups and individuals have specific needs and assistance will therefore be adapted accordingly. To this end, ACTED’s Protection Officer will ensure that information is provided through accessible channels, formats, languages and family and community support networks to ensure that the beneficiary community are kept fully informed of the assistance available under the project. ACTED’s AMEU will also ensure equal and impartial access to aid by overseeing the CRM, as well as by conducting various verification exercises. For example, ACTED’s AMEU will conduct a verification exercise to authenticate the fairness of the selection process and all applicants will be notified of the final decision and provided with the means to raise concerns regarding the selection process through ACTED’s CRM. Similarly, to ensure the agency of beneficiaries and further local ownership of project outcomes, the affected population will be actively consulted during the assessments to ensure BoQs and workplans are responsive to their self-identified most pressing needs. Where applicable, at least one male and one female per HH will be consulted to ensure gender-specific needs and concerns are taken into account, as well as those of boys, girls, PwDs, the elderly and other marginalised persons to ensure assistance reflects their unique vulnerabilities. In addition, ACTED and ARIJ staff will be present to ensure the integration of HH concerns into BoQ and workplan design. In the event beneficiaries are dissatisfied with their respective BoQ and/or workplan an appeal mechanism will be offered through the CBPCs and ACTED’s CRM. If ACTED/ARIJ staff consider the request justified, a new BoQ and/or workplan will be produced by a different agronomist. To mitigate this, all staff involved in the assessment process will undergo extensive refresher training in protection and shelter SPHERE standards prior to the assessments to ensure equal and impartial access to assistance. The COVID-19 crisis is disproportionately affecting vulnerable groups and deepening pre-existing vulnerabilities, especially for women, PWDs and elderly people. To ensure that limited resources reach the most in need, the Action places a strong emphasis on an inclusive and impartial approach, with a particular focus on the elderly, PwDs and/or chronic illnesses/underlying health conditions, widows, children, female-headed HHs and people living under the poverty line. This approach will ensure that those most at risk of COVID-19 benefit fully from all aspects of the programming and delivery of service and are equipped with the most effective means to protect themselves
