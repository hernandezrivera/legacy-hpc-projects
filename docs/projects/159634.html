Project Summary

Despite concerted efforts to avert food insecurity & emergency conditions, many parts of Somalia continue to register food insecurity and malnutrition. An estimated 2.1 million Somalis are in Crisis and Emergency (IPC Phases 3 & 4) and face significant consumption gaps through December 2019, an extended hunger season following the poor performance of the 2019 Gu season rainfall (FSNAU Set, 2019)..An estimated 2.6 million Somalis across Somalia remain displaced primarily due to conflict, lack of livelihood opportunities and drought. Mercy Corps field assessment in target regions reveal deepening effect of drought and conflict have led to high food consumption gaps and loss of livelihood assets.
Against this background of fragility and vulnerability. This project aims to provide 10,423 most vulnerable HHs (IDPs, agro pastoral, riverine & urban poor) with urgent lifesaving interventions to improve HH immediate access to food and livelihood support to mitigate risks of adoption of negative coping strategies. The project is designed to advance towards the food security cluster 2020 response strategy and priorities and proposed interventions are based on the principles of centrality of protection.The cash response interventions will be based on market assessments and guidance of the CWG and FSC recommendations. Mercy Corps will coordinate with other cash actors to leverage and avoid overlap in cash support

Project Activities

Mercy Corps in responding to the above identified needs and will closely work with the affected communities living in project target locations/districts in Bay, L. Shebelle, Galmudug, Benadir, and Awdal,regions of Somalia in the prioritizations of activities in responding to the needs identified by the project. In line with the cluster objectives, the proposed project will undertake the following activities aimed at increasing access to food and restoring livelihoods of populations affected by emergencies and crisis Objective 1: Improve households’ immediate access to food through provision of conditional and unconditional assistance depending on the severity of food insecurity phases, vulnerability and seasonality of the livelihoodsActivities:• 10,423 HHin emergencies and crisis identified through participatory process & criteria agreed with community ensuring equal opportunities for men, women and minorities. Data of beneficiaries will be disaggregated by age, sex, livelihood group. Clear information is provided on process of beneficiary selection and criteria, entitlements, complaints, feedback& accountability system. • Provide unconditional cash transfer to 10,423 HHs to save lives and protect livelihood assets. The use of electronic systems for registration and payment, coupled with rigorous beneficiary validation and monitoring tools to be instituted to minimize risks. Cash transfer value based on FSC recommended % of MEB . Market assessment conducted to inform impact and effectiveness of transfer modality• Provide nutrition education and promote appropriate IYCF practices. Awareness with nutrition actors, OTPs & MCH and caregivers especially women. Objective 2: Protect and restore livelihoods, related food and income sources, through provision of seasonally appropriate livelihood inputs and technical support in rural and (peri-) urban settings.• Provide 2500 female and male headed vulnerable farmers with appropriate agricultural inputs and support (farming tools, seeds, fuel for irrigation& storage facilities, tractor hours) to maximize productivity. Consult with women & men considering roles in production Use of tools provided will reduce field cultivation work load thereby allowing more time for female headed HHs to breast feed and attend to other household chores. • Animals belonging to 2500 HHs treated against endemic & production limiting diseases: CAHWs provided with relevant kits & drugs provided by pharmacies accredited by LPAs. Collaborate with FAO and other agencies on livestock treatment• Provide fodder for livestock to 1000 HHs during the stress periods. Provision of supplementary feeding targets core breeding herd to prevent losses and ensure milk availability for HH children. Supplementary feed to reduce the workload to the HH mostly women who traditionally look for fodder. Objective 3: Support household and community resilience to shocks, through rehabilitation and/or restoration of productive assets, enhance livelihood strategies and disaster preparedness• Selection and rehabilitation of local community infrastructures (water pans, soil bunds, irrigation canals, feeder roads) through cash for work approach: Women involved in selection & design, Technical assessment and CFW conflict/risk analysis carried out. Target group will be trained on management and maintenance for sustainability. Provide tools & training in management. HH that cannot work or are labour poor will be provided with unconditional cash grants• Training of community Early Warning committees (EWC) and development of community DRR plans• Selected Community Awareness and Sensitization on Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) through Radio MessagingSensitization and Awareness Raising.Activities:3.1 Training Flood and Drought Committees on Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR).3.2 Spot repairing of weak/broken river banks.3.3 Community Awareness and Sensitization on Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) through Radio Messaging.

Mitigation Measures

The project will put the following mitigation measures to the possible risks enumerated in the section above. • In order to strengthen participation and empowerment of women, girls and boys and other special interest groups, the project shalla) Involve and consult women, girls and boys in identification of priority FSL needs and appropriate responseb) Consult women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, chronically ill, older persons, pregnant and lactating women and marginalized persons to collect accurate information about their specific food and nutritional requirements and preferences for food distribution points and livelihood servicesc) Ensure adequate representation levels of women, boys and girls in the committee representative structure and encourage them to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response • To avoid instances of protection issues and in particular mitigate exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance, the project will:a) Work in coordination with protection actors to put in place measures to prevent and respond to physical/sexual violence arising from accessing humanitarian assistanceb) Ensure the location of and access routes to access humanitarian assistance and services are safec) Ensure the mode and frequency of humanitarian assistance (eg distribution) minimizes safety risk • To address barriers impeding beneficiaries to report concerns resulting from the interventions provided and overall ensure accountability to affected population, the project willa) Enhance transparency and accountability with the affected populations by providing them with accessible and timely information on project (eg selection criteria, procedures and processes, individual entitlements etc)b) Set up accessible, confidential and robust feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconductc) Ensure all project staff working with affected populations understand, sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries.

Please provide further explanation

The proposed activities such as cash transfers will address the food security and livelihoods needs of displaced people in a way that goes beyond care and maintenance to one that builds resilience and the ability to withstand future shocks, preserves safety and dignity in order to improve the lives and self-reliance of displaced populations while simultaneously addressing the impact on host communities. Mercy Corp’s long standing presence in the target regions endows the project with in-depth knowledge of the vulnerabilities and strengths of the target HHs. The project aims to protect & restore livelihoods whilst strengthening the coping capacities of population affected by shocks through a wide array of activities that contribute to resilience building. Mercy Corps recognizes the need for close collaboration and synergy with ongoing resilience programs and will strive to leverage through existing programming platforms. Moreover, the project design has flexibility on emergency preparedness and response to ensure timely response to new shocks

Caseload

- The Somalia Food Security Cluster (SFSC) aims to be the primary source of information on the humanitarian response by addressing food insecurity, and by providing its members with a strategic vision and guidance in their response to the acute and underlying causes of crisis. As per the rationale of the Global Food Security Cluster, the FSC aims to promote responses that are: •Proportionate. The FSC will promote proportionate needs-based responses and advocate adequate resources and support. •Appropriate. The FSC will promote seasonal and livelihood specific responses. •Timely. In Somalia in particular, the FSC will aim to improve the timeliness of responses in order to provide a more effective and cost-efficient response.
