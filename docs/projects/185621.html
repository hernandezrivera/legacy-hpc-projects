Project Summary

In 2022 DRC will continue GBV prevention and response programming to reduce the underlying causes and drivers of gender violence. DRC plans to continue providing specialized GBV case management services aimed at providing immediate support to the acute needs of survivors of GBV, including non-specialized psychosocial support, dignity kit distributions and referrals to specialized services. Response programming will be complemented with interventions aimed at preventing GBV by changing mindsets and harmful attitudes and practices within the community that result in violence against gender. GBV prevention programming will include engagement with men and boys in preventing violence against women using an adapted version of the IRC-EMAP curriculum, sessions on gender equality and the rights of women and girls through the GirlShine curriculum. DRC also plans to continue GBV programming in the Hijra safe space. Until now, activities have been limited to prevention activities including PSS and recreational activities with Hijra groups. DRC has relied on Bandhu Social Welfare Society, for technical guidance and capacity building of staff on ways to engage appropriately and safely with hijra communities.
DRC now intends to expand this program to begin providing tailored case management services for this population group. Wherever appropriate, DRC will seek to strengthen synergies with other protection areas (CP and GP) and between protection and non-protection sectors. This will include building capacities of GBV case workers on CP risks/case management principles, providing training to non-protection staff on GBV mainstreaming, identification and referral of GBV cases. DRC is committed to ensuring its GBV programming is relevant, appropriate and does not cause harm. DRC will also participate in safety audits for CM conducted from the GBVSS to ensure the services are provided adhering to the GBV guiding principles. Accordingly, protection monitoring activities will be strengthened to ensure more consistent documentation of risks including GBV. DRC also intends to strengthen an AGD approach to data collection to ensure information on risks can be disaggregated by age, gender and diversity profiles including survivors with disabilities and at a later stage hijra and other sexual minorities. This data will aim to complement data collected through the GBVIMS+. Trends and analyses from these different sources will be triangulated and used to inform GBV programming including its scale up. In addition, DRC started to train all staffs and volunteers on PSEA using the resources provided by the PSEA Network. In its efforts to ensure AAP, DRC will continue to strengthen its engagement with community groups to increase outreach and lead on GBV prevention activities. DRC will seek to increase participation of survivors that are at higher risk of marginalization (such as PwDs, sexual minorities, men/boys) in decision making processes. Feedback received during community meetings and sessions, CFRM, protection monitoring activities will be regularly incorporated in programmatic adjustments. Outreach to marginalized groups will be enhanced through increasing the pool of trained women volunteers that may have unique access to these groups as well as further capacitating staff and volunteers on disability inclusion, provided by a specialized agency (HI). DRC will continue to regularly share information with the GBVSS members, GBV camp-level focal points and coordinate assistance with the CFRM to avoid duplications and ensure transparency. Finally, DRC has a zero-tolerance policy towards SEA. As such DRC is an active member of the PSEA Network, has established a PSEA policy and reporting mechanism. This includes appointing PSEA focal points at the field and country office level, conducting SEA sensitization among communities on how to report.

1 - Identified Needs

The policy brief by Yale, IPA and Gage highlights while adolescents in camps and host communities are vulnerable to multiple forms of GBV, including sexual, harassment, child marriage and domestic violence, the normalization of certain forms of GBV likely contributes to the underreporting of incidences. Inter-agency report titled “Intersectional Analysis of Gender Amongst Rohingya Refugees and Host Communities in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh” produced by UNHCR, CARE and ActionAid in 2020 highlights the role of the policy restrictions on freedom of movement, income generation and livelihoods opportunities for Rohingya refugees leading to negative coping strategies and exacerbating risks to refugee women and girls including survival sex, child marriage, trafficking and reduced access to assistance items. According to the report, refugee men and boys are at risk of perpetrating IPV and sexual harassment and engaging in polygamy in this environment. Hijras and other gender diverse community remain underserved and unrepresented across sectors. These patterns entrench the consequences of systemic denial of rights faced by the refugees when in Myanmar, following experiences of acute conflict related sexual violence during flight. For displacement-affected host communities, where patriarchy and the needs of a development context already caused GBV risks, the influx threatened social cohesion, income generation and livelihood opportunities. DRC GBV’s implementation experience shows this intimately impacts the lives of women and girls, whose movement may be restricted as a measure to protect against strangers or who may self-impose restrictions (over and above the traditional restrictions on movement already present); who may have partners opting for polygamy as a coping method or who may be deprioritized for the very limited livelihoods opportunities available for the few women and girls able to work outside of home. In both the refugee and host locations, the COVID pandemic has deteriorated the protection environment. Two IRC reports (What Happened? How the COVID-19 Response Failed Women and Girls; and In the Shadows of the Pandemic: The Gendered Impact of COVID 19 on Refugee and Host Communities) complementarily illustrate this: the first says GBV accounted for only 0.48% of the Global Humanitarian Response and the second concludes that safety and security risks have increased during the pandemic for everyone, but pre-existing gendered risks are exacerbated. Also, GBV is increasing, including IPV, polygamy, transphobic violence, while access to support is more restricted. The IRC report shows that according to 42% of the refugee respondents, it is more unsafe for women and girls inside the house since the COVID-19 pandemic. For host communities, there is an information gap of both needs and services. This is also indicated by DRC GBV’s case management supervision experience. Thus, the relevance of DRC’s full package of GBV response and prevention services for host and refugee communities remains for 2022. This includes engagement of men and boys, and dedicated programming for child survivors and male survivors. DRC’s unique contribution in having run a safe space for hijras since 2019 anticipated gaps being flagged in the recent reports and is proposed for scale-up. In the interest of sustainability, DRC technical expertise is made available to partners and stakeholders in GBV through training, sensitization and mentoring. Government stakeholders will be prioritized in this regard. Strategies to aid recovery from the interruptions caused by COVID-19 are incorporated. This proposal is people centered approach that builds capacity of local actors.

2 - Activities or outputs

Capacity building for 600 local protection actors, including protection CBOs and volunteers (non GBV actors and GBV actors) on introductory packages specifically designed to ensure ethical referral and cover existing technical capacity gaps respectively with a commitment to follow ups with government and national partners. 450 GBV case management support including direct provision of PSS services for survivors (12 years and above) in refugee and host locations with capacity building to support child survivors and male survivors. 30 GBV case management support including direct provision of PSS services for survivors among gender diverse population. Life-skills, (Basic Literacy Numeracy) and PSS sessions with 2,000 women, girls, men, boys and gender diverse population among Host community and Rohingya Refugees.Provide support to 350 Hijras through the retention of Hijra Safe Space twice a week in Palongkhali. Capacity building for 100 staff and local CBOs on PSEA. Capacity building of 45 DRC GP and CP staff on specialized service of GBV Case Management. Maintenance of women and girls’ safe spaces and additional mobile service points for 2,500 targeted people in Palongkhali wards 5 and 6, Rajapalong wards 6 and 9 and Camp 6 and 10. Outreach and Women & Girls Safe Space sessions in consultation with 2,500 women and girls for psychosocial support, and with skills development and empowerment activities for adolescent girls and young women. Provide support through establishment of a hotline service for 100 GBV survivors. Specific prevention session for GBV engaging 2,500 men and boys in the sessions.
