Project Summary

Explosive ordnance contamination remains a major protection concern, severely impacting the lives and livelihoods of the population and further amplifying the social and economic crisis aggravated by the COVID-19 pandemic
While explosive ordnance risk education continues to be a key life-saving activity to for the vulnerable people to adopt safe behavior to minimize risks posed by explosive .
Based on 2022Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), approximately one in two people are at risk from explosive contamination, and a third of the communities are potentially contaminated by explosive ordnance.
Explosive ordnance accidents can result in serious injury and death, leading to long-term physical and psychological trauma for survivors and their families. Contamination severely impacts lives,
livelihoods and amplifies social and economic crisis aggravated by COVID-19 pandemic.
NAS will respond to the issues raised above by:
Implementing the Risk Education (RE) pillar of Mine Action Sector and raising the awareness and knowledge of civilians and humanitarian workers through implementing tailored RE programming in 9 sub-districts : Suran, Maaret Tamsrin, Kafr Takharim, Armanaz,Darkosh,Mare,Azaz,Harim and Dana.
This objective will be achieved through conducting RE sessions in the mentioned locations, targeting both children and adults in schools,community centres, camps, and public spaces.
NAS will mitigate the risk of Explosive Ordinance Risk Education (ERW) on the children, their caregivers, and humanitarian actors, and enhance the capacity of community and humanitarian actors on delivering awareness about the dangers and signs of explosives and mines. By conducting risk awareness sessions, disseminating RE material to children in and out schools in the most affected communities .
A) . Provide risk education sessions for beneficiaries through Explosive Ordinance Risk Education (ERW) mobile teams (three mobile teams ,each team (6 staff each; 1 team leader, 5 male and female educators) using Mine action sub-cluster materials to raise their awareness and avoid any accident related to explosive hazards. ERW Mobile teams will target the beneficiaries in camps, streets, mosques, markets , health center , nurses , farmers and Housewives. each session (45 minutes) using Mine action-sub-cluster materials. The session contains a lot of information (ERW definition, types of war remnants, evidence of war remnants, fire safety, mine awareness, warning signs and what should we do when we see any strange item).
Participants to EORE sessions: maximum number of participants to RE group sessions shall be 25 and minimum number is 8; applying social-distancing measures and abiding by WHO recommendations on COVID-19 related threats.
Modality: EORE sessions shall be provided on face-to-face modality, through group sessions and/or door-to-door sessions. Due to covid 19 access and movement restriction and social distancing measures.
B).Train 96 (Teachers, local councillors, nurses and community leaders, humanitarian workers) as community focal points in Idlib and Aleppo, to raise their capacity in delivering training and awareness on risk education, explosive hazards, mine awareness, and communication and facilitation skills (they will be able to conduct training on ERW and raise the awareness of people in need or at risks like farmers and their children about ERW)
The ERW focal points persons will be from different segments of society (teachers, local authorities, community leaders and IDP camp facilitators….etc.) The training contains a lot of information about ERWs and the best ways to report about any remnants, each focal point will be provided with MASC brochures. Before each session we will provide awareness about COVID-19 Reasons - symptoms –prevention in simple ways suitable to each age group
C). Providing safe, accessible, child-sensitive reporting mechanisms are in place for PSEA.

3 - Needs

Explosive ordnance contamination remains a major protection concern, severely impacting the lives and livelihoods of the population and further amplifying the social and economic crisis aggravated by the COVID-19 pandemicBased on 2021(HNO), approximately one in two people are at risk from explosive contamination, and a third of the communities are potentially contaminated by explosive ordnance.Based HNO 2022, men and boys are the most exposed to the direct, immediate threat of explosive incidents; while women and girls are more affected by their indirect impact, such as the deprivation of freedom of movement and the exacerbation of already-underlying vulnerabilities. According to needs assessment that NAS conducted in (Aleppo and Idleb), there was a big spread of explosive hazards in the agricultural land by 46%, roads were 34%, and the rest were private property and public buildings.NAS has received information from local people mentioning that the recent heavy rains and floods has affected and changed the locations of old ERW, and led to more IDPs movement which put more new community under risk especially during the new displacement.Explosive hazard contamination can have severe socio-economic implications on families and communities, depriving them of livelihoods and sources of income, increasing vulnerabilities. Farming, herding, moving or travelling, tampering or playing with items, and rubble removal are reported to be the most at-risk activities.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk:Familiarisation may cause the civilian populace to become overconﬁdent or complacent to the threat of explosive hazards. Likelihood :Medium-Impact :High Mitigation Measure :Emphasis on "Stop, Warn, and Report" messaging in risk education as well as qualitative evaluations to understand the issues underpinning risk behavior. Risk: Movement restriction to basic services due to the high increase in the number of COVID-19 cases and the high level of risk. Mitigation: Providing remote response services. Risk : Violate PSEA or CSG, CoC principles. Mitigation: - Signing PSEA and child safeguarding NAS’s policy by all staff. - getting training on PSEA for all staff. - PSEA awareness through brochures, videos. - Assigning complaint mechanism for SEA issues. - policies of Code of conduct, PSEA, child safeguarding is mandatory for all staff and vendors working with NAS. Risk : Tensions between IDP and host community. Mitigation: Children and BNFs are from IDPs and host community. Effective community engagement and mobilization will be adopted.

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: The political and security environment will remain conducive to the implementation process. Assumption: services are provided in safe and healthy environment. Assumption: Local authorities do not interfere with the project. Risk: Weakness of interaction of the beneﬁciaries of the activities. Mitigation: Preparing participatory activities. Risks: The targeted beneﬁciaries are not aware of the importance of the activities being provided. Mitigation: We will be announced in cooperation between mobile teams and local councils within the targeted villages. Risks: Airstrikes and bombed cars might happen in Idleb area. Mitigation: 1.security staff will do a daily, weekly and monthly assessment of the security situation to ensure the security of staff and beneﬁciaries. Mitigation: NAS team will conduct focus group discussion with all beneﬁciaries to explain that the activities of this project are not against the norms and culture of the target communities. Risk: COVID-19 PandemicMitigation: Participants to EORE sessions: maximum number of participants to RE group sessions shall be 25 and minimum number is 8; applying social-distancing measures and abiding by WHO recommendations on COVID-19 related threats.
