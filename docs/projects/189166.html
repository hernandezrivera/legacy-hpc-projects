Project Summary

Despite the signing of revitalized peace agreement and permanent ceasefire in 2018,, people in South Sudan continue to face deteriorating humanitarian conditions. Their situation is worsened by endemic violence, conflict, access constraints and operational interference, public health challenges such as direct and indirect effects of COVID-19 and climatic shocks resulting in the dual phenomena of extraordinary flooding and localized drought, which have a severe impact on people’s livelihoods, hampers access to health care services. People in South Sudan have limited access to optimal health services due to conflict, flooding, and a developed health care system. A multitude of drivers compound people’s well-being, including limited access to sufficient quantities of safe drinking water, poor hygiene practices, and poor sanitation. Only 8% reported health facilities are providing the full Basic Package of Health and Nutrition Services (BPHNS) and 53% of the facilities are moderately functional, implying inadequate access and unavailability of some health services to some communities in many parts of the country. 58% of the Community health system, (Bomas) in the country does not have health teams and only 59% of the population can access health facilities. The 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) shows that Unity State has only 8% functional health facilities in which out of 10 HFs, Koch has only 4 functional, Mayendit has 8 functional HFs out of 12 and Leer has 8 functional HFs out of 9, the rest are either by of due to funding constraint in HPF3 year4 or abandoned due to insecurity or submerged by the flood. The level in provision of healthcare services has been compromised and overstretched due to the high population and inadequate functional HFs, hence a significant need for scaling up the health service provision in the three project target counties is of high demand. This project is aiming at enhancing access and strengthening the quality provision of emergency lifesaving health care services in Leer, Mayendit and Koch counties through support of 2 PHCCs (Gany PHCC in Koch and Leer town PHCC), 3PHCUs (Ngony PHCU in Koch, Tutnyang PHCU and Thaker PHCU in Mayendit) and 4 mobile clinics response (Jaak in Koch, Pillieny in Leer, Pabuong and Malkuer in Mayendit). UNIDOR will encourage provision of CME or capacity building for HCWs to improve their skills and knowledge in order to improve on low EPI and DSA coverages across the three project targeted counties. UNIDOR will strengthen its awareness sessions on COVID-19 pandemic and other epidemic prone diseases especially at the congested IDPs sites and community level. This project proposal will target all gender and age groups with a total beneficiary of 90,000 individuals, 36,000 in Koch, 27,000 in Leer and 27,000 in Mayendit counties respectively. The target beneficiaries include 34,839 men, 36,261 women, 9,261 boys and 9,639 girls. The 2022 WHO classification noted, 5 out of 9 counties of UNITY manifested with GAM between 19.2%-15.2% above emergency threshold with inclusion of Leer, Mayendit and Koch counties. The project will also support 250 people with disabilities including 80 men, 100 women, 30 boys and 40 girls through case identification, psycho social support and basic mental health services, first aid counseling, treatment and referral of the clients to nearby health county or state hospitals.

1 - Needs

South Sudan’s health system relies heavily on international humanitarian assistance and is still unable to cope and respond to humanitarian emergencies and provide basic essential life-saving health services. Limited health facility functionality and capacity, and poor coverage, have made it difficult to meet additional health needs posed by floods, food insecurity and frequent conflicts. Women, children, the elderly and persons with disabilities are particularly vulnerable and most frequently face limited access to healthcare. Essential life-saving health care services including maternal, child, adolescent health provision through static health facilities, medical mobile teams and community health volunteers will be key priorities. Disease outbreaks need to be timely detected, investigated and responded to by supporting and strengthening disease outbreak surveillance and response systems. Maintaining the core pipeline for essential medicines and supplies to respond to emergency health needs will be vital. Strengthening routine immunization and vaccination campaigns will be necessary in preventing morbidity and mortality due to vaccine preventable diseases as well as improving the COVID-19 vaccination coverage. Humanitarian Health response needs to be COVID-19, EVD and NTDs response and prevention inclusive. Technical support and coordination to provide quality humanitarian health services will be needed at all levels. 2 - Project activities

UNIDOR seek to improve access and strengthening the emergency lifesaving response by establishing 4 Mobile clinics, Support 3 PHCUs and 2 PHCCs. The 2PHCCs will provide normal clinical packages and at the same time support referrals of SGBV survivors, CEmONC or surgical emergency cases to county or state hospitals. More than 55,000 people will benefit from OPD curative consultations with 21,290 A5 male, 22,160 A5 Female, 5,670 U5 male, 5,880 U5 female. The project will target 500 pregnant mothers for safe deliveries at health facility by SBA. Over 80 HCWs will be trained on IMNCI, Infection prevention and control, CMR and Quality of Care (QOC). To improve the routine immunization coverage especially on Penta3, the project will target over 3,920 males under 1 year and 4,080 females under 1 year. Over 1,500 children under 15 will be reached with reactive measles vaccination campaign across the three counties as to reduce the reoccurrence of measles outbreak in three project targeted counties. About 300 people will benefit from MHPSS services. 120 SGBV survivors will be receiving CMR services. The project will provide rehabilitation service to over 250 beneficiaries. The project will target 3,400 (M,1,666 and F, 1,734) above 6 years and adults to be reached with health education and promotion awareness sessions on general hygiene and sanitation, adherence to COVID-19 protocol, timely health seeking behavior, 6 months exclusive breast feeding and importance or benefit of routine EPI and COVID-19 vaccination. Through the EWARS and IDSR system, the project will report 15% of disease outbreak alert for investigation within 48 hours of notification. Improve resilience among vulnerable populations including disabled groups and mentally ill persons through psycho-social support services. The project will integrate response for health/Wash/Nutrition/ food security and protection including Management of SAM with medical complications. There shall be flow of information sharing on service gaps and achievements for planning and accountability to affected population. Quarterly joint assessments and monitoring of services through M&E and RRM team will be of priority. The project will enhance Advocacy for development emergency funding to support unforeseen emergencies through other development donors like DFID/HPF and World Bank.

3 - Project indicators

UNIDOR is committed to regular monitoring and evaluation (M&E) using indicators and targets as priority one to effectively track the progress of project to ensure implementation is guided by a results orientation and continuously informed by lessons learnt and beneficiary feedback. UNIDOR will develop a monitoring and evaluation plan and indicator tracking table to measure progress towards the expected results. The indicators on the tracking table will include those in the log frame corresponding to on-going and planned activities. The Monitoring tools (indicator tracking template) will include the Departmental Questionnaires, online system such as EWARs and DHIS2. Tally sheets, and Pictorial evidence especially during health facilities visits and trainings, will also use FGDs with the Health Care Workers and the local Authorities to collect views on how the project is impacting on their lives. Data will be collected by the relevant Programme Managers and weekly/monthly/quarterly reports will be produced for internal monitoring. UNIDOR will submit quantitative data, disaggregated by sex, age and IDP/host when relevant both progress and final programmatic and financial reports online in line with donor reporting templates like FTS and Health Cluster donor requirements within the stipulated period as will be evidenced in the contractual documents. A joint quarterly supervision visits to all health facilities will be conducted together with our CHD counterparts using QoC/QSC tools in order to see the HFs compliance as per South Sudan BPHNS.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Based on the recommendations received from the beneficiaries on how the project should be implemented, UNIDOR will adapt those beneficiaries' feedback to change the project implementation as to avoid DO NO HARM policy. This will be done with consultation with the donors especially where the adaptation will deviate so much from the agreed deliverables.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Through focus group discussions, the project will engage all community members including persons with disability to participant and share their special needs and concerns right from the project design and implementation. UNIDOR will also ensure the project engages literate employees from the community that they can address their feedback with the local languages. UNIDOR will also use Community meetings with other stake holders as a means of getting feedback from the project target community. UNIDOR will also endure suggestion boxes are placed at the health facilities for the literate beneficiaries to provide feedback and the box will be opened every month with FGD, with key stakeholder held to provide the feedback.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project will be guided by UNIDOR PSEA policy to ensure beneficiaries are protected against sexual exploitation and abuse-PSEA. staffs including field staff and community volunteers will be trained on PSEA to further create awareness to the beneficiaries on -SEA. during such training the information on reporting and communication pathway will be outlined and made accessible by the community. The PSEA focal person and project team will conduct PSEA awareness to the community and unveil SEA communication and feedback mechanism for UNIDOR. The organization through project provide information of PSEA focal point person and hotline (mobile contacts, for reporting allegations and incidences of SEA towards beneficiaries.All partners in contact with beneficiary must be informed of PSEA and child safeguarding policies. People with organizations contracts (Suppliers, Contractors, Consultants) must also be informed and sign PSEA and child safeguarding policies to protect beneficiaries.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

From the inception of the project through the implementation cycle the community will be consulted and involved in design including the duration of the projects and donors funding the project, roles and duties of the community and the organization. Decisions and opinions will be sought through avenues such as community sensitization and mobilizations, during focused group discussions and during the village health committees’ meetings. The project team will conduct monthly stakeholder meetings to discuss or review the project and share feedback. Lastly, UNIDOR will also place suggestion boxes in the respective health project sites for access to all beneficiaries and members of the community.
