Project Summary

The proposed project aims to upscale and improve the emergency preparedness and response within the West Bank.
Community wise, the project intends to support and empower men and women living in the harsh situation, politically, economically, and socially, by building a well-structured emergency response plan, integrating First Aid training, emergency response training, leadership and communication training and creating emergency teams in the targeted locations.
This activity intends to support community members living within the communities to obtain knowledge on communication and leadership, first aid, emergency training including evacuation, simulation training and Disaster Preparedness. By teaching First Aid and basic life support, we try to mitigate the sudden death related to cardiovascular disease, the primary cause of mortality according to the Palestinian Health Annual Report. In parallel, we foresee that the trainees will be capacitated to respond to any sudden onset disaster, and play major role, within their communities. Along with this training, we intend to provide to the trainees a First Aid Kit, giving them all the means to respond to emergencies as bystanders.
Hebron: Enab al Kabeer, Birein, Shweika, Arab Freijat, Wadi al Reem, Sensana, Deir al Asal, Beit al Roosh, Santana and Zanuta.
Jordan Valley and East Jerusalem: Mihtawish, Trefat, Kurshan, Mlaihat, Tabaneh, Buran, Abu Faisal, Abu Husein, Mikhmas, and Dawaheek1.
Out of the selected trainees, 50% will be trained on disaster management, allowing them to become a part of a bigger network of trained volunteers hence contribution to increase preparedness and response capacity of the target communities.
This project is built on the mobile clinics of PMRS supported by MAP, where PMRS and MAP built a decent trust and connection with the communities, and provide a comprehensive service, in addition to the quality health and mental health services provided by the mobile clinic teams, this comes to further empower the communities and increase their resilience.
On the other hand, the project will also enhance the Palestinian Health system’s emergency response and preparedness through building the capacity of health workers (nurses, doctors, & paramedics) at the Ministry of health (MoH) both non-governmental, governmental hospitals and emergency services providers (EMS) in the West Bank on trauma care and emergency lifesaving response and enhancing the preparedness further through providing medical equipment to be ready for immediate response during emergencies to ensure the sustainability of the essential health services during an emergency. MAP will target at least 200 health care workers (nurses, doctors, and paramedics) from MoH, NGOs, hospitals and EMS in all governorates in West Bank who will attend several trauma courses. The Training is certified by the American College of Surgeons and will be conducted in cooperation with the International Medical Education Trust (IMET 2000) at their accredited training center in Ramallah.
7 courses will be conducted over 2 training days/ course, with participation of 30 participants/course.
The training is dedicated to preventing death and disability from trauma through education and emergency trauma care. The training will provide health providers with the knowledge and hands-on skills to take better care of trauma patients, including rapid assessment, appropriate intervention and identification of immediate life threats. The course combines classroom learning, hands-on skills stations, and assessment stations that put learning to work in simulated trauma scenarios.

1 - Needs

The occupied Palestinian territories (oPt) is in the middle of a protracted humanitarian crisis, caused by years of occupation and blockade, escalating hostilities, poor infrastructure, movement restrictions imposed by Israel, an increase in conflict-related Palestinian casualties and settler violence in the West Bank (WB). Since the start of 2022, the number of casualties from the confrontations in Jerusalem and West Bank reached to 5,490 in oPt (OCHA’s Protection of Civilians Report, 04 June), this indicates the situation is considered hot and other events might occur during the remaining 6 months of 2022. This is in addition to movement restrictions for the entire population in WB and the separation wall are leaving the Palestinian Government and the population in general with few alternatives. The continuation of building the separation wall and the increasing number of settlements is leading to more land fragmentation on West Bank. At the same time the raise of settlers’ violation against the Palestinian civil population as well as the alarming escalation of house destruction in the area C precede a higher deterioration in the Palestinian humanitarian challenges particularly among the most vulnerable. The Israeli violations also reach the medical staff; the Palestine Red Crescent Society (PRCS) reports that 383 violations have been committed against their emergency medical teams in the occupied Palestinian territory in 2022. This includes 19 cases of obstruction of ambulances during their emergency response, 331 cases of denial of access for paramedics to the scene of emergencies, and 14 direct attacks on PRCS staff and volunteers. Escalating violence putting Palestinian lives at risk. At least 7,636 Palestinians, including 747 children, have also been injured by Israeli forces, according to the UN OCHA. The number of live ammunition injuries in August 2022 (128) in the West Bank was the highest since December 2015, except for two exceptional months where large-scale demonstrations took place (December 2017 and May 2021). This year has also seen a 34% increase in the monthly average of incidents of settler violence causing injury or property damage compared to last year, and an 83% increase when compared to 2020. To respond to any crisis of such, preparations should be considered to support the health system during emergencies in fast and effective way. As evidenced by the recent OCHA and UN reports, and our own assessment, the most severely affected demographics are those people with disabilities, elderly, and women (primarily mothers) especially in Area C most marginalized communities due to lack of access and restrictions with movement and lacking the health services provided by MOH.Providing lifesaving items for trauma patients is part of preparedness, and the training of health staff to be ready to respond. MAP has been responding to the increasing violence across the West Bank and East Jerusalem by supporting hospitals with emergency trauma response. In March 2022, we delivered a new ambulance to the PRCS to help bolster the vital work of their paramedic teams and will deliver a second one later this year. This comes on top of our emergency support to PRCS and Palestinian Medical Relief Society first responders and Al-Makassed and St. Joseph hospitals in East Jerusalem in 2021 by providing essential medical supplies for treating the wounded Palestinians.Exacerbating the issues faced because of restricted access and movement is the economic reality which is deteriorating and affecting populations Area C communities, who are threatened always for displacement and demolitions. The poor economic situation is negatively impacting people's lives, health, and safety. Several research surveys concluded that those living in Area C of the WB, are concerned by many barriers and threats to accessing healthcare, including restriction on movement, cost of healthcare services, distance from closest hospital in the cities, cost of transportation, threats of displacement and demolishing of houses, clinics and schools, settler violence and lack of health services. All the issues and challenges faced by Palestinians in this region, culminate to place extreme pressures on family units, and those populations exposed to increased levels of violence document increased levels of GBV, which PMRS and MAP have taken into consideration when designing any program of work, to ensure we are addressing these specific needs within our communities. We have incorporated gender mainstreaming needs. Underlining all our work, in the form of our mobile outreach, we also endeavor to work collaboratively with our communities and local organizations including CBO's. CBOs and local councils etc. to provide education, training, and awareness with respect to general and health topics, which includes first aid, emergency response, preparedness, and planning. Some of the scenarios identified in response to determining the need for the proposed action are and not limited to: clashes between Palestinians and Israeli Occupying Forces and Settler violence and restriction on movement, demolition of houses in area C, climate induced hazards, earthquakes, the already overburdened and under-resourced health system, water and hygiene issues in Area C communities due to lack of clean water, behavioral response to following established protocols, delays of supplies, economic impact on individuals and families who are affected or restricted areas, and provision for people with disabilities within the communities as they may not be able to easily access information or services, we need to ensure that they are being supported throughout the various stages of response. All could contribute to more deaths, injuries, more burden over the PMoH, impact on mental health of care providers and beneficiaries, more restriction on movement of goods, people and supplies, damage to health facilities and lifelines as sewage, water pipes, etc).

2 - Activities

As part of the community preparedness and response in the 20 most marginalized locations in Area C of the WB, each team will be trained on different topics, as shown: 1) 21 hours of first aid training 2) 20 hours of emergency training including evacuation (DRR) 3) 5 hours of training on communications and leadership4) 5 hours of simulation trainingEach team consists of 12 members from the twenty locations chosen, reaching a total of 240 person (140 male, 100 female), and each member will get an emergency equipment set including first aid bags. Each one DRR equipment cost $4,000 and is composed off: movable medical bed, oxygen cylinder, first aid bags, resuscitation equipment, side lamp, bed separation curtain for privacy, blood pressure machine, glucometer, diagnostic set, spinal board, stretcher and movable stairs. 20 DRR set will be procured and delivered for the 20 locations in the most marginalized communities in Area C. Within the trauma response and preparedness, the following are the activities:1) Provide training courses on emergency lifesaving and trauma care for 200 health care workers who will attend Trauma and Evaluation Mangement and Bleeding management courses in West Bank. (Doctors, nurses, and paramedics) - the training will be conducted in cooperation with IMET2000 organization at its accredited training center in Ramallah. IMET2000 will provide the training package at a cost of $170 per participant. As part of the package, trainers' fees are included as well as training materials, stationery, certificates, hall rent and breaks for two days, and coordination services. 2) Verify and procure the list of equipment needed for the West Bank hospitals to respond to the emergency situations. The medical equipment are ECG, Dinamap, Emergency bed, Portable X-Ray, Neck Caller, Chest Tube, Pelvic splint, Extremities Splint, Solid Board, Stylet, DC Shock, Emergency Trolly, Fast Ultrasound, Stretcher's.3) Verify and procure the list of medical equipment4) Conduct a technical committee to select the training items and equipment needed5) Delivery of the training items, and equipment to relevant hospitals6) Conduct visibility work that will be used during the delivery processes of all procured items and during the trainings. For the emergency medical equipment, it includes ECG, Dinamap, emergency bed, neck caller, chest tubes, pelvic splint, extremities splint, solid board, stylet, DC shock, emergency trolly, fast ultrasound and stretchers.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

MAP’s project targets women, men, and youth in the targeted communities in Area C. This ensures that community members have full access to PMRS interventions and services and trainings. Further, through our advocacy and capacity building activities, we aim to get the people’s voices heard and support them to eventually advocate for themselves especially women and youth through strengthening their capacities and improving their well-being. The project will run first aid trainings as well as empowerment activities to allow communities to be prepared for emergencies. Advocacy initiatives led by MAP such as bringing members of the UK parliament to visit these communities plays a significant role in advocating for their rights as well as coordinating with OCHA and other health cluster actors and humanitarian actors in case of risk such as threat of a demolition. MAP being part of the AIDA network adds to the layers of protection provided for these communities by publishing petitions, statements condemning attacks on communities, and appeals to politicians. MAP is also considering coordinating with legal actors to provide legal support for the targeted communities. MAP and PMRS have identified several risks that the project is expected to face and the measures to mitigate them. Regarding the of the political situation deteriorates further, the targeted communities are targeted for home demolitions by the Israeli military and demolitions leave families vulnerable. Usually, the PMRS team is not negatively affected by demolitions or by other military activity. The team will still be able to reach communities and if not, a nearby location is chosen which is convenient for patients. As for project staff and patients being unable to access the location for the training, the locations will be chosen to be mutually convenient for volunteers and staff. On the resources level, if the trainings lack necessary equipment and kits, MAP will ensure that the project budget for intervention is based on a needs’ assessment, and the team of PMRS will closely monitor the interventions and activities. MAP is ensuring compliance with the Core Humanitarian Standards, which includes safeguarding, accountability, participation and relevancy of interventions.
