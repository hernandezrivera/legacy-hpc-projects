Project Summary

The situation in Northern Borno has been deteriorating for the last three years, it does not seem likely that IDPs are going to return soon to their village of origin. July 2021, IOM Displacement Tracking matrix data indicates a total of 2.191 million internally displaced people (IDPs) in North-East Nigeria of which about 74.5% are displaced in Borno State, most of them living in and around the urban area of Maiduguri, the State’s Capital. The total number of IDPs fluctuates. While in southern Borno and in Adamawa State people are returning to their village of origin, in the rest of Borno people continue to flee from insecurity, created by attacks from BH and battles between Government Forces and BH fighters. These movements present a major humanitarian challenge as resources are often already overstretched in the locations in which these civilians arrive.
The proposed integrated project addresses the urgent WASH and Food security needs of 26,620 vulnerable IDPs and host communities in Jere and Monguno. The proposed project will build on the operational and implementation experience and consolidated gains by the former projects in the Jere and Monguno areas since 2017. The efforts by these projects have reduced protection and GBV concerns.
The proposed project to be implemented Jere(Gongulong and 5 satellite villages (Bulamari, Gabdori, Gumsuri, Ngonari and Yerimari) and 3 camps Monguno(Fulatari, GGSS and Waterboard) is designed to address the critical unmet humanitarian needs of displaced people. The project is well-aligned with the cluster strategic response plans and the priorities of the call by strengthening the resilience of communities and contributing to efforts to address vulnerabilities and underlying structural issues.
The project is designed with a conflict-sensitive approach concerning IDPs and host populations Specific vulnerable groups, such as female-headed households, landless people, and people with special needs will be prioritized. The project will contribute significantly to gender equality, protection concerns, and women and girls' specific needs will be addressed to ensure equitable access to essential humanitarian services. Gender-balanced staff will be recruited, and women will be engaged in all consultations and distributions. Gender and disability disaggregated M&E data will be collected for implementing barrier-removing interventions. Duty of Care, Safeguarding, conflict sensitivity, Do No Harm are crosscutting elements throughout all activities of the proposed intervention. For instance, latrines are designed to include a ramp, a handrail and a raised seat for people living with disabilities and the aged. Wet feeding activities will be designed taking into consideration access risks which limit the elderly, people with chronic illness, and PWD by ensuring there are priority areas set up specifically for these groups. To sustain gender and protection actions, the project will construct and rehabilitate Child Protection Spaces, Provide mental health, and counselling services, sensitization and awareness on child protection using print and electronic media and also engage in advocacy and information sharing. through media out reach programs, support to caregivers to influence positive parenting. The project will also engage with actors in the referral pathways on policy development and legislation.

1 - Needs

ZOA conducted a rapid needs assessment from 20th to 27th October 2021 in Gongulong and 5 satellite villages in Jere and 5 camps in Monguno. This assessment took place in liaison with the Salient humanitarian organization in Gongulong and SI in Monguno. ZOA engaged enumerators to carry out an assessment of WASH facilities using a household questionnaire designed in the ODK/ Kobo tool, Key informant interview (KII), and focus group discussion (FGD). As of 24th October 2021, the Borno state ministry of health reported 624 number of suspected cholera cases in Jere. Water:Since 2017, ZOA has been implementing lifesaving assistance to vulnerable individuals in Jere and Monguno. ZOA assessed 20 water facilities (hand pumps and solar boreholes) in Gongulong and 5 satellite villages. 85% of these water facilities were identified to be either dysfunctional or partly functional. 1 solar borehole was also identified to be dysfunctional in waterboard IDP camp Monguno. This altogether accounts for a water gap of 29,500 individuals who need access to safe and potable drinking water. Sanitation and Hygiene:The WASH rapid assessment report identified latrine gaps in Jere and Monguno. These emergency latrines have served beneficiaries for some time now and most of them will need to be decommissioned and rebuilt. Due to the increasing number of individuals who access latrines and bathing shelters, latrines are easily filled up. Facilities in the host communities continue to be overstretched due to the increasing number of IDPs who share those facilities. 47 blocks (5 stances per block) were identified for decommissioning as the facilities have become structurally weak and exceeded their original plan for utilization. New arrivals from satellite villages who recently relocated to Gongulong on a large security basis now share existing facilities with host communities and IDPs. These facilities as constructed to align with the WASH sector standards eventually get overstretched. The sanitary condition of these facilities gets compromised as a result of the increased number of users with little knowledge of proper latrine use. Hygiene promotion which is aimed at improving the general hygiene condition needs to continue in these locations for an impact. Wet feeding:Monguno has been hugely affected by the ongoing conflict and has continued to record attempted infiltration by the AOGs. Monguno, due to the presence of humanitarian actors plays host to displaced individuals mostly from neighbouring Kukawa, Marte and Guzamala that are hugely inaccessible. New arrivals are hosted at the reception centre before being relocated to the IDP camps. INTERSOS has been managing the reception centres in Monguno since January 2018. INTERSOS carried out a Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) at the reception centres to identify inter-sectoral humanitarian needs to identify needs and gaps as regards health, nutrition, WASH and protection in the reception centres of Monguno. Results from the needs assessment indicated the need for immediate feeding support for new arrivals.
