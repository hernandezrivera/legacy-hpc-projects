Project Summary

Aims of this project include Iidentification and assistance of unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) and other children at risk carried out through protection monitoring, community-based child protection mechanisms, and broad-level awareness raising within communities. Vulnerable children such as unaccompanied and separated minors, children with disabilities, trafficked children/former CAAGs and others will be provided with case management services (including best interest assessments, as required), psychosocial support (PSS) and material assistance at child friendly spaces (CFS) and through outreach towards urban IDP children. Family tracing/reunification services by ICRC and other actors will be leveraged, and through coordination and data sharing, UNHCR will advocate for the expansion of such services in the BAY States. Returning children (former CAAG) will be supported in the reintegration process through the above noted areas of intervention.
Alternative care arrangements and community-based child protection committees comprising of willing foster parents/caregivers (both men and women) will be established and/or maintained in all project locations. Both the community-based child protection committees and the alternative care arrangements will be regularly capacitated on children’s rights and protection. Capacity building and awareness raising on children’s rights and protection will be directed towards communities and duty bearers. Kids clubs will provide sports/games, art and creativity, human rights, debate, music, dance, and drama for children, mitigating child protection risks. UNHCR will continue enhancing the child protection legal framework, interagency coordination of humanitarian assistance, reporting, and information sharing through its active participation in the Child Protection Sub-Sector and the National Agency for the Prohibition of Trafficking of Persons (NAPTIP) at the state level.

1 - Needs

According to the Child Protection Guidance Note for the 2023 Humanitarian Response Plan produced by the CP AoR, approximately 2.1 million people need child protection services due to the absence of child protection systems and community-based safety nets, out which 1.3 million children need child protection services, The AoR aims to reach 1.5m PiN (1,050,000 children, 53% girls and 450,000 caregivers, 53% women). The CP AoR priorities 13 LGAs in Adamawa, 21 in Borno and 14 in Yobe with the highest severity ranking of child protection needs as per the Humanitarian Needs Overview. To ensure that all children boys and girls are free from violence, exploitation, abuse, neglect, trafficking, harmful practices and are safe /thriving to their full potential, the AoR members will focus on prevention and response with a systems strengthening approach in consideration of the humanitarian and development nexus as well as the evolving situation in the Northeast.Child Protection needs have increased in 2023 due to the sustained military offensive leading to high influx of people from NSAG, inflation, high poverty rates and relocations of IDPs etc. Priority needs identified by the CP AoR are case management services for victims of abuse, neglect and violence, family tracing/ reunification/ alternative community care, Mental Health and Psycho-Social Support (MHPSS), socio-economic reintegration of former CAAG and other vulnerable children, addressing child marriage, trafficking, child labor, low birth registration rates, social norms affecting children’s rights and critical support to children with disabilities. There is also a need to strengthen the child protection system, through setting up building blocks for the same, including through capacity building of social welfare workforce, community-based child protection committees, strengthening coordination at LGAs,focus on data and information systems for evidence based programming and advocacy, partners institutional capacity support through trainings.

2 - Activities

In collaboration and coordination with relevant stakeholders, a wide range of activities will be implemented to strengthen community-based child protection systems and structures, promote participation and well-being of children (case management, psychosocial support, recreational and educational activities), capacity building of duty bearers, sensitization of communities on children’s rights, and material support to children with heightened vulnerabilities (UAMs, children with disabilities, trafficked children/former CAAGs, etc.). The following activities will be implemented: Activity 1: Sensitization on child rights and protection - Awareness and sensitization sessions on child protection, children’s rights, and related issues with children and communities will be conducted reaching 106,000 individuals. Will include IEC materials and radio call in programmes. - International commemorative events such as International Day of the Girl Child, World Children’s days and 16 DoA will be used to bolster awareness raising and sensitization on children’s rights. - Awareness raising will include PSEA, and the available community-based complaint and feedback mechanisms, Activity 2: Strengthened community-based child protection strengthened for identifying, reintegrating and supporting children at risk - Identification of children at risk (UAMs, children with disabilities, trafficked children/former CAAGs, etc.) through child protection monitoring from partner staff and capacitated community-based child protection structures. - Approximately 65,000 children under the age of 5 will be identified and provided with birth certificates. - Training on children’s rights of 325 community-based child protection committees and alternative care structures, with regular meetings. Important to this capacity building will be to increase communities’ knowledge on feedback and reporting. - Alternative care providers will be provided with training on positive parenting skills as well as children’s rights. Activity 3: Case Management, PSS and other activities at Child Friendly Spaces - Management of Child Friendly Spaces in Banki, Bama and Monguno in Borno state. Through CFSs and urban outreach, 3,600 at risk children (e.g. UAMs, trafficked children/former CAAGs, etc.) will be provided with case management. Additional CFS activities include PSS (individual and group counselling for children and caregivers), life skills, literacy and other recreational and awareness raising activities. - Approximately 1,600 BIAs will be completed and 150 UAMs placed under alternative care, and 180 supported with FTS (leveraging other service providers). Activity 4: Provision of material assistance - Material support such as dignity and hygiene kits (gender appropriate) will be provided to 2,500 children at risk, including UAMs, children with disabilities, trafficked children/former CAAGs, etc. - Some 150 wheelchairs and visual/hearing aids provided to children with disabilities. - Back-to- school-kits (uniforms, scholastic materials) will be provided to 1,426 at risk children. - 150 foster parents provided with core relief items to strengthen the sustainability of the arrangement. Activity 5: Skills training for minors and alternative care providers - Approximately 2525 At risk aadolescent girls, boys and caregivers will be trained on life and eentrepreneurship skills and receive start-up material support. Activity 6: Safe School Initiatives - Inter-school/inter-class competitions (art, poetry, melody, debate, etc.) in selected schools to identify talent, engage children and youth in positive behaviour and promote empowerment will be supported, targeting 1,618 children. Children at risk will be identified in course of activities. 8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The key protection principles underpinning the provision of humanitarian assistance informed the design of this project and as such will be observed during the project implementation. UNHCR will adopt a rights-based approach in the planning, implementation and evaluation during the entirety of the project. To ensure comprehensive protection mainstreaming across this project, the following measures will be taken for each of the protection principles: • Prioritize safety and dignity and avoid doing harm during the project implementation, UNHCR will closely monitor the security situation of the area of intervention, and mobile teams will use safe and authorized roads to access people in need. • To ensure meaningful and equal access, in the identification of beneficiaries, men, women, boys and girls will be prioritized based on need alone. Particularly vulnerable groups such as female- and child-headed households, but also elderly and persons with disability will be prioritized. The composition of the assessment teams will reflect gender balance, to facilitate contact with women and girls. • If necessary, additional measures will be taken to reach persons with disabilities and others extremely vulnerable. This can be the case of disabled persons unable to reach the protection desks. • In order to promote accountability, participation and empowerment of beneficiaries, beneficiaries’ participation to the project will be promoted by involving, to the widest extent possible, men, women, boys and girls of different ages in the community-based structures.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

In adherence to UNHCR’s Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and Age Gender Diversity approach, UNHCR will strengthen community-based feedback mechanisms enabling men, women, boys and girls to express feedback/complaint. Beneficiaries will be informed of the existence of such mechanisms, and it will be ensured that they are comfortable in accessing it. Through these, UNHCR will ensure that issues of concern to individuals, as well as, to the community are brought to its attention and other partners, for intervention. Vulnerability screening will be used to identify and target those in need of child protection-based materials. UNHCR and child protection partner will also ensure that staff involved in the project comply with its code of conduct and the special measures for protection from sexual exploitation and abuse, child safeguarding and child protection policy. UNHCR’s firm commitment to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality strive to ensure the ‘Do No Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities are not inadvertently worsening tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance. For this reason, a strong Monitoring & Evaluation (M&E) as well as continuous communications with stakeholders, including local leaders and government authorities as well as beneficiaries through Participatory Approach, are key to guide activities and monitor the evolution of the conflict and insurgency.
