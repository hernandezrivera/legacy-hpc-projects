Project Summary

The overall objective of this one-year life-saving intervention is to increase access to and stabilise regular attendance in 43 primary/ECD/ALP schools (Ayod-26, Ulang-9, Longechuk-8) for 17,143 conflict-affected children and youth (7,371 girls) in IDP, returnee and host community areas, especially girls, through provision of sustaining activities such as school meal; and life-saving WASH in schools activities in Ayod, Ulang & Longechuk Counties of Jonglei and Upper Nile States. 8,572 learners will be supported in Ayod, 5,143 in Ulang and 3,428 in Longechuk.
The envisaged activities include establishing rapid project baseline to map out gaps for inception ; construction of temporary learning spaces to provide safer and protective for additional learners; provision of quality ECD teaching & learning resources, equipment and educational materials to 15 ECD Centres in Ayod (8), Ulang (4) & Longechuk (3); training 430 volunteer teachers on Child Rights, Child Protection and Prevention of Gender-based Violence (GBV) and Psycho-social support(PSS); enhancing the technical capacity of 3 County Education Departments (CEDs) in Ayod, Ulang & Longechuk; establishment/rehabilitation of school WASH facilities including provision of soap; setting up 43 School WASH Clubs to improve and sustain school WASH efforts. 1,548 girls will be provided with menstrual hygiene products/sanitary towels for dignity and continuity of schooling; (Including training); sensitising 1, 693 community members (Ayod-846, Ulang-508, Longechuk-339); and training 129 local education officials (CEDs/PES) on supportive school supervision.
1,956 local actors will be directly involved to provide a focus and structure to the schoolchildren. 81 county education officials will be involved to provide much needed first legal and social interface with the affected population and prepare local education systems for resilience in the face of unforeseen crises. School WASH Clubs will be strengthened to help disseminate key life-saving messages on cholera prevention, food crisis/ rationing and referral pathways for learners requiring psychosocial support. Overall, upwards of 22,980 individuals are planned to benefit directly through this plan.

1 - Needs

From a total of 2.8 million learners in need, children from Greater Upper Nile region make up 1.1 million. Girl enrollment is the lowest as compared to rest of the country. The counties of Ayod, Ulang and Longechuk have been repeatedly affected by the impact of the conflict and adverse weather (floods) over the past three years. There are 6 girls to every 10 boys in South Sudan, and lowest in Greater Upper Nile. Child abduction, child recruitment into armed forces, child marriage, food insecurity, lack of gender-sensitive WASH facilities especially for girl children to manage their menstrual cycle, lack of adequate and qualified teachers (especially female), have resulted to low attendance/ irregular or absenteeism (Education Cluster, 2019). The results of flooding in these counties have been severe; first causing accessibility challenges but also destruction of WASH facilities in schools. CMD assessments in schools during and after the floods indicate collapsed latrines and major destruction of part of the learning spaces. In 2020, it is anticipated that latrine access will be less than 20%, with the majority of leaners expected to openly practice inappropriate waste disposal habits in lack of intervention (REACH, 2019). Most of the schools in Ayod, Ulang and Longechuk Counties are overcrowded with inadequate water and sanitation facilities. For girls and youth in particular, early drop out and marriage have been ongoing effects of the food crisis, as families require the economic dowry for household survival. Non-existence of school feeding programme in hard to reach areas is also contributing to hunger, malnutrition and dropout of children from schools; no schools under these counties fall under WFP SF programme. A lack of intervention will erode gains achieved, and prevent children from accessing the protective factors of school (including psychosocial support, lifesaving messaging and access to other services), placing them at risk of abuse and exploitation. It is now more important than ever that children and youth are supported through school feeding and engaged in dissemination of life-saving messages. CMD sees schools as safe zones for the creation of secure and daily routines expressing normalcy during emergencies; and as a community model for establishment of good relationships, in which the child is being heard, cared for, respected. This is a potent reason for a call to action and plan is a potent step in that direction.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Children make up over 60% of South Sudanese internally displaced persons and refugees. Many have been separated from their parents and guardians and have undertaken arduous, risky journeys to safety unaccompanied. They face constant risks associated with explosive hazards, lack of civil documentation to prove their existence, and out of sheer desperation many girls and boys are married off at a young age and withdrawn from school to work, often in dangerous condition. This project recognizes vulnerable children’s rights to education and child protection during emergencies or early recovery by ensuring they have access to safe, inclusive and protective educational services and are not deprived of their psychosocial needs or exposed to traumatic events, recruitment into armed forces and armed groups, and sexual violence.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

All beneficiaries (Either as host or displaced as planned) have opportunities and the capacity to participate effectively and equitably, including both men and women, with special attention to stakeholder groups that may be marginalised such as persons with disabilities and Internally displaced.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Scaling up psychosocial support, capacity building to enhance outreach of essential services, establishing and operationalizing community child protection and resilience plans and supporting local education authorities to foster basic service delivery will lead to the improved restoration of services for the IDP, Returnee and Host population and greater social protection for vulnerable children and youth in Ayod, Ulang & Longechuk. Considering the current level of fragility in South Sudan, the project seeks to promote resilience-focused approaches to strengthen the ability of families and communities to withstand, adapt and to quickly recover from stress and shocks. This is an important step towards establishing or maintaining community educational services and child protection networks and Payam service delivery plans which will reduce the risk to fail in the long- term and contribute to the overall national capacity development. These could be potential entry points for integration of locally embedded early recovery and resilience mechanisms into programming.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

This one-year project, seeks to ensure prevention and risk mitigation interventions are sufficient in 3 Counties of Jonglei & Upper Nile States as returnees and hosts come to terms with rehabilitation and rebuilding. It will specifically provide community-based protection mechanisms in 15 Payams (5 in Ayod, 5 in Ulang & 5 in Longechuk) to make sure children’s and youth’s experiences of violence in their community, schools and homes, along with repeated displacements, loss of or separation from family members and friends, dramatic deterioration in living conditions, divisions in their communities, and lack of basic social services do not profoundly affect children’s and young people’s wellbeing and development.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Affected children’s access to protection from deprivation are the primary functions of emergency resilience. Emergency sanitation for children will be improved in particular through the consideration of children of different age groups and disability factors. This will include providing seats for young children; or using smaller key hole pedestals/squat plats on top of normal adult versions; provision of a ramp into the toilets for access for children with disabilities, and handles on inside of toilet; making toilet locks useable by children and making sure toilet doors close inwards to avoid children swinging on them.WASH facilities will be right sized and age-appropriate (easy to use, easy to clean, and easy for adopting good hygiene habits such as anal cleansing and hand- washing) and separate for boys and girls. For older students, girls’ menstrual hygiene needs must be met by ensuring handwashing area is available for before and after menstrual hygiene product use and discreet place for disposing/burning menstrual materials). Pit-latrines will be located at the back of the classroom within 10 meters; at least 15 meters (downhill) from a water source; and behind the classroom for privacy purposes. These measures will boost beneficiaries’ ease of access to facilities.CMD takes a serious and proactive approach to ensure all of its activities are safe for children and youth. This approach is outlined in its Child Protection Policy, which holds staff and volunteers to a high standard of responsibility for ensuring the safety and protection of the children and youth with whom they interact. The guidelines and code of conduct contained in the Policy apply to children under the age of 18.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The project will use capacity building of local actors (PES, PTAs/SMCs, BoGs, CEDs, etc.) to enhance outreach of essential services, establishing and operationalising community education and resilience plans and supporting local authorities to foster basic service delivery which will lead to the improved restoration of services for the IDP, Returnee and Host population and greater social protection for vulnerable children and youth in Jonglei Upper Nile. Sustainability is further enhanced by the project’s potential to contribute to the improvement in household food security through the provision of a meal that functions as an income transfer for some 34,286 parents (each child impacts at least 2 adults) in Ayod, Ulang & Longechuk Counties. School meals have been shown to facilitate access to school, increase enrolment and attendance rates and improve the nutritional status, health and cognitive development of children. Together with the Nutrition & WASH components, it will contribute to raising awareness amongst the local community on health/hygiene issues/ the value of education and the full participation of girls. That holds the potential of benefit against time.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

The first working assumption of the project is that the political and security situation remains conducive to the project. The associated risk is that the security situation may deteriorate in a way that the continuation of the project is no longer possible. To mitigate against this likelihood, CMD will sensitise key stakeholders and capacitate them in a way that enhances the likelihood that the protection of vulnerable children will be improved, even in the absence of the project. There may also be a situation of difficulty for women to participate in the intervention. Their active participation in the social protection of vulnerable children and youth may be undermined and effectively undercut. CMD will engage in community outreach to explain the project’s objectives and importance of engaging women and girls. The project will not address issues which require changes in policies and legal frameworks.In this early recovery and transitional context, CMD’s life-saving intervention will use child protection, GBV prevention and school feeding to assist in the restoration of education systems, to encourage the return of internally displaced persons (IDPs) and refugees, and to promote social cohesion among children. The project will focus primarily on IDPs, returnees and their hosts to encourage adaptation and integration. Ayod, Ulang & Longechuk Counties are accessible by air from Juba and by road from Bor, Malakal (South Sudan) or Gambella (Ethiopia). All project locations remain safe for carrying out project activities. It is hoped this intervention will work as an incentive to draw children to safe spaces that offer additional support and support local authorities in service delivery.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

CMD maintains a Stakeholder Participation Guidance, a policy guidebook detailing processes and procedures for fostering greater transparency, trust and effectiveness in empowering vulnerable, poor, rural and marginalised communities, particularly groups that are likely to be ignored, misrepresented or underrepresented such as girls and women, children, and youth. In addition, CMD’s staff are dedicated and have a sound understanding of SFP and WASH principles and standards in emergency response and recovery. They are also familiar with project planning methods and tools (e.g. use of the logical framework). CMD will remain accountable to all affected people in its areas of operation. School Management Committees (SMCs)/Parent-Teacher Associations (PTAs) and School WASH Clubs will be inclusive of persons with disability and will be involved to set priorities, provide feedback and monitor project activities at school level. Frequent and two-way communication with communities will enable CMD to access relevant information, express needs, ask questions and provide feedback on services. The IDP/returnee and host community children and their caregivers will participate in age-appropriate psychosocial and GBV prevention strategies and activities and children will be actively involved in the design and implementation of the activities. Children will help shape and design the psychosocial support activities and determine the times and days that are most convenient.
