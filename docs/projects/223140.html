Project Summary

The proposed programme aims to enhance access to quality education for out-of-school children in Yobe State by addressing the identified barriers. Providing scholastic materials to vulnerable out of school children to support their education needs. Training teachers on psychosocial support (PSS) and referral processes will improve the educational environment for conflict-affected children. Life skills education will equip children with resilience and personal development tools, while menstrual hygiene management support will promote girls’ attendance and health. Implementing the Accelerated Basic Education Program (ABEP) curriculum will provide foundational learning opportunities to out-of-school children, increasing enrolment and retention rates. This programme aligns with national priorities and policies, aiming to bridge the educational gaps caused by conflict and ensure that all children have access to safe and quality education.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

To ensure protection mainstreaming, the project will integrate child protection and gender equality principles into all activities. First, all staff will receive training on child safeguarding, gender sensitivity, and protection risks to ensure a safe and supportive environment for all children, especially those vulnerable due to displacement, gender, or disability. Gender-sensitive approaches will be incorporated in community engagement, ensuring that boys and girls, as well as children with disabilities, have equal access to education and support services. Additionally, child-friendly spaces will be created for psychosocial support, and mechanisms will be in place for reporting abuse or exploitation. The project will involve communities in the design and monitoring of activities through SBMCs, ensuring local accountability. Data collection will be disaggregated by age, gender, and disability to track and address disparities. These measures, alongside continuous feedback loops, will ensure that protection concerns are addressed in a holistic and inclusive manner throughout the project.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

To ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members, the project will employ several strategies: 1. **Inclusive Community Engagement**: We will involve community leaders, faith-based organizations, and local representatives in mobilizing and encouraging participation from vulnerable groups, such as girls, IDPs, children with disabilities, and those from marginalized communities. 2. **Focus Group Discussions (FGDs)**: Regular FGDs will be conducted with children, parents, and community members, ensuring that vulnerable individuals, especially women, girls, and disabled children, are represented and their voices heard. 3. **Anonymous Feedback Channels**: To avoid stigma or fear of retribution, we will establish anonymous feedback mechanisms such as suggestion boxes or hotlines, where vulnerable individuals can safely share their concerns and suggestions. 4. **Child-Friendly Surveys**: We will use simple, child-friendly surveys and tools to gather feedback directly from children, ensuring that even the most marginalized children have the opportunity to participate. 5. Community Monitoring Committees: School-Based Management Committees (SBMCs) and other local groups will be trained to gather and relay feedback from vulnerable populations, ensuring that their needs are continuously assessed and addressed. These strategies will help ensure that feedback from vulnerable community members is consistently collected and integrated into project implementation and adjustments.
