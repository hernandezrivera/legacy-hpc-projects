Project Summary

The project aims to increase food availability and food access for drought affected communities in Mogadishu and Lower Shabelle IDPs. This will be done through commodity food voucher distribution to the affected households. Voucher distributions are the most appropriate modality in all the targeted areas, because there are functional markets. Low purchasing power of vulnerable local inhabitants dampens market incentives and limits the incentive of traders and wholesalers to expand markets. Through the provision of food vouchers, this project will assist in stimulating these markets. Using vouchers is also preferential as there would be cost saving rather than cash transfers which is costly due to higher transfer costs charged by money vendors. Food vouchers will be provided on a monthly basis for 3 months of the project period to 4300 targeted households (25,800 Individuals) in Daynile, Kahda and Dharkenley IDPs in Mogadishu and Afgooye and Merka district IDPs of Lower Shabelle regions.
This assistance will address acute food needs by bolstering the purchasing power of targeted households through the projected lean season. The proposed voucher will provide access to a food basket consisting of national rice/Wheat flour/Pasta, beans/lentils/Tuna fish, and vegetable oil) supplying 80% of daily caloric needs of a family of 5-7 members. Conditionality is subject to completion of a variety of infrastructure and environmental rehabilitation projects aimed to improve local food security, environmental degradation and market access conditions. The monthly voucher value will be based on the FSNAU MEB for Somalia. The community members will identify and select assets that are not in proper working condition and those that have been damaged by human activities or natural calamities for rehabilitation/restoration. Possible cash for work activities will be bush clearing, erosion control, digging latrine pits, oad work to improve access to markets, basic services and other work to preserve community assets and recover from effects of drought. Women and men will be equally involved in determining the final gender appropriate cash for work activities. Care will be taken to select activities that allow a high percentage of women to participate. Work norms will be established to ensure a reasonable quantity of work but allow women time to carry out their family responsibilities. SOYDA will also provide agricultural input support targeted to 1500 HH. Agricultural input support shall be included seeds (15 kg of maize/sorghum, 8 kg of beans and 4 kg of sesame) for 1500 farmers HH and 1500 sets of farming tools, each set shall contain (1 hoes, 1 Stainless Pick & Mattock, 1 Machete, 1 Shovel and 1 Rake).
There will be regular coordination meetings at the field level between Food security and other relevant clusters to foster synergy, avoid duplication of efforts/ resources and share information on response efforts. SOYDA will keep the food security cluster, Ministry of Agriculture and UNOCHA regularly informed on progress of activities through sharing of progress reports and also share any challenges encountered for rapid assistance.

Project Activities

1. Provide food voucher value of USD 60 per month for 3 months and consisting of cereal 50 kg, oil 5 kg, sugar 15 kg and pulses 10 kg to 4300 households (25,800 beneficiaries) of vulnerable people in Afgooye, Marka district of lower shabelle region and Daynile and Dharkenley Districts-Benadir Region. 2. Provision of cash relief worth 65 USD per month for 3 months to 1250 HH (578 men headed households and 672 women headed households) in communities in crisis focusing more on the most vulnerable individuals both men and women in the target areas. 3. Creating of temporary job opportunities to 400 households (194 men headed households and 206 women headed households) just before the rainy seasons through cash for work activities and providing 5 USD daily rate to the laborers per 0.5 M3 for canals, 0.3 M3 for water catchments and 5 M2 for feeder roads working for 24 days a month. 4. SOYDA will conduct serial community mobilizations; sensitizations and participation at each target villages that will bring together with the local leaders, women, youth and disabled representatives among target beneficiaries to inform about the activities, agree with the beneficiary/farmers selection criteria and allocations of each targeted villages considering poor farmers and drought affected HHs with access to land, women headed households. However, once the selection criteria and allocations agreed, SOYDA field staff in collaboration with respected village elders/women and in coordination with the respective district authority will select the target number of beneficiaries as per agreed selection criteria and allocations.5. SOYDA will also provide agricultural input support targeted to 1500 HH. Agricultural input support shall be included seeds (15 kg of maize/sorghum, 8 kg of beans and 4 kg of sesame) for 1500 farmers HH and 1500 sets of farming tools, each set shall contain (1 hoes, 1 Stainless Pick & Mattock, 1 Machete, 1 Shovel and 1 Rake).6. Organize and conduct capacity building training on resource management, early warning system, disaster risk reduction, good agricultural practices and conflict resolution for the 400 community, farmers, religious/community leaders, women leaders (60% women) and (25 persons per week) selected from the targeted communities in Lower Shabelle.7. Provision of vocational trainings to 70 (30 young men and 40 young women) individuals especially the unemployed youth in urban area and provide them with materials to start up a business of their own. 8. Provision of vocational trainings to 30 unemployed young men in fishing communities and provide them with boats, fishing nets and other equipment to enable them start up a business for themselves. 9. SOYDA will hire consultant that work with SOYDA field staff to conduct the post distribution assessment (PDA) of agricultural input distribution to determine the degree of effectiveness, efficiency and impacts of the agricultural input distribution to the drought affected farmers HHs.

Average Coping Strategies Index (CSI) score for the target population: 45 The CSI is to be calculated according to WFP methodology (frequency x weight). The full (not the reduced) CSI should be the main outcome indicator for livelihoods projects and for multi-purpose assistance in combination with relevant sector-specic outcome indicators. This indicator will measure whether HHs have relied on certain behaviors within the past 7 days that would suggest they had been faced with food insecurity 4. % of the target population with acceptable Food Consumption Score (FCS): 50, The FCS measures dietary diversity, energy and macro and micro value of the food consumed at HH level. FCS score is calculated according to WFP methodology and definition of thresholds. The “acceptable” range is generally designed as a score greater than 35. However, in the context of Somalia, the score cut-off levels are higher, and achievement against indicator will be measured as the % of households who report an FCS equal or greater than 42. Range to be used will be >42: Acceptable, >28<42: Borderline, </=28: Poor 5. Number of households reporting to improved food access and protected livelihood assets. Target: 1800 6. Mitigation Measures

During displacement in emergency period there are many risk related to exclusion based on the displacement such risk factor are, child labor, sexual exploitation, discrimination and poor documentation on the specific needs of the people at risk. To mitigate this, SOYDA will however, put in place strict policy that will protect the right of women and children in emergency and conflict status, it will ensure all its staff are capacity build on child right, women, vulnerable groups and protection mainstreaming in its livelihood and food security activities, it shall also advocate community awareness in its implementation of livelihood through equal representation of community relief committee and village elders responsible for selecting the most vulnerable household as well ensure no harm policy within its implementation of the program. SOYDA will strive to adopt most reasonable project implementation methodology such as mobile outreach registration of beneficiaries in the far village to enable take services to the most vulnerable with less mobility status, through this, SOYDA will achieved handsomely on wider coverage to its target population. SOYDA will ensure to protects its beneficiary information and as well adopt reasonable program location that will ensure access and safety of the beneficiary. The following are some of the risk factors facing the target population such factors are, discrimination against race,sex or gender employment opportunity, sexual exploitation in exchange of relief food as well as poor documentation, monitoring and reporting within the program implementation. SOYDA will however, mitigated such factors without hinderance,it intends to provide equal opportunity for employment to all these qualified ad within the local areas as first priority and based on their skills, it shall also ensure both men and women boys and girls have equal access to project information and services in the target areas, through this program SOYDA will ensure its staff and community members were capacity built on “DO NO HARM” principle of neutrality as well as disseminates project information to the community members through consultative forums and in particular religious institution such as mosque. This will facilitate more inclusive of both gender and various community members hence create an atmosphere of more responsive coverage among the target group. SOYDA will ensure the safety and dignity of its beneficiaries by providing regular monitoring and reporting of the new challenges and opportunity in consultation and coordination withother stakeholders and community key point. The following are main risk factors that might impact negatively on the beneficiaries during implementation such factors are poor timing during distribution of foods or agricultural input to the beneficiary which may fall late in the day hence susceptible for theft, cross fire, intimidation as well as sexual exploitation among poor women and girls beneficiaries, it may also risk of beneficiary by registering one main clan against other dwellers in settlement through collusion with project staff hence this might create community tension in this case conflict might a raise thus dangering the safety of these vulnerable population. Also leaking of vital beneficiary data might be risk associated with arm conflict and violence this is because in the instance militia groups get the beneficiary details they might intimidate and force to surrender the little foods provided thus risking the life of the vulnerable women, men, boys and girls population. In mitigating the above SOYDA intends to establish strict beneficiary data protection for safety purpose as well as facilitates equal representation among the target community in selection of community village committee that will directly work with SOYDA food security teams. Staff and community capacity shall be build and ensure staff signed code of conduct
