1 - Needs

According to the HNO 2022, 8.4 million people are in need of assistance in Nigeria. The continuing conflict is mainly affecting populations located in Borno (the epicenter), Adamawa and Yobe States (BAY). 9 of the 19 most affected LGAs are located in Borno State. There are 2.2 million IDPs either in camps or host communities. As of beginning of November 2022, the Government National Emergency Management Agency (NEMA) reporting, captured by OCHA and IOM, indicates that 3.2 million people were affected by floods, with more than 600 dying and 1.4 million displaced.WASH needs remain precariously high in SI’s NE areas of operation with 14% of Borno Population facing a WaSH living standards gap and 32% of sites below standard for drinkable water (15l/p/d). As per SI needs assessment in NE, 17% of the population does not have access to drinking water – as per community criteria (smell, taste, color), and 89% declared the quantity available as insufficient to fulfil their cooking and drinking needs. Water points available are indeed getting stressed by extra demand due to IDP influx and the lack of functional infrastructures. For 33% of the surveyed populations, the average distance from their dwellings to the water points remains above Sphere standards (>500m). Similarly, 57% of sanitation facilities in Monguno and 86% in Ngala are partially or not functional and need maintenance or major repair. The caseload per latrine is also very high (35 persons/latrine on average in Monguno, Dikwa and Ngala) and justifies the rapid deterioration of the existing facilities which besides being considered unsafe and unhygienic, are not able to guarantee the dignity and privacy, especially of women, girls and PWD according to SI MSNA of 2022, 73% of respondents reported that the latrines have no functional lock and 89% reported that latrines have no functioning lighting. In addition, 10% of the latrines are located more than 50m from the dwellings. Open defecation that continues to be observed in many locations, increases the risks of waterborne disease: only just 51% of households stated having a place for open defecation nearby. On average, 26% of the surveyed individuals were not able to quote 3 key handwashing moments i.e., after defecation, before preparing food, after eating food. Furthermore, only 34% of women said they have acceptable material for menstrual hygiene (cotton cloth, this question was only asked to women). Access to soap seems to be the main barrier to handwashing with soap. IDPs who settle in host communities (HC) due to the camp over congestion suffer from the lack of WASH infrastructures, including in schools which also absorb the new arrivals flows and medium-term IDPs (30% pressure). The COVID-19 and the threat of other waterborne disease such as cholera also strengthen the need to have functional WASH infrastructures in place.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

All the SI staff are experienced and trained on mainstreaming protection as a cross-cutting theme across all programs to increase the efficiency of humanitarian assistance. All staff also sign a Code of Conduct, ensuring they provide the program services to all members in the targeted communities, regardless of gender, age, religious belief, and are inclusive to people with additional or special needs, such as disabled or elderly people. In addition, SI prioritizes the needs of various vulnerable groups in their programming (children, women, people with special needs, elderly). SI strategy program is based on a community approach and the organization is used to organize gender and disability inclusive Focus Group Discussion (FGDs) in order to better identify the specific needs of the most vulnerable beneficiaries. In addition, it has already developed monitoring tools that include protection mainstreaming indicators and has included questions related to the protection mainstreaming in all its surveys to measure that humanitarian assistance is delivered safely, accessible, accountable, and participatory. This monitoring tool will be used to monitor this project’s activities. Indeed, where ever possible, daily workers will be recommended by their own communities and grouped respecting gender equity, to increase local visibility and accountability, and reduces risks.
