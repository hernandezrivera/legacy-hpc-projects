Project Summary

During January to April 2020, food security situation is expected to deteriorate throughout Jonglei state, and Crisis outcomes are expected to persist in Uror, Pochalla and Twic East Counties. Admissions to Tearfund Feeding Centres continue to rise even in the post-harvest season when typically, malnutrition rates dip as the dry season brings improved access for nutrition and health services as well as humanitarian assistance, and a reduction in morbidity and outbreaks of disease. The high levels of food insecurity seen among children under 5 in the targeted areas can be partially attributed to the scarcity of food and the consequent lack of variety in diet. This is further compounded by the lack of knowledge of minimum dietary diversity required.
Tearfund is the sole organization supporting nutrition interventions in Uror, Twic East and Pochalla. Tearfund is currently responding to the crisis in Uror, Twic East and Pochalla, supporting 26 Feeding Centres and 5 Stabilization Centres. This project will continue to address high rates of GAM through the provision of Outpatient Therapeutic Programme (OTP) and Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programme (TSFP) services, thus alleviating malnutrition and reducing susceptibility to other health issues, including water-borne diseases. As children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women and girls are the most vulnerable to MAM and SAM, these individuals will be the primary target group for this intervention. A total of 6548 children under 5 will be targeted for treatment of SAM (1,226 Pochalla, 1,384 Twic East, 3,937 Uror). A further 21,888 children will be targeted for treatment of MAM (4,376 Pochalla, 6,922 Twic East, 10,590 Uror). As part of the MAM and IYCF component, a total of 29,684 PLWs will be targeted (17,679 Pochalla, 11,059 Twic East, 16,736 Uror).
Tearfund’s MIYCN component will address the issue of malnutrition and encourage prevention through mothers’ groups both within the community and at the feeding centres. Furthermore, Tearfund’s wider nutrition programme integrates WASH, GBV and FSL components, including training on kitchen gardening, which increases access to more diverse food items. Without this response, GAM rates would inevitably rise and morbidity and mortality would increase.

1 - Needs

According to the South Sudan Acute Food Insecurity and Acute Malnutrition Situation for August 2019 – April 2020, the former Jonglei State has the highest number of people estimated to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 1.25 million people (representing 64.8 percent of the state population), expected to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity between January and April 2020. According to the Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System Report 2019, high prevalence of acute malnutrition is observed during the lean season of 2019. Former Jonglei state is among the leading states, with a GAM rate of 24.2%, which is higher than the Country’s overall GAM rate of 16.2%. The burden of acute malnutrition for children aged 6-59 months in 2020 stands at 1,301,069 (SAM at 292, 373 and MAM at 1,008, 695). Over 234,000 MAM children aged 6-59 months are expected in Jonglei in 2020. The high rates of malnutrition in Jonglei state, specifically in Tearfund operational areas, are attributed to several factors including high levels of food insecurity, poor infant and young child feeding practices as well as high morbidity due to a weak health system in the country. Furthermore, poor access to nutrition services in all the three locations (Pochalla, Twic East and Uror) during the rainy season due to poor road infrastructure constrains service delivery to areas already affected by high malnutrition levels.In Twic East and Uror, Crisis (IPC Phase 3) outcomes have persisted due to broadly seasonal flooding which destroyed crops, limited food and income sources among displaced households and disrupted delivery of assistance and market functioning. Additionally, insecurity, cattle raiding and intercommunal conflict involving revenge killings have led to loss of lives, and disrupted livelihoods and restricted access to wild foods and fish. Based on the May 2019 SMART survey in Twic East, the prevalence of GAM in Twic East is estimated at 19.3 % (15.2 - 24.1 95% C.I.) and SAM was 4.6 % (2.9 - 7.1 95% C.I.) based on Weight-for-Height. This is well above the WHO emergency threshold of 15%. Key factors contributing to malnutrition in the area include seasonal changes to food security, flash floods, violence and disease burden, together with conflict. According to OCHA Situation Report (October 2019), floods caused by torrential rains have worsened the living conditions of South Sudanese people in many parts of the country in the past few months. The areas affected include Twic East, Pochalla and Uror counties in Jonglei (OCHA, 11 Oct 2019). Based on Tearfund’s nutrition experience and ongoing monitoring of the situation, in Uror and Pochalla there is collapse of the local economy and lack of access to markets, insufficient crop production limiting access to food, and restricted access for humanitarian agencies to provide an adequate response due to ongoing insecurity and flooding. The post-harvest decrease in GAM rates is short-lived and attributable to humanitarian assistance, food stocks from harvests, and the reduction in morbidity and disease outbreaks during the dry season. Inadequate water supply and poor sanitation and hygiene sustain the cycle of malnutrition prevalent in the target counties, leading to high rates of diarrhoea and water borne diseases and affecting nutrient uptake for infants and young children. The undernourished are more likely to suffer from higher incidences of and increased mortality rates from diarrhoea.In terms of vulnerable groups, children below the age of 5 years, pregnant and lactating women remain the most vulnerable to acute malnutrition due to their increased biological and physiological needs. Other vulnerable groups include the elderly and people living with HIV/AIDS and tuberculosis.

2 - Activities

In 2020, the project will focus on provision of quality preventative nutrition-specific services to children 6-59 months, pregnant and lactating women and adolescents in Twic East, Pochalla and Uror Counties. Activities will include: 1) Community mobilization, training of CNVs, active case finding, mass screening campaigns, TSFP services, and provision of deworming tablets and vitamin A supplements to <5 yr Children and PLWs 2) MIYCN: PLWs attending the feeding centres and within the community will be targeted for individual and group counselling (mother to mother support groups) on IYCF practices, following the UNICEF recommended approach. The project will also seek to increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition in the 3 Counties. Activities will include: 1) treatment of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) among children 6-59 months. All children with a MUAC of 11.5 cm – 12.5 cm and WHZ score <2SD will be admitted into the TSFP programme to prevent chronic malnutrition, and due to lack of adequate dietary intake during pregnancy, all pregnant and lactating mothers with a MUAC of less than 23cms will likewise be admitted in TSFP. 2) Treatment of moderate acute malnutrition among children 6-59m months, children meeting the criteria ( >-3 but < - 2 Z-score or =<11.5 cm but < 12.5 cm MUAC) will be admitted in TSFP program and treated with Ready to Use Supplementary Food (RUSF) and pregnant and lactating mothers who meet the criteria ( <23cm MUAC) will be admitted in TSFP and treated with super cereal plus (CSB++). 3) Referral of SAM with medical Complications to SCs - OTP/TSFP children with medical complication/failed appetite test will be admitted in Stabilization centre. All discharges will be through OTP where they will continue with treatment and follow-up. 4) Provision of quality MIYCN and micronutrient supplementation services in nutrition sites and community level. Admission and Treatment MAM PLW: Mothers of children less than 6 months or less than 4kgs who are not able to produce enough breast milk will be admitted to the TSFP for PLW. In order to increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, ECD and Protection sectors, the project will entail: 1) Malaria Testing - All children admitted into the OTP programme will be treated for malaria and referred accordingly. 2) Child Friendly Spaces - Tearfund will integrate child friendly spaces into the Feeding Centres, ensuring that Early Childhood Development activities are integrated and involving mothers enrolled in the IYCF programme. 3) Hygiene Promotion - nutrition staff will engage those attending to health facilities in order to deliver targeted health, nutrition and hygiene promotion messages. Tearfund will disseminate hygiene promotion messages, while providing complementary hygiene kits. 4) Tearfund will establish Kitchen garden in all Feeding centers and conduct food cooking demonstration for mothers with under five years old children. 5) Tearfund will facilitate GBV safety audits in the nutrition sites. Issues arising from the safety audits will inform the development of targeted actions to respond to safety issues around our nutrition sites and also trigger community dialogues on gender, protection and disability inclusion in target communities. In order to strengthen the health and nutrition systems, the project will: 1) train health and nutrition workers on management of acute malnutrition and maternal, infant and young child nutrition practices (MIYCN). 2) Monitor availability of core pipeline supplies at nutrition sites: Tearfund has an existing agreement with UNICEF and WFP for supply of RUTF and RUSF in three month lots. 3) Conduct nutrition SMART surveys in prioritized counties based on priority lists shared by cluster.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes.Tearfund continues to operate according to humanitarian principles such as the Red Cross Code of Conduct and the Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS) in all its sites, ensuring that neutrality and impartiality, particularly relevant to this conflict, are visible and communicated. Tearfund also continues to engage with community leaders to encourage their active engagement and support for its work and get feedback from the beneficiaries. This approach has meant that Tearfund continues to have good access to all project sites and is able to continue with nutrition activities with minimal disruption from the local authorities, aside from the general insecurity in the county. Tearfund has built strong relationships with local authorities and collaborates well with other partners at state and national level. Tearfund will endeavor to manage the distribution of food commodities received based on CMAM protocols and donor expectations.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes. This project will target host communities, IDPs and returnees in Twic East, Uror and Pochalla. The risk of acute malnutrition increases among children in distressed conditions, such as those living in active conflict or access-restricted areas, and GBV related safety risks can have a significant impact on communities’ ability to access life-saving nutrition services.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The proposed project aims to support the County Health Department (CHD) deliver high quality service of Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP) across each Payam to prevent unnecessary child mortality during the long hunger gap in drought affected areas and to prevent further deterioration of children and PLW with moderate acute malnutrition. The project will also strengthen the health and nutrition systems, through training health and nutrition workers on management of acute malnutrition and maternal, infant and young child nutrition practices; monitoring the availability of core pipeline supplies at nutrition sites; and conducting nutrition SMART surveys in prioritized counties.Tearfund is the nutrition cluster lead in Uror and an active member of the state and national level nutrition, Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) and WASH clusters, as well as the sole nutrition partner for UNICEF and WFP implementing OTP and TSFP in all three locations. Tearfund is a member of the Strategic Advisory Group (SAG) for Nutrition Cluster at the national level.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

This project is based on the Nutrition Cluster objectives for Humanitarian Response Plan 2020. The project will focus on provision of quality preventative nutrition-specific services to children 6-59 months, pregnant and lactating women and adolescents in Twic East, Pochalla and Uror Counties. The project will also seek to increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition; and to to increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, ECD and Protection sectors. The project will also strengthen the health and nutrition systems. Weekly, monthly, and quarterly reports for the nutrition cluster and other stakeholders shall be prepared and submitted; and the Nutrition Information System (NIS) updated accordingly.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes. Tearfund is committed to ensuring protection is mainstreamed across all nutrition sites. In terms of the physical protection of nutrition facilities, trained security guards are constantly on duty and ensure that only beneficiaries (primarily children under 5 years and Pregnant and Lactating Women) are allowed into the nutrition sites. To reduce protection risks, the services are provided daily and hence mothers will have ample time to come and go during daylight hours. Tearfund will work closely with Protection partners in Twic East, Uror and Pochalla and give referrals of vulnerable households.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

In each of the three Counties, Tearfund has a base office to support the nutrition sites for TSFP/IFP services with storage facilities and full time nutrition staff. Targeted communities will continue contributing their labor and poles towards the construction of waiting sheds, and with re-mudding the walls of the Feeding Centers. The community also actively engage in the transportation of supplies from one location to another, especially when the roads are impassable due to rains. Moreover, the community plays a critical role in securing the supplies and equipment in the warehouses. This enhances ownership and sustainability of Nutrition interventions.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

To propagate the centrality of protection, Tearfund will train staff and stakeholders to integrate sexual gender based violence, child protection and general protection and social inclusion issues in the planning, implementation, monitoring and review of nutrition activities. To ensure women, girls, boys and men are protected from project related protection risks, Tearfund will train nutrition staff in prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse including psychological first aid. Staff will receive training on safeguarding and child protection as part of enhancing our accountability to ensure that our programs and operations do no harm. Prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse messaging will be integrated in community meetings including OTP and feeding centre activities. Tearfund will forge linkages with protection actors in project sites to promote referrals to survivor centered GBV and child safeguarding services. Tearfund will use the safety audit findings to determine the timing, venue and location of nutrition activities to minimize safety risks that women, girls, boys and men face when they participate in nutrition activities. All nutrition activities will be conducted in locations with safe, accessible, private, and gender-sensitive institutional latrines, handwashing facilities, and bathing facilities to promote greater safety of women and men, girls and boys.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Before the inception of the projects, Tearfund facilitates extensive community consultations to identify the needs and priorities of women, men, girls and boys in relation to nutrition. Our program interventions and activities are informed by a gender and protection analysis of the challenges, needs, priorities, strengths and capacities of different groups of people within the target communities including women, men, children and persons with limited mobility. To promote ongoing engagement support and feedback between Tearfund staff and affected populations, Tearfund facilities the formation of community-based project review committees which work with different community-based structures including local protection actors to identify vulnerable women and men including persons with limited mobility such as old people and persons with disabilities to ensure get appropriate targeted support. Community feedback hotlines, suggestion boxes, and complaint and feedback desks will be used during all community meetings to enhance greater accountability of the project to affected populations. To ensure transparency in the complaints and feedback mechanism, women, children and other excluded groups are involved in designing it as well as ensuring it embeds a ‘referral pathway’ that explains who to contact when a child protection or safeguarding case is identified and outlines the required response. The community-based project review committee will be responsible for facilitating project review meetings, and they will work with the community engagement and accountability staff to address discrimination, exclusionary practices and other violations that might take place during project implementation. The feedback and review meetings will be conducted on an ongoing basis to open up spaces for regular engagements to help Tearfund staff to integrate well within the community and create functional communication and conflict transformation mechanisms.
