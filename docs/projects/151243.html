Needs

Since (2017 Gu) significant improvement in GAM has been noted among Northern inland pastoral and Hawd pastoral in the NW region, Addun pastoral and Coastal deeh in NE, Bakool Pastoral, Baidoa and Dhuamareeb IDPs (SC), Bakoole and Bay IDPs in South region of Somalia. A proximately 5 million people in Somalia required humanitarian assistance (including 1.1 million IDPs and almost 6000 refugees, more than 49000 returnees especially after recent Kenya government announcement of closure of Dadab camp in Kenya, and 10000 asylum seekers) and almost 3.3 million people, required emergency health services. A potential gap is eminent in 2017. Crowded conditions and compromised water and sanitation have triggered a high risk of disease, as well as nutrition concerns. Without support, additional outbreaks of epidemic-prone diseases are likely. Furthermore, states in the south and central part of the country have been experiencing major increase ofAWD/cholera cases in 2016. By the end of September 13,643 cases and 497 deaths (CFR3.6%) have been reported. Of these 6,471 (47.4%) are women while 7,918 (58.0%) are children below 5 years; a significant increase from the5,257 cases reported in all of 2015. The humanitarian situation in Somalia remains in a state of chronic catastrophe, characterized by a complex political environment, extreme poverty, food insecurity, conflict and instability resulted in total collapse of livelihood activities. According to FSNAU/FEWS NET Post-Deyr 2015/16 analyses indicate a median GAM (WHZ) prevalence was 17.4% to Serious in the current Gu 2018 with median GAM prevalence of 14.0%. Developing Capacity: All technical health staff will receive quarterly training in Hudur in line with the WHO guidelines, including the EPHS, the IMAM guidelines and the Basic Nutrition Services Package (BNSP), provided by the Hudur/Baidoa Based programme specialists.
