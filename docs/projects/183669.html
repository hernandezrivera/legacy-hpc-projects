Project Summary

The project will contribute to two strategic objectives of the 2022 Humanitarian Response Plan:
SO2: Ensure acutely vulnerable Palestinians under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank have access to essential services.
SO3: The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued
The project will serve approximately 30,380 people, including, 5,000 children under five years children, 500 neonates, 9,500 high-risk pregnant and lactating women, 200 health professionals, and 15,680 adults suffering from acute illnesses and or suspected of the COVID-19 infection.
UNICEF ensured the coordination with relevant authorities in assessing medical supplies and equipment, including for COVID-19 response, needed at targeted health facilities in the H2 area and East Jerusalem.
UNICEF has developed guidance to help ensure that children and adolescents with disabilities are included in all stages of humanitarian action – from preparing for emergencies to recovering from them. All UNICEF interventions are inclusive and include measurements to address the specific needs of people with disabilities. Through additional agency funding sources, the needs of persons with disabilities to access services are being addressed.
Through consultation meetings and accountability toward affected population mechanisms, UNICEF ensures engagement and participation of persons with disabilities to define responses, including services location, design, and time.

1 - Needs

The overburdened healthcare system in the State of Palestine has been facing added challenges resulting from the COVID-19 outbreak. As reported by the Ministry of Health (MOH), many hospitals in the West Bank had suspended elective surgeries and outpatient admissions at hospitals as a result of the over 50 per cent decline in resource reallocation, access restrictions due to COVID-19, and fear of contagion. About 173,000 Palestinians living in Area C, East Jerusalem, Hebron/H2, and the closed area behind the Barrier continue to experience limited or no access to primary healthcare and are in need of mobile clinic health care services. Per the 2021 Health cluster humanitarian need overview, the COVID 19 pandemic had a significant impact on the delivery of other essential healthcare services, which have been intermittently disrupted whenever there have been surges in COVID-19 cases. According to MoH 2020 Annual Report, there are about 130,000 adolescent girls, 90,000 vulnerable women, and 225,000 children, including 30,000 neonates, in need of the provision of sexual and reproductive healthcare, gender-based violence, obstetric and neonatal care, and emergency nutrition services across Palestine. While an estimated 125,967 children under five (35%) in the West Bank and the Gaza Strip are at risk of developmental delays and disabilities and are acutely vulnerable to malnutrition and anemia. Among these, more than 5,000 pregnancies in the West Bank are at high risk, with at least 3,000 neonates in need of transfers to neonatal intensive care units for immediate health care and early detection and intervention services. Furthermore, maternal and child health is deleteriously affected by the inconsistent supply of pharmaceuticals and other medical items affecting the quality of care. The July 2021 MSNA found that, on average, 47% of surveyed households in Gaza and 42% in the WB have at least one member with chronic disease. According to WHO Situation Report on occupied Palestinian Territory (June 2020), 232 items (45%) of essential medications were reported with less than one-month supply availability at the MoH Central Drugs Store (CDS), and 219 items were depleted (42%). Additionally, the impact of the COVID-19 outbreak on the lives of newborns, young children, and high-risk pregnant and lactating women is expected to be severe due to the scale down of MNCH services. The continued closure of East Jerusalem to Palestinians coming from the Gaza Strip or the rest of the West Bank affects the access of the patients in need of tertiary care at East Jerusalem hospitals. Furthermore, Palestinian children's access to health care continues to be severely affected. The Israeli permit system and the elaborated system of movement restrictions in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, have all posed serious challenges to accessing health care services. Hence, UNICEF will continue to work in close coordination with WHO, relevant authorities, and other health and nutrition partners in its immediate interventions to protect children and their families from exposure to COVID-19 and to minimize the risk of mortality. UNICEF will contribute to the efforts to strengthen the healthcare system's capacity to respond to emergencies and build community resilience to cope with the impact of current crises through the provision of medical supplies, including but not limited to ICUs, drugs, diagnostic kits, and PPEs, benefiting some 15,680 people and 200 health professionals which contributes to reach at least 10% of the people in need targeted by the cluster. UNICEF with the Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics jointly conducted the MICS6 in 2019-2020, and from the gathered data, disaggregated information on boys, girls, with disabilities was collected, and this information will be the baseline data for this project interventions to ensure the needs of children with disabilities are addressed.

2 - Activities

UNICEF and partners will focus on increasing access to health and nutrition services among the most vulnerable communities in the West Bank through sustaining and expansion of coverage to reach the most vulnerable populations with a clear multisectoral approach and build on the humanitarian-development nexus approach. Activity 1.1 Provide access to Health, nutrition and development care that prevents maternal and newborn morbidity and mortality by ensuring the availability of quality and safe delivery, essential life-saving newborn care and emergency obstetric services, including prepositioning of medical drugs in particular. Under this activity, UNICEF will work with relevant authorities to identify the needs of targeted health facilities in H2, Area C and East Jerusalem and to procure the necessary medical equipment; drugs and consumables and other medical supplies to support the provision of services for neonate children, children under-five and women This will also include training on emergency and life-saving skills for neonate, child and maternal care. UNICEF will also ensure leveraging resources through other partners to address issues related to maternal care, such as GBV and referral of cases to relevant services. Further activities with relevant authorities and nutrition technical partners will involve awareness of infant and young child feeding practices, including training on responsive feeding and integrating the IYCF complementary feeding bowl and COVID prevention. Activity 1.2 Provide support to MoH to ensure access to testing, case management and awareness campaigns on COVID 19. Under this activity, UNICEF will work with relevant authorities, WHO and health cluster partners to identify the needs of targeted health facilities in H2, Area C and East Jerusalem, and to procure the necessary medical equipment; drugs and consumables, diagnostic kits, PPEs, ICU equipment and IPC supplies to support the provision of services for suspected COVID-19 patients and support the COVID 19 early detection, and case management interventions. The needs of persons with disabilities are integrated into the COVID-19 contingency plan, and they will benefit from the designed activities. The project will consider gender- and sensitive interventions are designed and implemented in direct consultation with beneficiaries and authorities. The activities will also consider the needs of women and children, including those with disabilities for privacy and accessibility while receiving services at maternal and reproductive health facilities. During the project implementation, channels of communication for feedback, complaints and information sharing will be established through satisfaction survey and recording goods of services delivered to the end-user so that assistance is delivered in accordance to the needs and in adherence to the principles of "do no harm", these consultations include specific awareness sessions on handling of sensitive cases such as SEA (Sexual Exploitation Abuse). Through its partnerships with specialized community-based organizations, UNICEF ensures to target and address the needs of persons with disabilities identified in the intervention design phase. The agencies conduct focus group discussions to design interventions, and people with disabilities are among the vulnerable groups that will be consulted.

3 - Indicators

To measure the success of this project, UNICEF and its partners will use the below indicators:• of neonates receiving quality healthcare services in Neonatal Intensive Care Units (NICU) (Target: 500 neonates)• # of < 5 children receiving targeted health, development and nutrition including IYCF interventions (Target: 5,000 <5 children, 2,500 boys and 2,500 girls)• # of women in reproductive age, pregnant and lactating women receiving quality maternal and nutrition services, including counseling on IYCF in emergencies (Target: 9,500 women) • # of healthcare workers trained on neonate life-saving interventions and management of Malnutrition (Target: 200 healthcare workers, of which 70% female)• # of people benefited from COVID medical emergency supplies and equipment (Target: 15,680 people of which 50% female) UNICEF will oversee project monitoring and will ensure timely reporting. Programme oﬃcers will conduct regular ﬁeld visits to the project sites and review progress reports from implementing partners. Quality checklists will be used to ensure the quality of the interventions. Staff will monitor progress against agreed indicators and verify the validity of reports. The programmatic visits will also assess barriers and challenges and lessons learned. Data will be collected, analyzed, and disaggregated by gender and by age group. Gender will also be mainstreamed by working with other clusters to ensure that clinical management of GBV is improved. Early engagement of selected communities will be enhanced by involving communities and their representatives at all stages of project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. Target beneﬁciaries have been consulted during the project design through focus group discussion and will be continually involved in its implementation. During the project implementation, channels of communication for feedback, complaints and information sharing will be established through satisfaction surveys and recording goods of services delivered to the end-user, so that assistance is delivered in accordance to the needs and in adherence to the principles of “do no harm”.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

UNICEF is strengthening Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) in programmes by systematizing commitments in agreements with all implementing partners. UNICEF is implementing a project to strengthen the capacity building of 12 national NGOs on AAP & PSEA. The work includes the development of AAP & PSEA Context Analysis, capacity building, and AAP/ PSEA system strengthening. UNICEF SoP has also initiated a new project to digitalize AAP-PSEA mechanisms in the country office. UNICEF has expanded support to the SAWA hotline to be able to handle SEA claims at the interagency level, which includes referral of claims among agencies. Awareness and communication actions have started to progressively publicize the PSEA system and the right of affected populations to the complaint.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

UNICEF safeguards beneficiaries' rights to safe and dignified interventions. Consent of beneficiaries is usually considered and obtained through partners and implementing agencies joining training and community interventions. UNICEF ensures applying international standards and WHO standards for medical equipment and medical supplies procured. UNICEF developed feedback mechanisms from beneficiaries to ensure accountability toward affected populations and mitigate any risks identified by end-users.
