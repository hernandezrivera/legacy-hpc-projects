Project Summary

Through this project, Mehad aims to provide 30 health facilities in Northern Aleppo with solar Photovoltaic (PV) Systems with the following expected results:
• Installing solar energy systems to power thirty health facilities in Northern Aleppo
o A total of 30 health facilities have been provided with solar energy systems; three (3) hospitals and 27 PHCCs;
o A total of 30 health facilities in Al Bab, Azaz, Jarablus, have full access to solar PV systems; and
o A total of 30 solar PV systems have been installed, tested and are ready to secure power.
• Strengthening the capacity and raising awareness of the LCs, FAHD, and key stakeholders (NGOs) about the importance of using renewable, safe energy and managing the solar PV systems.
o A total of 30 staff from 30 health facilities attended the technical training about the solar PV systems; and
o Ten (10) individuals from LCs, communities, FAHD, and key stakeholders (NGOs) have attended the technical training on solar PV systems.
• An impact study is reported as an outcome indicator to show the effectiveness of the installation of the solar PV systems.
Project Activities
As for the activities proposed for the solar power project in NW Syria, they are as follows:
1. Providing in-depth technical assessment for each of the 30 targeted health facilities included in the project region regarding their
needs for solar power and their needs for any construction works to accommodate these systems if needed
2. Procuring solar power systems according to the capacity needed for each health facility based on the technical assessment
3. Installing the systems at the health facilities including construction activities, if needed, to accommodate the power systems.
4. Training one technician from each health facility on the day-to-day maintenance of the system
5. Raising awareness training sessions for participants from different stakeholders on the importance of solar power for the
efficiency and sustainability of the healthcare service, and the environment.
6. Providing advance maintenance for the systems if needed
7. Proving monitoring and evaluation activities for the project activities
8. Conduct an impact study about the projects; post-phase.
The project aims to support the sustainability of the primary healthcare centers through:
- Reducing the load of fuel and electricity consumption.
- Creating a sustainable clean and safe source of energy.
Mehad has a plan to replicate a similar project in Idleb region, and will approach the donors for advocating the necessity of this project for the resilience of the health system. Moreover, the project can be considered an important step for the transitional period toward the early recovery phase.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Mehad (formerly UOSSM France) conducted a need assessment (Sep 2022) to assess the state of health facilities in three districts in the Northern Aleppo area: Azaz, Al-Bab, and Jarablus in terms of the possibility of providing these facilities with solar energy system to reduce the cost of electrical energy through generators, protect the environment, support the early recovery in the project intervention area, and contribute to the sustainability in the provision of health services.Mehad contracted a consultant engineer (expert in PV systems) to perform the technical need assessment through field visits and surveys to 30 health facilities. Based on the assessment, Mehad developed a project design to support 30 health facilities in NW Syria with PV systems to power the health facilities through the provision of clean, renewable energy that can reduce the financial burden on the targeted facilities. Moreover, the proposed project will contribute to environmental resilience by reducing the CO2 emissions from burning fossil fuels for electricity generation. Finally, the project will contribute to building local communities' capacities regarding using clean and renewable energy sources in health services. Cost RecoveryWith regards to cost efficiency, the initial calculations predict that the facilities running on solar energy, instead of generator and grid electricity, would require three (3) to four (4) years as a cost recovery period. However, the facilities running on a hybrid system are expected to have cost-recovery within three (3) years. For further details, find the technical studies (Annex 2).Calculation of Annual Deficit vs cost recovery/return-on-investment:Some PV systems will meet the needs of the facilities < 99%, so there is a small deficit in production against the full need. For these systems, this deficit has been added to the cost recovery in order to accurately calculate the time required to recover the cost. Meanwhile, if calculated on actual working hours (7 hours instead of 8 for PHCCs) few of these PV systems will achieve cost recovery. So, increasing the capacity of the systems here does not meet the cost-efficiency criterion as most of these health facilities provide one service (maternity) for 24/7 while all other services are provided during the day. Also, this gap can be covered through UBS devices. On the other hand, 19 PV systems meet the needs of the health facilities in proportions ranging from 100% to 165%, thus greater saving in production. For systems that produce more than the needs of the facility, no deficit has been added because of its non-existence, but here it should be noted that these systems produce more energy than the facility needs and therefore can be used later to charge referral cars running on electricity and link the assignment between the facilities. The project prioritizes incorporating gender mainstreaming considerations and implementing PSEA measures thorughout project phases/cycle.
