Project Summary

The project responds to Cluster Objective 1: Increase equitable and safe access to inclusive quality educational services for vulnerable children. The project will support 75,000 poorest students aged 6-17 (50% girls), who may be at risk of dropout due to poverty, to continue in their education by providing basic, urgent supplies including school bags, school uniforms, warm clothes, and shoes. The provision of the winterization items to 3,300 students will be through the e-voucher programme with WFP to avoid stigmatizing children and families and allowing the targeted beneficiaries the option to select their own supplies. However, the provision of school’s bags and stationery to 30,000 students will be based on their vulnerability. The project beneficiaries will be selected based on vulnerabilities criteria with due verification processes. Priorities will be given to families of female-headed households, children with disabilities and children affected by the great march of return. In addition, 100 schools (out of 409) vulnerable schools in Gaza will be supported to continue functioning through the provision of necessary teaching and learning materials. The schools will be selected based on the vulnerability mapping conducted by the Islamic Relief, UK, 2017.

1 - Needs

In Gaza, the capacity of the education system to deliver adequate education to more than half a million children is compromised by deteriorating living conditions: teachers receiving partial salaries; 414 public schools (serving 50 per cent of children in Gaza) lacking operational budgets; limited accessibility, in particular for children with disability (CWDs); and psychological distress that children and teachers face. Around 70 per cent of UNRWA schools and 63 per cent of those run by the Ministry of Education (MoE) already operate on a double- or triple-shift system, resulting in crowded classrooms, reducinginstructional hours in core subjects and foundation learning. The deterioration in the humanitarian situation has pushed more families into poverty, with 54 per cent of the population in Gaza and 13.9 per cent in West Bank considered poor according to the Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS), undermining the affordability of basic education supplies and transportation costs and increasing the risk of school drop-out. The findings of a Household Expenditure and Consumption Survey, released by PCBS in May 2017, show a significant increase in poverty rates in the Gaza Strip: from 38.8 per cent in 2011 to 53 per cent by the end of 2017, which is equivalent to around 1.01 million people, including over 400,000 children. The already dire living conditions for children across the Gaza Strip further deteriorated since 30th of March 2018. The electricity crisis, cuts in civil servants’ salaries including teachers, UNRWA funding crisis and overall economic decline, has led to an increased level of poverty and increased household vulnerability. Parents are increasingly not able to afford basic school supplies and schools for their children, placing them at risk of dropping out. Girls are more vulnerable in this context as parents focus their expenses on family basic needs and prefer to offer education opportunities to boys. According to education needs assessments conducted by Educaid 2018, “based on parents and school personnel the poor educationalenvironment including the poor facilities, unsafe furniture resulted in huge psychosocial stress on students due to the financial burden and losing hope in future, more willing to drop out.” Similarly, due to lack of operational budget, schools are not able to procure basic teaching supplies and the new school year has been ushered in with many schools, in the most vulnerable areas, struggling to afford basic stationery, paper or chalk. The education needs assessments reports 2018 released by Educaid released that education has been negatively affected in terms of quality and quantity of services provided by schools due to the financial crises in the Gaza Strip and the very limited ability of MoE, UNRWA and families to financially support the education services. On 19 September 2019, UNICEF received an official request from relevant stakeholders to support provision of braille papers to two public schools that enroll deaf people. Through this project, UNICEF is seeking to support the vulnerable schools with stationery and teaching materials while children will be supported with school bags, uniforms, warm clothes and shoes. UNICEF wishes to ensure that vulnerable children boys and girls not only access schools but learn in an enabling environment. Under the project, UNICEF will ensure that schools located in marginalized or underserved areas have the resources and teaching materials that enable teachers to teach and the learning/teaching process to continue.

2 - Activities

The following activities will be conducted after the vulnerable children and schools have been selected. The criteria for the selection of schools will include the number of vulnerable children in the poorest localities indicated in the poverty report issued by the PCBS and the location of schools in highly vulnerable localities as per Islamic Relief survey. The names of beneficiaries will be verified through the OCHA list of hardship cases. 1. Provision of school supplies to 100 public school in Gaza. Schools will be selected based on the vulnerability mapping conducted by Islamic Relief in 2017, that included specific criteria such as, located in highly vulnerable localities, schools located in Access Restricted Areas (ARA), and schools serving poor children and communities. 2. Provision of school bags and basic stationery to 30,000 children ages 6-17 from the most vulnerable households. The target beneficiaries will be selected based on vulnerability criteria, such as female-headed households. School children in these families will be considered the top priority. Disaggregated data about male and female students will be verified by relevant stakeholders through the School Management Information System (SMIS). 3. Provision of school uniforms, jackets and shoes/ rain boots to approximately 3,300 children in need using e-voucher programme (with WFP). Families affected by environmental threats/ natural disasters e.g. seasonal foods will be prioritized. Priorities will also be given to female-headed households. Through the provision of the school bags, stationery and winterization items, the priority will be given to the female-headed household, children with disabilities and those affected by the great march of return. The selection of the beneficiaries will be based on vulnerability criteria with due verificationprocesses. In addition, through the distribution of these education supplies UNICEF will make sure that all the beneficiaries can easily access the locations where the supplies will be distributed. Moreover, UNICEF will make sure that the beneficiaries are not subject to be stigmatized. Based on the feedback received from the beneficiaries where they mentioned that the UNICEF branded bags were stigmatizing them, the distribution of the education supplies will minimize stigmatization of the targeted students by not putting logos or any branding. A proper methodology will be conducted to ensure that the beneficiaries can express themselves and deliver a feedback in a confidential manner. UNICEF will work in close collaboration with child protection working group under Protection Cluster to strengthen protection mainstreaming. Children with disabilities and those affected by the great march of return will be targeted.
