Project Summary

Farmers will be selected based on a set of criteria, while income/poverty level will also be taken into account to support the most vulnerable. Prior to implementation, beneficiaries will be surveyed and consulted to tailor training and inputs to best leverage their resources and accommodate their needs. Much as the need for cash and land was expressed during assessments, lessons from past interventions show that most farmers in the target area report high and unaffordable costs of farm inputs. Organic fertilizers are expensive, and it is the main reason most of them use chemical fertilizers. Samaritan's Purse is planning to provide one-off seed and irrigation kits. Prior to each distribution, the program agronomist will conduct an assessment of each farm to determine the appropriate quantity and type of each input. This is part of the project start-up tool kits available for operational and procurement use. Inputs will be distributed at strategic intervals based on the season and facilitated by technical expertise.
Samaritan's Purse (SP) will assess the input needs, e.g. seed and organic fertilizer dealers of the farmers and provide in-kind assorted seeds and organic fertilizers. Existing input dealers within the project area will supply seeds, fertilizers, and other inputs. For sustainability purposes, beneficiary farmers will be encouraged to establish long-term relationships with these actors. Where viable, SP will encourage these actors to participate in the urban livelihood development project. Vegetable traders are key actors in the produce value chain, responsible for trading vegetables from production pockets to wholesale and retail markets. Their trading activities include buying and assembling, repacking, sorting, selling to intermediaries, transporting, and/or selling to wholesale markets. Where viable, SP will encourage these actors to apply to participate in the project development.
In collaboration with the agricultural and food security working groups, SP identified rural farmers in the Hassakah Governorate as a key beneficiary population, given the severity of the effects of the crisis on their livelihoods and food security, as well as the trickle-down effect that limited food production has on the local population. With a history of agricultural cultivation in this area, the farming families in Hassakah are dedicated to their trade and well-connected to their land. The proposed project will build upon the HH's existing skills and resources to increase their resilience against drought and ultimately protect against food insecurity and famine. At least two members of each beneficiary household (HH), where possible, will participate in technical training sessions that cover entrepreneurial, agricultural, and/or basic health and nutrition topics. While the technical training will cover the majority of need-to-know information for each phase in the farming cycle, the project will also offer mentorship and ad-hoc advice to beneficiaries, particularly at such critical times as irrigation infrastructure installation, harvesting, etc. Additionally, Samaritan's Purse will continue targeted monthly food distributions to food-insecure HHs that will not meet the requirements from their participation in the rural livelihoods project.

3 - Needs

The project aims to increase the vegetable production and sales of 86 low-income farming families, ensuring their food security and resilience against drought. Additionally, Samaritan's Purse aims to increase food security among 120 vulnerable HHs while also, over the length of the project, to continue building the capacity of local partner through regular monitoring and feedback, including technical training on distribution methods warehousing, finance, and M&E methodologies.

4 - Implementation of activities

The project will support the most vulnerable HHs through:• Agricultural inputs: of drought-resistant seeds, organic fertilizers, and water delivery inputs. Prior to each distribution, the SP will assess each farm to determine each input's appropriate quantity and type. Inputs will be distributed at strategic intervals based on the season and facilitated by technical expertise. The project will also offer mentorship and ad-hoc advice, particularly at such critical times as irrigation infrastructure installation, harvesting, etc. Training on entrepreneurial, agricultural, health and nutrition topics will be offered. • General Food Distribution: 2 months of food distributions to food insecure HHs that will not meet the requirements for the project.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

•Beneficiaries being harassed to and from distribution sites (for example, beneficiaries are at risk because they may be in need of transport to/from the distribution sites, which makes them vulnerable to those who may commit coercion or aim to exploit the beneficiary sexually)•Access issues at distribution sites (e.g. poor lighting, little room to modify the queuing system if needed) To mitigate these risks and ensure a high level of service delivery as a whole, the quality of activity implementation is monitored by two complaint mechanisms, as well as through SP quality control at distribution sites. Beneficiaries have the opportunity to leave an anonymous complaint at the distribution point via a complaint box or by making a formal complaint at the HAO office. SP then determines how to improve and build further the capacity of the local partner. Samaritan's Purse regularly visits distribution sites to monitor distribution practices. Samaritan's Purse receives feedback from beneficiaries via Syriac Cross on commodity and services. Protection is streamlined through this program by ensuring that all SP staff is involved in the project are trained in managing and referring specific gender and protection concerns that may arise in the program and have a good operational awareness of protection risks and issues.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Beneficiaries exploited/abused by Samaritans Purse / Syriac Cross (SC) staff members, vendors and other actors participating in the implementation of the projects. This risk is mitigated by background checks on all personnel, partners and vendors. Safeguarding training for all staff is provided, protection messaging is displayed at distribution points and reporting systems. There is a feedback and complaints processing department to support the mitigation of this risk. Our implementing partner, Syriac Cross (SC), work on the ground to collect and verify anonymous feedback (and if not anonymous, they will anonymize the information) and provide a weekly summary to Samaritan's Purse. The feedback is discussed and acted on, with SC and SP working together with their respective safeguarding and protection focal points to follow up on PSEA issues. Additionally, Samaritan's Purse has the following measures:• Background checks on all personnel, safeguarding training for all staff, protection messaging displayed in the clinic, clear reporting system for violations. There is an HR and complaints processing department to support this risk mitigation. •A safeguarding focal point is assigned, and the complaints/feedback mechanism means that feedback is captured from remote/off-site locations and acted upon.•Reporting mechanisms for each project•Hotline•Email hotline


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

•Access issues (closure, disruption) might make informal border crossing (Feshkabour- Semalka) inaccessible. Mitigate through protocols for remote working, including remote programming and support to a local implementing partner, Syriac Cross. •Security situation or border restrictions cause the team to lose access to NES for a period of time. This is mitigated by ensuring strong communication lines with the Forum and KRI to obtain approvals in a timely manner and with NES staff and partners to ensure there are no gaps in programming. Implementation will be maintained through a local partner, Syriac Cross, providing an added layer of programmatic stability as programs can continue in the event that IFS are not able to be in the country. o Samaritan's Purse partnership with Syriac Cross also mitigates risks in the areas SP is working, providing added security and sustainability. Working with Syriac Cross also enables SP to empower local organizations by providing dignified employment and protecting local capacity.
