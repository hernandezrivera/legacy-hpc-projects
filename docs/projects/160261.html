Project Summary

This project aims at addressing the Shelter/NFI needs of the affected population in Donetsk and Luhansk Oblasts of Eastern Ukraine. The interventions articulated further below in the document strictly apply to the goals defined in the respective monitoring framework 2019-2020, i.e.:
- Contribute to adequate transitional solutions related to shelter and NFI needs meeting minimal international and national shelter standards;
- Address essential shelter and NFI needs of the most vulnerable IDPs and conflict-affected population through in-kind assistance and contingency.
This will be achieved through the implementation of the following activities:
- rehabilitation of collective/territorial centres for provision of social services (roofing, windows replacement, rehabilitation of the heating systems, etc);
- rehabilitation of private households (Light, medium, structural repairs ("heavy repairs");
- distribution of coal to private HHs and public institutions.
It will enable to cover needs of up to 27 950 affected individuals who will be benefiting from the intervention. For that reason, it is planning to attract approx. USD 1.16 M of funding.

1 - Needs

After more than five years of an armed conflict started in Ukraine, the local population still is suffering from its repercussions. Based on the ongoing verification of data in the Shelter Cluster’s damage database, the Cluster estimates that as of the beginning of 2018, some 5,300 vulnerable households in GCA alone continued to require humanitarian shelter interventions. Additionally, the conflict continues to cause new damage to residential housing: some 520 homes were damaged in the first half of 2018. In the first half of 2019, the conflict caused new damages to more than 400 houses on both sides of the line of contact (LoC). There are entire communities of damaged or destroyed houses along the LoC where security conditions do not allow repairs to take place. Most families with homes in these areas have been displaced. Furthermore, humanitarian shelter partners provide only minimum interventions to create sufficient living conditions in line with national standards. This normally does not extend to full reconstruction of destroyed homes, since the costs are high. Thus, communities along the LoC continue to have many shelter-related needs which go beyond the scope of humanitarian interventions. The population along the LoC in GCA is disproportionately female – 57 %, with female-headed households - 66%, significant vulnerability linked to age – 13%, displacement – 9%, or disability – 5%. Humanitarian partners have noted that some families continue to live in substandard conditions due to the conflict, but are currently not considered for assistance, because they do not match humanitarian organizations’ vulnerability criteria, such as households where working-aged adults face persistent unemployment. Preliminary assessments claim that problem related to the heating systems is still considered as one of the most acute. Given the high frequency of electricity outages both triggered by conflict and instances of intentional power cuts imposed by local service providers (especially in Luhanska Oblast), put people in a situation of total uncertainty and despair leaving them without alternative sources to sustain adequate temperature conditions both in private households and public institutions. Aссording to the recommendation of the Shelter Cluster, winterization assistance to Collective Centres and other social institutions should also be considered as a priority even outside the 0-20 km zone (away from the LoC). Also, it is crucial to mention that settlements located in the vicinity to the LoC or just relatively small with fewer numbers of people usually stay off the radar of humanitarian interventions. Since most of the roads at these locations are unpaved they are significantly dependent on the seasonal peculiarities. As such, in winter the roads can be completely snowed under and respectively wash-out in early spring. It is a high time to set up new goals and priorities in the modality of humanitarian aid distribution for the sake of the most vulnerable layers of the affected population. All humanitarian interventions that will be mentioned further will strive to address the needs of every specific gender and age group and will be tailored in coordination with the affected population.
