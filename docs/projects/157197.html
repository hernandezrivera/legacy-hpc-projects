Project Summary

The project will deliver livelihood support to the SSRs and the most vulnerable host communities’ members surrounding the camps or living together with them outside the camps. The livelihood interventions will include support with agricultural inputs and training to enable the beneficiaries to produce their own food and enhance self-reliance among them. The interventions also include support with livestock supplies and services as well as support with income generating activities and women training on food processing, conservation, and environmental interventions to contribute to protection and conservation of the natural resources.

Needs

The South Sudanese refugee situation is the largest refugee response in Africa, and Sudan hosts the largest number of South Sudanese refugees in the region – over 850,000 as of 31 December 2018. According to UNHCR records, refugees from South Sudan account for approximately 78% of all reported refugees in Sudan, with additional new arrivals each month. Arrival rates peaked at nearly 200,000 people in 2017, but slowed in 2018, with just over 33,000 individuals arriving. While the Revitalized Agreement for the Resolution of the Conflict in South Sudan (R-ARCSS) was signed in September 2018, its full implementation will take time and ongoing violations to the agreement are reported, making sustainable return to their areas of origin in South Sudan unlikely for most refugees in the near future. It is expected that South Sudanese refugees will continue to arrive in smaller numbers in 2019. Inter-agency partners’ capacity to respond to the needs of South Sudanese refugees continues to be stretched, exacerbated by critical funding gaps and Sudan’s ongoing economic crisis, as well as fuel and cash shortages. While progress has been made to meet assistance standards and some areas, significant gaps remain in all sectors; for example high food insecurity situation is faced by all South Sudanese refugees in Sudan, with most of them unable to afford the local food basket or purchase firewood; and critical malnutrition rates in many camps and settlements is prevailing. These issues are compounded by limited access to livelihoods and household income, with knock-on effects including increased tensions with host communities, prevalence of child labour, early marriage and other child protection issues, and SGBV risks. Continued investment and scale-up is required in order to better meet the protection and basic service needs of South Sudanese refugees. As explained in the above paragraph, the impacts of the economic situation on South Sudanese refugees’ communities is compounded by limited access to livelihoods across the response. Against this backdrop, the increase in the cost of living due to high inflation has affected the purchasing power of the South Sudanese refugees and undermined their capacity to earn enough income to become self-reliant, heightening assistance needs and dependency across the response. South Sudanese Refugees in White Nile, South Kordofan, WestKordofan, South Darfur and North Darfur states with be targeted under this project proposal with agriculture and livestock based livelihood interventions as well as with Income Generating Activities such as restocking with small ruminants, distribution of donkey carts and training of on food processing and support with productive kits. The beneficiaries will also be supported with environmental interventions such as production, distribution and planting of multipurpose trees’ seedlings and training of communities’ members especially women on production of Fuel Efficient Stoves.

Activities

The proposed livelihood and environmental activities include Output/Activity1: Provision of support with Agricultural inputs (Seeds, tools, other inputs) and training, using FFS approach; this will be delivered through provision of assistance to produce nutrition rich crops, legume and vegetables, to be delivered timely to SSRs who have access to farming land and 25% of the target will be from the vulnerable host communities members surrounding them in the targeted locations. Output/Activity 2: Provision of support with livestock supplies and services (vaccination, treatment, deworming, concentrate animal feed) to the SSRs who own livestock; 25% of the target will be from the vulnerable host communities members surrounding them in the targeted locations.Output/Activity 3: Provision of support for Income Generating Activities (IGA) ' donkey carts, restocking, vocational training', women traing on food processing and conservation;Output/Activity 4: The beneficiaries will also be supported with environmental interventions such as production, distribution and planting of multipurpose trees’ seedlings and training of communities’ members especially women on production of Fuel Efficient Stoves (FES).
