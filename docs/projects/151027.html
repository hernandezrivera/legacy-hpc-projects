Project Summary

This Response will target among the vulnerable 2.6 million people that internally displaced and host communities that requires assistance in the greater Somalia/land. DRC will target over 3000HHs(18000 beneficiaries) in both livelihoods with conditional and unconditional cash transfers. The injection of cash is meant to increase the purchasing power for target households with limited access to food commodities that are available in the local markets. The provision of cash will be a dignified means of supporting beneficiaries as they allow them to make their own choices in terms of the types and quantities of food to purchase. Under cash for work activities component, community members will be engaged to rehabilitate productive community assets to restore them to usable condition. The community members will identify and select assets that are not in proper working condition and those that have been damaged by human activities or natural calamities for rehabilitation/restoration. Possible cash for work activities will be bush clearing, erosion control, digging latrine pits, road work to improve access to markets, basic services and other work to preserve community assets and recover from effects of drought. Women and men will be equally involved in determining the final gender appropriate cash for work activities. Care will be taken to select activities that allow a high percentage of women to participate. Work norms will be established to ensure a reasonable quantity of work but allow women time to carry out their family responsibilities. The Danish Demining Group will complement DRC’s efforts and provide mine risk education to the population in target areas.Acoountability to affected population (AAP) will be applied Additionally, unconditional cash transfers covering at least 100% of MEB will be provided to HHs that do not meet the cash for work criteria and do not have an available adult laborer within the family to be involved. Examples include female-headed households where all members of the family are children except for the caregiver

Needs

Two in five Somalis remain acutely food insecure, more than 2.6 million people are internally displaced and the seasonal assessment results indicates severe levels of acute food insecurity and malnutrition among IDPs. The residual impact from the 2016/17 drought, combined with the recent floods, cyclone, displacement, evictions etc., continue to undermine opportunities for food security improvements especially for the most vulnerable Somalis. The forecasted El Nino phenomena and associated abnormal rainfall in last quarter of 2018 is expected contribute to an increase in production while at the same time risks of shocks, including loss of production are real. Indeed, the expected above normal rains may cause the deterioration of the situation of IDPs who are already in a precarious situation (FSNAU/FEWNET Sep 2018). According to DRC Integrated Emergency Response assessment (IER) 2018 most parts Somalia have not only received rains but it has come with floods, destruction, cyclones which affected the households coping strategies. The area most affected by floods are the riverine Regions (Shebelle and Juba rivers) while Puntland and Somali land have been affected cyclone which destroyed both Human and Animals Immediate livelihood support must also be augmented to strengthen the capacity of affected people to improve their food security in the immediate and near terms. In response, the Food Security Cluster objectives focus on (1) increasing immediate access to food; (2) protecting and restoring livelihoods, related food and income sources; and (3) building resilience against current and future shocks through early and timely action. DRC will apply the FSC strategy to Prioritize areas of food insecurity based on, seasonal food security and nutrition assessments outcomes and available early warning information and analyses. Activities

Provision of farming inputs (spade, wheelbarrow, panga, shovels,seeds ,fertilizers) to support irrigation and increase agricultural production for small scale subsistence farmers from asset poor HHs and minority people who lack the necessary collateral to access credit.. This activity will target 40% female headed households to reduce negative survival mechanisms including livelihoods that increase their risk of violence and exploitation.Provision of Cash for Work activities to generate sufficient income for households in Emergency and Crisis to access at least 75% of the cost of the MEB while maintaining and rehabilitating communal assets. DRC will target 60% female participants. Gender roles and cultural context will be a key consideration, so that women can undertake activities that are consistent with Somali cultural tradition.Provision of unconditional cash transfers to vulnerable households and those at particular risk of exclusion from accessing livelihoods to increase their access to food, and strengthen purchasing power for essential household items and services. These activities will target at least 50% female beneficiaries. Provision of unconditional food vouchers to vulnerable households in urban areas in emergency and crisis in order to increase access to essential food and nonfood items through supply of selected quantities of dry food items from selected vendors on a monthly basis. The project will target the most vulnerable persons including boys, girls, men and women. At least 50% of the targeted households will be female headed and will be given at least 100% of MEB. Specific food that is culturally and religiously appropriate to the beneficiaries may be provided on a monthly basis

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

project will contribute to durable/ resilience of vulnerable populations against shocks as part of FSC objectives. This will include protecting and conserving natural resources on which lives, and livelihoods depend, such as water, soil, land and forest resources. The activities will be directed at repairing and effectively managing small-scale productive infrastructure to mitigate shocks (e.g. river de-silting and embankment repair to prevent seasonal floods) using a combination of machinery and human works as appropriate. As well, life skills training activities will be prioritized to increase household income for the vulnerable and food insecure households. Community-based preparedness, surveillance and early warning systems/networks will contribute to prevent damage or losses to crops, livestock and coastal fisheries

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

DRC will ensure the vulnerable, minorities needy women, girls, men and boys are given priorities. Consultations will be held in focus group discussions with women, girls, boys and men to explain the project to them and hear their views about it in relation to issues such as selection criteria and activities appropriate for all groups. Such consultations are expected to give traditionally marginalized and displaced groups, women and girls a sense of ownership and empowerment. The Do No Harm policy will applied to target affected population. All interventions will be implemented so as not to exacerbate existing gender imbalances.DRC has opened up transparency website for stakeholders and beneficiaries to access and report freely(www.drc.ngo/about -drc/open-drc)

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Protection mainstreaming and do no harm principles form the backbone of DRC programs globally and are incorporated in emergency, durable solutions and resilience programming. DRC works on the basis of the rights based approach with beneficiaries and its own staff. DRC protection mainstreaming ensures that the protective impact of programming is maximized. Through the integration of protection principles into all activities, the most vulnerable are targeted and their safety enhanced and dignity promoted as part of AAP. All efforts are taken to ensure that discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect and exploitation are not perpetuated

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

DRC is working in the area for long time and has skilled local staff with good knowledge of the area of implementation, DRC have good relation and reputation with the community which increases acceptance.. Dialogue with local authorities and information sharing on project activities will be privileged. DRC safety coordinator ensure that security and context assessments before the start of the project and advises the management accordingly. DRC will also engage with conflict affected communities and parties (national and international) in order to minimize disproportionate and indiscriminate targeting of civilians and civilian assets vital for survival, including through enhanced delivery of assistance and support to community based self-protection mechanisms.
