Project Summary

Over 123000 people in Lower Shabelle Region are in stress and if not supported might end up in crisis and emergency. Most of these people are those affected by 2017 drought and those displaced by the armed conflicts that has prevailed since 20213.
In a bid to avoid people in stress going to crisis, New Ways plans to support stressed community by restoring their livelihood through rehabilitation of assets. To realize this New Ways plans to rehabilitate 3 secondary canals ( 2 in Marka riverine and 1 in Kuntuwarey riverine) and 2 Water catchments (1 in Qoryoley and Kuntuwarey agropastoral liveligoods). Farmers in flood prone areas will also be supported with 20000 sand bags for river embankment. This will prevent crop destruction due to preventable river flooding.The project will also train and provide with fishing gear to 120 households in Barawe District. Selection of beneficiaries will be based on vulnerability index criteria. Community project committees will be mobilized and formed at each target locations. Project officers will ensure that committees consist of women, elders, young girls and young boys. Special focus will be given to select people with disability and women-headed households during the selection process. Active community participation will be encouraged for ownership and we feeling aspects. Beneficiaries will be involved all the project stages and complain mechanism will be introduced.

Needs

Due to prolonged conflicts and recurring droughts major parts of the Somali population has been depending on humanitarian support in one way or the other. The droughts that prevailed between 2015 and 2017 were one of the latest emergency situations vulnerable communities experienced. According to FSNAU 2018 post gu seasonal assessment conducted between June and July, there are pressing humanitarian needs in Somalia despite improvement. According to the same report, the improvement is associated with average or above average rainfalls received in parts of the country in the last two rainy seasons (deyr 2017 and gu 2018). This did not, however, bring the situation to normal as those who lost their livestock, their source of livelihood, to the 2017 drought and the protracted IDPs living in some of the big towns are still in crisis (FSNAU post gu assessment report). Lower Shabelle Region, New Ways area of operation is never an exception. 123000 people are in stress according to FSNAU 2018 post gu assessment report. Past droughts and prolonged armed conflicts have contributed to the crisis facing communities in the Lower Shabelle Region. Marka has been hotspot for inter-clan conflict since 2012 while armed conflict has been recurrent in Qoryoley, parts of Barawe and Kuntuwarey districts in past years. Clashes between Alshabaab on one side and AMISOM and government troops on the other have contributed to displacement of populations leading to lose of livelihood. The 2017 droughts also led to lose of livelihood.Community groups in IPC2 phase need some support in order to prevent them advancing to crisis. Such groups are found in all livelihood zones of Lower Shabelle Region. The most affected are in IDP camps, agropastoral zones of Marka, Qoryoley, Kuntuwarey and Barawe. Those in riverine livelihood of Marka, Kuntuwarey and Qoryoley are also among the affected communities. To avoid situation worsening, there is need to restore livelihood for affected communities.

Activities

The project aims at realizing project objective through execution of the following activities: 1. Rehabilitation of three secondary canals (10 km long) through cash for work/asset targeting 1000 hhs (6000 beneficiaries). 2000 households will also be supported with agricultural inputs for farming. Mattocks, hoes, spades and matches will be provided with farmers.2. Rehabilitation of 2 water catchments through cash for work/asset targeting 1000 hhs (6000 beneficiaries).3. Provision of fishing gear to 120 fishing hhs (720 beneficiaries) targeting Barawe IDPs and poor host communities4. Provision of training to 120 fishermen5. Provision of 20000 sand bags to 2 communities living flood prone areas for river embankment Based on the food security situation of target districts, New Ways plans to target people in IPC2 as per FSNAU post gu assessment report. The project targets 12720 direct beneficiaries (2120 HHs) living in the three districts of Marka, Barawe, Kuntuwarey and Qoryoley. The project targets agropastoral, riverine and peri-urban livelihoods. The 3 secondary canals will be rehabilitated in Marka and Kuntuwarey riverine livelihood zones. The 2 Water catchments will be rehabilitated in Qoryoley and Kuntuwarey agro-pastoral livelihood zones. For fishery, the project targets Barawe IDPs and poor host communities. Fisher men will be provided with fishing kits where each HH will receive thread, different sizes of fishing hooks, small buoyant, big buoyant, anchors, compasses and safety jacket.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The project is linked to durable resilience as it empowers communities and prepares avenues to help them produce via asset rehabilitation. Rehabilitation of secondary canals will help them access water for irrigation hence farm production in rural agricultural areas improve. Lack of functioning irrigation facilities are some of the major challenges facing subsistence farmers in rural riverine livelihood. Once the 3 canals are rehabilitated, farmers can produce their food for consumption and sell the surplus in order to meet other household needs.Provision of 20000 sand bags to flood prone areas will help farmers embank breakage areas thus preventing crop destruction by preventable flooding. Rehabilitation of water catchment in agro-pastoral livelihood will help livestock owners get water for their animals. Once water holding capacities of the catchments are improved livestock owners will cease travelling long distances with their flock/herds in search of water. This will sustain body weight of animals hence better production of milk and meat.Provision of fishing skills and fishing gear will help the 120 households who benefit from the project to earn living. It will also help improve availability of fish in the local market. Fish is nutritious meat and there is always ready market in coastal towns of Somalia. The environment also enjoys sufficient protection as projects will be executed in areas which fall government control. New Ways will closely work with the local authorities representing the government and all other stakeholders including women, clan elders, male and female youth with all clans represented. Involving all stakeholders in all project stages will enhance project's durability and success.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The target population may not be open to welcome minority groups in the project. The project will ensure the inclusion of minority groups in project implementation committee, staff and training participants. Providing education to communities affected by 2017 drought and armed conflicts will help reduce exclusion. New Ways will involve all communities and social groups in all stages of the project in order to avoid exclusion. New Ways will work with the local authority, local community elders, youth (male and female), boys and girls and women in all stages of the project in order to promote communities coexistence. The project will also conduct awareness and advocacy on protection of vulnerable groups. In order to prevent exclusion, special criteria will be drawn for selection of beneficiaries, committees working with the project and staff recruitment. The rights of minority groups and IDPs will be safeguarded in the set criteria.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Men elders might try to sideline women and youth in project involvement. The project will ensure gender equity in all stages of project implementation including project staff. Risks of exclusion based on gender has been major issue in many parts of Somalia. New Ways will, however, work with all gender groups in all stages of project implementation. The project will set a suitable criteria that will ensure equal access to services by men, women, boys and girls. To make this a reality, half of the beneficiaries participating in the cash for work activities will be women. Half of those receiving fishing kits will also be women.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Recurring inter-clan conflict and other armed groups might be a threat to the local population. The project will ensure inclusiveness of all social groups, clans and local authority in its implementation. New Ways will involve all clans in the project implementation committee to avoid conflicts arising from competition for limited resources/opportunities and exclusion of participation. Well designed selection criteria will be used in beneficiary identification and selection. New Ways will always advocate for peaceful coexistence among communities living in its projects coverage areas.
