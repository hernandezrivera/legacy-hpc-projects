Project Summary

IOM plans to provide tailored shelter/non-food item (NFI) and cash assistance through rental subsidies to the most vulnerable Internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees identified through vulnerability assessments in the North-West, South-West (NWSW) regions throughout 2021, to increase the overall capacity of the cluster/sector to address urgent needs and alleviate suffering. Vulnerability characteristics will be based on various household criteria, including household size and current state and condition of existing shelter. Interventions will be in line with the Shelter/NFI cluster guidelines, be done through different intervention modalities applicable to Cameroon (directing in-kind distributions, cash interventions, vouchers, among others), as well as be aimed at the targeted populations depending on vulnerability criteria.
IOM will use the displacement data collected through its Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) and notably the Emergency Tracking Tool (ETT) in NWSW regions to target and provide rapid emergency shelter/NFI support to the most vulnerable, newly displaced populations and returnees. The sectoral assistance will focus on addressing the needs of individuals who are forced into repeated short-term displacements and are seeking refuge in makeshift shelters or agricultural structures in the surrounding bushes of their villages. Repeated displacements, even if short-term, are forcing the affected populations to sell their assets to survive and resort to negative coping mechanisms, which in turn increases their vulnerability.
Meanwhile, and in response to the regular ETTs updates reported by the DTM on the most recent displacement situations, IOM seeks to preposition NFI and Emergency Shelter kits in its warehouses in Kumba and Bemanda in NWSW ensure rapid and effective response in both regions to crises and shocks. The NFI and shelter kits will be distributed to most vulnerable beneficiaries with the support of trusted and experienced local partners to increase operational access to hard-to-reach affected areas, based on experience developed through UN CERF funded shelter/NFI emergency interventions since early 2020 in the NWSW. The NFI kits composition will be based on the sector standards for the region. In addition, IOM will provide a 3-month rental subsidy for affected populations living in urban areas. The specific amount disbursed to beneficiaries will be defined in coordination with the shelter/NFI sector partners.

1 - Besoins / Needs

In the NWSW, a socio-political crisis erupted in 2017. What started as corporatist peaceful protests against a perceived encroachment of the regions cultural practices and autonomy turned into an armed conflict between the government defense forces and Non-State Armed Groups (NSAGs), triggering very dynamic and massive internal displacements and related humanitarian needs. Since December 2019, a significant spike in military operations is forcing more individuals to flee their homes as property destructions as well as human rights violations are being reported on a regular basis. According to the HNO June update for 2020, approximately 3.0 million people are in need of assistance, which corresponds to a 135 per cent increase compared to 2019. Since the beginning of the year, IOM’s ETT has identified more than 90,000 new forced displacements and around 30,000 return movements, mainly linked to violence and school closures related to COVID-19 governmental measures. IOM’s latest ETT dashboards have highlighted the increasing trends of recurring short-term displacements characterized by individuals seeking refuge in makeshift shelters or agricultural structures in the surrounding bushes of their villages. Priority needs, as indicated by displaced populations through the ETT assessments include protection, access to food as well as shelter and non-food items. Access in NWSW for humanitarian actors continues to be an issue, with frequent sporadic road blocks and Monday ‘Ghost-towns’ having a considerable impact on the delivery of reliable and effective humanitarian assistance. Increasing violence and hostilities as well as the lack of progress towards a political solution in the NWSW regions are predicted to cause further displacements and increase humanitarian needs. In addition, access to the affected populations will remain challenging due to increasing insecurity, COVID-19 restrictions and health measures, sporadic attacks on aid workers and poor road infrastructure.A further challenge is the limited financial and human capacity of actors in NWSW to quickly respond in hard-to-reach locations. In light of this, IOM aims to continue strengthening the shelter/NFI response capacity of the humanitarian community for most vulnerable beneficiaries in NWSW identified through the ETT.The intended results through Shelter/NFI interventions in NWSW will be to ensure that throughout 2021 conflict-affected populations in these regions benefit from an immediate improvement of living standards, well-being, and dignity, with greater access to basic, safe and dignified emergency sheltering and Non-Food Items.
