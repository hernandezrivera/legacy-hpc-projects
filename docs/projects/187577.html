Project Summary

This project aims to increase the food security and enhance the livelihoods of IDPs and host communities (50% IDPs, 50 % host communities) in Idlib and Aleppo Governorates through supporting the agricultural and livestock value chains during 2022,2023 as follows:
The first stage: (Support Value Chains for strategic crops such as wheat and olive crops).
This intervention will seek to support these households to begin to restore this production, as the main source of livelihoods and to enhance their resilience by enabling them to have more food availability through personal production of the crop products in addition to improving the crops value chain by addressing the gaps during the chain (Production, processing, marketing).
The number of beneficiaries from agriculture interventions will be 25000 HHs.
Key interventions will include:
• Provision vouchers to buy agricultural inputs, accompanied by related extension services.
• Capacity building targeting farmers and local community members.
• Support olive oil marketing and wheat marketing by purchasing olive oil and wheat from beneficiaries to help farmers to market their products.
• Support the IDPs by creating job opportunities in wheat and olive harvest season (activities related to purchasing wheat from farmers and transporting it to mills, olive harvesting etc.).
• Support the production of bread through milling of wheat and providing free flour for bakeries to produce bread.
• Distribution of free bread to vulnerable IDPs in camps.
The M&E department will conduct an assessment of gaps and bottlenecks within the value chains of strategic crops and ATAA will design an intervention that addresses these gaps along the chain stages.
The second stage:(Support the Value Chain for livestock).
This intervention will include:
1- Supporting 7000 livestock breeders with veterinary services (vaccines, anti-parasitic, artificial insemination, and treatments).
2- Supporting 1500 livestock breeders by distributing 1000 MT fodder.
3- Support 1500 HHs with poultry (9 Layer hens and 1 rooster, feed, and chicken coop).
4- Establishing a veterinary laboratory to support animal breeders and veterinarians by providing appropriate diagnosis and effective treatment of antimicrobials quickly and effectively.
The number of beneficiaries from livestock interventions will be 10000 HHs.
ATAA also plans to increase the awareness of the host community and the IDPs related to the Coronavirus risk and the need to take preventive measures in addition.
 ATAA will identify, verify, and profile target beneficiaries through local councils according to the eligibility selection criteria:
• The shortlist will be identified depending on the vulnerability criteria
• Women headed households (widows, divorced, GBV survivors).
• Large families (more than 5).
• Families with disabled members.
• Families with low income (less than 50 USD)
• Families with elderly (men-women).

4 - Needs

Across Syria, at least 12.4 million people are estimated to be food insecure, of which 1.3 million people are considered severely food insecure. (HNO 2021). ATAA MEAL and technical team conducted need assessment during last year in order to get more clarity and information on current situation in NWS. 50 interviews were conducted with farmers, livelihoods KIIs and households from the local community and authority. The assessment covered 39 communities in 14 sub-districts in 7 different districts in Aleppo and Idleb governorate the following were the Key Findings: 1-There are many areas that mainly depend on agriculture production (strategic crops) and contain irrigation sources. High prices of agricultural inputs (fertilizers, fuel, and pesticides) led to the reluctance of farmers to cultivate their lands. 2-There are many gaps within the value chains of strategic crops in terms of agricultural inputs and stages of processing in addition to marketing. 3-Lack of modern techniques and laboratory service for the diagnosing infectious animal diseases and low numbers of well-trained field veterinarians increase the period and price of treatment, decreases the productivity of animal and the daily income of breeders. 4- Epidemic and infectious diseases (PPR, FMD, smallpox) lead to increase the mortality of animals. It is worth noting that the need is still going for the worse, according to ATAA's staff and their direct observation.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

ATAA PSEA policy was updated in December 2021 and was shared with donors, and set a dedicated PSEA committee including (PSEA focal point, HR focal point, MEAL focal point, Grants and partnership focal point).ATAA adopted a core concept in all interventions to make PSEA and CSG mandatory in all projects and activities, in this regard, all ATAA staff, volunteers, and contractors will got orientation sessions about PSEA and CSG policies and sign both PSEA and CSG code of conducts.All project locations will include banners and complaint boxes with delivering messages from accountability teams regarding the complaints and feedback channels in addition to ATAA Feedback and Complaints system regarding reporting and response of any PSEA case starting from receiving the report, to the investigation process, and providing aid and support to the survivor till the closure of the case after achieving the objectives of the investigation.ATAA is a PSEA network member participates in all regular network meetings, in addition to submit monthly reports to the network, in addition to conduct training for all staff about PSEA and targeting humanitarian workers in the intervention location to support the role of the PSEA network.Gender balance will be a core point in the project within all teams (programs, media, MEAL) and locations will be accessed by women and girls safely.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Markets are functioning and all the inputs are available. The crops varieties are stapled and adapted to the environment. There is social acceptance for the project and the people are prepared to interact with it. Risk has been classified into environmental and security risks. Environmental risks: 1-Plant diseases will be addressed through the distribution of pesticides in a timely manner in addition to strict observation by the competent agronomists through continuous tours of farmers' fields to investigate any diseases and advise farmers ac2- Fire during the harvest season: there is a high risk during the harvest due to factors such as high temperature or neglect, in addition to the Deliberate shelling during harvest. Raising the awareness of the local community about the dangers of fire and methods to mitigate it by: -Removing the weeds on the roadside -Work the lines of fire (firebreaks)between farmers' lands to prevent the spread of fire from one field to another.Security risk: This will be processed with strict follow-up by the S&S Department and develop contingency and evacuation plans.For livestock intervention the risks will be addressed as follows: In the case of difficulty in securing the quantity of required sheep from one location: We will purchase from more than one supplier according to the approved procurement policies and alternative supply plans.
