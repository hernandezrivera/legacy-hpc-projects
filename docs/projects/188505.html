Project Summary

As a lead agency for the Shelter Sector, UNHCR is coordinating the shelter responses from inside Syria as well as from Gaziantep (Turkey). Through this project UNHCR will support the communities of IDPs and returnees in targeted areas through emergency response and long-term shelter solutions from inside Syria as well as from Gaziantep (Turkey).
The project is in line with both the priorities of the HRP and 2022 Sector Strategy and the project will be completed in line with Sector guidance. A summary of the project indicator targets, including both Syria and Gaziantep hub targets, is outlined below:
The shelter programme in 2022 aims to reach in total 1,194,650 individuals/ 238,930 families by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure addressing shelter needs of 7,500 vulnerable households/37,500 individuals through damaged house repairs. Returnee communities will be supported with debris removal, common area repair, installation of solar streetlights to support 633,200 individuals/126,640 returnee families and host community.
Through emergency response, 75,000 individuals/15,000 households will receive with emergency shelters packages, and 347,750 individuals/89,550 households will benefit from 20,000 tents distribution, camp construction and maintenance and 35 collective shelters rehabilitation.
UNHCR’s cross-border shelter programme aims to provide emergency shelter (tents) to address emergency needs of 20,000 vulnerable households/100,000 individuals through tents distribution and installation, and roads repair and construction inside IDPs camps.
In 2023 UNHCR’s will continue the similar activities with expected slight decrease to reach in total 1,075,000 IDPs, returnees and hosting community.
The role and responsibility of the local and national authorities will continue in close partnership with MoLAE and partners (NGOs/INGOs) in the implementation of shelter and infrastructure works. Technical training will be provided to 1,200 targeted engineers..

4 - Needs

According to the 2022 HNO, 5.91 million people need shelter assistance. This increase is driven by loss of capital, the destruction of housing infrastructure and the deterioration of shelter conditions across the country. At least 28% of the population lives in damaged buildings and an estimated 54 % of IDPs live in inadequate shelters, damaged buildings, public buildings, and other non-residential buildings. The range of shelter needs are diverse across different groups, and an increasing number of IDPs and refugees are returning to their original houses/apartments. Experience to date in engaging with return communities has demonstrated that a multisector, area-based approach is the most effective way to ensure the viability of returns. Shelter and support to the rehabilitation of basic services and community infrastructure has been particularly relevant to anchor return. As such, UNHCR will continue its current response to address the needs of voluntarily returned IDPs and refugees, and support their initial reintegration, through shelter rehabilitation programmes and small-scale community infrastructure repair, in partnership with other humanitarian and recovery actors as well as with the local authorities. Vulnerable groups, including children, youth, women, disabled and older people, will be given consideration in the shelter response. Compared with previous years, UNHCR will have a stronger strategic focus on building the resilience of persons of concern.

5 - Implementation of activities

Shelter activities include rehabilitation of collective shelters, distribution of materials for minor shelter repairs, in coordination with local authorities, landlords, and other sector partners. In camps, UNHCR provides operational maintenance in ten camps in NES. UNHCR will provide 40,000 tents in camps, rehabilitate 175 rooms inside collective shelters. Regarding returnees, UNHCR plans to repair 7,500 damaged houses and rehabilitate common areas of 80 buildings, provide 9,000 solar streetlights, as well as provide safe access to shelter through repair and remove the debris for about 30 kilometers. Also, 15,000 houses will receive shelter materials such as doors and windows, WASH, electrical, and solar packages for minor repairs. UNHCR has access to the locations and will be able to deliver the planned activities resources permit. UNHCR will achieve the planned results through different implementation modalities. The most appropriate approach will depend on different factors, including access, context, community capacity, vulnerability, and selection criteria. UNHCR and its partners will work closely with all stakeholders, including people in need, governmental entities during the project’s lifecycle starting from assessment, area selection, beneficiaries’ clearance, official approval, implementation, to monitoring. The monitoring will be conducted both through UNHCR teams in the field, partners, as well as third party monitors.
