Project Summary

The project is planned to respond emergency education services of IDP and host community children in 10 former States of South Sudan. The main goal of the project is to increase access to safe and protective learning environments for conflict affected children, adolescents/ youth.
The project has two components; front-line and pipeline. Under Front-Line UNICEF will support 729,000 (Boys-419,175; Girls – 309,825) children to access education services in the 10 former states of South Sudan. This includes setting up temporary learning spaces (TLS), rehabilitation of WASH facilities. This component will further focus on training 5,500 teachers and 8,500 PTA/SMCs to improve school management and quality of education is South Sudan.
UNICEF will strengthen the provision of child protection services to children in and out of school through school and community based initiatives. The involvement of adolescents and youth in community based activities to prevent and respond appropriately to GBV and create awareness within the community. GBV issues will be addressed in schools through the sensitization of teachers and by linking them to an established referral system. In addition, in collaboration with the MoGEI, a teachers’ Code of Conduct will be implemented to mitigate the risks of GBV and, more specifically, prevent sexual exploitation and abuse.

Needs

The conflict in South Sudan has had an inevitable impact on an already fragile education system. From the ongoing conflict large number of schools were destroyed and resulted almost 41% schools non-functional and deserted (Education Cluster 2017). The condition has worsened children’s access to education even greater, particularly for girls. Most of the schools are operated in the open sky or under the trees. The situation has left an estimated 2.2 million children of primary school age are out of school due to the ongoing violence across the country (UNESCO 2017). The quality of teaching in the country remains a challenge due to several factors; such as crowded classrooms, insufficient teaching and learning materials and an overwhelming proportion of unqualified teachers. Approximately 63 per cent of teachers in South Sudan are untrained and, in the schools supported by the EEP, the proportion of untrained teachers are even higher (UNICEF press release April 24, 2017 - 25 Million Children out of Schools in Conflict Zones – UNICEF). Primary completion rate remains alarmingly low, with 10 per cent of boys and girls completing primary education (EMIS 2016). Arguably, the low levels of education attainment among children has also contributed to a high level of child marriages, child pregnancies, gender-based and family violence, and low health indicators in general, but especially for women and girls in South Sudan. The conflict has also reinforced existing inequities and power asymmetries between males and females, particularly in favour of males. As of 2018, a total of 2,851,201 (2,808,299 girls and boys between 3 and 18 years of age and 42,902 teaching personnel) are in need of education services (HRP 2019). UNICEF launched in 2017-18 the Integrated Essential Emergency Education Services (IEEES) with funding from USAID, which was based on a needs assessment carried out in the conflict affected states. As a result, more than 600,000 children have been reached through the emergency education response.This project will build on the results achieved in 2018 under IEEES project. It is designed to increase enrollment of OOSC, with the focus sustained learning opportunities, level of protection, resilience and recovery of an additional 729,000 Children (Boys-419,175; Girls – 309,825) living amidst violent conflict. UNICEF will provide capacity development of 5,500 (25% women) teachers and 8,500 (30% women) for improved teaching and learning. The project has two components; frontline and pipeline. Activities

Output 1: Equitable access to safe learning opportunities for out-of-school, IDP and host community children and adolescents/ youth, aged 3-18. Proposed Activities:1. Increase access to inclusive and protective lifesaving education (formal and non-formal) 1.1. Establish/rehabilitate temporary learning spaces and classrooms in the conflict affected area.1.2. Establish and rehabilitate gender segregated latrines and water points with hygiene promotion 1.3. Provide incentives at existing Education Cluster rates to 5,500 recruited volunteer teachers. 1.4. Provide cross-cutting emergency lifesaving and life sustaining information to children and youth2. Capacity development of teachers to ensure quality education 2.1. Train 5,500 teachers, educators and ECD facilitators on EiE, GBV, psycho-social support and child centered teaching.2.3. Conduct training of school supervisors/inspectors on supportive school supervision and monitoring initiatives, as well as mentoring to volunteer teachers 3. Strengthen the response capacity of communities and education actors to mitigate the impact of emergencies on children and youth3.1. Mobilize community through the Back to Learning (BtL) initiative to support the enrolment and retention of children, especially girls. 3.2. Train PTAs/SMCs members on school supervision and implementation of School Development Plans. 4. Support education Cluster Activities 4.1. Conduct education Cluster needs assessment (lighter exercise) 4.2. Conduct 3-days cluster coordination training for sub-cluster coordinator/s and state MoGEI counterparts.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

UNICEF and its implementing partners have established community structure and trained Parents Teachers Association with School management committee to provide and get regular feedback to the community and parents regarding the projects/education services. There are regular meetings held with parents and community influential to enroll children to schools and provide support for functioning of schools. Social mobilization is part of the education intervention.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages are context-specific and tailored for gender and age groups. This includes consulting community leaders, religious leaders, schools, women's groups, youth groups and children. All the messages have been presented and discussed with relevant clusters and working groups and task force. All messages have been field tested to ensure cultural and context specifics and involvement of affected population as well.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

It is part of UNICEF's set standard protocol of zero tolerance for sexual exploitation. All Implementing partners have to submit formal deceleration on reducing and eliminating sexual exploitation

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

UNICEF has conducted a detailed situation analysis through community based consultations including INGOs and NNGOs. Recently education cluster has carried out education need assessment in South Sudan where community and relevant stakeholders have been consulted. UNICEF Has standard arrangements for Annual reviews, mid-year reviews have been conducted in 2018 as preparation for next country program which is three years 2019-2021. UNICEF's annual work plan is approved by the Government, there are also state level action plans and child protection working groups where issues raised by communities can be resolved. Community based child protection committees also provide a mechanism for consultation with communities. Focus group discussions are another means of consultation used in child protection projects.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

UNICEF education section has informal process of getting feedback from the community, parents and children regarding the education services provided. These feedback are mainly collected through PTA/SMC for further improvement

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

UNICEF and its implementing partner engage PTA/SMCs in the monitoring of all education intervention at the school level. PTA/SMC has the formal role in ensuring education project including setting up of TLS and Supplies are provided timely and effectively.

UNICEF's programme is based on a protection risk analysis which considers the specific needs of different population groups including GBV survivors, CAAFAG and extremely vulnerable children

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The UNICEF programme identifies and addresses barriers of accessing to education services. UNICEF is supporting both formal education services and alternative learning programme for children those who cannot access formal schools due to different reasons. UNICEF is providing mobile school services to cattle camps and pastoralist communities.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

All of the education interventions conducted as part of UNICEF's programme seek to address inequalities and in a protected environment. education services are provided to all vulnerable groups having no access to education services in a safe school environment.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

All of the education project interventions conducted as part of UNICEF's programme seek to address inequalities and protection threats and violence. The programme includes working with CAAFAG, PSS, and case management, prevention and response to GBV, and reduction of corporal punishment in schools.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

UNICEF education section has robust monitoring and evaluation system in place. UNICEF has 10 field offices with national education officers who continuously monitor the project implementation. Data is collected on regularly bases and shortcomings are addressed on time with the implementing partners.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

UNICEF ensure safety of the project beneficiaries on all sorts of monitoring and data collection. UNICEF as a protection expert in humanitarian action, will not interview or use any information without consent of any affected population including children and women themselves.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

UNICEF and the partners will not use any information without consent of any affected population including children and women themselves. Affected population including children, youth and women will be part of planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of project. UNICEF will apply the do no harm approach during the project implementation.
