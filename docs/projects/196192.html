Project Summary

The emergency food security and vulnerable livelihood (EFSVL) department of Oxfam Rohingya Response Programme aims to respond to the combined risk of COVID-19 and pre-existing vulnerabilities of women and girls, men and boys, and at risks individuals among them in Rohingya and Host communities. Refugee influx Emergency Vulnerability Assessment-5 found that overall vulnerability levels have remained alarmingly high since 2020 among Rohingya households. The latest findings showed 95 percent of the Rohingya households are moderate to highly vulnerable and remain entirely dependent on humanitarian assistance like 2020 (96%). Overall vulnerability in the host community also remained as high as 2020, with 52% of the population being moderately to 51% in 2020. Considering the vulnerability situation both Rohingya and Host community, EFSVL department of Oxfam will undertake a project to reduce vulnerability of the both communities (Rohingya and Host). This project proposes to cintinue organizing skill development training support to the vulnerable women, men, youth boys and girls of the Rohingya and host community people to enhance livelihood opportunity and improve access to adequate diversified job opportunities. Intervention includes different skill development training like tailoring, handicrafts production, catering service and pickle production etc. In addition, this project will introduce TVET interventions espacially mason, mobile servicing in the Rohingya camps which create job opening in camps and these acquired knowledge and skills help them to search a job when they will repatriate in the Myanmar as well. Alongside, the host community women, youth boys and girs will receive a lot of training in different trades like tailoring, handicrafts production, block-batuque printing in cloth, mason, mobile servicing, carpenter, motor mechanics, electrical house-wiring, tiles setting, air cular and refrigerator etc. Alterwards, these training will create job prospect in private sector or by self-employment. After successful completion skill development training, all project partcipant will get input/tools suppor to start up their jobs/business in private sector and for self-employment. For quick promotion of product, market linkage to established by organizing workshop with different level of stakeholders like market actors (input seller and output buyers), member of market management committee, representative of Govt. line department like Youth development, Social welfare, Women and child affairs), microfinancing institute, Bank and leader of producer groups resulting will develop common understanding among them and build linkages with producer groups. Oxfam in Bangladesh will also explore ICT-based livelihood skills opportunities (A2i, Muktapoth) for the host communities and possible market linkages. Youth led green enterprises, youth and women groups institutionalization will also be prioritized.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Access to Livelihoods and skill continues to be an obstacle for the refugee population residing in Cox’s Bazar camps and host community areas due to the ongoing Rohingya crises. A recent assessment conducted by WFP which is known as REVA (Refugee influx Emergency Vulnerable Assessment) shows that Overall vulnerability levels have remained alarmingly high since 2020 among Rohingya households. The latest findings showed that 95 percent of all Rohingya households are moderately to highly vulnerable and remain entirely dependent on humanitarian assistance, similar to 2020 (96 percent). These results reflect the slow economic recovery of an already fragile population with no income sources nor livelihood opportunities. Overall vulnerability in the host community also remained as high as 2020, with 52 percent of the population being moderately to highly vulnerable in 2021 compared to 51 percent in 2020. The main drivers were the economic contraction and decline in economic activity across most sectors during the COVID-19 lockdown in a population highly dependent on daily wage labour. The proportion of Rohingya households with inadequate food consumption (poor and borderline) improved in 2021 reaching 45 percent, compared to 50 percent in the previous year – yet remains higher than pre-COVID-19 levels in 2019 (42 percent). In the host community, the proportion of inadequate food consumption increased in 2021 reaching 38 percent of households surveyed, driven by the increase in the proportion of households with borderline food consumption – showing continued challenges for the host population in meeting their food consumption needs after COVID-19. Discounting the value of assistance, a simulated scenario showed that the economic vulnerability would remain high with 94 percent of Rohingya households consuming below the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB); reflecting the fragility of the camp economy and its full dependence on assistance to cover the essential needs of almost all households. Despite the current level of humanitarian assistance, 51 percent of Rohingya households cannot afford the minimum expenditure basket (MEB). Compared to 2020, economic vulnerability has slightly deteriorated among Rohingya (by 2 percent) and host communities (2 percent). This indicates a significant dependency on humanitarian assistance when discounting humanitarian assistance. This also indicates the assistance is only able to offset the needs because of the underlying fragilityAs the Rohingya crisis evolves and becomes more protracted, needs are increasingly becoming diverse and complex. The long-term humanitarian responses call for a review of the response modalities currently in place and a rethink of humanitarian strategies. Greater emphasis should be placed on strengthening linkages between sectors with a drive towards an integrated approach to enable meeting the essential needs of affected populations.As Covid-19 measures were in place in Cox’s Bazar, food access reduced and became more insufficient for refugees, and this trend was more for single mothers, households headed by persons with disabilities, pregnant and lactating women, and the elderly. Disproportionate realities of women and girls were exacerbated during the Covid-19 pandemic which impacted on their mobility thereby reducing access to basic needs, further reducing their limited access to income, and increased the burden care work due to stay at home protocols. Women who are mostly in the informal economy and rely on daily wage and minimum have very few safety nets especially in host communities. Across host and Rohingya communities, experience of GBV also increased including IPV and early marriage. (Rapid Gender Analysis on Covid-19, October 2020).

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

The Project will mitigate the risks and needs will be addressed by increasing Rohingya and host community, facilitating safe programming (e.g., Produce project Risk matrix), safeguarding and timely access to services, fostering social cohesion. Project beneficiaries will receive training that will improve their knowledge and economically empowered and increase decision making power. The project will also support Rohingya and Host community members in building their awareness of Gender-Based Violence and empowering women in leadership. Oxfam will orient project staffs and others revenant stakeholder’s regarding prevention of Sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), Safeguarding and the project participants as well .

3.2 - Gender

The project will conduct detailed gender analysis at project inception to support equitable project planning and implementation process. Gender Mainstreaming actions in the project will make significant contribution to the process of mitigating the impact of women’s exclusion from decision-making processes and in ensuring that we are better able to meet the needs expressly identified by our female participants. Oxfam follows a strong community-based approach at all steps of project implementation. The integration of project team and other relevant stakeholders will help ensuring the implementation of key activities beyond basic safe programming and Do No Harm approaches. Pregnant and lactating women, persons with disabilities will be able to access any distribution or other’s services via priority basis. They will be encouraged to participate in the project activities as well as in our monitoring activities.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

Oxfam commits to creating and maintaining a safe organisational culture where all those whom Oxfam serves and who work for Oxfam feel empowered to insist on non- discriminatory and respectful behaviour from each other, where poor behaviour is not accepted, and where power is not abused. The safeguarding of children within Oxfam’s work is all our responsibility and must be considered in every aspect of our work. A commitment to good practice is expected of all Oxfam’s employees. Oxfam is open to feedback, continual learning, and improvement to ensure that safeguarding children is at the project location of our work.

3.4 - Diversity

During the participant selection process project team will involve and ensure diverse group of people as for example, Persons with disabilities, different ethnic people, gender, races, religions and intersectionality with also incorporated different types of diversified livelihoods and skill development income generation activities considering the beneficiaries, context and market demandable.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) Principles will be incorporated into the project through meetings/Training with project staffs. Further, the staff works closely with the PQ team where the response to all urgent matters is in community level and tracking the project deliverable to the beneficiaries by implementation different complain feedback and response mechanism as like establishing help desk, post distribution monitoring (PDM), Hotline, complain box, Feedback hub, conduct session etc. During the whole projectcycle the feedback and response of the communities will be incorporated keeping them in the centre of activities.
