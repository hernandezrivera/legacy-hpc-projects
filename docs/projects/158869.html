1 - Needs

a) Need for Mine Action Response: Existing ERW contamination still poses a threat to Gaza communities. Since 2018, there have been 16 ERW accidents resulting in the injury and death of 28 people, 18 of whom are children. 10 of those accidents occurred in 2019 alone showing a slight increase since 2018.In total since 2014, 186 people have been killed or injured by ERW, 80% of whom were men and boys and 20% of whom were women and girls. 149 known and accessible Deep Buried Bombs (DBB) were cleared by UNMAS, and there remains cases that still pose a threat to the safety of communities in Gaza, and which will need to be cleared. While the exact number of cases will only be known as and when bombs are discovered, there are still uncleared unexploded ordnance, clearance of which would support vulnerable communities to become more resilient to the impact of ERW’s. Additionally, the instability of the political situation increases the likelihood of an escalation in hostilities, and consequently future new ERW contamination, posing new threats to the population of Gaza. b) Need for Risk Education: While the threat of explosive ERW is indiscriminate, an individual’s risk of exposure varies on factors such as where they live and work and the information they have at hand to allow them to protect and prepare themselves against ERW threat. UN staff, humanitarian workers, IDPs, engineers, construction workers, and other at risk populations including children, vulnerable groups and people with special needs, people in border areas, particularly boys and men required need speciﬁc knowledge and awareness to identify and safely respond to residual ERW contamination, as well as concrete skills to prepare and protect themselves and their families against future ERW contamination and armed conﬂict. Vulnerable communities, especially in the border areas continue to display a lack of knowledge of attitudes and practices to remain safe from existing ERW and from the threat of continued violence. Moreover, communities in Gaza are unprepared and unprotected in the event of ongoing and future escalations; which was evidenced in the death of two UNRWA school children in tin Israeli airstrikes. The delivery of timely risk education is imperative to reduce the risks of ERW contamination on the lives of men, women, children, farmers and other at-risk civilians. As part of an UNMAS Conflict Protection and Preparedness risk education project, Norwegian People’s Aid (NPA), conducted a survey in Gaza in 2017 that revealed an increase in awareness and retention of ERW threats following risk education sessions in communities. c) Need for support to reconstruction of infrastructure: The presence of ERW in Gaza impedes the clean up and reconstruction of vital housing and infrastructure, as well as the implementation of humanitarian projects. Explosive items remain hidden in debris, agricultural ﬁelds and urban areas that must be addressed. While the majority of ERW originates from the 2014 war, ongoing clashes bring in additional explosive hazards, evidenced in the discoveries in 2019 of 3 ERWs in infrastructure project sites which UNMAS coordinated clearance of. Subsurface contamination, as well as deep buried contamination, real or suspected, complicates and delays reconstruction efforts and poses a danger to IDPs returning home, farmers tending their land, children playing on the beach, construction workers, and entire vulnerable communities. UN agencies, NGOs and contractors do not have the specialised required skills to assess potential risks of ERWs when implementing a new reconstruction project, therefore it is necessary for coordination of clearance to occur. All UNMAS activities are done in collaboration with the community, to ensure that the needs of the communities are being met. This is put in practice through, surveys and impact assessments on risk education provision and risk assessments at the community level.

2 - Activities

EO1: The clearance of ERW contamination in Gaza mitigates the risk of injury and death to most at risk population. O1: EOD and bomb clearance capacity is maintained to remove and destroy existing contamination which poses a threat to Gaza communities. Activities:• Coordination with UN, relevant authorities, NGOs and others, to ensure a coordinated and rapid response to existing ERW contamination and emergency response in case of an escalation in conﬂict; •Continued assessment and adjustment of contingency plans to rapidly and effectively implement emergency response in case of conﬂict escalation to ensure protection of civilians and resumption of humanitarian activity; •Maintain a database of information on ERW accidents and victims disaggregated by gender and age• Maintain a robust referral mechanism to allow for proper care and follow up of the victims, disaggregated by gender and age. EO 2: The provision of ERW risk education, particularly to the population most at risk, mitigates the risk posed by residual ERW hazards from past conﬂicts (partially implemented by MAAN with supervision and oversight by UNMAS). O1: Consistent ERW safety messages are delivered to communities in Gaza, with a speciﬁc attention to children, farmers, displaced persons, and people living in access restricted areas. Activities: •Provision of ERW risk education to at risk civilians, of all ages and gender particularly men and boys who are most at risk, UN and NGO personnel, and workers involved in rubble removal and reconstruction projects; •Provision of risk education to schools and children (boys and girls) in coordination with UNWRA (UNRWA schools) and UNICEF (government schools); •Ensure consistency and quality of risk education materials and activities, including review of gender and culturally appropriate content provided by other agencies/stakeholders; •Record numbers of all those who have received RE in Gaza, disaggregated by gender and age. EO3: The provision of emergency preparedness focused risk education mitigates the risk posed to communities by ongoing contamination as well as future armed conﬂicts by providing protection and preparedness. O1: Concrete knowledge and skills on safety preparedness and protection is delivered to the civilian population in Gaza. Activities: •Provision of concrete knowledge and skills on safety and risk education through training's to Local Emergency Committees, and women’s groups; •Ensure consistency and quality of risk education materials and activities including review of gender and culturally appropriate content provided by other agencies/stakeholders; •Record numbers of all those who have received emergency preparedness risk education in Gaza, disaggregated by gender and age. EO4: Risk mitigation support reduces the threat of ERW hazards during humanitarian, reconstruction and recovery efforts and help ensure they take place safely and without delay. O1: Risk mitigation support is provided for reconstruction and humanitarian projects by other stakeholders, to ensure safety. Activities: •Provision of ERW training sessions to men and women; and Provision of ERW risk assessments (systematic and investigative process that involves identifying explosive hazards, predicting possible incidents, and determining the impact of hazards and mitigation measures that can be implemented or planned); •Provision of 24/7 Explosive Ordnance Disposal support to reconstruction projects that aim to construct roads, public facilities, public parks, water wells and other infrastructure, which enables equal and impartial access by men women boys and girls. . Activity and output data is collected through implementing partners or directly and managed through the UNMAS data information management systems. The data is monitored on a weekly, monthly and quarterly basis by UNMAS community liaison assistants and the chief of operations. Evaluation is conducted monthly to ensure project effectiveness.
