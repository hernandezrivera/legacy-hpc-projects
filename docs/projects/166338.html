Project Summary

The COVID-19 pandemic is changing the environments in which children live. Disruptions to families and the wider community can have negative consequences for children’s well-being and development. As the virus spread, people face multiple new stresses, including physical and psychological health risks - Through all that, children are particularly vulnerable.
At Monguno, congestion caused by the ongoing insurgency placed children at significant risks already while the COVID-19 preventive measures such as restriction on movement, closures of schools and business can further exacerbate children’s protection risks. These measure being put in place to curb the spread of the corona virus in many cases only mildly consider gender-specific needs and vulnerabilities of women and girls as well as Persons with Disabilities (PwD). Furthermore, access to information is often a barrier for persons with disability who have specific communication needs. These may increase their protection risks and lead to negative coping mechanisms. Also due to the congestion caused by the ongoing crisis at Monguno, COVID-19 can be a major multiplier of child protection risks and is likely to increase number of separated and unaccompanied children as caregivers are at high risk of mortality.
This project aims to strengthen capacity of Community Based Child Protection Committees (CBCPC) to respond to child protection risks generated by COVID-19 while also supporting affected, at-risk families with in kind assistance and sustain availability and access to Child Protection services.
The following are deliverable of the project:
a. Access to sustainable case management and Psycho social support services
b. Capacity of CBCPC to prepare and response to COVID-19 child protection risks is strengthened
c. Awareness activities targeting households and vulnerable sites are well aligned with government strategies specific to the disease prevention
d. Communities are aware of COVID-19, it's mode of transmission, signs of infection and mitigation as well as services available
e. Health and dignity risks is reduced particularly for persons with disabilities through provision of special Hand Washing Stations
f. Children and caregivers affected by COVID-19 received protective and specialized, psychosocial support, including access basic services
g. Communities have access to adequate Hygiene kits suitable for COVID-19 prevention.

1 - Needs

COVID-19 is quickly changing the context in which children live - Children are at risk of losing parental care when their caregivers die, are hospitalized, fall ill or are quarantined as a result of contracting the virus. Children who may themselves be hospitalized or quarantined may also be deprived of parental care. Psychological distress is already being experienced by children as a direct result of COVID-19 pandemic or the fear of contracting the disease, as well as the impact of containment measures such as school closure. This can be worsened by the lack of peer-support, undermined parental support and cessation of social connections for children.Quarantine measures such restrictions on movements disrupt children's routine and social support while also placing new stresses on parents and caregivers who may have to find new childcare options or forgo work. Stigma and discrimination related to COVID-19 make children more vulnerable to violence and psychosocial distress. Disease control measures that do not consider the gender-specific needs and vulnerabilities of women and girls as well as PwD may also increase their protection risks and lead to negative coping mechanisms. Children and families in Monguno who are already vulnerable due to socio-economic exclusion or those who live in the overcrowded settings are particularly at risk. COVID-19 threatens to not only directly conflict affected children but also act as a major multiplier of child protection threats such as physical and sexual violence, exploitation and abuse, child marriage, child labour, gender-based violence and is also likely to increase the number of separated and unaccompanied children as caregivers are at high risk of mortality from the virus.Family environments marked by poverty or limited resources will bear the full brunt of measures used to prevent and control COVID-19, such as restrictions on movement and the use of public transport, leading to reduced income/employment, closure of schools, access to social services and support, hunger and social isolation. High stress home environments augment the likelihood of domestic abuse, as well as family conflicts and violence. It can be expected that the number of children at risk of separation and in need of alternative care will increase – where containment measures may lead to separation of children from families, and as a result of the long-term socioeconomic impact of the COVID-19 crisis on families’ capacity to care. Many children are also at risk of exposure to violence, including sexual violence, physical and emotional abuse, as well as witnessing intimate partner violence. This may result from caregivers and other adult family members becoming increasingly stressed and distressed, a sense of support and belonging to a community being disrupted and the use of dysfunctional coping mechanisms to cope with the challenging environment. These risks are compounded by the fact that containment measures can impact access to and availability of child protection and Gender Base Violence (GBV) support services.

2 - Activities

The project will support the Child Protection global objectives in the context of COVID 19 pandemic strategy plan by provide case management services to 100 unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC) generated by the COVID 19 pandemic and 10,000 girls, boys, women and men that require emergency Pychososcial support using Community Based Therapy (CBT) approach. The project intend to address the needs identified under the following activities: 1. Case management Services to 100 UASC and other protection risks:- Identification and documentation of unaccompanied, separated and children affected by other protection risks generated by COVID-19 pandemic.-Placement of unaccompanied, separated and other vulnerable children in alternative care-Follow-up of cases of unaccompanied, separated and other vulnerable children-Reunification of unaccompanied and separated children with their primary caregivers-Referral and support to access basic services2. Mental Health and Psychosocial support for 10,000 girls, boys, women and men with mental distress resulting from COVID-19 pandemic that require emergency Pychososcial support using Community Based Therapy (CBT) approach under the following activities; -Structured recreational/creative/social activities for girls and boys in a group of 20 participants.3. Other activities include:- Development and dissemination of appropriate pre-recorded and other IEC key messages on the prevention and detection of COVID-19 in local languages.- Provision of information in accessible, multiple forms of communication, signed videos, text captioning through a tailored approach to meet individual needs of persons with disabilities.- Mobilization and training of Community-Based Child Protection Committees – CBCPC (i.e CPC/MSG/CV) on COVID-19 preparedness and response- In collaboration with the CBCPC, identify alternative psychosocial support activities in the case of children under quarantine.- Procurement and distribution of hygiene kits to families (i.e Soap, Hand sanitizers, face mask)- Development and establishment of special hand washing point which will also meets the specific need of persons with disabilities- Development and dissemination of specific messages to explain the risk for elderly and how to care for them. Targeting family members, health care providers and caregivers.
