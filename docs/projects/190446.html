Project Summary

Ethiopia is experiencing prolonged drought with three consecutive poor rainy seasons. Due to the failure of the March – May 2021 rains (Belg,Gu/Genna, Diraac/Sugum), in certain areas approximately 70% of crop production failed. Widespread below-average rainfall during the October to December 2021 deyr/hageya caused shortage of pasture and water, resulting in a sharp deterioration of livestock body conditions including livestock deaths due to starvation and drought-induced diseases. According to a FAO assessment (November 2021), in Borana Liben and Dawa zones, approximately 68 000 animals died and over 1 million were in poor condition. In addition to this, water and pasture situation will start to improve with the onset of the next season, by March/April 2022. During this lean period, more animals are expected to die, and more households to become destitute.
Objective 2: Protect and restore livelihoods Following the delayed onset, erratic and poor autumn Deyr rain and the poor regeneration of browse and pasture, livestock that were already in poor condition have continued to lose weight with the result that pastoral households have little or no access to milk and increasing numbers of households are reporting livestock deaths.
Recognizing the forecast for intensifying drought conditions and the escalating threats to livestock, the SAG issued a Livestock Contingency Plan ( 2022) that prioritized livestock feed supplementation of core breeding animals, destocking – commercial and slaughter - and animal health, in particular for those animals receiving supplementary livestock feed. The 2022 Deyr rains have largely failed in the Somali region, leading to critical water and pasture shortage, abnormal migration of herds, deterioration in the body condition, productivity and sale value of animals, unfavorable terms of trade due to prolonged dry spell following failure of the seasonal rains, and overall negative impact on the lives and livelihoods of the predominantly pastoralist population of Somali region. The worst affected areas include entire Zones in the region . Current pasture and water conditions are the worst in decades in many areas, surpassing those observed during the 2010/11 drought.
In response to this crisis AID has planned to implement the Emergency Livestock Response for Drought Affected Pastoralists in Liben Zone, Somali Region, Ethiopia, to support the drought affected community living in three woreda of liban zone in Somali region Namely, Karsadula, Gorobakaksa and Gura dhamole with emergency Livestock Response intervention through provision of livestock feed, animal health intervention, provision of Agricultural inputs, restocking/destocking and income generating activities (IGA) for a total of 20,000 households across three woredas with the allocated budget of1000,000 USD for this response within a period of 6 months.
For the effective implementation of this project AID will closely coordinate and works with the implementing partners, stakeholders and the beneficiary community at large. During the targeting of beneficiaries, AID will ensure the inclusiveness of the most vulnerable community members including people with disabilities, children’s, pregnant and lactating women. Priority will be given to those who are mostly vulnerable as they are the victims of the disasters their involvement will be ensured during the targeting and verification process. Throughout the project period AID will ensure the environmental and climate factor will be taken into consideration, thus the project activities will be implemented in accordance with environmental friendly, meanwhile local authority and beneficiary communities will be consulted and activities will be implemented in a way that could not harm the local environment, during the agricultural/ farming activities beneficiary will be provided with awareness on the importance of environmental protection while doing farming activities to prevent the deforestation.

1. - Projects activities

Project Activities The project will be implemented over a period of 6 months while AID has the full support of the regional, zonal and woreda authorities for the implementation of the project. Through this project 100,002individuals 20,000 Households of IDPs and returnees, 41,340 individuals from Gorobaqaqsa, 43318individuals from Guradhamole and 15344 from Qersadula Woreds of Liben Zone Somali region will be reached. To determine the specific intervention modality applicable in all three project geographic locations, AID will conduct a market assessment to assess market accessibility, functionality, the capacity of vendors and to identify appropriate intervention modality. The project targeted 210444 heads of livestock Detail activities 1.1 Animal feed interventions As parts of the animal feed intervention, AID has proposed to reach 6283 drought affected households through the following sub activities• Procurements and Provision of livestock supplementary feed for a total of 6,911 household. Out of this 3,609 beneficiaries are from Goro baqaqsa and the remaining 3,302 are from Guradamole• Distributions of livestock feeds to 6,911HH drought affected community for a total of 13,822 livestock heads • Capacity building training on livestock feeds ( composition and preparation of animal feeds, how to feed the livestock etc) 1.2 Animal health interventions for 9,214 households • Supply and provision of livestock treatment and vaccination for 193,502 livestock heads• Community mobilization and organizing woreda animal health experts and CAHWS1.3 Provision of agricultural inputs for 44,140 individuals from all 3 woredas • Procurements and distribution of agricultural tools ( hoes, shovels, sickles and forks) and forage seeds such as sudan grass, alfalfa, and rodhus grass. Each households will receive 1 pc of agricultural tools and 5 kg of forage seeds from all types of seeds1.4 Animal destocking • A total of 312 Households which is 10 HH are from Qersadula, 158 HH from Gorobakaksa and the remaining 144 HH from Guradamole will be benefited from animal destocking program. The animal destocking activities will be conducted through cash modalities (assessment will be done to identify the appropriate modalities)1.5 Forage seed provision • Procurements and distributions of forage seeds. Forage seeds consisting of sudan grass, alfalfa Maize and rodhus grass will be procured and distributed. A total of 6,911 beneficiary households across three woreda will be provided with forage seeds.1.5 IGA support • To support the drought affected community AID will target the most vulnerable community members and support with IGA activities these will includes animal marketing, animal fattening program and milk and meat production activities. 3,413 small group of community members preferably women group will benefited1.6 Restocking/destocking will be provided for 312 House holds • To support the drought affected communities the most vulnerable community members will be targeted with restocking program, 8 breeding goats (one male goats and 7 she males goats )will be provided for 312 HH .
