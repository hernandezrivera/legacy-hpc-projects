Project Summary

This project aims to reach 181345 target beneficiaries (excluding overlap), building on the experience, expertise and relationships from its previous project cycles to reach the most vulnerable and in-need, and with protection mainstreaming and sustainability at the core of its programme. Medair will focus on rehabilitating and/or re-equipping primary healthcare facilities in areas with high needs, prioritizing hard to reach and newly accessible areas, as well as rehabilitating a maternity ward. Given the high needs and likely reduction in the presence of other actors. Following infrastructure rehabilitation and/or re-equipment, a 12-month programme of monitoring visits and capacity building training for staff will maximise the quality of service provision.
The community health programme (CHP) will target key health, hygiene and nutrition behaviours through messaging at household level in the clinic catchment population.
For the 8 PHCCs rehabilitated and/or re-equipped by Medair in 2020, the supervision, training and CHP will continue into 2021. Medair plans to rehabilitate and/or re-equip up to 6 PHCCs during 2021, as well as one maternity ward.
Additionally, 2 hospitals will be provided with necessary measures for COVID-19 prevention and management, including training for all health care staff, PPE for visitors, and equipment to support COVID-19 ICU beds.
MHPSS services, healthcare for people living with disabilities and initial support of GBV cases will be integrated into the services provided by the PHC facility. Nutrition screening and treatment will also be integrated-details of this have been submitted separately as per sector guidance.
Service provision is by existing public sector health staff. Medair Health Officers (4) conduct field visits and training. Support staff consist of Health Coordinator, Health Advisor and senior management.To ensure sustained access to healthcare, Medair will exit the location at the end of the support period by handover of each PHCC to national or regional health authorities.
Medair provides capacity building training for both clinic staff and CHWs working in the clinic catchment area. 615 healthworkers and 430 CHWs will be trained in 2021. The training package for both is extensive, and delivered over a 12 month period by Medair staff, qualified MOH staff, and external trainers. Medair provides transport /accommodation costs for trainers if needed. Healthworker training comprises 28 days per governorate (140 training days in 2021), covering key gaps (RH (ANC, PNC, FP), mhGAP, PFA, Disability inclusion, NCDs, and IMCI) and COVID-19.
CHWs receive 8 days of initial training and 6 refresher days, delivered per clinic (98 days total during 2021) . Supervisors also attend M+E days. Topics include ANC/PNC, nutrition, MHPSS awareness, PFA, hygiene, communicable diseases, NCDs, GBV awareness, and disability awareness. CHWs will also identify up to 395 PLWMIs in order to support them with Assistive Devices.
Consultation target calculated based on catchment population, a clinic utilization rate of 1 and number of months Medair will support service provision.

4 - Needs

After over 10 years of crisis in Syria, 13 million people (GHO 2021) need assistance and its health system is left damaged and weakened. Syria’s already fragile health system was not ready for COVID-19, announced as a pandemic by the WHO in March 2020. The numbers of COVID-19 cases and deaths are vastly underreported. The availability of ICU beds and ventilators in Damascus and Rural Damascus is limited, and already at a 100% capacity according to the WHO. Moreover, the majority of all KIIs by Medair report a lack of testing, isolating, treating and quarantining capacity.In terms of Health needs, RH is a priority. A survey conducted among Medair’s CHWs in 2020 has shown that one-third of female CHWs (know someone who) gave birth at home without any professional assistance. The last recorded MMR is from 2017 (31 per 100,000 live births). Generally, in Syria at least 15% of women and newborns will require more lifesaving comprehensive care (HNO draft, 2021). The conflict has affected the way women deliver. According to WHO standards, the ideal C-section rate is 10-15% of all deliveries. Yet, since the start of the conflict, C-section delivery increased in all governorates, reaching 46% overall (HeRAMS, 2020). This is due to various factors, including an inadequate maternity infrastructure, lack of equipment and trained staff, and ease of timing C-section delivery and discharge from the hospital. DEZ has no women on record receiving BEOC services in Q2 2020, making it a high priority area for interventions addressing RH needs.

5 - Implementation of activities

Medair will rehabilitate and re-equip 2 PHCCs, 1 Maternity ward, and re-equip up to 4 PHCCs to ensure delivery of Syria BPHS. Priority will be given to clinics in high need areas. Rehabilitation of clinics includes appropriate WASH, privacy, and accessibility for PLWMI. Outpatient consultations are provided with a focus on vulnerable groups incl. CU5, PLW, elderly, people with MHPSS needs, and PLWMI. Initial management of emergencies and referrals to secondary care are provided, as well as NCD screening, diagnosis and management. EPI services will be strengthened. EWARS notifiable cases will be identified and reported. Accessible and sustainable MHPSS will be provided through PFA and mhGAP provision in PHCCs. ANC/PNC incl. safe delivery planning, family planning, STI treatment, and initial management of GBV cases will be provided. Medair will implement a curriculum for health care staff. The focus will be on meeting key gaps in the current health system: RH, GBV, IMCI, NCDs, PFA, mhGAP HIG, disability inclusion and nutrition. Where security and access allows, a CHP will accompany each supported PHCC. Trained CHWs will share targeted health messages at HH level to improve key health and hygiene behaviours, and identify PLWMI for assistive device distribution.Where possible, joint monthly supervision visits will be done with the DoH. Standardised tools are used to assess service availability/quality. Clinic registration data will be collated monthly. Beneficiary feedback will be collected where possible. Additionally, Medair will increase COVID-19 preparedness of the healthcare workers by increasing the health facility capacities of IPC and COVID-19 management. Both at primary care as well as secondary care levels.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

• Confidentiality of sensitive data is breached, risking beneficiary stigmatisation: train staff on data protection; clinic staff to ensure privacy during consultations. • Reluctance of community leaders to accept GBV awareness-raising: involve influential parties from start of health programmes. Clearly communicate issues and encourage community contribution. Support social structures. • Inequity of access to clinics between MWBG: Awareness of wider community context e.g. transport availability, market times. Ensure service providers available at suitable times for women and men. Train clinic staff/CHWs. Monitor data for adverse trends. HH visit awareness-raising of importance of access for all children.• Head of HHs or PLWs reluctant to be screened, access services, or receive HH visits: CHWs mainly female (85%) to achieve acceptance for HH visits.• Inequity of access to clinics for people with mobility impairments: accessibility adaptations to be installed during rehabilitation; disability awareness and inclusion training to all staff and CHWs• Children at risk from accidents during building works: Explanation in advance to communities; Contractual requirements for tidy work sites. Site engineer to oversee contractors. Spot checks.
