Project Summary

The objective of the proposed intervention is to provide assistance to newly displaced and vulnerable populations, as well as to promote social cohesion and community resilience in areas impacted by conflict and displacement by improving the quality of shelter and services for conflict-affected populations, benefitting a total of 1,850 households / 9,250 individuals. In total, the proposed action will target 425 IDP households, 625 non-displaced households, 550 returnee households, and 250 migrant households across Libya. ACTED will provide NFI kits among newly displaced IDPs arriving in Benghazi and Ajdabiya. Through the provision of NFI kits, ACTED aims to reach 150 households / 750 individuals in Benghazi, and 150 households / 750 individuals in Ajdabiyah. To support peaceful coexistence and community resilience, ACTED will conduct participatory assessments to identify the needs and priorities of targeted communities, based on which feasibility assessments will be carried out for the selection of community-based Quick Impact Projects in the East, West and South of Libya. These projects will include the rehabilitation of communal buildings and infrastucture, benefitting a total of 1,500 households / 7,500 individuals: 500 households in Tripoli, 500 households in Derna, and 500 households in Sebha. In parallel, shelter rehabilitation is aimed at private infrastructures, improving the living conditions of vulnerable returnees, currently living in damaged and sub-standard shelters, through light shelter repairs, benefitting a total of 50 households / 250 individuals in Tripoli.

1 - Needs

The protracted conflict in Libya has led to a humanitarian emergency across the country, with an estimated 1,2 million people identified as in need of humanitarian assistance in the 2020 HNO. Following 14 months of intensive fighting, culminating in a siege on Tripoli by the Libyan National Army (LNA), which was characterized by repeated and indiscriminate shelling into the city, late May and June 2020 saw substantial and unexpected shifts in the conflict dynamics and frontlines in Libya. From 20 May onwards, Government of National Accord (GNA) forces pushed back the LNA advance, re-taking Al-Wattiya airbase, Tripoli International Airport, and a swathe of southern suburbs surrounding the capital, giving GNA full control of Tripoli, for the first time since 2019. On 5 June, the GNA entered Tarhouna, effectively ending the LNA offensive on Tripoli, and causing 27,750 people to flee to the relative safety of Eastern Libya, primarily Benghazi. Since this time, the conflict has been characterized by a tense build-up and positioning of forces on both sides surrounding Sirte.Based on ACTED’s protection monitoring of newly arrived IDPs, surveyed on their urgent needs, households indicated the most pressing needs are various non-food items (NFI) (38%) and food (33%). Other reported needs include health care (12%) and shelter (11%). Of the surveyed households, 20% reported having never received humanitarian assistance. Findings from REACH’s 2020 MSNA show that IDPs in the East of Libya are resorting to negative coping mechanisms including: reducing expenses on health or medicine (30% in Ajdabiya and 34% in Benghazi); borrowing money (41% in Ajdabiya and 57% in Benghazi); selling productive HH assets or means of transport (23% in Ajdabiya and 28% in Benghazi); or reducing expenditures on essential non-food items (35% in Ajdabiya and 29% in Benghazi). The spike in violence, as the conflict has moved East since June 2020, has also led to emergency level negative coping mechanisms including asking strangers for food or money (28% in Ajdabiya and 23% in Benghazi).To support the sustainable return of IDPs to their area of origin, ACTED aims to provide essential recovery assistance to those most deeply affected by the 2019-2020 conflict. Although the will to return may be there, among both the population and local municipalities, there is an urgent need to rehabilitate public and private infrastructure, in order to pave the way for the return of essential goods and services, to ensure that returns can be safe, dignified, and sustainable. Private shelters have been damaged extensively as a result of shelling, leading to many returnees, non-displaced, IDPs, migrants, and refugees, living in damaged or sub-standard shelters. Moreover, across Libya, public infrastructures have been damaged as a result of the protracted conflict. Buildings have been damaged as a result of shelling or have suffered damage as a result of hosting a large number of IDPs, as has happened in schools. Moreover, in areas where large influxes of IDPs occurred, social tensions between IDP and host communities have risen as public services such as healthcare and education struggle to adapt to the new population numbers and services become harder to access.

2 - Activities

ACTED will aim to provide NFI kits to vulnerable households who have been recently displaced and have not received assistance yet, and to incoming waves of IDPs, in line with the standardised list of basic NFI items (i.e. blankets, solar lamps, mattress) provided by the SNFI Working Group. In addition, ACTED will provide NFI assistance to vulnerable beneficiaries through its Cash for Protection Fund in case cash is not an appropriate response modality. In such instances, ACTED’s response can include direct procurement of household needs or specialized NFI procurement to reduce the household’s vulnerability to protection risks. Moreover, ACTED will implement QIPs across Libya to rehabilitate communal buildings and infrastructure and increase social cohesion. ACTED will conduct community consultations in targeted areas to identify potential QIP sites. These pre-identified QPI sites will be subjected to a feasibility assessment to select the final QIP sites. ACTED intends to implement the QIPs through service contracts with result-based payments to contractors, CSOs, or other community-based organizations. Following the completion of the QIPs, they will be formally handed over to the relevant authorities with a certificate of completion. In parallel, shelter rehabilitation is aimed at private infrastructures, improving the living conditions of vulnerable returnees, currently living in damaged and sub-standard shelters, through light shelter repairs. ACTED will consult local authorities to gather lists of returnee households whose housing were damaged by the conflict in the targeted neighbourhoods. ACTED will screen the shelters of the potential beneficiaries to ensure the households with the most damage, benefit from the intervention, using both technical shelter assessments and vulnerability assessments. Following the identification of the beneficiary households and the contracting of local service providers, ACTED will carry out shelter rehabilitation works.

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

ACTED has a dedicated Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM), consisting of two helplines and an email. The helplines are operated from Benghazi, covering the Eastern region of Libya, and from Tripoli, covering the West and the South of Libya. This allows both direct beneficiaries and community members in all of ACTED’s intervention areas to reach out to ACTED directly via phone or email in order to express their concerns, grievances, or inquire and give feedback on ACTED’s programs. ACTED will ensure that beneficiaries are sensitized to the existence, the purpose, and the processes of the CRM and will adapt interventions accordingly to both specific information and emerging trends identified through the CRM.
