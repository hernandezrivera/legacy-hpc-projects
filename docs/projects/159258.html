Project Summary

The project aims at providing direct support to victims and their families, and strengthening protection of the conflict affected population including through the physical presence of human rights monitors in conflict affected areas, which could deter further human rights violations and serve in itself as a protection mechanism. By engaging directly with victims and witnesses of human rights violations, this will allow to refer victims to life saving humanitarian services, promote an environment that is conducive to the promotion and respect for human rights and contribute to protect the humanitarian space.
An increased presence of the UN Human Rights Monitoring Mission in Ukraine in the conflict affected areas, both in the areas controlled by armed groups and areas controlled by the Government of Ukraine, would allow for an establishment of continuous monitoring of the situation with rights of the conflict-affected population with geographical coverage throughout the Donetsk and Luhansk regions, including the ability to cover the grey zones.
The analysis stemming out of the human rights monitoring will be utilised by key humanitarian actors, and thus contribute to shape their humanitarian response to the protection needs of the conflict affected population. A human rights approach including gender analysis to the humanitarian response is vital where the population is suffering due to lack of protection and failure of accountability mechanisms.
To ensure Accountability to Affected Population (AAP), OHCHR intends to systematically communicate with the affected populations using relevant feedback and communication mechanisms, such public briefing and reporting and other response communications initiatives, documentation of feedback through targeted advocacy and through monitoring and interviewing, as well as ensuring that accountability to affected populations is effectively integrated throughout the implementation of projects.

1 - Needs

More than five years since its onset, the armed conflict in eastern Ukraine continues to cause deaths, injuries and destruction, and disrupt civilian life, affecting 5.2 million people in the country. Periodic spikes and lulls in civilian deaths and injuries reflect the volatile situation and sustained atmosphere of insecurity and anxiety, characteristic of civilian life on both sides of the contact line. The conflict amplified existing human rights challenges and brought along a breakdown of the rule of law with serious human rights violations including illegal detentions, hostage taking, torture and ill treatment, sexual and gender based violence in the territory under the control of the armed groups. OHCHR has also documented numerous human rights violations in the areas under control of the Government of Ukraine. Coupled with repeated indiscriminate shelling of residential areas and civilian infrastructure and reports of international humanitarian law violations, this has led to displacement and protection needs for a large group of people. As a result, access to socio-economic rights as well as to justice and accountability has deteriorated. In that context, human rights monitoring is critical to document human rights violations, to identify critical human rights violations and subsequent need of protection services and design protection/humanitarian responses. There is a need for a stable presence in the field. Field offices will make it logistically easier for the population to access the monitoring mission as it can reach out to the most remote or marginalized parts of society. The more accessible Human Rights Officers are to rights holders, the better. It can help to build confidence, which in turn is likely to increase cooperation with the community, which is essential for human rights monitoring and fact finding to be effective. Confidence building measures are crucial to reverse the entrenchment of the divide between the population living on both sides of the contact line. HRMMU’s field presence will be able to maintain a direct and regular contact with local authorities and greater access to a larger number of communities, which can enable prompt local responses. Experience has shown that the presence in the field considerably bolsters protection at the local level. HRMMU’s field offices help to “regionalize” the analysis, thus reflecting more accurately local conditions and leading to strategies tailored to the local peculiarities. It enables the establishment of more systematic communication with both the humanitarian community and the authorities to be used for advocacy for change. HRMMU also looks at the following needs: Civil and Political rights. Women and men having been held in conflict related detention by armed groups and government of Ukraine entities; women and men subjected to conflict related ill treatment, torture including conflict related sexual violence by the armed groups and the government forces; women and men who are missing. Social and Economic rights. Vulnerable individuals including older men and women, boys and girls whose rights have been impacted by the conflict, including right to health, right to food, right to water, right to freedom of movement, right to social protection, such as pension and etc. Individuals at particular risk, such as women living in proximity of military personnel who may be at risk of sexual exploitation; civilians near the contact line at risk of shelling as well as Explosive Remnants of War (ERW), where men and boys often at higher risk; human rights defenders and journalists in areas with lack of rule of law and limited space for civil society and freedom of expression.

2 - Activities & outcomes

1) Daily interviews, field visits, monitoring and reporting contributing to protection by presence. Identifying particular risks of men and women will be part of the monitoring work.2) Utilising the findings from the monitoring to track trends; informing advocacy actions, early warning and providing referrals to the humanitarian community for a follow up. This includes both Government controlled areas and territory controlled by the armed groups. 3) Utilizing the findings to raise awareness of human rights violations among Ukrainian stakeholders and parties to the conflict, the UN system and broader international community. Supporting human rights approach in humanitarian coordination mechanisms, particularly Protection meetings, Child protection, Gender Based Violence, Mine Action and Housing Land and Property. Working to inform humanitarian actors of the human rights situation allowing humanitarian actors to respond to needs of persons whose rights have been violated.4) Addressing relevant authorities with urgent interventions such as torture in detention, enforced disappearance and support related to civil documentation. 5) Civilian Casualty monitoring (deaths and injuries with gender and age disaggregated data) to advocate and inform parties to the conflict on measures to be taken and their obligations as per International Humanitarian Law (IHL), and to inform humanitarian actors of where the needs are greatest, e.g. need for Mine Risk Education in areas with high number of casualties due to ERWs. 6) Developing capacity of local stakeholders to identify and address human rights violations and vulnerabilities and how to address such challenges. 7) Monitoring possible conflict related sexual violence: documenting and reporting cases of sexual gender based violence with a view to ensure referrals to relevant GBV actors and conducting advocacy with relevant authorities on this issue. Expected outcome:1) Government of Ukraine, armed groups of the self-proclaimed ‘Donetsk people’s republic’ and ‘Luhansk people’s republic’, civil society counterparts, the UN and wider international community are better informed of the human rights situation in the east of Ukraine, in particular in the Donetsk and Luhansk regions.2) Increased understanding and capacity of the civil society counterparts and authorities to address human rights violations; Government of Ukraine, armed groups of the self-proclaimed ‘Donetsk people’s republic’ and ‘Luhansk people’s republic’ and civil society counterparts to have better understanding of accountability mechanisms and the conduct of effective investigations of most egregious human rights violations, including conflict related GBV and sexual exploitation.3) Humanitarian organizations use human rights analysis as a basis for designing a response with a human rights based approach to the protection needs of the conflict-affected population.4) A reduction in human rights violations due to the overall work of the UN Human Rights Monitoring Mission in Ukraine and its partners, e.g. individual follow up on cases leading to release of persons illegally detained; advocacy based on civilian casualty recording may lead to decrease of targeting of civilians, decrease of civilian casualties in general and procedural changes such as amendments to the Temporary Order on Freedom of Movement.
