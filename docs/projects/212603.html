Project Summary

The protracted crisis in Syria and the devastating earthquakes in early 2023 heavily affected its health system. In northwest Syria (NWS), access to lifesaving services has been disrupted due to the destruction of health facilities, a shortage of specialized medical staff caused by emigration and the limited access to specialized health care services such as oncology, cardiovascular, maternity, and pediatrics. Preliminary fundings from 2024 HNO show that almost 40 per cent of primary and secondary care health facilities, serving over 4.8 million people in need of life-saving health services, are either partially operating or not functioning.
The lack of access to antenatal (ANC) and postnatal (PNC) healthcare is a significant concern, impacting 2.3 million women of reproductive age, including 500,000 pregnant and lactating women while compounded emergencies, including the February earthquake and its aftershocks, combined with the socio-economic decline, have caused widespread mental distress among the population, and are exacerbating existing conditions. It is estimated that approximately 1 in 10 people in Syria lives with a mild to moderate mental health condition, 1 in 10 children need focused mental health care and, 1 in 30 is likely suffering from a more severe condition.
In this context, IOM will contribute to improve the accessibility and affordability of maternal and child healthcare along with integrated sexual and reproductive health (SRH) services, in a maternal and child health facility located in northern Aleppo or Idleb.
The selection of the health facility will be guided by recommendations from the health cluster and will be informed by a gap analysis conducted in the target areas. The project will cover pediatric and maternity specialized staff salaries and operational costs, procuring necessary medical supplies for patients, and offering outpatient and inpatient services to beneficiaries. Additionally, IOM will contribute to the ongoing maintenance of the health facility premises.
IOM also proposes to extend the support to the primary health care (PHC) in Barisha (Idleb). IOM will cover procurement of the facility’s medical supplies, running costs, salaries of essential staff, and urgent rehabilitation, allowing the health facility to be fully functional and retain its staff. IOM will provide consultations in the PHC, encompassing a comprehensive range of preventive and curative health services aligned to the WHO Essential PHC Service Package. These services will include: basic trauma and injury care, management of communicable diseases, SRH services, child health, basic mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), and non-communicable disease management.
Furthermore, referrals will be made for any identified individuals and families in need of specialized and non-specialized services, including psychosocial support services. Individuals in need of referrals will include self-identified cases and referrals from health staff.
In addition, IOM, through an IP, will operate one mobile health clinic to provide health care services in two camps, Kafr Nabi and Mashhad Ruhin, that are in remote locations and without any current access to health services. The mobile health team will provide services including the management of communicable disease, child health, MHPSS services, non-communicable disease management, SHR as well as awareness sessions on various health and hygiene topics.
IOM will also support the Syrian Immunization Group (SIG), which is the only entity authorized by the WHO to implement vaccine activities in NWS, with the procurement of necessary equipment to ensure continuity of services and expand the coverage of 200 mobile vaccination teams.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

PSEAH (Protection from Sexual Exploitation, Abuse and Harassment) strategy:IOM ensures that risk mitigation and prevention of SEAH is mainstreamed into all activities and gaps and risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse are assessed through the use of PSEAH Field Checklists. In order to ensure that SEAH risks are properly mitigated, the organization maintains minimum PSEAH standards by ensuring that: the code of conduct includes PSEAH language; all staff (including IPs and health staff) are trained on PSEAH; internal SEAH reporting mechanism and investigative capacity exist for both staff and beneficiaries and are known within the organization; PSEAH awareness-raising activities with beneficiaries and local community are conducted during the activities. Protection Risk Analysis: IOM will mainstream protection and gender throughout all project stages, ensuring that program design aligns with beneficiaries' needs and mitigating potential harm during implementation. This involves implementing a thorough needs assessment, conducted separately for males, females and vulnerable groups such as people with disabilities and pregnant/lactating women. Furthermore, IOM will ensure the presence of female staff in the medical teams so preventing power imbalances and prevent coercion, discrimination, or violence during the delivery of health services. To prevent harm and minimize the risk of abuse or neglect, IOM will implement a comprehensive environment safeguarding strategy. Additionally, a confidential complaints and protection reporting mechanism will be established, ensuring beneficiaries’ awareness on how to report incidents. Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures. Assumptions: 1: Access to target area remains sufficiently secure and free from any detrimental interference by local authorities or armed groups to implement activities 2: Target population accepts and engages in the provided services 3: Coordination with the targeted health facilities is in place and functional Risks: 1: Not adherence to preventive and IPC related measures can increase the risk of transmission of COVID 19 and Cholera to beneficiaries. 2: Attacks on health care facilities, including aerial strikes and shelling. 3: Local actors' interferences into program activities. Mitigation Measures: 1: IOM will monitor the adherence to preventive measures. If a rise in the number of staff with confirmed/suspected infection occurs, IOM will reduce the number of beneficiaries for group activities and/or suspend the service provision. 2: IOM Access Unit is monitoring the security situation closely, receiving reports from the field on security incidents and guiding IPs on security measures and contingency planning. 3: All IOM implementing partners report interference attempts impacting principled humanitarian access through a dedicated access constraints reporting tool, including attempts of aid diversion, influencing programming.
