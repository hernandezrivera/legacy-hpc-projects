Project Summary

The objective of the proposed project is to address urgent shelter needs of vulnerable households severely affected by the October 2020 wildfires in the government-controlled areas of Luhansk Oblast.
To assist households in fire affected areas, IOM proposes to distribute construction kits to enable the households who had their homes destroyed by the fire to rehabilitate the structures.
IOM intends to target 5 out of the 15 villages that were affected by the September fires with a total of 450 homes that have been completely destroyed. During the distributions of tools for the removal of debris of destroyed or damaged houses in the immediate aftermath of the fires, IOM identified that construction kits were the primary request from local authorities and community memebers. To facilitate the development of construction kit content, IOM civil engineers will carry out assessments of the locations to assess the content and quantity of supplies required for the construction kits for the damaged households.
As many of the residents of affected areas are elderly, IOM will also provide conditional cash grants to enable the residents to hire labourers who can assist in the reconstruction of the damaged houses. IOM discussions with affected community members indicated that they would probably be able to manage the reconstruction through communally pooling their collective skills. However, given the level of damage, a conditional cash grant will facilitate a more rapid recovery for the communities by enabling them to hire in additional assistance.
Lastly, IOM intends to provide support to the forestry and emergency services for them to conduct training with local communities at risk on fire management to mitigate against future risks. IOM will support through essential equipment provision for the emergency services as well as through training and awareness raising among community members with the intended outcome that communities will be better capacitated to manage future fire threats and protect their properties and livelihoods.

1 - Needs

Adding to the effects of the protracted conflict and the impact of COVID-19 Ukraine is becoming increasingly affected by natural disaster, particularly wildfires with the number of incidents reportedly tripling since 2019. In April 2020, the Chernobyl exclusion zone was ravaged by wildfires on over 66,000 hectares for two weeks. Acrid smoke from the fires affected many nearby communities, including Kyiv, (100km away) for over 10 days, spiking respiratory issues in affected areas. The Luhansk region has suffered two substantial wildfire outbreaks this year. The first fires in early July affected 120 homes and other buildings and over 5,000 hectares of forest area in the Novoaidarsky district. This was followed by wildfires in September, affecting over 14,000 hectares of land and completely or partially damaging 15 villages, (575 houses total destroyed and 149 partially damaged as reported by regional authorities). In October 2020 the Luhansk regional authorities reported that the July and September wildfires have damaged more homes in the area than the conflict itself ,and the impact on the local ecosystem has been devastating. Both the communities and the emergency response services in the region have called for support in terms of equipment provision to mitigate future fire risks. The region was assessed to be critically lacking the financial and human resources, as well as the equipment needed to mitigate risks of wildfires Local authorities have reported that approximately 2,500 people are in need of humanitarian assistance, with a particular focus on construction materials, construction tools as well as assistance in rehabilitation itself. The communities most in need reside in close proximity to Severodonetsk, and within the 5-20+ km zone from the line of contact in GCA. With the continued focus to primarily assist communities along the contact line, these communities are at risk of being underserved by humanitarian assistance and their needs going unmet. Despite the launch of an immediate response by IOM and other humanitarian actors, affected communities and local and regional authorities expressed concern about the lack of more comprehensive assistance to affected properties beyond immediate needs in the direct aftermath of the fires. The impact of the wildfires is compounding the economic insecurities of already vulnerable communities affected by the conflict. Many of the affected communities were either engaged in livelihood, subsistence agriculture or husbandry activities, which also have been substantially hampered by the fires. This has affected and will continue affecting many households’ capacity to restore their livelihood. The loss of income will result in them not being able to afford the restoration of their damaged properties, replace their damaged belongings and meeting other basic needs. The Luhansk Regional Administration noted the immediate need for support with fire management equipment for emergency services and local communities to mitigate against future risks. Without community engagement in fire management and a properly equipped emergency response mechanism, further damage to the forests could have a devastating effect on the ecosystem and the sustainability of communities.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

Outcome: Fire affected communities can restore housing capacities and are more resilient to future fire threats Output 1: Fire affected households have received construction kits and conditional cash grants to facilitate rebuilding works Activities:1.1 Distribution of construction kits to fire affected households. The composition of a repair kit that will be provided to the project beneficiaries will include and not be limited: bricks, cement, sand, wooden materials and tools for repair. 1.2 Distribution of conditional cash grants to fire affected households to enable households to hire labour to assist with the rebuilding.1.3 Post-distribution monitoring and assessment of structural quality of restored houses Output 2: Forestry and emergency services are supported with equipment and training material for training and distribution to fire affected communities Activities: 2.1 Procurement and distribution of fire fighting equipment to state emergency services2.2 Development of training material in coordination with state emergency services2.3 Organization of community training sessions to raise awareness on the risk of fires and share preventative measures
