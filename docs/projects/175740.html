Project Summary

Quality health service delivery is key in reduction of mortality and morbidity and especially maternal and child Mortality rates. South Sudan struggles to meet the health needs of its population due to years of political instability and economic meltdown that has made it even worse for the affected populations to access health care. Health infrastructure has been severely hit and lack of essential medical supplies makes the situation deplorable.
In 2020. South Sudan has experienced severe flooding with more than 850000 people in 33 counties affected by floods devastating populations and cutting down delivery of essential services including health services. (Flood Snapshot issue 4 Oct. 2020)
COVID 19 pandemic has severely hit the globe with south Sudan also having cases of the disease. This has greatly health service delivery with weak health systems and economic crisis in the country putting pressure on the inadequately equipped health facilities.
More than 44% of the populations in South Sudan travel more than 5 km to access health services and vaccination coverage remains less than 50% (UNICEF 2020).
IPC analysis Oct 2020 shows that more than 6.7million people are in cris due to acute food insecurity and multiple countries with GAM rates of more than 15%.the WHO emergency threshold.
The country is still grappling with multiple health challenges with outbreak of vaccine preventable infections still a major challenge with 2020 having over 8 Counties with confirmed measles outbreak(IDSR wk 52 2020 Report)
TADO will therefore improve access to integrated health services through provision of OPD consultations, Skilled birth attendant deliveries, immunization services, reproductive health services, GBV prevention and case management of rape, Nutrition services including referral to nutrition centers, mental health services, COVID 19 prevention measures and management of cases health education and disease surveillance and alert reporting through timely reports.
This will be done through mobile health services and static facilities with 6 mobile health facilities, 4 PHCUs and 2 PHCCs across all the 6 target Counties.
TADO will target 16260 vulnerable and affected populations with 7967 male and 8293 women of which 1673 will be boys and 1688 girls under five years. Out of the total 16260 TADO will target 2439 people with disabilities.

1 - Needs

Weak heath systems in South Sudan are a great impediment to health service delivery for South Sudan. The ratio of physicians to doctors is on of the highest in the world with one doctor service 65574 people and one midwife serving 39088 women far more than the recommended 1: 10000 people (WHO 2021). This therefore has increased the disease burden in the communities and increased the mortality and morbidity of children under five to 99/1000 livebirths (UNICEF 2020) The country is experiencing acute food insecurity with more than 6.7 million people in crisis and in dire need of humanitarian assistance (IPC report Oct 2020 ). Lack of health services strains the existing resources as communities use the out of pocket contribution to access health services in the health facilities. COVID 19 pandemic in the country has also greatly affected health service delivery in the country and shocked the already weak health systems and continues to be a serious public health emergency. With little or no health infrastructure available for proper management of COVID 19 the situation remains dire. In 2020, the country has experienced more than average rainfall that has also resulted to floods in the country with Jonglei and Upper Nile states worst hit by the flooding that affected more than 33 counties and 850,000 people were affected. (Flood Snapshot issue 4 Oct 2020). The effects of the flooding have been devastating with displacement of populations, destruction of health infrastructure and mass destruction of farm lands exposing the communities to adverse food insecurity and vulnerability to diseases due to conducive breeding environments for vectors and contamination of water sources leading to consumption of contaminated water.Maternal health services remains a big gap in the target counties and in the country with South Sudan having a very high maternal mortality rates 789/10000 live births (UNICEF 2020) that can be attributed to lack of health services in the community, poor health infrastructure and low skilled birth attendants and delivery in the health facilitiesVaccine preventable infections continue to be a big challenge in the country with 8 counties in 2020 having confirmed measles outbreaks (IDSR WK 52 2020). This can be attributed to low immunization coverage and lack of cold chain facilities with immunization coverage of 49% and cold chain coverage of 50% low below the 80% threshold (UNICEF 2020)According to the health facility functionality bulleting. Akobo County has a EPI coverage of 29% skilled birth attendant of 44% and less than 25% of the health facilities are highly functional. The trend does not improve across all other target counties of Ulang, Nasir, Aweil East, Tonj East and South and Magwi.Tonj East and South are currently in catastrophe with IPC report Oct 2020 showing the two counties in IPC 5 and the situation is not expected to improve until July 2021. This has increased food insecurity compounded by inter clan clashes leading to displacements and lack of health services.This therefore calls for integrated health services to ensure that the vulnerable food insecure, flood and conflict affected populations are able to get health services by provision of mobile health services and static facilities thus reducing crude mortality and morbidity rates in the target Counties.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

TADO will ensure that the communities are aware of the non tolerance to sexual exploitation and abuse by TADO as an organization. During the project design and implementation, TADO will create awareness on what entails in sexual exploitation and abuse through setting up posters and banners with a language understandable to the beneficiaries

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

TADO will embrace the use of disability inclusion facilitators who will be able to communicate with people with disabilities, where possible sign language interpreters will be used in order to get feedback and audio recordings for the visually impaired. TADO will employ local community members to be able to get feedback from the illiterate and women representatives to get feedback from the women and girls.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

TADO will have community engagement meetings with the vulnerable and affected populations. during the engagement meetings TADO will inform the beneficiaries about the project activities and their entitlements and form beneficiary accountability committees that will be responsible for giving feedback to the organization and vise versa. TADO will also put suggestion boxes for collecting feedback ad a designated hotline for complaints which will be acted upon as soon as possible.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Positive feedback will be used to improve the project implementation. Negative feedback will be used as a measure to correct mistakes and improve programming as well to the satisfaction of the beneficiaries.
