Project Summary

The on-going crisis in the North West and South West Regions of Cameroon continues to impact negatively on children. The closure of schools in most rural areas of the two regions because of the conflict, removed a very important protective environment for children. Consequently, they face numerous protection risks, including recruitment and use by armed groups. While in the armed groups, they experience physical and mental violence. Even though a majority of these children are forcefully recruited, there are those who join voluntarily for lack anything better to do with their lives. The number of children currently associated with armed groups is not known but is said to be prevalent as narrated by those children who have managed to exit the armed groups on their own. These children exiting armed groups face additional risks. On the one hand, government security forces coming across them regard them as terrorists and they face detention prior to being arraigned in courts. While in detention, some of them are used by the government security forces to go point out the camps of the non-state armed groups that they were associated with, putting them at the further risk of being targeted by the armed groups. On the other hand, the armed groups that they exited consider them as deserters and as potential informers to government security forces and constantly pursue them for re-recruitment. This project seeks to support the community-based reintegration of these children and youths into society by addressing their protection needs in a holistic manner. Their mental health and psychosocial needs have to be addressed in tandem with their medical, education and legal. In this light, the project’s long-term goal offers an evidence-based approach to the children’s and youths’ reintegration into their families and communities, guided by child rights and child protection principles, including the best interests of the child and child participation. The reintegration support will also involve children and youths from the communities of reintegration to avoid stigmatization and stem the tide of child recruitment by offering them better alternatives.
General Objective: The global objective of this project is to provide reintegration opportunities to children and youths exiting armed groups in the NW and SW Regions of Cameroon
Specific Objectives:
- To provide MHPSS to 300 children and youths exiting armed groups
- Carry out sensitization at sub divisional level for the prevention of recruitment and re-recruitment
- Reintegration of ex fighters into families and communities to enhance social cohesion

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The project will ensure the existence of activities that support children and youths formerly associated with armed groups, other children at risk of being recruited, their families, and communities. In alignment with the Paris Principles, these actions will be inclusive, and community based. Mental health and Psycho-Social Support (MHPSS): A counselling program drawing inspiration from existing social care systems will be put in place to support 300 children/youths in reclaiming self-esteem. This psychosocial assistance will support these children and youths to become more resilient individuals who can empower and help protect themselves and contribute to society through reintegration.Case Management: Each of the children/youths exiting armed groups will be supported according to their individual needs. A direct social work program will be put in place to support individual children/youths and families in need of more targeted support services. The project will use an individualized but flexible case management system that ensures that reintegration support addresses individual needs. A community-based designated focal person will conduct ongoing assessment of the protection issues and risks faced or experienced by the children/youths. A holistic and collaborative care plan responding to their needs will then be developed to facilitate referral services, and provide consistent, confidential follow-up support to these children, their families or caregivers until the protection concerns are addressed. Family-Based and Community- Based support systems: Most children and youths exiting armed groups are usually far-removed from their families and communities. They need to be reintegrated back in their families and communities through family and community tracing. Families and communities have an essential role in reintegration – their capacity to support and invest in their children will be incorporated early on, from a reintegration programming perspective as well as preventive approach. Family-based support will be offered to families of children/youths affected by the armed conflict and community members who experienced severe violence in the conflict. This approach will progressively reunite these children/youths with their families/caregivers and communities.
