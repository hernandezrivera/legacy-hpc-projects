Project Summary

The proposed project will implement a multi-sectoral approach to improve access to equitable, non-discriminatory and gender sensitive humanitarian needs of 19200 returnees along with increased access to life-saving nutrition treatment and prevention services for returnee girls and boys under 5 and PLW who were violently and rapidly displaced en masse from North Waziristan Agency (NWA) in 2014 and are now in the process of repatriation to their home towns. This aligns directly with two of the strategic objectives (SO1 and SO3) from the Pakistan Humanitarian Strategic Plan. Access to quality health care services will be improved through training for health care staff and support for two static health clinics and 1 mobile health clinic which will assist in reaching people in areas where services do not exist. These clinics will provide a safe option for women in need of pre-natal and post-natal care as well as basic emergency obstetric care through provision of clean delivery and dignity kits. The proposed set of activities will target returnees of Kurram Agency in FATA, with a focus on the specific needs of women and children. A total of 19200 (women, girls, boys and men) will be reached through the proposed health and nutrition activities in 20 villages falling within 5 main tribes (group of people) of Kurram Agency - Upper Kurram which were some of the worst affected areas during the military operation. Improved community-based nutrition practices supported at community level will empower 19200 family members with knowledge and techniques for improved infant and young child feeding practices, and basic means to screen and monitor the nutritional status of their children as well as access to referral systems for further care when needed. The project proposes to contribute to Gender Mainstreaming through producing an impact working on the Gender Marker Code 2 (a) with equal and increased participation of women’s with men as decision makers, and being the recipients of the services provided by the project.

Needs

Latest UN-OCHA update on returnees (Nov, 2016) indicates that 72% of KurramTDP’s have returned in the last one year. Multi-cluster assessment identified basic health units (BHUs) the most accessible health facility due to its deep roots in the community. In FATA, around 58% of returnees do not have an access to PHC services and 66% cannot avail essential RH (delivery) services. During the last 3 months, 47% of the responding HHs in FATA (compared to 34% in KP) reported they were unable to get the health services despite the needs, due to unavailability of staff and services. Of a critical emergency health care need, 66% of females in return areas of FATA cannot avail essential reproductive health services (delivery services) due to non-availability of female doctors (53%) trained relevant paramedical staff (54%). Inaccessibility challenges become even more pronounced in remote areas where according to WFP no mobile health facilities are available in Kurram agency. Lack of essential health services and facilities will exacerbate existing malnutrition problems among children, and pregnant and lactating women (PLW), which could reach more alarming levels than already exist. Recent inter-cluster needs re-verification mission to Kurrama gency updates by March 2017 only 2 out of 16 health facilities in Upper Kurram are functional while the rest of the 14 facilities are damaged and nonfunctional. Health facilities existing in the Upper Kurram Area reported with need of mother and child health services alongside non-availability of staff, equipment and medicines were prominently indicated. Lack of female health staff is an important hindrance in availing routine and emergency reproductive healthcare services, as well as will also affect nutrition interventions as they are totally dependent on functional health facilities and availability of female health staff. Also BMONC and CMONC services are required. Nutrition indicators of Kurram agency have tended to be worse than in other areas of Pakistan. Malnutrition is a recognized health problem that plays a significant role in elevated child morbidity and mortality rates across Pakistan and it is being exacerbated by prolonged displacement and a return to areas where basic needs are not being adequately met. Over 19% of children are malnourished (almost 24% of children in female headed households), well exceeding the emergency limit of 15%. Nutrition indicators in FATA have historically tended to be worse than in other areas of Pakistan. (GAM) rates among children (6-59 months) remain at 17% and (SAM) rate at 5%. Gender-wise analysis of malnutrition shows significantly higher rates of malnutrition in girls (22.3 % GAM with 5.6 % SAM) than boys (16 % GAM with 4.3 % SAM) among both KP IDP and FATA return areas. Children in female-headed households tend to have even higher levels of malnutrition (23.9 % GAM with 5.7 % SAM) than male-headed households (18.9 % GAM with 4.8 % SAM). Food consumption scores for returnee place 22% of households in the poor category, 61% in the borderline and 17% in the acceptable bracket, worse scores overall than IDPs. When this data is disaggregated, 30% of female-headed households have poor food consumption scores, which is significantly higher than male headed households (21%).
