Project Summary

The project aims at providing livelihoods opportunities to the most vulnerable IDPs in host communities, returnees and members of host communities including women, men and youths in Bui, Mezam and Donga Mantung in NW and Fako, Meme in SW and MIFI and Mifi and Noun in West region This will be done through multiple livelihoods interventions, such as provision of skill and vocational training women, men and youths, setting up of small scale business with start-up kits to the participants, in order to support their income-generating activities. In addition, the project will carry out community discussions with relevant stakeholders as well as carry out awareness and sensitization on peace and social co-existence, GBV and human rights to ensure and enhance socio-economic cohesion among IDPs, returnees and members of host communities in project location.
The project will focus on 3sector objectives. The first aspect will be addressing Objective1, Livelihood stabilized and income recovered. The second component of the project, focus on Inclusiveness & peaceful co-existence among IDPs, returnees and host communities. it will encourage, engagement and participation of women men and youths including persons with disabilities in peace processes. These will be achieved through the following activities:
Act2 Carry out assessments to determine preferred and innovative context-demand-driven vocational and income-generating activities for women, men and youth, including persons with disabilities.
Act3 Rehabiltation of 2 H4BF centers (Nkambe &Bafffousam respectively) to carryout business and vocational trainings
Act4. Microenterprise identification on the basis of assessment, community members would identify promising areas for micro enterprise development by women- either businesses that fill gaps in communities (e.g internet centers) or activities that leverage competitive advantage of communities (e.g. agricultural specialties)
Act5. Provision of skills and vocational training with startup kit/capital for 800IDP's (500women (especially female headed households), 50men and 250youths) and link them to have access to IGAs to boost their socio-economic capital and self-reliance.
Act6. Business plan training structured around the development of business plan. The business plan follows a specified, standardized template
Act7. support 800IDP's (500women (especially female headed households), 50men and 250youths) shall be provided with start-up kits to establish small-scale businesses.
Act8. Organize and support 200women, men and youths to form 4 groups of Village loans and saving associations (VLSA). (25members per group) made up of those provided with startup kits/funds.
Act9. Establish Business support network: Provide 800IDP's (500women (especially female headed households), 50men and 250youths) with 2cycles of 3days training course in basic business skills, including bookkeeping, saving, and basic business management skills. We will also seek additional participants encouraging current partners and participants to guide new ones through peer training.
Ac10. Develop a least Micro Franchise model
Act11. Carry out 8 discussion sessions on Peace and gender-based violence (GBV) prevention including community leaders, community security officials, returnees, IDPs and members of the host communities.
Act12. Sensitize 15000community members on gender equality, peaceful co-existence and human rights.
Act13. Dissemination of peace and reconciliation messages within target communities.
The proposed project is designed such that gender is mainstreamed at all stages of the project (design, implementation and monitoring and evaluation). It will enhance women empowerment as the project targets female beneficiaries including persons living with disabilities to promote gender equality. Also, project will ensure compliance with COVID-19 principles and sensitization on Prevention of SEA, ensure the strict implementation of Do No Harm Principle.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The insurgency affecting NWSW of Cameroon has left many communities wanting as families have recorded loss of lives and means of livelihoods. As a matter of affect women reported in some locations that, they do not have income generating opportunities to build their self-reliance, rather they solely depend on humanitarian assistance to meet their crucial needs. This further expose then to negative coping mechanism, such as begging on the street, while young boys and girls are involved in hocking; in addition, reduction of number in daily number of food by men, women in order to satisfy those of children has been recorded as a means of coping with imposing condition driven by the insurgency. While, the limitation to accessing dignified sanitation has continued to cause several cases of rapes across hot spots in Bui, Donga Mantung in the North West region, Manyu and Meme in the southwest and Noun in the West region. Furthermore, the long queue observed in water facilities has fueled cases of communal conflicts between IDPs and host populations. One of the most significant attributes of the insurgency, which has ravaged Cameroon’s NWSW regions since late 2016, is the widespread destruction of infrastructure and basic services, with some entire communities completely broken down. According UNOCHA situation report, Over 3 million people are affected in NWSW, 1.4M Targeted for assistance in NWSW and 705.8K IDPs within or displaced from NWSW 360.5K Returnees (former IDP) in NWSW and 60.9K Cameroonian refugees in NigeriaThis alarming situation has placed many households’ especially adolescent girls and single female-headed household at risk of protection concerns such as sexual exploitation and Abuse, rape, and trafficking. Several Boys, girls, men and women are abducted, enrolled into non-state armed group (NSAG) while others have been killed, several have been kidnap and huge ransoms paid to secure their freedom. the growing insecurity coupled with frequent attacks and military operations have destroyed means of livelihoods of both men and women thus affecting their means of managing MHM. Women and girls of reproductive age use rags and undignified materials for menstrual hygiene management. This exposes them to infectious diseases and limit their access to services as well as their involvement into decision-making process. in addition, vulnerable women, men, and girls, boys at risk live on begging as a result of limited access of livelihoods opportunities to build their self-reliance.In the midst of the crisis, on COVID-19 outbreak, Between 1 and 14 October, the Cameroonian Ministry of Health recorded 406 new confirmed COVID-19 cases, bringing the total number to 21,203, including 423 deaths and 20,117 recoveries. An overall of 863 healthcare workers have been infected, including 20 deaths. Lack of facemasks, handwashing stations and sufficient physical distancing in schools and host communities in the North-West, West and SouthWest regions of Cameroon may expose women and students especially young girls to COVID-19 contamination.
