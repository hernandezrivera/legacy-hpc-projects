Project Summary

The Project seeks to respond to the humanitarian situation through the integration PSS into disaster preparedness plans and response plans. It seeks to strengthen the capacity of humanitarian responders to mainstream PSS in humanitarian situations and to increase access to PSS for persons distressed by disaster.

1 - Activities

Vulnerable children in Zimbabwe are faced with multiple challenges in realizing the fulfilment of their rights to basic services needed to grow and to realise their full potential. The poor performance of the economy, coupled with the climate change induced disasters has resulted in increased numbers of vulnerable children. 26% of children in Zimbabwe aged between 0 to 17 living in extreme poverty. The crisis situation has caused hunger, incidents of neglect, abuse and exploitation. Children and adolescents become distressed, and they subsequently drop out of school and forced to adopt risky coping mechanisms. The negative coping mechanisms for adolescent girls might include inter-generational transactional sex. As a result of the difficult circumstances they face, older boys and girls might be forced to seek menial jobs where they risk exploitation, and violence and even death. According to a 2019 World bank review, “the country has declining literacy, increase in child marriage rates, high stunting, circular migration between cities and rural areas and an impoverishment of the urban population, and one of the highest levels of teenage fertility in the region. These indicators point to the continuous decline of prospects for children in Zimbabwe.” The prospects of a climate induced disaster for the 2019-2020 season are very high. Information gathered so far by key community personnel including REPSSI volunteers on the ground from 4 districts is as follows: Binga -20 houses have been destroyed by the rains affecting 60 people who have also been displaced. This translates to at least 40 children being affected. 7 schools have also been damaged by the rains affecting an approximate 4590 children (3010 girls and 1580 boys) Rushinga- There is a disturbing number of cases of Dysentery and diarrhea that have been reported this year and they are both indicators of poor hygiene which may also lead to a cholera outbreak affecting the psychological wellbeing of affected children and families. 12 cases of Cholera have been reported so far. Current rains have partially and fully destroyed buildings from 48 households and 6 schools have also been damaged affecting an estimated 240 people and 4 800 learners. This further deteriorates and inhibits favourable child protection outcomes in the district. Epworth and Chitungwiza - Urban poverty is often overlooked and while programming it was noted that teenage pregnancies, food insecurity, lack of birth registration, sexual abuse, neglect and lack of SRH information were affecting adolescents, adolescent and young mothers. Both districts are famous for being volatile and for succumbing to political based violence thus putting thousands of children at risk. Faced with the situation described above, it is imperative that REPSSI intervenes through building the capacity of district stakeholders, key government personnel (district social Workers) and community cadres, to respond promptly to address the needs of vulnerable populations in 8 districts. REPSSI will focus on mainstreaming PSS to enhance individual, family and community wellbeing. REPSSI will work with the community to conduct a rapid assessment of the humanitarian situation in 8 districts. Refresher trainings for staff will be done respond to the humanitarian crisis Humanitarian service providers, including Government and civil society organizations (CSOs) will be provided with orientation on PSS in emergencies focusing on PFA, CPiE and PSEA. Their capacity will be strengthened to identify and provide gender responsive PSS to survivors of sexual exploitation and abuse including GBV. To strengthen the resilience, community dialogues will be facilitated and will focus on prevention, mitigation and response.
