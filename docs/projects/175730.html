Project Summary

In order to reduce the exposure to protection risks that are caused by the lack of dignified shelter, this project aims to provide culturally accepted semi-temporary shelter and NFIs to the vulnerable IDP population in Metuge, Ancuabe and Namuno districts, while ensuring a strong participation of the community.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project aims to improve the shelter conditions of the IDP community and to decrease the exposure to protection threats by providing apt and adaptable shelter solutions in the different stages of displacement. These include1. A reusable and transportable trapulin with basic framing materials in order to respond to the immediate needs of newly arrived displaced persons.2. A temporary shelter of local materials with a tarpaulin roof in order to respond to mid term shelter needs of IDPs arriving to accommodation/resettlement sites.3. These temporary shelters are composed of the commonly used local materials, which allow for an extension and improvement of the shelter to a long-term dwelling. Therefore only local materials (such as bamboo or thatch need to be added. It will target the IDP population living in temporary/long-term settlements and with host families, as well as the host families themselves, with a special focus on the most vulnerable groups. Host communities will be targeted through the distribution of necessary NFIs, as well as through vocational trainings on more resilient construction techniques. 2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Objective1: Activity 1: Distribution of emergency shelter-NFIsTo 2.000 newly arrived IDP HH in all 3 districts most urgent and life-saving assistance will be provided in the form of the most necessary NFIs. Distributions will be based on a rapid needs assessment and include tarps, blankets, sleeping mats, kitchen utensils and buckets/jerry cans. Metuge, Ancuabe, NamunoObjective 2: Activity 2: Training of local artisansUpon community decision on the proposed shelter model, local artisans of both host, as well as displaced community will be trained in resilient construction. They will then supervise and guide the construction works, which in their essence will be carried out by the regarding IDP-family. Vulnerable families not able to construct themselves will receive additional support. Metuge, Ancuabe, NamunoActivity 3: Construction of adaptable multi-phase shelters Under this activity 2.000 emergency shelters will be built. The proposed model consists of plastic sheet, poles, bamboos, tire wire and nails. It complies with the sphere and local standards. It is transformative throughout the different stages of the emergency:1. Phase one emergency tarp2. Phase two emergency shelter of light structure 3. Improved long term shelter (additional local materials required)Tarpaulins from the first phase are then reusable in case of secondary displacement.Metuge, Ancuabe, NamunoObjective 3: Activity 4: FGD Through continuous FGD, community participation and cultural acceptance will be assured. As such, the shelter-model will be proposed and adjusted where necessary to the context and needs.Metuge, Ancuabe, Namuno

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Protection will be mainstreamed throughout the entire project, which itself aims to reduce the exposure to protection threats that arises from the lack of shelter. A conflict-sensitivity assessment will be carried out previously to the implementation phase in order to avoid inflicting harm through the action. In order to decrease ground for tensions and conflicts, both, host and IDP community will be participating in the project activites.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

Meaningful access is assured on the basis of a vulnerability assessment among the IDP population, which will be carried out prior to the implementation phase and which will inform the beneficiary selection. Targeting will thus be need-based, not status based and the project will consider the specific needs that arise from different vulnerabilities.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Effective disability inclusion will be assured through identification of persons with disabilities including different disability status in an exhaustive vulnerability assessment and using the fundamental tools (Washington Group Questions). Persons with disabilities will be prioritized as especially vulnerable group.The proposed shelter models and specific shelter needs will then be discussed will then be discussed in continuous FGD with both, persons with disabilities and their families. On the basis of their inputs, shelters will be adapted to be accessible and adjusted to their specific needs (distances to services, additional spacing, proximity to support networks etc).

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project aims too reduce the environmental footprint wherever possible. All distributed materials, including construction materials as well as NFIs are locally purchased in order to reduce transport and the related emissions. Furthermore, wherever possible, without comproising safety, locally sourced construction materials are preferred. In order to reduce the effects of deforestation, the Bamboo is the preferred (however not only) construction material, and grass roofs are preferred over plastic/zinc sheeting. Furthermore, the project aims to provide one shelter solution to serveral stages of emergency in order to reduce single use and allow for reutilization of materials. The provided materials will serve for a first phase emergency shelter, a light shelter construction, from which families can then expand to a complete house according to local standards by adding locally sourced materials (such as addional bamboo or thatch).
