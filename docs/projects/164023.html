Project Summary

The United Nations (UN) estimates that the number of crisis-affected population in need of humanitarian assistance in Syria is 11.06 million (49.8% male and 50.2% female), of which 4.6 million are in acute need. This figure of people in need, includes 6.1 million protracted internally displaced persons (IDPs); estimated 341,000 spontaneous IDPs return; 75,000 refugees return; 480,000 Palestine refugees and the rest overburdened host communities. More than 70% of the population in need of humanitarian assistance are accessible from within Syria.
Overall, 7.9 million of people are estimated to be food insecure (including 0.57 million severely food insecure and 7.3 million moderately food insecure) and 1.9 million at risk of food insecurity. In this context, FAO has designed a project to assist 114,400 vulnerable crisis-affected food insecure farming households (equivalent to 686,400 people) in Syria. The objective of the proposed action is to improve access to staple foods for vulnerable farmers through increased cereal and pulse production.
Output 1: Access to agricultural inputs to facilitate production of cereals and pulses for 111,400 households increased.
The response package under this output will be in line with sector recommendations for provision of 200 kg of wheat/barley seeds (adequate to plant 1 ha), 50 kg NPK 10:10:10 (adequate for 1 ha). An additional 40 to 50 kg of various legume seeds (adequate to plant approximately 0.25 0.4 ha) or 200 kg per dunum for potatoes will be provided per household.
Output 2: Skills for cereal and pulses production of 111,400 targeted households enhanced.
This output aims at building the capacity of the 111,400 households that will benefit from cereal and pulses production inputs. Beneficiaries will receive training to support use of the inputs and the training programme will be build on the following FAO concepts: “Save & Grow”, climate-smart agriculture and conservation agriculture.
Output 3: Enhanced production and preservation of high quality wheat seed for 3,000 households.
This output aims to enhance wheat seed production and preservation capacity through training and input provision.
It is expected that the introduction of agriculture-based livelihood protection support in current protracted nearly nine years crisis will be able to increase local availability of food, self-reliance and improve food and nutrition security of the Syrian people, especially the most vulnerable ones.

4 - Needs

Cereal and pulse production is vital to ensuring food security for millions of Syrians. It increases food availability at household level, increases the local supply of staple food and puts money in the hands of farmers of different ages and genders, thereby enabling them to increase their food purchases in local markets. Although there was a slight increase in wheat production in 2019 at 2.2 million tonnes due to relatively better rainfall, the current production is still much lower than the pre-crisis average of 4.1 million tonnes. The implication of the current level of production is that there is a significant shortfall to meet national cereal requirements even after considering potential government, commercial and food assistance imports. Agricultural production has been hampered by insecurity and other shocks to production such as floods, fires, frost, high temperature, and crop pests and diseases which have affected large number of farmers across the country. The major underlying constraints to agricultural production include dependency on rain-fed farming, decreasing availability of quality farming inputs coupled with increased prices, severe disruption of input and output markets, destruction of infrastructure and services (irrigation, storage, veterinary services and roads), contamination, land degradation and sanctions.The Crop and Food Security Assessment Mission (CFSAM) 2019 report indicates that there is huge shortage of quality seed in Syria. GOSM, the Government body responsible for the production and distribution of seed of improved cereal varieties, has not managed to meet the seed needs of producers in Syria and only 75,000 tonnes of the national requirement of 400,000 tonnes of seeds will be available for the current (2019/2020) planting season. With a shortage of clean certified seed, most farmers will not have seeds for the next planting season and the few that may through their own means will face quality concerns.

5 - Implementation of activities

Currently FAO is operating in thirteen governorates – Aleppo, Al Hassakeh, Ar Raqqa, Dar’a, Deir e Zor, Hama, Homs, Idleb, As Sweida, Quneitra, Rural Damascus, Tartous and Lattakia. In governorates where UN staff presence is not permitted by UN security regulations, FAO operates through government seconded officers and NGO service providers who are directly supporting implementation of activities and report to FAO.The FAO Representation in Syria will be responsible for the overall implementation of the activities, backstopped by FAO headquarters with the technical support of FAO’s multidisciplinary team in the Regional Office for the Near East and North Africa. FAO will work closely with MAAR to ensure that the project goals will be achieved through proper technical expertise and monitoring capacity. The implementation will be further supported by:• A central coordination committee that includes representatives from FAO, relevant MAAR directorates, community-based organizations and implementing partners. The committee’s role is to ensure facilitation of the project implementation and application of the criteria for beneficiary selection• Local team(s), at village/district level, led by FAO and composed of local community representatives (usually the traditional leader or “Moukhtar”), extension workers and implementing partners, respectively. The team will be in charge of selecting project beneficiaries in line with FAO-established set of criteria that take into account gender-sensitive considerations.• FAO Resilience Officers in the targeted governorates will operationally and technically supervise inputs distribution, training and monitoring of the project.The proposed actions will benefit from existing capacity which can be scaled up at short notice whenever required and aligned to sector response packages. 6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Risk: Beneficiaries being targeted as a result of being selected for project/Risk of community tension arising from beneficiaries selection. Mitigation measures: Inclusion of different categories of affected population (for example IDPs and host communities; returnees and refugees; men, women, boys, girls and youth) based on their specific needs and clear selection criteria agreed upon by local committees.2) Risk: Inclusion and exclusion errors. Mitigation measures: Establishing local beneficiary selection committees with equitable representation of vulnerable groups; putting in place clear and precise guidance on beneficiary selection criteria and process; and ensuring effective communication for guidance in local language.3) Risk of diversion of inputs/assistance. Mitigation measures: Establish community-based beneficiary selection and community-based monitoring.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Risks: Deterioration of the security situation precluding access to areas of implementation. Probability: Likely. Impact: Severe. Mitigation measures: Preventive: continue to gather information from range of sources; advocate with RC/HC and other stakeholders to regain access; develop contingency plans in coordination with service providers and, if necessary, move activities to alternative locations.2. Risks: Major displacements that could put more pressure on livelihood strategies and/or limit the impact of activities. Probability: Possible. Impact: Insignificant. Mitigation measures: Continue advocacy in order to scale up programmes to cover the additional requirements to promote livelihoods opportunities and conduct advocacy with other organizations to include new arrivals in assistance programmes.3. Risks: Changes in the conditions related to cross-border movements and operations. Probability: Likely. Impact: Moderate. Mitigation measures: Ensure that service providers have capacity to deliver the programme within the changed legal framework. Diversify activities complementing international procurement with local procurement and ensure different delivery modalities (scaling up cross lines to balance reduced cross borders) through more coordination with the hub in Damascus.4.
