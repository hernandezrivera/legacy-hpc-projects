Project Summary

UNHCR’s interventions will cover accessible areas in all Governorates, with specific focus on areas hosting large number of returns. As lead agency for the Shelter Sector, UNHCR is coordinating the shelter responses from inside Syria as well as from Gaziantep (Turkey), and acting as the last resort for the Sector when resources are available. Through this project UNHCR will support the communities of IDPs and returnees in targeted areas through emergency response and long term shelter solutions from inside Syria as well as from Gaziantep (Turkey). The project is in line with both the priorities of the HRP and 2021 Sector Strategy and the project will be completed in line with Sector guidance. Project indicator targets represent a sum of Syria and Gaziantep hub targets, outlined below:
The shelter programme aims to improve housing and related community/public infrastructure addressing shelter needs of 10,000 vulnerable households/50,000 individuals through basic house repairs. Returnee communities will be supported with debris removal and installation of solar streetlights to support 837,500 individuals/167,500 returnee families and host community.
Through emergency response in North East and North West, 228,500 individuals/45,700 households will receive with emergency shelters kits, and 150,000 individuals/30,000 households will benefit from tents distribution, camp construction and collective shelter rehabilitation
UNHCR’s cross-border shelter programme aims to provide emergency shelter (tents) to 35,000 households/175,000 individuals and improve IDP sites conditions with road, drainage and lightening provision. The target for the road improvement in IDP sites is 80 km. The role and responsibility of the local and national authorities will continue in close partnership with MoLAE and partners (NGOs/INGOs) in the implementation of shelter and infrastructure works. Technical training will be provided to 2,000 engineers from local government.

4 - Needs

According to the 2021 Needs and Response Summary, 5.9 million people are in need of shelter assistance. This increase is driven by loss of capital, the destruction of housing infrastructure and the deterioration of shelter conditions across the country. An estimated 31 per cent of the overall population continue to live in inadequate shelter conditions, with returnees and IDPs being the worst affected. The range of shelter needs are diverse across different groups, and an increasing number of IDPs and refugees are returning to their original houses/apartments, especially in Homs, Eastern Aleppo, Rural Damascus, Dar’a, Deir-ez-Zour, north Lattakia and Quneitra Governorates. Experience to date in engaging with return communities has demonstrated that a multisector, area-based approach is the most effective way to ensure the viability of returns. Shelter and support to the rehabilitation of basic services and community infrastructure has been particularly relevant to anchor return. As such, UNHCR will continue its current response to address the needs of voluntarily returned IDPs and refugees, and support their initial reintegration, through shelter rehabilitation programmes and small-scale community infrastructure repair, in partnership with other humanitarian and recovery actors as well as with the local authorities. Vulnerable groups, including children, youth, women, disabled and older people, will be given particular consideration in the shelter response. Compared with previous years, UNHCR will have a stronger strategic focus on building the resilience of IDPs and returnees and host communities.

5 - Implementation of activities

Syria hub: Emergency shelter will be required for newly displaced people in North East Syria (NES), Idleb and Western Aleppo Governorates. Shelter support will also be provided to returnees. Shelter activities include rehabilitation of collective shelters, distribution of materials for minor shelter repairs, and upgrading unfinished buildings in coordination with local authorities, landlords and other sector partners. In camps, UNHCR provides operational maintenance in six camps in NES, including installing solar streetlights, rehabilitating roads, upgrading and constructing communal kitchens, and maintaining of sewage and water networks with WASH partners. In 2021, UNHCR will provide 12,500 tents in camps, transit sites, and spontaneous settlements, and rehabilitate and maintain 850 rooms inside collective shelters. With regard to returnees, UNHCR plans to repair 5,400 damaged houses and rehabilitate common areas of 345 buildings. UNHCR will provide 12,205 solar streetlights to 244 neighbourhoods and removing 115,000m³ of debris in six locations. In rural areas 28,000 houses will receive shelter materials such as doors and windows, WASH, electrical, and solar packages for minor repairs. Monitoring will be conducted both through UNHCR teams in the eld, partners, as well as third party monitors. UNHCR will report to the sector while mainstreaming AAP and Gender thought out the response. UNHCR Cross-Border support will address the needs of IDPs with three different activities: provision of emergency shelters (tents) to 35,000 families/175,000 individuals. Second, after facing many floods in IDP sites in NWS, UNHCR will focus on the IDP sites improvement. Lasty 1,090 improved shelters (RHUs) installation will be completed.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Increased community tension due to the assistance, and/or unclear beneficiary selection criteria leading to perceived discrimination. Mitigating measures: Provision of standardized assistance packages with guidelines followed by all sector partners. Harmonized and transparent selection criteria used by all partners. Awareness raising conducted with communities. Regular monitoring, consultation, post distribution monitoring and adjustments to the program to prevent and manage any tensions which may arise. Risk 2: Returnees are exposed to risk of eviction and/or exploitation with major protection implications, while there are also risks of rights violations, either of owners or occupants, and future disputes due to the difficulties in demonstrating ownership documentation (HLP). Mitigating measures: Gather available information locally through a structured due diligence assessment that would help to identify and prevent/mitigate HLP risks specific to each intervention. Risk 3: Interference with beneficiary selection may lead to potential fraud and aid diversion resulting in financial or reputational risks. Mitigating measures: Capacity building of partners to improve vulnerability assessments in accordance with AGD. Advocate with relevant authorities and partners to apply shelter sector criteria implementation in beneficiary selection process. Emphasize importance of impartiality to partners and systematically monitor adherence and feedback from beneficiaries, with corresponding corrective measures. Establish an active and informed complaint mechanism. Risk 4: Explosive materials. Mitigating measures: Close coordination with UNDSS and relevant expert agencies to obtain updated information.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Limited ability to conduct monitoring of delivery of assistance results in potential loss of resources. Mitigating measures: Advocacy with authorities for access to beneficiary data. Strengthen risk based monitoring tools & establishing remote/third party monitoring arrangements where UNHCR does not have direct access. Support partners to establish harmonized monitoring systems and provide training to partner staff on monitoring. Strengthen communication with communities and feedback mechanisms. Risk 2: Planned activities obstructed by the volatile economic situation, such as price inflation, currency depreciation and shortage of supply. Mitigating measures: Early release of resources to partners to allow more time for implementation. Flexibilities in planning & implementation in response to market changes, such as allowing for increased price and reduced target. Risk 3: Delay in government clearance of shelter projects and beneficiary lists. Mitigating measures: Advocacy through UNCT, Sectors, as well as UNHCR Representative with senior government counterparts to expedite the process. Early assessment & beneficiary selection to ensure sufficient time for implementation. Risk 4: Security risks which impede access. Mitigating measures: Close coordination with UNDSS to obtain updated information. . Prioritise locations where security & access is not a challenge. Risk 5: Continued pandemic & related restrictive measures. Mitigating measures: Flexible project design which plans for possible delays due to lockdown, shortage of supply, etc. Adhere to guidelines shared by Shelter Sector. Advance procurement to ensure sufficient stock to ensure emergency preparedness. Awareness raising & PPE provision to Partners
