Project Summary

The situation in Somaliland IDPs remains extremely complex and continues to demand humanitarian assistance to vulnerable populations. Despite significant efforts by the Government and its partners, including the international community, to consolidate peace and stability, progress remains limited, in part due to the continued droughts and conflicts throughout the country. The impact of this chronical disruption of a secure and protective environment expose children in wide areas of the country to physical, psychological and emotional harm.
Girls and boys living in Somaliland continue to experience an array of rights violations manifested in various forms of violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect. Women and girls in Somaliland, like women and girls throughout the world, face the threat of rape. Sex crimes are present in both urban and rural areas. At least 3,819 serious violations of children’s rights have been documented in the recent years by the Monitoring and Reporting Mechanism (MRM) across Somalia (42 % increase from 2016). The rapes and gender violence remains of major concern. More than 1,400 cases have been reported in 2017, mostly in IDPs (52 %), asylum seekers (37%), returns (5% each). In addition, many children are killed or wounded and thousands of children are separated from their family or survivors of sexual violence.
Children who are Victims of abuse often do not receive adequate assistance due lack of effective and less equipped safeguarding focal points and because of those reasons the focal points can not perform effectively or provide quality and sufficient assistance. Without adequate equipment and the necessary support and funding to carry out the required inquiry into questioning child victims appropriately in order to engage in a systematic evidence collection in order considerable number of child abuse cases.
Thus, privission of equipment and additional funding are required to strengthen child protection and capacities of local child safeguarding focal points to prevent and respond to violence/abuse against boys and girls, to provide adequate temporary care to children victims of violence but also, and mostly, to ensure the sustainable reintegration of thousands of children in their families and communities. The aim is to support and prevent the vulnerable children and sustainable reintegration of an increased number of girls and boys affected by conflicts and droughts in Somaliland.
Reintegration strategies for children consider access to non-formal education including life skills and discussion of gender-related issues, health services, and financial literacy courses.

Project Activities

1. Registration for the children with violence (such as torture, sexual violence, abuse, neglect, exploitation and abduction) 2. Referring victims to medical and legal support services centres and give appropriated support services.3. Contributing to strengthening and promoting human rights and safeguarding of children in the focal points and other state authorities responsible for protecting children by working with partners to collaborate on executing and providing better protection for all children and those in IDP camps. 4. Monitoring how the whole protection mechanism in place are being applied and advocating for better child protection and safeguarding through workshops of community committee and advocators. 5. Conduct family tracing, mediation and reunification.6. Support children with vocational skill training and income generating activities in reintegration centres.

Mitigation Measures

According the project, SOMRAR Organization will ensure all staff operating in a childsafeguarding have capacity building and trained inthe Humanitarian Principles and Do No Harm and abide by the Code of Conductincluding PSEA, conduct safety audits of sites in collaboration with the other partnersand coordinate with GBV partners to ensure that GBV referral pathways are operationaland known to community members in sites, ensure diverse and inclusive governancestructures are present in sites and that responsive community feedback mechanismsare in place and operational. We will also ensure the participation of women throughactivities specically geared towards reducing barriers to women participation andpersons with disabilities. SOMRAR will support the establishment of inclusivecommunity governance structures where there are no governance structures and willwork with Child Protection partners to ensure that the children have their rights and there will no violence remained withrelevant advocacy with government authorities are carried out. The community leadersand respective committees will be fully involved in project design, implementation,monitoring and representation and that the community committees are inclusive andthat segments of the communities are well represented at all levels such as age andgender diversity.
