Project Summary

SAMS Nutrition program aims to continue its focus on strengthening lifesaving preventive and curative nutrition services for vulnerable population groups (Children U5 and PLWs), which include providing appropriate infant and young child feeding practices in emergencies (IYCF) and community management of acute malnutrition (CMAM). SAMS will work on the integration of nutrition services into SAMS-supported health facilities, through implementing a holistic and multi-disciplinary program. In order to achieve this objective, SAMS will seek through its funding to strengthen CMAM/IYCF services and improve its quality, through:
- Hiring qualified (Nutritionists and CHWs) at SAMS-supported health facilities, and build their capacities.
- Provision of outpatient treatment (OTP) at SAMS-supported health facilities.
- Provision of Inpatient treatment (SC) in one of the SAMS-supported health facilities (Idleb National Hospital).
- Provision of targeted Supplementary feeding program (TSFP).
- Provision of community outreach services through the CHWs.
- Provide emergency IYCF counseling for mothers by trained nutritionists.
- Disseminate messages on IYCF-E, by nutritionists and CHWs.
To complement its health intervention, SAMS will support nutrition services (CMAM and IYCF practices) in its facilities: For the IYCF component, SAMS will contextually appropriate social and behavior change communication to prevent and manage malnutrition of infants, while via CMAM practices SAMS will strengthen the capacity of the health staff to integrate these nutrition services in the facilities. These include screening and treatment of acute malnutrition, multiple, micronutrient supplementation,
the support will be through capacity building of health staff and provision of nutrition supplies for facilities in ldleb and Aleppo governorate where the targeted facilities will also be part of the nutrition surveillance system.
For more than three years, SAMS have been applying CMAM and IYCF-E in its supported facilities. In coordination with the Nutrition Cluster and nutrition actors, SAMS conducted training on both CMAM and IYCF-E for medical staff in the HFs (Paediatricians, midwives, and nurses) and CHWs. Within this program, the outpatient departments of the supported HFs and CHWs will provide the nutrition activities which include CMAM and IYCF components such as MUAC screening to children under 5 and PLW in all health facilities, reach the families with IEC materials, provide preventive and curative nutritional supplies to all health facilities, IYCF counseling, and group sessions. It is important to mention that the CHWs will be working and delivering their tasks separate from RRTs.
Where possible, a dedicated space will be made available in the supported HFs for promoting IYCF practices, especially lactating exercises. Private space to conduct IYCF activities, particularly for teaching mothers how to breastfeed or manage re-lactating issues, is provided in some health facilities depending on the availability of space. In smaller health facilities that do not have space available, IYCF activities are conducted in reproductive health clinics or labor rooms.
SAMS will continue to coordinate with other health actors through the Nutrition Cluster, Health Cluster, and the NGO Forum where routine data is gathered and shared through the 4Ws and HeRAMS, and the DHIS2 system. In addition, SAMS is also coordinating with other humanitarian actors on the field level to assess the needs, strengthen referral pathways, and respond to emergencies, among other ways. Moreover, SAMS will closely coordinate to share information about needs, ensure an effective and coordinated response to new emergencies, harmonize the salary scales and staffing structures across HFs.

4 - Needs

According to the 2021 Needs and Response Summary, urgent and life-saving nutrition needs for 4.9 M mothers and children in Syria are anticipated in 2021. Chronic malnutrition is anticipated to increase, with nearly 0.5 M children losing their learning and developmental potentials every year in Syria. The prevalence of maternal anemia and maternal malnutrition is expected to continue rising due to deteriorating socioeconomic conditions. In 2021, an estimated 1.2 M mothers and women of child-bearing age will continue to suffer the consequences of maternal anemia and/or malnutrition if not treated appropriately.It is expected that acute malnutrition among children 6-59 months in hard-to-reach areas and IDP camps will continue to increase, hazarding the survival and wellbeing of nearly 90,000 children in Syria. Without appropriate care, as high as 50 % of these children might lose their lives because of acute malnutrition-related complications. Poor dietary diversity among children and mothers in Syria will impact their capability to the optimal breastfeeding. Hence, the already low levels of poor infant feeding and caring practices will continue. Poor infant feeding and caring practices will deteriorate acute and chronic malnutrition among children 6-59 months in Syria. A similar effect will be potentiated by maternal anemia and malnutrition. As the malnutrition among children in Syria lasted for about ten years, and it is expected to continue for a long time, the stunting between children increased dramatically. 34% of all children, or more than one in three under the age of 5, suffer from stunting in NWS, and child stunting increased by 5% in 2020. Addressing this problem needs to continue implementing the CMAM program

5 - Implementation of activities

SAMS will work on the integration of nutrition services into 12 SAMS-supported health facilities: Alshifaa Hospital, Idleb National Hospital, Qah Maternity Hospital, Alebzimo PHC, Sarmin PHC, Kafr Driyan PHC, Kafr Losin PHC, Hazzano PHC, Freriyeh PHC, Sejjo hospital, Alikhlas Hospital and finally, Batbu PHC. The provided nutrition activities include:• Hiring qualified (Nutritionists and CHWs) at SAMS supported health facilities, and build their capacities.• Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) screening for Children Under 5 years old (CU5) and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLWs).• Provision of outpatient treatment (OTP) for the malnutrition cases at SAMS supported health facilities • Cases management and follow up the cases• Provision of Inpatient treatment in Idlib National hospital• Provision of targeted Supplementary feeding program (TSFP)• Provision of community outreach services through the CHWs which include MUAC screening for CU5 and PLWs, referral of suspected cases, IYCF activities (raising awareness sessions, providing IYCF messages), and follow up the referred cases.• Provide emergency IYCF counseling for mothers by trained nutritionists• Disseminate messages on IYCF-E, by nutritionists and CHWs MEAL Plan: Beside to SAMS CRM channels, the MEAL team will be responsible for ensuring the quality of Nutrition services provided to beneficiaries by conducting: 1) patient satisfaction surveys (exit interviews, SAMS incorporated the protection mainstreaming indicators within this tool), 2) spot-checks according to EHSP standards, 3) key informant interviews (KIIs) with local stakeholders including health facility staff, 4) KAP baseline/end line.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: COVID19 spread among nutrition workers and beneficiaries. Mitigation: SAMS to maintain social, mental, and physical well-being including training staff on infection prevention and control of COVID19 and other infections as well as distribution of PPEs. Risk: Gender inequality and GBV - Mitigation: Ensure IYCF programs have private spaces for breastfeeding mothers and help decrease the risk of harassment or violence against females. Train IYCF counselors on how to supportively engage with GBV survivors and provide information in an ethical, safe, and confidential manner about their rights and options to report risk and access care, and ensure referral mechanisms to appropriate support. Provide a confidential environment for at-risk groups seeking information about where to report risk or access care for exposure to GBV. Provide nutritional support to survivors. Ensure locations, times, and methods of nutrition services are safe and accessible for women and other at-risk groups. This includes distance to be traveled, times convenient and safe, treatment is offered at the same time as children, avoid long waiting periods, nutrition sites are designed to ensure accessibility for all persons, including PWD. Risk: Children are not sufficiently protected. Mitigation: Have Child Safeguarding policies and systems in place and these should be regularly monitored and corrective action taken when needed. Risk: PSEA - Mitigation: Policies should be in place, practiced, and monitored. Polices should cover standards of behavior for the organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and include accountability measures.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Interruption of UNICEF Nutritional Supplies. Mitigation: Identify other partners to secure the supplies or as a last resort, SAMS to procure these items by itself. Risk: Control by unaccepted groups or GoS. Mitigation: SAMS will be re-running all anti-terrorism/background checks on all staff, volunteers, partners, Hawala, and other vendors associated with the current award; Make the civil and local authorities (HDs) participate in the process of service provision and determining its type; Activate immediate reporting concerning any intervention incidents; Ensure that the staff in the center is not involved with any military faction or political party; Conduct training workshops for the staff on how to deal with cases of Intervention with humanitarian action. Risk: Attack by GoS and others. Mitigation: Selecting fortified locations; Security assessment (site & building); Activation of contingency plans for each center in cases of near bombing or close clashes or intrusion or siege or fire; Evacuation SOP; Sourcing alternative locations, in case of need for relocation. Risk: Theft. Mitigation: Safety and security upgrades for SAMS facilities, including CCTV cameras and guards; Photo verification of received shipments, signed waybills, and notification if any missing items noted in HF count within 72 hours; Review of stock/inventory records during monitoring visits; Hostile Environment and Safety Management Training; Access and movement SOPs; Local networks to monitor security situation and trends; Communication plan and local acceptance; Quarterly physical inventory of warehouses; review of monthly consumption reports.
