Project Summary

By enhancing the access to NFIs and shelters for vulnerable people, TGH will improve living conditions of new returnees, IDPs and conflict/natural disaster affected population in West and Central Darfur. The distribution of Improved Emergency Shelters (IES), sleeping mats and blanket by TGH will ensure beneficiaries are protected from wind, cold and rain and thus reduce the beneficiaries’ health hazards. Jerry-cans and soaps will have a positive impact on the prevalence of water borne diseases, particularly acute during the rainy season. Cooking sets will help the beneficiaries sustaining basic living standards and to cook in hygienic clean containers to improve food safety. With the introduction of “dignity items” specific to women/girls hygiene and security needs TGH will provide a specific answer to women’s needs. Persons with special needs will also receive items adapted to their specific disabilities i.e. crutches, canes, walkers and food millers.
The organization of NFI fairs through vouchers modality enables to respond to dire NFI needs while giving the choice to the beneficiaries to procure what they really need and therefore to restore their dignity and support more efficiently people in need.
Fire prevention activity will be implemented along the shelters distribution. Fire prevention tool will be distributed to a fire warden unit and sensitization sessions will be conducted among the communities.
This project will target 5.000 beneficiaries through NFI fairs; 400 individuals with special needs, 2.000 beneficiaries with IES distribution and 4.000 beneficiaries will be covered by the buffer stock modality. This project targets West Darfur state, i.e. Geneina, Kreinik and Beida localities and Central Darfur state, i.e. Um Dukhun, Bindizi and Jebel Marra localities.

1 - Needs

Sudan remains a large protracted humanitarian crisis and the severity and scale of needs remain far reaching. Humanitarian needs in Sudan are driven by several factors including localized armed clashes and inter communal violence, displacement, climatic shocks and hazards, disease outbreaks, food security, malnutrition and protection risks. Based on information available this year and considering trends of the past five years, the evolution of humanitarian needs and its impact on people in 2020, will likely remain similar (HNO 2020). New population movements (IDPs and/or returnees) or natural disasters are to be expected and a response in terms of IES and NFIs would be required to cover urgent needs and to mitigate protection risks among populations in need. Throughout the past year, TGH implemented several interventions to cover NFI and IES needs faced by displaced populations, flood-affected or fire-affected populations. The presence of TGH in its areas of interventions, i.e. West and Central Darfur, enabled the organizations to conduct multiple assessments that highlight the needs of NFI kits and shelters, especially in Kreinik and Geneina localities (West Darfur) where many households suffered from 2019 rainy season and in Um Dukhun locality where many population displacements are occurring including arrival of refugees from CAR. Besides the current needs in terms of NFI and shelters, a new wave of returnees from Chad is expected for the coming year. Indeed, return dynamic is expected to continue and to potentially increase in Central and West Darfur while new population displacements are expected considering the formal restart of the tripartite agreement between Chad, Sudan and UNHCR for Sudanese refugees repatriation and also the persistence of a relatively stable security context. In parallel to this “normalization” process, localized and violent clashes are likely to occur particularly in Jebel Marra, causing massive population displacements. Thanks to its collaboration with the other humanitarian stakeholders, TGH is included in the IA missions that were conducted in both states of intervention. IA missions in West Darfur in July and August 2019, highlighted the strong needs in terms of shelters and NFIs caused by the floods. In November 2019, an IA assessment was organized in some villages of Golo locality, under control of non-governmental groups. The conclusions of the mission attested the dire needs regarding NFI kits and emergency shelter kits. Under all its interventions, TGH systematically identifies the most vulnerable people, following UNHCR criteria. When conflicts or naturals hazards occurred, inducing population displacements, most individuals leave all their belongings behind, including basic NFIs. They need therefore both shelters and NFIs. Previous TGH experiences have highlighted that the most important IES and NFI items are the following: 1 complete cooking set, 1 plastic sheet (tarpaulin), 1 jerrycan 20L, 2 blankets, 1 large mat (NFI), 1 plastic sheet (tarpaulin), 1 bamboo frame composed of 6 pieces of bamboo measuring 4*4 m (ESI). Previous experiences have enabled to precisely price such kits. However, the recent assessments highlighted specific needs for women and teenagers. Indeed, the current economic crisis and local conflict situation presents specific dangers for women. In order to mitigate these risks, TGH will continue to distribute “dignity kits” (solar flashlight, headscarf, hygiene items, etc.) for teenagers and women. The items aim at helping women and girls maintain their dignity and security during humanitarian crises. Moreover, TGH will distribute material for people with special needs, i.e. cans, crutches, food mills, as they are often neglected within their communities. The localities targeted under this project belong to the priority scope of the sector. Indeed, Beida falls under Priority 1, Um Dukhun under Priority 2 and Jebel Marra, Kreinik and Binizi under Priority 3.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

In the communities targeted by this project, most of the population is entirely dependent on agro-pastoral activities. Also, the deterioration of the environment (reduction of water, forest cover, grazing land) contributes to the fragility and degradation of the livelihoods of households in the area as well as to increased tensions between farmers and herders. The environmental issues identified are related to the reduction of the majority of forest cover and grazing lands near and moderately close to villages. Over the course of successive conflicts, these resources have been overused by the population due to a lack of alternatives (fuelwood, shelter construction, animal feed, urban construction). Also, the available grazing land for ruminants has been drastically reduced as a result of the separation between Sudan and South-Sudan, resulting in the loss of winter grazing land now owned by South-Sudan. This reduction has contributed to the accumulation of animals on small areas and thus to overgrazing. Finally, climate change and the increase in desert areas have contributed to a reduction in the presence of trees and shrubs. The design of the shelters that will be distributed within this intervention is adapted to the local environment. Indeed, the shelters are made of bamboo sheets, material that can be found locally and its use do not cause any harm to the environment. Shelter design, which respects the standards of the sector, is based on locally and extensively available materials (bamboo): it is therefore natural, renewable and does not require long transportation. The shelters intervention of TGH is a community-based approach, as the beneficiaries participate to the shelter erection with TGH support. While supporting the communities to build the shelters, TGH team explains why bamboo is used, and not wood for example, to protect the environment. TGH will follow the indications of the sector to ensure that all mitigation measures are taken. If needed, TGH would adapt its methodology to fit with the sector recommendations in terms of environment protection.Finally, the fire prevention component of the hereby presented project will reduce the fires’ propagation in the villages targeted and therefore reduce the damages on the environment. Indeed, every year fire hazards provoke important damages in Darfur regions, destroying trees and grazing land. The shelters are built respecting SPHERE standards, a fire unit with preventive tools (shovels, wheelbarrows, sand) will be installed and fire wardens will be trained to raise awareness among the population on fire damages.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Through the organization of fairs, instead of direct distribution, TGH ensures that the beneficiaries can select the items they really need, without deciding for them. This allows the beneficiaries to restore their dignity and enhance their resilience. Throughout the project, TGH will pay attention to protection mainstreaming regarding women. Indeed, for the second year, TGH will preposition dignity kits for girl adolescents and women, composed of hygiene items. Furthermore, the hereby presented project includes a component that addresses the needs of people with disabilities through specific material distribution that will respond to their individual needs. After a household verification, TGH will distribute crutches, can or food mills to people with walking, eating issues and blind people.Regarding shelter, the provision of IES respecting the standards of the sector will mitigate protection risks that families, that very often include women and children, are facing in this urgent and dire situation (lack of privacy, promiscuity, abuse, etc.).

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

TGH puts in place mechanisms to enable beneficiaries and affected populations to provide feedback and complaints. At the beginning of the project implementation, TGH will organize a meeting with leaders and other relevant stakeholders to present the activities and explain that community complaints and advices are necessary to the action’s improvement. TGH will present to the beneficiaries the different ways to communicate their issues (special complains during distribution, direct contact with TGH staff, visit to TGH office, etc.). When a complaint is addressed to TGH, it is acknowledged by the sector managers who provide the necessary information to the affected population to ensure their comprehension of TGH operations, to address answers to beneficiaries concerns and complaints prioritization criteria and operational or budget limitations. According to the relevance of the concerns addressed by the community to the Base Managers and Program team in the field, these can also be analyzed in further details and considered in activities planning. While explaining the complaint process, TGH team will mention GBV and SEA and highlight the importance to raise any concerns regarding these topics. PSEA policy, that is common for all TGH countries of intervention, is signed automatically by TGH employees and suppliers who work with TGH. Transparency is ensured through adequate information sharing and communication to beneficiaries and affected populations have already been mentioned above and include meetings and specific complaint mechanisms and responses with affected populations, Sheikhs or other community leaders and more importantly, availability of TGH staff. At the beginning of each project, TGH team conducts FGDs to explain the objectives of the project, the different steps that will be implemented, the selection criteria, etc. This prior communication with the population avoids to create misunderstanding during the activities’ implementation and to create tensions within the communities. In addition, TGH systematically conducts Post Distribution Monitoring for NFI and shelter activities. The PDM is conducted through Kobo tablets. Coordination team in Khartoum, in collaboration with field-based staff, designed the questionnaire. The data are collected on the field and analyzed at Khartoum level. This survey allows gathering beneficiaries’ feedback about the quality, the relevance and the timely delivery of the support they have received and to take into account recommendations to improve future interventions.
