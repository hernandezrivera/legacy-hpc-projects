Needs

An in-depth analysis of the currently available secondary data for the Rohingya refugee population as well as observations in the camps and host communities indicates• A continuing very high degree of overall economic vulnerability within the Rohingya refugee population resulting in equally high levels of dependence on humanitarian aid to cover even the most basic needs; • A strong correlation between access to income-generating activities and improvements in food security (78% of refugees who only rely on external assistance were classified as vulnerable to food insecurity against 72% of those earning some form of income according to a WFP VAM survey); and WFP-VAM data show that nearly 12% of the newly arrived Rohingya resorted to unsustainable livelihood coping strategies (such as selling a portion of the assistance received, borrowing money and buying complementary food, including vegetables or condiments, on credit) in order to meet basic needs including health-related costs. Key informants met stressed that the lack of self-reliance opportunities has been exposing refugees, especially women and adolescent girls, to protection risks and potentially harmful coping mechanisms such as trafficking, exploitation, survival sex, child marriage, and participation in illegal drug tradeThe economic vulnerability, although with a different magnitude compared to the situation of the Rohingya refugees, has also been affecting the host communities. Participants of host community FGD and individual HH interviews conducted by the HELVETAS consultant generally estimated the drop in income from casual labour to be around 50%. Higher financial resources of Bangladeshi living in host communities ensure relatively higher access to food and mitigates food insecurity in the absence of direct food assistance. However, financial resources are not sufficient to meet all food and non-food related needs for the vast majority of Bangladeshi living in areas with a high presence of refugees. WFP classified on average 10% of the host community population as highly vulnerable to food insecurity while 21 to 30% were seen as being vulnerable. Finally, the host community households headed by women within host communities are more vulnerable to food insecurity (45 percent are vulnerable or highly vulnerable) than those headed by men (35 percent)

Activities or outputs

The proposed HELVETAS response seeks to address the above listed priority needs through four inter-linked project components. Project Components A, B and C focus on the needs of Rohingya refugees in the camps, while project Component D is being put in place to sustainably improve agriculture-based livelihoods and productivity in affected host communities. Project Component A will boost the refugee households’ capacity to produce a certain amount of own vegetables inside the camps. Project Component B will provide selected refugee households with additional temporary income opportunities that will allow them to subsequently buy essential items without having to resort to corrosive and potentially damaging coping strategies. Project Component C will allow a limited number of refugees with specific vulnerabilities (including inability to participate in cash-for-work programming) to improve their dietary diversify thanks to complementary fresh food vouchers. Fresh vegetables distributed through Component C will be produced and collected as part of Component D. A porter service (operated by paid volunteers under Component B) will be put in place to help any beneficiaries that are unable to themselves carry the assistance from the distribution point to their shelters. Project Component D will create lasting benefits and sustainably improve the income of host community farming households through productivity gains, production increases, asset improvements and market system development
