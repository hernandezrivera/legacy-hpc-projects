Project Summary

Nile Hope (NH) is a national non-governmental Organization founded in 2004. It operates in South Sudan in the states of; Central Equatoria, Upper Nile, Unity Jonglei, Western Bahr el Ghazal and Abyei Administrative. The organization has its headquarters in Akobo County, Jonglei State and as well as satellite coordination offices in Juba and Nairobi. Nile Hope is an important player in the Education Sector, SS Education Cluster, strategic advisory group (SAG), NEC, Inter-Agency for Education in Emergencies (INEE) Network and NEAR Global network and continues to participate in critical forums and policy gatherings to generate such tools as the South Sudan Minimum Standards for Education in Emergencies (INEE). Education is a basic human right and as such, all children, regardless of gender or disability are entitled to access it. Education empowers people to live healthier and sustainable lives. It is also crucial for fostering tolerance between people and contributes to more peaceful societies, which is very relevant to the South Sudanese context where target communities have been exposed to conflict and protracted crises, widespread flooding, deepening food insecurity, inflation, high food prices, and lack of access to basic services. The 2023 South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) focuses on the humanitarian needs of the internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees and vulnerable host communities/non-displaced people in South Sudan. The project is aimed to improve equitable access to quality, Safe and protective education for 5818 dis-aggregated in (3,491 B, 2,327 G) children and adolescents (3-17 years) including children and youth with Disabilities of the host (2980 M, 1907 F), IDPs (300 M, 210 F) and (211 M, 210 F ) Returnees and Communities in Akobo, Nyirol, Nasir and Pibor Counties of Jonglei and Upper Nile States. The intervention will mainly focus on ECD, Primary and Alternative Education Program and secondary school levels of learning in all targeted locations. The project is mainly designed to create access of learners to schools and inclusive learning centers while ensuring that barriers to education are addressed. This project will improve the quality of education by building the capacity of 250 (205 M, 45 F) teachers on pedagogical skills and child-centers methodologies and including teachers' code of conduct to improve teaching and learning process for emergency-affected children. Temporary learning spaces established/rehabilitated in line with INEE's Minimum Standards for Education and Standards in Education Cluster Strategy, gender sensitive WASH facilities built or rehabilitated in line with MoGEI/Education Cluster standard. Learning and recreational materials distributed to children and youth (6-17 years, and ; ECD kits distributed to children (3-5 years), girls in education receiving dignity kits, Train 300 (160 F, 140 M) SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management, governance, psycho social support (PSS) and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR)/ climate change; this will ensure proper management and maintenance of school infrastructure in their respective schools. Sustainably engage the communities through social mobilizations and awareness raising on the importance of education with emphasis on education of girls and children with different disabilities including pastoral, IDPs and Returnees Communities whose vulnerabilities have been aggravated by the conflict, flooding and economic depreciation. The project equally hopes to enhance psycho social development of learners besides improving hygiene and sanitation. The project will use its existing M&E system to monitor, provide reports including 5 WS response, Establish help-desk, complaint mechanism to address Sexual Exploitation&Abuse and to tracks perception of the local population about the project and give feedback

1. Needs

Education is a basic human right and as such, all children, regardless of gender or disability are entitled to access it. Education empowers people to live healthier and sustainable lives. It is also crucial for fostering tolerance between people and contributes to more peaceful societies, which is very relevant to the South Sudanese context where target communities have been exposed to conflict and protracted crises, widespread flooding, deepening food insecurity, inflation, high food prices, and lack of access to basic services. The 2023 South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) focuses on the humanitarian needs of the internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees and vulnerable host communities/non-displaced people in South Sudan. In response to the above problems, Akobo, Nyirol, Nasir and Pibor all falls under catastrophic category based on the data collected between Jun-August 2023 through ISNA and FSNMS. Akobo and Nasir counties are situated at the border with Ethiopia. Ethiopia refugees in the recent past experienced food crises and internal conflict among the inhabitant of Gambella region that target some refugees based on their ethnicity, the situation became dire leading to huge influx of the refugees to Akobo and Nasir. School functionality has also been severely impacted by flooding in 2021 and was mentioned as impediment for learner's access to school in the qualitative component of the FSNMS+ 2021. Jonglei, Unity and Upper Nile have been the worst impacted by the floods, and officials in these states reported in the ENA that over 190 schools closed due to flooding. Prior to COVID-19 school closures, about 2.2 million children were already out of school. The school closures further impacted the education of over 2.7 million additional children. In May 2021, after 14 months of closure due to COVID-19, schools in South Sudan re-opened. The current estimated number of out of school children is 2.8 million. Through this project, Nile Hope will deliver a holistic program that will improve equitable access to protective, quality education and create a conducive and safe learning environment for both boys and girls and children with disabilities (CWD) age 3 to 17 years). Sustainably engage the communities through social mobilizations and awareness raising on the importance of education with emphasis on education of girls and children with disabilities.
