Project Summary

Under a protracted psychological distress within a context characterized by deteriorated living conditions resulted from prolonged occupation, harsh escalations, COVID-19 pandemic and intra division that caused protection and health crises, the GCMHP project aims at contributing to prevent and mitigate these crises through delivering quality specialized mental health services to the vulnerable people as per the following interventions:
1) Provision of specialized mental health services for 1000 children (girls and boys) and adults/parents (women and men) who have displayed symptoms of psychosocial distress. The clinical services will be provided by specialized multidisciplinary teams at GCMHP three community centers located in Gaza city, Deir El Balah and Khan Younis.
2) Provision of Mental Health and Psychosocial Services (MHPSS) for 1200 adults/parents and children affected by conflict related violence and COVID-19 pandemic in 5 mobile clinics. 5 Psychologists will provide psychotherapy and counseling sessions to affected persons at 5 mobile clinics that will be hosted at CBOs distributed among the 5 governorates to facilitate reaching the cases in their communities, with less stigma, and free of charge.
3) Provision of free telephone counseling service for 1200 children (girls and boys) and adults/parents (women and men) who couldn’t reach mental health centers due to economic, social or emergency settings' barriers. Through this service, the telephone counselors will provide psychoeducation, individual and family counseling, and also detect and refer children and adults with severe symptoms to GCMHP community centers to receive specialized mental health services including medications if necessary.
4) Building the capacities of 50 protection (GBV, CP, MHPSS) staff through training and supporting them in self-care and stress management.
5) Building the capacities of 50 healthcare providers and community workers through training them on MHPSS, including mhGAP.
6) Building the capacities of 40 staff (project staff and GCMHP staff) on the understanding and application of the PSEA. The training will use adopted material in consultation with experts. Two training groups will receive 15 training hours for three days on PSEA.
7) Conducting 5 mental health awareness / de-stigmatization online, live interactive discussions via GCMHP professionals. It is expected that 500 audience will effectively participate.
8) Produce one fact sheet, including gender and age disaggregated data, to document trends/patterns, findings and observations among those assisted to be shared with all stakeholders to help improve the prevention and protection response and for joint coordinated advocacy messages and actions.
In addition, GCMHP will share the project updates on OCHA's 4Ws and 5Ws online system under the protection and health clusters including area, gender and age disaggregated information.

1 - Needs

GCMHP is considered the only NGO that provides specialized mental health services to the population of the Gaza strip. It cooperates with the most of local service providers in the Gaza Strip in many aspects regarding joint needs assessment, referral, training, supervision, awareness, advocacy, therapy, and rehabilitation. For the period from 1/1/2021 to 30/9/2021, GCMHP noticed a significant increase in the community needs to the community MH services which included psychoeducation, consultations, counseling, psychotherapy, rehabilitation, psychological first aid (PFA), capacity building, supportive supervision, stress management training and caring for caregivers. This increase came in light of the current complicated situation resulting from the occupation, blockade, intra division, COVID-19 pandemic, and all offensives on the Gaza Strip including the last May offensive. These crises have increased the complexity and harm of the general humanitarian situation among the population. In such conditions, GCMHP in cooperation with other service providers provided its community mental health services to the affected population. For the period from 1/1/2021 to 15/10/2021, GCMHP coordinated with MoH and MOSD and received lists of cases affected by the pandemic and May offensive. GCMHP mobile teams provided PFA services to 16,289 cases (4814 men, 4411 women, 3692 boys, 3372 girls), 195 among them were PwDs, and referred 1,049 cases (329 men, 510 women, 119 boys, 91 girls) to the specialized MH services provided at the GCMHP three community centers. It is worth mentioning that these ministries are still referring lists of affected people to GCMHP on weekly basis (around 200 families a week). Moreover, GCMHP received 622 cases (57 men, 154 women, 247 boys, 164 girls), 20 among them were PwDs at its five mobile clinics distributed on the five Gaza strip governorates. On the other side, and within the same period, different service providers including NGOs, CBOs, and ministries referred 767 cases (92 men, 143 women, 335 boys, 197 girls) to the GCMHP three community centres. These cases were geographically distributed as follows (47% Gaza and North areas, 20% middle area, 33% Khanyonus and Rafah areas). The year 2021 witnessed also a noticeable increase in the numbers of callers who were seeking psychological counseling. The free telephone counseling service benefited 1,951 cases (703 women, 729 men, 212 girls and 307 boys). The telephone counselors referred also 379 cases to the GCMHP community centers. Regarding the clinical work, 3,177 cases received psychotherapy at GCMHP community centers in the year 2020, while within only three quarters of the year 2021 the clinical services reached 3,339 cases ((1,236 Men (450 from the 19-30 years, 695 from 31 to 59 years, 91 from 60 years and above), 1,140 Women (356 from the 19-30 years, 676 from 31 to 59 years, 108 from 60 years and above), 609 Boys (383 from 0-10 years, 226 from 11 to 18), 354 Girls (203 from 0-10 years, 151 from 11 to 18)). The last clinical assessment and analysis conducted by GCMHP on 30/9/2021 showed that differences between men and women in the symptoms. as 25% of men were diagnosed with depressive disorders, 20% with anxiety disorders, and 10% with obsessive-compulsive disorder, and psychosis with 19%. While 46% of women were diagnosed with depressive disorders, and 10% with other anxiety disorders and 10% with obsessive-compulsive disorder, psychosis with 23%, and the remaining rates distributed on other disorders. Regarding boys, 50% of them were diagnosed with anxiety disorders, and 5% with Depressive Disorders, and 8% OCD, and 12% with conduct disorders, and psychosis with 3%. While 55 % of the girls were diagnosed with anxiety disorders, and 8% with Depressive Disorders, and 5% OCD, and 3% with conduct disorders, and psychosis with 5%., and the remaining rates distributed on other disorders. GCMHP applied a DASS-21 survey with 2,304 COVID-19 affected persons who benefited from PFA services. This survey was applied with 1,355 men and 949 women in order to measure the emotional states of depression, anxiety, and stress among COVID-19 affected persons. The data analysis conducted on 31 August 2021 showed that 15.5% of women had depression compared to 13.6% men; 13% of women had anxiety compared to 11% men; 27% of women had stress compared to 26.7% men. On the other hand, the analysis of severity showed also that 6.1% of women had severe and very severe depression compared to 4.4% men, and 11.2% of women had severe and very severe anxiety compared to 7.4% men.Unprecedented daily requests from many service providers (especially those who are working in the front line) are still submitted to GCMHP to receive training, supervision, and caring. In the first half of the year 2021, the GCMHP’s training courses targeted 739 professionals and carers (including 417 men, 322 women) who are providing services to different vulnerable groups. Also, GCMHP provided training on strategies of Helping the Helpers for 205 doctors, nurses, and ambulance staff from MoH (120 men, 85 women) in coordination with Médecins du Monde Suisse-Switzerland (MDM-Swiss). Also, 320 Psychological First Aid workers who come from several organizations were given booster training on how to provide PFA during the 1st week after the ceasefire. The training was carried out in cooperation with the Ministry of Social Development which is the main body currently responsible for coordinating the fieldwork related to the recent offensive. In addition, GCMHP targeted 160 journalists working in the Gaza Strip. Information mentioned above are in line with all facts, assessments, and reports issued by the UN agencies and international organizations within the current year, which confirm the increasing need to community mental health services

2 - Activities

To achieve the project objectives, GCMHP will implement the following activities: 1) Provision of specialized mental health services for 1000 people including 400 children (164 girls and 236 boys) and 600 adults/parents (270 women and 330 men), 5% PwDs who have displayed symptoms of psychosocial distress. The clinical services will be provided by specialized multidisciplinary teams at GCMHP three community centers located in Gaza city, Deir El Balah and Khan Younis. GCMHP used clinical procedures manual as a main guideline for providing the specialized mental health services. It is worth mentioning that GCMHP community centers and facilities are equipped and suitable for the inclusion of all age and gender categories and also people with disabilities and facilitates the accessibility to its services through different tools such as the Free hotlines, platforms, transportation and home visits. The trained therapists will provide an orientation to the patients about PSEA and relevant complaints mechanism. 2) Provision of Mental Health and Psychosocial Services (MHPSS) for 1200 persons including 720 adults/parents (324 women and 396 men) and 480 children (196 girls and 284 boys), 5% PwDs affected by conflict related violence and COVID-19 pandemic. 5 Psychologists will provide psychotherapy and counseling sessions to affected persons at 5 mobile clinics that will be hosted at community-based organizations CBOs covering all Gaza Strip governorates to facilitate reaching the cases in their communities, with less stigma, and free of charge. The psychologists at mobile clinics will provide psychotherapy and counseling sessions to cases with mild and moderate symptoms who do not need medications. Severe cases and other cases in need for medications will be referred to GCMHP community centers. The trained psychologists will provide an orientation to the patients about PSEA and relevant complaints mechanism. 3) Provision of free telephone counseling service for 1200 persons including 303 children ( 123 girls and 180 boys) and 897 adults/parents (422 women and 475 men) who couldn’t reach mental health centers due to economic, social or emergency settings' barriers. Through this service, the telephone counselors will provide psychoeducation, individual and family counseling, and also detect and refer children and adults with severe symptoms to GCMHP community centers. This service provides another differentiated aspect, where all GBV and protection cases will be detected and referred to women protection organizations and other service providers in Gaza Strip. The trained counsellors will provide an orientation to the patients about PSEA and relevant complaints mechanism. 4) Building the capacities of 50 protection (GBV, CP, MHPSS) staff (25 men and 25 women) through training and supporting them in self-care and stress management. GCMHP is using international manuals for self-care for women and men. This will be provided through two days training course, for each of the five groups. The trainers will provide also an orientation to the trainees about PSEA and relevant complaints mechanism within the training days 5) Building the capacities of 50 healthcare providers and community workers (25 men and 25 women) through providing them with three training days on MHPSS, including mhGAP, that would contribute to integrating mental health into health facilities. Each training for the three constructing groups will be five hours per day. GCMHP is using the IASC Guidelines on Mental Health and Psychosocial Support in Emergency Settings as a training guideline, in addition to using the Mental Health GAP intervention Guide v2. The trainers will provide an orientation to the trainees about PSEA and relevant complaints mechanism within the training days. 6) Provide 40 project staff (20 men, 20 women) with 2 training courses on PSEA. The training will use adopted material in consultation with experts. Two training groups will receive 15 training hours for three days on PSEA.7) Conducting 5 mental health awareness/de-stigmatization online, live interactive discussions via GCMHP professionals using social media platforms. The GCMHP professionals will answer the audience questions and interact to their concerns lively. PSEA component will be addressed during the online dissuasions. 8) Produce one fact sheet, including gender and age disaggregated data, to document trends/patterns, findings and observations among those assisted to be shared with all stakeholders to help improve the prevention and protection response and for joint coordinated advocacy messages and actions. GCMHP Users: the users of GCMHP community centers are adults/parents and children who are direct or indirect victims of human rights violations, e.g. any individual has direct/indirect personal experience in the context of violence and COVID-19. The access of beneficiaries to the community centers is ensured through addressing any economic, physical or social limitations (eg. providing transportation for cases, conducting home visits and availability of free telephone counseling (hotline). Complaints Mechanism: GCMHP is always in place to respond to and address complaints through the following :1) Complaints boxes at community centers and Head Quarter of GCMHP. All these boxes are easily accessed by beneficiaries. 2) A specific part at its account page on official website and Facebook to receive complaints and inquires, these tools are available for all audience). 3)An employee is allocated and responsible for following up these complaints if founded and sent them to the High management. 4) All issues and publications of GCMHP such as brochures, billboards, radio spots, etc) 5) PSEA complaints mechanism is integrated in the general GCMHP complaints policy and its relevant tools, forms and channels.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

GCMHP has been committed to being accountable to the people benefited from its intervention. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

First: GCMHP will implement the project activities at its community centers with all arrangements and safe measures for the clients and staff are guaranteed, with full adherence to precautionary safety measures, including physical distancing and hygiene promotion, as defined by health authorities. In addition, all the staff are regularly monitored about upholding hygiene and prevention measures via a designated staff member. Besides, GCMHP has established clear policies and procedures for the status of emergency during the COVID-19. In addition, the GCMHP Therapeutic services provided to clients at the community centers were continued with taking mitigation measures which include reducing the exposure through increasing the duration between the follow-up sessions for clients (from 3-5 weeks instead of 1-2 weeks), in addition to conduct home visits and deliver medications needed to clients. GCMHP also set a protocol for COVID-19 preventive measures and impose strong protection measures in the GCMHP facilities including reception offices, therapeutic rooms, etc. It also distribute sterilizers, thermometers in all GCMHP community centers. The sterilizers will be used by clients and staff according to the instructions. GCMHP also has three PFA satellite teams including a male and female in each team and these teams are providing the PFA services and distributing sanitization and educational kits among people in the most marginalized places and who probably can’t access the remote services. Second: GCMHP made adaptations such as setting ramps and railings to its facilities so that all individuals and groups can access and use the facilities in safety and with dignity. Third: all GCMHP facilities, policies, clinical procedures, systems, forms, templates and instructions ensure that condentiality and privacy of patients and respected all forms of consultation, counseling and personal information sharing. On the other hands, the therapy rooms are well separated from public spaces or the waiting area. GCMHP facilities and services respect the gender needs and concerns especially the women and girls’ needs, (GCMHP services can be accessed by women and girls, as more than 50% of GCMHP beneficiaries are women and girls). GCMHP was and still is requesting consent to collect and use data and makes sure the beneficiary is clearly informed of all services and has the capacity to give informed consent. GCMHP has also a well-designed and protected MIS system for patients that secure data storage. Fifth: GCMHP has many policies such as a gender mainstreaming policy, child safety policy and security policy that make sure that there is a variety of staff (male, female) who are well trained on Gender policy. Sixth: GCMHP was and still distributing PFA toolkits/gifts to the children and medicine in the times of lockdown at home away from the public eye. Seventh: The GCMHP services including telephone counseling ensure that services are provided at an adequate time when individuals can realistically access them. Eighth: GCMHP has M&E system that considers data disaggregated by age, gender, and location or specific group (e.g. persons with disabilities). Ninth: to ensure and strengthen the PSEA policy, GCMHP updated the recruitment forms and contracts templates and included standard clause on PSEA in accordance with IASC principles and guidance. These templates will be used also in all projects implemented by GCMHP.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

GCMHP is always working to strengthen its accessibility to the vulnerable groups considering the crises and emergency context. GCMHP adapts and equipped its facilities whether the community centres or the mobile clinics with hygiene and safety measures, and considered the barriers that may prevent affected communities, including the COVID-19 crisis affected groups, from accessing your services, so for example, GCMHP tried to overcome the movement restrictions via reaching the communities via online facilities such Zoom and Skype, WhatsApp, Social media outlets and SMSs. In addition, GCMHP established mobile clinics in the most marginalized communities and also mobilize PFA teams to conduct home visits. Also, GCMHP is coordinating with MoH and MOSD to deliver the mental health services to all affected beneficiaries whom can benefit from the services either remotely or via home visits. Also, GCMHP disseminated its psychiatrists and psychologists work specific phone numbers to reach the service anytime, anywhere. Moreover, the GCMHP staff had trained on delivering the needed services remotely in addition to delivering that training to many service providers and staff to be able to delivering quality remote services. Different awareness materials through different outlets and encouraging referral for those in need of mental health support had always considered. A special emphasis is placed on reaching out to the most vulnerable including persons living with a disability or suffering from high levels of trauma. A flexible response approach ensuring marginalized groups, areas impacted by new eruptions of violence, or emerging and urgent needs can be responded to. The GCMHP strategy has also a value regarding the independence and non-political affiliation, as GCMHP is a non- political, independent organization. It is not influenced by political parties or ideologies. Mental health care is provided solely on the basis of need, without discrimination or preference. GCMHP is a key member in many clusters such as PC, HP, MHPSS and CP WGs, and PNGO’s rehabilitation cluster. GCMHP is always consults with the Protection Cluster including the GBV and Child Protection sub-clusters, about power dynamics in the area of intervention and use this information to inform monitoring activities and identify any barriers to access or discrimination against particular groups. GCMHP coordinate also with specialized agencies to identify individuals and include them in the program interventions. On the other hands, GCMHP has an existing referral system to receive referrals from other organizations. This system is generalized among all PC members and well operated by GCMHP and partners. GCMHP was and still is sharing factsheets and information with the Protection cluster, including the Child Protection sub-clusters by using many forms like 4 Ws and 5Ws matrix and other templates. GCMHP do not assume an “open door” policy is enough. It makes sure that there are other possibilities for submitting complaints that do not require the beneficiaries to expose themselves to project staff. It opened all social media platforms and websites to public to provide any feedback or complaints.
