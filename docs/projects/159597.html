Project Summary

The protracted protection crisis in Palestine, and its impact on gender and family dynamics, has exacerbated GBV in all its forms. In view of the deteriorating situation in both the West Bank and Gaza Strip, the project will assist most vulnerable women, adolescent girls and boys who are survivors of GBV, including women and girls with disabilities and those affected by the GMR in Gaza, by improving their access to essential, safe and high quality GBV multi-sectoral services.

1 - Needs

GBV continues to be a key protection and health concern in Palestine. The protracted protection crisis, and its impact on gender and family dynamics, has exacerbated GBV in all its forms, including sexual violence, intimate partner violence, and child marriage. In 2017 and 2018, the GBV Sub-Cluster lead by UNFPA registered more than 51,000 GBV cases as illustrated in the GBV Information Management System of the GBV Sub-Cluster. Out of these registered GBV survivors, around 60% were women, 30% girls, 5% men and 5% boys. This clearly illustrated the fundamental need to continue providing quality GBV services, with particular focus on meeting the needs of women and girls, especially those living with disability. Based on a study conducted by UNFPA in Palestine in 2019, a large proportion of women and girls with disabilities experience various forms of psychological, social, physical and sexual violence. They often live under the authority of a guardian and survivors are deprived of the right to make their own decisions, coerced to adopt certain behaviors and experience humiliation, degradation and exploitation, in addition to different forms of discrimination, exploitation, abuse, confinement and lack of inclusion in family or public sphere, inevitably leading to increased risk and higher vulnerability to violence, compared to other social groups. Out of the 9 GBV projects that were approved in the 2019 HRP, only 2 received funding as of September 2019, highlighting the profound challenges humanitarian actors face in responding to the needs of GBV survivors throughout the West Bank and Gaza. GBV Sub-Cluster partners have reported increased ﬁnancial diﬃculties in order to guarantee the continuity of services to the most vulnerable and marginalized women and girls. This has resulted in cancelled activities and reduced working hours.The humanitarian situation in the Gaza Strip continues to deteriorate. Since 30 March 2018, the Gaza Strip has been tense with weekly demonstrations as part of the ‘Great March of Return’. According to OCHA, as of 31 August 2019, 207 Palestinians were killed in the context of the demonstrations, including 44 children, and more than 34,440 were injured. Focus group discussions with affected communities have conﬁrmed that women and girls from families affected by casualties and injuries have an increased risk of GBV. An advocacy paper published by GBV Sub Cluster in 2018 demonstrated that the electricity and fuel crisis in Gaza Strip has exacerbated the severity of the situation for women and girls, disrupting almost all aspects of life. The breakdown of the reconciliation process between the Palestinian Authority and the de facto government has resulted in the PA's failure in transferring salaries to civil servants of Gaza, which is increasing the tension within families resulting in increased GBV of the affected families. The continuous UNRWA ﬁnancial crisis has further destabilized the oPt and especially the Gaza Strip, where 1.3 million out of a population of 1.9 million are refugees receiving assistance. In the West Bank, the deteriorating economic conditions, especially in Area C and H2, related high unemployment due to the blockade and policies of the occupying power are among the main drivers of GBV. Additionally, forced displacement, and the risk thereof, increases pressure on households, and may lead to increased GBV. While most women and girls at this point can be said to be vulnerable to GBV, some groups face particularly extreme vulnerability: women and girls with disabilities, Bedouins, internally displaced refugees and poor and female-headed households. Based on the above, this project will seek to assist women, adolescent girls and boys affected by the deteriorating situation in the Gaza Strip. In West Bank, the project will target areas identiﬁed as having a high vulnerability level in the ‘Protection of Civilian Vulnerability Analysis’ by OCHA, including WGWDs and youth.

2 - Activities

Outcome 1: Improved availability of safe and high quality multi sectoral services and protection for vulnerable women and girls including women and girls with disabilities in the affected communities in Gaza Strip and West Bank. • Providing case management services for 6,000 GBV survivors including 1000 women with disabilities in the established safe spaces in the West Bank and Gaza Strip. • Provide GBV health care and sexual and reproductive health services including occupational therapy and assistive devices for 2,500 (out of the total 6000) GBV survivors including women with disabilities through counselling centers or health clinics in close partnership with the established safe spaces. • Providing MHPSS services (psychological counselling, and individual and group MHPSS support) for 6,000 GBV survivors through counselling centers or health clinics in close partnership with the established safe spaces. • Provide MHPSS services to 1,000 (out of the total 6000) GBV survivors reached through mobile clinics in Area C • Refer 200 GBV survivors to mental health services and advanced psychosocial support services including 50 WGWD. • Conduct 40 open recreational days for a total of 2,000 (out of the total 6000) GBV survivors. • Providing dignity kits for 6,000 women and girls including women with disabilities (survivors of violence). • Provide vocational training courses for 1000 (out of the total 6000) GBV survivors including women with disabilities and include them in the Safe spaces of each implementing partner, and enable them to run economic activities. •Provide small grants to establish shared small businesses for 200 (out of the total 6000) GBV survivors including women with disabilities to empower them and benefit from their various skills to produce and guarantee their income source. • Provide counselling sessions and psychosocial support for service providers for 150 GBV service providers. • Provide counselling sessions for 300 perpetrators of GBV through awareness raising sessions and couple therapy. • Conduct focus groups interview to assess the survivor’s satisfaction on the provided services (communication with communities). • Provide young women and girls survivors of violence with resilience building initiatives, including learning opportunities and vocational skills, and referrals to other services including case management for protection. Focus will be on the ﬁves Governorates of Gaza Strip and areas market as highly vulnerable in the Protection of Civilian database by OCHA in the West Bank. Outcome 2: Enhanced knowledge and awareness among vulnerable groups of women and adolescent girls and boys, women and girls with disabilities, GBV service providers and non-GBV specialists in the affected communities on the GBV, its consequences and the available services in their communities. • Conduct needs assessment in each governorate of the West Bank and Gaza Strip with women, adolescent girls and boys in risk of GBV to assess their knowledge about GBV and the available services (focus will be on the ﬁves Governorates of Gaza Strip and areas market as highly vulnerable in the Protection of Civilian database by OCHA in the West Bank). • Raise awareness on GBV and available GBV services among 1,070 GBV service providers, including on the inclusion of WGWDs survivors of GBV in their services. • Raise awareness on GBV and available GBV services among 2,000 non-GBV specialists including humanitarian actors, community and religious leaders in highly vulnerable areas • Raise awareness on GBV available GBV services and rights of WGWDs among 10,000 women, adolescent girls and boys in high risk of GBV. • Produce material to be broadcasted through TV, radio, printed material and internet, including material adapted to all types of disabilities, on GBV issues and available services.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

“Do No harm” is one of the main GBV guiding principles and approaches according to UNFPA minimum standards and all our partner GBV service providers know how to apply these principles in their work with GBV survivors. The service providers have been trained to protect the identity and safety of any GBV survivor by not disclosing any personal information about the survivor or the incident to anyone without the survivor’s explicit permission and consent. Also, they deal with survivors on a case-by-case basis and have robust experience in ensuring GBV survivors are protected from harm and in providing psychological and emotional safety to survivors who may be at high risk from the perpetrators or from their family members due to notions of family honor. In addition, survivors always have the right to choose which service to access.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project is mainly designed to improve the availability and accessibility of multi-sectoral services and protection for vulnerable women and girls through UNFPA implementing partners. It will target most vulnerable communities through existing counselling centers and health clinics and more specifically in the West Bank, will improve access of isolated communities to services through mobile clinics. In addition, the project will focus on areas identified as having a high vulnerability level in the ‘Protection of Civilian Vulnerability Analysis’ by OCHA, including youth, and has been designed based on the experience and feedback from partners. Through its awareness and outreach activities, it will ensure that all targeted communities have a better knowledge of available GBV services as well as gender and GBV issues.
