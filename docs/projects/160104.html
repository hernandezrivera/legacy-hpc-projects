Project Summary

More than five years after the conflict had been started, the affected population’s needs in affected areas of eastern Ukraine are great, exceeding the capacity of Government of Ukraine (GoU) and requiring continuation of strong assistance from international humanitarian community. Data collection and analyses conducted as part of NRC’s responses over 2017-2019 years show that number of food insecure people, although somewhat decreased, is still very high. In average, the share of severely food insecure ranges around 1% of population in conflict affected areas, whereas in localities next to the Line of Contact (LoC) it is estimated to be at least 50% greater. As confirmed by several analyses done by relevant stakeholders, the most vulnerable groups are single member and elderly households, families with several children or with disabled members, and those whose breadwinners are chronically unemployed or without regular employment. In addition, residents of rural areas are significantly more vulnerable and surprisingly more often food insecure compared to
those living in urban or semi-urban areas. Due to various factors, such as limited access to land, difficult access to markets, unfavourable prices and depleted savings, agricultural households can hardly maintain their own food production. In addition, given the high unemployment rate in the country, especially IDPs have difficulties in getting a job, and therefore, according to IOM, in March 2019 only 48% of IDPs were employed. To be able to meet their basic needs, these categories regularly resort to negative coping strategies; according to IOM, 62% of IDPs were applying at least one coping strategy in February 2019 in GCA. To address these various needs of conflict affected population, NRC will continue its comprehensive support to the most vulnerable conflict-affected population in GCA to enable their: (1) access to food, (2) restoration of productive assets, and (3) employment and income generation opportunities. NRC has been implementing activities addressing access to food and early recovery in east Ukraine GCA since 2016. Programme activities are aligned with relevant GoU strategies and attempt to create synergies/linkages with the activities of recovery/development actors (HDN). Targeting primarily the most vulnerable IDPs and conflict-affected host communities along the LoC, beneficiaries will be selected from different age and social groups prioritizing elderly, female-headed and/or households with 2 or more children, persons with disabilities/medical cases and with no regular incomes.

1 - Needs

Although in recent period there were positive developments in Ukraine, such as gradual increase in salary and pensions’ levels, due to the protracted character of the crises disrupted livelihoods in eastern Ukraine and affected population’s inability to regain economic security remain the main issues. Food access remains among the main concerns in the affected areas, especially along the LoC and in winter season. Due to high share of population with incomes below the poverty level, food inflation and very high actual food basket costs, access to food particularly for IDPs and vulnerable families along the LoC remains a challenge. As their savings and resources have been largely depleted many of them have to continue resorting to negative coping mechanisms, including reduction of food intake, disposal of productive assets particularly livestock, borrowing money/buying food on credit, and reduction in other essential expenditures. According to IOM’s NMS Report, in general 62% of households in February 2019 in GCA reported using at least one coping strategy, whereas most commonly used strategies were reduction of essential health expenditures (35%), spending savings (34%) and borrowing money (27%). WFP reports that consumer price inflation weakened to 9.8% in December 2018 year on year from 13.7% in December 2017, while food inflation fell down from 17.7 to 7.8%. However, actual food basket costs in Ukraine reached the highest historical level of UAH 1,043 in December 2018, which is 13% higher year on year. Finally, 2018 Ukraine Humanitarian Response Plan has found some 1.2 million people on both sides of the LoC to be either severely or moderately food insecure. Of these, more than 400,000 in GCA are in urgent need of food assistance – mainly households headed by women with children, elderly persons living alone and chronically ill or disabled people. According to the same source, among this population up to 26,000 people have been identified as severely food insecure in GCA. Later on, the FSLC has corrected this estimation to approximately 13,000 people. IOM’s NMSR, March 2019, showed that the employment situation of IDPs slightly improved compared to the previous round, and as of January–March 2019, the share of employed IDPs amounted to 48%. Among the total population of Ukraine, the level of employment remained stable and as of the fourth quarter of 2018 amounted to 57% of the population aged 15–70 years. The NMSR has also found that the average monthly income per person varied depending on settlement type: in cities it was the highest, i.e. UAH 3,090, in towns it equalled to UAH 2,374, while in rural areas it was the lowest, UAH 1,710 only. The Ukraine 2019 HNO highlights that the rural population close to the LoC are still food insecure due to limited financial resources to buy enough food, as consequence of very low income per household member. Therefore, the needs for agricultural type of assistance in rural areas along the LoC remain high. According to HNO 2019, around 21,000 rural households residing within 20 km from the LoC in GCA and 39,000 households in NGCA rely on self-production of their food and are in critical need of agricultural inputs to support their income generating and food production activities. In line with these findings, according to Economic Security Assessment, published by REACH in March 2019, rural households were significantly more likely to have utilised at least one coping strategy (62%) than were urban households (40%). Likewise, households within 0-5 km of the LoC were more likely (58%) than were households between 5-20 km of LoC (38%). Rural areas within 0-5km of LoC were particularly likely to have used livelihoods coping strategies, with 63% of households reporting having used at least one in the 30 days prior to assessment.

2 - Activities & outcomes

The response will be focused on achieving the three main goals/outcomes via a set of activities, as follows: 1.The most vulnerable and food insecure conflict-affected households have access to food – Distribution of cash grants for basic food needs: · Season: winter months, when needs are greater, · Beneficiaries identified and registered per FSLC vulnerability criteria, prioritizing those residing along the LoC. · Amount of cash grants established in line with FSLC and CWG’s recommendations. · Transfers done via national postal system Ukrposhta as direct delivery to beneficiaries, to minimize the risks, · Selected households receive monthly cash grants labelled for meeting basic food needs, · Post distribution monitoring (PDM) carried out 3 weeks after distributions. 2.Improved food security and self-sustenance of conflict-affected population via restoration of agricultural productive capacity along the LoC – via technical and financial assistance: · Early spring: Provision of labelled cash grants for procurement of agricultural inputs to selected vulnerable households for re-establishment of agricultural activities. · Year-round: Rehabilitation of agricultural productive assets such as irrigation systems, greenhouses and orchards. through Food For Assets (FFA) type of responses, · Year-round: Provision of agricultural training and field advisory assistance to support application of adequate technologies and secure good yields, quality and incomes. 3.Improved economic security and access to employment/income-generation opportunities for conflict-affected population via early recovery assistance - Year-round, consisting of: · Enhancing local household economy and incomes/job-creation for vulnerable population via grants intended to ( a) enable their self-employment or (b) support their employment through sustained –micro/small enterprises. This will enable them to improve self-reliance and meeting their basic needs initiative. · Support of skills training and building capacity of relevant employment/business-support service providers (LPs), with priority given to women, unemployed youth and young adults, · Production, management and marketing capacity building of income-oriented rural/producers’ organisations. · Rehabilitation of non-agricultural livelihoods/well-being assets - FFA. Geography: With priority given to the LoC areas, vast majority of assistance will be provided in areas up to 5 km from the LoC in Luhansk and Donetsk oblasts GCA, while in areas 5-20 km identified pockets of needs will be targeted. General priority will be given to hard to reach areas and those with higher concentration of IDP population. Coordination: NRC will continue working closely and coordinating with the FSLC, CWG and relevant stakeholders throughout the project’s planning and implementation. Gender Mainstreaming: NRC will ensure that there is active participation of women, girls, men and boys in all project activities. Taking into consideration unique needs of individuals/social groups, the programme will promote women’s role as producers and providers of incomes, and equitable access to food and livelihood opportunities to the elderly, adults, boys, girls, men and women. Female-headed households will be prioritized and majority of the project beneficiaries will be women. Participatory approach will be used in analyzing needs/preferences and in identifying the most vulnerable. Beneficiary data will be disaggregated as follows: elderly (60+; male/female); adults (18-59 male/female); children (5-17 male/female) and children (<5 male/female). Advocacy: NRC will continue advocating for: access to land for livelihoods (demining of farmland, cessation of the use of property by the military, access to documentation), especially in areas with existing food needs and hard to reach; for freedom of movement of people, goods and capital across the LoC.

3 - Indicators & targets

CO 1 ( Cluster Objective 1): Most vulnerable conflict-affected groups have immediate access to food: · % of the target population with acceptable Food Consumption Score (FCS) at end of implementation (Target 70%) · # of individuals receiving cash transfers to improve their immediate access to food (target: 8,000) · Total value of cash for food and basic needs distributed (target: $1,300,000) CO 2: Affected populations improve food security status through provision of emergency and time-critical agriculture assistance: · % of beneficiary HHs who report/are observed using food production inputs as intended within 3 months of provision (Target 90%) · # of HHs receiving emergency agriculture assistance (target: 1,600 HHs) · Total value of cash for agricultural inputs distributed (target: $250,000) CO 3: Affected populations improve their basic economic security and self-sustenance of their households: · # of individuals receiving emergency livelihood and/or income generation support (target: 200) · % of targeted HHs that increased income as a result of assistance provided (Target 70%) · Total value of cash and in-kind assistance for income generation and employment creation distributed to targeted beneficiaries (target $150,000) CO4 Affected populations improve their resilience through rehabilitated agriculture and/or increased agricultural production for sustainable livelihoods · # of HHs benefiting from agricultural support: · - grants: target: 60; · - rehabilitation of assets: target: 200; · - Ag trainings: target 100; · # of assets restored or maintained to targeted beneficiaries, by type and unit of measure (target:10) CO5 Affected populations have access to employment and income generating opportunities through rehabilitation/building of sustainable livelihood assets · # of vulnerable HHs receiving start-up capital to start/rehabilitate micro/small businesses (target: 120) · # of jobs created, in addition to recipients’ HHs - for women, youth and adults (target 40) · # of people - women, youth and young adults benefitting via improved incomes or employability (target 600) · # of individuals benefiting from livelihood and/or income generation support (rehabilitation of assets: target: 1,000) · # of people - women, youth and young adults benefitting via improved access to/and or skills training or business-development services (target 400) · # of organisations benefitting via improved capacity (target 10) · # of people benefitting via improved incomes and/or access to markets through the supported organisations (target 250) Protection mainstreaming indicators: · % of feedbacks/complaints responded to or forwarded to the appropriate actor (Target 100%) · % of the affected population reporting being aware of how to use the feedback and complaints mechanism - CFM (Target 90%) · % of beneficiaries reporting that humanitarian assistance is delivered in a safe and dignified manner (Target 90%) · % of beneficiaries reporting they understand how humanitarian services were prioritized and selected (targeting criteria) (Target 80%) M&E: Output and outcome monitoring will be done throughout the whole project. Beneficiaries will be randomly sampled and post distribution monitoring (PDM) carried out after each distribution cycle. Data will be collected through focus group discussions as well as HH interviews. NRC will also carry out a baseline studies as well as an end of project evaluations. Protection Mainstreaming and AAP: A full protection risk analysis (tensions, landmines/UXOs, abuse, exploitation, GBV, access by women/at-risk groups) will be carried out and mitigation strategies developed beforehand. NRC policy places a heavy emphasis on ‘rights based’ programming, in which accountability plays an important role. NRC has set up a CFM which is independent from programme operations.
