Project Summary

The ongoing conflict has led to high levels of displacement and continues to severely disrupt food production systems and supply chains in North East Nigeria, thereby contributing to extensive human and socioeconomic losses and negatively affecting production systems leading to reduced crop yields ,livestock productivity and exposure to protection risks for women and girls across the region. The volatile security situation is gradually eroding the gains choked over the years and further intensifying the need.
In 2022, FAO will continue to support IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities to rebuild their agricultural based livelihoods that has been decimated by the conflict. This includes plans to support 12400 households with improved seeds and other agri-inputs. Households for whom livestock was the primary source of income will be supported with restocking interventions aimed at restoring their traditional livelihoods.
The oncoming season will be very crucial in terms of the anticipated looming catastrophic food insecurity situation within the northeast of Nigeria. This has led to strong advocacy by all actors within the Food Security Sector including FAO on the need to scale up support on agricultural based livelihoods for the upcoming year, 2022.
Further, the increased displacement in recent months in some Local Governments Areas (LGA) in Borno states (Bama, Banki and Dikwa) due to continued attacks by the arm groups requires urgent actions by FAO to meet the urgent needs of the displaced persons. Over 5000 persons including women and children have been displaced in the month of August 2021 alone in these two LGAs and the numbers are expected to increase in the coming months.
As a result, FAO is responding by scaling up its emergency intervention programme in the north east Nigeria in 2022 to reach more vulnerable communities and households with various forms of agricultural support including quality seeds and fertilizer, livestock & poultry restocking, aquaculture production, Safe Access to Fuel and Energy and critical agriculture based value chain development to increase food production and household food availability.

1 - Needs

In 2022 FAO will continue to support people in need by improving food security, nutritional status and incomes among vulnerable conflict- and pandemic-affected households in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states in Northeast Nigeria. The now more than decade-long insurgency has decimated agricultural livelihoods upon which more than 80 percent of the population relies as a primary source of food and household income. The COVID-19 pandemic has further compounded this already highly vulnerable humanitarian context, generating interconnected vulnerabilities that negatively affect availability and access to food, agriculture inputs, finance, farming land, extension services, markets and the fundamental viability of agricultural livelihoods. It therefore remains evident that support to the restoration and protection of agricultural livelihoods is key to improving food insecurity and nutrition outcomes as well as mitigating exposure to protection risks.

2 - Activities

Support to Food Production - Support to land preparation including distribution of rainy season and dry season kits (seeds and fertilizer) for crop production as well as essential inputs for backyard vegetable gardening to restore/protect agricultural livelihoods and promote household nutrition. Rainy season crop beneficiaries will also benefit from a Fresh Food Voucher to prevent premature consumption of food stocks during the lean season. - Training of existing seed producers on improved production techniques, good crop production management practices, post-harvest handling, storage and record keeping, including establishment of community-based seed out-growers to promote the production of improved quality and climate-resilient seeds through seed multiplication - Creation of market linkages between seed producers and buyers (agricultural support projects, farmer cooperatives, government) Support to Livestock-based Livelihoods including animal restocking, value chain development and veterinary support. The support will specifically entail: - Animal restocking targeting women and youth with goat and poultry kits for income generation and improved nutrition. Targeted beneficiaries will also benefit from FAO’s ‘cash+’ approach through which they will receive a cash transfer aimed at protecting the livestock assets from premature consumption or sale, and from market linkages. - Vaccination campaign to control animal diseases aimed at protecting livestock-based livelihoods, including biosecurity/biocontainment training for disease control, food safety and animal welfare to key associations/groups involved in livestock production/processing. - Building the capacity of state veterinary services and Community-based Animal Health Workers (CBAHW) to provide animal health services to project beneficiaries and other vulnerable livestock owners. Support to Fisheries - Traditional fishing households that have lost their livelihoods owing to the conflict around the Lake Chad basin will be supported through: - Provision of fish farm starter kits (tanks, fingerlings and feed) for aquaculture production, mainly targeting male youth, including a cash transfer for protection of assets. - Establishment and/or scale-up of private/community-based fingerling production enterprises in close collaboration with the Federal College of Freshwater Fisheries Technology, Baga. - Establishment of local fish feed manufacturing cooperatives through local products assessment and capacity building programmes. - Promotion of good hygiene and handling practices through capacity building activities and provision of handling goods and tools to enhance the quality of fish products at market, thereby increasing sale potential. - Provision of fish processing kits (drying, smoking) and trainings to women’s groups to maximize the utilization of fish catch, limit production losses and increase the shelf life of fish products. Protection - Establishment/upgrading of fuel-efficient stove production centres and briquette production centres for income generation. - Distribution of fuel-efficient stoves to households facing heightened protection and health risks; mitigate poor energy access and adoption of negative coping strategies. Increasing Access to Credit To enhance access to credit among vulnerable groups – particularly women – Group Savings and Loans Associations (GLSAs) will be established to facilitate access to financial services through group-mobilized funds. This will promote upscaling and resilience-building by enabling beneficiaries to procure productive assets and develop micro-businesses with the loans and productive resources generated. Food Security Coordination, Data Collection and Analysis - Critical support to the functioning of the Food Security Sector for coordination of food security activities in the BAY states - Critical support to food security data collection and analysis to enable evidence-based programming by food security actors.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

FAO will undertake a broad scale protection risk analysis to understand the risks of specific groups across agricultural value chains in the northeast and is well placed to design projects which consider the specific risks facing at-risk groups. FAO has field experts including female field assistants and staff who speak the common local languages in the BAY states and are frequently deployed during trainings, distributions, monitoring and planning missions. To greater understand the protection risks faced by HHs, FAO will undertake a protection risk assessment prior to the implementation of the activity. The Organization will also reinforce training to staff on GBV, PSEA, AAP and CwC. By adopting a CBA, ensuring beneficiary participation in decision-making, and involving local and traditional authorities, the project will promote accountability, capacity building & empowerment. Five percent of the budget will be allocated for the recruitment of a protection expert, training of stakeholders communication and advocacy.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

FAO will ensure that feedback mechanisms such as toll free numbers, complaint boxes, complaint desks are available throughout the life cycle of the project and that vulnerable community members have access to these mechanisms. The organization will also ensure that the views and experiences of the most vulnerable are integrated in post-activity monitoring and reporting. FAO will engage a wide cross section of stakeholders to identify the most-vulnerable and those least likely to utilize feedback mechanisms . These individuals will be identified and reached in person where possible and their specific requirements and needs will be taken into account in project design, implementation and appraisal.
