Project Summary

Relief International (RI) proposes the 12-month project “Improved Access to Primary Health Care for 7,000 vulnerable Lebanese and refugees of all nationalities (IAPHC)” that will significantly contribute to improving the well-being of individuals of all nationalities who were directly or indirectly affected by the Beirut Blast of August 4, 2020. The IAPHC project will focus on: rehabilitating five Primary Health Care Centers (PHCCs) that were affected by the blast and supporting access to primary health care (PHC) services for Lebanese and refugees of all nationalities in Beirut by subsidizing a comprehensive package of services at eight PHCCs. RI will also integrate Mental Health (MH) services in all of its supported PHCCs and provide specialized and non-specialized counseling.

1 - Needs

Following the tragic blast that hit Beirut on August 4, the Ministry of Public Health (MOPH) in coordination with the Health Sector launched an Appeal for the Beirut Port Explosion Response. Some of the main needs and requirements are: i) physical rehabilitation of the 19 damaged PHCCs, estimated to cost 37,806 USD each as per the preliminary assessment conducted by the Health Sector; ii) Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) for PHCC staff to ensure both beneficiary and healthcare providers’ safety amidst the COVID-19 pandemic, estimated to cost 800,000 USD; and iii) medical supplies and consumables essential for the provision of routine and first aid services at the PHCCs estimated to cost 4,000,000 USD per year. In August 2020, RI’s Public Health Officers conducted a needs assessment through phone surveys and field visits to 10 PHCCs that are located in the explosion circumference and included in the list shared by MOPH. The PHCCs have major damage to their buildings, mainly glass doors, wooden doors, cupboards, curtains, and cracks in the walls. Most of the PHCCs have still not estimated the cost for repairs. The PHCC Directors mentioned shortages in medical consumables due to the increase in prices and the large consumption that happened immediately after the explosion in response to the large load of injured patients seeking immediate health care. Shortage in medical staff and in acute and chronic medication was also noted. After the explosion, there are many cases seeking psychological first aid and support to cope with the impact of the incident which is difficult for the PHCCs to cover within their existing capacities. Consultation fees at the targeted PHCCs vary between 15,000 LBP and 18,000 LBP, adding to the cost of diagnostic tests that might go from 15,000 LBP to 30,000 LBP or more. With the deteriorating economic situation, access to health care services for all nationalities might be jeopardized.

2 - Activities

Access to PHC and MH services is improved by repairing damages at PHCCs, improving capacity of service delivery at PHCCs, subsidizing consultations, integrating MH services in close coordination with the National Mental Health Program, upgrading one PHCC to become a community mental health center and increasing community knowledge on access to services and key health topics
