Project Summary

-This project will be implemented in Aleppo and Idleb governorates.
-100% of the subdistricts, the severity of them will be between 4,5,6.
-The number of disabilities in this project will be 192 students.
-The cost per student is 82 USD.
-The estimated funding that Al-Ameen organization received in 2019 for education is about 535K USD.
-The total persons that Al-Ameen organization reached (all the Sectors) in 2019 is 360830 Beneficiaries.
Al-Ameen organization will support formal and non-formal education for 12000 (5-17, 6500 boys, 5500 girls) who are dropping out or at risk of school drop-out, in addition to 380 (212 males and 168 females) education personal through supporting schools and educational centers that have ended fund or will end in 2020, in addition to providing the dropped-out children with non-formal education. This project is critical due to its significance in mitigating social risks that might lead to educational gaps if they were not dealt with immediately. These risks include early marriage, recruitment into armed groups, and child labor.
the interventions of the project will be as the following:
1- Al-Ameen organization will support formal education in 20 schools where those schools include 8300 students (4700 boys and 3600 girls) and their fund has ended or will end soon. The intervention minimizes the risk of students dropping out, where monthly incentives to teachers will be provided, as they don’t have any alternative fund.
2-Al-Ameen will support the educational process by supporting 3700 Out of School Children (OOSC), (1900 boys and 1800 girls), (65% IDPs and 35% HCs) with non-formal education in 15 schools through catch up and remedial classes.
3- 380 Teachers (212 males and 168 females) will be provided with incentives, as they don't receive any financial support from any other NGOs.
4-monthly operational costs, and heating materials to face the cold of winter.
5-PSS weekly sessions will also help children, who spent a long time away from school, ridding of the harsh experience of war conditions and motivate them to continue their studies at school.
6- The 20 targeted schools will be equipped with safety and security tools, also, the project will involve 195 children with disability, who drop out of school, and overcome the difficulties that they may face through providing them with transportation service in addition to providing schools with tools for children with disability.
7- Teaching materials will be distributed to Schools.
8- education kits will distribute for students, teachers, in addition, recreational kits to facilitate PSS sessions.
9- at the beginning of the project Al-Ameen organization will conduct a meeting with the local community and local actors to ensure their participation in the project, explain the project goals and take their suggestions and notes to develop the project activities and implementation.
10- AlAmeen organization will coordinate with the Education cluster and with other organization who works in the same sector and field to reach the right beneficiaries and avoid any duplication.
11-since the wash facilities are playing big role in increasing the access to the schools, the project will give this issue high priority and will take in our consideration the gender issues and the segregation between the toiles of boys and girls, in addition, supporting the schools by ramps, hand drills and the toilets for the disabilities.
-the project will contribute to increasing the quality of education in the targeted schools by training the teachers (males and females) in several topics like EIE, PSS, Child protection and teaching methodologies.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk/Threat :Learning facilities could be attacked. Attacks may include airstrikes, bombs/explosions/shelling, fighting/gunshots, arson/fire, looting/theft; kidnapping/arrest of a teacher or students, a direct attack on teacher or student, intimidation of school staff/students or military use of the school.Mitigating Measures: Learning facilities can be de-conflicted using the de-confliction mechanism administered by UN-OCHA. De-confliction does not guarantee that learning facilities will not be attacked. An organization should undertake an informed process before it decides to de-conflict or not de-conflict. Please contact your Coordinator or OCHA if you need help in this process.Learning facilities should have safety protocols in place. These protocols should be adequately resourced and regularly practiced. Resources may include walkie-talkies, basic firefighting equipment, basic first aid supplies, flashlights, exit signs, etc. Protection Risk/Threat:Teachers or other education personnel participate in physical punishment or bullying of students. Students participate in the bullying of students.Mitigating Measure: Learning facilities should have an enforced code of conduct and policy on bullying. Protection Risk/Threat: Children are over-exposed to the risk of, or experience, harm by teachers, school personnel, organization staff or related entities.Mitigating measures: Learning facilities should have Child Safeguarding policies and systems in place and these should be regularly monitored and corrective action taken when needed.Protection Risk/Threat: Members of the community or local authorities try to over-influence or take actions that prevent, or try to change, the type of support provided to education services or who will benefit from the support provided.Mitigating Measures: Projects should be designed to be context and conflict-sensitive. This may include issues related to ethnicity, religion, language, economic classe etc.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Some of the targeted schools may be funded from other donors, which leads to duplication of services. Likelihood: Low Impact: Medium MS: Before signing the contracts with schools’ directors, a confirmation document will be provided from the targeted schools confirms that the school does not receive funds from another donor. In addition, we will coordinate with the education directory to make sure that the targeted schools have no secured fund.Risk 2: Changes in the security situation or authorities/groups in control of project areas may make the project activities less appropriate to the new situation. Likelihood: Medium Impact: High MS:1- Closely monitor the security situation in coordination with partners, civil authorities and relevant security and access stakeholders. 2- Ensure that staff members, teachers, and education personnel are trained in safety and security, including mine risk education. 3- All critical documents will be saved as soft copies so that they will not get lost due to an attack.Risk 3: parents may not accept to register their dropped-out children in school. Likelihood: low Impact: high MS: we will arrange some meetings with local actors to explain the importance of education and to give a background about upcoming classes for dropped out and this important chance for their children to continue their education and they will take the role to inform the community, as well as there will be brochures published in each location to push parents to come and register their children.Risk 4: The fluctuating of fuel prices may affect the amount of distributed fuel. Likelihood: Medium Impact: Medium MS: we will sign contracts with vendors at the beginning of the winter season, so prices will be identified.Risk 6: Delay or inability to deliver supplies in a timely manner, insecurity on transit routes or near warehouses. Likelihood: Low Impact: Medium: almost most of the supplies/materials to be purchased from local markets.
