Project Summary

This project aims to contribute to reduction of morbidity and mortality rate especially among women and children (girls, boys, women and men) for vulnerable communities in South Darfur, East Darfur, South Kordofan and Blue Nile states
Through provision of essential full package of health services that include treatment of non- communicable diseases of preventive and curative PHC services according to the MoH policy and guidelines while taking into consideration of local context and needs of the community to increase access to quality Primary Health Care services for vulnerable communities (girls, boys, women and men) in South Darfur, East Darfur, South Kordofan and Blue Nile States, Procure and distribute essential drugs for vulnerable communities (girls, boys, women and men) , medical equipments and supplies to targeted PHC clinics , constructions, rehabilitation and maintenance of health facility structures to improve space availability and to maintain standards, Support male and female staffing to health facilities according o the MoH policy and guidelines, conduct health education session to male and female communities at the health facility and community level on various health topics to increase awareness and health seeking behavior , conduct quarterly joint supervision and monitoring visits by male and female staff with the MoH to all targeted health facilities, Strengthen existing referral system, linking all levels of health delivery, Strengthened emergency response preparedness ,compile and submit accurate & timely weekly clinic surveillance (EWARS) reports to SMoH, Conduct community health awareness sessions for female and male on prevention and mitigation of communicable diseases, actively participates in joint planning and response to communicable disease outbreaks through health sector and cluster coordination, Improve infection control procedures for health facility female and male staff through training on infection prevention and control, and universal precaution procedures.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in Sudan continues to be complex, driven by continuous man-made and natural disasters localized conflict, floods, and disease outbreaks. Furthermore, increasing economic challenges negatively affect the delivery of and access to health services (HNO 2020).According to HRP health sector 2020, people in need about 8.6 million and overall number people targeted by the health sector are 5 million. Number of people targeted per vulnerable group 2 million pregnant women, 860,000 under five years, 100,000 elderly and 1,300,000 IDPs. The percentage of male 41 percent/female59 percent /children 63 percent/adult 29 percent and percentage of people with disparities 15 percent of total populationThe protracted crisis in Sudan and inadequate funding have had a major impact on all levels of health care, especially in Darfur, South Kordofan ,Blue Nile and ED states. During 2019, some 350,000 people were affected by the seasonal floods. The lack of vector control programs and lack of optimal access to WASH services contributed to the appearance of several outbreaks - Cholera, Dengue, Chikungunya, and Rift Valley fevers - in the months after the flooding, with a significant increase in Malaria cases that reached 1.8 M cases by the end of November 2019. The overall vaccination coverage including DPT3 containing vaccine continues to be only around 60 per cent exposing children under 5 years of age to vaccine preventable diseases and increasing the chances of outbreaks. Communities affected by conflict and newly accessible areas under peace agreements such as areas in south Kordofan, Blue Nile, and Central Darfur still lack basic and life-saving health services with intermittent access to population due ongoing security and safety challenges (HRP health sector 2020). Maternal and infant mortality will continue to be a priority problem particularly among vulnerable populations especially under five boys and girl children and pregnant and lactating women. In addition, the prevalence of non-communicable diseases is on the rise. Availability of essential medicines declined during 2019 to reach 43 per cent across surveyed health facilities with increased cost of 60 per cent. The full essential healthcare package is available only in 30 per cent of primary healthcare centers including maternal and child health services. In addition, 1.9 million IDPs residing in and outside camps are in need to sustainable access to basic health services. People living with disabilities, mental health services including for GBV survivors, and outreach services for remote communities and displaced population male and female are topics in need for further strengthening. Ensuring holistic health coverage across Sudan based on needs and updated assessments is essential to address any emergencies and lessen the impact of seasonal hazards and disease outbreaks (HRP sector draft 2020). Health sector needs to ensure continued access to basic and life-saving primary healthcare packages and referral services to girls, boys, men and women, while improving maternal and child services in localities with significant health needs. Absence of functional community based health services, poor education and information of communities on communicable and other diseases creates huge gaps on health seeking behavior and best practice of personal and environmental hygiene.current disease surveillance system covers 40 per cent of designated health facilities, the coverage is disproportional with gaps that can delay early detection of outbreaks. The health human resources are continuously in need for training and re-training to address the inability of health authorities to retain health staff and maintain national and local resources. Supporting programs that aim to ensure mitigating the effects of emergencies and sustainable responses will enhance health sector capacities to predict, prepare, and respond to emergencies in timely manner(HRP health sector 2020)

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

WV applies the “Do No Harm” program analysis framework in each of its programs, ensuring that project activities do not exacerbate tensions and conflicts in the proposed program areas. Staff from all different sectors have been or will shortly be trained in this approach.When conducting routine community assessments and monitoring, WV also includes an analysis of protection issues to better understand the community’s special protection needs and concerns. For example, factors which might increase the exposure of women and young girls to violence when collecting water and firewood or using latrines and bathing facilities are identified in order to minimize risks in designing program activities. Special focus group discussions with women and other vulnerable groups are also routinely undertaken.In the health sector, WV already incorporates certain protection features in the design of health activities. For example, in order to be as inclusive as possible, health care is provided at no cost to all patients of primary health care clinics, irrespective of age, gender, social status, whether disabled or abled, or tribal or group affiliation. These clinics have also been strategically located within the IDP camps so that they can be easily accessed by everyone. Furthermore, to reduce social and cultural barriers in accessing health care, appropriate health staff are hired by the program to provide specific types of care such as antenatal and postnatal care. For example, as pregnancy and delivery are traditionally attended to by midwives, the program ensures that experienced midwives are hired to provide these services.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

This will be through Participation and involvement of the female and male targeted affected population in design, monitoring and evaluation of this project, establishment of strong communication systems to ensure feedback is received, processed and responded to timely and effectively. The project approach focuses on assessing and addressing the needs of all within the communities, including men, women, boys, girls, elders, persons with disabilities among other vulnerable and or marginalized groups. Community participation will be promoted throughout all the stages of the program; communities’ feedback was the most significant element for the needs assessments conducted prior to proposing this action- the proposed intervention is based on communities’ needs. Community consultation meetings and orientation sessions will be held at the beginning of the project to verify needs, gauge acceptance, ensure that the project is well understood by target communities and raise the communities’ awareness with regard to their participatory roles and responsibilities, and the responsibilities of the program partners toward them. Participation will be promoted through the involvement of beneficiaries in the formation of community committees and planning the implementation schedule. The community committees will be created from female and male to organize and mobilize the beneficiaries, participate in the development of beneficiary selection criteria, distribution of inputs and monitoring and evaluation of the intervention. Representatives from a cross-section (with respect to age, gender, and diversity) will be ensured to participate as community committee members and in the monitoring and evaluation of the project. The consortium partners have intensive experience working with the target communities and established relationships and inclusive community structures which will facilitate the establishment of effective feedback mechanisms. The most appropriate mechanisms will be established in consultation with community members, taking into consideration literacy rates, accessibility to communication devices and the presence of suitable sites for establishment of complaint / suggestion boxes; to ensure accessibility of all community members (men, women, children, elders and disabled people) and provision of timely response. Good internal procedures and confidential reporting systems will be implemented to ensure confidentiality and safety of all community members. The communities’ feedback will be acquired and verified through different approaches including regular monitoring activities and feedback, complaints and response mechanisms including focus group discussions, semi-structured interviews, key informant interviews, community meetings, establishment of complaint / suggestion boxes in project sites and communicating the partners’ contacts and addresses with community members and committees. Feedback and complaints would be reflected by the field staff
