Project Summary

Since the onset of the anglophone crisis in 2018, the situation has further deteriorated with the invasions of NSAGs on the population such as kidnappings with ransom demands, executions, destruction of property and infrastructure, the destruction and burning down of schools and hospitals. This situation has therefore led to a massive displacement of people fleeing the armed conflict and famine towards neighbouring Nigeria and to safer areas.
According to September 2020 SITREP of OCHA, with more than 705 8000 IDPs, 360 5000 returnees and 609 000 Cameroonian refugees in Nigeria and increasing number of causalities on the military side, non-state actors and civilian end as well. A major national dialogue was organized last September 2019 to provide a solution to this situation; however, this has yielded no results so far because most of the resolutions taken were rejected by the Non State Armed groups (NSAGs). This has led to an increase in violence as could be observed especially with the approach of the regional election planned for December 6, 2020 and Municipal/Legislative elections which took place in February 9,2020 . These events are characterized by an increase in the number of ghost town days and armed conflicts.
“Cadre Harmonisé” results conducted in Cameroon in October 2020 showed that 1076257 people in the North-West and South-West regions are food and nutritionally insecure (crisis to emergency phases). This represents 24% of the population of the North-West and South-West and in the 10 regions of Cameroon that was analyzed, an average of 21.8% of the total population falls within the crisis to emergency phases. With continuous humanitarian aid, anticipated peace and next agricultural season running good, it is expected that the number of people suffering from food insecurity would have decreased by the end of the 2021 farming season but remains very worrying.Moreover, the current conflict has plunged the North West and South West regions into a drastic slowdown of economic activities in general, and agro pastoral production in particular.
The priority of this project is to revamp the livelihoods and productive assets of internally displaced people, PWD and their host communities by setting up and monitoring highly productive micro-units of high-nutrient agro-pastoral products with high potential for financial revenue. It is reported that more than 80% of those affected by the crisis have experienced a continuous deterioration in their living standards and conditions for more than four years. In this deteriorating scenario, the scarcity of food in quantity and quality is highly noticeable. For example, the total quantity of meat (beef, egg, fish, poultry, etc.) available decreased from 27 kg/capita/year before the crisis to less than 20 kg/capita/year in these regions. In the same regard, a decrease is observed in the availability of staple foods such as maize, vegetable, cassava, plantain, and children's foods (FAOSTAT, 2019). In order to reverse the ongoing situation, the project aims to improve the food security vulnerable population in these regions by implementing sustainable vegetables, eggs, roots and tubers production systems. On the other hand, by providing a sustainable means of livelihoods, the project also aims to reduce the wide use of negative coping mechanisms enrolment of youths into armed groups, women vulnerability, PWD, IDPs and promote dialogue and governance in peace finding solutions and implementation.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Main activities to be done throughout this project are as follows: 1) Managerial, organizational and technical capacity building: beneficiaries will be trained on human nutrition, sustainable farming and farm resource management;2) Implementing home gardens made of rapid growth crops: targeted crops are potato, sweet potato, vegetables, dry vegetables, etc.3) Implementing sustainable poultry production units: providing veterinary products, animal and breeding materials, and follow-up on egg production4) Coordinating, monitoring and evaluating: strengthening of food security cluster 5) Sensitization of beneficiaries on COVID 19 prevention measures by the government and WHOThe main outputs expected from these activities are the following:1) At least 6845 direct beneficiaries of the project trained on human nutrition and sustainable farm and resource management;2) Gross production of about 50000 tons of roots, tubers, vegetables, and legumes obtained;3) Gross production of about 8000,000 table-eggs obtained from the beneficiaries;4) At least 6800 benefitting households targeted (including 70% of women headed households and 10% of PWD) ) sponsored 5) At least 90% of beneficiary households sensitised on COVID 19 preventive measures6) At least 150 local or community-based monitoring personnel mobilized and trained for the follow-up of the beneficiaries
