Project Summary

The projects seek to provide critical humanitarian needs through emergency lifesaving activities with integration of solutions for sustainability. Vulnerable people in need and Persons with Specific Needs (PSNs) including IDPs, returnees and the host community will benefit from scaled up protection monitoring with a focus on critical lifesaving and resilience targeted assistance linked to General Protection which includes protection monitoring as well as GBV, CP and HLP provided to the most vulnerable IDPs. Well established Protection Monitoring tools used at household H/H or individual level will be used to collect data to identify needs and individuals. This will offer standardized methodology of collecting data aggregated by age gender and diversity to ensure no one is left behind by promoting inclusion for all groups. Static and mobile teams will also support data collection.
The UNHCR Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) for 2025 will prioritize life-saving humanitarian interventions, emphasizing the following areas:
· General Protection - Conducting monitoring and analysis to identify needs and deliver tailored protection responses.
· Gender-Based Violence - Providing case management, cash-based assistance, psychosocial support, and awareness programs to prevent
· GBV.
· Child Protection - Identifying at-risk children and offering cash-based assistance, physical support, and training for caregivers through community-based protection networks and referral pathways.
· Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) Rights - Delivering legal aid and counseling, cash assistance, and strengthening alternative dispute resolution mechanisms.

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to face a plethora of challenges that have led to multi-dimensional protection risks creating a complex protection environment and heightened needs. The continued violence in Sudan, exacerbated an already critical situation marked by inter-communal conflict driven by competition over scarce resources, politically instigated violence, cattle raiding, revenge killings, and the deleterious effects of climate change, including severe droughts and floods. Since the outbreak of violence in Sudan on 15 April 2023, South Sudan has received returns in adverse conditions and refugees comprising of 222,000 HH comprising of 882,700 individuals, and the figures are rising as they flee in search of safety. The country also grapples with large scale internal displacement occasioned by conflict and climate change induced displacement, with 1.94 million internally displaced (IOM DTM Round 15). This brings the total number of people affected by displacement close to 3 million. Significant humanitarian concerns pose multifaceted threats to civilians, and the limited presence and ability of state institutions to provide basic services or security amplifies the magnitude of the challenges confronting South Sudan. This plan will target life saving assistance in the following locations: Abyei Administrative Area, CES, EES, Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Unity, Upper Nile, Warrap, Western Bahr el Ghazal, WES. This HRP is largely informed by the 2024 Participatory Assessment conducted countrywide by UNHCR and its partners. The assessment comprised of 313 FDGs and 73 KIIs and included 4087 individuals (IDPs and returnees) consisting of 2200F/1887M. Men, boys, women and girls, the youth, elderly and the disabled were included, this integrated the Age, Gender and Diversity approach into the data collection. Evidence based data has therefore informed the identification of needs among displaced people and the proposed solutions. Assessed needs are further complemented by: (i)South Sudan: Protection Monitoring Mid-year Report, July 2024; (ii) South Sudan, Protection Monitoring System Snapshot; and (iii) Numerous assessments conducted by UNHCR field offices and its partners. Persons of concern interviewed revealed critical needs related to the General Protection, GBV, CP, HLP and other assesses areas. The information collected reveals that the humanitarian needs in all communities remain dire and enormous. The situation is exacerbated by insecurity originating from repeated episodes of violence, climate shock, weak government institutions, limited access to basic services in education, health, water and sanitation facilities, high unemployment rates due to the lack of skills and limited job opportunities. Increasing vulnerability among IDPs, returnees and host community populations remains a major concern with the most affected being female head-households, children, and elderly. In particular the reduced humanitarian assistance reaching people in need, contributed to: sexual violence targeting women and children, coupled with heightened reports of child labour due to the state of the economy as people sought negative coping mechanisms. Displaced persons reported multiple experiences of GBV, poor health, inadequate nutrition, and challenging living conditions as they struggled to meet their basic needs. Respondents highlighted several priority thematic areas for interventions, including: support for GBV survivors with dignity kits, cash assistance, income-generating activities, and child protection measures such as family reunification, psychosocial support, and case management. HLP challenges related to lack of access to legal aid and counseling to address illegal occupation and land grabbing featured prominently in discussions. A combination of protection activities including support to livelihoods and cash assistance will be used to address needs identified, enhance resilience and scale up response.

2. Project activities

The protection environment in South Sudan reaffirms the need for continuation of integrating solutions and resilience building activities into lifesaving assistance from the start. Equally important is the collection of desegregated data on affected persons to guide interventions and establishment of feedback mechanisms to create avenues of reporting abuse and critical issues. To ensure targeted and tailored assistance based on vulnerabilities, UNCHR has an existing methodology for identifying and assisting persons with specific needs including disabled persons and the elderly, through assigned household codes to easily identify their needs. UNHCR identified Persons with Specific Needs through fields mission assessments with implementing partners, using mobile teams to distinguish vulnerable people from the wider displaced populations. Persons with Specific Needs (PSN) codes are deliberately included in assessments, consultations and decision making. Once identified, PSNs are classified into codes to establish the nature of assistance they may require as follows: Single Parent (SP) without support,Woman at risk (WR), SGBV (SV), Child at risk (CR), Unaccompanied or separated child (SC),Elderly at risk (ER), Person with Disabilities (DS), This plan will respond to needs through activities aligned to: the 2025 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) objectives and the South Sudan Protection Cluster Objectives as follows: (i)Ensure mobile or static provision of critical protection assistance and services (lifesaving). Responses are tailored to address the specific needs of the individuals, considering age, gender, and disability. (ii)Ensuring that human rights are upheld and promoted, centrality of protection is promoted and protection risks reduced. (ii)Promoting nexus to development and solutions as the transition between the delivery of emergency humanitarian assistance to long-term development assistance to meet peoples’ needs. Activities will center on life saving interventions, general protection, HLP, child protection and GVB. Throughout the delivery of assistance efforts will be made to ensure communication is maintained with persons of concern and approved activities on the ground are based on participatory assessments and inclusion of the needs and views of affected people. Feedback mechanisms which will be diverse ranging from hotlines, FGD, suggestion boxes, and protection desks will not only collect information but share feedback with persons of concern on the action taken to ensure the communication circuit is complete; they will also facilitate reporting on PSEA. In doing so the intervention will remain accountable to affected people. Activities to address these needs will be scaled up as follows: GP: Equitable life saving assistance tailored to specific needs based on vulnerabilities will be guided by Age Gender and Diversity. Tailored protection interventions will address heightened, vulnerabilities and protection gaps through direct implementation, collaborative efforts with partners, referrals, and community-based structures. The following will be conducted: Protection monitoring through KIIs, FGDs and observation, monitoring new arrivals, case management and protection case management, cash assistance, mobile protection monitoring to support identification and referrals of cases, establishing and enhancing community based protection structures. CP: A significant portion of the returnee population, estimated at 60%, consists of children who have experienced trauma resulting from the conflict in Sudan. Specific response activities will include case management supported by cash-based interventions, support will be provided through comprehensive cash management, case management material support, mapping of referral pathways, community-based mental health and child friendly psychosocial support, aware raising and training on risk mitigation training for humanitarian workers not specializing in CP response.
