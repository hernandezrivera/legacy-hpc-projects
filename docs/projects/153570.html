Project Summary

Due to dire needs identified throughout its previous Child Protection and Education activities implemented in 2018, and in line with the desire to implement life-saving activities, enhance protection and strengthen coping capacities with an eye to support the latter, this proposal aims to work in hard-to-reach areas where INTERSOS has identified compounded risks and observed the impact of protracted periods of under-servicing, denoting the priority of this intervention. More specifically, INTERSOS proposes a response that is static in 5 locations: 3 locations in Ayod (Jiech, Mogok, Karmoun), Mayom (Mayom) and Leer (Mayendit) with regular field-based roving activities to other hard-to-reach areas.
INTERSOS aims to provide child protection to vulnerable, conflict-affected children through a holistic approach consisting of various activities which strengthen each other. The primary activity proposed intends to directly address life-saving needs of children through structured and comprehensive case management, coupled with PSS provision (including PFA) and FTR services for girls and boys with protection needs (UASC, CAAFAG and GBV survivors), orphans and children with other specific needs identified including children with disability. As an additional channel for the identification of protection needs as well as a space to provide PSS activities, malnutrition screenings and referrals for children, a CFS with daily activities in each location has proven to be successful. The intervention will also be characterized by a focus on community engagement activities, awareness-raising and risk mitigation achieved through a strong system of ToT whereby INTERSOS will provide training and engage community members in specific techniques for contextualized approaches to strengthen the protective environment for children. INTERSOS will also strengthen caregivers’ coping mechanisms and resilience through thematic workshops as per the needs identified in each micro-community, this is vital to maintain since children directly suffer as a consequence of distress experienced by their parents. Customary negative discipline measures can contribute to further enhancing the sense of insecurity already perceived due to the tension in the surrounded context. To ensure the protection of children throughout the referral pathway, INTERSOS also aims to provide support and training to non-CP actors in the areas of intervention
As a holistic protection intervention, the project will directly support 6,450 (1,950 adults and 4,500 children) in Ayod County, 2,150 (650 adults and 1,500 children) in Mayendit and 2,150 (650 adults and 1,500 children) in Mayom, and approximately 5,000 indirect beneficiaries in each location. While approximately 70% of the direct beneficiaries are part of the IDP community, an estimated 30% of the host community can be considered as directly targeted too. The objective of this intervention is to provide at least 1,000 children with comprehensive case management support (CP and GBV). As stated before, the past has shown that the CFS is an important part of the integrated approach of this project. Therefore, INTERSOS child protection interventions aim to reach at least 7,500 children through CBCPSS activities, through accesses to a total of 5 CFS and others. Other direct beneficiaries include all the community members targeted in relation to extending child protection by training adult caregivers and other community members on child protection, child rights, parenting skills and positive discipline methods. More specifically, numbers estimated for direct intervention include 250 adults through the participation in workshops and regular meetings, as part of the CBCPNs, 100 foster caregivers targeted to attend specific training on parenting skills and positive discipline methods. Furthermore, awareness raising activities seek to target at least 5,000 individuals across the population at large between host and hosted community.

Needs

The proposed areas of intervention are both on the frontline between the Sudan People’s Liberation Army (SPLA) forces and the SPLA-In Opposition (SPLA-IO) elements. The instability and the common attempts of SPLA-IG as well as the counter offensives of SPLA-IO is one of the most evident causes of population movement around. The serious humanitarian situation in Unity State, particularly central and southern Unity, is well articulated in the findings from a recent investigation of into fighting between Sudan People’s Liberation Army (SPLA) forces and SPLA-In Opposition (SPLA-IO) elements in Unity State released on July 10 by the UN Mission in the Republic of South Sudan (UNMISS) and the Office of the UN High Commissioner for Human Rights released. Among many other findings, the UN investigation found evidence of extreme violence against civilians in Unity, with armed actors killing more than 230 civilians, committing sexual violence against at least 120 women and girls and abducting more than 130 others. The security situation in Northern Jonglei and, specifically, in Ayod County, remains volatile with the SPLA-IO moving up North in March 2018. Population movement mainly occurs from the locations surrounding the government-controlled area toward northern or eastern side, therefore Mogok, Jiech and Karmoun can be classified as the main common destination points for IDPs. A recent study highlighted that children recruited and sued by armed groups reportedly receive the same treatment as adult soldiers, often forced to walk long distances for days and receiving inadequate food. Children under 14 often serve as bodyguards, cooks or servants for commanders. While boys are more commonly report to have received orders to kill civilians or loot property, girls are reported to have been used for sexual purposes. In May 2018, UNICEF reported a total of 806 children from the beginning of 2018 that have been released by armed groups, mainly recruited by SPLA-IO and NSF, most of them in Jonglei State but still many are recruited. While men often take part in the armed conflict or are away with the cattle of the families, women are mostly the sole breadwinners of the households and are forced to leave their children alone all day while walking long distances to fetch water, collect firewood and wild food. This leads not only to physical and emotional neglect of the children but also increases their exposure to significant protection threats such as exploitation, abuse and recruitment within armed forces or armed groups. The collection of water itself has been identified as one of the most dangerous activities which is many times a task given to young girls, exposing them to violence and child abuse. The heavy movements of IDPs during the last years and the lack of resources have increased the number of vulnerable children and, raising the importance of UASC making FTR services. Among the most urgent child protection risks identified are the high number of unaccompanied, separated children, orphans, and children-headed households. Also prevalent are high levels of psychosocial distress of the community in general, among both children and adults, given by the high level of insecurity and the spread of various forms of violence. This is exacerbated by the lack of structures able to respond to the basic needs of the population which often lead to negative coping mechanisms. All the above issues are due to several reasons, including insecurity within the area, lack of economic opportunities and resources that force movements of the population and lead to abandonment of children, lack of awareness on fundamental principles and sustainable positive coping mechanisms.

Activities

Activities related to Comprehensive Case Management- Identification, documentation, family tracing and reunification services for UASC or families of missing children conducted by CP Case Workers. - Identification of other vulnerable children (including orphans), elaboration of individual case plans, referrals and regular monitoring and follow-up visits. - Provision of safe interim care arrangements for UASC and orphans and training of foster families on principles of interim care provision and positive parenting skills. - Provision of individual material assistance to the most vulnerable UASC and vulnerable children as per their needs. - Facilitation of tracing process in close collaboration with FTR partners and accompaniment of children during reunification flights. Activities related to CBPSS activities in the CFS: - Provision of structured and unstructured PSS activities in the CFS through the use of recreational items (local games and materials) with a focus on improving children’s wellbeing. - Personal hygiene and health promotion activities in the CFS. - Provision of focus on specialized CBPSS and MHPSS activities for adolescents. Activities related to community engagement activities, awareness-raising and risk mitigation:- Establishment and strengthening of community protection mechanisms through community meetings, awareness sessions, FGDs and house-to-house visits including advocacy on the importance of child protection, the rights of the children and signs and symptoms of elevated psychological distress. - Establishment and strengthening of CBCPNs. - Workshops with CBCPNs (parents, caregivers, PTA, authorities and community duty bearers) on children’s wellbeing, needs in different development stages, risks and threats in the community, positive parenting skills through active listening and constructive conflict resolution and strategies to prevent family separation. - Awareness events, campaigns and public drama sessions on child protection issues facilitated by children and adolescents (girls and boys) attending the CFS to advocate for children’s right, inclusion of the most vulnerable children and community protection. - Initiatives designed and implemented by children and adolescents to improve safety and security in the community and safety mapping exercises. Note - the locations of intervention considered include counties in Unity to ensure the possibility of having an integrated response component connected ot the presence of INTERSOS run schools. This will only done in the case of no presence or inability of local/present/NNGO partner in the vicinity to service the specific payam's CP needs. Nevertheless this would be done with an eye to capacity building and handover

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Through participatory community engagement meetings, and also awareness and social mobilisation campaigns. Children are consulted through participatory, child friendly techniques in safe and protective environments.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages are context specific and tailored for gender and age groups. INTERSOS uses facilitation guides for children, adolescents and women which are age appropriate for facilitating sessions with children in the Women and Girls Friendly Spaces. Specific tailored tools are also used during community engagement and awareness raising activities.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

INTERSOS staff who come into contact with beneficiaries sign a Prevention of SEA Code of Conduct to ensure that they prevent sexual exploitation and abuse of project beneficiaries. Child protection and safeguarding trainings are also provided for staff and community structures that work with children. Internal trainings are also conducted to refresh staff on their obligations to prevent SEA by timely reporting cases.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Consultations were conducted at community level through focus group discussions, key informant interviews and observation. The consultations covered men, women, boys and girls as well as traditional and local leaders.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

The project conducts regular gender and age appropriate focus group discussions in order to encourage feedback from the communities. Within INTERSOS supported schools, suggestion boxes exist to allow feedback from children.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Communities are engaged in the monitoring and evaluation processes through use of participatory methodologies such as focus group discussions, community dialogues and interviews.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project conducts an initial risk analysis as part of the safety audits and needs assessment conducted at the beginning of the project. The risk analysis focuses on protection needs, available services and gaps and allows for determining the needs or special needs groups.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project identifies barriers to access during the needs assessment and also during the routine monitoring process.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project seeks to address gender and age based inequalities and protection threats through addressing the drivers of violence against children and gender based violence, particularly social norms, gender roles, community conflicts, exclusion and deprivation. Violence will be addressed through community engagement, provision of services to survivors of GBV and child victims of violence as well as strengthening of community based protection mechanisms.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project utilities protection assessments, safety audits and ongoing monitoring to assess inequalities and protection threats to children, women and their families.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Access to services will be measured through tracking the number of children provided with child protection services through the CPMIS and other monitoring processes.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Through safety audits and needs assessments, the project identifies potential safety threats to beneficiaries and implements mitigation measures.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

The project monitoring process is participatory, age and gender specific, ensuring the dignity, confidentiality and protection of beneficiaries.
