Project Summary

This project aims to facilitate the creation of a favorable protection environment in Darfur (with particular focus on North and West Darfur) to bring positive impacts on the lives of at least 45,000 crisis-affected population, including internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees. The proposed outputs and activities will contribute to all 3 Protection Cluster objectives of the 2022 Humanitarian Response Plan as well as Objective 1 and 2 of Gender-based violence (GBV) Cluster.
In North Darfur, the project will target Kabkabiya locality (Sortony and Kabkabiya town), Dar Es Salam locality (Shangil Tobaya and Abu Zereiga), El Fasher locality (Korma and Kusa), and Kutum locality (Hashaba). In West Darfur, IOM will target Kerenik locality (Abu Naema, Fedus, and Mazrub) and Geneina locality (Anjimi, Gokar and Noro).
In relation to Protection Objective 1 and GBV Objective 1, IOM will aim to improve access of vulnerable crisis-affected people, especially that of girls and women, to protection services including GBV response services. IOM will strive for improving their access to life-saving services, such as primary healthcare and non-food items (NFI) in coordination with IOM’s Health and NFI teams. IOM will also work towards strengthening the capacity of GBV service providers through trainings and deployment of social workers and psychologists. These psychologists will be also available for providing psychosocial support (PSS) to other crisis-affected population in the target areas.
To contribute to Protection Objective 2, IOM will engage in capacity building of informal mediators and dispute resolvers so as to improve the crisis-affected people’s access to community-based dispute resolution mechanisms. IOM will work with the existing committees in the communities, including, but not limited to, Native Administrations, Peaceful Co-existence Committees, Agricultural Protection Committees as well as community leaders and representatives from other community-based organizations including women’s and youth groups, and those belonging to farmers and herders. IOM staff will also contribute to “protection by presence” through monitoring the protection situations in concerned locations and collect relevant information on protection needs, gaps and trends for advocacy, programmatic and ad hoc assistance purposes.
Through this project, IOM will coordinate with its other projects under key programme areas in the target States, such as Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) and emergency response as well as with WASH and health activities, to ensure effective synergies between programme activities. In addition, IOM will work in close coordination with international and national partners to ensure project’s efficiency and effectiveness. Besides, IOM with its partners and DTM teams operating in field locations will enhance protection monitoring and help in verifying the most vulnerable households to be assisted on a priority basis.
Concerning Protection Objective 3, IOM will support the local government to strengthen their capacity and resources to improve birth registration of IDPs and service provision to people with disabilities through the provision of office equipment, capacity building trainings, and facilitating regular visits of government staff to remote areas to provide the services for the concerned population. For the activity related to civil documentation, IOM will closely coordinate with the Civil Registry and UNHCR in the target states to maximize complementarity and to avoid duplication.
In relation to the GBV Objective 2, IOM will support the establishment and/or reactivation of women’s groups and Women Centres in the target areas to ensure safe platforms are available for women and girls’ engagement and their access to the information on protection services including GBV response services. Community awareness raising sessions, life skills/recreational activities will be organized as part of Women Centres activities.

1 - Needs

A protection environment remains fragile in Darfur region, in particular North Darfur and West Darfur states where a multitude of protection risks exist, including inter-communal violence, presence of non-state armed groups, and activities of foreign armed groups, given that the States share porous land borders with Chad, Egypt and Libya. Gaps in the protection environment have been widened due to the withdrawal of UNAMID at the end of 2020. In the past few years, serious inter-communal violence incidents erupted throughout Darfur, including West and North Darfur states. Many of such inter-communal violence cases originated in disputes over access to natural resources (i.e., land and water) and are exacerbated by lack of State presence and rule of law. Additionally, IDP farmers across Darfur are frequently prevented from accessing their farmlands, especially by nomadic herders and subjected to physical attacks, threats, harassment and the destruction of crops. While there is the need for a stronger presence of law enforcement and judicial institutions in Darfur, factors such as land occupation and lack of basic services constitute a considerable obstacle to peaceful coexistence between communities. This points to the urgent need for improving access to community-based dispute resolution mechanisms among the population in the target states so that the people can resolve disputes in a peaceful way, before they get escalated and become violent. This needs to be coupled with sensitization of the people to the available informal and formal dispute resolution mechanisms. Respect for peace-building and peaceful co-existence need to be nurtured from early ages, thus sensitization of school children to these topics is also important. In line with the National Plan for the Protection of Civilians, the proposed action aims to promote peaceful coexistence between communities, by enhancing dispute resolutions mechanisms and improving access to basic services. In particular, the interventions enhance and/or reactivate, where necessary, dispute resolution mechanisms, including, inter alia, peaceful coexistence committees, agricultural protection committees, women’s groups. Moreover, the proposed action intends to strengthen protection monitoring and protection by presence in target locations, especially during the current transition period, to ensure the rights of women, men, boys and girls are upheld. The fragile protection environment in both states is hindering in particular way vulnerable crisis-affected people’s access to protection services. Sudan’s 2021 Gender-based violence (GBV) response dashboard indicates both West and North Darfur states as the GBV hotspots where many interventions are planned. IOM’s GBV expert, who carried out her own assessment during the visit to West Darfur in June 2021, confirmed the significant GBV risks in the state and advised IOM to support the capacity building of GBV service providers, deploy GBV social workers and psychologists and create safe spaces for women and girls with access to the information on available GBV and protection services. The protracted crisis situations in Darfur also hamper the people’s access to basic services. Many people in remote areas, especially IDPs, lack access to birth registration, which is creating a situation where the people cannot access basic services which require birth certificates, such as public health insurance and enrolling children in local schools. Services that cater to the needs of people with disabilities are limited, and given their specific vulnerabilities, there is a need for building capacity of health and social welfare staff on addressing their needs.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Output 1 (contributing to Protection and GBV Objectives 1): Vulnerable crisis- affected people in the target areas, especially girls and women, have access to protection services including GBV response services. To achieve this output, IOM will undertake: 1.1. Strengthen access to primary healthcare services among the target population in coordination with IOM’s Health team. 1.2. Strengthen access to NFI support (incl. dignity kits) in displacement contexts, in coordination with IOM’s NFI team. 1.3. Capacity building of GBV service providers (life-saving response) including referrals to external specialists, caring for child and adolescent survivors, PSS, and GBV case management (incl. non-therapeutic interventions to help cope with stressors). All PSS activities will be in line with the IOM Manual on Community-Based Mental Health and PSS in Emergencies and Displacement 1.4. Deployment of experienced GBV social workers and psychologists for providing case management support and PSS (e.g. psychological counselling, peer-support groups, organization of socio-relational activities). The psychologists will also provide PSS to other crisis-affected population, including the youth, elderly and people with disabilities. 1.5. Enhancement of existing referral mechanisms to address GBV issues and cases. Output 2.1 (contributing to Protection Objective 2): People in the target areas have access to community-based dispute resolution mechanisms. To achieve this output, IOM will undertake: 2.1.1. Build capacity of informal mediators and dispute resolvers, including the representatives from existing various committees in the target areas and other community leaders such as village chiefs, imams, women group leaders and youth group leaders. 2.1.2. Raise awareness of available informal dispute resolution avenues among the target population. 2.1.3. Sensitize school youth to the importance of peacebuilding and co-existence. Output 2.2 (contributing to Protection Objective 2): “Protection by presence” and protection monitoring mechanisms are enhanced, in coordination with local communities and partner organizations. To achieve this output, IOM will undertake: 2.2.1. Monitor the protection situation in concerned locations and collect relevant information on protection needs, gaps and trends for advocacy, programmatic and ad hoc assistance purposes. IOM will enhance protection monitoring mechanisms, tracking population movements in synergy with its DTM programme, and identifying vulnerable persons in need of assistance. Relevant findings will be shared regularly with the Protection Sector/PWGs/GBV Sub PWGs of the targets states. Output 3 (contributing to Protection Objective 3): The local government has the capacity and resources to improve birth registration of IDPs and service provision to people with disabilities. To achieve this output, IOM will undertake: 3.1. Increase the local government’s capacity to improve access to birth registration services among IDPs (provision of computers, printers, internet access, a generator and solar power). 3.2 Support the local government in organizing a mobile registration team to remote localities (provision of transportation / motorbikes). 3.3 Capacity building of health and social welfare staff on addressing the needs of persons with disabilities. 3.4 Support the health and social welfare staff to make regular visits to people with disabilities living in remote localities (provision of transportation / motorbikes). Output 4 (contributing to GBV Objective 2): Safe platforms for engagement are made available for women and girls to access information related to women’s empowerment and available protection services including GBV services. To achieve this output, IOM will undertake: 4.1 Support the establishment and/or reactivation of women’s groups and Women Centres in the target areas. 4.2. Awareness raising of available GBV and protection services among women and girls.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project will solely focus on protection issues including GBV response, thus when designing and implementing all project activities, protection of the crisis-affected population, in particular girls, women and persons with disabilities, will be mainstreamed. When designing and implementing the activities that will have direct impact on GBV survivors, IOM will ensure to mainstream survivor-centered and trauma- informed approaches. Furthermore, IOM will keep local authorities/communities and other relevant partners involved and/or informed on the implementation of field activities as well as on the selection criteria in order to avoid risks of tensions with local communities. Moreover, vulnerable individuals from the receiving communities will also be targeted. Key actions will therefore include: identifying local authorities and community leaders and strengthen and support their role where possible; displaying and/or explaining criteria in identifying individuals to be selected for the activities (for example, for the distribution of NFIs); and, including individuals from any group (IDPs, returnees, vulnerable individuals form the local communities). Other relevant actions will include: organizing distributions at the safest time of the day; providing information on protection from exploitation and abuse and other protection related concern; involving in the project’s implementation male and female staff; and, keeping a list of individuals involved and related data confidential.
