Project Summary

SEMA aim to respond through this project to protection concerns through integrated Child Protection program into SEMA’s current Nutrition and health programs in the same targeted locations (AZZAZ-ALBAB-AFRIN) through center-based activities in child friendly spaces, community-based interventions (CPN), and community outreach activities, using different modality of intervention for each type of service .Also SEMA through its IP HRO, will respond to the new IDP’s in Jisr-Ash-Shugur through outreach teams ,delivering a package of integrated ,comprehensive protection services including CP services.
The outreach teams will also do awareness raising on Mine Risk Education, Recreational activities, Semi-structured PSS, CP, GBV, Civil Documentation, and Alternative Care in schools, orphanage, alternative care centers, IDP camps and communities. Case workers will identify, document and refer cases that need specialized services to the appropriate service providers through case management team who will conform to CP and GBV sub-cluster SOPs, and referral pathway, as well as UNICEF Sub-cluster IEC materials, targeting children, children with disability, parents, caregivers and community stakeholders including religious leaders, local council members, teachers, etc. In addition to the above, SEMA will deliver structured PSS activities to children, adolescents and adults, psychodrama sessions, recreational activities, early childhood development activities, parenting skills sessions and life skills. Boys and girls will have access to SEMA CFSs for five days per week. PSS workers will be trained on the CPMS, CPIE, CSGP, PFA, GBVMS, CM, FTR, CAAFAG, PSS, PSEA..
SEMA will collect data of children, who were formally associated with armed groups (CAAFAG), and were self or informally released and are back in their communities. In order to best respond to this, SEMA will establish Child Protection Networks (CPN) in each location, and will support two initiatives suggested by the CPN related to CP concerns within the community5, to identify protection concerns especially of vulnerabilities, and to conduct active family tracing including reunification, follow up and the final visits to assure the process is done as planned.
SEMA will follow up the factors that lead to the child labor or recruitment whether children were forcibly recruited, join armed groups in order to escape poverty, hunger or violence, or out of vengeance, then collaborate with other Education and FSL Programs available in the area, to provide YEP activities to adolescents. The identified children and adolescents will receive educational support packages of admission, school uniforms or respectable clothes, stationary, and access to livelihood opportunities.
SEMA will identify and register UASC Cases. All collected information will be shared with FTR task force, the case will be managed by case workers.
Furthermore, SEMA will provide Child Protection Monitoring Task Force (CPMTF) with surveys on CP concerns on a monthly basis. And will seek the participation in the regular CPMTF meeting.
In order to assure the effective participation of the beneficiaries in the project cycle, SEMA will conduct FGDs for children and mothers, since the inception, about the introduced activities. Then, will follow up with surveys and RFM, considering taking related corrective action where needed.

Needs

A generation of Syrian children faces psychological ruin and ever-increasing danger, with escalating threats of a litany of abuses including forced labor, marriages, food scarcity and minimal access to health or education, with child deaths soaring by 50% (in 2017) which was the worst year of the war for Syrian children, with 910 killed, and a tripled number of recruited children since 2015. 361 children were injured, 244 children were detained, and over two million school-age children are out-of-school1. According to Humanitarian Inclusive, 99% of the assessed communities reported at least indirect evidence of contamination, 54% report presence of Explosive Remnants of War (ERW). This has led to growing risks of killing and maiming of girls and boys due to the persistent use of explosive weapons in civilian areas2. The prevalence of child labor appears to be the predominant reason for the withdrawal of children from schools in many parts of Syria. As many households have now lost their source of livelihoods, children often are taken to the labor force in order to provide their families. 86% of assessed communities in Aleppo Governorate, reported child labor preventing school attendance was an issue of concern and adolescent boys 12-14 and 15-17 years were considered the most affected child population groups, Moreover, the reported “issue of concern” has significantly varied as the follows: 38% of child recruitment, 25% of unaccompanied and separated children, 30% of family violence, 50% of child marriage3. 79% of lack/ loss of civil documentation as a result of the long-standing conflict in Syria since 2011 that affected the majority of civilians with frequent displacement movement towards surrounding towns and villages3. According to the HNO 2018, people in need in northern Syria reached an overwhelming number of 2.086.000 people, with women and girls accounting for 49% (1.022.140) of the affected population while children accounted for 50% (1.043.000). Protection is the most needed service in Idleb Governorate with 1,545,600 individuals in need of one or more protection services.According to the PNO 2018, protection needs are characterized as ‘major’, with child labor preventing school attendance and loss of civil documentation, early marriage and HLP issues reported as common. According to HNO 2018, recruitment and use of children by armed actors continue to be reported. Children, particularly adolescent boys, are used in combat roles as well in support roles. 18 per cent of the 300 verified cases of child recruitment involved children under the age of 15. Payment of salaries, more so than ideology, continues to be a major contributing factor. Family and community influence, indoctrination, abduction, arrest and coercion of children are also factors. The UN estimates that an anticipated Syrian Arab Republic Government (SARG) offensive on Idlib Governorate could displace up to 900,000 people in northwestern Syria due to insecurity and shifting lines of control. In preparation, the UN recently released a Northwestern Response Readiness Plan to provide humanitarian assistance to populations affected by an offensive. Relief organizations—including U.S. Government (USG) partners—are pre-positioning relief commodities and preparing to expand response efforts to mitigate the effects of potential loss of access to affected areas. (Syria - complex emergency – USAID).

Ensure consultation with communities and neighboring communities related to location of services to understand perspectives of communities are all understood and addressed. Including access barriers for those with injuries/disabilities and communication mechanisms for persons with visual/hearing impairments. Utilize sector agreed key messages to ensure consistency and inform sector of discrepancies or if new messaging is required. 3. Girls, boys, women, and men’s physical security is put at risk as a result of the violence associated with civilian populations/spaces being targeted by armed parties to the conflict; a lack of consideration for the safety and security to access/receive services; lack of consideration of contextual information (e.g. aerial bombardment) and targeting of civilian infrastructure en route to, during and directly after service provision LIKELIHOOD: medium Impact: medium Mitigation Consider conflict patterns and contextual analysis as part of decisions for establishing a fixed site or mobile service provision. Engage with diverse members of community on safety and security in the community (to ensure a diverse perspective and perceptions are considered). Ensure consultation and mobilization of key persons in the community. Provide culturally adapted information on key child protection issues and responsibilities of different people in the growth and development of children through CPN’s. Availability of contingency plan and duty of care. 4.Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information are linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups. LIKELIHOOD: High Impact: High Mitigation Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and who services are for. Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1 : Changing the security situation that interfere with implementation of the project.Mitigation Strategies: We will establish a response plan that can be activated according to the situation. Designing the activities in a flexible way where we can change the way of conducting the activity according to the situation so as to achieve the wanted results. and we can focus on more suitable activities like doing PFA. Stopping the activities for short period. moving the center to another place if deemed necessary after negotiation with OCHA..Risk: The local authorities decide to prevent us from implementing the project or part of it.Mitigation: as preventive measures we will coordinate with the local authorities and negotiate with them about the best way to implement the project and discuss the importance the project that will help. .Risk 3: changing in the attitudes or priorities of the population that will affect their willingness to benefit from the project activities.Mitigation strategies: Will work on awareness raising throughout the project period and will get the feedback from the beneficiaries about the services and adapt accordingly . Risk 4 : Due to lack of experienced people in the targeted communities, IDP may be recruited based on their previous experience.Mitigation Strategies: equality in opportunities among host community and IDPs will be ensured throughout selecting people who are residing in the targeted area regardless of being IDP or from host community. Job vacancies advertisements will be shared with the local councils. Risk 5 : Risk of triggering sensitivity with the local vendors during the procurement and purchasing materials from outside the targeted areas.Mitigation Strategies: All materials that are available in the targeted area should be purchased locally in accordance with SEMA procurement policies. Risk 6 : Risk of demands on case management fund by people who are not in comply with the required standards. Mitigation Strategies: The influence of the local council and community leaders will be utilized in order to justify that this amount of money is not relief assistance and it should be in accordance with specific criteria to target the fragile categories.
