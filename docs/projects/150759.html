Project Summary

PUI will build on its extensive experience in shelter rehabilitation in the Gaza Strip and in the West Bank (providing emergency responses and protective measures to victims of settler violence) to improve the living conditions and increase protection of highly vulnerable Palestinian families who suffer from settler violence and inability to rehabilitate their shelters. PUI will coordinate its intervention with key actors working under the Shelter Cluster and within the area of intervention. PUI will partner with a registered cluster partner to become an implementing partner of this project. The registered cluster partner will take the lead in implementing this project within their geographical coverage where their teams are deployed to implement their programmes in Tubas Governorate, while PUI will implement the project in the areas of its geographical coverage of Tulkarem, Salfit and Nablus.
Protection threats, as well as vulnerabilities affect groups differently. The communities identified are mainly herding and Bedouin communities mostly living in shacks and tents. The types of shelter and their specific setting increase household’s exposure to potential consequences of rough weather conditions such as floodings, winter storms and extreme heat in the summer as well as access to basic services and geographical isolation. When it comes to settler related incidents isolated and vulnerable households are more likely to be attacked by settlers and most often in times where only a few people are around, in many cases only lonely women, elderly people and children.
Protection threats combined with the absence of adequate shelters are particular hard on the most vulnerable groups such as women, elderly, children and PwDs, which the project will take into consideration by including different groups in all stages of the project. Especially due to an often sharp gendered division in housework, women’s participation in all phases of the programing will be essential as well as the focus on special needs of particular groups (e.g. the household’s different needs of space for privacy and socializing to ensure children’s possibilities of finding space for studying for instance). Special concerns to risks of GBV will be counted for in all phases of the program as well as if any signs of GBV taking place within households are identified. The staff implementing the project will be trained to identify and take proper actions as non-GBV-specialists. In cases where outstanding needs are identified during the action they will be referred to relevant stakeholders in agreement with the households/communities.
The project includes the rehabilitation and/or upgrading of shelters, which aims to reduce the exposure of targeted beneficiaries to both man-made and natural disasters, weather conditions, risk of water borne diseases and health problems, by enabling them to live with adequate standards. In addition, the action includes the provision of protective assets to households most at risk of settler violence. Specifically, the project intends to rehabilitate/upgrade 40 shelters in the first year and provide protective assets to 40 households in 23 communities in four governorates of the West Bank: Nablus, Tulkarem, Salfit, and Tubas. Special concerns to risks of GBV will be counted for in all phases of the program as well as if any signs of GBV taking place within households are identified. The staff implementing the project will be trained to identify and take proper actions as non-GBV-specialists. In cases where outstanding needs are identified during the action they will be referred to relevant stakeholders in agreement with the households/communities.
This project complements the work done by PUI under the West Bank Protection Consortium, which consist in providing emergency response to victims of settler violence incidents and protective measures at community level. PUI will avoid any duplication of activity by providing protective assets at household level only.
The action will contribute to the following strategic and shelter cluster objectives:
SO2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL.
SO3: The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued.
CO: Support to vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns.
Finally, the project complies with the minimum SPHERE standards for adequate housing, Shelter Cluster minimum agreed standards, gender, and adequate access to housing by people with disabilities.

Needs

In 2018, PUI participated in a Substandard Shelter Assessment led by the Shelter Cluster in area C of the West Bank. The methodology included a unified assessment form for all partners (PUI, ACF, GVC, PHC, MAAN, PARC, UAWC and ACTED). 10 indicators, based on technical criteria (no socio-economic data was collected) assessed the vulnerability extracted from the minimum standards. In total, 264 communities were included in the assessment, out of an initial list of 305 (41 communities with no significant shelter vulnerabilities were excluded). Out of the 20,846 residential shelters surveyed, 16% (3,425) of them were considered substandard and in need of rehabilitation. Particular vulnerable group were herding and Bedouin communities (representing 60% of substandard shelters). PUI surveyed 55 communities in the North of the West Bank in the governorates of Nablus, Salfit, Tulkarem, Qalqelia, and Tubas. Among them, PUI identified 23 communities most at risk of settler violence (i.e. suffering daily, weekly, monthly or less frequently settler attacks), based on OCHA Settler Violence Severity Interactive Map (https://public.tableau.com/profile/ocha.opt#!/vizhome/SettlerViolence/DashSv). Within these 23 communities, 778 families (for a total population number of 5590) resided in 838 residential shelters. Out of these 838 shelters, 231 were considered substandard. The type of shelters in need of rehabilitation were as follow:• Block with zinc roof: 25 shelters• Concrete with zinc ceiling: 6 shelters• Concrete: 65 shelters• Tent: 78 shelters• Zinc and tent: 41 shelters• Zinc: 16 shelters The findings of the variability to shelter minimum standards are summarized below for the 23 communities surveyed by PUI:1. 61% have frame materials that are not able to support the weight of the roof and any additional live or dead load expected during diﬀerent seasons like wind and snow (engineering experience)2. 87% have roof which is not suitable to control heat (thermal insulation).3. 83% have roof and external walls which are not sealed and there are leaks.4. 87% have external walls which are not insulated for thermal comfort.5. 65% don’t have a minimum 5 m2 of covered space per person (including kitchen, corridors, bathroom, or toilet facilities).6. 70% don’t have smooth, level, anti-slip ﬂoor in all spaces (suitable for children, elderly and disabled movement) with minimum one layer protection from natural soil.7. 70% don’t have solid and lockable external door into the shelter.8. 78% have window units which are not sealed (from rain water and wind).9. 61% don’t have functioning mobile or ﬁxed internal or external latrine per shelter.10. 65% don’t have kitchen with adequate space which may be located inside the house unit or outside the unit based on household preferences, including counter and connected to water sources and drainage system. Next step will be to identify the most vulnerable households in these 23 communities who suffer from settler violence and inability to rehabilitate and upgrade their shelters. The assessment of specific needs and selection of beneficiaries will be based on a comprehensive technical assessment at household level and socio-economic criteria. Beneficiaries will be consulted on the most appropriate intervention that will have an impact on their shelter conditions. Moreover the local committees, representing the diversity of the communities and established at community level will be consulted to identify common needs regarding the implementation. In addition, PUI-registered cluster partner will include households to identify the appropriate responses / special needs for people with disabilities, women, girls...etc. PUI-registered cluster partner will scrutinize every part of the process to ensure that interventions will not negatively affect the communities. Furthermore, whenever designing an intervention, a focus will be given to the legal context as the whole project is targeting Area C.

Activities

Activity 1: Community outreach, beneficiary selection and technical assessment.PUI and the registered cluster partner will reach out to the 23 targeted communities through different channels such as posters in public infrastructure, mosques and village councils as well as through CBOs with information regarding project’s objectives and criteria for eligibility. In addition, local committees will be established at community level to secure active community participation in all phases of the action. The local committees will reflect the diversity of the community including representation of different groups based on gender, age, and disability. Technical assessments including socio-economic criteria will be conducted at household level to assess the needs and identify potential beneficiaries. This activity will be implemented by PUI and the registered cluster partner based on complementary capacities.Activity 2: Shelter rehabilitation at household level.PUI and the partner will upgrade 40 substandard housing units located in the 23 targeted communities. Women, men, girls and boys will be consulted during all the project phases (targeting, design and implementation). Special care will be granted to specific needs in relation to the different groups and in their use of the shelters both for privacy, socializing and work. This project will place particular attention on families with people with disabilities and female-headed households as these are the beneficiary families with members spending more time at home in the area of intervention. Special attention will be put on specific needs of vulnerable beneficiaries and their participation will be encouraged for the design and implementation of activities. PUI and the partner will consider both in-kind interventions and cash when the in-kind component is not applicable. Activity 2: Protective assets at household level.PUI and the partner will provide protective assets to 40 households residing in the 23 targeted communities. These communities are often subjected to recurrent IHL/IHRL violations (e.g. harassment/incursions by settlers or IF, stoning/arson, cutting of waterlines) due to their geographic proximity to settlements, outposts and military bases. PUI will develop protective responses together with the local committees and the targeted households to reduce the magnitude or/and likelihood of incidents, through the provision of protective assets (such as fences, tents for water tanks, window shutters, fencing, first aid kits, and fire extinguishers. Emphasis will be given to improve the feeling of safety and dignity of the most vulnerable of the households. The above activities will be divided by both actors based on geographical division respecting the partner's expertise in the Northern Jordan valley and PUI in the governorates of Nablus, Salfit, and Tulkarem. All phases of the project will be implemented in collaboration with local committees established at community levels. PUI and the partner will ensure during the different phases of the project to do no harm to the beneficiaries. It will ensure the beneficiaries are aware of the interventions and in case there is any implications that might affect them. In cases of households with pending legal procedures, lawyers will be consulted before any intervention. PUI – the partner will keep assessing the interventions and adapt to any challenges they might encounter during the implementation through continuously consulting the affected communities. PUI has an active complaint and response mechanism in the West Bank for its current activities. PUI will scale up this platform to ensure the communities benefited from the shelter project can access all necessary information and give feedback that require additional follow up. In the second year, PUI and the partner will rehabilitate 40 shelters and provide protective assets to 40 households to reach to the target indicated in the need assessment.

Indicators

PUI and the partner M&E teams will develop a monitoring and evaluation plan at the beginning of the project to manage the process of assessing and reporting progress against project targets, insuring in each step disaggregated representation. This plan will describe the whole M&E system, including indicators, tools, breakdown of responsibilities and communication channels within the organizations and between them. • Monthly monitoring visits will be conducted to follow up the progress of project activities and to ensure that the activities are being implemented in accordance with the work plan developed at the beginning of the activity. Written report summarizing the main findings will be issued after each monitoring visit.• Introductory and information meetings with targeted population and community representatives will be arranged to provide accessible and timely information on organizational procedures, types of project activities, beneficiary selection process, criteria of selection, etc.• A complaints and feedback mechanism will be set up to make sure that the opinions of target population is heard and taken into consideration. Complaints regarding the beneficiary selection process or suggestions related to the design or the implementation of project activities will be timely responded to.• Focus group discussions and/or semi structured surveys will be carried out to assess the impact of project activities against baseline survey results.Indicators- By the end of the project 85% of beneficiaries (disaggregated between age, gender and PwD) report positive impacts on their privacy, health, safety, and/or well-being thanks to the implemented upgrading works.- By the end of the project 85% of the beneficiaries (disaggregated between age, gender and PwD) report feeling more secure after receiving protective assets. - By the end of the project 85% beneficiaries (disaggregated between age, gender and PwD) are satisfied with the house upgrading works.- By the end of the project 85% of the PwD/women/children/elderly people reported that their needs were met for the rehabilitation of the shelter. - By the end of the project 85% of the PwD/women/children/elderly people reported that their needs were met for the provision of protective assets.- All local committees founded for the purpose of the project include a diverse representation of the communities.In addition to PUI’s own indicators, Shelter Cluster’s specific indicators will also be measured (see section below).
