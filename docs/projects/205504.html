Project Summary

South Sudan continues to face multiple intersecting crises. Insecurity, subnational violence, regional and national macroeconomic challenges compounded by the global economic downturn, the effects of climate change and the ongoing Sudan conflict, all adversely impact the WASH outlook of South Sudanese families. (WFP South Sudan Situation Report #315)
RCO through our vast experience in emergencies proposes viable and holistic actions that aims to contribute to the reduction of morbidity and mortality attributable to the consumption and use of unsafe water, the lack of or inadequate sanitation facilities during emergency situations and inadequate hygiene practices. The project will respond to the prevailing WASH gaps affecting the vulnerable populations in most complicated emergency zones – replicating our past experiences and lesson learnt in both WASH in emergency and WASH in development. RCO’s project seeks to target 15,495 beneﬁciaries amongst the most vulnerable emergency affected IDPs, returnees from Sudan and host communities directly affected by the floods & conﬂict with integrated response in line with 2024 HRP WASH cluster strategic objectives.
Akobo and Nyirol counties are among the other counties in the country where WASH needs are dire particularly among host communities, internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees from Sudan. This is attributed to by the impact of inter communal clashes and the ongoing conflict experienced Sudan that led to influx of high number of returnees that have overwhelmed the already limited existing WASH infrastructures.
The project seeks to Contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through WASH integrated programming with Nutrition (WASHCO1) by providing WASH services at OTP/SC centers and PHCC/PHCU health facilities as well as addressing the root causes of active of WASH related diseases and protection especially among IDPs, Sudan-returnees, women and girls who face GBV, a key protection concern while they trekking for long distances in search for water among others.
To ensure this succeeds, the project will train WASH hygiene promoters on core WASH’GBV competencies including referrals and case reporting and they will in turn train others and conduct community mass hygiene promotion campaigns.
Boreholes and hand dug wells will be rehabilitated with a flood-resilient design (WASHCO4)
by the trained pump mechanics and Water Management Committees who will be flushing out to ensure they (bore holes & hand dug well) are well cleaned and removed turbidity by air compressors and treated with chlorine for disinfection and improve water quality as well as replacing the worn-out parts.
RCO will address the sanitation situation both at the communal level and institutions like OTP/SC, schools and PHCC/PHCU health centers through rehabilitations of gender/disability segregated latrines with a flood-resilient design (WASHCO4) with doors and locks as well as installation of a hand washing facilities at each latrine.
Finally, the project will synergize and integrate multi sector response by coordinating with counterpart agencies on ground to ensure effectiveness and referrals of other cluster objectives in addition to mainstreaming cross-cutting programmatic approaches like COVID-19, AAP principles, protection including GBV/ Sexual exploitation mitigation measures, Do No Harm, conﬂict context analysis, gender equity, HIV/AIDS awareness and prevention and sustainability.

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to face multiple intersecting crises which is having a profound and adverse impact on food security in the country. Some of the difficulties the nation is experiencing include insecurity, subnational violence, developing regional and national socioeconomic challenges exacerbated by the global economic slump, and the effects of climate change.The ongoing conflict in Sudan continues to exacerbate an already dire situation, forcing thousands of people to cross into South Sudan. As of 31 August, 252,242 people had crossed into South Sudan, and is anticipated to rise to at least 600,000 through end of the year. Of these, 91 percent were South Sudanese returning to a country hosting over 300,000 refugees and 2.3 million internally displaced, with limited WASH facilities and strained livelihood opportunities. Access to primary WASH services for people living in South Sudan and new arrivals remains strained, further complicating the provision of humanitarian assistance. (WFP South Sudan Situation Report #316)Akobo & Nyirol counties counties are historically under-developed and seriously affected by insecurity and intercommunal conflict, now exacerbated by influx of refugees and returnees from Sudan and currently from Ethiopia due to ongoing conflict in Gambela region of Ethiopia. Further, the routine revenge killings and cattle raiding displaces communities in these cointies, forcing them to concentrate in smaller locations with limited access to clean water, hygiene and sanitation facilities, etc. Climatic shocks such as flooding, unseasonal rains and dry spells as well as crop and animal diseases and pests have reduced food availability. This has contributed to deteriorating IPC 4 and beyond as well as acute malnutrition situations in these counties. These counties are currently characterized by limited access to clean water, relying on seasonal streams, unsafe ground water, unprotected wells, and rampant open defecation. Which has been the main course of waterborne and diarrheal diseases in the region. Notably, poor sanitation coverage and lack of hand washing facilities, poor knowledge and practices on personal and environmental further fuel the situation which constitute to nearly 50% of the total AWD and cholera cases. (South Sudan Situation Reports on Cholera 2023). Latrine coverage in the target counties remains critically low (3%); thus, open defecation is predominant in these areas and considering the current heavy rains and flooding, water sources may get contaminated by fecal matter hence potential for water borne disease epidemics such as cholera. The available limited facilities are not gender disaggregated and do not take care of the needs of those with disabilities. Community Knowledge Attitudes and Practices related to water safety, COVID-19, cholera preparedness/control, hygiene and sanitation practices is still low, with open defecation, lack of hand washing, poor disposal of solid waste etc being widespread. (FSNMS round 28 report). Deteriorating WASH conditions are further exacerbated by increased cases of protection concerns among girls, boys, men and women with subsequent conflict related sexual violence and abuse. Women and girls have been particularly vulnerable as they move long unsafe distances in search of water and other social amenities Should these conditions persist, the food security, WASH and IPC situation is expected to deteriorate further.

2. Project activities

1. Conduct project introductory sessions with the community beneficiaries including their representatives to share the project objectives and buy in. 2. Carry out GBV risk analysis with girls and women and RCO female staffs and GBV staff in the community, schools, OTP/TSFP centers and market centers and map safe areas of implementation so as to select the safe locations and rehabilitate 60 flood-resilient boreholes and water points to ensure fully functional and equitable access to safe and suﬃcient water to vulnerable and affected 60,000 beneﬁciaries in the target counties. 3. Carry out risk analysis with girls and women and RCO female staffs in the community, schools, OTP/TSFP centers and market centers and rehabilitate of 120 safe and lockable latrines with flood resistant nature that are gender and disability tailored and equipping them with hand-washing facilities to ensure 60,000 targeted beneﬁciaries practice safe sanitation that is digniﬁed and gender appropriate. 4. Sanitation - Carry out gender appropriate response starting from a risk assessment, for secure access, location and design of water points, toilets and showers in in target counties. 5. Provision of WASH NFIs, including WASH/Hygiene and dignity kits to 5000 parents of children admitted for SAM and discharged at OTP/SC till the household level 6. Mobilize and train 300 hygiene promoters/ Health/Nutrition workers/volunteers trained on the WASH minimum package of activities 7. Carry out hygiene promotion campaigns among 60,000 beneﬁciaries on appropriate hygiene practices to prevent and mitigate WASH related diseases. 8. Provide timely WASH minimum package of interventions targeting 10,000HHs on an ad-hoc basis, adapted to the speciﬁcities of the related-outbreaks, at an institution level as well as for the wider community in the target counties. 9. Train 60 Water Management Committees to manage and maintain the 60 rehabilitated water points. 10. Train enumerators and hand pump mechanics who will help the community in borehole mapping and repair of the boreholes and protected hand dug wells with a flood-resilient design to ensure sustainability of the project. 11. Distribute WASH NFIs including Pur, Satchets, MHM kits (to women and girls at adolescent stage) and jerry cans to the identiﬁed vulnerable communities in the targeted states. 12. Training of adolescent girls on Menstrual Hygiene management by female WASH staff as well distributing of menstrual hygiene kits both at household and institutional level. 13. Establishment of hand washing stations both at the community and institutions to enable hand washing practices are adhered to. The hand washing facilities will encourage change of attitude and behavior towards hand washing and hence improved hygiene in general. 14.Beneﬁciary identiﬁcation, registration and provision of unconditional cash transfers and vouchers to 200 households
