Project Summary

According to FSC, there are estimated 3,541,095 people in need of urgent food secuty intervetions in Somalia, 68% of whom are host communities & about 1,034,379 (29%) people being IDPs while the rest constituting 52, 984, 1.5 %, are refugees & asylum seekers) and 38,895 (1.1%) being refugees returnees. According to Somalia 2020 Post Gu Food Security and Nutrition Outcomes and Projections (30 September 2020), in pastoral communities in northern Somalia, the erratic past rainfal patterns, stretching from 2017 have affected quality sustained pasture and water for the livestock. In Sanaag, the anticipated La Nina will most likely produce below average rainfall in 2020 with FSC projecting a most likely devastating drought in 2021. There are estimated 53,679 people in need(PiN) in Laasqoray district in Sanaag out of which 47,436 are host community and the remainder - 6,242 people displaced. Out of this population, 37,593 directly derive their daily livelihood from livestock production. There are about 32,000 combined population from Sanaag region who are in emergency (drawn from urban, rural and IDP communities).
The onset of Covid-19 not only affected the economic activities and lowering the cashflow system within the already poor communities, it also restricted movements of the pastoralists and their livestocks in search of water which further excerbated the already precarious food security and livelihood situation. The responses below are structured to support efforts in Laasqoray in Sanaag.
475 households (2,850 beneficiaries) drawn from the most affected pastoral HHs will be targeted through unconditional cash transfer. This target of the MPC is to enable the vulnerable target HHs to have access to nutritious food and other basic needs in their local markets. The cash transfer provides the beneficiaries with the robust ability to identify and prioritize their needs and as well purchase the most pressing food commodities and household needs.
The project will target, and identify, vulnerable HHs and host communities using needs-based inclusive community based targeting approach with the support of village elders and local authority with a special focus on women-headed hoseholds, HHs with malnourished children under the age of five, disable and elderly people with no income. These beneficiaries will receive unconditional cash transfer value of $110 based on Somalia Cash and Market Quarterly Dashboard (June-June 2019)
Furthermore, the organization will select 275 households (1,650 beneficiaries) for conditional assistance. The targeted beneficiaries will engage in rehabilitating community assets like water catchment, feeder roads, canal rehabilitation and market rehabilitation. Moreover, able bodied members of the target HHs will carry out physical and manual activities. The rehabilitation of the community owned assets is set help the community to have resilience against possible shocks. The conditional activities will be conducted using MoH approved Covid-19 prevention protcals including social distancing, using face maks, sanitising before the work begins and not sharing tools.
Moreover, ADEC will provide fodder to 300 HHs (3,000 livestock) comprised of goats and sheep with each household allocated fodder to 10 heads of goat/sheep during targeted dry seasosn. The livestock input (fodder) will be given to pastoral households who cannot afford to purchase fodder from the local market for their livestock and are extremly vulnerable. Leveraging the local cultural onneness, the project will train the beneficiary HHs to extend form of help to at least a nother HH once they stabilized, have built assets and can access fodder. This is expected to have impact on the livestock productivity i.e. milk production during the lean season hence strengthens household’s resilience against food security shocks. The CFW participants will be mainly semi-skilled and works for 15days/month at the rate of $5 per day.

Project Activities

Activity1: Unconditional Cash Transfer Activity 1.1: BENEFICIARIES IDENTIFICATION Identification of 475 most vulnerable households preferably PLW and children <5 years - Verification of beneficiaries, sensitization on project modalities. The initial community mobilization exercise will include creation of awareness on aims and objectives of the project and those targeted to receive the support being provided. The criteria will include gender disaggregated needs targeting. Beneficiaries will be registered and profiled disaggregated by sex and age as well as asset ownership. Selection will prioritize most food insecure, poor women or men headed households, aged and physically challenged, IDPs and returnees. The action will endeavor to attain 70% of the beneficiaries’ households to be female headed. The selection will be done with full participation of village leaders and other stakeholders. During the registration, ADEC ensures all beneficiaries have mobile account matching with their name where they can easily receive their money without travelling. ACTIVITY 1.2: SIGNING AGREEMENT FRAMEWORK. Identify and signing of framework agreements with Local Service Provider (Xawaala) to transfer the cash to the beneficiaries. A pre-qualification exercise will be conducted for all potential service providers that have linkage with the targeted locations. Activity ACTIVITY1.3: CASH TRANSFER Provide 475 vulnerable households in humanitarian crisis with cash through cash transfer system. They will received MPC to access food and other basic needs from their local market. The current recommended transfer value for multipurpose cash is $110 for Sanaag region, therefore each target household will received $110 per month. The seasonal calendar will dictate the period three months that the food vouchers will be distributed to avoid impacting negatively on productive activities and in order to promote self-dependency. A post distribution monitoring will be conducted after every cycle of voucher distribution. ACTIVITY 2: CONDITIONAL CASH TRANSFER (CFW) Activity 2.1: MAPPING INFRASTRUCTURE FOR REHABILITATION The identification will start with community leaders and community committee providing information on the infrastructure they prefer to be rehabilitated. Activity 2.2: Engage 275 HH in rehabilitation of productive infrastructure over a period of 6 months through CFW approach: Beneficiaries will work 15 days/month for 6 months, earning USD 75 per month. The timing for the CFW will be determined by the seasonal calendar to avoid disruption of livelihood activities. Women headed households will be equally enrolled in this activity as long as the work does not conflict with other household priorities. Activity 2.3: Conduct post activity survey after cash distribution and infrastructure rehabilitation: ADEC will collect information to assess the added value the rehabilitated infrastructures have brought to the supported community. Activity 2.4: Signing agreements with Local Service Provider (Xawaala) to transfer the cash to the beneficiaries. Activity 2.5: Cash distribution: Activity 2.6: Cash receipt verification: After the distribution of the cash ADEC field staffs will verify the receipt of the cash and its quantity while cross-checking with the list from the service provider data base. Activity 2.7: Post distribution Monitoring ACTIVITY 3: PROVISION OF LIVELIHOOD INPUTS Activity 3.1: Selection and registration of 300 vulnerable pastoral households. The identification will be done through community elders, community committee and local authority to ensure the most vulnerable who are not in a position to buy fodder for their livestock. Vulnerable Female headed pastoral households will be given priority. Activity 3.2: Procurement of the fodder: ADEC will procure 300 units fodder and deliver at beneficiaries’ location. Activity 3.3: Distribution of the fodder.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The targeted group beneficiaries comes from very difficult environment with low food security and are thus in need of urgent food provision both for survival and for rehabilitation of the acutely malnourished children. The current food insecurity situation in the area also merits the interventions going by the projects of food insecurity in many parts of Sanaag.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

The project determined market performance, characteristics, food security nature, availability of produce, inflation and the reasonability which determines food availability.
