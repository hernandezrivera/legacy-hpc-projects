Project Summary

This project is in line with and contributes to the Protection Cluster Objective Two, which calls for putting in place protection response mechanisms to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence. This project will support the delivery of quality, integrated child protection and psychosocial support services to the most vulnerable boys and girls aged 4-18 in Gaza, especially those associated with injuries during the ‘Great March of Return’ protests. The crisis has demonstrated the almost absence of available child protection services to address critical needs. The project will ensure that 11 Family Centers and 30 outreach sites in Gaza, are fully operational, responding to the pressing mental health and psychosocial, as well as child protection needs, of affected children. Service delivery will be targeted to the most affected locations and the most vulnerable children through a coordinated continuum of services in Gaza. Each Family Center will be connected to a community based child protection committee who informs the family center of community needs and acts as a focal point for identifying vulnerable children for referral. Family Centers will operate through established Community based organizations (CBOs) and mobile units, and will assess/screen and refer children for further services through:
1) a case management approach for the worst affected;
2) referral of complex child protection cases to child protection services (Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) and Civil Society Organizations (CSOs)); and
3) referral of children with further psychosocial support needs to focused and specialized psychosocial support.
Services will also strengthen outreach capacities to detect and link up children in need with service providers, whilst providing mental health psychosocial support (MHPSS) support through the family centres.

Implementing Partners

UNICEF will select partners among the cluster working group and those who have experience and expertise working in the area of child protection, in particular provision of community level structured mental health and psychosocial support. The organisations should meet the following selection criteria:
1) Active within the child protection working group;
2) Expertise in operating community level child friendly spaces, and direct expertise in participatory case management, life skills programming, psychosocial support services and recreational activities in Gaza.
3) Excellent outreach capacities and geographical proximity to children and families in the most affected areas of the Gaza Strip with specialist trained staff with expertise in case management, strong commitment to the case management approach, strong coordination and relationships with MoSD and other relevant organizations.
4) Are guided by contextualized manuals and materials developed and selected in the best interests of children, taking into consideration the do no harm approach during the various stages of the project life cycle in in line with the Child Protection Minimum Standards (CPMS) and IASC MHPSS standards.
5) Have referral expertise and networks for cross-sectoral referrals, especially with Education, and health cluster partners.
6) Proven monitoring and evaluation capacities to ensure tracking of progress and monitoring of results in line with UN Harmonised Approach to Cash Transfer (HACT) modalities

Needs

According to 2019 HNO, it is estimated that 290,988 children in Gaza (50% boys) are in need of structured psychosocial support (PSS) and child protection services. This is supported by findings from the 2018 child protection assessment, which showed the great need for PSS services in Gaza. Around 11,067 children are estimated to be in need for case management (50% girls). This includes children not reached in 2018, injured and dropout children, and children involved in child labor. Forming an integral part of the Gaza referral pathways, the need to have fully functional family centers is a necessity. The MoSD, facing a civil service salary crisis, in 2017 was only able to reach 135 children with case mgt. The demand for child protection services has come further to the fore with the recent escalation of violence at the fence causing significant deaths and injuries affecting children and caregivers. Reports of children being discharged with serious injuries and no follow up support reported to the CP Working Group. The ‘Great March of Return” has resulted in the injury of more than 19,690 people between 30 March-14 September 2018, affecting 118,140 people, including 59,070 children. Approximately 29,535 persons (50% children), corresponding to 25% will require moderate MHPSS support, of which 5% need specialized support according to WHO standards. The energy crisis, the slash of the salaries of Gaza PA employees, and doubts surrounding the continuation of UNRWA operations in Gaza have increased distress levels and exasperated the already fragile economic situation. Families are struggling to provide basic needs of their children, necessitating support to poor families. At least 1,800 children have dropped out from Ministry of Education (MOE) schools between Jan.-May 2018. Normally male children are engaged in child labor, making them vulnerable to violence and exploitation. They also are more likely to take part in the demonstrations at the borders. Analysis of injured children data visited, show that 93% of them are boys, of which 76% are aged between 14-17. This reflects the sense of hopelessness and despair impacting adolescents at this sensitive age. MOE data also shows that 43% of the drop out children are females. Those are likely to get married before the age of 18. UNICEF, the lead of the Child Protection Working Group, has been proactive in identifying and visiting injured children. The Governorate Protection Focal Points, as part of the Gaza Emergency preparedness mechanism, and family centers case managers have visited 951 injured children, to assess their situation and to provide them with psychological first aid. Results from their ongoing field assessment have helped refer children to required services, incl. 201 children to case mgt. (90% boys) and 111 to PSS (95% boys) services. Through case mgt. of injured children, access to medical, PSS, rehabilitation, and educational support services is facilitated in line with the Gaza referral pathways. Limited and intermittent funding to the family centers limits their capacity to meet the spikes in needs, and significant resources are needed to expand centers’ outreach and responsive capacities to ensure critical services are in place. Through case mgt, this project will respond to all protection needs of children and adolescent boys and girls within the scope of the family centers, in particular injuries from demonstrations and ERWs, as well as child labor, PSS distress, neglect and violence cases. Family centers will outreach, coordinate and follow up with key service providers, to ensure access to needed service.The reality though is unless critically under-resourced child protection services are invested in, children will remain at significant risk and may develop, serious mental health issues. Training of field staff on MHPSS minimum standards, in line with IASC Guidelines is needed to ensure no harm to benefiting children

Activities

Individual protection needs of both girls and boys (4-18), especially adolescents aged 14-17, will be addressed through the project, including, but not limited to, case management of female children who might be affected by early marriage and boys affected in the demonstrations and by Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) injuries and child labor. Both men and women will be targeted through the project, making sure that they understand their contribution to their children’s well being and ensuring their safety and protection.Since the majority of children injured at the borders are adolescent boys, support through family centers will take this into consideration including adaptation of the life skills manuals used. Both male and female case managers will be available to provide support and follow up through in centre, as well as outreach activities. Project outputs include: Output 1:Holistic child protection services equally provided to boys and girls aged 4 18 through 11 family centers and 5 Child Protection Networks. Activity 1.1 Provide structured child protection and psychosocial programs - multi week life skills (including sessions on ERW awareness), individual and group counselling using gender and age sensitive resilience building approaches, parent/child interaction programs and expressive arts programs, especially for injured children. Activity 1.2 Provide case management services for children with protection needs accessing the family center and identified through screening. Priority will be given to children who have been injured during the March of Return demonstrations and by ERWs, children who lost caregivers, and those subject to abuse, neglect, and exploitation, as well as children with disabilities. Output 2: Integrated child protection services supported to strengthen parental capacity to respond to children 's needs. Activity 2.1 Organize awareness raising sessions for parents on gender specific child protection issues and positive parenting (including a session on ERW awareness). Output 3: Capacity for professionals working on mental health and psychosocial support strengthened. Activity 3.1Conduct trainings on mental health and psychosocial support guidelines for field and emergency workers.

Indicators

In order to track project deliverable, the following indicators will be used; Indicator 1.1 Number of children (50% boys and 50% girls), including the injured, aged between 6-18 benefiting from structured child protection interventions including life skills, group and individual counselling and child-parent interaction programs by December 2019 (Target 11,000). Indicator 1.2Number of children (60% boys and 40% girls), including the injured, benefiting from case management support by December 2019 (Target 2,500). Indicator 2.1 Number of caregivers (25% males) benefiting from awareness raising on child protection & positive parenting and community initiatives by December 2019 (Target 6,120). Indicator 3.1 Number of MHPSS and child protection workers (50% females), trained on guidelines on mental health and psychosocial support by December 2019 (Target 130). UNICEF will ensure monitoring and evaluation of the project on the basis of outputs delivered, including the number of referrals to emergency services, child protection case management and psychosocial support services. Monitoring the adherence of service providers to the case management standard operating procedures will also be included, as well as use of resilience building approaches. The project will be monitored using UNICEF regular monitoring framework. Project implementation will be reviewed as part of the Annual Programme Review jointly conducted by UNICEF and project implementing partners. Regular field visits to project sites will be conducted. Partners will submit quarterly progress reports as a component of the project monitoring mechanism. In addition, UNICEF will provide technical support to partners through regular coordination. partners will assess benefit to children, caregivers and professionals through pre and post questionnaires, as well as focus group discussions. To ensure sustainability of child protection work within the community, UNICEF and its implementing partners will work with at least 11 formed Child Protection Community Committees (CPCC), this will ensure community based capacity to capture child protection issues even if the center ceases to exist. Working with volunteers, both males and females, from the same community will also contribute towards creating child protection capacities that will provide the support in absence of the centers.
