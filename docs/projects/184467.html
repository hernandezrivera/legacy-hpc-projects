Project Summary

According to 2022 HNO an estimated 2.3 million including IDP and Non-IDPs (737,100 and 1.5million respectively) are in need of multi-service of GBV response. Moreover, women, girls, persons with disabilities, women minority clans, older women, divorced women and widows face heightened risks of GBV that exacerbated displacements and forced evictions due to armed conflicts, droughts and flooding. Men and boys are also targeted for rape, forceful recruitment and kidnapping and psychological abuse and trauma. Loss of livelihoods and food insecurity due to multiple displacements forced young boys to step into the roles of provision thereby increasing levels of school dropouts.
GBVIMS report of 2021 indicate 62 per cent of reported GBV incidents were physical assault, 11 per cent rape, 10 per cent sexual assault, 8 per cent denial of resources, 6 per cent psychological/emotional abuse, and the remaining 4 per cent percent forced and early marriage.
Women and girls living in IDP Camps are more prone to sexual violence and abuse than those living in host communities with a better housing infrastructure and service sites and markets.
The JMCNA 2021 informs that 13 per cent of households were concerned that girls in host communities and 17 per cent in IDP camps were exposed to sexual violence and harassment, while 16 per cent (host community) and 18 per cent (IDP) households were concerned that girls were to be forcibly married. In this context, Banadir, Lower Shabelle, Bay, and Hiran regions are of the regions which have the most acute protection needs with emergency level needs at severity score 4.
As a result, this project is designed to support 21,332 people (3555hhs) – (women 3413, men 3413, girls 6826, boys 6826, and elderly 853, and disability 3200) including GBV survivors and vulnerable women and girls in IDP sites and host communities affected by natural disasters in Daynile and Kaxda districts in Banadir, Afgoye and Marka in Lower Shabelle, Baidoa in Bay, and Belet Weyne in Hiiraan regions with multi-service GBV response through the following intervention: rape treatment for rape survivors, case management, psycho-social support (PSS) and higher levels of mental health care for traumatized women and girls in ways that respect the safety, dignity and rights survivors to expand provision of timely and quality GBV response, as well as legal support to encourage survivors to build their confidence to report GBV cases through courts (Access to justice), community awareness campaigns on FGM and GBV risks and consequences and the need for social distancing, and women and girls friendly spaces, also dignity protection and materials support such as dignity kits, solar lanterns, torches, mats and mattresses. HINNA as women –led NGO with more than 8 years experience of GBV programming will improve coordination mechanisms among local actors in service delivery to ensure timely and quality comprehensive services to GBV survivors. Importantly, HINNA will provide transportation cost to GBV survivors who need high level medical treatment to main hospitals. During implementation, there is a need to strengthening the involvement of local authorities and local actors including Non-GBV actors to identify major barriers to localization of GBV services and to improve knowledge and practice as well as coordination in service delivery to ensure timely and quality comprehensive services to GBV survivors and to enable vulnerable women and girls and GBV survivors to heal from the trauma through GBV one-stop centers. The project will give more priority to community response and feedback mechanisms in order to prevent them from sexual exploitation and abuse while delivering humanitarian services to communities for safety and accessibility to all persons.

1. - Projects activities

The proposed project will provide the following main services as a holistic GBV response taking into account guiding principles and skills to care for GBV survivors:1. Clinical Management of Rape (CMR), Psychosocial Support (PSS), Legal aid assistance/Justice, case management services to 6,050 GBV survivors and referral (with full respect of the survivor’s rights and dignity) to main health facilities for those who are in need of high level medical treatment with transportation and services cost.2. Procure and distribute 4,500 solar lanterns, 4,500 dignity kits for Female headed households, widows, divorced women, persons with disabilities, older women, women minority clans who are at risk of GBV in IDPs and host community and GBV survivors based on individual assessments in project locations.3. Organize and conduct 144 practical forum debates (awareness raising) on GBV and FGM information on prevention and mitigation of GBV/FGM with 5,760 people – 40 participants per forum from various levels of the community including members from minority clans, people living with disabilities and marginalized groups as they are primary vulnerable groups by highlighting useful information about the risks of FGM, GBV and their consequences, mitigation and prevention measures, referral network and service available. 4. Conduct capacity building training on Forensic evidence collection to 18 Legal aid assistants GBV service providers and 82 police officers from police stations “with a total 100 persons” in the project target regions to support the sexual assault survivors. 5. Provide legal aid services to 54 of GBV survivors who are in need of formal legal assistance through courts as well as 98 survivors who are in need of informal mechanism through customary law ‘totaled 152 survivors” while HINNA to facilitate and organize spaces by bringing all relevant parties of community elders together until the case settled in a confidential manner with written documents for finals.6. Conduct three days on clinical care of sexual assault training (CCSA) for 90 of GBV nurses/health workers from GBV service providers in project locations.7. Conduct three days on case management training for 90 of GBV case workers from GBV service providers in the project locations.8. Conduct three days on psychosocial support (PSS) training for 90 people from various GBV service providers in al project locations.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

4.1)-Minority clans/ethnic groups are the main part of project designing and implementation and they are included during the consultations and decision-making process. Apart from these, any important information about the whole project will be shared with them to avoid any action that may possibly make them excluded. The full participation of these people in the project is crucial to the achievement of objectives, the project will give more priority to the equality of gender, and "Do No Harm principles will be adopted among project stakeholders.4.4)-Risk of exposure of violence as a result of distance to and location of humanitarian assistance/services or indiscriminate attacks from armed actors.On this risk, HINNA will carefully select distribution locations taking into account distance as well as priorities to persons with disabilities, pregnant women, sick and older people where the beneficiaries will be notified in advance with enough staff members in close coordination with local authorities for stability and security wise and collaboration with the established community committees in the sites to ensure smooth implementation and distribution process. This will mitigate the said risk of violence and indiscrimination attacks.
