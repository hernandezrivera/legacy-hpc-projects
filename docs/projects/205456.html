Project Summary

Four Mine Action Teams will conduct clearance of mines and cluster munitions in order to release as much land as possible to the local population with the aim to alleviate humanitarian needs. The MATs will also have the capacity to respond to emergency explosive ordnance disposal (EOD) spot tasks. Four Community Liaison teams will deliver explosive ordnance risk education (EORE) sessions with a focus on returnees and internally displaced people (IDPs) given their heightened risk profile. These sessions will raise awareness of the threats posed by explosive remnants of war (ERW) and provide communities with the tools needed to avoid these items and report them for removal. Combined, this intervention will protect communities from life-threatening risks posed by mines and ERW and facilitate access to specialised life-saving services.

1. Needs

Nationally, more than 16 million square meters of land, covering nearly half of the counties in South Sudan, remain contaminated by landmines and ERW. These hazardous areas (HAs) present a key challenge to the country’s reconstruction efforts and daily life-threatening risks to civilians. Explosive hazards inhibit civilians from collecting water or firewood, cultivating land, attending schools, and receiving health care, among other essential services. They further prevent humanitarian organizations from accessing vulnerable communities or providing life-saving assistance in conflict-affected areas. IDPs and returnees are at increased risk as they may lack local knowledge of contaminated areas, and may traverse and settle in marginal and hard-to-reach lands that have previously not been assessed. Given the high levels of acute food insecurity in South Sudan, access to safe land for agriculture is crucial. MAG operates in the Equatoria region which remains the most densely contaminated part of South Sudan with mines and ERW. Central and Eastern Equatoria states contain 55% of all known contaminated land in South Sudan despite only constituting 18% of the country’s overall land mass. In spite of the level of contamination, these areas are located along major supply roads from Uganda to Juba and are key for the transport of both people and goods. These states are also part of the country’s greenbelt region, meaning they have some of the highest agricultural potential in the country and supply much of the food produce for the rest of South Sudan. Given the area’s proximity to the Ugandan border, the majority of those returning from refugee camps in Uganda settle in the Equatorias. A series of cuts in humanitarian assistance to Uganda since 2020 has resulted in an increase of returnees to Central and Eastern Equatorias, which in turn has increased demand for adequate food, safe land, and shelter. Therefore, MAG’s intervention will increase access to safe land, facilitating safe returns and boosting agricultural livelihoods. Expansion of farmland increases incomes and improves quality of life of beneficiaries as they are better able to cover the basic needs of their families, including food, school and medical fees. Access to more safe land also reduces tensions over resources and can contribute to enhanced stabilisation. Land clearance also ensures people can safely and directly access natural resources, boreholes, medical facilities and schools. All these outcomes facilitate economic development, more resilient communities and sustainable development.
