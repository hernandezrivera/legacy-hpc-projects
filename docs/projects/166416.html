Project Summary

The UNICEF COVID-19 health response will follow two main tracks. UNICEF will provide COVID-19 isolation and treatment for Rohingya refugees and members of the Bangladeshi host communities through the construction and operation of a 200-bedded severe acute respiratory infection (SARI) isolation and treatment centre (ITC) in Teknaf for the estimated period of 9 months.
In parallel, technical and financial support and supplies will be provided to the Civil Surgeon’s Office for the preparation and operation of isolation and treatment units at the eight Upazilla Health Complexes and Cox's Bazar District Hospital when there is a need according to gaps analysis. UNICEF also support government on training for the medical staff in 8 upazilas and Sadar Hospital in taking sample for testing with the facilitator from IEDCR.
The second track will see UNICEF reinforce and ensure the continuity of quality, equitable and gender-sensitive maternal, newborn, child and adolescent health services, sexual and reproductive health (SRH) services including counselling and referral for GBV, with attention to people with disabilities. This will ensuring that COVID-prevention supplies, including personal protective equipment (PPE) and supplies for infection prevention and control (IPC) are available and utilized at 14 UNICEF-supported health facilities in camps (5 primary health centres and 9 health posts) and 1 diarrhoea treatment centres as well as at government health facilities in adjacent host communities (2 union sub-centres and 16 community clinics). These services will include maternal, newborn care, immunization, integrated management of childhood illnesses, nutrition, acute watery diarrhoea prevention and management, prevention of mother-to-child transmission of HIV services and community engagement.

Objectives

Sector objective 1. Reduce to the extent possible the morbidity, mortality and spread of COVID-19 by rapid identification and isolation of all cases, including most vulnerable providing them with appropriate care, and tracing, quarantining, and supporting all contacts. Sector objective 2. Prevent transmission and amplification of COVID-19,by enhancing infection prevention and control in community and health care settings and effectively communicate critical risks (counter misinformation) to men, women, boys and girls. Sector objective 3. Provide technical leadership, coordination, collaboration and information management support to all partners responding to the COVID-19 pandemic. Sector objective 4. Ensure continuity of equitable access to and utilization of quality lifesaving and comprehensive primary and secondary health services for all crisis-affected populations with a special focus on sexual, reproductive, maternal, neonatal, child and adolescent health; mental health and psychosocial support; and non-communicable diseases while adapting services to the current context.

Needs

Cox’s Bazar has a small but increasing number of COVID-19 cases. Once community transmission is confirmed, cases could escalate rapidly in congested urban areas. The 859,808 Rohingya refugees living in the 34 camps in the district face particular aggravating factors including: underlying health concerns and high levels of food insecurity and acute malnutrition; high population density, shared WASH infrastructure and reliance on humanitarian aid distributions; and suboptimal hygiene behaviours, with 33 per cent households practicing handwashing at critical times (ISCG MSNA October 2019). Capacity to isolate and treat cases needs to be urgently scaled up, to meet demand as cases rise. UNICEF supported health facilities are in camp 3, 4, 5, 8W, 10, 12, 13, 16, 17 and 18, and the DTC in Teknaf. The fear of COVID-19 and movement restrictions are constraining access to health services. Primary health care consultations for children under 5 have decreased from around 200 per day to around 70 per day. Thirty per cent fewer vaccination activities ran in March 2020 than March 2019. Antenatal care, postnatal care and facility delivery rates are all in decline. All UNICEF-supported health facilities in the Rohingya camps and host communities will be strengthened for triage, early detection, isolation and referral for suspected cases. Health workers are being trained for COVID-19 surveillance and response, and IPC training is ongoing. PPE will be procured for UNICEF supported health facilities and GoB facilities (based on the gaps analysis) in the host community and Cox’s Bazar. The current studies so far show that COVID-19 have impacted mostly the elderly, males, and vulnerable people with pre-existing medical condition but this in not inclusive. Different setting show different results and children also significantly affected both direct or indirect from the pandemic. For example, if the immunization coverage is going down as one of the impact of the pandemic, children would be more vulnerable to the vaccine preventable diseases (VPD). In addition regarding the age and gender analysis, here are some important points need to be noted:- caregivers are mostly women - In all public health crisis the forefront are nurses, midwives, and community health workers and their role is frequently overlooked- Possibility of increases of Gender-based violence (GBV) especially to women and girls- special attention must be given to children and elderly who already in needs for nutrition support - breastfeeding must continue and it might be affected when the mothers have to be isolated- Disruption to health services e.g. reproductive and maternal health like in any other outbreak with big scale Therefore, in this response, UNICEF will ensure:- Data will be disagregated with age and gender and they should be available, analysed and actionable- Age and gender will be considered in the layout of the SARI ITC (separate ward for children, women and men; separate latrines, etc)- nutrition support for children and elderly with malnutrition- protection will be embedded in the response

Activities or outputs

Objective 1Activity 1.1: In partnership with icddrb, construct and operate a 200-bedded SARI isolation and treatment centre in Teknaf, serving both the Rohingya and Bangladeshi populations with appropiate care and tracing the contacts Activity 1.2: Support the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoH&FW)/the Civil Surgeon’s Office, provide technical and financial support and supplies for COVID-19 isolation and treatment units in the eight Upazilla Health Complexes and District Hospital. The support based on the needs from the gaps analysis Objective 2Activity 2.1: Together with MoH&FW, PHD, RTMI and IEDCR, with coordination with WHO, train health care workers on COVID-19 case management from 5 Primary Health Care Centres, 9 Health Posts and 1 Diarrhoea Treatment Centres in the camps and health facilities in adjacent host communities (2 USCs and 16 community clinics)Activity 2.2: Together with the MoH&FW, PHD and RTMI, establish triage and referral for potential COVID-19 cases that present themselves to UNICEF-supported health facilities, including ambulancesActivity 2.3: Strengthen community engagement both through UNICEF partners in UNICEF supported health faciliites' catchment areas and with C4D and risk communication task force to enhance the infection and prevention in the community and health care setting Objective 3Activity 3.1 Strengthen data and information management, supporting the 8 upazilas and refugees camps and build partner's capacity in reporting the data in DHIS2 Objective 4Activity 4.1 Together with MoH&FW, PHD and RTMI, provide supplies including personal protective equipment and supplies to ensure infection prevention and control to 5 Primary Health Care Centres, 9 Health Posts and 1 Diarrhoea Treatment Centres for both host comunities and the refugees; health facilities in adjacent host communities (2 USCs and 16 community clinics); and the Special Care Newborn Unit at the district hospital and 4 Newborn Stabilization Units in Ukhiya, Teknaf, Ramu and Chakariya.
