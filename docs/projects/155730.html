Project Summary

This Humanitarian WASH assistance project is designed in light of supporting return population in merged tribal district Kurram, enabling them to meet their immediate humanitarian WASH needs and hence protecting them from WASH related risks.
It is likely that the provision of improved water, sanitation and hygiene facilities along with increased knowledge and capacity building of target population would reduce their vulnerability and would meet their humanitarian WASH needs to great extent.
In this regards adequate access to safe & clean drinking water facilities would cater through rehabilitation/construction of 2 gravity water supply schemes, along with provision of 80 water storage tanks for HHs lacking safe storage means. In parallel, water safety plan would be developed and operation and maintenance training and tools will be provided for ensuring sustainability, community participation and ownership aspect.
For addressing immediate humanitarian sanitation needs, households/ compound sanitation facilities to extremely vulnerable households would be provided through community participation i.e mobilization for skilled and unskilled labour provision of locally available materials for latrine construction , that are culturally appropriate, secure, user friendly with focus on gender mainstreaming at community and institutional level. Humanitarian assistance would be provided through construction/rehabilitation of 190 low cost latrines at HHs level on component sharing approach focusing on PWDs, women/widow headed HHs, elderly, child headed, extended family size with low income for reducing open defecation along with the provision of twinset latrine and hand washing facility at one school level.
In parallel MH would adopt Participatory, Hygiene Sanitation Transformation Training (PHAST) approach and would conduct hygiene awareness raising sessions in targeted area with the aim to improving their hygiene practices, to promote positive hygiene behaviors and increased understanding of key health WASH related risks to prevent most common waterborne diseases and poor sanitation related risks in targeted villages of Zaimusht tribe in Central Kurram. Through participatory hygiene awareness raising the targeted community including men, women, girls & children would be sensitized keeping in due consideration the socio-cultural dynamics. This would be achieved through conducting 30 Hygiene sessions (20 women focused sessions for 500 women and adolescent girls @25 participants each session & 09 sessions targeting 225 men and 1 session targeting 01 government primary schools @25 participants each session. Alongside, 10 PHAST sessions would be conducted in 10 villages (1 session per village @20 participants/ session). In addition, 100 schools diaries with printed hygiene messages, 1000 IEC material & 950 hygiene kits would be distributed in areas of intervention.
Overall 6422 beneficiaries i.e. 5138 beneficiaries for water, 3853 for sanitation & 6422 through health & hygiene improved measures would be reached@ of 27% women, 26% men, 24% Girls and 23% Boys. The project would contribute to Gender Mainstreaming through producing an impact working on Gender Marker Code 2 (a) with equal and increased participation of women with men as decision makers, acting as a way in improving & maintaining female dignity. For this purpose separate men and women village WASH committees would be established for equal representation at all stages of project cycle.
A comprehensive M& E work Frame would be developed and practice for quality assurance at field level, whereas regional and country office random visits would be a value added to quality assurance in terms of monitoring and advising on the management of triple constraints of project.
Throughout the project life cycle closed coordination with line departments including F/PDMA, civil/ military administration, community elders and other relevant stakeholders would be maintained.

Needs

Based on MH current WASH interventions in Central Kurram, Pre KAP assessment, daily field visits, meetings with district Planning Officer, PHED and Rapid Needs Assessment (RNA), access to WASH services across district Kurram is still extremely poor due to various factors i.e poverty, unavailable resources, harsh weather, limited access and low or no coping mechanism made return families more vulnerable in terms of lack of WASH services.Based on RNA held by MH, meetings with major stakeholders at district level, very limited access of humanitarian actors was recorded in recently return area of Zaimusht/ Khwidad Khel. The tribe was found most vulnerable & needy in terms of provision of humanitarian WASH facilities during group discussions at field visits. According to RNA, springs are main sources of drinking water for more than 80% residents of Central Kurram. Residents of Zaimusht/ Khwidad Khel were found most vulnerable in terms of lack of WASH facilities. They live in hilly terrain with no water source except unprotected natural springs and damaged pipelines. Group discussions shows that less than 30% returned families have access to clean drinking water central kurram, while in Zaimusht 80% population of assessed villages have no access to clean & safe drinking water. Women & children are at high risk, as they fetch water from distance of more than 20 minutes. It is more risky in harsh cold weather and rainy season. Women & children were found carrying water in jerry cans from unprotected springs from distance of more than 1 Km and still per person daily water use recorded less than sphere standard within assessed population.In order to reduce vulnerability of women & children, the need of day is to protect natural water sources and repair pipe lines carrying contaminated drinking water, rehab of public gravity flow schemes. There is inadequate drinking water storage capacity at HH level, 80% women of target families are in the habit of storing water in unprotected small water pots. It is also obvious from multi-cluster sector report that, community in general (around 75%) is unaware about significance of hygiene, community in large do not practice healthy habits. With reference to RNA and current interaction in area of return 80% returnee families lack toilet facilities, open defecation is common among children & adults, whereas women throw it outside wall, causing various diseases. Lack of sanitation facilities are contributing factors in increased ratio of diarrhea and other relevant diseases. Critical Need: Increased access of returnee families to safe and clean drinking water, access to improved sanitation facilities, installation /Rehab of WASH facilities at community, school & health facilities, along with, in order to adopt good hygiene behavior through emphasis on hygiene promotion sessions specifically for 27% Women, 26%, Men, 24% Girls and 23 % Boys among target community are in critical need of return families to be immediately responded. Vulnerability: 80% of return families using unsafe/contaminated drinking water in Zaimusht/ Khwidad Khel and prone to water borne diseases, Women & adolescent girls are most vulnerable in terms of fetching water on distance of more than 1 Km and cannot spare time for children care/study/playing. Carrying heavy water loads, on head/back by women, is associated with pain & muscular-skeletal damage. The situation is generating chronic stress among women because they are responsible for more than bringing water i.e cooking, laundry, child care etc. Furthermore, unavailability of latrines for girls in schools in view of menstrual hygiene management also impedes girls’ educational achievement.Unhealthy habits and unhygienic living standard, especially women and children are prone to common WASH related risks, which may easily switch over to healthy habits through provision of increased sustainable access to WASH facilities in central Kurram.

Activities

Activity 1: Provision of safe drinking water to the targeted population to meet their humanitarian needs. (5138 beneficiaries for water)• Rehabilitation/construction of 2 Gravity flow Schemes • 4 Pre and Post Water quality tests • Operation and maintenance Training • Provision of O&M Tool kits • Provision of 80 water storage tanks Activity 2: Provision of appropriate sanitation facilities to the targeted population to meet their humanitarian needs (3853 for sanitation) • Construction of 190 component sharing latrine at HHs/compound level • Construction of 1 improved twin set latrine along with hand wash facility at institutional level. Activity 3: Awareness raising activities for the targeted population aimed at improving their hygiene status and positive behavior change ( 6422 individuals)• Formations of 10 WASH Committees. • Conducting of 10 PHAST Training. • Conducting of 30 H&H sessions in schools/community. • Formation of 1 WASH club among school’s children. • Distribution of 950 Hygiene kits. • Distribution of 1000 copies of IEC materials. • Development of 10 Water safety Plans. • Pre & Post KAP Surveys. • Printing of 100 school diaries with hygiene messages.Implementation PlanImplementation Plan revolves around the community management of water, sanitation and hygiene activities at community, household and institutional level with a predominant participatory approach.Coordination meetings with district administration would lead MH to collect final endorsed Village’s list from concern authorities. Village selection criteria in view of WASH need, Age, gender, disability and protection would be considered in designing to address the humanitarian needs of marginalized group of returnee population in area of return.Broad Based Community Meetings would provide the opportunity to brief about project details, community participation and project deliverable. Participatory approach would be adopted and ensured for project interventions.Gender based men and women WASH committee would be established at village level. The WASH committees would be formed in BBCMs. Two members of each committee would be provided a one-day Operation and Maintenance training for sustainability purpose.Apart from this WASH committees would also be the part of Participatory Hygiene and sanitation transformation training to be held at village level and will sensitize residents of the village and catchment area. To identify WASH related hazards, analyze problems, assess risk, make plan of action to protect their families from WASH related health risks, ensuring good hygiene & safe sanitation practice among men, women, children, elders & PWDs.WASH committee members would be able to develop water safety plan in PHAST training.To know about the knowledge, attitude and practice of WASH in target community a pre-survey will held at each village. Based on survey, H& H sessions would be held and hygiene kits, IEC materials would be distributed among participants. Similarly WASH committee would also provide assistance in identification of project schemes sites, identification of most deserving/ most vulnerable families for humanitarian interventions. To measure project output and impact a post KAP survey would be held at the end of project. Detail BoQs and designs would be developed and rehab/construction of gravity schemes and institutional WASH facilities would be initiated by selected vendor through tendering process, whereas HHs/compound latrine would be constructed through component sharing approach, with the assurance of community participation. Handing/taking over ceremony of Water supply schemes and institutional WASH would be held in presence of district administration and PHED.Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E Unit) of project activities, community mobilization with Participatory approach i.e community involvement; contribution, ownership and stakeholder’s engagement would be the key feature of humanitarian WASH assistance project.
