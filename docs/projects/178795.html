Project Summary

Strengthening and sustaining delivery of standardized secondary and specialized health care services addressing obstetric care, Neonatal Resuscitation services and referral services across the country. The main objectives are to Improving sustainable and quality life-saving secondary health care, Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric Care (CemOC), Neonatal Resuscitation services and referral services across the country men, women, boys and girls requiring life-saving surgical interventions and secondary health care, in addition to women of reproductive age requiring emergency obstetric care; and to improving access to essential medicines and strengthening the capacity of secondary care at hospitals’ level to cope with the emerging demands is important.
Proposed HRP project is focusing on improving the quality of care for trauma, secondary and tertiary healthcare services in Syria through the provision of continued education and training, endorsement of standardized clinical guidelines, treatment protocol, and standard operation procedure of different subdivision of secondary healthcare services including trauma, emergency, and critical care services and Comprehensive emergency obstetric and newborn care (CEmONC). Furthermore, the project aims to infection prevention and control activities through delivery of Basic IPC training, provision of technical support, field monitoring, and measure the quality of healthcare in terms of inputs, the material, resources needed to provide IPC standard measures, and outcomes of the activities based on IPC assessment framework (IPCAF) for health facility. In addition, the WHO will focus on supporting critical gaps of secondary and tertiary health care, based on a needs assessment to ensure the vulnerable population has access to emergency care services.
Within the integration approach to essentially service delivery the access to quality care and referral to specialized health services will be introduced through implementation of this project, which includes dialysis, cancer and thalassemia treatment, as well as trauma care and rehabilitation, including provision of life-saving medical supplies and medicine required for the provision of specialized and trauma services.

4 - Needs

Based on HeRAMS report in 2020, 50% of public hospitals are fully functioning and 24% non-functioning. By the end of 2020, 79% hospitals were reported accessible, 8% hard-to-access, and 13 % were inaccessible. This resulted in limited provision of health services across governorates.Availability of medical equipment and medicines remains of high priority with the key identified gaps of medicines and consumables at functional hospitals. No vaccine pronouncement will be done under STHC project. As COVID cases is increasing with the 3rd wave there is need to expand current capacity to manage the cases: ICU medications, Anastasia medicines, critical medicinal equipment, oxygen supplies, ICU beds, ventilators, and consumables.It is imperative to enhance national health resilience and to reactivate hospitals’ sector by supporting the rehabilitation of non-functioning public hospitals, to repair extensive damage to crucial infrastructure, support ongoing medical training for human resource for health ,sustain availability of EML ( medicines/equipment) and to undertake ongoing needs’ assessments to fulfill gaps on the mid- and long term.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Hostilities in Syria continue to take a heavy toll on the lives and livelihoods of civilians and the availability, access and sustainability of essential health services and medical supplies was and continues to be always in risk due to the security situation, internal displacement and donors’ support.According to the HNO 2020, Attacks on health care have left 14.5 million people in 42 districts below emergency standards for hospital beds. Regarding the health system risks the recent assessments indicate the need for referral capacity, including ambulances to overcome gaps in secondary care and connect patients to life-saving care, Risks:1. Shortages of qualified staff and health workers.2. Lack of emergency and secondary health care services for PIN among targeted population, due to non-functionality of hospitals, which in turn contribute to an increased burden of diseases (morbidity & mortality rates).3. Violence against health facilities and health care providers. Mitigation measures:1. Strengthening the capacity building of existing health workers and promoting partnerships.2. Advocacy for rehabilitation of hospitals and priority units.3. Continue reporting on attacks via SSA & MRM and health Custer flash updates.4. Provide preparedness training for the facilities and staff-care in case on incidents; this is supported under trauma projects ( Incident Management Training, HAZMAT courses,... ).

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks:1.Non-functioning hospitals 2.high turnover of staff 3. Lack of human resources especially in eastern areas of the country.4.Lack of adequate and updated data on hospital admission, services and needs which affects planning & response process;5. Lack of the knowledge and prace on infection control principles lead to infection outbreaks in hospitals 6.Ending of UNSCR: UN Security Resolution will negatively affect access to humanitarian aid , especially for X-border shipments for NES.7.Lack of approvals for deliveries/ distributions of medicines; negatively affects the timely response.8.Diversion of aid, to other parties other than targeted PIN and loss of optimal impact. Mitigation measures. 1.Support the minor rehabilitation & equipment for partially damaged priority units at hospitals to enhance health coverage; 2.Maintain capacity building activities for existing staff and health workers including training of trainer’s activities; 3.Strengthen the capacity of ministry of health to implement new human resources strategy in affected areas; 4.Advocate for hospital information system at national level, in process by WHO; 5.Build capacity on up to date curricula on hospital management and infection control for health professionals across the country; and Maintain supply chain of critical medicines and supplies based on the prioritized EML.6.Prepositioning of health aid at NES/NWS hubs, Damascus 7. Prepositioning of health aid at hubs for contingency stocks.8. Ongoing coordination with concerned parties to make health aid accessible to PIN.
