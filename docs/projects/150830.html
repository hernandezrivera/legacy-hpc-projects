Needs

In Hiran Region 78% of the population experienced displacement due to floods and conflicts during 2018. Due to the process of migration, families have been left separated. Separation of families increased due to the migration of pastoralists in search of less flooded and greener pastures for their livestock. Children and women are the most affected. Many IDPs camps have been erected in towns to host these populations. Due to the shortage of water, firewood and food, in the IDP camps young girls and women have to walk long distances to get them. In the process, they pass through bush areas or night falls when still on their way. This exposes them to risks of sexual assaults or other GBV forms. Also, the camps lack lighting and security provisions. This exposes girls and women to the various forms of GVB. Indictors reveal that 85% of girls and boys, men and women are victims of GBV and mostly happen within the IDPs settlements. Poor access to basic human right protection and in Emergency tends to pose risks of human right violation, therefore RRP with its presences in Hiraan region have witnessed a close relation between poor shelter to the rising cases of GBV, early marriages, and even FGM, The recent floods in the area rendered many home states homeless hence the cases of rape both reported and unreported were many. Evidence from the JMCNA 2018 data that was collected in 58 districts in Somalia show that 80% of the IDPs live in makeshift shelters, temporary emergency shelters, tents, or under trees; 35% reported damage to their shelters in the last three months; 70% reported that the shelters they live in have no internal separation; 16% reported thefts, therefore most of these vulnerable groups fall victims of their rights being violated. RRP in Jalalaqsi district will respond to these interventions to try and save the affected beneficiaries and enlighten them.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The selection and registration of the beneficiaries will be done with the consultation of local authorities, IDP committees including women by giving priority the new arrived IDPs; women headed households and households with disabilities and/or seek persons. Post distribution monitoring will be carried out to ensure that the right beneficiaries benefited from the supplies without anyones rights being violated.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Women, children and marginalized communities are especially at risk and face specific protection concerns. The protection crisis is also characterized by entrenched but fluid societal divisions, resulting in competition for resources across social groups. Three interlinked priority areas where protection is lacking in different parts of Somalia and poses a significant challenge to the entire humanitarian response have been identified: Differential risks of exclusion and discrimination, including those based on societal discrimination, power structures, vulnerability, age, and gender. Somalia’s societal structure is highly complex, including numerous social groups, clans, sub-clans and ethnic minority groups that are not members of a specific clan. Divisions and constantly evolving relations among those groups remains one of the prevailing characteristics of the society, and a driving variable within the ongoing conflict and drought. Some groups having comparatively less social capital, resulting in reduced clan-based protection and less support, including financial, during crises. Displacement can aggravate existing inequalities, as well as introduce new dimensions of marginalization and exclusion.

Protection of communities in conﬂict-affected areas subjected to disproportionate and indiscriminate targeting of civilians and civilian assets vital for their survival. Localized conflict dynamics, consisting of inter-communal rivalries and competition, are influenced by a broader conflict between the state and allied stakeholders, and armed non-state actors. In this diverse and highly complex landscape, with a multitude of militarizes and non-state armed actors, violations of international humanitarian law (IHL) and human rights law regularly occur with impunity. Civilians bear the brunt of the ongoing conflict, through death and injury, destruction of property, taxation of communities including through forced child recruitment, land grabbing, destruction of livelihoods, limited freedom of movement and limited access to services and humanitarian assistance, and diversion of assistance.
