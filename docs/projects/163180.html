Project Summary

The project, Building Early Recovery Support in 4 villages of the Konye Municipality, aims in supporting the implementation of recovery strategies in building the capacity of IDP's and host community members in seeking ways in implementing developing strategies thereby, ensuring dignifying standards of living to some 400 internally displaced and host community members whose livelihoods based has been destroyed by the crisis. Beneficiaries of the project are from the villages of Ekiliwindi, Baduma, Bakwa Suoer and Konye in Meme Division of the South West Region of Cameroon. The project which is to begin on March 1, 2020 and ends in August 31, 2020, has the following specific objectives:
1. Protection of Community Members in Konye Municipality is Enhanced. Social cohesion approach will be used to identifies conflicts and uses dialogue and builds capacity to address them. Community members will visualize conflict, and build a shared vision of social cohesion by creating space for individual self - transformation, strengthening relationships, and mutual understanding within identity groups in the community, develop trust and confidence among the groups. Thus, conflicting groups and individual will think and act positively for introspection, dialogue and joint action for transformational change. Social cohesion will supplement existing peaceful institutions and processes, and will encourage peaceful resolution of conflicts. As such, the project will build capacities of IDP's and affected persons, to identify, prevent and mitigate conflicts, and support community development initiatives.
2. IDP's and affected persons' livelihoods are improved. The project will focus in mapping out strategies in meeting the needs of the IDP's and host community members to follow up the peace process. The capacity of 400 IDP's and affected persons' households will be drilled on social cohesion integration, business through income generating activities, maintenance of farms to market roads, sporting activities and fields maintenance This diversified approach is to ensure that improvements to livelihoods are sustainable and well- targeted to specific IDP's and affected household needs to boost the standard of living through the provision of capacity building training, working tools as well as training and sporting materials. . The project will conduct follow- up and monitoring visits through out the project phase to observe beneficiaries' response to training, provide support and ongoing technical assistance. In so doing, beneficiaries will achieve the following results:
Result 1.1. Vulnerable IDP's and affected persons identified and their needs implemented through small community development project
Result 1.2. Support the integration of early recovery by the affected persons
Result 1.3. Vulnerable IDP's and affected persons especially (women, disabled, elderly, widows, orphans, children, idp's, youth) generate income from small business ventures.
,Gender equality approach will be integrated at all levels of the project with women as well as men represented in all the decision- making committees of the project. 280 women and 120 men will take part of the project.
A monitoring and evaluation system will be put in place at the start of the project. monitoring forms containing key indicators will be prepared and shared amongst the team. Field Monitor with the entire WARDA team will monitor the project on a daily basis while UNDP Recovery team will follow up the supervision. All information will be shared and progress report written based on the information shared and also visits the project once a month. In order to enhance accountability, in addition to planning and reviewing meetings that will be conducted in the field every month and reports of the project distributed to all stake holders ( beneficiaries,local authorities, UNDP).Feedback mechanisms such as open numbers, drop boxes will be put at the level of beneficiaries.

Besoins / Needs

The armed conflict in the North and South West of Cameroon has caused over 437.000 people to flee and settled in bushes, other towns, villages and regions, as well as to neighboring Nigeria. The abrupt and unexpected movement of the population has caused untold hardship, thereby increasing hunger, poverty, social vices and pressure on the resources of the communities that are hosting the internally displaced and the affected population. The displaced and host community population have little or nothing to feed on or enhance their livelihoods.According to the UN Officer for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs Report of November 30, 2018, more than 437.500 people are displaced in the North West and South West regions of Cameroon. Over 4 million people are affected and majority of them are in need of food, shelter, health, education, logistics, nutrition, protection, water and sanitation services. This situation erupted in 2016 as a result of a strike action by lawyers followed by teachers of the two Anglophone regions protesting against the interference of the French language in courts and schools of the English origin. This situation led to casualties and continues to have serious consequences on livelihoods and living conditions of the affected populations.In late 2017, responding to the violence and loss of lives, humanitarian organizations reported that families were fleeing the 2 affected regions and taking refuge in bushes and some in the French speaking parts of the country. While more than 200.000 are in neighbouring Nigeria. Displacement was further compounded by limited access to education for children due to a ban and attacks on schools by non -state armed groups. .Thus, war has been waged and both sides are accused of human rights violations, including killings, torture, ambushes and destruction of properties. Despite several support from the United Nations High Commission for Refugees (UNHCR) providing shelter and other non-food items and supports from other NGOs , food and shelter continue to be the major crisis in this area where the displaced population have limited access to basic necessities. The food insecurity situation is worsening due to farmers' inability to go to the farm for fear of being shot or kidnapped as a result of the armed conflict situation.Following a feasibility study conducted by WARDA in October 2019, more than 90% of the internally displaced and affected persons continue to be food insecure. Within the past months, the IDP's and the affected persons have been depending exclusively on external and internal assistance Humanitarian aid has been the major source of food. The quantity provided is far below expectation. As a result people were forced to escape leaving behind all their resources. This has led to acute malnutrition among children below school going age, nursing and pregnant women. Since a majority of those who are displaced are women and most are adopting new social and gender roles such as women burying their lost ones and heading their families as most men have either been killed or still in their hideouts, 70% of women are identified for assistance since they still have complications in going out of the vulnerability circle while 30 % are men. As such, owning shelter, food items, households and kitchen utensils as well as basic necessities are the major difficulties encountered by these displaced population. Field assessment also revealed that the internally displaced women are the bread winners of their families and thus, need assistance to feed their families. This could only be achieved through advocating for more assistance from both the national and international communities to provide funds to rescue these women and children who are desperately in need of help for survival through the provision of capacity building training, working tools, income generating activities. farm roads maintenance and the provision of sporting materials.
