Project Summary

As the Syria crisis enters its sixth year, civilians continue to bear the brunt of a conflict marked by unparalleled suffering, destruction and disregard for human life. 13.5 million people require humanitarian assistance, including 4.9 million people in need trapped in besieged and hard-to-reach areas, where they are exposed to grave protection threats. There are 1.75 M children out of schools in Syria (HNO 2020). Grave child rights violations continue unabated, with countless children killed and maimed due to the persistent use of explosive weapons in civilian areas, resulting in enormous loss of life and destruction of civilian infrastructure, particularly schools and hospitals
This project will be implemented in two locations in AL Raqq
a Governorate - The population number in Raqqa city are 297,710 and TABQA are 31,227,These locations have recently witnessed heavy fighting, culminating in the retreat of ISIS where children were subjected to the most grievous violations and injustice
Due to the extensive presence of explosive hazards, the project will continue to priorities the delivery of life saving risk education messaging to provide children and families with the information they need to reduce their risk of death and injury, through age and gender tailored massages and where possible, taking into consideration the localized threat and activity patterns of different at risk groups. Mothers and girls, apart from being at risk of death and injury through UXOs and explosive remnants, also play a critical role in instilling safe behavior among their children and peers. Accordingly, the project will ensure equal access to mine risk education for women and girls in school and community based initiatives.
Activity: The RE part of the project provides two main activities. The first RE activity is delivery of lectures and recreational programs to groups of teachers and students; these will form a curriculum for mines and ERW awareness in schools. The curriculum contains lessons and planned activities and games which will raise children's awareness of ERW and their ability to identify them and act safely around them. in AL Raqq and TABQA is mostly contaminated with ERW rather than landmines; in this environment the curriculum will prioritize and concentrate its focus on ERW, cluster bombs, unexploded barrel bombs and other relevant hazardous items. Teams will provide the targeted schools with training and booklets containing this curriculum, and thereby try to integrate education about ERWs and living safely with ERWs into the school program so that children will internalize the information rather than just recall it. White Hats organisations feel that ERWs are a long-term problem, and that as such, long impact approaches for children must be sustained. The second RE activity is distribution of leaflets and posters with basic instructions about the avoidance of ERW, including information about Improvised explosive devices (IEDs) and precautions to be taken, to parents and to communities in coordination with local councils.
In addition to the RE component, White Hats proposes that the teams begin gathering data on contamination and causalities with the use of Kobo. This will require technical training which will be provided via skype to each team member from White Hats office in Gaziantep, Turkey. The teams will gather data by talking to local councils, hospitals and medical centres, who will point hem in the direction of affected families and communities. The information collected will be recorded into a database which will be designed using White Hats experience of survivor interviews and ERW mapping, and white hats experience within Syria of data collection and remote monitoring; through it information will be made available to share with other humanitarian actors. Data collected will then be used as a tool to plan ERW clearance in the future.

4 - Needs

Presence of explosive hazards, including landmines, explosive remnants of war, and improvised explosive devices, continue to threaten the lives of girls and boys and families, livelihoods of affected communities and endanger humanitarian actors. According to the 2020 HNO assessed communities reported the presence of explosive hazards and assessed communities reported known cases of injury or death as a result of explosive hazard contamination. Explosive hazards are often buried, even when above the ground it can be hard to recognize posing a significant danger to civilians. Key infrastructure such as schools, roads, housing, and water/sanitation systems have been contaminated as a result of conflictWhile all population groups are exposed to the dangers posed by these hazards, men and boys are more directly at risk – with the former representing 60 percent of recorded casualties – particularly when engaging in farming, herding, moving or traveling, and rubble removal. Women and girls are more indirectly impacted by the long-term consequences of contamination which can see them deprived of breadwinners when male family members are injured or killed. Children, IDPs, and returnees, often unaware of the dangers in their communities, are also particularly vulnerable. Schools in 212 communities are contaminated by explosive hazards, with assessed communities in the following Governorates reporting the highest level of explosive hazards present in schools: 30 percent in Rural Damascus, 20 percent in Ar-Raqqa, 17 percent in Aleppo, and 14 percent in Deir ez Zor. While all people living in areas contaminated by explosive hazards have specific needs in terms of risk education, these needs are especially acute among children who are more likely to unknowingly pick up suspect items or be playing in a contaminated area. Indeed, 11.5 M people in Syria live at risk of explosive hazards

5 - Implementation of activities

Risk education (RE) is an essential, lifesaving component of the humanitarian response in Syria; it is the first step to preventing civilian casualties from explosive hazards. Raising awareness helps mitigate the threat by providing people with the necessary knowledge to stay out of harm’s way. Tailored RE messaging (age, gender, and localized threats) ensures communities are aware of the risks from explosive hazards and are encouraged to behave in ways that reduce the risk to people, property and the environment. White Hats Response will undertake delivery of audience specific RE for vulnerable groups identified as being most at risk, such as IDPs and settled communities affected by the conflict, as well as those exposed to explosive hazards on a regular basis, such as children, farmers, rubble removers, and humanitarian actors. The messages will also empower both women and men to make safer decisions.Explosive incidents expose 8.2 million men, women, and children to severe, debilitating psychological trauma, physical injury, and death on a daily basis. In addition to socio economic impact and the erosion of security, explosive incidents have a psychologically detrimental effect on survivors and their families, increasing hopelessness, fear, and fatigue. Victim assistance is currently underfunded and an identified need in Syria. White Hats Response will implement victim assistance initiatives, provision psychosocial support and case management for most acute needs.White Hats have an office in Ar Raqqa city which will be responsible for the direct management of the implementation. The remote management of the project will be through WHOSD office in Erbil that will be technically supported by GZT office. WHOSD will recruit and train staff to deliver the required assistance.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Familiarisation may cause the civilian populace to become overconfident or complacent to the threat of explosive hazards. Mitigation:- Emphasis on "Stop, Warn, and Report" messaging in risk education.- Conduct regular KAPB surveys to monitor changes in risk behaviour, as well as qualitative evaluations to understand the issues underpinning risk behaviour. Trained EOD capacity may become overconfident/complacent when dealing with explosives, exposing themselves and others to risk of injury or death. Mitigation:- Local capacity is trained to IMAS standards.- Assurance that regular internal and external quality assurance and quality control is undertaken. - Assurance that regular internal and external quality assurance and quality control is undertaken.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: - Communities will be receptive to RE facilitators and willing to participate in ERW-RE sessions- The security situation remains stable enough to reach all proposed communities - Local authorities will facilitate the movement of risk educators Risks: - Security situation becomes unstable, making ERW-RE in proposed communities unsafe - Communities are not receptive to RE facilitators and are unwilling to participate in ERW-RE sessions- Movement is inhibited by road blocks, ERW’s etc.
