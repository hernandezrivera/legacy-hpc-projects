Project Summary

The proposed project aims to protect children (girls and boys) from violence, abuse, exploitation, and neglect through a mix of prevention and response intervention in the targeted communities. IRC will offer age and gender appropriate structured psychosocial support activities for children and will enhance the capacities of parents to contribute to healthy development and psychosocial wellbeing of children at a family level. Whereas at the community level, the project will establish community-based child protection mechanisms comprised of child protection committees and an efficient and response child protection case management system.
The project will reach an estimated total 78,066 individuals (50% girls/women, 50% boys/men) in 6 targeted districts (3 in KP and 3 in Balochistan). Targeted boys and girls (aged 5-17) will include out-of-school children (OOSC), children with disabilities (CWDs) or those living with disabled or financially disadvantaged caregivers/parents, children experiencing protection concerns (child exploitation, neglect, gender-based violence, and physical and emotional abuse) etc. The proposed services provided through this project will be in line with the HRP protection sector strategy and the Minimum Standards for Child Protection in Humanitarian Action (CPMS).

1- Project Activities

Objective 1: Protect children (girls and boys) from violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect Key Activities:1. Provision of Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) Services to the most vulnerable children (girls and boys): a. Safe Healing and Learning Spaces (SHLS) will be established for the provision of PSS services (at level 2 and 3) for children (age 5-17 years old) in a safe and supportive environment, and in line with COVID-19 IPC protocols. PSS activities will range from informal recreational activities and games to more focused and structured group psychosocial and/or life skills interventions and offered in small groups of up to 15 children in each session, while also ensuring a safe distance. IRC will be using Social Emotional Learning (SEL) curriculum for children 5-11 years old and Supporting Adolescents and their families in Emergencies(SAFE) for 12-17 years old girls and boys. b. Mobile SHLS, through outreach facilitators, to provide PSS, life skill, recreational activities for children and their caregivers at their doorsteps. c. Engagement with parents and caregivers through the Families Make the Difference (FMD) parenting intervention skills sessions on self-care and stress reduction, positive disciplining, and promotion of psychosocial wellbeing of children. d. The SHLS will serve as an entry point for specialized services to respond to child protection cases (such as abuse or violence survivors) and provide them with a safe and confidential space to heal.e. PSS kits will be distributed to vulnerable / out of school children (specifically, in the lock down areas). f. Conduct PFA sessions for schoolteachers / facilities in targeted communities g. IRC has established toll free MHPSS helpline in Balochistan and KP; the project will ensure access of children and caregivers to tele counselling for PSS through a trained and qualified psychologist. 2. Establish and support the operations of a case management and referral mechanisms to ensure access of vulnerable children to basic services:a. The project will establish and strengthen community-based child protection committees and build their capacity to monitor, identify and refer CP issues (i.e. child labour, child marriage, violence against children, etc.). b. IRC in coordination with SWD will conduct mapping of key CP service providers and establish and/or update CP referral pathways in targeted districts. The referral pathways will be translated in Urdu and disseminated in the targeted communities. c. IRC will conduct CP case management training for key service providers and case workers. d. While working closely with SWD-Child protection units, IRC will offer CP case management services as per international and national (provincial) CP case management protocols and best practice standards. 3. Disseminate information and promote dialogue among families and communities on stigma and Violence Against Children (VAC) and GBV prevention. a. IRC will conduct joint awareness raising messaging on COVID-19 prevention, the consequences of child abuse, how to access CP response services, and other relevant topics through community mobilization in the targeted communities.b. Organize community discussion groups to increase community awareness on the harmful effects of VAC and GBV; the role of families to protect children; as well as increased awareness on how to access CP services.
