Project Summary

In 2019, 2 children died in the bush as there went for hunting. This year 2020 4 have died still hunting. These children should have been in school by the hour they died. This project proposal targets 4000 children who have not gone to school this academic year in Njikwa of age 2-14 including boys and girls for quality literacy, numeracy, environmental sciences, social sciences, health and hygiene, safety/security and protection. It lays emphasis on 1000 adolescents and young boys and girls of 14-24 years training in vocational skills in quick income generating projects. It considers 400 teenage mothers as victims of the crisis to be trained and empowered on livelihood activities. 25 Child Protection and Social Learning Centers and 12 Vocational Centers in 9 villages in community spaces and churches. 150 Volunteers teachers/ trainers will be recruited and trained in child protection in emergency tips. 9 Community Education Advisory Committees in emergency situations created in the various villages and trained on community emergency response, child protection, safety and security tips. They will help formulate cultural child protection policies in their various villages. The committees will design quality learning programme at various levels, produce manuals and train volunteers on the usage. Radio Education will be considered and gargets and didactic materials distributed. Volunteers trainer will be motivated and children refreshed for 2 months with a school meal to boost enrolment. Giving vulnerability rate of young girls and boys in Njikwa, this proposal will be concerned with GBV and SEA education, WASH lessons, civic education and morality.. Special centers created for the Muslim Mbororo children to meet their specificity in 4 zones and their needs supplied accordingly
Given that we live in the Covid - 19 Pandemic era, the project will do serious sensitization talks on covid -19 to 9 communities and provide 6000 locally but standard face masks to the children and teachers, 200 handwash buckets, 200 cartons of soap, placed in the various learning centers and disinfection of 37 learning centers against Covid - 19 as measures to curb it if not eradicate the spread of the virus.
Given that education in emergency is at the heart of humanitarian action as a basic need of children caught in emergencies, it remains the fundamental right of children. The project will be of great relief to communities in Njikwa for obvious reasons; First, education provides immediate physical, psychosocial and cognitive protection in protracted crisis. Out-of-school children are more vulnerable and exposed to protection risks, eg. about 400 teenage mothers in 4 years, GBV, child labour and there estimated 300 children connected to NSAG in Njikwa.
Second, education is a major contributor to individual, community and societal resilience. The only industry in Njikwa is Education as their backbone. Therefore access to safe, quality education combined with the provision of lifesaving skills, social and emotional learning, and psychosocial support, help children cope with, and recover from crisis. The skills and knowledge learned in education enhances access to opportunities for the next generation, breaking cycles of vulnerability to shocks.
In our preliminary need assessment, education was prioritized by communities, parents and children to build their future, even in challenging humanitarian situations. That is why Community education in NW started in Njikwa. In line with the principle of accountability, humanitarian responders should listen to the self identified needs of the affected by crisis and respond accordingly.
Humanitarian responders are uniquely placed to meet education needs in crisis situations, with the agility and capacity to reach children that development actors cannot provide. Consideration to this project as top need will be appreciated. Lastly much ground work is done for the communities to accept the project by IVSPD

1 - Besoins / Needs

The protracted crisis in NW-SW Regions of Cameroon has plagued the country with thousands of school dropout and the rural areas are the most affected as no formal education is going on. Schools institutions and premises are either destroyed or deserted, and some hosting armed groups. Teachers and children in formal learning centres are under serious threats of life or ransoms for daring to go to school. As a consequence, children in rural areas have missed four school cycles with devastating impact on the children especially adolescents and young adults in local communities. Njikwa Sub Division is highly hit especially Ngwo and Oshie Villages where most public and social services have been shut down. Njikwa had been calm and at peace until the killing of the Police Commander at Police Post in Oshie on a matter that was totally unconnected with the crisis (eye witness confession) which provoked the burning down of Oshie Market and other private home on the May 4, 2018. Now in Njikwa there are no schools, no healthcare services, no administrative services, markets and churches occasionally shut down. The main and biggest market in the sub division which serves not just the sub division but also parts of Ngie, Widikum-Menka, and Akwaya sub divisions was burnt down. The biggest district hospital in Momo Division which is in Ngwo - Njikwa, the Health Centre in Oshie and other health posts in the various villages were shut down and all the doctors and nurses fled. All the schools in Njikwa - 7 secondary/technical/SAR-SM and 28 primary are closed and teachers fled affecting over 7 000 children who dropped out of school and over 3000 deprived from the opportunity to start schooling. 2 secondary schools burnt down and one badly damaged and looted. 68 homes were burnt down affecting 80 families directly who lost basic needs - shelters, clothing and bedding. 51 shops were burnt affecting 51 direct families as the only source of supply of their needs. About 1000 houses have been damaged and looted causing over 3000 people to desert them. The central administrative area is abandoned to the military with no other inhabitant. From our preliminary inquiries, about 400 school dropped out girls have been impregnated as teenage mothers excluding the over 200 cases of abortions. Some are now unwillingly married while a good number remain single teenage mothers. Unofficial verbal report from the nurses in Njikwa says HIV/AIDS is most prevalent in Njikwa than any other sub division in Momo. Now, promiscuity is at its peak especially situation of GBV and SEA. Njikwa has neither electricity, nor any communication media or network, and broken water pipelines with no repairs since all technicians fled and other water points left in thick bushes. The common playing grounds for children had been the many large school fields which are all deserted and no go zones. There are no community recreational or friendly spaces and no social services for the children. The Mbororo Muslim community in Njikwa is highly affected and threatened for their purported involvement in the crisis supporting the government that caused much pains and loses for the people. As a consequence trust was lost and the Mborolos are being chased away and have suffered a great losses of their livelihood - cattle and sheep and some have been killed in the course of the crisis. Their children are unable to attend the few learning centres we established. The need to create theirs is an urgent necessity. From our findings, apart from Indigenous Volunteers for Sustainable Peace and Development that is doing some humanitarian work in Njikwa, no other organisation has visited the communities for any assistance but for data collections. Given these varying problems in the sub division, we hope that this project proposal if funded will bring some relief to the traumatizing population especially the children who are out of school for four years.
