Project Summary

The purpose of this project is to promote optimal nutrition practices, prevent and treat acute malnutrition in the most vulnerable population groups i.e. children under five years, pregnant and lactating mothers through implementation of community management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) and maternal infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN) interventions in Rumbek Centre, Wulu, Cueibet counties of Lakes state and Pibor county of GPAA. The project will be implemented from January 1, 2022 - December 31, 2022 and shall benefit the most vulnerable groups of the population of IDPs, returnees and host population. PI will ensure that people most affected by crisis and marginalized groups (like those with disability, minority ethnic groups and other groups due to economic, political, social and cultural factors) are actively participating throughout the project cycle. This project will focus on the management of children with moderate and severe acute malnutrition (MAM and SAM), pregnant and lactating mothers with acute malnutrition and promotion and prevention of acute malnutrition through maternal infant and young child nutrition interventions. Furthermore, preventive activities like MIYCN counselling, health, nutrition and Wash education /promotion, backyard gardening, complementary food cooking demonstration and distribution of wash and other preventive NFIs (like soap, aqua tab, mosquito net etc.) will be done at nutrition sites and at community level. As part of treatment protocol, all children with MAM and SAM will be screened and treated for malaria according to the national protocol / or referred to nearby health facilities. Furthermore, preventive kits (jerrycans, Mosquito nets, soaps) shall be provided to mothers /care givers of children who are enrolled for treatment in OTP or TSFP or SCs sites as these children are at higher risk of getting infected and also to promote proper personal hygiene and sanitation practice. All children who will be discharged cured from OTPs will be enrolled to TSFP programs for full nutritional recovery and those deteriorated will be linked to SC. Besides, if a child in TSFP is nutritionally deteriorated, he/she will be enrolled/referred to OTP/SC program. Those who recovered shall be given protection ration and referred to other (FSL, BSFP) services and will be provided counselling. When there is deworming and vitamin A supplementation campaign, this project will support to ensure children under five year age in the counties get the services in collaboration with SMOH/CHD, UNICEF, WFP and the health partner in the county. Furthermore, PI will participate in FSNMS, SMART and other joint assessments happening in the counties of operation. PI will implement OTP and TSFP programme together in Rumbek Centre (21 nutrition sites), Wulu (13) and Cueibet (14) counties and Stabilization centres in Rumbek Centre (1) and Cueibet (1) and only TSFP programme in Pibor county (21 static sites). In addition to the above mentioned interventions, PI will implement BSFP in Boma, Labrab and Maruwa payams of Pibor County. In total, this project will benefit 192, 078 populations (boys, girls, men, women, people with disability) in different services like treatment for acute malnutrition, MIYCN counselling, Vitamin A supplementation, deworming, blanket supplementary feeding and other interventions. Of the total beneficiaries, 84644 are children under five years (boys: 45,707 Girls:38936) in host communities, 36275 (boys: 20314 Girls:15961) under five years children in IDPs and returnees, 64043 are host community member adults (Men: 32662, Women:31381) and 7116 (Men: 3629, Women:3487) IDPs and returnee adults. Furthermore, this project will be implemented in integration with health, WASH, CP and food security and livelihoods interventions in the counties. All interventions will put in place the necessary measures to preventive COVID-19.

1 - Needs

Based on the Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) for Acute food insecurity and acute malnutrition (AMN) - analysis that was conducted in South Sudan from October 26th to November 16th, 2020- it was projected that from April to July 2021, 7.2 million people or 60% of the people analysed will likely face high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above). According to FSNMS 26 report (Data collected in August 2020) based on WFP corporate indicator, the Consolidated Approach to Reporting Indicators of Food Security (CARI), 67.3 percent of households across South Sudan are food insecure. Of these households, 50.1 percent are moderately food insecure, implying the presence of significant food consumption gaps while 17.1 percent are assessed to be severely food insecure and have extreme food consumption gaps or face extreme loss of livelihood assets. Despite the significant downturn in economic and livelihood activities induced by COVID-19 related movement restriction for the greater part 2020, the proportion of food insecure households decreased by 8 percent when compared to the same period in 2019. In the same report, Global acute Malnutrition (GAM) at national level was estimated at 15.3 percent based on modelling exercise. In Eastern Equatoria and Lakes States, the modellin estimates showed higher malnutrition rates which increased from serious level in 2019 to critical level for 2020 estimates. Malnutrition level at state level was estimated to fall within the same classification as previous surveys in most states. Jonglei, Upper Nile and Unity maintained malnutrition levels above the emergency level (>15%). In eastern Equatoria and Lakes, the estimates showed higher malnutrition rates in 2020 compared to 2019 survey, phase classification got worst changing from serious levels in 2019 to critical levels for 2020 estimates. Likewise, malnutrition in Pibor is always a serious problem. According to a March 2021 SMART survey conducted in Pibor by IMC, there is a GAM rate of 21.6% (17.6-26.3 95% CI) and SAM rate of 4.9% (3.0- 8.1 95% CI) based on Weight-for-Height Z score which is beyond critical thresholds compared to the results of SMART Survey of 2017 with GAM: 26.8%, May 2018 GAM:20.7% and November 2018 with a GAM: 20.8%. This shows that there is no improving in the GAM rate trends in the last 4 years. The survey further showed an enrolment rate of 39.3% of malnourished children into nutrition program. This enrolment rate is very low compared to other regions. Inadequate Infant and young feeding practices affects food intake, making it one of the immediate causes of malnutrition based on the nutrition causal framework. In the same FSNMS 26 report, Exclusive breastfeeding (EBF) was practiced by 51 percent of respondents, representing a 17-percentage decrease from the previous survey which estimated EBF at 68.1 percent. 77.2 percent of new born babies were initiated to breast milk within one hour of birth as recommended. This is similar to findings from similar assessment conducted in August and January 2019. Results for the assessment showed sub-optimal complementary feeding practices with only 24.8 percent of children 6-23 months received required quality of food diversification while 28.7 percent of children received the required meal frequency. The minimum acceptable diet (MAD) is an indicator that shows children who received required minimum dietary diversity and minimum meal frequency. Some 5.9 percent of children met the minimum acceptable diet, these rates continue to be low as observed from historical trends of previous surveys. Hence, the continued food insecurity with the alarming rate of acute malnutrition, instability that hamper food production, the poor health infrastructure, frequent diseases outbreak and relatively low optimal breast feeding and complementary feeding practice, there is the need to continue large scale humanitarian response including nutrition services in these proposed counties.

3 - Project indicators

Monitoring and reporting activities are planned and included in this project. Monitoring of the activities and budgets will be undertaken at all levels of project implementation. Project nutrition staff will be responsible for day to day management of the project with support from the M&E officer, Program Implementation Area Managers and nutrition coordinators/manager at FO level and head office. The standard nutrition programme (CMAM and MIYCN) reporting and recording tools will be used at each level (i.e. nutrition site, county and Plan’s head office in Juba). Furthermore, PI will select key indicators and monitor the progress throughout the implementation of the project through using IPTT. Reports, (weekly, monthly and quarterly) shall be sent by each nutrition sites and will be compiled by nutrition coordinator/officer/manager at county headquarters. There will be continuous analysis of collected reports, community feedback and correction measures will be taken accordingly. Monthly project performance trackers will be filled and shared by the M&E officer to inform the project management on the performance of the project and quarterly review meetings will be conducted to share successes, challenges and lessons learnt. Quarterly, midterm and final project reports will be written and submitted to donor (s) as per the agreement. In addition, Plan International will submit monthly reports to the nutrition cluster and SMOH. Furthermore, Plan international will also give updates on the project performance/progress when requested. Also, joint monitoring and supervision of project activities shall be done with UN agencies (WFP, UNICEF), partners implementing similar programs in the counties, SMOH and NC (sub cluster). Besides, PI will invite these partners and stakeholder during project start up meetings, review meetings and close up workshops. 8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Pl will apply high quality risk management standards to manage all possible risks of the project in all the three counties where this project is going to be implemented. The key identified risks and mitigation measures are listed below. Insecurity: PI has a Security and Access Manager at Juba level and a Security Coordinator in Pibor and Lakes who continuously monitors and updates staff on security issues to ensure safety when moving between locations in all project areas including. Pl has also conducted a security assessment of its operations in GPAA and Lakes counties and has in place security SOPs and response measures for identified risks. Furthermore, PI shares security information with different partners and UNDSS operating in the counties/states. When there is real or perceived security threat and limited accessibility to target locations staff will adhere to the security policies and procedures. PI’s policy of recruiting majority of staff and volunteers from project communities provide an additional layer in managing security risks at the local level. Financial: Financial risks, including fraud, will be managed by applying strong controls in procurement, payment and documentation. All procurement and financial transactions will be subjected to relevant checks and scrutiny based on delegated levels of authority. Contractors will be thoroughly screened against required watch lists to ensure regulatory compliance. Operational: Operational risks such as access and logistical difficulties are analyzed and managed by a dedicated project manager and the Program Area Implementation Manager (PIAM). Weekly operational reviews will be held between the PIAM and the project manager to ensure that new challenges are attended to with speed. Where needed, additional operational support will be provided from relevant PI staff in Juba. Personnel Management: Personnel related risks will be managed through line managers and specialized teams within the human resource department. An HR officer has been stationed in Pibor to help in managing personnel risks. Weather conditions – rainy season in Pibor hinders access and running out of supplies in nutrition sites. This will be mitigated by prepositioning supplies and strategically planning activity periods. Wrongful conduct (e.g. fraud, Sexual Abuse and Exploitation) by staff, local actors and / or others: - This could result in harm to beneficiaries and negatively impact on the efficiency and effectiveness of the operation. Plan International staff will sign the child protection policy and a code of conduct making commitments to refrain from wrongful conduct and enhance PSEA, anti-fraud and anti-terrorism practices as well as follow protocol reporting system. The organization will exercise absolute zero tolerance to any breach. Remind Plan International’s staff on these through several instruments / documents including the sub-agreement and the purchase orders contain these at the back Looting of food affecting project performance and beneficiaries not getting service: - PI will work closely with local leaders and community to ensure nutrition supplies are kept safe and if they fail to secure it is difficult to provide service to malnourished children and PLWs. Further, PI will employ enough security guards after background check and fences nutrition sites and any construct storage space when possible.
