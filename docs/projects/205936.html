Project Summary

This project will be implemented in Awerial and Yirol West counties, Lakes State. Its main objective is to reduce protection risks for women and girls. In this project NEPWU will conduct awareness-raising sessions with women, men, boys and girls on GBV and HIV risks, available response services and timely reporting; support women, girls with GBV risk mitigation interventions such as income generating activities and fuel efficient stoves; conduct KI Interviews, at Payam level, covered by the Protection Monitoring System; support women, men, boys and girls with specialized GBV response services such as GBV case management, referrals, legal aid and WGFS programming; support women, men, boys and girls with individual and group psychosocial support including recreational activities; provide women and adolescent girls with dignity kits; establish and/or strengthen GBV referral pathway and establish community-based protection structures or enhance the existing structures in Awerial and Yirol West.
Project monitoring will be undertaken as an integral part of project implementation and will focus on the inputs, activities and outputs, and the review of program achievements and accomplishments against targets. Through this process, NEPWU will identify issues and concerns affecting program implementation, and design courses of action necessary to address those issues. Monitoring exercises will focus on the context and the decision-making processes, and it will mainly be done through regular and periodic visits to project sites, reflection and learning events as well as through a system of reporting.

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to face unprecedented and persistent humanitarian crises exacerbated by inter communal conflict, climate change and diminishing value of local currency. According to Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2023 physical violence, rape, and other forms of GBV towards women and girls are pervasive. It stated that societal norms, values, practices and gender related power imbalances were key drivers of the various forms of GBV women and girls face, and that an estimated 2.8 million people, with a majority being women and girls, face GBV risk in 2023. The report further stated that there is a 9 per cent increase in GBV risk in 2023 compared to 2022 estimates caused by conflict, perennial flooding, and drought. It noted that the percentage of GBV risk is higher for returnees compared to IDP households (45.5 per cent) and host community (42.7 per cent). In addition, 43.3 per cent of IDPs in Eastern Equatoria, 17.2 per cent returnees in Lakes states, and 20.8 per cent of IDPs in Western Bahr el Ghazal reported that women and girls avoid firewood collection sites, water points, and latrines because of fear of GBV. In addition to that NEPWU also noted and HIV related issues such as disclosure has contributed to intimate partner’s violence and family rejection.According to protection monitoring monthly report for August 2023, 65% of interviewed Key informants (KIs) reported fear of stigma as most common barrier to reporting. 30% of KI note armed actors as contributors to the risk. The report also indicated that 32% of interviewed KIs who identified Lack of Access to Justice, reported women and girls being particularly affected, and 34% of KIs mentioned corruption as main obstacle to access to justice.The above mentioned issues require immediate intervention. In our view if not addressed now this may continue exposing Women and Girls to further protection risks and therefore this project is intended to provide GBV prevention and response services to most vulnerable communities in Awerial and Yirol west counties, lakes state.
