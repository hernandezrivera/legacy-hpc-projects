Project Summary

Approximately 15 percent of any community may be persons with disabilities. These rates may be higher in communities in Somalia where they have fled conflict or disaster, as during crisis people may acquire new impairments and have limited access to medical treatment. Persons with disabilities are among the most vulnerable and socially excluded groups in any crisis-affected community like Somalia and according to a recent rapid GBV/disability need assessment conducted by DAF, It is evident that accessing humanitarian assistance programs is hard near to impossible due to a variety of societal, environmental and communication barriers which increasing their protection risks for children, women and girls with disabilities including to child protection and gender-based violence (GBV). With the double-fold intersection of gender inequality and disability, Women and girls with disabilities remain vulnerable to several protection risk especially vulnerable to GBV. In addition, social norms often designate women and girls to be caregivers of people with disabilities, which can reinforce their isolation and further limit their access to social, economic and material support, increasing their vulnerability to violence and exploitation. Although there has been a significant effort of advocacy to make GBV programs and services integral to humanitarian response from the earliest stages of an emergency. Yet, In Somalia context persons with disabilities and their caregivers face particular barriers to accessing those services where they still remain isolated in their homes, overlooked during needs assessments and not consulted in the design of programs. As DAF acknowledges that persons with disabilities and caregivers, particularly children, women and girls in recognition of their increased exposure to violence, have a right to protection in situations of humanitarian crisis, we aim to ensure in by this project to promote and facilitate access to services and participate in child protection, GBV programs on an equal basis like any other group. DAF will focus on community based child-protection intervention and GBV prevention and access to safe reporting and referrals for GBV survivors, while at the same time will work on quality, age and gender appropriate multi-sectorial GBV response to survivors, through capacity building of service providers. Interventions at community levels will include: promoting safe reporting through trained community protection focal points (FP). DAF through its community workers (40 Females) will conduct community outreach to disseminate key information on
Child protection, including information on the availability of services, and promote positive social norms to improve the safety and well-being of boys and girls e. SGBV,harmful practice, family separation, recruitment/CAAFAG, and Children’s rights while identify and link willing survivors with disabilities to Women and Girls Safe Spaces for receiving support. The community worker will be trained on survivor-centered approaches, including how to promote safe reporting by upholding conﬁdentiality, considering survivors’ privacy, dignity, safety/security and PSS needs while referring them for service provision in an accessible manner. These community focal points will be sensitized on existing situations of vulnerability of all the survivors that require extra attention, in particular among marginalized groups of girls and women at heightened risk of GBV. These groups include: the elderly women, girls and women living with disability, IDP, returnees, refugees and stateless girls and women, girls and women heads of households, girls and women from minority clans among other marginalization factors. The project proactively plans to reached marginalized groups at risk and to improve the immediate access for GBV reporting from service delivery points to community level. To tackle GBV prevalence, engaging men is essential, given their inﬂuence in the lives of girls

1. - Projects activities

DAF will work with men with advisability as allies and advocates where they will use ‘village volunteer’ model. Here 30 men Volunteer alongside with community worker will act as a focal point for assessments, support and advice, educating villages about women’s rights and gender-based violence, supporting individual families through home visits and tailored interventions to prevent or reduce domestic violence; DAF will also use men in conducting advocacy/ mobilization workshops with various categories of men including religious leaders (400 males) with the aim of getting them to identify actions they could implement in their respective roles, to prevent and address GBV in the community. Following the workshops, supportive religious leaders will lead GBV awareness raising in their community, in particular among men and boys, to address harmful and discriminatory social and cultural attitudes and practices. DAF with its links with the education actors and managing several schools in southcentral Somalia will lobby for stake holders in to provide accessible and safe toilets to ensure girls are protected whilst at school. DAF also will train local police, courts and service providers (such as health centers and hospitals) to increase their understanding of disabled women’s rights and enhance their sensitivity to the needs of women with disabilities’ such as the availability of sign language interpreters at medical and judicial services where in future we aim to have Somali police to have subsequently developed a proactive approach when dealing with issues of disability. DAF will do identiﬁcation, training and deployment of community protection focal points and ensure the focal points come from diverse groups in the community including people living with disability where they will be sensitized on marginalization, access barriers of people living with disability and facilitated to support this group through community outreach, the project will support community outreach for people living with disability to ensure messages are passed in various modes; Safety and GBV risk mapping in the new IDP camps where people affected by ﬂoods, drought and conﬂicts have relocated to and mitigation measures put in place, people with disabilities will be targeted as part of community participating in the safety audit and ensure risks they faced are captured and addressed ; - Religious leaders and men GBV advocacy/ mobilization workshops; - GBV prevention, mitigation and response awareness raising activities, including use of radio, IEC materials among other avenues that are sensitive to the needs of people living with disabilities, communities will be sensitized on heightened sexual violence risks faced by girls and women living with disability and support they need to be protected or seek help; - Door to door community outreach by the protection focal points following COVID prevention protocols, households with people with disabilities will be targeted among other households; - Construction /rehabilitation of Women and Girls Safe Spaces within health facilities including making them disability friendly and accessible; - Training of clinical and non-clinical staff on survivor-centered GBV response, CMR, PSS and Psychological First Aid gender and disability sensitive service provision; -Multi-sectorial response through referrals for essential services including special services that may be needed by survivors with disabilities; - - Provision of psychosocial support, parenting messages, positive parenting and other kind support leading to improved relationships with the children under their care- provision of medical services, Mental health and PSS services; - Provision of NFI (including dignity kits, solar lanterns for the parents of children with disabilities

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Project will look at safety audit and identify mitigation plans. will intentionally facilitate communities to review conditions for ensuring safety of girls, boys, women and men accessing the static and mobile WGSSs and other critical services including markets within the community; and to identify ways of making access safe for all including for persons living with disability, elderly persons, boys and girls. Girls, boys, women, men, PLWDs will be consulted for identifying sites of construction of static WGSS and locations where mobile WGSS will be stationed. WGSSs facilitators and community protection focal points will raise awareness among girls, boys, women, men, PLWDs and elderly about safety planning such as moving in groups, avoiding walking while it is dark. - Mitigation measures for barriers faced by beneﬁciaries to access complaints and feedback mechanisms; DAF will hold consultations with different members of community (boys, girls, women, men, IDP, minority clans, host community, PLWDs and elderly) to gather their preferred mode of providing feedback, identify issues of access, safety and community conﬁdence in the feedback mechanism. These inputs will be factored in the design and operationalization of the feedback mechanisms.
