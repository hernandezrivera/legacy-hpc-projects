Project Summary

The project seeks to improve the living conditions of highly vulnerable newly displaced, protracted IDPs, returnees and host communities /affected non- displaced who are unable to meet their S/NFI needs in areas of Upper Nile(Ulang, Nasir and Maiwut), Jonglei (Akobo West) and Warrap State (Tonj North and Twice ).
According to UNOCHA more than 908,000 people have been affected by unusually heavy rainfall and subsequent flooding since July of whom 620,000 people are in need of immediate humanitarian assistance. The Government of South Sudan declared a state of emergency in the affected areas on 27 October.
Other than flooding, some of the driving factors on the ground that triggered present displacements include; Inter Communal Conflicts in settlement areas, inter-communal/tribal fighting in refugee camps in neighboring countries, signing of the revitalized peace agreement in 2018 giving hope for peace and the continuous flooding in almost every part of the country as a result of heavy rains.
Floods caused by torrential rains have worsened the living conditions of South Sudanese people in many parts of the country in the past few months. The heavy rains have destroyed shelters, crops, water sources, public infrastructure like schools and health facilities, increased the risk of water-borne diseases among communities, and rendered many roads impassable, constraining access to affected areas.
TADO response will be jointly in collaboration with Humanitarians partners e.g SSUDA, Nile Hope,Medair,PAH, CIDO ,CHADO, ADA, CCOSS, NCA and World Vision International in the identified affected counties with highly severity and gaps after SSHF-A2 intervention by the partners. The mode of intervention will be in-kind since there are no markets and previously existing markets have been destroyed by either floods or conflicts.
The organization intends to target 20,000 direct beneficiaries segregated as follows, Ulang 7500, Nasir 4,600, Akobo West 3000, Tony North and Twice 2900 and Maiwut 2000, comprising of newly displaced people, (IDPs) in protracted settlement , returnees and most vulnerable host communities that are unable to meet their S/NFI needs. Further, it will target lactating and pregnant women, households with malnourished children, unaccompanied children and unaccompanied elderly and persons with disabilities. The project will undertake timely delivery of life-saving assistance through the deployment of rapid response teams in line with humanitarian priorities outlined in the Humanitarian Response plan 2020 (HRP).
TADO has a static presence in both Ulang and Nasir counties of Upper Nile state ,Gogrial East in Warrap,Akobo West in Jonglei state, and was thus recently nominated as site focal point for Eastern Upper Nile.
The project team will adopt conflict sensitive approaches embracing “Do No Harm” principles through community engagements on GBV, HLP and integrating the work across other clusters where TADO and other partners on the ground are active.
TADO will significantly mainstream GBV throughout all stages of S/NFI response, to ensure gender and protection risks are addressed directly thus mitigating chances of exacerbated vulnerabilities.

1 - Needs

The project seeks to support the provision and rehabilitation of emergency shelter and life-saving Non-Food-Items to Returnees, IDPs and the most Vulnerable Host Communities. According to reports highlighted by different specialized mobility tracking agencies (UNOCHA, I OM-DTM and WFP) and coordinated assessment by different partners in alert areas identified, there have been increased displacement in almost the whole country due to either Inter-Communal Conflicts and floods,The torrential rainfalls worsened the living conditions of South Sudanese people in many parts of the country in the past few months. The heavy rains have destroyed shelters, crops, water sources, public infrastructure like schools and health facilities, increased the risk of water-borne diseases among communities, and rendered many roads impassable, constraining access to affected areas.As per the above, TADO response will targeted the identified affected and highly severity counties that were uncovered during the first SSHF-A2 allocation by partners . TADO interventions will response through in-kind based assist to identified beneficiaries as follows; Ulang (7,500), Nasir (4,600), Maiwut (2000), Akobo West (3,000) and Tonj North and Twice (2,900).According to recent IRNA conducted in Ulang County from 8th -13th May 2019, its estimated more than 20,000 beneficiaries are in dire need of the S/NFI based intervention. TADO responded using SSHF-A1 allocation in 4 payams of Ulang to 3,300 beneficiaries. World Vision a mobile partner later did a scale up on 2 locations in Ulang Center and Ying covering 6,000 beneficiaries. No other partner has intervened ever since. Heavy floods have also been reported in the area thus the increase in the number of vulnerable.In Mandeng IRNA of March 23-27 March 2019, an estimated 30,000 beneficiaries were reported and S/NFI cluster partners were requested to provide assistance immediately before rain season begins. TADO under SSHF-A1 responded to 4,452 beneficiaries, Medair a mobile partner (6,966) and Nile-Hope is expected to cover only four locations under SSHF-A2 allocation. An inter-agency multi-sectorial IRNA report conducted in October 2019 on flooding indicates 22,507 beneficiaries were affected by floods in 6-payams of Nasir County.In Maiwut since 2014, the county has been peripheral to the national conflict and economic crisis in the country until July 2017, when a Government campaign to secure the strategic town of Pagak deliberately disrupted people’s livelihoods causing large scale displacement of people to Ethiopia and livestock to the marshes along River Baro. According to the IRNA conducted, Maiwut is estimated to host 25,750 while Pagak has between 2-3,000 returnees, IDPs and vulnerable communities. World Vision and partners responded to almost half of the affected population with the rest unattended. On flooding, according to OCHA report of 27 September 2019, an estimated 46,000 people are displaced in Maiwut County.In May 2019 this year there were reports of flooding from the former Warrap State. Twic, Gogrial East Gogrial west and Tonj North. An IRNA was conducted between 11-17 September 2019 and estimated 325, 940 have been displaced in Gogrial West, Gogrial East and Tonj North. OCHA according to 27 September report estimates the figure as 300,000 displaced.According to local authorities the internally displaced due to inter communal fight in Aliek (estimated 370 people), and in Wunlit (about 1,000) are still displaced and staying with relatives and host communities as tensions between the affected communities persist. Local leadership reportedly working to resolve the tensions. Beside inter-communal conflict, flooding has also been a major factor leading to displacement.

2 - Activities

1.1.1 Initial Community Engagement meetings to understand the community and enhance their capacity to participate in the project. Training and orientation sessions on the processes of engagement will be emphasized Do no harm, protection and gender considerations. A conflict sensitive framework for programing will be put in place.1. 1.2 Training IDPs,returnees and Communities and staff in Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP) Principles to enhance safe programming and accountability to affected populations will be undertaken.1.1.3 Strengthen and train the community and TADO staff on the formation of Beneficiary Accountability Committees and other feedback and complaints mechanisms throughout the project cycle. A feedback desk will be setup during distribution. Committees will take into account gender and age, ensuring that committee members are representative of women, youth and elderly as well as people with special needs.1.1.4 Initial Needs Assessment to be conducted both direct and inter agency to identify beneficiaries’ needs for Emergency Shelter and Non Food Items paying attention to the needs of women, men, girls and boys. Existing lists by WFP will be considered but verified and validated. 1.1.5 Carry out verification and registration of persons in readiness for distribution (focusing on the specific needs of women, men, girls and boys). Household interviews will be used where feasible. Beneficiary Accountability Committees will be formed and participate. 1.1.6 Awareness raising including mainstreaming protection and gender. Beneficiary Accountability Committees formed will be trained on generating and dissemination of key messages. Do no harm principles will be adopted? Awareness creation teams will be made up of local members of the community, trained and led by a member of staff.1.1. 7 Distribution of Emergency Shelter and Non Food Items as per identified and verified needs of the Beneficiaries. Efficient and equitable distribution approaches will be planned and implemented in consultation with the affected populations through the Beneficiary Accountability Committees and in Coordination with cluster and state authorities. Logistical arrangements will be made with core pipeline and ensure that there is adequate storage and security arrangements. Relevant Stakeholders will be consulted on which distribution locations are most suitable for safe and dignified access to and receipt of shelter and NFIs. Beneficiary committees, other S/NFI and protection partners and local authorities will be looped in.1.1.8 Monitoring and Evaluation and Learning including post distribution monitoring. This will be an ongoing activity culminating into post-distribution monitoring. This will be done at least 3 weeks after distribution to evaluate effectiveness, appropriateness and coverage. 1.1.9 Reporting to funding partners and Beneficiaries as well as sharing with cluster and implementing partners will be an ongoing activity. NOTE: TADO will make use of planned Humanitarian Hubs for its operation in the counties targeted. TADO's reliance on these humanitarian hubs is out of:- 1. They are strategically placed in our target counties and their proximity to our target locations. 2.The need to access reliable communication and internet for report writing. 3.Networking with other agencies implementing similar or various other projects and easier involvement in joint missions 4.Safety of staff and safety of S/NFI items. An example of these hubs will be the Mandeng Humanitarian Hub which will serve both Nasir and Ulang Counties.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

The project is legally binding according to the laws of South Sudan, It targets each segment of the population differently depending on the needs identified. Women and girls are generally affected disproportionately to men and boys and this is taken into account when designing protection interventions. For instance, Women & young girls walking long distances to access water or food are exposed to greater risk of abduction and SGBV compared to men and boys. Parties would be required to adhere to the rules before any intervention is carried out, TADO in collaboration with Protection clusters/partners on the ground and the local authority will make sure the areas are safe from any danger to the populations/beneficiaries and environment for successful implementation of the project.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

TADO is a static partner in all target locations with long-term presence and interventions in other sectors such as Health and Education. TADO will deliberately and pro-actively Increase engagement with local actors, including authorities and NNGOs to both strengthen the local networks for humanitarian response and at the same time inculcate ownership of the interventions. Local partners and local authorities will be encouraged to take an active role in the implementation and sustainability of the project as a core exit strategy. Beneficiaries will be expected to take a lead role in the ownership of the interventions. In principle, the exit point for emergency assistance is the moment when the needs which initially justified the intervention have been satisfactorily and durably addressed. This may be in absolute terms, meeting local or global humanitarian standards; relative to the capacity of humanitarian interventions to address such needs; or may take into account other donors, partners, and local capacity, and their respective comparative advantages. In practice, however, time constraints of emergency funding will often dictate the end of the assistance. Consequently, emphasis on the durable outcomes of emergency assistance, and on establishing synergies with development and/or other social protection programs, will be a priority for TADO from the outset. TADO will prioritize interventions that will move the beneficiary communities from emergency to recovery involving participatory planning and implementation processes to identify priorities and to optimize capacity building, ownership and sustainability by beneficiaries. Exit strategy activities will include but not limited to training on hazard-resistant construction, support for repair and retrofitting, and linking beneficiaries to existing financial and livelihood assistance programs.
