Project Summary

An estimated 41% of primary schools in Nigeria have access to improved sanitation facilities, while 51% have access to improved water sources. Government ministries, departments and agencies, as well as development partners, donors, non-governmental organizations and host communities, are all involved in implementing WASH in Schools. A poor enabling environment was identified as the major bottleneck, followed by inadequate supply of WASH facilities and soap for hand washing.
These project interventions aim at the ﬁrst emergency response to provide protective, inclusive, safe access to WASH for Covid-19 and conﬂict affected children in NE Nigeria. The project will achieve improved access for girls/boys to equitable inclusive access to WASH facilities, improve education and learning opportunities and ensure key messages delivered to target beneﬁciaries on how to protect from Covid-19 and assure regular hand washing and health and hygiene practices.
The aim of this project will be:
• expanding the geographic reach of the WASH interventions in schools by working with school-based structures including school-based management committees, teachers, head teachers and students;
• providing WASH facilities and improving health and hygiene practices through awareness raising and training programmes
• supply WASH materials like soaps, hygiene kits, hand washing facilities, water tanks, toilets and where it is needed most;

1 - Needs

Reports from DTM R.XXIV released by IOM and NEMA in November 2019 and the Initial Rapid Needs Assessment (IRNA) conducted by the Adamawa State Inter-sectorial Working Group (ISWG) in October 2019 has shown that most of the cholera prone and affected communities are vulnerable to water borne and water related diseases due to generally poor access to Water supply, poor Sanitation and poor Hygiene practices. Both reports have recommended the following to mitigate the re-occurrence and spread of the disease in the future.1. Increase access and use of WASH facilities among school children and teachers. a. Rehabilitation of solar powered Boreholes in schools b. Drilling of Solar powered borehole in schools c. Rehabilitation of dysfunctional Hand Pump Boreholes. in schools d. Distribution of Aquatabs67mg to the schools and host communities e. Pool Tester (Dual test kit) two per school for onsite water quality testing Free Residual Chlorine FRC f. DPD 1 tablet (tablet X250) for FRC test in schools g. Phenol Red Tablet (tablet) for Ph test in schools h. HTH Granular Chlorine (HTH 65-70) for water treatment i. Rehabilitate/construct schools’ gender segregated latrines (2 blocks of latrines), latrine cleaning kits (rain boots, overcoats, plastic aprons, nose masks, heavy-duty hand gloves, germicide) and Hand Washing Facilities 2. Reduce the incidence and prevalence of water- and sanitation-related diseases among schoolchildren. a. Development, printing and distribution of WASH IEC materials (i.e. posters, leaﬂets, comics) on coronavirus prevention in local language b. Disinfection of the school premises for quality education, Plan will procure two buckets (45 kg.) of HTH chlorine for Environmental disinfection and knapsack/sprayers of schoolc. Provide hygiene kits to learners and schools for use of teachers/SBMC members 3. Promote good hygiene practices and ensure a child-friendly school environment for effective learning and Increase awareness of hygiene practices among schoolchildren, which can be passed on to other members of households and the community a. Selection and Training of Volunteer Hygiene Promoters (VHPs) for school disinfection and chlorination one per school b. Formation and training of School health clubs, 25 members of School Health Clubs (SHCs) per School who will be trained on hygiene promotion using child to child approach of hygiene promotion. The CHCs will participate in hygiene promotion sessions in the respective SHLS and schools and also during celebrations of WASH days such as World Water Day, Global Handwashing Day, World Toilet Day and Menstrual Hygiene Dayc. Formation and training of WASHCOMs which include teachers and studentsd. Distribution of 20liter solid waste disposal bins (at a ratio of one per class) as well as 240 litre waste collection bin per school Standards for digging solid waste disposal sites will be followed to ensure that pits are a safe distance from the school to avoid contamination of water or spread of other communicable diseases resulting from accumulation of waste within the school and host communities e. Provide tools (e.g. wheelbarrows, spades, rakes, and shovels) to aid environmental health activities such digging of waste pits and emptying waste in the pits. f. Plan will continue to organize celebrations and awareness-raising events to mark key international WASH days (e.g. World Water Day, Global Toilet Day, and Global Handwashing Day).
