Project Summary

In spite, there have been efforts done by humanitarian organizations including UN agencies to improve the nutritional situation and restoring livelihood communities in Somaliland, the child, and maternal mortality rates in Somaliland continue to be among the highest worldwide, and also Favorable rainfall between April and June 2018 improved pasture and water availability for livestock and boosted market conditions in some areas. however, Malnutrition rates across Somaliland remain very high and, in 2019, nearly 1 million children will be acutely malnourished in the whole of Somalia, including more than 177,000 children suffering from severe acute malnutrition. (OCHA, 20 Jan 2019).
Findings from 33 separate nutrition surveys conducted by FSNAU and partners in June and July 2019 indicate the persistence of widespread acute malnutrition in Somalia due to a combination of factors, including food insecurity, high morbidity, low immunization, and vitamin-A supplementation, and poor care practices. In addition, At the national level, the median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) has remained Serious (10–14.9%) over the past three seasons (13.8% in 2019 Gu, 12.6% in 2018 Deyr and 14.0% in 2018 Gu). A Critical (15-29.9%) prevalence of GAM was observed in 10 out of the 33 population groups surveyed, with more areas likely to deteriorate from Serious to Critical between August and October 2019. FSNAU FEWS-NET Technical Release, 1 September 2019 [EN/SO]
In response to the aforementioned negative impacts of protracted and recurrent drought left loss of community livelihoods assets mainly livestock, resulted huge displacement created a large number of IDPs camps, and disease outbreaks- as result of severe malnutrition situation. in line with 2019 Somalia HRP, Somaliland Ministry of heathy development strategy of nutrition as well as ADO commitment to support of most drought-affected people living now in IDPs camps specifically vulnerable groups (mother, children, disabled and elderly people), The project will treat Severe Acute Malnourished children for the purpose of reducing morbidity and mortality related to malnutrition through integrated Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM). The aim is to achieve improved health and nutrition status of children 0-59 months old and pregnant and Lactating Women through integrated CMAM and increase access to health and nutrition facilities and services through community mobilization and sensitization in Ainabo district of Sool region. the proposed project also will have interventions including the provision of life-saving services to address critical malnutrition, mortality rate in Sool region, for improving nutritional situation and health through provision of SFP to 990 HHs in IDPs camp and OTPs for severe malnourished children under 5 years and and also constructing and strengthening 4 health centers in two main IDPs camps in Ainabo town having more than 4000 HHs and other IDPs camps in Oog village hosting 3000 HHs, and training 12 health workers/ volunteers capacities with skills and Basic Nutrition Services Package including immunization at facility and community based approach to reduce high acute malnutrition, or depletion of assets indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3)1 or worse outcomes through December 2019 as predicted by Somalia 2019 Post Gu FSNAU FEWS-NET.
The project will target most drought-affected people those yet susceptible to nutritional situations particularly U5 children, pregnant, lactating women, disabled and elderly people living in the IDP Camps in the Ainabo district of the Sool region.

Mitigation Measures

2a> 1. Involve and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to Nutrition needs. Different criteria may affect the power dynamics. 2. Consult women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, chronically ill, older persons, pregnant and lactating women and marginalised persons to collect accurate information about their specific food and nutritional requirements and preferences for food distribution points and livelihood services. 3. Reinforce the capacity of the community - encourage collective livelihood projects over those that benefit individuals. Collective practices contribute to community protection and resilience, particularly in rural community economies which are closely linked with social networks, community structures and cultural practices. 4d. 1. Be transparent with the affected populations by providing them with accessible and timely information on selection criteria for targeted assistance, organisational procedures and processes that affect them. 2. Set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Eg. place feedback surveys in food baskets, feedback box at distribution site, phone lines. 3. Nutrition staff and relief committees should be representative of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, disability) and all staff and members should receive protection mainstreaming training. 4b> Protection mainstreaming 1. Collect and use data disaggregated by age, gender and diversity to effectively inform programming. 2. Ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable - Identify and prioritise the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalised groups (refer to Vulnerable Categories guidance note). 3. Apply a systematic outreach process in registering beneficiaries – otherwise vulnerable persons, such as older people, may easily be excluded as other more mobile and vocal population groups are registered.
