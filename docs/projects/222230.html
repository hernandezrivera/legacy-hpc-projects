Project Summary

The acute food insecurity and malnutrition situation in South Sudan continues to deteriorate due to an economic crisis, climatic shocks—primarily widespread flooding—conflict, and insecurity. During the harvest and post-harvest period from December 2024 to March 2025, an estimated 6.1 million people (45%) are expected to experience IPC Phase 3 (Crisis) or above. Of these, 1.71 million people are projected to be in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency), while 31,000 South Sudanese returnees are anticipated to face IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe).
The latest IPC analysis highlights that over 85% of returnees fleeing Sudan will face acute food insecurity during the upcoming lean season starting in April. These returnees will constitute nearly half of those facing catastrophic hunger, struggling to rebuild their lives amid an unprecedented economic crisis, severe flooding, and resource prioritization challenges as needs outpace funding. With the onset of the lean season, food security is expected to deteriorate further, with an estimated 7.69 million people (57%) projected to be in IPC Phase 3 or above. This includes 2.53 million in IPC Phase 4 and 63,000 in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) (South Sudan IPC, November 2024).
The nutrition situation is also projected to worsen, with 2.1 million children aged 6–59 months suffering or expected to suffer from elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025, including 650,000 cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). Additionally, approximately 1.11 million pregnant and breastfeeding women (PBW) are either suffering or expected to experience elevated levels of acute malnutrition during the same period. An estimated 67% of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr El Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity, and Warrap.
CINA's Contributions to Address Food Security and Livelihoods
FSL CO 1: Humanitarian Food Assistance
CINA will provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity, and coping strategies for vulnerable populations. Key interventions include:
Reaching individuals from the most food-insecure households in IPC 4 and 5 areas not covered by WFP through cash-based responses and in-kind food assistance such as GFD (General Food Distribution) for blanket, seasonal, or targeted needs.
Scaling up hybrid rations (in-kind + cash components).
Deploying cash and voucher-based transfers to meet food security and nutrition objectives.
Distributing food, fishing kits, and quick-maturing vegetable seeds via IRRM (Integrated Rapid Response Mechanism).
Providing conditional cash transfers to enhance social safety nets through investments in assets, training, and community work groups/planning committees.
Offering conditional food assistance through food-for-assets initiatives.
FSL CO 2 & 3: Livelihoods and Resilience
CINA aims to enhance and sustain emergency food production and strengthen resilience through:
Complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing, and livestock support.
Livestock treatment and vaccination, disease surveillance, and training for Community Animal Health Workers and extension service providers.
Capacity building in agronomic practices, pest management, fisheries, and irrigation (e.g., training on water pump use, ox ploughing, and storage granaries).
Provision of tools and materials for fishing, boating, and bee-keeping groups.
Support for Village Savings and Loan Associations, value addition training, and vocational/business-related activities.
Pre-positioning of grain and seed protection kits.
Establishing or strengthening community-based Early Warning Systems (EWS) with necessary equipment and dissemination of flood warning information through radio programs.
Conducting assessments and reviewing existing early action protocols.
By implementing these targeted strategies, CINA seeks to alleviate the ongoing food crisis and build resilience among the most vulnerable populations.

1. Needs

There is going to be an acute food insecurity and malnutrition situation in South Sudan because of the economic crisis, climatic shocks - primarily widespread flooding - conflict and insecurity. The inflow of returnees and refugees fleeing the conflict in Sudan has exacerbated the situation, putting additional pressure on an already fragile country. Between September and November 2024, an estimated 6.3 million people (47 percent of the population analysed) are classified in IPC Phase 3, 4 and 5 (Crisis, Emergency and/or Catastrophe). Of this total, 1.71 million people are facing critical levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) – and a further 41,000 people are facing catastrophic levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). The population in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) includes 10,000 people in Malakal County (Upper Nile State) and an estimated 31,000 South Sudanese returnees who have fled Sudan because of the ongoing conflict. Compared to the same period last year, this indicates an increase of approximately 500,000 people in Phase 3 or above. About 3.03 million people in Upper Nile and Jonglei states will face acute food insecurity situation starting December 2024 - July 2025 (South Sudan IPC, 18 November 2024) with 1.16 million in stressed, 1.316 in crisis and 555,000 in emergency. The nutrition situation has also deteriorated, with an estimated 2.1 million children aged 6-59 months suffering and/or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025,including 650,000 cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). About 1.11 million pregnant or breastfeeding women (PBW) are also suffering and/or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition in the sameperiod. An estimated 67 percent of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr El Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap. Jonglei, Upper Nile and Lakes states carried the bulky of 24 counties (crisis - 20 counties and emergency - 9 counties) with elevated levels of acute malnutrition. In the current analysis period (July to September 2024), 53 counties are classified in IPC AMN Phase 3 or above (Serious or worse). During the post-harvest period of October 2024 to March 2025, the acute malnutrition situation is expected to deteriorate in 13 counties, while improvement in acute malnutrition is expected in only four counties. The AMN classification in 76 counties is projected to remain the same in the post-harvest projection period. All these are due to South Sudan’s economy which is experiencing a macroeconomic crisis, characterized by worsening currency depreciation, high food prices and declining household purchasing power. Forced displacement, interruptions in humanitarian aid delivery, and localized conflict and insecurity - primarily in the Greater Luakpiny/Nassir County, Upper Nile, and parts of Jonglei and Lakes states. Floods and dry spells contribute to low agricultural production. Flooding was 10 percent higher in 2024 compared to 2023 and the floods not only displaced people but also disrupted farming, delivery of humanitarian support, and contributed to low access to health services in the face of increased disease burden, driven by poor sanitation practices, thus increasing the risk of acute malnutrition. Unpredictable and extreme weather patterns, poor access to quality seeds and use of traditional farming methods negatively impacted agricultural production, resulting in increased humanitarian food assistance needs. Therefore, CINA will require USD 500,000 in 2025 to implement activities that will provide access to lifesaving, resilience and reduction of food insecurity and initiate activities that will cushion communities in time of disaster and help to reduce seasonal starvation and hunger among the lactating mothers and children.

2. Project activities

The overall target population for the 11 counties in Upper Nile, Jonglei and Lakes States is 75,000 people (45,000 women, 30,000 men) both in IPC 5, 4 and 3. CINA will implement the below FSL interventions in the target counties in line with FSL COs for HNRP 2025: 1. Unconditional food assistance in IPC4+ locations not reached by WFP (through cash response) and in-kind food assistance (including GFD: blanket/ seasonal/ targeted and GFD plus); 2. Food, fishing & quick maturing vegetable seeds; 3. Conditional cash transfers (social skills & safety nets) through investments in assets, training, or communal work groups/ planning committees etc.; 4. Conditional food assistance through food for assets; 5 Complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support; 6. Distribution of agriculture seeds & tools, fishing, vegetable & crop kits; 7. Nutrition vouchers (plus poultry & goat distribution and Seed fairs; 8. Emergency food & livelihood support through distribution of fishing kits/ vegetable kits, rapid response kits (fish kit/ cow pea & three variety of fast maturing vegetable seed); 9. Livestock treatment and vaccination, livestock disease surveillance, Community Animal Health Workers and community-based extension service providers (CBESP); 10. Training and inputs support; 11. Good Agronomic practices (e.g. farmer field schools, Demo plots, post-harvest management etc); 12. Integrated Pest Management (e.g. FAW, Desert locusts, Pesticides etc); 13. Fisheries Training; 14. Provision of water pumps; 15. Irrigation training/support; 16. Ox Ploughs & training; 17. Establish PHL Storage granaries (using locally available resources as much as possible); 18. Provision of Canoe, Fishing, Boating materials/tools; 19. Procurement from local traders (Agro traders, Market groups etc) through seed fair; Produce/products aggregation; 20. Establish and support Village Savings/Loans Associations; 21. Processing Equipment for value addition (fish, meat, vegetables, fruit, milk preservation); 22. Training on value addition (fish, meat, vegetables, fruit, milk preservation, honey); 23. Vocational & other enterprise/business related support; 24. Provision of hives for beekeeping livelihood groups; 25. Install or strengthen community based EWS by providing require equipment (rain gauge, disaster prediction information tools, etc.); 26. Support downscaling of climate information-flood early warning information through radio programme (Radio show, solar radios etc.); 27. Pre-position grain and seed protection kits (hermetic bags, tilted buckets, grain bag etc.); 28. Provide flood adaptive seeds and approaches (rice, floating gardens), tools and fishing inputs to farmers; 29. Conduct assessment and review existing early action protocol (EAP); and 30. Provision of Fuel efficient stoves. Therefore, CINA will require an envelope of USD 500,000 in 2025 to implement the above mentioned activities that will provide access to lifesaving, resilience and reduction of food insecurity and initiate activities that will cushion communities in time of disaster and help to reduce seasonal starvation and hunger among the lactating mothers and children. CINA has FSL projects running in Lakes and Upper Nile into 2025 and will align this projects with FSL Cluster HNRP 2025 strategy, by following the guidelines on Harmonization of Food Ration, Intervention Modality, Target Complementarities, Conditionality of food, cash assistance and Response Strategy for returnees from Sudan as well as working with HDP actors to ensure sustainability and localization of food aid. The project will be coordinated with other sectors (Nutrition, WASH, Education) to strengthen synergies and will integrate protection dimensions by caring out FSL activities that contribute to safety, dignity, and integrity of at-risk individuals/groups
