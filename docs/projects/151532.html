Project Summary

UNICEF proposes to enhance access of the conflict-affected population in eastern Ukraine to quality maternal and child health services by strengthening capacity of health workers, increasing awareness of Infant and Young Child feeding practices and immunisation, supporting rehabilitation of health facilities essentially through the procurement of supplies and equipment, and provision of supportive services for people living with HIV, children and adolescents, and where possible improvement of water and sanitation facilities.

Needs

As the conflict in eastern Ukraine enters its fifth year, quality of care and access to health care continues to worsen, particularly in areas along the contact line of both non-government (NGCA) and government-controlled areas (GCA) of Luhansk and Donetsk. An estimated 1.3 million people remain in need of essential health services, 40% of which are children and 60% of which are female, and face continuous barriers to accessing care. For households with children within 5km of the contact line in GCA, barriers include cost of medicine (30%), crossing checkpoints (31%), distance to facilities (23%), cost of travel to facility (11%) and lack of facilities (11%). Local health networks continue to face disruptions in provision of services due to lack of supplies, drugs, and equipment, limited preventative care, facilities requiring rehabilitation, lack of referral pathways, and separation of facilities from major health centres on both sides. According to a Health Resources Availability Mapping System (HeRAMS) assessment, 52% of GCA health facilities were damaged partially or fully and 16% were partially or fully non-functional. As of 2017, 130 health facilities still require rehabilitation. Furthermore, lack of adequately trained medical staff continues to be a major constraint, and was identified with HeRAMS as a major cause (73%) of health facilities in GCA not functioning to full capacity and resulting in poor quality of service delivery. The main needs of NGCA are similar to those of GCA but pose additional operational challenges. Isolated and underfunded NGCA facilities are lacking qualified staff, with many health workers having left NGCA at the start of the conflict. Challenges for women and children requiring basic and specialised health services include, lack of evidence-based maternal, obstetric and pediatric care, shortage of medication, qualified staff, and equipment for deliveries and postpartum care, and shortage of maternity wards. As of 2017, around 27,500 women were in need of maternal health services in NGCA and GCA. As of 2017, women in NGCA continue to cross the contact line to use maternities in GCA, putting themselves at risk at crossing points and overburdening facilities that remain functional. Infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices remain low (25.5%), and a third of mothers reporting cessation of breastfeeding due to stress, and inadequate support from health workers. Routine immunisation continues to be a concern nationwide, including in conflict-affected areas. Low coverage pre-conflict resulted in outbreaks of preventable diseases, including the ongoing measles outbreak, and any new outbreak is not be ruled out. While data on coverage is available for GCA, lack of access and reporting limits the understanding of the situation in NGCA. Stockouts of certain vaccines as well as health worker and public perceptions of vaccination continue to pose challenges to increasing coverage and ensuring population immunity, highlighting the need for strengthened health education and communication campaigns for caregivers and health workers. The ongoing conflict has increased vulnerability of the population to HIV/AIDS, especially considering the increase in substance abuse, termination of substitution therapy for injecting drug users, and limited access that children and adolescents have to HIV preventative and treatment services. Furthermore, AIDS centres have been separated by the contact line, leaving newly created centres lacking experience and capacity to implement HIV control programmes in their respective territories. There is a high need to implement the “Test and Treat” approach recommended by WHO, as well as to support case management for HIV/AIDS positive children and adolescents on antiretroviral therapy (ART), support testing and prevention services, and support prevention of Mother-to Child transmission (PMTCT), in both NGCA and GCA.

Activities & outcomes

Objective 1: Children, adolescents and women equitably access essential Maternal and Child Health (MCH) and HIV/AIDS services in conflict-affected areas. - Activity 1.1: Provision of essential medicines for pregnant and lactating women and children under 5 through e-vouchers - Activity 1.2: Prevent and respond to vaccine preventable diseases through targeted vaccination interventions - Activity 1.3: Scale up “Test and Treat” approach among children and adolescents in conflict-affected areas - Activity 1.4: Support conflict-affected regions towards elimination of HIV Mother-to-Child Transmission Objective 2: Caregivers and parents in conflict-affected areas access behaviour-change communication of key MCH and promotional messages to improve health and nutrition practices, (including Early Child Development, IYCF, emergency first aid, and immunisation). - Activity 2.1: Information outreach through local media and dissemination of MCH education materials for families living in conflict-affected areas - Activity 2.2: Training programmes and information materials for parents and caregivers distributed on emergency first aid for children- Activity 2.3: Support and promotion of breastfeeding counselling in affected areas for pregnant and lactating women - Activity 2.4: Support to Mother’s clubs groups Objective 3: Disrupted essential care services for women and children in facilities in conflict-affected areas are re-established through capacity development and provision of essential drugs, medical supplies, and equipment. - Activity 3.1: Needs assessment on gaps in healthcare facilities in GCA and NGCA - Activity 3.2: Capacity building of health workers on evidence-based nutrition, immunisation, and MCH and HIV/AIDS practices - Activity 3.3: Provision of medical supplies and equipment to health facilities in GCA and NGCA
