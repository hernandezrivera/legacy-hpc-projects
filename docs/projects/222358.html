1. Needs

At least 4.9 million children are in need of humanitarian assistance in 2024. Most vulnerable school-aged children including those with special needs/ disabilities (3 to 17 years) continue to face an array of shocks including climate change impacts with current flooding which continues to disrupt education, with at least 422 education facilities affected and 158,832 leaners including 92,169 girls directly affected (UNICEF, October 2024). This in addition to submerging of key facilities including WASH facilities; with ongoing cholera outbreak in the country, presents a further implication more so in remote, flooded and underserved school facilities more so in hard to reach areas of Pibor, Jur River and Rumbek North counties. The floods have affected 42 out of 79 counties with submerging and destruction of learning facilities, multiple displacement of boys and girls including teachers have continued to lead to learning loss and increased drop-out rates. Further, Sudan crisis continued influx of boys, girls, men and women; presents a further strain on meagre existing education facilities. The education sector is further impacted by a protracted conflict which has been compounded by school destruction by floods and displacements due to conflict (MOGEI, 2024; UNICEF Country Reports 2024). This is in addition to conflict induced displacement of teachers and children, killings of teachers and the risk of revenge killings of boys in schools which have further contributed to lack of retention and access to schools. With the country facing unprecedented food insecurity levels (6.3 million people currently facing severe food insecurity in 2025 –IPC November 2024); could further hamper school attendance in addition to significant impacts on brain development of learners, with some resorting to other livelihood activities instead of schools. Girls have in addition been victim of early and forced marriages, attributed to cultural norms tied to high dowries, further impacting their access to education. Children with disabilities continue to face additional risks attributed to lack of assistive or mobility devices further leading to low retention rates among children with special needs; further compounded by irregular payment of teacher salaries, inadequate learning spaces, lack of school supplies and untrained teachers (HNO 2023; IPC Nov 2024). Stop Poverty Communal Initiative (SPOCI) has recently successfully implemented education in emergences projects in Rumbek North via UNICEF support in addition to previous projects supported via SSHF/ UNICEF in Pibor and Wau, that will scale up 2025 initiatives, that will support a total of 3,296 people comprised of 1500 girls and 1500 boys including those with disabilities, 148 men and 148 women which are in line and correspond to the education cluster objectives aimed at improving equitable access to safe and protective education services for emergency affected IDPs, returnees, spontaneous refugees, and host community children including children with disabilities and youth (girls and boys); Improve the quality of gender and disability-inclusive education services including where appropriate use of assistive devices for those with disabilities and teachers' capacity to protect the wellbeing of all children and prevent the negative impact of the emergencies on learning environments; and strengthening the education system’s response capacity of the school community to mitigate the impact of existing emergencies on learners, teachers, including PTAs and communities. Proposed actions will be integrated with WASH, Protection, Health and Nutrition integrated programming while taking into consideration environmentally friendly approaches. SPOCI is a national NGO with over 8 years programmatic presence in Pibor and Wau in addition to strong and competent team including staff redeployment to scale up interventions based on needs.

2. Project activities

SPOCI will support; • Enrolment of children (girls and boys) in pre-primary, primary and secondary schools/learning centers •Establishment of temporary learning spaces and classrooms(established in line with Inter-INEE's Minimum Standards for Education classrooms • Establishment of gender segregated latrines and water points • Rehabilitation of gender segregated latrines and water points • Provision of sanitation and hygiene materials (hand washing soap, sanitizers, masks, etc.) and PPE for students and teachers. • Provision of emergency-affected children quality education recreational materials, teaching and learning materials for ECD, primary, and secondary school education • Training of teachers on EiE pedagogical skills and child-centred methodologies to improve teaching and learning process for emergency-affected children • Training and updating of teachers and learners on PSS, CP, and referral mechanisms including CP/GBV, health and nutrition. • Train SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management, governance, psychosocial support, and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR)/ climate change •Support prevention and mitigation of communicable diseases through training school communities by providing appropriate Information and Education and Communication (IEC) materials(i.e. posters, leaflets) and support conducting back to learning campaigns. Routine monitoring and reporting including Cluster reporting, 5Ws submission.
