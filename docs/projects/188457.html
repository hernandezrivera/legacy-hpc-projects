Project Summary

Syria is one of the 92 AMC (Advanced Market Commitment) countries to be supported under the COVAX Facility with COVID-19 vaccines.
The aim of this project is to provide financial and technical support to cover 70% of the population (14,391,409 population) during 2022 with 2 doses of COVID 19 vaccines.
The targeted population is over 18 years old with more emphasize on priority categories, The health workforce (including frontline social workers and teachers), older adults (>55 years) and people with comorbidities as identified in the National Deployment Vacciantion Plan (NDVP are based on the CTAG’s recommendations, the Strategic Advisory Group of Experts on Immunization (SAGE) values framework as well as the COVAX facility fair allocation prioritization roadmap).
This project will support the operational cost of vaccine roll out in 14 Syrian governorates, including Northeast Syria and part of Northwest Syria GOS controlled areas. Under the current project, 92 hospitals will be used as service delivery points to provide vaccinations, along with 979 primary health care facilities all over the country. In rural areas and camps, 341 mobile teams will be deployed to cover that targeted population.
Services will be provided by trained hospital teams and routine immunization personnel in mobile teams. This number of facilities and associated teams is preliminary and subject to change based on ongoing microplanning. In total more that 5000 vaccinators will participate in covid vaccination activities during the year.
Implementation across Northeast Syria will follow the current experiences of the Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI) microplanning through 109 fixed facilities (hospitals and primary healthcare centers) and 94 mobile teams. Formal and informal settlements will be targeted in the same way. Microplanning is covering camps across Northeast Syria.
For North West Syria NWS, In the absence of the ministry of health, the WHO in partnership with the Syrian immunization group (SIG) and implementing partner supports the COVID-19 vaccination services in NWS. The project will expand vaccination sites and fully support the human resources, operation costs including logistics, transportation and maintaining technical support, supervision, training at the governorate, district, and vaccination site level. Under the guidance of WHO, immunization technical committee will be responsible for the technical aspect of the implementation of COVID-19 vaccination ensuring proper micro-planning, smooth deployment, implementation, and monitoring of COVID-19 vaccination in accordance with national deployment vaccination plan (NDVP) updated strategy. This project will support and sustain the operation cost of 200 COVID-19 vaccination sites. Each vaccination site consists of four team members including two vaccinators, one data registry, one social mobilizer. During the implementation, different approaches will be applied, such as fixed, outreach, and mobile vaccination sites to reach the most vulnerable populations residing in host communities, camps, and hard-to-reach communities. In addition, the WHO through implementing partner will conduct independent qualitative and quantitative assessment and monitoring of performance, coverage, and quality of campaign-related activities for all targeted communities. This project through trained staff and using of standardized checklist/ questionnaire, will assess COVID-19 pre, intra and post-vaccination campaign including vaccination team, social mobilization performance and beneficiaries’ satisfaction surveys at community level to evaluate the vaccination status during the campaign and identify gaps and areas for improvement.

4 - Needs

In Syria, as of 15 January 2022 a total number of 180,983 laboratory-confirmed cases have been reported in WoS. The CFR was highest in GoS areas 5.8% and the total confirmed death cases associates with COVID-19 in NWS was 2,349 (account to 34.48% of total deaths). Given the limited testing across Syria, it is likely the actual number of cases far exceeds official figures, with significant numbers of asymptomatic and mild cases in particular going undetected. The number of health workers tested positive for COVID-19 was 6,736 healthcare workers of them 1,543 health workers reported by MoH/ GoS including 46 deceased cases. AR was highest in Al-Hasakeh (2,066), Idleb (1,937), and Aleppo (1,183). The lowest incidence rates were in Rural Damascus, Dier Ezzor, and Dar’a. Epidemiological analysis indicates to three waves of COVID-19 in Syria at WoS level, the first peak was in November 2020, the second peak was April 2021 and the third wave peak was in October 2021 with (1,240) 7- days average cases. COVID 19 vaccination rollout has started in May 2022 in Syria, until mid-January 2022 8.5% received at least one dose of COVID-19 vaccines. This includes 4.5% who are fully vaccinated out of the total population. WHO and UNICEF along with local NGOs and other health will continue to support health authorities to increase access and demand for vaccination.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

There is a protection risk for health workers and beneficiaries who may be exposed to transmission of covid19 infection, this will be mitigated by using PPEs by vaccination teams during trainings and vaccine administration. The pre-registration system will help in avoiding mass gathering at health facility level. Hostilities in Syria continue to take a heavy toll on the lives and livelihoods of civilians who have already endured a decade of violence, multiple displacements, and a major economic downturn. The COVID-19 pandemic has only worsened the situation, adding new layers of complexity and hardship. Therefore, the availability, access and sustainability of essential health services and medical supplies was and continues to be always in risk due to the security situation, internal displacement, and donors’ support. internal displacement and donors’ support.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Low vaccine acceptance might be the main risk beyond project capacity. WHO and UNICEF developed special communication strategy for demand generation as mitigation measures. Access in some areas may not be possible due security problems. Therefore, the design of the service delivery mechanism under this project using different approaches, including fixed facility base teams (Hospitals and health centres in each district) and mobile teams to cover hard-to-reach areas and camps. Deploying health workers for the same areas could reduce the security risk in addition to coordination with all parties in the ground is being addressed and used in routine immunization and mass vaccination campaigns. Regarding the COVID-19 vaccination roll out there are several major risks: • Vaccine hesitancy and vaccine acceptance. • Options to secure the vaccines, in the long run, may be limited resulting in increased humanitarian needs and the needs for booster doses. • Availability of funding for COVAX Vaccine to support the roll out of COVID-19 vaccination• Vaccination in security compromised areas • Frequent population movement and new displacement due to ongoing clashes in parts of NWS• Potential impact of cesation of UNSCR in July 2022 mainly on vaccines logistic and storage
