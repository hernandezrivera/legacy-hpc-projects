Project Summary

It is estimated that 3.6 million learners will be in need of education services in 2023. According to the prioritization for South Sudan Education Cluster 2023, about 244,233 people are in need of essential education services from the six counties of Lakes state (Yirol East: 41,840, Yirol West: 35,701, Awerial: 56,299), Jonglei state (Duk: 64,819, Twic East: 32,925) and Western Equatoria State (Tambura: 12,649). Following the COVID-19, flooding and intercommunal violence, the closure of schools and broader socio-economic hardship exacerbated existing education challenges and inequities. About 2.2 million children were already out of school. Additionally, gross enrolment decreased by roughly 7 per cent from 2022 (2,523,204).
According to Education Needs Assessment (ENA 2022) and data from SAMS, there are 5,904 schools (744 ECDs, 4,441 primary schools, and 413 secondary schools) across South Sudan which reported enrolment in 2022 and are thus, presumed to be open. County level data from 66 county level education officials interviewed in the ENA reported 802 ECD, 3,762 primary schools and 298 secondary schools (4,862) as open and functional across the country.
The main reasons for school closures from May 2022, as reported by headteachers were flooding, followed by a reported lack of support/systems in place to support re-opening after closures, and then looting by armed groups. Teachers fleeing, students fleeing and insecurity in or around the schools were the top most commonly reported reasons for schools not functioning. Other reasons for students' dropout from schools include cost associated with school fees, marriage, pregnancy, lack of food, domestic duties as well as cattle rearing. The most needs requested by all schools to operate were: recruitment of additional teachers, provision of WASH facilities, school feeding programme, teachers salaries/incentives, teaching and learning supplies, security for schools, rehabilitation of school infrastructures and training of teachers.
To meet the Education Cluster Objectives for HRP 2023, CINA will target 4,404 learners, teachers and community members: To provide access to schools by children and adolescents; build and/rehabilitate schools or TLS in line with INEE's minimum standards; Provide sanitation and hygiene materials for learners; Conduct assessment enrolment of out of school children; Provide food for education; Provide recreational materials and ECD kits; Provide assistive devices for children with disabilities and ensure classrooms and latrines are accessible to children with disabilities; Provide teaching and learning materials; Conduct training of teachers on EiE, basic pedagogy, learner centered methodologies, psychosocial support, child protection and referral mechanisms; Training of youth and adolescents on life-skills and basic vocational skills training; training of community education committees, local education officials and school management (PTAs/SMCs) on school management, psychosocial support, and disaster risk reduction in education; Provide appropriate IEC prevention and mitigation materials, Organize campaigns on back to school/learning, and Provide cash for young mothers to remain in school.
CINA has qualified personnel, outreach, governance and organizational structures and is currently implementing GESS and ECW MYRPSS besides child protection, MHPSS, GBV, EORE, and FSL programs in Lakes (Yirol West, Yirol East and Awerial). CINA will maximize its education capacity to accelerate learning for the crises affected out of school children to increase enrolment and retention while maintaining the quality of education, safeguarding and protection of learners. To ensure success, education services will be coordinated at state and national levels, while optimizing nexus efforts with humanitarian-peace-development actors towards promoting and enabling education during and beyond emergencies.

1 - Needs

According to the HNO 2022 and ENA 2021, an estimated 3.6 million girls and boys between ages 3 and 17 in conflict and crisis-affected areas are estimated to have inadequate or no access to education in 2022, including more than 125,000 teachers and members of school management committees requiring humanitarian assistance. This situation may not change much in 2023. In addition, over 139,000 refugee children will need education support in 2023. Increasing climate change, food insecurity and localized conflict exacerbate existing barriers, including inadequate school infrastructure, costs associated with accessing quality education, untrained education personnel, and irregular and insufficient teacher payments. Needs are particularly dire and may worsen in Unity, Jonglei, Upper Nile and Warrap states. In the ENA, fees associated with education, such as school contributions, volunteer teacher payments, uniforms and examination fees, were cited as one of the most significant barriers to regular attendance and drop-out. Distance to school, inadequate learning spaces, lack of school supplies and untrained teachers are other barriers to learners accessing education. Barriers are amplified for marginalized groups. For girls, early and forced marriage, and pregnancy were widely reported in the ENA as reasons for drop-out. During school closures, evidence suggests daughters were married to alleviate household economic stress through the “bride price”. Children with Disabilities (CwDs) also face numerous challenges in accessing quality education. According to the findings, most schools reported having no mobility or assistive devices. Over half of schools also reported their latrines were inaccessible for CwDs. Teachers are not trained to foster or deliver inclusive education and school communities are not sensitized on the need to create enabling environments for CwDs. Most learners do not get a meal before school and over 60 per cent of assessed schools do not provide food for learners. Hunger is a key barrier to education, resulting in poor academic performance, inconsistent attendance and eventual dropout. In South Sudan, findings from the Food Security and Livelihoods needs analysis indicate that over two-third of South Sudanese are at risk of severe hunger in 2023 (UN OCHA reports 3 November 2022) and children may drop out out of school due to hunger. One in three schools reported having no access to a functioning safe water source and less than half of schools have clean water and soap available. This increases the risk of illness, resulting in irregular attendance of learners and teachers. A lack of menstrual health management is a significant barrier for female learner retention and progression. Over 70 per cent of schools reported having a washroom for girls and half said they had never received dignity kits for female learners. Over 80% of assessed schools reported a rise in child protection cases during COVID-19 school closures. The number of pregnant learners has nearly doubled between from 2020-2021 in some schools. Schools can be key institutions to identify and refer children requiring protective services and case management. Over a third of schools assessed said they had no referral mechanisms in place and less than a quarter of schools reported having a general protection referral mechanism. In order to ray hope to the future of South Sudanese children, they must be provided with unhindered education opportunities, now and not later. Therefore, this project will be implemented in line with Education Cluster Objectives for HRP 2023 and Education Sector Standards to ensure that all children, including those with disabilities, girls and young mothers can access education, remain in schools and complete their learning both during and after emergencies.

2 - Project activities

CINA will target 4,404 out of the above population, under Sectoral Objective 1: Improve equitable access to safe and protective education services for 860,000 emergency affected IDPs, returnees, spontaneous refugees, and host community children including children with disabilities and youth (girls and boys). CINA will provide 4,404 children, adolescents and youth (3-17 years old) with access and attend schools in affected areas; Build 6 temporary learning spaces in line with Inter-INEE's Minimum Standards for Education in Emergencies and Standards in Education Cluster Strategy 2019; Rehabilitate 8 temporary learning spaces or schools; Build gender sensitive WASH facilities for 600 children; Rehabilitate gender-sensitive WASH facilities for 1,000 children in line with INEE and sphere standards. (unit of measurement: 1:60 for boys, 1:30 for girls), Provide 1,250 learners with sanitation and hygiene materials; Conduct enrolment and Out of school assessments and provide food for education feeding program for children to encourage school attendance. Sectoral Objective 2: Improve the quality of gender and disability-inclusive education services and teachers' capacity to protect the wellbeing of all children and prevent the negative impact of the emergencies on learning environments, CINA will provide 400 children and youth (6-17 years) with recreational material supplies; and 100 children (3-5 years) with Early Childhood and Development (ECD) kits; 550 children (5-17) and teachers with teaching and learning materials; 100 teachers will be trained on EiE, basic pedagogy and learner-centered methodologies, 100 teachers trained and updated on PSS, CP and referral mechanisms and 104 youth and adolescents (14-17 years) will be trained on life-skills and basic vocational training. Sectoral Objective 3. Strengthen/enhance the education system’s response capacity of the school community and national/local education actors to mitigate the impact of existing emergencies on learners, teachers, and communities, CINA will train 50 community education committee members and local education officials on school management, Psychosocial Support (PSS) and Disaster Risk Reduction in Education (DRR). 150 children, teachers and PTA members will be provided with appropriate IEC prevention and mitigation information and materials and 5 school campaigns will be organized on back to school/learning and Young Mothers identified will be supported with Cash to encourage them to go back to school and remain in school.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

CINA Cash transfer programming will be inform of both unrestricted and unconditional grants for support to girls and young mothers to remain in schools and conditional for schools operations. However, the use will be limited only to Protection and essentials education concerns only within the sectors. This project therefore seeks first to know how education functionalities can be part of project design to make an expansion of the use of cash in other sectors that can be supported by improved market analysis, an increased focus on income and impact measurement, absence of evidence of harming the market, functional and healthy market, increased availability of products in the market, increased of products in the market, resilience of households, Gender, protection and inclusion of vulnerable people, shifts in income and genderperception, decreased disease rate, quality occupancy of housing and increased labour capacity in construction

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

CINA Cash assistance will strive to provide the most appropriate, effective and efficient responses to the needs of people affected by a crisis. It will be provided in many ways depending on needs and context. The choice of appropriate transfers (in-kind, cash or vouchers), or their combination, must be based on a thorough assessment and context-specific analysis, including cost effectiveness and efficiency, secondary market impacts, the flexibility of the transfer, local availability of goods and services, prioritization and targeting, gender and protection considerations, and risks of insecurity and corruption. Cash will be specifically provided to girls and young mothers to remain in schools and to schools as a capitation grants for schools operations. CINA in the past has been delivering Cash Based assistance to learners and schools through (GESS project) and also does so in protection programming. Therefore, Cash-based intervention has the potential to respond quickly and efficiently to a variety of urgent needs offering dignity, flexibility of choice to beneficiaries and supporting local markets growth for long term development. assistance covers both cash and vouchers that are provided to individuals, households or communities as emergency relief and which contribute to meeting their basic needs or to assisting them in protecting or reestablishing their livelihoods. Cash-based assistance, particularly when delivered in the form of unconditional cash is a people-centered approach

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will target 15% children with disabilities, children and their caregivers. Their views and preferences will be used to realign provision of Cash Based intervention support in terms of cash transfers to girls and young mothers for education retention. In particular, girls out of schools, pastoralists children, young mothers, children with disabilities, children released from armed forces and groups will be targeted and reached.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Community will be engaged through the Community Based Education Committees such as Child Protection Help Desks, School Management Committees and Parents' Teachers Association forums, Caregivers' group. Beneficiary Complaint mechanisms will be established at the community level and feedback will be collected using a form, complaint box or hotline number. Banners on SEA awareness and reporting will also be displayed at the distribution centers, will be used to receive feedback from community members. During the project inception at the community, beneficiaries will be informed about the project objectives, targets, activities, and their participation in the project. Committees will be formed/strengthened to convey any project information to the beneficiaries during the implementation. CINA will also use feedback forms during M&E activities.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CINA staff and volunteers will be trained to ensure adherence to IASC PSEA guidelines throughout the project cycle. Each will sign a Code of Conduct. All CINA staffs are subjected to enhanced reference checks up on taking job offer. A commitment statement on PSEA is found on CINA’s job adverts. CINA will ensure that staff are trained and uphold humanitarian principles including, of neutrality, impartiality and participation, and where feasible, CINA team will remain diverse as much as possible to achieve PSEA objective. CINA will ensure project beneficiaries are sensitized about the PSEA, and their role to identify and report it appropriately. CINA will display PSEA messages on the banner during awareness raising activities. A PSEA reporting form will be kept at schools, Community centers, clubs and beneficiaries well be sensitized about it. They will use this to report cases directly to PSEA focal person at CINA's head Office. A hotline number and email address will also be displayed on the PSEA banner which beneficiaries can use to call and report PSEA cases in the community.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The beneficiaries' feedback about services rendered by CINA in their location will be synthesized by CINA management used this information during the project planning and/or implementation to adjust next phase of response.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

This project will strengthen beneficiaries to be resilience to the extreme weather events in an urgent and critical way around South Sudan. Because this extreme weather often causes population displacement that compromises human security, educational services by laying environment-induced placement of multifaceted response into climate extremes, population, and socio-economic conditions, among other factors which converge to influence individuals' decisions to move. Catastrophic floods occur and people tend to move both suddenly and rapidly for survival. This project will quantify the patterns and mechanisms of displacement at provide essential education support areas at high risk for climate-induced displacement.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

The project will reach beneficiaries at risk of acute food insecurity in the lean season, and will aim to include persons with disabilities, female headed households, very sick, and GBV survivors) with food for education assistance and livelihood opportunities to reduce the shocks and improve wellbeing and recovery. This project will integrate of education needs into famine prevention (WASH, health, nutrition) to ensure mental and physical growth and education attainment contribute to increased livelihood opportunities later in life; to ensure vulnerable groups (women, elderly, PWDs, etc.) are healthy enough to carry out manual livelihood tasks; safe drinking water for safe preparation and utilization in the body. Protection will also be mainstreamed to ensure that conflict, displacement and GBV do not disrupt people's educational services and to ensure that educational activities contribute to safety, dignity and integrity of people at risks.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

This project will provide food for education assistance to support directly children of families in IPC 4 and will reduce the overall dropout proportions of children in schools. The project will specifically target vulnerable families of children with special needs and living with vulnerable caregivers including the pregnant and lactating mothers, elderly, people with disabilities, and people with Catastrophic level 5 (priority 3 and 4) with food for education.
