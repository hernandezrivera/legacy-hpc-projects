Project Summary

The project aims to reduce vulnerability and improve physical and mental well-being of crisis-affected population with particular attention to the specific vulnerabilities linked to gender, age and disability. The organization’s action will focus on providing multi-disciplinary health services tailored to the needs of people with physical and functional limitations and their caregivers through direct intervention or through the support of local partners in North-West, North-East and GoS-controlled parts of Syria.
The intervention includes the provision of a multidisciplinary physical rehabilitation services (including the provision of assistive mobility devices, prostheses and orthoses and education sessions for caregivers) and the provision of psychosocial support services to physical rehabilitation beneficiaries and their caregivers.
The organization will consolidate and strengthen its support to health facilities so as to set up sustainable physical rehabilitation and prosthetic & orthotic services while the organization’s outreach teams reach out to IDPs; remote and underserved communities; as well as persons who are incapable of reaching the service centers. These mobile teams will work on assessing and referring beneficiaries to relevant health service providers. In GoS- controlled areas, the organization will focus on developing rehabilitation services in areas where services are not present or functioning through the support of registered local health partners.
These services not only help in the recovery of physical rehabilitation beneficiaries and the improvement of their functional independence but also targets caregivers in a holistic approach in order to alleviate stress, fatigue and other psychological impacts related to their new caregiving responsibilities.
The organization’s intervention in GoS- controlled areas and North-west of Syria will also include the provision of MHPSS to people suffering from psychological distress without having physical rehabilitation needs. MHPSS services include psychological first aid, psychoeducation, and individual and/or group session therapy.
In parallel, and in order to ensure the sustainability and quality of the action, the organization will provide support to its local health partners through technical, financial and managerial support as well as the development of capacity building plans tailored to the needs of these partners.
The organization’s services include:
1- Physical and functional rehabilitation (including physical and functional rehabilitation sessions, provision of assistive devices, education sessions for caregivers),
2- P&O services (fitting sessions and provision of P&O items),
3- PSS sessions to people with functional limitations and their caregivers
4- MHPSS services (psychological first aid, psychoeducation, individual session therapy or MHPSS group sessions).
5- Capacity building plans for local partners.

4 - Needs

12 million people are in need of health services in Syria with over 2.7 million in the North West and 1.2 million in North East Syria. (WHO) Countless civilians have been killed and injured as a direct result of hostilities, with 45 per cent of the injured expected to sustain a permanent impairment, and 3 million people live with a disability in Syria (HNO 2019). The Physical Rehabilitation and Disability Working Group of the Health Cluster conducted a survey in 2018 in Aleppo, Idleb and Ar-Raqqa governorates. The prevalence of Persons with disabilities was reported at 33%- double the world average (WHO 2011 world report on disability). Respondents with disabilities were asked if the Syrian conflict had worsened their difficulties, 50% said `yes' and most commonly reported the following reasons: lack of access to medical/health care, lack of assistive devices or resources, and difficulties in addressing their needs, due to the crisis. Based on the organization's past interventions, of the people receiving physical rehabilitation 32% of the beneficiaries had fractures, 7% had been amputated, 9% had peripheral nerve damage, and 4% had a Spinal Cord Injury (SCI) leading to paralysis. WHO estimated that the conflict has led 1 in 5 Syrians to develop moderate Mental Health issues and 1 in 30 at risk of developing acute or severe MH needs. IMC 2017 report identified severe emotional disorders (54% of the MH issues reported) as the most common MH issue, including depression, anxiety, with main emotional stressors to be security and protection concerns, access and availability of basic services and lack of medical staff due to the disruption or destruction of key infrastructures. The Syrian Arab Association of Psychiatrists stated that 92% of severe MH cases cannot have adequate follow up. However, not addressing the MH impact of violence exacerbates profound and persistent existential concerns of safety, trust, identity, social cohesion and community resilience.

5 - Implementation of activities

Provide primary and secondary health care service including Trauma care. Persons in needs of services are identified through other services (i.e.: presence in health facilities, coordination fora, partners) and through outreach teams. The INGO will provide identification and needs assessment of persons with injuries and/or disabilities in hospitals, rehab centres, IDP camps, communities. The INGO will deliver multidisciplinary rehab services adapted to the needs of each beneficiary, through gender balanced teams, including: physical and functional rehab sessions, provision of assistive devices, production and provision of prosthetic and orthotic devices, education sessions and/or complementary PSS sessions (also to caregivers) and provision of orientation and PSS sessions to caregivers. The INGO will provide also MH and PSS for Syrians who suffer from psychosocial and psychological issues caused by conflict, displacement and multiple losses. Persons in needs of services additional to MHPSS and rehab will be referred to complementary services providers. Health Sector Objective 3: The INGO intervenes in support to the national health system and to strengthen the national technical capacity. including:- The provision of financial, material, technical / HR support to hospitals and rehab centers- Provision of face to face /remote training, technical supervision by dedicated staff, technical support, follow up and coaching to build the capacity of the INGO partners and other health structures- Provision of tailored technical support (identification of barriers/discriminatory practices, production of inclusion assessment report with practical recommendations and action plan, training, coaching and follow up) to health actors to improve the inclusiveness of their services (with attention to GAD factors).- Physical rehab and inclusion specialist to ensure support to clusters- MEAL teams and technical teams in charge of implementing the Quality Monitoring Framework

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

- Protection of Field staff and partners: the INGO keeps low visibility to protect the identities of its staff. In case of an accident, the INGO activates its duty of care policy and provide the field staff with psychological support.- PSEA: The INGO applies its PSEA policy for the staff, partner staff and suppliers. Proper accountability measures are ensured through investigation and follow up of reported cases. The INGO also raises awareness on the organization’s policy on PSEA and its complaints and feedback mechanisms to follow up. As part of the inclusion mainstreaming strategy, training to HI staff and its partners to highlight specific vulnerabilities faced by persons with disabilities (GBV, sexual exploitation, etc.) is implemented. The INGO has a protection task force which ensures protection mainstreaming is optimized. Additionality, the referral task force also works towards improving protection referrals and its processes. A GAD approach guides all these initiatives. - Healthcare Provision for vulnerable groups and equitable access to health services: staff is regularly trained on Humanitarian principles and how to interact, communicate and engage with people with various disabilities and needs. Gender balanced Mobile field teams reach out to remote communities or to people who cannot leave their home. Technical and financial support to local partners and health service providers to remove access barriers.- Safe closure of facilities and protecting data: Field workers are trained on safe withdrawal and evacuation measures including ways to protect/destroy data.- Logistic and fund transfer issues: Fund transfer issues will be mitigated by assessing many transfer partners and by enhancing coordination with others INGOs.-Working through implementing partners: before becoming an implementing partner, each actor goes through a thorough assessment and vetting process. reporting is required, monitoring visits, feedback and response mechanism are in place

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Loss of access to intervention areas: Mitigation measures: the INGO developed an access strategy that is regularly reviewed and adapted to the context. The INGO also continuously monitors the security situation in its intervention areas and adapts its access strategy to the regular conflict analysis and how it affects its operations. The strategy includes the possibility of uspension/relocation of activities to safer areas. It also includes adapting the team’s work modalities to a form of remote management of activities in the field.The organization continues advocacy efforts to engage the UN and all parties to the conflict on access to health and protection topics and respecting the international humanitarian law.- Acceptance of local community and authorities is maintained. In order to successfully carry out activities in the community, the organization needs to maintain the approval, acceptance and support of the community as well as the local authorities. Mitigation measures: the INGO teams and partners are well-perceived on the ground and always make sure to keep close coordination with the local authorities and maintain positive relations with the community. The organization will also focus on increasing the community’s participation in its programs; setting in place community satisfaction surveys to measure the satisfaction of the community with its services, developing various feedback mechanisms to adapt the services to the community’s needs and also focusing on supporting the local partners on building their capacities to sustain and improve the provision of their services.- Loss of UN funding: Mitigation measure: HI is maintaining close communication with various donors, and would be able to negotiate directly with some donors to seek additional funding.
