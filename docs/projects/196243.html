Project Summary

The project’s main objective is to meet the basic life-saving SNFI needs of affected populations due to conflict, violence or natural disasters within South Sudan. The Emergency Response Team can respond in any location at short notice. The project will target 45,000 individuals who are deemed the most vulnerable using the cluster’s and Medair’s vulnerability criteria. Medair’s criteria will be newly displaced persons, protracted displaced persons, returnees and vulnerable host communities. Within these groups, the most vulnerable will be defined as those sharing limited resources, over-burdened households (including those contributing to COVID-19 transmission), people with disability, female-headed households, pregnant and lactating females. Accountability to Affected Populations and protection mainstreaming are the core of all programming to ensure meaningful access to services and protect beneficiaries’ safety/dignity. With continued violence throughout South Sudan, particularly at the sub-national level, Medair continues to see many people displaced without the ability to bring NFI with them. In some cases, homes have been looted and burned down. IDPs are found sharing resources with already vulnerable host households. So, multiple families will share one saucepan, mosquito net, blanket, etc. Medair aim to address these needs by responding to sudden onset displacement due to violence. Additionally, Medair respond to natural disasters, such as abnormal flooding, that adversely affect both IDPs and host communities. Medair will complete an effective assessment of the population’s needs using the cluster’s tools. The analysis (stakeholder analysis, protection risk analysis, research, observation, FGDs, KIIs, and Medair’s findings) determines if an intervention is appropriate and what modalities are possible. Consultation with the affected communities help determine an intervention design that is appropriate for their needs. Medair's ERT team is multi-sectoral and can supplement a SNFI intervention with WASH, Protection, Nutrition, and/or Health when necessary. Once an intervention is confirmed, Medair works closely with the SNFI cluster and IOM core pipeline to receive items needed for in-kind distributions in a timely manner for beneficiaries. Medair set up a community distribution committee comprised of representative community members to discuss the most appropriate and safest distribution that will allow meaningful access. Guidelines are provided for the selection criteria, which is disseminated to communities. The team are available to answer criteria questions throughout the intervention. Before implementation, a safety audit is completed to ensure a safe and accessible distribution site. As an alternative to in-kind distributions, Medair will respond with CVA programming when/where appropriate to meet SNFI emergency needs per the assessment recommendations. If CVA is recommended, additional market and other assessments will be undertaken to determine the most effective implementation. For the distribution, Medair employ and train representative casual labourers to safeguard a feedback mechanism that swiftly resolves any issues the same day. After the distribution, Medair will complete exit interviews to remain accountable to the beneficiaries, to deal with any urgent issues whilst in the field, to identify any gaps or additional needs, and to inform internal lessons learnt. A PDM will be completed 4 weeks to 3 months after a distribution to fully assess its impact: if the community felt their most basic needs were met, and if the intervention was completed in a safe/dignified manner. For Medair programmes to remain accountable and transparent, findings from the intervention will be reported to the stakeholders: cluster, donors and sometimes communities. If Medair identifies further needs that the ERT team are unable to respond to, then the team will share those needs with other partners and the cluster.

1 - Needs

There is a significant risk—despite improved security—that populations will continue to be displaced by armed groups, cattle-raiders, inter-communal fighting and natural disasters, specifically flooding. It is likely that the humanitarian situation will continue to deteriorate in 2023 due to repercussions of the formation of the new government, a result of eight years of conflict and a continued reduction in access to basic services. Emergency Shelter and NFI assistance is still urgently needed to serve those who have fled their homes and left their belongings behind. An estimated 2 million people are currently displaced (HNO South Sudan, February 2022) and most are in desperate need of some form of SNFI assistance. With the delayed implementation of the revitalized peace agreement, including delayed national elections, the humanitarian situation will continue to be impacted with an increase in shifting populations. The SNFI Cluster estimates 2,400,000 individuals will need SNFI assistance within South Sudan (Draft HRP ES/NFI cluster response plan, February 2022). Medair teams continue to find multiple IDP and returnee families sharing very limited and basic resources with vulnerable hosts. Looting and the burning of houses have caused the destruction of belongings, while for others, items have been washed away by flood waters. Most times IDPs flee the emergency crises, whether flood or conflict, without any possessions. In particular, IDPs outside camps require basic household items, especially those who have relocated several times. Medair have found that natural disasters, such as abnormal flooding and the COVID-19 pandemic, adversely affect both IDPs and the host communities; both have need for SNFI. Generally, those who are newly displaced will have priority over protracted displaced populations. Women and children are particularly vulnerable to protection risks due to lack of shelter. In some instances, host community members will also need support due to pressure on their limited resources to help IDPs especially as the risk of COVID-19 continues and spreads more easily in crowded living conditions. Men remain at risk of violence in some areas where they can be targets. Child abductions are an on-going concern. Since the signing of the R-ARCSS peace agreement in September 2018, there has being an increase in refugee and IDP returnees throughout South Sudan. According to round twelve of the IOM DTM, returnee IDPs continued to increase into 2022, as of January 31 2022, 1,924,368 returnee IDPs were recorded. This met expectations for an increase in returnees after the establishment of the new government in 2020. Many IDPs, refugees, and returnees need SNFI support. Medair aims to address the needs of the most vulnerable IDPs and returnees particularly pregnant and lactating women, female-headed households, the elderly, and people with disabilities. In this specific context, Medair responds to recently displaced populations fleeing conflict or abnormal flooding. If warranted, Medair will also support some of the most vulnerable host community members in order to build their resilience and ease community tensions. This aligns with the cluster response for 2022 where targeting includes prioritising people unable to support themselves with life-saving shelter and non-food items, including flood-affected populations. The goal is to support those in need by giving them life-saving SNFI through modalities that support people without eroding their existing coping mechanisms. CVA modalities will be prioritised over in-kind distributions whenever Medair assessments show feasibility. Medair will also continue to prioritise multi-sectoral, multi-agency responses promoting a holistic approach to emergency response. These interventions will target multiple lifesaving needs in coordination with partners and the cluster hubs, which meet the current cluster HRP plan for 2022.

2 - Project activities

Carry out SNFI needs analysis upon receiving cluster alerts • Respond with the appropriate modality (flood kit, quick intervention, integrated response, CVA) providing SNFI and training to meet the life-saving needs of communities • Conduct PDMs, exit interviews, and positive/negative feedback registration to ensure AAP. Implement feedback mechanisms before/during/after distribution to consider affected population’s input • Coordinate with other partners by attending national- and state-level cluster meetings, networking with other agencies, and interacting with other agency partners on the ground • Localize every intervention by partnering with local NGOs or coordinating a handover to local partners. Coordinate with Humanitarian Hubs in an emergency to allow for an efficient and timely response, reduce the likelihood of activity duplication, effectively use prepositioned supplies and contribute towards general coordination • Recognise that the increase in returnees and ongoing high numbers of IDPs need an area-based approach covering multiple sectors to meet all the basic needs of a population. To understand the complex needs in a changing context, more time should be spent within a community and more collaboration with partners/specialists. Medair will spend as much time as possible with communities to ensure a full needs assessment has been completed, effective AAP is being implemented and the mechanisms are being used. Medair will coordinate with other partners for a holistic approach to address multiple needs faced by returnees and IDPs. Medair will engage HLP and protection specialists as necessary and coordinate with other actors to avoid activity duplication. Medair ERT will coordinate with its sectors (WASH, Protection, Nutrition, Health) to deliver a multi-sectoral response where needed and possible • Participate in the Strategic Advisory Group (SAG), Technical Working Group when selected • Participate in the South Sudan Cash Working Group • Mainstream Protection: “do no harm” analysis, ensure most vulnerable people have priority access to distributions, and serve the population according to Medair values and Code of Ethics, incorporate accepted AAP good practices, train staff in protection mainstreaming, utilize cluster protection tools during FDGs, KIIs, enumerator training, etc. • Consider gender inclusion throughout project implementation through gender analysis in needs assessments, gender-sensitive activities (hire women enumerators/casual labourers, female community leaders as key informants) and gender-sensitive outcomes • State Focal Point for Central Equatoria State: proactively visit field sites across state to understand the specific needs; network to build up key contacts and relationships across the state; organise monthly state coordination meetings; build partner capacity on emergency responses; engage and build relationships with key RRC/ROSS officials • Monitor IDP and returnee movement in initial needs analysis to understand where IDPs plan to settle (helps determine response plan). Monitor returnee, both refugee and IDP, movement motivations (if they plan to stay) and help identify the number of people returning/planning to return. This information is shared with the cluster • Contribute to community resilience through providing lifesaving SNFI, employing local casual labour, training enumerators to use digital data collection tools, and training beneficiaries to use NFI or build shelters • Innovate to find more durable, sustainable solutions, especially related to flood resilient shelters. Mainstream DRR • Conduct environmental assessments using NEAT+ • Stock minimum plastic sheets, rubber ropes, mosquito nets to mitigate CPL stock outages. In collaboration with external partners, Medair is currently developing/testing an innovative shelter design. The project seeks to improve flood resilience of shelter assistance in South Sudan by developing durable shelter solutions using local materials.

3 - Project indicators

To ensure a robust process, Medair has a monitoring, evaluation and learning (MEAL) team who are responsible for analysing data using custom and standard cluster monitoring tools. These tools collect quantitative disaggregated age, gender, and vulnerability data in addition to qualitative data through focus group discussions and key informant interviews. The data collected will be the basis of the project’s design and ensure the community—diverse groups of age, gender, vulnerabilities—are consulted throughout the intervention and the targeted population are considered throughout all phases of the project. Special efforts will be made to reach the most vulnerable and employ them in the data collection process. The SNFI team also play a pivotal role in making sure all the critical project indicators are accurately traced, compiled, and reported to the respective donors, clusters and other stakeholders. Medair’s SNFI team will undertake exit interviews at the conclusion of every intervention. When possible and feasible, a PDM will be carried out four weeks to three months after the intervention. The data obtained will be collected and analysed on a regular basis—ensuring accountability to the affected population—and reported via standard cluster reporting templates to all stakeholders. Medair will also continue to record all project-related data in their project data sheet for quick reference and to adapt future programming to lessons learned. SNFI distributions will be conducted after house-to-house (when possible) beneficiary registrations that list names, household information and verify beneficiaries meet targeting criteria. The MEAL team will continue to ensure all collected data are accurate, without double counting, and evaluated against the project indicators. If gaps are identified and the project is not on track, corrective measures will be taken and the project will be re-aligned according to community feedback. Throughout project indicator data collection, Medair’s and any additional Ministry of Health COVID-19 prevention protocols/requirements will be utilised to protect the health of the community/staff and to curtail community transmission.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The team will consult with the community to identify the most vulnerable in collaboration with specialist protection partners. Special assistance will be given to those who need it. The Medair team ensure there is a clear and well-organised feedback mechanism so that any issues or suggestions are considered and quickly resolved during the project implementation. While opportunities for written feedback are provided, beneficiaries can also provide verbal feedback to be recorded by enumerators. Additionally, Medair actively seek out vulnerable members for inclusion in the assessment FGDs and KIIs. Exit interviews will be conducted with the most vulnerable and their caregivers. The Medair team also seek out any at-risk individuals who are not connected or are outside the local communities to receive their feedback. Medair aim to work with protection partners that can also help identify any particularly vulnerable households that may otherwise be overlooked.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The Medair team closely consults with local authorities and the community through a thorough assessment that identifies beneficiaries’ preferred communication methods in addition to informing them about the project’s goals. Additionally, feedback mechanisms are provided throughout the intervention by means of FDGs, KIIs, a feedback desk, exit interviews and PDMs.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

All Medair staff receive regular training in Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Medair staff remain vigilant in the field to provide casual labourers and beneficiaries at risk of exploitation the feedback mechanism to report any incidents. Enumerators are also trained in Protection Principles and Medair’s Code of Ethics before they start working.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The analysis, interventions and PDMs include input from local authorities, community leaders, elders, women and youth groups (teens, not children), church leaders, people with disability, and other NGOs. IDPs, returnees, and hosts are consulted to explore different contextual perspectives and increase the team’s understanding of local tribes’ different views. Prior to the distribution the Medair team will be available and accessible for any feedback the community wishes to give. If feedback indicates that a multi-sectoral response is required, Medair's ERT also cover WASH, Protection, Nutrition, and Health sectors.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

8.1 Medair complete a protection risk/mitigation analysis and safety audit to assess any risks for beneficiaries attending activities. Medair then mitigates against these risks and will act to make sure beneficiaries’ safety is a priority at all times. One example: where distance to humanitarian assistance is identified as a risk, Medair aims to mitigate this by establishing several distribution sites that will require less travel time for beneficiaries. 8.5 Medair actively seek out marginalised groups to participate in every stage of the project with the aid of local authorities and community leaders. Their voices are included in the assessment and subsequent project design. Marginalised groups are also prioritised during verifications and registrations. When needed, extra measures are employed to ease the participation burden to marginalised groups such as older persons and those with disabilities. Additionally, women are actively recruited for casual labour roles used to implement the project. 8.8 There are many external stakeholders that try to influence beneficiary selection for targeted distributions. The Medair team work hard to triangulate information received through multiple sources, and field teams verify the information in person before intervening. This reduces missing the most vulnerable households or favouring one community/tribe over another which can exacerbate tensions and lead to conflict. Medair teams will always complete a stakeholder analysis before entering the field and will adjust on the ground as necessary. Medair also runs trainings on humanitarian principles and have clear selection criteria that are communicated to stakeholders. 8.9 Medair continues to monitor land disputes and specifically asks questions on this in the analysis survey and FGDs to fully understand any HLP issues when assessing an area. If any major concerns are reported, then Medair would seek appropriate advice from partners who specialise in HLP. Additionally, the Medair team seek to mitigate beneficiaries' HLP risk by involving the host community and its leadership, state ministry of land and housing, and properly assessing the area to gain an understanding of traditional and cultural influence on HLP issues.
