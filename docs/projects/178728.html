Project Summary

The project endeavors to implement integrated activities in the humanitarian field in western rural of Aleppo province. This intervention will make contribution towards addressing critical food insecurity, instability and develop the essential household’s livelihood assets through supporting the wheat value chain, access to agriculture, livestock breeders, bakeries, and IDPs/returnees. The project includes implementation of the main activities:
1. Supplementing of wheat value chain through purchasing 500 kg of wheat from each beneficiary (based on specific criteria) after that, the wheat will be milled, as flour and bran. So, we will have approximately 80% flour equivalent to 192 tons and about 20% bran equivalent to 48-tons in order to:
A. Support the bakeries by wheat flour to produce 211.2 tons of bread to distribute it for free to 2400 families in camps and returnees for two consecutive months.
B. As for the 48-tons of wheat bran, we will support 240 livestock breeders by distributing of 200 kg of bran for each beneficiary.
2. Improving access to agriculture by distributing of agricultural inputs for 600 beneficiaries. Every beneficiary will receive 250 kg of wheat seeds, 150 kg of composed fertilizer, 200 kg of Azote fertilizer, 1 liter of Herbicide,1 liter of Insecticide, one fuel voucher for irrigation 50 $ and harvesting costs 50 $. Moreover, the support will include provision of three technical training sessions about GAP.

4 - Needs

The overall humanitarian situation remains dire for the people living in NW Syria. According to HNO 2021, an estimated of 13.4 million people are still in need of humanitarian assistance with increase comparing to 2020 (11 million) and the number of people in need of humanitarian assistance in NW Syria has increased from 2.8 to 3.4 million people. 2.7 million are IDPs, many of whom are living in over-crowded locations with limited access to essential services. As of February 2021, approximately 1.7 million IDPs were living in 1,385 camps or informal sites (including 24,800 people displaced in January and February 2021 alone). Women and children represent 80 per cent of this caseload while more than 22,000 IDPs are reported to be persons with specific needs and the expanding impact of the COVID-19 pandemic has further complicated the lives of people in humanitarian settings in northwest Syria.During April and May 2020, MASRRAT has conducted a multi- sectoral assessment to determine the best practices and most suitable methodologies to support the food security and livelihoods of families. The primary data has been collected by MASRAAT team from LCs/farmers and compared with the secondary data from OCHA/flash update2020- Care/Multisectoral assessment – OCHA/Covid19 rapid assessment April and May 2020 as a result, there is an urgent need to respond to the mentioned gaps such as:1. supporting the wheat value chain and supporting the wheat farmers with agricultural inputs2. Supporting livestock breeders with fodder.3. Supporting bakeries with flour to distribute it to IDPs and returnees by free bread.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will start implementation on 01 July 2020, the project duration will be 12 months wherein, the first month will include preparations, community mobilization process, MOUs signing, procurement and contracting and one month for the closure and conducting the end-line assessment. The staff will be recruited directly to carry out project implementation. The project will support 600 beneficiaries by agricultural inputs and 240 beneficiaries by livestock breeders, in addition to 2400 IDPs and returnees' families with daily their needs from bread for three months. At the beginning of the implementation of the project, MASRRAT's team will sign a Memorandum of Understanding (MOUs) with the local councils in the area. The process of engaging the Local Councils will start with advertising about the project in the communities of intervention, explaining the mechanism of selection, and the number of potential beneficiaries under this project. Advertisements of the activity will be published and will include selection priorities. The application form will include questions about basic personal information of the family. Data collection of the beneficiaries will be conducted at the beginning of the project (in the preparation month), and this process usually has no risks or any threats to beneficiaries. The data will be verified based on the criteria of selection. Delivery of agricultural inputs will be according to voucher and according to distribution lists. Beneficiaries will sign on the distribution lists, and they will be verified through their IDs. The project will follow the procurement policy of MASRRAT, as the all items of support will be procured from local markets.
