Project Summary

The Continuous exposure to violence,inadequate living standards continue causing heightened Protection and Child Protection risks. In'23, 15.1M people will need Protection services in Syria, of whom 44% are children (HNO, 2023).In’23, NGO conducted CP needs assessment, employing surveys in the Deir-ez-Zor, Aleppo, and Ar-Raqqa governorates. In addition to Protection risk analysis to capture key Protection Mainstreaming risks mitigation measures such as easy accessibility for PWD, safe accessibility for women adolescent girls and others, NGO will continue its focus in engaging with the community through contenuous community consultations to build on peer support networks and reach more socially isolated groups, particularly women and youth, implement activities with respect to gender separation. To mitigate lack of quality services which expose beneficiaries to harm,stigmatization and trauma. Where only trained personnel will conduct CPcase management with a continuous capacity buildingfor protection team and other internal sector onCP, safe identification, referral system.
Following,data indication revealed signs of UASC in communities 47% separated children, 14.5% unaccompanied children. 84% of HH consider children above 16 years old are adults; the highest risks of children injuries and death are in informal settlement 15% and on the way to work 14%. Furthermore, 68% of HH have workingchildren, 55% aged 6-14 and 29% above 14, 19% working in farming, 15% harsh labor, 14% begging on the street with no difference between genders. 91% ofHH observed changes in theirchildren'sbehavior, with 55% noting instances of violence. Caregivers,facing challenges such as food scarcity, inadequate shelter,loss of livelihood, reported noticeable shifts in their attitudes, manifesting in reduced attention to children, less time spent with them, and increased use of physicalpunishment. These changes in behavior are reflected in both boys girls, with increased crying, aggression, unwillingness to attend school, nightmares.NGO will continue todeliver comprehensive, servicebased protection programs prioritizing needs of the most atrisk and vulnerable individuals including IDPs and host community in isolated communities:children, adolescent girls, boys, caregivers in line with SO4, through increasing access to PSS activities for children to improve their wellbeing and resilience, and their caregivers by providing positive parenting support to ensure wellbeing and +coping skills of their children, through static and mobile safe space, in addition to CPcase management service to support, cash for protection to ensure atrisk individuals are provided within an inter-sectoral and multi-sectoral project. For cases, requiring specialist protection services such GBVservices and others will be referred to other agencies within NES in line with interagency guidelines on referral pathways, also through the NGOintensive coordination with Protection,MHPSS,CMTF,and GBV WG and actors to better inform NGOimplementation under the DONOHarm principles. MEALsystem will built on regular data collection,analysis and reporting, with accountability,data monitoring. Tracking tools to track outcome data to beneficiaries using registration records, attendance records, Pre-post-tests, CPIMS+ facilitates effectivecase management of vulnerable individual children promoting best practice and accountability, adding to a satisfactory surveys to help our CPprograms in providing highquality services and to quality,customer satisfaction contributions, following technical SoPs.The NGOwill inform its clients and the community about the beneficiaries' response mechanisms to allow them to communicate their feedback on the program delivery.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Mitigating Gender-Based Violence (GBV) risks and preventing sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) with a comprehensive approach that promotes awareness, and establishes safeguards. which can be mitigatedas follow :Policy Framework and Codes of ConductStaff Training and Capacity Building by provide trainings on GBV core concept and safe referrals Community Engagement and Awareness: Conduct awareness-raising within the community to challenge harmful gender norms, promote gender equality, and increase understanding of GBV and SEA.Safe Reporting Mechanisms: with reosect of confidentiality principls and accessible reporting mechanisms for GBV and SEA incidents, helplines, suggestion boxes, or trusted focal points within the organization.Risk Assessments and Mitigation Plans: Conduct regular Protection risk assessments to identify potential GBV and SEA risks within project activities, locations, and beneficiary populations. Develop tailored mitigation plans that address identified risks, strengthen protective measures, and integrate GBV prevention into all aspects of project planning and implementation.Staff Supervision and Accountability: monitoring, and accountability mechanisms to oversee staff conduct and adherence to GBV and SEA policies. Conduct background checks, vetting processes, and regular performance reviews to identify and address any signs of misconduct or inappropriate behavior.Inclusive Programming: Ensure that project activities are designed and implemented in a manner that promotes gender equality, respects diversity, and addresses the specific needs and vulnerabilities of different groups, including women, children, individuals, and persons with disabilities. In addition, we are an active member in Protection WG and its subsectors, with a close coordination and collaboration with all AORs. The result of this active participation and after official endorsement of the Protection Area based Coordination mechanism from PWG, we were selected to lead in 3 areas out of 10, where our focus will be to to support the WGs in having a clear and functional referral pathways, updated service mapping, highlighting gaps and support in capacity building. Finally, Regarding Implementation Strategy, the Syria Management Team (SMT) provides technical support to overall Syria response, led by the Country director. Our activities will be provided by PSS facilitators and Case workers, supervised by a PSS and Case management Assistants, managed by PSS and CM Officers and a Protection Program Manager with advisory support from Protection Coordinator. MEAL Field facilitators, assistants and Program Officers in Syria will be responsible for the day-to-day monitoring of activities. Finance and HR staff will and conduct key organizational policy awareness sessions, including Code of Conduct, ethics, security incident reporting and management policy, safeguarding, combating human trafficking, conflict of interest.
