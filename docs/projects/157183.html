Project Summary

This project aims to improve the food security, nutrition and livelihood status of 96,980 individuals (19,630 HHs) of IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities in West Jabal Marra (Nertiti), Central Jabal Marra (Golo) and North Jabal Marra (Rokero) Localities in Central Darfur State and Krenik and Habila localities in West Darfur State. According to needs assessment and pre-harvest assessment carried out by VSF-G and partners, post-harvest assessment conducted by the State Ministry of Agriculture and other relevant reports revealed, although there has been an improvement in the rainy season in 2017 from 2016, the targeted localities still experienced rainfall variability and dry spells, as a result, there was extremely poor harvest, affecting mostly vulnerable IDPs, returnees and destitute host communities who have suffered destruction of crops and livestock, loss of livelihood assets & income and market disruptions. High levels of displacement are also placing severe pressure on the host communities, who already face elevated levels of vulnerability and food insecurity. This situation was exacerbated by the volatility of market prices. Deterioration of natural resources, lack of basic infrastructure and services in the targeted localities have affected livestock with poor pastures and disease outbreaks as a result of high concentrations of livestock around limited pastures and water sources. The main objective of this project is to assist vulnerable conflict-affected populations in achieving their own food security and improving their livelihoods by increasing their production and income through integrated FSL and cash interventions. VSF-G will work together with Local Partner (Mubadiroon) to provide emergency agricultural inputs and services to 4,500 farming HHs including: improved seeds of staple cereal crops (22,500 Kg of sorghum and 22,500 Kg of millet), cash crops (45,000 Kg of groundnut) and vegetables (450 Kg of okra, 90 Kg of tomato and 225 Kg of watermelon), hand tools (4,500 rakes and 4,500 hoes) and extension training to 150 farming HHs. The project will also provide fresh meet to 300 HHs in addition to emergency animal health services including: provision of refresher training, veterinary drugs and replenishment of equipment to 30 active CAHWs, vaccination of 250,000 animals belonging to 10,000 HHs, voucher-based treatment of animals belonging to 3,525 HHs and extension services to beneficiaries. Animal health interventions through voucher-based treatment contribute to save livestock assets of poor HHs, selection of beneficiary HHs for this activity will be based on prior assessment. each selected HH will receive veterinary services which include treatment of internal and external parasites and other infectious diseases. This activity will be implemented through the engagement of community members, private pharmacies, CAHWs and the Government. This modality will focus targeting beneficiaries and was selected upon proven VSF-G experience. The project will include supporting vulnerable 225 female-headed HHs from the IDPs and returnees with goats restocking, to provide replacement herd for those who have lost their livestock assets and produce milk for HH consumption. To enhance the productivity of lactating goats concentrated animal feed will be provided for the first three months, additional 300 HHs from DPs and host communities will also be provided with animal feed to improve their milk production. Involvement of the communities in cash for work program (450HHs) to rehabilitate natural resources (rangelands) will help protecting rangelands from further degradation and provide cash injection for vulnerable HHs, while increasing their awareness towards environmental issues through orientation sessions. Extremely poor HHs (300 HHs) who cannot participate in CFW program will be targeted with unconditional cash transfer program to cover part of the HH food basket and improve their food security status.

Needs

fight diseases and eventually prevent losses of key livelihood asset. In Nertit Locality there are about 93,000 IDPs, 21,000 of them are new IDPs in urgent need of humanitarian assistance in all sectors including FSL. According to State HAC, there are 36,757 and 14,000 DPs (returnees) in Rokero and Golo localities respectively, who have recently returned to their villages, and need urgent humanitarian assistance particularly FSL. According to FEWS NET, although food access is improving among IDPs and poor households in Jabal Marra area of Central Darfur, however, restrictions on access to land, limited agricultural labor opportunities, and continued low asset holdings as a result of conflict will continue to limit household productive capacity. Households in these areas will continue to require humanitarian assistance through May 2018. Sorghum and millet prices are expected to remain 15-20 percent higher than the recent five-year average and 10-15 percent higher than during the same period last year, due in part to increased production costs. According to Sudan IPC Technical Working Group, October 2017 all of Central Darfur Localities are in crisis (IPC Phase 3) and targeted communities face high risk of moderate and severe acute malnutrition among children under five.The current fluctuation of currency rate, shortage of fuel and flue of liquidity contributed in soaring of prices particularly, food items which is worsened the food security situation of the targeted beneficiaries.

Activities

This action intends to respond to the complex and protracted crisis in Central Darfur State by adopting a flexible approach to enhance food security and nutritional status of displaced populations and vulnerable host communities in Central Darfur, to improve their livelihoods, protect their assets and to strengthen their resilience to withstand future shocks. This will be achieved by improving agricultural and livestock production through provision of livestock and agricultural inputs including seed, tools, animal vaccines, veterinary drugs, training of CAHWs, provision of extension services to improve livestock management and farming practices, restocking with lactating goats to produce milk for beneficiary HHs especially HHs with malnourished children. Animal health interventions through voucher-based treatment will contribute to save livestock assets of poor HHs. Voucher based animal health service delivery system was selected upon proven VSF-G experience, adopting this scheme will focus targeting of beneficiaries, and reaching those most in need of assistance. Rangeland rehabilitation will be carried out through cash for work, this will help in protecting land from further degradation, prepare it for fodder growth and provide cash injection for vulnerable HHs. In addition, highly vulnerable HHs unable to participate in Cash For Work activities will benefit from Unconditional Cash Grants. In this way, HHs can meet their basic needs without further liquidation of their productive assets.

Impact on environment/natural resources

The proposed grant will enhance improved resource management and equal access to available resources. Furthermore, VSF G has considered seriously the ‘environmental marker across all the project designed activities. ’The project will protect and restore the natural resource base in the livelihoods systems of the poor and vulnerable households through redressing environmental degradation, increasing efficiency and promoting conservation of soil, water and biomass resources. Due to potential waste generation through medical supplies (vaccination and Treatment campaigns) VSF G will ensure mitigation measures are in place such as proper waste management of the disposable elements of animal vaccination and treatment. The waste will be incinerated safely and each training will include element of environment protection.
