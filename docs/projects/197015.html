Project Summary

The project titled ‘‘Community Engagement and Women’s Empowerment & Sexual and Gender Based Violence in the East, Adamawa and North regions” aims to empower communities and achieve gender equality by ensuring that People of concern to UNHCR receive proper assistance, including livelihood opportunities, cash-based interventions and inclusion initiatives that will ensure a dignified life and the respect of beneficiary rights. Activities planned to be implemented in 2023 seek to improve the protection and living conditions of 60,450 direct beneficiaries (42,315 refugees and 18,135 Cameroonians) and 181,350 indirect beneficiaries during lifetime of project. The project targets East, Adamawa and northern regions focusing on the most vulnerable groups, with 52.33% representing female (women & girls) and 53.63% of children (girls & boys) respectively. Based on IMC-UK October 2022 statistics, these regions host 327,976 Central African Refugees (CAR) in both developed sites (74,503 refugees) and off-sites (253,473 refugees).2 In addition, among these Central African refugees, 57,726 are people with specific needs, including 12,103 women at risk and 6,474 persons living with physical disability.
As part of this project, it is necessary to strengthen community anchoring, thus facilitating the exit and sustainability strategy, which are based on the full participation of all stakeholders. Sustainability therefore aims at a transfer of competence to the government and its decentralized structures, including the communities. In other words, it is about preparing them to take charge of the protection of refugees and vulnerable populations. To do this, the proposed project will be implemented through four main strategic axes:
Capacity building of community-based organizations for self-ownership of all activities carried out for their benefit, in particular protection activities. In doing so, it will be able to identify its problems and provide solutions based on resources that can be mobilized locally. This involves revitalizing all the protection committees (School for Husbands, Women's Committees, Youth Committees, Gender Club, Girl Shine, etc.) within refugee sites, building the capacity of Local Protection Committees (LPC) by providing them with working materials, the strengthening of community mechanisms for the identification, reporting and care at community level of people with specific needs, in particular survivors of GBV, abandoned children or children living alone, the elderly, people living with disabilities, the chronically ill and large families (under the lead of the CLPs).
Capacity building of decentralized State structures (MINAS, MINPROFF, MINSANTE, MINJUSTICE, MINJEC, FMO, etc.) so that they are better equipped to take care of vulnerable people. The sectors seem to consider community-based protection activities as the competence of humanitarian actors. When they are called upon to be involved, they sometimes make their participation conditional on financial motivation. At both the operational and strategic levels, there is a lack of knowledge of subjects such as GBV by all sectors. This does not facilitate better appropriation of interventions and weakens coordination.
Safe spaces for women and girls will be converted into centers of wealth production. To do so, the beneficiaries of the services (skills building and informational activities) offered by these centers will be trained and organized in such a way as to ensure proper management of the center and of the products resulting from their work. These structures will be technically supported by the MINPROFF with financial support from the Councill of the locality.
A multi-sectoral approach that is psychosocial, medical, security and legal assistance will be necessary to ensure the success of our main objective. IMC-UK will focus on the services most crucial to ensure maximum impact on the target populations (refugee and vulnerable host community).

1 - Besoins / Needs

The socio-political crisis in CAR is unfolding in the North, Adamawa and East regions, which are considered as some of the poorest in Cameroon.3 With the complex and dynamic context of massive movements of refugee populations fleeing violence in CAR since 2014, there are currently more than 327,976 CAR refugees living in the Eastern, Adamawa and Northern regions. The situation has greatly weakened the already precarious socio-economic atmosphere in these regions and at a time when humanitarian assistance is being drastically reduced. This situation negatively affects the whole protection system of the population of concern, including CAR refugees and vulnerable host communities, thereby justifying conjoint humanitarian intervention of international and national organizations. With regards to community engagement, participation of the population of concern and the inclusion of women, men, girls, and boys in the humanitarian program cycle remains a weakness despite past interventions in community mobilization. There are limited opportunities for the crisis-affected population to seek durable solutions to their problems and to improve their self-reliance. UNHCR, which has an international mandate for the protection of refugees, has partnered with organizations to provide them with multifaceted assistance in order to meet their primordial basic needs. IMC UK is one of the organizations working with UNCHR since July 2014 providing a refugee health and Protection assistance project in East, Adamawa, and North regions of Cameroon. Several hundred thousand refugees of various nationalities but mainly Central African refugees have found asylum in East side of Cameroon over several decades. Despite the voluntary repatriation that took place in June, July and August 2022, Cameroon continues to host—as of October 2022—327,976 Central African refugees in the East, Adamawa and the North regions. East, Adamawa and North regions hosting refugees in Cameroon are some of the poorest regions of Cameroon where the local population itself is already highly vulnerable. Without the basic infrastructure to absorb the influx of refugees, the North and Adamawa regions specifically recorded some of the highest rates of poverty in the country in 2019, at 68% and 47% respectively.5 Poverty in the region disproportionally affects women and girls, who must spend additional time carrying out income generating activities to support their subsistence. Forty-four percent of the surveyed households in the 2019 UNHCR & WFP JAM6 were headed by females with an average household size of six with four children under 18 years. These female-headed households experience a double load of caring for the family while engaging in income generating activities. In the context of protracted violence in the North, Adamawa, and the east of Cameroon, there is a need for integrated emergency assistance that meets the immediate protection needs of women while strengthening community structures that lay the foundation for sustainable improvements in the protection of vulnerable women and girls. Women's participation in community self-management structures was low and there was a slight growing acceptance and tolerance for women's leadership in communities concerning young adult women aged 18-30 years old. Similarly, women in committees do not most of the time hold leadership positions and there are not opportunities for refugee women to be integrated into the local associations set up by the women of the host community. However, a great move has been observed from 37% in January 2022 to 60% in October 2022 with respect to the participation of women in decision-making positions in community groups and committees since sensitization and advocacy activities on women leadership are carried-out by IMC.
