Project Summary

The goal of the project is to provide life-saving child protection assistance to Rohingya refugees and host communities in Cox’s Bazar.
To achieve this goal, the project aims to ensure that children and adolescents have access to structured (CFS/MPC) and unstructured psychosocial support and age-appropriate life-skills-based knowledge strategy. It also seeks to establish and strengthen community-based child protection mechanisms to enhance the well-being of children and build resilient communities.
The project will focus on engaging key stakeholders, including children aged 0-10 and adolescents aged 11-18, particularly at-risk groups such as unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), children affected by armed forces or armed groups (CAAFAG), child laborers, and survivors of sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV). Parents and caregivers will receive training on positive parenting skills and psychosocial support to better support children’s development, including at least 3% children with disabilities following intensive assessment, set criteria of selection with WGQ tools and risk assessment. Local child protection/ youth group will be empowered to promote positive social norms, address harmful practices and advocate protection issues. Capacity-building initiatives will also target CP & non-CP actors, including humanitarian and multi-sector service providers, ensuring integration of child protection principles across programming. Close coordination with the Child Protection Sub-Sector (CPSS), Education Sector, and Gender-Based Violence Sub-Sector (GBVSS) will ensure alignment and resource optimization.
WVB will provide robust psychosocial support and life skills to children and adolescents through structured and non-structured programs in safe, inclusive, and high-quality learning environments. Activities will include leadership, social cohesion, and resilience training. Additionally, case management services will support children at risk, including UASC, CAAFAG, and child survivors of SGBV, with referrals to specialized multi-sectoral services. Improving existing referral systems and strengthening community-based mechanisms will remain key priorities.
Awareness campaigns, workshops, and social media platforms will be utilized to educate children, caregivers, and community members on child protection and GBV risks. Materials such as infographics, videos, and stories will be disseminated through platforms like Facebook, WhatsApp, WV webprtal including donor website. These activities will ensure cultural appropriateness, minimize risks through pre-testing, and measure engagement using analytics while safeguarding sensitive data. Dignity kits will also be distributed to vulnerable groups to enhance protection and reduce social stigma.
To ensure inclusivity and effectiveness, the project will employ participatory community mapping and establish updated referral pathways for child protection services. Child-friendly feedback mechanisms will allow input from program participants and community members, enhancing accountability and responsiveness. The project will integrate child protection principles across sectors, avoiding duplication of efforts through stakeholder mapping and alignment with community-based mechanisms.
The project’s impact will be measured through a robust monitoring and evaluation system, employing surveys, spot-checks, satisfaction surveys, and group discussions. ECHO-specific assessments, regular reporting, and feedback from program participants will guide continuous improvement. WV will produce reports for CPSS and internal use to ensure ongoing learning and adaptation.
In essence, WVB’s project will be a comprehensive approach aimed in child protection for vulnerable children, empowering communities, and establishing sustainable mechanisms for child protection in Cox's bazar.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Cox's Bazar is a socio-economically vulnerable district in Bangladesh, with the situation of children and adolescents exacerbated by the influx of Rohingya refugees since 2017, the COVID-19 pandemic, and the global fund crisis. Currently, 1,010,298 (Old - 960,298 and New-50,000) Rohingya refugees reside in 33 congested camps in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazila of Cox’s Bazar District, with 51.72% being children aged 0-17, including 25.25% girls and 26.46% living with disabilities (Source: JRP-25 Refugee population fact sheet). Child marriage, child labor and CAAFAG cases persist as critical issues Child marriage poses health risks, disrupts education, and perpetuates gender inequality. The severity level for child marriages in the region is catastrophic, with an incidence rate of 4.53 (Source: Refugee/CPIMS+/Expert Judgement, 2024).The Multi-Sectoral Need Assessment found rates of child marriage at 1.7% among refugees and 0.3% in the host community (Source: JMSNA 2024). Child labor is also a signification concern in child protection issues where 3.6% of host communities and 3.8% of refugee children ages 5-17 were reportedly contributing to household income. 3.3 % of the host community and 3.1% of the refugee households reported that one of their children (under 18 years old) was not residing with them (JMSNA-2024). 0.25% of households reported at least one child (<18) associated with armed forces or armed groups (CAAFAG) at rohingya camp. (Source: ISNA 2024).The report and data sources show that the Rohingya refugee crisis has created a unique and challenging environment for child protection. Rohingya children are particularly vulnerable to child labor, child marriage, and trafficking due to their displacement, poverty, and lack of access to education and other protective services. The host communities in Cox's Bazar are also facing significant challenges in protecting their children. The influx of Rohingya refugees has put a strain on local resources, and host children are also at risk of child labor, child marriage, child trafficking and other forms of exploitation. To address these challenges, WV is focusing on community engagement, awareness raising, coordinated interventions, and establishing child-friendly spaces.

2.3 - Age & Disability

In a Child Protection Project focusing on age and disability, the project team plans to execute a thorough needs assessment. This assessment will specifically address the vulnerabilities and requirements of children across various age brackets and those with disabilities. Its aim is to ensure that services provided are accessible, pertinent, and fitting for children of all ages and abilities.To enable inclusive communication, the project aims to develop strategies that encompass children with disabilities and those in different age groups. Currently, there are 16 safe spaces and child-friendly environments established. There's a plan to enhance community sustainability and ownership by implementing materials such as braille, sign language, and simplified resources easily comprehensible by everyone. An integral task within the project is Child-Centered Case Management, which involves implementing case management strategies sensitive to the age and disability of children. Training for case managers will focus on identifying and addressing the distinct needs of each child. The project emphasizes the establishment of a robust Monitoring and Evaluation Function. This mechanism will track the participation and progress of children across different age groups and those with disabilities. The data obtained will drive adjustments and enhancements to project activities. For comprehensive community engagement, the project will undertake Community Awareness and Sensitization efforts. This involves conducting awareness campaigns within the community to foster inclusion, diminish stigma related to disability, and encourage active participation of children with disabilities in community life and activities.

WVB is committed to ongoing efforts to create a safe and supportive environment for all children involved in the project that aligns with PSEA policy and the PSEA network membership. It will continue to roll out PSEA training for Training of Trainers (ToT) sessions for WVB staff in the protect timeline. WVB will sensitize committees, parents, and children on PSEA, which would be instituted to ensure the safety of all children and adolescent learners in MPCs/CFSc, and other child-related activities. 2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

WVB integrates climate change adaptation and mitigation into its Disaster Risk Management (DRM) and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) initiatives to enhance community resilience. As part of the Rapid Protection Assessment Team and Protection Emergency Response Unit (PERU), WVB provides immediate crisis responses while coordinating with Site Management, Shelter Management, and Camp-in-Charge (CiC) offices. It also supports Disaster Management Unit (DMU) volunteers with training on DRR and safe referrals for gender-based violence (GBV), ensuring alignment with the government’s Standing Orders on Disaster 2019 and contributing to national disaster strategies. To align with the Climate Action strategy, WVB focuses on: House-to-house visits by trained volunteers address climate issues, disaster preparedness, and environmental risks, with tailored messaging for children & adolescent groups, including individuals with disabilities. Life-skills sessions include modules on climate change impacts, mitigation practices, and sustainable behaviors to promote resilience. PSS sessions build coping mechanisms for climate-related stressors and enhance resilience among children, adolescents, and caregivers. WVB integrates climate considerations into contingency and Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) plans for Rohingya and host communities. Key activities include: Training on evacuation, first aid, and disaster response for community members, including children, youth and women and Stockpiling essential items such as clothing and dignity kits for emergencies. Child Protection in Disasters: Awareness campaigns on child protection messages to safeguard children before, during, and after disasters. By linking disaster response with climate adaptation, WVB ensures that vulnerable populations, especially children and individuals with disabilities, are equipped to understand, mitigate, and adapt to climate challenges, fostering stronger community resilience.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

The project integrates strong Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) initiatives, focusing on providing essential child protection services during crises such as floods, cyclones, and fires in both Rohingya and host communities. Aligned with the ISCG EPR strategies, we are committed to continuous collaboration and harmonization. WV BRCR have a comprehensive emergency response plan with contingency fund including three emergency cyclone shelters at Cox's Bazar, Mega Kitchen in Ukhiya and many facility centers in projected community. Following comprehensive response plan an ERRT (Emergency Rapid Response Team) formed with details role of actions covering children relocation, family reunification and case management. The contingency fund allows the project to quickly scale activities to address urgent needs.In terms of contingency capacity, the project plans to cover children and caregivers during emergencies, with in-kind resources such as food, water, medical supplies, and child protection kits. The allocated contingency funds ensure rapid mobilization of resources. Additionally, the project prioritizes the relocation and safeguarding of children in relocation centers, with trained child protection staff, safe spaces for children, and psychosocial support programs to address the emotional needs of displaced children. These actions aim to mitigate harm and ensure the safety and well-being of children during emergencies.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

The project actively engages youth aged 15–24 from both Rohingya and host communities through targeted child protection activities. To ensure inclusion and age-appropriate interventions, youth are categorized into adolescent youth (under 18) and adult youth (18 and above): Adolescent Youth (Under 18): - Capacity Building: Adolescents receive training in child protection, life skills, and leadership tailored to their developmental needs. - Peer Education: They serve as peer educators, raising awareness on child protection issues within their communities. - Participation in Campaigns: Adolescents are actively involved in events such as International Youth Day and the 16 Days of Activism, giving them a platform to express their perspectives and advocate for child protection. Adult Youth (18 and Above): - Alignment with Child Protection: Adult youth act as community advocates and mentors, complementing child protection interventions by supporting younger peers and strengthening community-based protection mechanisms. - Skill Development: Training focuses on advanced life skills, leadership, and advocacy, equipping them to address child protection issues more effectively. - Role in Awareness Campaigns: They lead awareness campaigns and serve as role models, amplifying child protection messages at both community and institutional levels. - Advocacy: Youth and adult will act as catalyst in their neighborhood level to advocate child protection issues in coordination with project teamThrough these activities, the project ensures meaningful engagement for both adolescent and adult youth. By empowering young people with skills, knowledge, and platforms for advocacy, the project fosters a generation that is informed, resilient, and equipped to prevent and respond to child protection issues, ultimately strengthening community-based protection systems.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

To enhance refugee self-reliance and resilience by focusing on life skill development, community engagement, and leadership. Through structured skills training and psychosocial support activities, individuals and families gain the necessary tools to foster economic independence and better cope with daily challenges. This approach not only helps refugees manage adversities but also strengthens their ability to recover and thrive in the long term. Community engagement plays a key role, with a particular emphasis on developing leadership among women, youth, and community leaders, which fosters local advocacy and strengthens support networks. Additionally, by involving parents and caregivers, along with promoting social norm transformation, the project encourages the creation of supportive environments. A number of Rohingya people will be engaged in project as Volunteers for project implementation period and enjoy monthly remuneration for increasing their self-resilience & self-esteem.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

The project aligns with Cox’s Bazar's District Development and Growth Priorities (DDGP) by supporting child protection, skill-building, and social cohesion initiatives in Palongkhali and Ratnapalong Union. It addresses key DDGP goals such as strengthening community-based child protection systems, enhancing capacity-building efforts, and reducing violence related to child protection issues. WVB will collaborate closely with the government and other actors to link diversified IGA and other livelihood opportunities for community peoples. By offering relevant skill-building initiative will be key contribution to the district’s long-term development. Community engagement activities foster social cohesion between refugees and host populations as emphasized in the DDGP. These targeted efforts not only meet immediate protection needs but also contribute to long-term development goals, enhancing the safety, dignity, and economic stability of vulnerable host communities in Cox’s Bazar.
