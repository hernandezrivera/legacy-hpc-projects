Project Summary

Through the proposed project, Relief International (RI) intends to reach 691,168 (M:
331,761 and F: 359,407) individuals and will provide quality emergency lifesaving and
recovery services to conflict- and natural disaster-affected IDPs and host communities in
6localities in North Darfur (Elfasher 193,870, Mellit 21,732, Tawila 21,000, Kalemando
5500, Dar Al Salam 22,578 and Almaha 17,222), 4 localities in Blue Nile (Al Rosaries
22,729, Damazine 31,846, Geissan 46,956, El Mahi 5500), two localities in Al Jazirah (Wad
Madani43,707 and South Al Jazirah 39,528)two localities in Sennar (Sennar 45,000 and
Singa 36,000), two localities in River Nile (At bara 25,000 and Shendi 32,000) and two
localities in Red Sea (Port Sudan 46,000 and Suakin 35,000). RI plans to reach 135313
(M: 64950 and F: 70363) individuals in ND, 51376 (M: 24660 and F: 26716) individuals in
BN, 29160 (F: 15163, M: 13997) in River State, 27200 (F: 14144, M: 13056) in River Nile,
38880 (F: 20218, M: 18662) in Sennar and 39953 (F: 20776, M: 19177) in Al Jazirah with
emergency health response. RI will ensure that its interventions are integrated with other
services such as nutrition, protection, and WASH.
Under the 2024 Humanitarian Response Plan, RI will align its emergency health
interventions to the Health Strategic Objectives: SO1: Provide safe, timely, principled and
gender-responsive life-saving humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable groups to
reduce mortality and morbidity in areas with high severity of need or at risk of a further
deterioration of critical humanitarian needs; SO2: directly respond to the conflict-driven
protection needs of crisis-affected people through principled and conflict-sensitive
assistance and advocacy as well as prevent and mitigate emerging protection risks, with
due regard to international norms and standards; and SO3: Provide safe, equitable,
dignified and unhindered access to critical basic services and livelihoods opportunities
for the most vulnerable groups to prevent further erosion of their coping abilities. RI will
improve access to health care and ensure that the beneficiaries are provided timely and
quality care through management and treatment of diseases at the outpatient health (MCH) services, including sexual reproductive health, antenatal (ANC), safe
deliveries, postnatal (PNC), expanded program on immunization (EPI), sexual and genderbased
violence (SGBV) care, integrated community case management (ICCM), mental
health and psychosocial support services (MHPSS) and other services such as laboratory
services and health education in 81 primary healthcare facilities (47 in ND, 12 in BN, 4 in
Sennar, 4 in River Nile, 4 in Red Sea, and 10 in Al Jazirah). Through the proposed
lifesaving emergency health services, RI will build the capacity of567 (M: 200 and F: 367)
health workers through formal and on-the-job training will strengthen the disease
surveillance system at the community and health facilities level, and will be active in all
activities that aim at preventing and responding to common non-communicable diseases
and communicable diseases, including disease outbreak prevention, management and
control. Furthermore, RI will strengthen the referral mechanism and will ensure that
gynecological, obstetrical, surgical, and medical cases have access to secondary
healthcare levels. This will require 24/7 functional ambulance service and a motivated
health workforce and support staff, including ambulance drivers.
RI will further strengthen the integrated comprehensive community-based Protection and
response component, using a survivor-centered approach. To reduce and mitigate GBV
risks, the project will engage with community-based protection network groups forming
one in each of the health facilities to strengthen the referral mechanism. The GBV
response will be overseen by well-trained and experienced RI GBV Managers and officers.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

RI aims at promoting equitable access to health services which ensures that theprojects reach the most deprived members of the targeted communities. Theproposed intervention will be aligned with the three 2024 HNRP strategic objectives.Nearly all the 2024 health indicators are gender-disaggregated, and it is expected thatover 50% of beneficiaries will be women. Special attention will be paid to mothers andPLWs through the provision of reproductive health services. Privacy andconfidentiality of patients will be respected and shall be ensured at all times of servicedelivery. RI will ensure the 24/7 functionality of ambulance service and will deploy skilled village-based midwives will ensure safe home deliveries are conducted.Community Health Workers(CHW) and village health committees (VHC) will identifypeople with disabilities and/or the elderly who require home visits for treatment. VHCswill have both men and women representatives; the proposed project shall aim at a50:50 gender representation. RI will take a socio-inclusion approach and shall ensurethat people with disabilities (PwD), persons with mental health challenges, those whoare in hard-to-reach locations/villages, and those with special needs can access theneeded health services by working on both supply and demand sides of healthcareservices. Service providers will be sensitized on the rights and needs of PwDs/ thosewith special needs; and will be trained on inclusive health services provision looking atthe dimensions of gender, age, and abilities. Finally, RI will ensure to enforcement ofzero tolerance for discrimination/stigmatization and lack of respect towards PwDamong staff. RI will engage with clan and religious leaders to get their support tospread awareness messages in the community. Working with influential communityallies to engage men, boys, women, and girls is expected to foster communityacceptance for the participation and access to health services of deprived individualsand groups, including PwD, women and girls, and the elderly. Timing and location forat-risk groups will be considered to facilitate access to assistance. Medical responsesto GBV will be offered, through professional support, clinical care, and referral, and willbe guided by standards of IASC Guidelines for Integrating GBV Interventions inHumanitarian Action. There will be posters in the HFs describing the services offeredat the PHCCs and discouraging any form of abuse. RI will establish a specialcommittee, to discuss women's reproductive and child health issues and addressthem within the available resources. RI will use seconded staff to work withpopulations with unique needs.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Relief International has a standard operating procedure (SOP) that guides the proper implementation and management of the accountability to community principles including safeguarding issues such as PSEA and child abuse. RI is working to strengthen the accountability system to be able to capture the needs and voices of the program recipients at each stage of the project cycle management: need assessment, designing, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation. RI has also rolled out its own Child Safeguarding Policy across the operation areas to reinforce existing policies such as Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) within RI and shall maintain a protective environment for the beneficiaries including children attending all RI-supported services. All staff receive mandatory training on this policy. Adding to existing feedback and complaint mechanisms, RI also aims to strengthen its accountability system, putting a mechanism that enables coordination with the inter-agency to facilitate learning of best practices and collaboration with other partners for a comprehensive response, including psycho-social support to SGBV survivors. RI will strengthen the capacity of staff to manage complaints and feedback to inform project design, improve service delivery, and enhance program recipients' participation in each state of the project management cycle. As part of the existing accountability system, RI also documents community satisfaction with services which enables informed decisions on program design and adjustment, budget allocations, and future programming.
