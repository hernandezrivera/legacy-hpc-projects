Project Summary

According to the 2021 Syria Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), in early 2021, the humanitarian situation in many parts of North East Syria remains dire with approximately 1.41 million people in acute need of assistance including an estimated 670,000 people who remain long-term-displaced. Lack of access to safe water and sanitation services continues to affect the health of hundreds of thousands of households.
The program seeks to improve WASH services by rehabilitating water stations, networks and sewage systems, undertake vector control, chlorination mapping, capacity building for local water departments (training, water safety plans, essential supplies and equipment), emergency WASH in camps and settlements (water trucking, installation of latrines and water distribution points), and sustainable water supply solutions for camps in the form of a water network, with a focus on those areas of where populations are currently reliant on unsafe water direct from the Euphrates or via water trucking. There will be a particular focus on Deir Ez Zor where it is estimated that around 100,000 individuals are currently displaced and where following the territorial defeat of IS there have been a large number of spontaneous returns to hard-to-reach areas, and where services such as electricity or water supply are much damaged after years of IS rule.

4 - Needs

According to the 2021 Syria Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 12.2 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance, of whom 5.9 million people are in acute need. 6.7 million people remain internally displaced with many families living in overcrowded camps and collective shelters. WASH services at locations affected by an influx of IDPs and ongoing conflict are often limited. Sporadic conflict and inter-communal tensions have led to heavily damaged WASH infrastructure and challenges to humanitarian access, particularly in Deir-ez-Zor, as well as the ongoing spread of COVID-19 pandemic. According to the WASH HNO 2021, 78% of the population in Deir-ez-Zor (residing in areas under the control of the SDF) are in acute need with limited access to a safe and sufficient water supply, and effective waste management and challenges accessing basic hygiene items. Moreover, 48% of households in Ar-raqqa reported acute WASH needs in the HNO whilst 18% of households have acute WASH needs in Aleppo. The majority of the population in northeast Syria has limited access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene services. The lack of access to clean water impacts the lives and health of all community members and is of increasing concern with the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic and the increasing need for vulnerable individuals to be able to maintain good hygiene practices to limit virus transmission. A lack of sanitation facilities in schools, health facilities present a COVID-19 transmission risk and act as a barrier to education. Many emergency latrines which were constructed at the beginning of the conflict have fallen into a state of disrepair due to a lack of maintenance.

5 - Implementation of activities

This NGO will rehabilitate and re-commission 23 water supply systems, 9 water station and 14 water networks focusing specially in Deir Ez Zour, but working as well in Raqqa and Aleppo governorates. These stations will be rehabilitated (were possible) to meet the designed capacity.To increase the water quality/safety, a total of 25 chlorination systems will be installed in water station lacking chlorination systems or non-functional ones. Chlorine will be provided to these water stations, and to the rehabilitated stations as well for the lifetime of the project. Also, this NGO will rehabilitate 3 sewage systems. In order to detect the most urgent needs this NGO will engage with authorities and the cluster, and will as well prepare exhaustive assessments to clearly identify the needs.The rehabilitation of WASH facilities will ensure that IDPs have access to gender-segregated latrines including hand washing stations and that the ratio of people per latrine does not exceed 20 as per SPHERE standards in the Syrian context. Vector Control activities to control the spread of vector borne diseases, specially Leshmaniasis, will target 62 communities in Deir Ez Zor and Membij, with each community on average comprising 3,000 individuals. This NGO will also distribute 1,500 hygiene kits to IDP HH in informal IDP camps and collective centers. Hygiene promotion activities will be conducted in these same areas where hygiene kits are distributed. Distributions of NFI/hygiene/shelter kits and food rations/vouchers/cash, plus emergency WASH activities will target all new IDPs displaced within the previous month according to their needs and in coordination with other organizations.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Unaccompanied minors as well as female-headed households are currently at the greatest risk of protection related issues. They are exposed to a range of different forms of harm, from SGBV, to conflict related trauma and early marriage. Related to WASH, the design of latrines include, in the blocks for women, privacy walls with basins inside the wall space to provide privacy. All blocks of latrines, not only women’s but men’s as well, have solar lights to reduce the risks of GBV (the lights are included as well in men’s latrines as well to avoid creating a social hub in the women’s latrines by night). This NGO is improving its designs and will include a specific latrine in each block to be used by children. Water supply infrastructure rehabilitation will be designed and implemented to allow for community members to access water in their home, or in some cases a water source nearby their residence, the water distribution points are designed to be used by children with taps at a comfortable height. Accessible latrines and wash facilities are also installed. Any vulnerable protection cases identified by this NGO WASH team in the course of implementing the activities listed above will be referred to other service providers in NES. To aid this, in-house protection specialists will provide comprehensive training to all staff and partners on the referral mechanism and pathways. These protection specialists will also join different field teams (WASH, FSL etc) on a regular basis to provide protection advice and on-the-job mentoring to other staff. This NGO has a specific policy in place that all staff and contractors have to sign and abide, and that clearly states the expected behaviour to avoid abuse and exploitation.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks- Further escalation of the conﬂict, or deterioration in the security situation, in NES as a result of any of the following: - Escalation in the ongoing conflict between the SDF and Government of Turkey, extending beyond the current areas of operation. -IS resurgence in Deir ez Zour with extension of attacks and activities to surrounding areas -Tension between authorities and communities or inter-community conﬂict, jeopardising access to affected project areas. - Markets affected by continued currency devaluation, border closures or escalation of conflict, leading to shortage of supplies and market price fluctuations - COVID-19 pandemic escalates, new lockdowns restrict access Assumptions- This project will be able to access beneficiaries, assess their specific needs and implement the programme accordingly. There will be no signiﬁcant shifts in control over key locations in NES - This project will be able to access beneﬁciaries, assess their speciﬁc needs and implement the programme accordingly - Markets continue to function, internal and regional borders remain open and goods are available locally/regionally Mitigating Measures- Strengthen operational resilience. In the event of increased insecurity in an area, shift operations to nearest unaffected base of operations. • Flexibly shifting modalities as required between in-kind, voucher and cash depending on cross-border access, the state of markets and availability of key goods. • Full engagement in, and the development of robust, continuity of services plans to minimise the potential humanitarian consequences of any disruption in services to target area • COVID-19 SOPs for general IPC in place as well as activity level SOPs.
