Project Summary

As part of SAMS mandates in Health sector, SAMS 2022-2023 programs will be in line with Health Sector 1st , 2nd and 3rd strategic objectives, and as SAMS runs 33 HFs in Idleb, Aleppo, Al Hasakeh and Ar Raqqa governorates, SAMS will continue to provide health support for population in 2022-23 through continuing implementing activities of strengthen Health capacity to prepare for, detect and deliver a timely response to disease outbreaks, including C19 and strengthen health system capacity to support continuity of care, strengthen community resilience, and respond to IDP movements. In this program, SAMS will prioritize the reduction of morbidity and mortality through improving the access to quality health care (primary and secondary, specialized services, MCs, and an ambulatory system, C19 response) in 33 HFs and MCs, they are:

SAMS will support facilities on different levels including coverage of staff incentives, running costs, provision of pharmaceuticals and other medical and non-medical commodities, rehabilitation/maintenance of the HFs. SAMS will provide recruitment support, capacity building, and medical supervision to ensure the HFs are staffed with qualified medical personnel and to enhance compliance with standard treatment protocols. SAMS will provide the following services at the primary healthcare level: SRH services (ante-natal care, post-natal care, family planning, normal deliveries, gynecological consultations, and clinical management of rape), EPI, communicable and non-communicable diseases, Nutrition, MHPSS, health awareness, and basic laboratory and radiology services. Services at the quality secondary health care level will include gynecologic surgeries and cesarean sections, High-risk pregnancies trauma management, major surgeries, hospitalization services, blood transfusion, hemodialysis, and ICUs, in addition to C19 response (CM, Triage, PPE/IPC, CHWs). This program will also provide cancer treatment to Breast, colon testicular and ovarian, Hodgkin, and non-Hodgkin Lymphoma patients, Cath Lab, Pathology, GI endoscopy, Dental services, Paediatric ALL, ICU, NICU, PICU, Kidney transplant medications. Additionally, SAMS will support the ambulatory system (10 ambulances, part of which will be dedicated to transferring C19 patients). In addition, as part of SAMS focus on contributing to its goal of increasing the capacity of the health care professional in NWS through advancing SAMS’ investment in its medical education program: Supporting Residents Doctors; Supporting Midwifery and Nursing Education; and Mental Health Education. SAMS will coordinate Health Cluster through 4Ws and HeRAMS and constant participation and active membership in TWG: COVID-19 TF, SRH, MHPSS, Non-Communicable Diseases, SIG, Midwifery Capacity Building Strategy for NWS, Secondary Health Care, and Drug Supply Line, in addition, to coordinate with other NGOs on the referral, and the contribution to DHIS2, notwithstanding its membership in the NGO Forum, SNA, HD & others.

4 - Needs

Syria is facing the eleventh year of conflict, along with economic rapture and devastation accompanied by multiple waves of the COVID-19 pandemic, resulting in increased trauma, poverty, and insecurity. The fragile healthcare system across Syria and in Northwest (NWS) in particular, continue to face concurrent emergencies and chronic challenges which affect the availability and quality of health services across Syria, as well as the physical and mental wellbeing of the population. Moreover, only 65% (56.8% Idleb, 39.3% Aleppo) of the HFs are fully functional in NWS, of these, 17.2% hospitals, 42.1% fixed PHCs, 17.4% mobile clinics, 10.1% are specialized care centers, and 13.2% are “other” HFs (HeRAMS Q3). Millions of people have been living below emergency standards for healthcare and have limited access to services. Chronic shortages of healthcare workers ( HeRAMS indicating the total number of health workers in NWS is less than half of SPHERE), particularly in NWS, prevent full functionalization of health facilities and essential services with RH and maternal health services heavily impacted. Significant psychological trauma is evident in survivors of explosive incidents seeking mental health, psychosocial support, and physiotherapy services yet are unreported due to lack of resources to assess and address the specific needs. With more support to health systems and HFs in NWS SAMS will be able to continue providing life-saving health services and enhance the health system

5 - Implementation of activities

SAMS will work on the following activities: Recruitment and Support the salaries of the HF staff, Rehab works for HF and support the operational costs, Capacity building for medical and non-medical staff, Provide PHC services according to updated EHSP including CD, NCD, child health, SRH and mhGAP, provide trauma, surgeries, SHC, and in-patients services in SAMS hospitals and specialized centers including ICU, PICU, NICU, maternal and child health, IM, hemodialysis, oncology, MHPSS, and C19 designated hospitals. In addition to procure medical/non-medical commodities to the HFs, Raising awareness sessions, health-promotion, to the community through CHWs, self-care and staff care activities, Provision of ambulatory and referral services, Implementation of safe and effective medical waste management protocols, Sustain services of EPI, Provide medical education (midwifery, nursing, and mental health), in addition to the support of the resident doctors’ project, and Provision of PSEA and safeguarding training and support the DHIS2. MEAL Field monitors will conduct quarterly visits using checklists/spot checks, including verifying attendance sheets, payment receipts, accuracy, and patient data entry, in order to monitor activities and assess impact. Besides SAMS CRM channels, MEAL team will conduct patient satisfaction surveys (including protection mainstreaming indicators).

Impact: High Mitigation: Electronic data and information is properly protected and backed up according to SAMS SOPs (SAMS EMR system is already in place). Risk: Women and girls are prevented from attending certain training or sessions. Possibility: medium, Impact: High, Mitigation: Coordinate and communicate with the community leaders to identify our work, in addition, to conducting outreach visits. 10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Beneficiaries are subject to PSEA risk. Possibility: Low. Impact: High. Mitigation: The availability of a clear complaint mechanism within the service centers and the definition of it during the definition of the beneficiaries of the services of the Center and explain the mechanism of its flag "complaints box and mechanisms" follows the complaints section directly to the private line and mail moderator complaints section and brochures and the mechanism of regular complaints, in addition to the availability ofperiodic training for service providers and ensure supervision of effective technical service.SAMS displays various posters to enhance the awareness of both the health facility staff and the beneficiaries regarding the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), the services provided, the patients’ rights and responsibilities, and the Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM). The CRM poster includes the complaints/feedback channels (Phone hotline number, WhatsApp, Viber, and email), and SAMS installed a designated ‘Questions/Comments/Concerns’ box. Moreover, SAMS developed a short animation video displaying patients’ rights and SAMS’ CRM channels, the video is published via SAMS social media channels.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Control by unaccepted groups / GoS. Mitigation measures: (ATC), Information security SOP; Relocating the activities locations; Inform the partner civil authorities (HD and Civil Local Authorities), of the suspension and the reasons, in order to play their role in negotiation and advocacy, and apply the agreements of neutralizing humanitarian facilities.Risk: UNSC XB non-renewal. Impact: High. Mitigation: SAMS has multiple channels of funding and has cross-border access to pass commodities to Syria with its wide local coordination with local authorities. Alongside contingency plans from OCHA and UN in place prior to implementation.Risk: Potential high turnover of staff receiving salaries. Mitigation measure: Potential high turnover of staff receiving salaries; Repeating the training regularly to ensure that all new staff delivering it; Adopt appropriate salary-scale to retain the staff as possible.Risk: Theft. Mitigation Measures: Safety and security upgrades for facilities, including CCTV cameras and guards; Photo verification of received shipments, signed waybills, and notification if any missing items noted in HF count within 72 hours; Review of stock/inventory records.Risk: Financial risks (Cash Transfer/ Financial Diversion). Mitigation: SAMS utilizes official channels, authorized by the Government of Turkey, to transfer money from Turkey to Syria.
