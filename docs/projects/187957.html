Project Summary

Through this action, NGO X will address the multiple vulnerabilities that households and communities are facing after ten years of conflict. At the household level, the program design creates links between its multi-donor funded emergency response program to its food security, nutrition, winterization and shelter programming, and complements food assistance programming with survival needs assistance via Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA).
The proposed intervention is in line with the objectives and strategic direction of the various Northwest Syria Cluster Response Plans. These activities have been informed by Mutli-Sectoral Needs Assessments (MSRNAs) conducted in Idleb and North Aleppo during August and December 2021 as well as lessons learned from previous projects, ongoing monitoring, feedback from beneficiaries and other assessments conducted by NGO X. For the one off emergency MPCA component, a total of 20,000 households will be targeted under this project.
For the regular MPCA component, a total of 58,350 most vulnerable households who are receiving regular food assistance will be targeted under this project.
Funding for 52% of the planned project for 2022 has been full secured. While the remainder is highly likely to be secured, the funding application process has not yet been completed.

4 - Needs

The 2021 HNO saw a 21% increase of people in need in Syria compared with 2020, with 4.9mn in NWS alone – food and nutrition assistance is their top priority need. Economic decline continues to exacerbate needs, with the Syrian pound plummeting, and 82% of HHs reporting a significant reduction in ability to meet basic needs – 90% of people are now below the poverty line. Shelling continues to impact NWS with 273,320 displacements recorded last year (CCCM cluster). Additionally, an annual pattern of seasonal flooding continues; in Dec 2021, 169 sites hosting 9,138 HHs/54,212 individuals were affected by floods. NGO X’s needs assessment show the top priority needs were similar for people affected by shelling and flooding, and included MPCA and NFIs—including tents and plastic sheets, water, hygiene items and kitchen sets. In both cases, cash was identified as the most appropriate modality. A lack of financial resources has been preventing households from meeting survival needs. NGO X’s PDMs shows that households that receive MPCA and vouchers, and households receiving only vouchers both still spend the majority of their vouchers on food staples. However, those that have MPCA in addition to vouchers have a higher expenditure on nutrient-dense foods. Therefore, there is a need for vulnerable households with little to no income to receive regular assistance to meet their needs for survival needs.

5 - Implementation of activities

1 off MPCA: NGO X will ensure that cash can be deployed immediately in response to an emergency, crisis or sudden shock, where conditions for cash response are present. NGO X will first conduct a multi-sectoral needs assessment. NGO X will carry out 100% selection to inform this one-time emergency assistance. NGO X will distribute one-off emergency multi-purpose cash transfers to households experiencing sudden shocks/onset. The currency of the distributions is USD and the value in line with the SMEB tool used to calculate the MPC transfer value. NGO X will conduct Distribution Monitoring (DM) during the distributions and Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) surveys following distributions. In the PDM process/phase, as part of the PDM harmonization, NGO X adheres and uses the harmonized PDM tool developed by the Cash Working Group. The distribution of regular MPCA for survival needs (food & non-food), where necessary conditions exist, will be alongside regular food assistance. This consists of 6 rounds, each round being on average approximately $25. This will be carried out in 2022, however, due to current funding limitations, it is set to be discontinued in 2023. NGO X will conduct DM and PDM activities for each round of distribution. DM is carried out by the MEL team at up to 60% of distributions to ensure that distributions are carried out according to pre-defined criteria and distribution guidelines, and report irregularities, if any.

NGOX ensures they are accessible and safe for women and children to travel to in daylight and all distributions are undertaken in daylight. If beneficiaries cant attend distribution points they can authorize someone to collect their assistance for them, or home delivery can be organisedRisk: Child-headed/Orphans: Abuse from the children guardians / authorized people for the assistance Mitigation: • Commitment from the authorized people on the PSEA policy • The protection team try to contact the children regularly (after obtaining the consent from the delegates)to check their status • Awareness sessions for the child-headed on how to prevent/ report such cases
