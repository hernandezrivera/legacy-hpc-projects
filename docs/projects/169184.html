Project Summary

The Beirut Port explosion that occurred on August 4, 2020, caused widespread damage and casualties. The education sector was heavily impacted, as the explosion led to the partial or complete destruction of 162 public and private schools in Beirut and Mount Lebanon. Such devastation threatens to disrupt the new academic year and deprive about 85,000 Lebanese and non-Lebanese students enrolled in these schools of their right to education. Out of the total 81 affected schools are in Beirut 52 out of which are public noting that the total number of public schools in Beirut are 55. The Director of the UNESCO Regional Bureau for Education in the Arab States – Beirut stated that “the rehabilitation of schools damaged by the Beirut explosion is one of the basic conditions for ensuring access to primary and secondary education, and it also contributes to achieving Sustainable Development Goal 4 (SDG4), that is – students’ access to inclusive and equitable quality education, even in emergency and crisis situations where the provision of educational services may be disrupted”.
In summary, Beirut blast created multi-level challenges which requires close coordination among education actors to avoid duplication and ensure harmony of interventions.
For this RMF, based on its remarkable experience in school restoration and equipment, will be limiting its intervention to school rehabilitation as an entry point for securing the rigid infrastructure through which all other components can be achieved.
Under this scope RMF has formed an Emergency Response Unit to rehabilitate Beirut comprising of RMF partners Dar Al Handasa (a leading international consulting organization in engineering), and MAN Enterprise (one of the largest engineering and rehabilitation companies in Lebanon) both of which will be contributing their assessment and recommendation services for rehabilitation free of charge.
This will be conducted through a comprehensive approach that includes: Selection of most affected schools based on MEHE and UNOCHA recommendations; Conduct of structural assessment to ensure safety; Conduct of full Damage Assessment; Defining basic rehabilitation / restoration works; Launching procurement of contractors through open tenders and best procurement policies and procedures; Support cash for work of local population; Ensuring safety for Covid-19 in ongoing works; Collaborating with concerned decision makers and stakeholders; Monitoring works and ensuring quality control in rehabilitation and delivery
It is worthy to mention that RMF has over 25 years of experience in managing highly complex project and its development and humanitarian footprints are a result of a sound organizational, operational and financial management. For instance, RMF, in close collaboration with the Engineering unit at MEHE, through a USAID funded project LEAD has restored and equipped 85 public schools all over Lebanon.
At the organizational level, RMF’s structure, management and policies and procedures in HR, finance, procurement, code of conduct, documentation and filing, security, IT and others to ensure efficiency, transparency, accountability.
At the operational level, RMF has 220 staff members with experience in various field, facilities, tools, equipment and materials that can be mobilized towards achievement of objectives. RMF has a team of 20 engineers that design, implement and oversee infrastructural development projects following the highest local and international standards. At the financial level, RMF advanced financial department and RMF accounts are audited by KPMG and its financial and narrative reporting meet local and international donor requirements. RMF is currently managing 12 projects with a total value 40 million USD RMF’s main contributors include: UNHCR, UNDP, EU, Kingdom of the Netherlands, OCHA, USAID, UNICEF and Plan International.

1 - Needs

The academic year 2019-2020 is marked as the toughest year Lebanese and non-Lebanese population have witnessed. The academic year started with disruptions in learning and school closures, brought on by the onset of October Revolution and with a tightening noose around parents’ necks as many lost their jobs or saw their salaries cut as the effects of the economic crisis began to accelerate. It also ended with nationwide school closures courtesy of COVID-19 response measures and a shift to distance learning. In summary, students’ lives and routines have been turned upside down in a short period. Besides all of the aforementioned, the Beirut blast, in addition to the direct damage caused by explosion, led to several consequences:- Put the continuity of education at risk especially that 107 schools are identified as severely and moderately damaged;- Increased risk of out of school children with difficulty to access school because of families’ home displacement or personal injuries.- Amplified vulnerability of marginalized children and special needs children.- Increased financial pressure to cover education fees for families who lost their homes and jobs.- Imposed psychological effects on children.- Increased the risk of COVID-19 pandemic spread.- Threatened teachers’ attendance who are either wounded or psychologically affected. Furthermore, the table below shows the effects of Beirut Blast on public school both in Beirut central area:# of schools in need for structural rehabilitationConcrete False ceiling Bricks Fence Tiles18 schools 12 schools 3 schools 5 schools 3 schools # of schools in need for openingsDoors Windows Glazed Façade37 schools 35 schools 21 schools # of schools in need for equipmentDesk Computers Lighting4 schools 10 schools 4 schools In summary, out of 52 public schools located in Beirut Central area; 21 schools are in need for structural rehabilitation, 39 for openings and 8 for equipment. For all of the aforementioned, RMF chooses to intervene in rehabilitating 6 public schools located in Beirut Central area district identified as moderately and / lightly damaged for transforming them to safe haven for children willing to continue their education and enhance all aspects of school readiness and school access after Beirut blast.

2 - Activities

Overall Objective: Restore access to education services by undertaking repair works of selected affected school facilities Activity 1: Establish technical committee including representatives from RMF, Engineering unit at MEHE, Dal Al Handasa and MAN enterpriseActivity 2: Selection of 6 public schools identified as moderately and / or lightly damaged Activity 3: Conduct detailed safety and rehabilitation assessment of the school Activity 4: Develop bill of quantity for rehabilitation works from Dar Al Handasa side that will be free of chargeActivity 5: Conduct open public tender for the rehabilitation of the selected affected schools. As such RMF has a network and list of contractors who have excellent deliverables, with profiles and data to suppliers who are willing and able to provide the needed materials. These contractors will be invited to submit through the open tender. The selection of contractor through a joint steering committee between RMF, Dar El Handasa and MAN where at least top 3 finalists who have cost effectiveness and quality assurance will be evaluated and transparency conducted for the selection. Activity 6: Ensure participation of at least 50% Lebanese’s and affected population through cash for work mechanism while ensuring Covid-19 safety measures in line with the Ministry of Public Health and Ministry of Labour requirements.Activity 7: Monitor implementation of rehabilitation works as part of quality control through MAN engineering and consultancy firm as well as ensuring overall project implementation by RMF project management team respecting the highest level of quality assurance.
