Project Summary

This project aims at supporting a comprehensive package of health and outreach services including Primary Health Care and community health awareness services in Four of the existing health facilities in northwest Syria and through outreach activities in the surrounding areas:
1- G0703062!Azmarin PHC: Harim, Salqin, Idleb
2- G0703051 ! Morek Alaez camp PHCC/ Morek Alaez Camp
3- G0703088 ! Dier Hassan camp PHCC/ Dier Hassan Camp
Those centers were selected based on coordination with the Health cluster.
The motivation of this project is to continue providing services in the already established and equipped centers and through trained teams to the population in rural Idleb focusing on the most vulnerable people (women, boys, girls, and PWDs) with outreach and referral activities to the higher level of health services. The project will comprise two convergent components: primary health care and outreach services.
PHC centers are providing a package of health services as per the essential primary health care package; these services include pediatric services, Reproductive Health services, nutrition activities, management of communicable and non-communicable diseases, providing essential medicines and medical supplies. Through the proposed project, BINAA will continue supporting the outreach component, which will comprise services related to raising awareness and referral activities according to WHO Guidelines.

3 - Needs

Based on HNO 2023, the prolonged crisis has disrupted the health system across the country. Essential health services are the backbone of any resilient health system, yet only 58% of hospitals and 53% of primary health care centers (PHCs) in Syria are fully functional, numbers which drop to 6% among public health facilities in northeast Syria. The intense demands of the COVID-19 response over the course of 2020-2021 and an ongoing cholera outbreak response have engaged resources and capacity that normally would be expended in support of routine health and nutrition services delivery. Thus, the severely strained health system in Syria is impacting access to both health and nutrition services and leaving millions of people living below emergency standards for health care. Due to the cumulative effects of concurrent crises, the cost of health services; lack of medicines, supplies, or health staff; and fear of exposure to COVID-19 have replaced distance, over-crowding, and long wait times as the main self-reported barriers to health access with persons with disabilities disproportionately affected. Disrupted medication supply chains, particularly for psychotropic drugs and Noncommunicable Disease (NCD) medicines, were previously identified as a key gap in the health response and a contributor to child mortality, BINAA designed this project to cover these gaps in the targeted areas.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Syria is the country where the most major attacks took place during the last 2 years. And Community health workers in humanitarian aid face a unique constellation of risks regarding both potential perpetrators of violence and the context/location of attacks. Moreover, the risks of non-renewal of UN resolution in Syria becomes very critical due to the dependency of humanitarian assistance in NWS on the border of Bab Al Hawa where this assistance is supplied almost entirely via this operation, The outreach nutrition activities are often the first, and in many occasions the only point of contact for GBV survivors seeking assistance, without necessarily disclose the abuse due to shame or fear. In order to facilitate the required care, survivors must have safe access to health facilities, and it is critical that Nutrition service providers are equipped to offer survivor-centered, right-based, non-discriminatory, quality services. Mitigating measures: - Prevention and management of sexual violence and Intimate Partner Violence. Accordingly, affected population should have access to the reproductive health services of the Minimum Initial Service Package. -Specialized nutrition and outreach health staff should be trained on providing first-line support. - Identification of safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases connecting nutrition and health services and clinical management with social services, and privacy of the survivor.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA: is to protect vulnerable people from sexual exploitation and abuse by staff and associated personnel. During the course of their work, aid providers come into contact with vulnerable people. Humanitarian workers have access to goods and services that put them in a position of power over the community. Unfortunately, some humanitarian workers have used this imbalance of power to exploit and abuse vulnerable members of the community. The Nutrition sector and Community Nutrition workers should strive and commit to preventing sexual exploitation and abuse both on organizational and programmatic levels. Mitigating measures: - A PSEA policy describes standard of behavior for organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual. -Provide information to communities and people affected by crisis about the organization.
