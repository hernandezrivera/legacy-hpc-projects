Project Summary

This project focuses on building resilience in education systems across the BAY states of the Northeast. The project focuses on establishing safe learning spaces for children to learn, an opportunity for teachers to deliver quality lessons and groups and associations are strengthened to sustain education efforts at all the locations. Children who are out of school or have poor academic grades are selected and provided with non formal education using the government approved Accelerated Basic Education Curriculum (ABEC).
Intensive monitoring activities will be undertaken in collaboration with government stakeholders to ensure accountability and quality program implementation.
Throughout the period of the project, continuous engagement with the communities will be conducted to discuss around project results , challenges and successes, a key accountability to affected population to be implored on the project.

1 - Needs

The Boko Haram crisis has severely impacted women, men, boys and girls in the North-East. Women and girls have become more vulnerable due to the conﬂict, further exacerbating existing inequalities regarding access to basic services including education, the right to dignity and the right to the acquisition of land and property. As a consequence of the crisis, some women and girls have been reportedly forced into marriage or used as sex slaves or subjected to sexual violence. The conﬂict has impacted negatively on women’s economic situation, limiting their access to livelihoods and increasing the number of women-headed households. Men and boys also confront a range of threats within the context of the conﬂict, including gender-based violence, abduction and forceful recruitment and detention on suspicion of sympathies with the diﬀerent armed actors involved in the conﬂict. Loss in assets, combined with lack of access to education, skills training, and opportunities to earn an income, leave men and male youth unable to fulﬁl often-exacting norms of manhood and masculine identity as protectors and providers. Children and adolescents are among the worst aﬀected groups: they have lost out on education, skills acquisition and employment opportunities, and routinely are forcibly recruited by armed groups. The crisis has eroded social cohesion and triggered serious human rights violations including sexual and gender-based violence, abductions, forced recruitment of children by NSAGs, and deployment of women and children as “human bombs.” Family and community structures, such as traditional hierarchies, have broken down as a result of the conﬂict.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The project focuses mainly on creating safety and protecting the rights and dignity of beneficiaries. Through the education area, the project will focus on the safety of children in and around learning environment through trainings, setting up early warning systems and working with groups and associations to reduce and/or remove barriers to safety in and around learning environments. The Safe Schools Declaration concept through the government developed manuals and policies will be utilized. Also, the protection area focuses on protecting the children in and around schools through quality case management services to ensure that children's right are protected against social vices and extremism.During the period of the project, ROHI will work with groups and associations to deliver conflict sensitive trainings to teachers and ensure teachers understands the principles of Do No Harm. Also, through the project period, ROHI will work with the groups and associations in analyzing the Do No Harm framework which will be communicated to teachers and other community members through community engagement meetings.Furthermore, the project will take into consideration the needs of girls and adolescent girls in schools an at the communities. It will ensure that they are able to speak up boldly in safe spaces and access gender segregated facilities. The needs of the disable will be identified for child friendly spaces and other recreational activities while at construction, disability friendly structures will be constructed.While the capacity to provide support for children who are blind, deaf and dumb may be low for ROHI, we will refer them to other partners who are able to support or better work with the option of engaging extensively with the communities to refer them to the special needs school at Adamawa.Deliberate efforts will be made towards targeting girls who will be provided with accelerated leaning in learning environments through girls only sessions. Women will also be targeted for lesson delivery and also as MHPSS counsellors. Also children living with various forms of disability will be identified and data entry forms will record their details for further reporting to the NHF. In the mapping and selection of groups and associations, women groups and possibly disability groups will be selected and partner with ROHI.Lastly, the partnership with the groups and associations will be one that will build their capacities to support the affected population to claim their rights. Through accountability meetings, and advocacies, that will be conducted by the groups, they will provide information to the communities on the outcomes.Through the case management sessions and MHPSS sessions, the children will be able to build self protection capacities and coping mechanism. Parents and caregivers will be supported to provide better parenting to children through the parenting sessions and also other community awareness activities.

ROHI's presence at the proposed communities have allowed for the conflict affected population to participate in the needs assessment towards deriving the activities proposed. Regular and periodic engagement meetings will bringbeneficiaries across different age, gender, disability groups together and led by different groups and associations on a rotation basis to discuss issues around service delivery, gaps and develop action plans.
