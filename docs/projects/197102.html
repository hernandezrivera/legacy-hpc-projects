Project Summary

In response to the immense needs resulting from the conflict in the Northwest (NW) and Southwest (SW) regions in Cameroon, CRS is implementing twelve-month third phase to its current Anglophone Crisis Emergency Response (ACER III). ACER III is building on the success and learning of the previous phases of the response. Changes and adaptations are based on community consultations and input, needs assessments, coordination and harmonization with other humanitarian actors, and changes in the security context and accessibility.
The goal of the ACER III project is to improve food security and alleviate suffering of displaced and host households in the Northwest Region of Cameroon. The project seek to achieve this goal through two purposes: (1) vulnerable displaced and host households in the Northwest meet their basic food and NFI needs, and (2) vulnerable displaced and host households in the Northwest improve poultry production. In partnership with the Archdiocese of Bamenda, CRS is delivering an integrated package of assistance, providing vulnerable displaced and host households with poultry production training and support, as well as emergency food and NFIs. The project is using market-based approaches to support 10,800 people in 1,800 households over twelve months.
The project’s theory of change is: If households have improved access to sufficient quantity, quality, and diversity of food; and if they replace essential NFIs; and if they raise poultry to improve their livelihoods and generate income; and assuming that markets remain accessible and functional; then they will improve their food security and experience reduced suffering; because the first two phases of the ACER project have demonstrated that market access to food reduces household hunger scores, broader humanitarian evidence shows that food assistance leads to improved food security, and poultry interventions in Cameroon and Nigeria have demonstrated the potential to improve income and facilitate more sustainable access to food.
ACER III responds directly to the normative and felt needs of the affected population. In the International Organization for Migration (IOM)’s multisector needs assessment (MSNA) conducted in August 2021, food and non-food items (NFIs) continue to be priority needs. In the NW, nearly one million people are food insecure and cannot meet basic household needs. In response, ACER III will provide food assistance and NFIs using an e-voucher modality that leverages local markets and stimulates the local economy. In CRS community consultations, affected populations have expressed interest in receiving support to restart their livelihoods and generate income so that they do not continue relying on humanitarian assistance. Poultry production was among the most cited and viable livelihoods options considering the context – difficult access to agricultural land and a tradition of a booming livestock sector including egg production. In ACER III, CRS will pilot an intervention to support poultry production among a small subset of the food assistance participants. The poultry pilot intervention includes training, input distribution and creating linkages with the Ministry of Livestock, Fisheries and Animal Industries (MINEPIA) and Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs).
ACER III will continue to focus on two divisions in the NW – Mezam and Momo. ACER III will expand its geographic focus within those divisions where there is demonstrated need and major gaps in humanitarian assistance. ACER III will continue to work in Bamenda I, II and III, Santa, Bafut and Batibo sub-divisions, and will explore the possibility of expanding to Bali and Tubah in Mezam and to Mbengwi in Momo.

1 - Besoins / Needs

In the NW, conflict, displacement, and disrupted livelihoods have affected people throughout the region. IDPs and the households that host them are especially affected. Many IDP households have sold or abandoned household assets or lost them to theft, arson, or destruction. Host households stretch their resources to accommodate IDPs. Many households with the financial means to migrate have travelled to Bafoussam, Douala, and Yaoundé. As humanitarian access has been limited throughout the crisis, thousands of affected individuals remain unserved and in need of assistance. As the FSC noted, due to limited financial resources, the cluster was only able to target 406,000 people out of 960,000 food insecure people in need in the 2021 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP). The project will address Food, NFI and livelihood needs in Mezam and Momo divisions of the Northwest Region. It aims to reach 1,800 HH (10,800 individuals with Food and NFI). A subset of participants 60 HH (360 individuals) will receive poultry assistance through a livelihood pilot.
