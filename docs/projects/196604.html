Project Summary

Since 2016 a socio-political crisis has been underway in the NW-SW regions of Cameroon, causing internal displacement to neighbouring regions. The proposed project will be implemented in the Noun, Menoua and Bamboutos departments, due to the high number of IDPs present in these areas. In fact, an estimated number of 162726 IDPs currently reside in the Western region (UNHCR, Aug 2022); of these, 4301 in Noun, 10887 in Menoua and 21100 in Bamboutos (OCHA, MSNA 2022). Among the most recent population flows NGOs active on the field have reported relevant movements in the Nous and Bamboutos departments.
The overall objective of the project is to contribute to the improvement of the living and health conditions of IDPs and their host communities in the Western region; its specific objective is to ensure improved hygienic conditions and provide access to drinking water for IDPs in the Menoua, Bamboutos and Noun departments.
The majority of the IDP population lives in situations of overcrowding, without adequate shelter and in poor sanitary conditions. Therefore, with a view to improving the living standards of IDPs and limiting the spread of waterborne diseases, access to safe drinking water and basic sanitation services remains a priority in the proposed zones of intervention. In line with the Sector Response Plan, this project aims to reduce mortality and diseases by ensuring safe access to basic WASH services and by strengthening communities’ resilience and capacity. It is designed to respond to a crisis (internal displacement) that is both emergency and chronic, and it does so by paying particular attention to the most vulnerable sections of the population (IDPs, host communities).
The project beneficiaries are the educational communities of 9 secondary schools in the targeted departments, identified on the basis of the high percentage of IDPs and the lack of basic WASH services (drinking water points, gender-sensitive latrines, pedal hand washers). The facilities targeted are broken down as follows: Noun: 2 bilingual high schools and 1 secondary school solely for IDPs (6641 students, of which 3486 IDPs); Bamboutos: 4 bilingual high schools (5271 students, of which 1782 IDPs); Menoua: 2 bilingual high schools (2463 students, of which 885 IDPs).
Based on the needs collected on the field and given the fastly growing percentage of IDPs in the areas of intervention, the project aims to provide access to 9 water points, 9 blocks of gender sensitive latrines (6 latrines per block, for a total of 54 latrines in 9 schools) and to 36 hand washers to 14375 members of the educational communities of the 9 targeted schools. The latter, will receive learning materials, through the distribution of hygiene kits paired with awareness raising materials on WASH issues. Moreover, 9 Committees for the management of the water points composed by 5 persons each, for a total of 45 individuals (parents, teachers, supervisors etc.) will also benefit from training in the mission of school management and school governance.
Lastly, about 30000 members of IDPs and host communities (19990 people in Bamboutos, 7186 in Menoua and 2824 in the Noun) will receive support through awareness-raising sessions on good hygiene and sanitation practices - including Covid-19 prevention - and will be given a family WASH kit. Households, will be identifies in collaboration with local authorities on the basis of pre-established vulnerability criteria including: household size, presence of IDPs, presence of minors or single women heads of household, members with disabilities. Among the community members, 30 community relays will be trained on main WASH good practices and deployed on the field to raise awareness in their communities.

1 - Besoins / Needs

In the West Region, mass displacement has negatively impacted already precarious health and education services, forcing IDPs and their host communities to live in situations of overcrowding and promiscuity, with poor standards of hygiene. In such conditions, the risk of exposure to GBV increases, which calls for a gender-sensitive intervention (for instance, through the construction of gender-sensitive latrines).The primary needs identified are (1) lack of access to drinking water points and adequate sanitation, (2) lack of good hygiene and sanitation practices, which can lead to waterborne diseases.In fact, data shows that in 61,7% of villages assessed in the Menoua department, 77,5% in Noun department and 35,7% in Bamboutos department, nearly no one has access to hand washing facilities (OCHA, MSNA 2022). With regards to latrines, there are considerable access problems: in 60% of villages assessed in the Western region (ibid) latrines are unhygienic, whereas in 55% there are not enough toilets. Lastly, it has been estimated that in Noun 40,8% of the population sources their water supplies from "surface water or open unprotected sources”; numbers rise to 44,3% in Menoua and 76,3% in Bamboutos (ibid). As a result, the cholera epidemics has worsened, especially in the Menoua (WHO report 2022).According to data collected on the field, out of 9 targeted schools only 2 have water points, and they both need rehabilitation; 8 schools have latrines, of which 7 aren’t gender-sensitive, and 6 schools have handwashing facilities but they are insufficient in number or inefficient due to water scarcity. In terms of activities and expected results, the project provides for the following actions and results: Activity 1:1.1: Construction/rehabilitation of 9 water points (boreholes and taps) in 9 bilingual schools; the quality of the water will be certified as drinkable prior to distribution; 1.2: Realisation/rehabilitation of 9 gender-sensitive latrine blocks (6 latrines per block) in target schools to improve general health conditions and limit the spread of diseases in overcrowded schools; gender-differentiation will be ensured to reduce the risk of GBV;1.3: Construction of 4 pedal hand washers at each of the 9 water points to guarantee proper hygiene and to limit the spread of water-borne diseases among students and visitors; Result 1: Improved access to drinking water and sanitation in bilingual schools in the departments of Menoua, Bamboutos and Noun. Activity 2:2.1: Awareness raising sessions within educational institutions on key WASH topics, including Covid-19 awareness; for this activity, ad hoc sensitization material will be produced. In each school a “WASH Club” will be established, with the scope of carrying out peer-to-peer education on good WASH practices. 2.2: Distribution of 9 hygiene kits and creation of "WASH Committees" within schools, composed by students, the educational community and representatives of the Committee for the management of water points. They will monitor the implementation of good WASH practices in their school by means of a "WASH Promotion Plan”.2.3: Training of the "Committees for the management" of the 9 water points; for each water point, a Committee of 5 people, with the necessary skills to manage its maintenance will be instituted and accompanied in the elaboration of a Maintenance Plan, in order to maximize the sustainability of the systems.2.4: Community awareness on good WASH practices and distribution of 3000 WASH kits (1999 in Bamboutos, 738 Menoua and 263 Noun) to vulnerable households (IDPs and host) by 30 trained community relays.Result 2: Good sanitation practices in schools, health centres and within displaced and host communities are promoted.
