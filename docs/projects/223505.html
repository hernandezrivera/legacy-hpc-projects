Project Summary

The 2024 Nigerian Humanitarian Needs Overview projects that 7.9 million people, including 2.3 million internally displaced persons (IDPs), will require urgent humanitarian and protection assistance. Alarmingly, nearly 5.4 million children aged 0-59 months are currently suffering—and are projected to continue suffering—from acute malnutrition through 2025. Among these, 1.8 million children are affected by Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM), as indicated by the weight-for-height z-score (WHZ). The highest GAM prevalence was recorded in Borno (15.7%), followed by Yobe (11.9%) and Adamawa (6.6%), according to the NFSS Round 14 report. To address these alarming nutrition challenges, Pro-Wellness Humanitarian Initiative (PROWI) is proposing a preventive approach to malnutrition, with the aim to improve the overall well-being of children, women, and communities in Gubio, Jere, Machina, and Madagali LGAs of Borno, Yobe, and Adamawa States by addressing the root causes of malnutrition and promoting sustainable practices. This initiative will deliver comprehensive preventive nutrition services to vulnerable populations across these areas over a 12-month period. Key components of this project include: Comprehensive MIYCN Promotion and Support: This will include counseling sessions and the formation of support groups for both mothers and fathers. Skilled IYCF Counseling: Providing expert counseling on Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) to promote optimal feeding practices in the community. Micronutrient Supplementation: Distributing essential vitamins and minerals to prevent nutritional deficiencies. Hygiene Promotion: Educating caregivers on optimal hygiene practices to prevent illness and malnutrition. Backyard Gardening and Cooking Demonstrations: Promoting healthy feeding practices and effective behavioral change communication aimed at optimizing infant and young child nutrition. Through partnerships with primary healthcare agencies, PROWI will train community health workers to deliver sustainable services at the community level. This project seeks to create a robust support network, ensuring that the most vulnerable populations receive the necessary nutrition and care for a healthier future. By addressing the root causes of malnutrition and promoting sustainable practices, PROWI strives to improve the overall well-being of children, women, and communities in these regions.

1 - Needs

The protracted conflict in northeastern Nigeria has led to a significant humanitarian and protection crisis, with an estimated 7.8 million people expected to need assistance in 2025, according to OCHA. Children, particularly girls, and women are the most affected, comprising 83% of those in need. There is an urgent need for protection for women and girls, who often lack access to services, justice, and proper protection. These protection gaps have contributed to an increasing burden of malnutrition among women and children over the past five years. The Integrated Food Security Phase Classification for Acute Malnutrition (2023) reports that at least 206,000 pregnant and lactating women are malnourished in the BAY states, a 16% increase from the previous year. The analysis, which included 133 Local Government Areas (LGAs) across Nigeria, found that more than half (84) of the LGAs were classified in either IPC AMN Phase 3 (Serious) or Phase 4 (Critical). In the northeast, 10 LGAs were in Phase 4 (Critical), and 21 were in Phase 3 (Serious). Key factors contributing to acute malnutrition include poor food consumption, inadequate feeding practices, poor health services, disease prevalence, and low health-seeking behaviors. Economic challenges, food insecurity, limited WASH services, banditry, conflict, population displacement, flooding, and general insecurity further exacerbate the situation by restricting access to vulnerable populations. Nearly 5.4 million children aged 0-59 months are currently suffering and are expected to continue suffering from acute malnutrition through 2025. This includes 1.8 million children with Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). Additionally, 787,000 pregnant and lactating women are acutely malnourished and in need of treatment. The number of acutely malnourished children requiring treatment has increased by 23%, with SAM cases rising by 69% compared to the last IPC AMN analysis in 2023. There has also been a 35% increase in acutely malnourished pregnant and lactating mothers requiring treatment. The projection period of October to December 2024 indicates that acute malnutrition levels in both the northeast and northwest regions will remain largely stable, except for Northern Yobe, where conditions are likely to worsen. During the second projection period (January to April 2025), the situation in the northeast is expected to remain similar, with slight improvements in the northwest. In Borno State, the situation is particularly severe, with 10 LGAs (Maiduguri, Jere, Damboa, Gubio, Kaga, Konduga, Mafa, Magumeri, Marte, and Monguno) classified as Critical (IPC AMN Phase 4), and seven (Bama, Dikwa, Gwoza, Kala/Balge, Ngala, Mobbar, and Nganzai) as Serious (IPC Phase 3). The 2024 Humanitarian Response Plan further indicates that approximately 1.53 million children under the age of five are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition, including around 511,800 who are likely to be severely malnourished. Additionally, it is projected that 207,000 pregnant and breastfeeding women and girls will also experience acute malnutrition throughout the year. Without sustained nutrition assistance, the risks of mortality and lifelong disabilities among survivors may significantly increase. 2 - Activities

Active Case Finding and Screening: Deploy trained community nutrition mobilizers (CNMs) within communities to identify children under five with Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) or Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) using Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) screening. This proactive approach ensures early detection and intervention. Mother/Family MUAC Strategy: Introduce the Mother/Family MUAC strategy to empower caregivers to screen their children at home. This enables timely identification and referral for treatment, increasing the chances of successful intervention and reducing the burden on healthcare facilities. Supplementation: Provide Micronutrient Powders (MNP) to children to improve their nutritional intake and prevent deficiencies. This supplementation is crucial for supporting overall health and development. Maternal, Infant, and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN): Implement a comprehensive MIYCN program to support the nutritional needs of mothers and young children. Skilled IYCF Counseling: Offer expert counseling on Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) for pregnant and lactating women (PLWs). This counseling will promote optimal feeding practices, ensuring that children receive the best possible start in life. Formation of Support Groups: Establish mother-to-mother and father-to-father support groups. These groups will provide a platform for sharing knowledge, experiences, and support, enhancing community engagement and sustained positive health outcomes. Conduct food demonstrations to educate families on nutritious meal preparation, promoting healthier eating habits. Capacity Building and System Strengthening: Train health workers and community mobilizers on Community-based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM), MIYCN, and Family MUAC strategies. This training will ensure high-quality and timely service delivery. Strengthen referral systems to enhance coordination and follow-up of complicated cases, ensuring that all patients receive appropriate care. Collaboration with State Partners: Collaborate closely with the State Primary Health Care Development Agencies (SPHCDA) to align efforts with state health programs. This partnership will help ensure the sustainability and scalability of interventions. Establish micro-gardening initiatives to support food security at the household level, encouraging self-sufficiency and improving access to fresh produce. Establish Community Care Groups (CCG): Form Community Care Groups (CCG) and select Care Group Leaders (CGL) to spearhead community health initiatives. Engage CNMs to conduct routine house-to-house MUAC screenings for Pregnant, Breastfeeding Women and Girls (PBWG) and children aged 6-59 months. Organize quarterly community mass MUAC screenings for PBWG and children aged 6-59 months, referring those identified with acute malnutrition to appropriate care sites. Train caregivers on how to screen their children using color-coded MUAC tapes, and distribute these tapes. Routine and Mass Screenings: Conduct regular MUAC and oedema screenings of children aged 6-59 months and refer identified cases of acute malnutrition to care sites. Perform quarterly community mass MUAC screenings for children aged 6-59 months, ensuring timely referrals for those with acute malnutrition. Train health workers to provide skilled MIYCN counseling for caregivers in the community and within care groups. Train MIYCN promoters, health workers, and CNMs on MIYCN practices, screening for feeding difficulties, and anthropometric screening (Weight-for-Age and Weight-for-Height) of 1-5 month-old infants. Support for Breastfeeding and Nutrition: Screen PBWG and 1-5 month-old infants for breastfeeding difficulties and provide necessary support, including supplementary suckling techniques and wet nursing. Conduct quarterly mass anthropometric screenings of under-6 infants, with routine community screenings by MIYCN promoters

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

To ensure protection mainstreaming, Pro-Wellness Humanitarian Initiative (PROWI), will integrate gender equality throughout its projects. This includes inclusive implementation processes, equitable staff recruitment, and the active participation of both men and women in project structures and evaluations. Women will be highly encouraged to apply for positions, fostering gender equity, community inclusiveness, and sustainability.The project will also mainstream protection through the following modalities:Formation of Women/Mother Support Groups: These groups will empower women to exchange ideas, share experiences, and improve family and child nutrition. They will address gender barriers by equipping women with the knowledge and practical skills to manage nutritional needs effectively.Awareness and Inclusion of Men: Awareness campaigns, focus group discussions, and home visits will engage men, emphasizing their role in supporting household nutrition, exclusive breastfeeding, and nutritious meal preparation.Capacity Building and Collaboration with Government: The project will strengthen the capacity of community nutrition and health workers through training on the effects of gender inequality, power dynamics, and harmful traditional practices. This includes addressing barriers to education, particularly for girls and children with disabilities, and raising awareness about child protection and safeguarding issues affecting boys, girls, and children with disabilities (CWD).PROWI adheres to the principle of "Do No Harm" ensuring that all activities are conducted responsibly to minimize risks and protect beneficiaries through specific measures, such as participatory beneficiary selection, involving community members through implementation, transparently informing communities about the projects aims and activities, and collecting informed consent from participants, while ensuring risk assessment is conducted prior to engagement, with a responsive feedback and complaint mechanism established at the community level to address concerns promptly, mitigate risks, and prevent conflicts.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will establish a complaints and feedback mechanism that utilizes different types of mechanisms to solicit feedback and complaints from different groups (including child-friendly mechanisms). Child-friendly Mechanism will be used through listening to children, young people, and communities using simple language and illustrations to communicate key messages about the feedback mechanism. PROWI has a toll-free line for Complaints and Feedback which will be communicated to beneficiaries. This is to ensure the confidentiality of the complaints and feedback. The project participants will be sensitized on the channel of providing feedback. PROWI will also develop a feedback register that will be used at the facilities to track feedback received by the project participants on the services rendered in the facility and suggested areas of improvement. This feedback will be used to strengthen the quality of services delivered. comprehensive orientation to all project staff on the principle of accountability and feedback/complaints handling and code of conduct.
