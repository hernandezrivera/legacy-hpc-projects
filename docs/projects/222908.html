Project Summary

The project will ensure accessibility for persons with disabilities to various service points in the Rohingya camps as well as in host communities. This will be achieved by installing accessibility features at targeted community facilities and household levels to enhance their participation. Additionally, different types of assistive devices will be provided to improve the independence of persons with disabilities. Persons with disabilities will be meaningfully engaged at the beginning of the project. They will form community monitoring groups to monitor the outcomes of the project initiatives. These groups will identify areas for improvement to ensure the project positively impacts participants' independence and overall well-being. The project will undertake initiatives to enhance the capacity of various stakeholders, including SCCCM actors and other sectors, to equip them with the necessary skills and knowledge to install accessible facilities.
The overall objective of this project is to Improve the well-being of people with disabilities through accessible and inclusive healthcare services to the Rohingya and the host community of Cox's Bazar district
The project will be implemented in camps 8W, 8E, 11, 13, 18, and 19, as well as in the Rajapalong, Ratanapalong, and Holdiapalong Unions within the host community. No other agencies provide the same or similar services in the proposed project locations.
The project will carry out the following activities:
• Home and community accessibility modifications: The project will install accessibility features for persons with disabilities household considering the individual requirements to improve their independence.
• Accessibility at the community facilities: to create better access for people with disabilities to the services.
• Provision of Assistive devices: to enhance the independence & participation of the person with disabilities, different types of assistive devices will be provided based on individual clinical assessment.
• Capacity development for the different accessibility & creating inclusive environment.
• Forming community monitoring groups of persons with disabilities.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Accessibility remains a significant challenge for persons with disabilities within the Rohingya camps. This is primarily due to the uneven terrain and the lack of inclusivity in the design of shelters. These barriers limit the participation of persons with disabilities and restrict their access to various services. Moreover, the lack of accessibility increases their dependency on family members or others for daily living activities.According to the 2023 Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (J-MSNA), 5% of Rohingya and 2% of host community individuals with disabilities experienced challenges in accessing different services due to their impairments. This is primarily because the specific treatment or service needed (Rehab) is not available, the facility is too far away and the facility is too far away and inaccessible of the facilities. This report also revealed that 8%host and 15%of refugee households with at least one member with a disability. The Age and Disability Inclusion Needs Assessment (ADINA) published by REACH in May 2021 which involved 2,530 household interviews found the following:- A significant proportion of persons with disabilities face mobility-related barriers, with 52% experiencing challenges inside shelters and 76% around camps.- The inaccessibility of meeting venues created a barrier for persons with disabilities to participate in community meetings and events.- Physical accessibility of WASH facilities creates barriers for persons with disabilities to use them independently.-Most of the service facilities are inaccessible for the persons with disabilities which restricting them from accessing the services. CBM and CDD also found similar gaps in the mobility of persons with disabilities and older people through community consultations. Considering the above-mentioned needs and gaps, CBMG and CDD designed the proposed project. Under this initiative, CBMG and CDD will install various accessibility features at both the household level and in service facilities, including health centers. Additionally, CBMG and CDD will repair pathways and construct stairs in different locations within the proposed working areas to ensure safe and easy access for persons with disabilities and older people.

2.3 - Age & Disability

The project mainly focused on ensuring better accessibility for the persons with disabilities & older people at the different facilities and house level. Based on the findings of the “Bangladesh: Age and Disability Inclusion Needs Assessment - Rohingya Refugee Response (May 2021)” CBMG & CDD designed the project. CBMG & CDD will conduct a screening process to identify the project participant by using the Washington Group Short Set of Questions Enhanced (WGSS-E). A comprehensive GEDSI action plan will be developed in the inception phase. Across all outcomes, WGSS-E will be used to identify persons with disabilities. The persons with disabilities will be involve at all stages of the project cycle from the beginning. During the project design phase, CDD consulted with members of the Self-Help Group and Disability Support Committees of persons with disabilities to gather their opinions and insights about the current needs. Furthermore, the proposed interventions were selected based on recommendations and opinions from project participants who have disabilities. We collected information through an inclusive feedback and response mechanism. Persons with disabilities, especially SHG/DSC members, will be actively engaged throughout the project cycle. They will be also engage in the monitoring system as the project will form community monitoring group to monitor the project activities & it’s outcome. Additionally, persons with disabilities will be also engaged in project mid-year & Endline evaluation. The project will prioritize the disability movement through various interventions, involving SHG/DSC members on different platforms to advocate for the rights of persons with disabilities. The project will also improve the capacity of persons with disabilities in the camp and host community to improve the active participation of persons with disabilities in the humanitarian response through trainings

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Project beneficiaries will be selected through a transparent and inclusive process aligned with Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) commitments. This will include community consultations, prioritizing the most vulnerable groups such as persons with disabilities, older people, and those with limited accessibility. During the community consultation the selection criteria’s will be inform to the community people. CBMG-CDD will ensure accountability to affected populations by using already functional inclusive complaints, feedback, and response mechanisms that proactively capture systematic feedback from beneficiaries with disabilities and set strategies to remove barriers accordingly. The iFRM will follow the following five steps. Step 1: Embed inclusive feedback & and reporting (iFRM) mechanism in all new projects.Step 2: Analyze the community profile and local context.Step 3: Consult with communities.Step 4: Decide on the most appropriate feedback and reporting channels.Step 5: Develop a plan of action for handling feedback and training staff. CBMG and CDD will form a community monitoring group comprising persons with disabilities. Additionally, CDD will share the project evaluation findings with the project participants. Any changes to the project plan will be discussed with the community, and their opinions and suggestions will be taken into consideration.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, the project incorporates Climate Action by addressing Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), Disaster Risk Management (DRM), and anticipatory actions. It strengthens Disability Support Committees (DSCs) and Self-Help Groups (SHGs) to promote resilience among persons with disabilities.It achieves this by enhancing their capacity for advocacy, resource mobilization, and community engagement, enabling them to address challenges effectively and promote inclusive development. Key actions include: Inclusive DRR and DRM training for DSC and SHG members, with accessible emergency preparedness plans.Anticipatory actions such as early warning systems accessible to persons with disabilities.Climate adaptation through sustainable practices, reducing vulnerabilities, and building community resilience.These actions ensure that climate response efforts are inclusive and protective of persons with disabilities.The materials of the construction & home modification will be environment friendly.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project includes Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) measures. It incorporates:• Emergency Response Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) tailored to ensure inclusive response actions, with specific protocols for assisting people with disabilities.• Contingency Funds are allocated for rapid mobilization of resources to meet urgent needs.These measures enhance the project’s ability to respond quickly and effectively to emergencies, ensuring the protection and inclusion of vulnerable community members.

Yes, the project promotes self-reliance and resilience for refugees by empowering Disability Support Committees (DSCs) and creating skill-building opportunities for people with disabilities through the referral linkage.
