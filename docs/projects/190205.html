Project Summary

Ethiopia continues to face severe humanitarian crises as needs emanating from conflict, climatic shocks, disease outbreaks, desert locust and the adverse effects of the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic continue to grow. Conflict has remained the main driver of humanitarian needs in the country in 2021. According to Ethiopian Humanitarian Needs Overview 2021, more than 23 million people nationwide were projected to have humanitarian needs in 2021, a majority of whom were in Oromia (6.7 million); followed by Somali (3.8 million). One of the biggest impacts from drought shocks in Ethiopia is population displacement. As the agriculture dashboard (published in October 2021) shows, only 1.4 million peoples have been reached so far, which is only 15% of the cluster target (9 million) to enhance emergency food production. Out of 12.9 million people, who require urgent action to save lives, reduce food gaps, restore livelihoods and reduce malnutrition, 15% in Somali. The Somali region reached only 105,000 (6.9%) people with a target of 1.53 million in the agriculture sector. Tuliguled woreda of Fafen zone and Kelafo and Gode woreda of Shebele zone in Somali region is highly affected by drought and several households are displaced and many households lose their assets and become malnourished.
Therefore, OWDA proposes an eight-month emergency project which will target to assist 9000 drought and conflict affected IDPs and host community households (1782 IDP households and 7218 host community households) in three woredas. 1952 drought affected households in Gode, 3021 drought affected households in Kelafo and 4027 drought affected households in Tuliguled will be assisted in this intervention. The project will address 3600 women households, 1800 elderly women households, 2700 men households and 900 elderly men households are assisted in the project. Protection, gender and disability will be mainstreamed in the project and 630 households with disability people will be assisted. The project will assist the affected people through provision of animal feeds, animal treatment and vaccination, provision of agricultural inputs for crop production, provision of climate resilient and drought tolerant forage seeds and promotion of income generating activities to make the affected people more resilient for natural and man-made disasters. In addition, targeted host and IDP communities will get capacity strengthening trainings to improve agricultural production and productivity as well enhance resilient capacity of the people.
This project will be given high emphasis to mainstreaming gender and protection issues. Distinct needs and realities of women, girls, men, and boys are integrated in a meaningful way during the monitoring and project intervention. During process monitoring, separate focus group discussions with women, men, children, older persons, and persons with disabilities will be conducted and the needs of girls, boys, men, women, and persons with disabilities will be known that the needs and priorities of both men, women, girls and boys addressed or not. The monitoring assistants will also work to report on access to assistance by both men and women and will provide data dis-aggregated by sex and age. Mechanisms will be established for beneficiaries to provide feedback/complaints and address concerns. Child protection and safety and security of women will be ensured through awareness on potential safety concerns before, during and after implementation of the project in close collaboration and consultation with Government and the community. Under the lens of Do No Harm, OWDA will aim to mitigate the potential negative impacts of the intervention on vulnerable groups. OWDA’s staff shall adhere to the organizational Code of Conduct and Child Protection policies which have been developed to ensure the maximum protection of program participants from exploitation, and to clarify the responsibilities of OWDA stakeholders.
