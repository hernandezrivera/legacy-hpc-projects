Project Summary

Mission Trust Aid will implement this project in an integrated way and in coordination with relevant clusters, recognizing that schools are platforms through which WASH, nutrition, hygiene, health and psycho-social issues can be addressed and provided to 4799 learners, 80 teachers, 20 PTA/SMCs in the three counties of Fangak, Pochalla and Uror in Jonglei state. There will be establishment of 6 temporary learning spaces to create conducive and protective learning environment for the learners. 8(20 female 60male) teachers will be trained on education pedagogy. To improve learning, this project aims to distribution of 13 kits of learning and recreational materials, 30 Early Child Development kits. With the current gaps of sanitation facilities in schools, MTA plans to construction or rehabilitate 12 latrines which are gender sensitive while taking into consideration the needs for children with disability to create a conducive learning environment, MTA will also access some add-ons slabs from WASH Cluster and or UNICEF to provide disability friendly latrine stance within the school. these DFL will be positioned in an accessible location for the ability disadvantaged children within the targeted schools. All Latrines constructed will be equipped with hand washing stations and joint establishment of school hygiene/peace clubs to improve children and environmental hygiene. This Actions are in line with the Education Cluster CO1: Emergency-affected children and youth have access to safe, protective, inclusive education services that are appropriate to their age, learning level and need, CO2: Children and youth are provided with quality teaching and learning services that support retention and progress through the education cycle, CO3: Coordination and crisis management capacity is strengthened to promote efficient, accountable, and timely education in emergencies response, MTA will also ensure WASH integration within the schools as well as its’ Nutrition programs to harvest short/mid and long term impact in proposed integrated Education, WASH, Nutrition and FSL aimed at improving learning services, reducing needs of learning spaces for children, facilitating access to learning materials, to vulnerable children in Jonglei State (Fangak, Duk, Pochalla counties) South Sudan. Internally Displaced People (IDPs), host communities and returnees in these proposed hard-to-reach locations will be provided with education and WASH services. The intervention will work with stakeholders’ in schools, including the County Education Departments (CED), as well as functional community structures (PTAs, SMCs, Boma and Payam leaders, etc.), focusing on the development of skills on the management of learning infrastructures.

1. Needs

According to HNO 2023, Multiple crisis drivers continue to have a negative impact on education services, disrupting access to education and learning. The development and reach of South Sudan’s national education system has been constrained by chronic crisis conditions over the last decade. Most recently, the Covid-19 pandemic has had a significant impact on education services – schools in South Sudan were closed for over one year. The closure of schools due to COVID-19 and the broader socio-economic hardship continue to exacerbate the existing education challenges and inequities. Since 2021, an estimated 2.8 million children, almost 65 per cent of all school-aged children, are believed to be out of school. Dropout has been higher for boys during the last three years, while more girls tend to drop out after completing their primary education. The enrolment rate is almost four times higher for the primary level of education than for the secondary level (62.9 per cent and 13.4 per cent respectively). Transition from primary to secondary education is significantly low and most children do not graduate from high school. Approximately 150,434 refugee children, of whom 51 per cent are girls, need support to access quality education. An estimated 3.3 million girls and boys between ages 3 and 17 in conflict- and crisis-affected areas are estimated to have inadequate or no access to education in 2023. More than 43,000 teachers (13 per cent female) and members of school management committees require humanitarian assistance. Some 150,434 refugee children will need education support in 2023. Increasing effects of climate change, food insecurity and localized conflict exacerbate existing barriers, including inadequate school infrastructure, costs associated with accessing quality education, untrained education personnel and irregular and insufficient teacher payments. Needs are particularly dire and may worsen in Unity, Jonglei, Upper Nile and Warrap states.Schools are commonly used as evacuation centres and safe spaces for the displaced population in Upper Nile, Jonglei, Warrap, Unity States and Greater Pibor and the Abyei Administrative Area. Active conflict and recurring communal violence throughout 2022 up to date led to school closures and affected the education of 1,160 pre-primary and 2,882 primary school children. Some 887 schools were affected by flooding in Jonglei, Warrap, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Unity and Upper Nile states and disrupted education services. Food insecurity is another key factor leading parents with school-aged children to adopt negative coping mechanisms, such as child marriage, child labour and even recruitment into armed groups or gangs that prevent school enrolment and attendance. Uror and Fangak counties are rated as counties in emergency (IPC 4). Most students do not get a meal before school and over 60 per cent of the schools assessed for ENA do not provide food for students. Hunger is therefore a key barrier to education, resulting in poor academic performance, and stunting cognitive development and children’s overall well-being. This is a key causal factor that underpins school dropout. The lack of access to safe drinking water and sex-segregated latrines is a critical barrier to a safe and quality learning environment. An estimated 17,030 classrooms are operating under trees or in open air, exposing children to protection risks and harsh weather conditions. Almost 63 per cent of primary schools are without any latrines and 52 per cent have either limited or no access to safe drinking water. According to the Education Needs Assessment (ENA), more than 60 per cent of these latrines are inaccessible to students with disabilities. Overcrowded classrooms resulting in unhygienic conditions and the lack of essential teaching and learning materials are some of the key barriers to a quality learning experience, leading to more dropouts.

2. Project activities

These are also in line with the South Sudan Education Cluster strategy 2023-20251. Provide temporary learning spaces (TLS), semi-permanent structures that are safe, inclusive and gender sensitive.2. Provide gender-segregated and accessible WASH facilities (latrines, hand-washing facilities, girls' change rooms) for girls and boys3. Community mobilization targeting out of school children and youth to improve access to education4. Enrolment of boys and girls in ECD and primary education 5. Distribute sanitation/hygiene kits for boys and girls in schools to prevent communicable diseases and dignity kits for girls 7. Develop and implement child safeguarding policies, staff and teacher Codes of Conduct, reporting mechanisms and GBV risk mitigation.8. Train teachers, SMCs, PTAs and learners on referral mechanism9. Implement school gardening programs 10. Provide teacher training on psychosocial support (PSS)11. Provide teacher training on identification of children at risk and on referral mechanisms12. Provide peace building and social cohesion programmes in schools13. Provide teaching and learning materials for learners and teachers14. Conduct EiE related teacher training and continuous professional development15. Support development of national and state-level EiE preparedness and response plans16. Train School Management Committees (SMC), Parent Teacher Associations (PTA), and education authorities on school management, governance, climate change and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR)17. Establish child-friendly, accessible complaints and feedback mechanisms in schools
