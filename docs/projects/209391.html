Project Summary

The project aims at ensuring vulnerable children, especially girls, live in resilient communities and have free and safe access to life-saving services during and after natural disasters and conflicts, this will be achieved through integrated Multi-Sectoral project targeting 60,000 (14112 men, 1528 women, 14688 boys, 15912 girls) refugees and 28,739 (4439 men, 42287 women, 26278 boys, 27984 girls) refugees & host communities in White Nile, Khartoum, Gadaref and Aj Jazeera States through ; Food Security and livelihoods (FSL); Education; Child Protection (CP); Health, and Nutrition interventions.
The project will provide safe learning spaces and education supplies to decrease financial pressure on households to improve school retention rates. Provision of emergency education supplies, safe gender responsive semi-permanent learning facilities and life-saving hygiene messages are included in schools. The project improves quality of education through teacher and PTA capacity building to respond to gender specific emergency education, protection and safeguarding needs.
To ensure quality learning trainings target teachers on core subjects, PTAs on school management, mother committee on context specific gender issues, hygiene. Awareness will be provided to girls and boys on child rights. Children with special needs will be prioritized through integrated and inclusive learning environments. Children and parents will be oriented on how to access information on child protection referrals, provision of feedback.
Child Protection will be incorporated across all sector interventions to promote meaningful access, safety and dignity. The approach will be based on Protection Principles as per the Humanitarian Charter and Minimum Standards of Child Protection in Humanitarian Action (CPMS) including avoiding causing harm by preventing and minimizing any unintended negative effects.
The project will also contribute to strengthening the health and nutrition services in the targeted locations through the provision of life-saving health and nutrition intervention by supporting 4 PHC / 4 OTPs / 4 TSFPs integrated (2 in White Nile and 2 in Gadaref) and 2 OTPs / 2 TSFPs alone Aj Jazeera. The proposed programming will support 48,576 direct beneficiaries including 5,912 girls, 5,694 boys, 28,789 women, 8,181 men for health services, while proposed programming will support 11,369 direct beneficiaries including 3,715 girls, 3,620 boys, 4,034 PLW for nutrition services.
To address severe food insecurity and malnutrition status in the said States, the project will support the implementation of an integrated EFSL intervention in above mentioned localities. The key project intervention will aim at increasing household food security and improving access to alternative household income therefore he project will address the food security and livelihoods of the targeted population through provision of productive agriculture and livestock inputs (seeds, tools, animal vaccination and treatment), cash grants for income generation activities and skill development through training. The project is designed to come out with increased agricultural production, increased and diversified household income that improves the food security and livelihoods of targeted beneficiaries.
The consortium will ensure Accountability to Affected Persons (AAP) through a methodical consultation, planning and joint design of the component of accountability in line with the humanitarian standards. Line ministries, target communities men and women, as well as girls and boys will be actively involved

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Plan International maintains high standards of Safeguarding and PSHEA (preventingsexual harassment, exploitation and abuse), more-so in emergency situations. Plan’sSafeguarding Policy and procedures are implemented during the design andimplementation of project interventions with additional vigilance and we adapt andstrengthen existing interventions to identify and reduce risks to girls, boys, children,young people and beneficiaries. Safeguarding and PSHEA risk analysis andassessment of project interventions will be conducted with mitigationmeasures/contingency plans identified for each risk. Plan will work to ensure staff,partners, and beneficiaries and communities are aware of the safeguarding policy, itsminimum codes of behavior, and requirements to uphold high standards ofprofessional behavior. The implementing partners were assessed to ensure theadequacy of the partner’s safeguarding and PSHEA policies and guidelines. Mitigationstrategies take account of particular risks or vulnerabilities associated with girls, boys,children and young people. Children and young people are respected and their safetyand protection is not compromised when obtaining and using information on andimages of them.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Plan International will ensure accountability to affected people, as well as the donor,local partner, relevant government stakeholders. The project will involve the targetcommunity members, where appropriate working through existing communitystructures whilst ensuring representation of women, youth, people with disabilities,elderly and the related CBOs, in the project cycle management. Target groups andother related project stakeholders will be fully oriented on the project objectives anddeliverables using safe methods and forums such messages to ensure that, a senseof ownership is created among target communities and increased accountabilitytowards the beneficiaries targeted by the project. Plan International Sudan has a childfriendly Community Feedback & Response Mechanism (CFRM) system rolled out atstate level in all its locations. This CFRM are in line with the Core HumanitarianStandards and will enable community members to provide their feedback, concernsand reect their opinions on the project performance in a safe and confidential manner.The accountability systems have multiple entry points, designed to be accessible foryoung people, women, PWDs, and other vulnerable groups through introduction ofdifferent approaches in collaboration with the communities. Entry points will includehotline/Sim numbers, WhatsApp and boxes as safe entry points to allow targetbeneficiaries to share their feedback/complaints. Beneficiaries are oriented andencouraged to use the system, which established in each target community. Thereceived feedbacks/complaints are analyzed and addressed following the internalprocedures that are aligned to the Common Humanitarian standards.Feedback/complaints concerning other project stakeholders (government staff,partners) will be referred to their senior management as appropriate where concernedorganization can take the necessary actions. Plan will also seek through safemethods through community volunteers and CBOs members to allow children to their opinions and feedback about the project as well as using logbooks at schoolsthat target beneficiaries can register feedbacks. Externally Plan is a member of theAAP/PSEA network and mechanisms and interacts with UNCT/UNOPS call centersand AAP focal points in other organizations that is set up by the humanitarian actorsin Sudan. Plan will contribute to enhancing technical standards and improving AAPand PSEA mechanisms and approaches based on lessons learned and experiencefrom Plan globally, has strong internal Safeguarding policies, standards, proceduresas well as robust training and capacity building manuals that can enhance AAP andPSEA roll out, reporting and responding. Plan will also ensure to participate in needs,information and assessments on AAP and PSEA as initiated by the AAP network asappropriate. Plan will commit to adhere to implement the AAP and PSEA in its designand delivery of projects and reporting requirements.
