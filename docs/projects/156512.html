Project Summary

The proposed intervention will provide assistance to a total of 5500 (3600 women, 1500 girls, 200 men and 200 boys) conflict-affected populations across Syria. WATAN has designed the intervention to respond to the direct costs and impacts of conflict and to shore up existing capacity among the civilian population in order to prevent a further decline in sustainable development outcomes. WATAN intends to provide support to the most vulnerable groups in particular female-headed households, women and girls victims of Gender-Based Violence (GBV) which continues to be pervasive in the lives of women and girls. The aim is to establish WGSS in the targeted areas to create as a safe environment where women can interact and socialize with women coming from similar backgrounds, thereby allowing the center serve the psychosocial needs of the women and helping the women and girls, including the GBV survivors, in their coping mechanism.

Needs

Across Syria, the risk of gender based violence for women and girls in particular has increased dramatically due to the armed conflict and the displacement, the subsequent breakdown of law and the erosion of social structures and norms, as well as limited access to basic formal and informal protection mechanisms. As described in the HNO, reports of sexual harassment, early/forced marriage and domestic violence (including intimate partner violence) continue to affect the lives of women and girls inside and outside the house. In addition, the changes in traditional gender roles and responsibilities within the families, due to the displacement and the current living conditions, resulted often in an increasing of stress and tensions within the families leading to an increase of GBV episodes. Although all women and girls are vulnerable to GBV in the public and private sphere, the assessments found that adolescent girls are perceived to be at particular risk of sexual violence, and divorcees and widows face stigmatization and discrimination, potentially threatening their access to humanitarian aid. This reflects the need to provide specialized GBV services including case management, focused and non focused psycho-social activities and awareness raising sessions on GBV related topics, mitigating and addressing protection risks, ensuring a protective environment and empowering communities and individuals on how to address protection concerns.

Implementation of activities

1-Providing GBV specialized services to including case management and Psychosocial Support services: the project will provide individual support, including specialized GBV case management to women and girls at risk and GBV survivors through trained case workers, the establishment of GBV referral pathways and community mobilization to promote access to the services. Thorough follow-up and ongoing support on strengthening sustainability of the case management services and long-term improvement of survivors’ coping mechanisms, the individualized care plans are designed to include transition strategies that encourage GBV survivors, vulnerable women and girls to develop the capacity for self-care and foster improved access to health and social services within their communities. The survivor centered approach will guide the service provision of individual support and case management to allow survivors to access the information they need to make informed decisions and will be based on consent at each stage in the process. In particular, the consent and confidentiality, non-discrimination, dignity, safety, and empowerment of the survivor will be prioritized. 2- Provide activities empowering women and girls and GBV prevention activities: through enhancing strategies to empower women and girls and prevent GBV, with a particular focus on adolescent girls. WATAN will conduct psychosocial support groups activities, women empowerment sessions and adolescent girls’ self care and self protection session. WATAN aims to establish women’s and girls’ safe spaces (WGSS) in the targeted areas to deliver skills building, psychosocial and awareness activities, in addition to recreational activities, community events and celebrations. The skills building activities will include hairdressing, recycling, handicraft, language courses. They aim at bringing women together, improving social connectedness, empowering women through teaching them new skills, and ultimately facilitating the access of women to potential livelihood opportunities. The activities and awareness sessions in the safe spaces will be identified and altered, based on the changing concerns and needs of the women in the field. The awareness sessions will empower women by providing them information on various subjects including women’s health, positive parenting, early marriage, and gender-based violence. 3-Increase the capacity of GBV actors to respond and prevent GBV through strategic and structured approaches: The project gives considerable weight to capacity building of service providers, stakeholders and other local organizations into the GBV-related trainings, covering subjects such as GBV guiding principles, referral mechanisms, and early marriage, GBV prevention mainstreaming. By providing these trainings, WATAN aims to enable service providers in various sectors to identify GBV survivors, and thereby strengthen the referral mechanism between different sectors. 4-Provide livelihood opportunities to empower women and girls and strengthen their resilience: Economic empowerment of women is one of the essential parts of supporting GBV survivors in resilience. Thus, in addition to the psycho-social support and case management services, the project also proposes the integration of a livelihood component. In this regard, the women who are specifically successful in the skill-building activities will be supported by in-kind aid. The selection will be made based on vulnerability, level of participation, level of productivity, quality of work, to be judged by the activity facilitators. Women will be provided with equipment to continue practicing their skill for livelihood purposes as professionals. Monitoring of activities will be carried out by WATAN to assess progress towards the attainment of project objectives and ensure to include appropriate data collection tools, databases, monitoring checklist, data analysis and reporting mechanism to measure project progress towards targets

Protection Risk Analysis

Some risks that may affect activities implementation is the lack of awareness about GBV importance among non-specialized staff within the organization and other service providers which increase the need to have sensitization sessions and GBV mainstreaming trainings for non-specialized staff on GBV to ensure safe referrals within the organization and to other external service providers. Also, GBV services are not usually prioritized in any emergency response during the need assessment and this makes identifying women’s needs more difficult to address especially for the staff who involved in the assessment without GBV or protection background. WATAN will ensure to continuously build the capacity of the staff using the technical resources to avoid any harm on beneficiaries through providing response and prevention services and loss of capacity and skills. The lack of quality services and coordination among service providers in regards to GBV services may lead to fewer disclosure by GBV survivors and decrease the entry points in the community to reach more vulnerable women and girls.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The security risks in the Syrian context are unavoidable and the intervention have been designed with careful consideration of the complex operating environment. In light of risks, the scale and scope of program activities remains flexible and adjusted to the context. To ensure women and girls have safe access to the WGSS through suitable selection of the location to be accessible for all women in the targeted areas to prevent further risks. Also, the risk of having our WGSS attacked due to the security situation and this requires including community engagement throughout the whole project cycle including community leaders and other stakeholders and also uses low visibility to make sure that beneficiaries are safe. Lack of funding is considered to be a risk for the project, so WATAN will have coordination and collaboration with different actors to secure fund and continue the project. Remote management of the project activities is still challenging, WATAN will make sure to find the qualified staff and have a capacity building plan to ensure capable staff to deal with GBV survivors, women and girls.
