Project Summary

In Borno State, structural needs are compounded by the needs triggered by the COVID crisis, leading to an increased vulnerability of the conflict-affected people. The State lacks adequate water supply infrastructure and the quantity of water p/d is below the 15L SPHERE standards necessary to cover basic water needs in areas within Monguno and Dikwa LGA. COVID19 transmission amongst these highly vulnerable will ramp up the transmission risks and threaten the continuity of the already challenging humanitarian response. In addition, most people within the State are not aware of the handwashing and other key behaviours to protect themselves from COVID-19.
Therefore, SI is proposing an intervention that will contribute to contain the spread of the COVID 19 pandemic while decreasing morbidity and mortality amongst affected populations (global humanitarian response plan COVID 19 – Strategic Priority). For this purpose, SI will improve knowledge on covid 19 prevention and spread, and ensure that people are able to implement IPC measures within camps and host communities. Learning from the successful management of the last cholera outbreak, SI will continue to play a key role in WASH/COVID coordination.
Under the proposed project, SI will prepare and respond to the COVID-19 outbreak in its areas of operation, namely Maiduguri, Dikwa, Ngala and Monguno. The project is built on different strategic objectives as follows:
o Provision of items necessary to implement IPC measures: SI will distribute COVID/hygiene kits to the most-vulnerable/affected people and in pre-identified hotspots. Latrine maintenance kits to disinfect this infrastructure will be distributed to already established sanitation committees.
o Increased safe water: through reticulation of water points, chlorination of water points and water trucking; in community and health facilities. SI will also conduct regular water quality tests.
o Increased knowledge on COVID 19 prevention and spread: Communication and mass awareness raising campaigns will be carried out with strong community involvement; including production and dissemination of IEC materials on COVID-19. The strategies that will be implemented include door to door hygiene messaging, dialogue with religious and traditional rulers as advocate and champions of COVID 19 preventive message dissemination and use of megaphones to spread the messages widely in camps.
o Promote and sustain healthy environment to prevent the survival of the virus: Hand washing stations will be set up at strategic points like markets, latrines in Monguno, Dikwa, Maiduguri and Ngala. SI will disinfect wash infrastructures and markets on a regular basis.
o Efficient coordination and information management
In addition, SI as lead of the Cholera/COVID TWG will strongly contribute to the efficiency of the COVID coordination in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa States.

1 - Needs

Northeast Nigeria is characterised by conflict, mass population displacement, malnutrition, and heavy burdens of malaria, diarrheal and respiratory disease. In 2020, 7.9 million people are forecasted to need humanitarian assistance, 11% increase from 2019 resulting mainly from rising violence and insecurity. However, these projections were made before the COVID-19 crisis and this figure is likely to be multiplied and reached unprecedented levels. The epicentre of this protracted crisis remains Borno State with over 80% of the total IDPs forces to share the already stretched resources with host communities. The Borno state PHEOC risk assessment showed that MMC is very high risk LGA, while, Ngala and Monguno are high risk regarding COVID-19 outbreak. The emergence of COVID19 (Feb 2020) in Nigeria presents a direct threat to people and to the delivery of the essential WASH services upon which they depend. Malnutrition, deteriorated healthcare facilities as well as poor access to clean water and basic sanitation are common problems in Borno State, resulting in high rates of disease and death; which are further compounded by outbreaks during and post the rainy season. According to SI’s surveys, most people in the 4 assessed LGAs are not aware of the handwashing and other key behaviours to protect themselves from COVID-19. The state is lacking water supply infrastructure and the quantity of water p/d is below the 15L SPHERE standards necessary to cover basic water needs in some areas. Indeed, the DTM round 28 conducted in November 2019 showed that 1% of the people in camps in Borno state have access to less that 5l/p/d of water, 11.1% between 5-10l/p/d and 64% between 10-15l/p/d. The problem raised is twofold: an increased concentration of people waiting for collecting water and an insufficient quantity of water per person and per day, thus hampering the implementation of recommended hygiene and safety measures for the COVID19 prevention. Regarding sanitation, only 3% of the people in camps have good and hygienic toilets and 97% have unhygienic toilets. These conditions predispose the communities to water borne diseases and have made cholera endemic in BAY states. The most vulnerable population groups are children, women, elderly, disabled people and people with chronic medical conditions. The biggest fear is the effects of COVID-19 on their decreased immunity associated with diseases such as malnutrition, measles, malaria, lassa fever and cholera. The risk of COVID19 transmission amongst these vulnerable population is high and threaten the continuity of the already challenging humanitarian response. Myths and stigmatization are already becoming an issue and will probably hinder the humanitarian response. Solidarités International leads the Cholera/COVID-19 TWG which coordinates COVID-19 preparedness and response in BAY states. Enhanced coordination of cholera preparedness and response was crucial in the control and timely response to outbreaks in 2019 and best practices will be used for COVID-19. Targeting hot spots, partner mapping, compilation of available contingency stock/need, development of LGA/state preparedness and response plans, training on SOPs and updating of outbreak response monitoring tools will be conducted to help mitigate the impact and spread of the virus. This plan will include activities that strengthen partner and sector preparedness for COVID-19 response in order to mitigate the impact of disease in the BAY states in 2020.

2 - Activities

SI specific COVID-19 preparedness and response activities are as follows: - Distribution of COVID-19/hygiene kits (including disinfectant and reusable mask) – designed as per Sector guidance. SI will distribute these kits as a response measure to infected households and as a preventive measure to the most-vulnerable/affected people and in pre-identified hotspots. Capacity to distribute 12,500 kits, consumables will be refilled after 3 months. One refill planned under this project. - Distribution of latrine maintenance kits to already established sanitation committees so that they can disinfect the latrines, leading to a rapid die-off of the virus. Refresh training/Sensitization will be done with a focus on how to maintain and clean a latrine in the context of a COVID epidemic. Capacity to distribute 1,600 kits. - Increased water quality and quantity in congested areas of the 4 targeted LGAs – in camps, host communities and health facilities – to meet the sphere standard of 15l/p/d through reticulation of boreholes (8), water trucking and water point chlorination (40). - SI’s sensitization strategy is in compliance with the health instructions of the States and the WHO (social distancing and quarantine if symptoms in particular). Prior to starting, SI will conduct a survey to understand the risk perceptions, behaviours, existing barriers, myths, specific needs, and key audiences; and to define the messaging. Actions will also be undertaken to counter stigmatizing attitudes: (I) deconstruct and correct misconceptions/myths, rumors and misinformation (II) involve key influencers (bulema, religious leader,...). SI will use a community-based approach with Community Hygiene Promoters (CHPs) to foster acceptance and ensure better understanding of the messages. The strategy will be multichannel organized around mass media campaigns to avoid gathering of population and door-to-door sensitization. In camps, public address systems will be prioritized but roving teams with megaphones/hailers might be considered. Sensitization will be coupled with soaps distributions: 122,850 individuals expected to be reached by HP activities will receive 2 soaps. Handwashing stations (800) will also be set-up in all strategic locations (markets, institutions, schools, entry points, camps and crowded areas). - Disinfection of wash infrastructures and public spaces such as market, on a regular basis. - In coordination with Health actors, SI will provide training to health workers on Wash/IPC measures. The Cholera/COVID TWG led by SI will carry out the following activities for the three states in Northern Nigeria as part of its preparedness plan: - Mapping of partners’ funding capacity for COVID response - Compilation of available contingency stock from partners for response - Development and Update of reporting and monitoring tools - Printing of water chlorination, household disinfection and hygiene promotion SOPs in collaboration with the Hygiene promotion TWG - Preparation and dissemination of dashboard for partners reporting - Participation in all PHEOC meetings to get update on the evolution of the COVID pandemic in the state so as to guide intense response

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

SI strives to include all stakeholders throughout the project implementation. Joint planning and review meetings will be held between the project staff and relevant stakeholders including the beneficiaries to capture lessons learnt for better programming. During assessment and monitoring phases, beneficiaries will be interviewed (mainly through household surveys during covid outbreak) and will thus be able to influence the design and implementation of the project. SI will also involve communities via staff hiring (Daily Workers, CHPs), so that they can benefit from the project interventions, not only as beneficiaries but also actively supporting implementation of activity. SI will mainstream Core Humanitarian Standards to ensure quality and effectiveness of assistance and demonstrate greater accountability to affected populations. Throughout the project, SI will strive to respect the five Accountability to Affected Population commitments, as defined by the IASC. The project beneficiaries are systematically included in the decision-making process at each and every step of project cycle. SI is committed to ensuring beneficiaries’ involvement to ensure the project fits their expectations and needs and to avoid negative side effects. The methodology followed is inspired by URD guidebook for participation. SI will ensure the respect of the Do No Harm principle through analyzing the possible unintended effect of each step of the project implementation to better mitigate them. In addition, SI will ensure that its existing Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM), managed by the MEAL department, will be accessible to all beneficiaries under this project, and that affected communities have been properly sensitized on how to access and use the CRM.
