Project Summary

DRC will provide food security assistance in the form of cash and voucher assistance to IDPs, Returnees and Host Community. DRC proposes a cash assistance programme to improve household food security, reduce vulnerability, and reduce negative coping strategies. Unconditional cash distributions in this case are suited to meeting the food security objectives of the proposed response, especially when it comes to diversification of diet and beneficiary needs. Cash transfers allow beneficiaries to make their own choices, with experience demonstrating that extremely food insecure households typically spend a significant proportion of the cash on food with high nutritional value. DRC will also training farmers through coordinating the Ministry of Agriculture and provide agricultural inputs that includes seeds and tools. DRC will target 3,060 households with Food Assistance, 450 households with Agricultural Resilience Support and 950 households with agricultural related trainings. This project will be implemented in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States.

1 - Needs

The number of people in need of urgent assistance in north-east Nigeria rose from 7.9 million at the beginning of 2020 to 10.6 million since the onset of COVID-19. Up to 5.1 million people risk being critically food insecure during the next lean season (June - August 2021), a level similar to 2016-2017 when famine was looming over Borno State. Specifically in the proposed locations, latest Cadre Harmonise findings show that currently (October 2020) 441,700 individuals are between IPC Phases 3 and 5. Furthermore 806,802 people are projected to be between IPC Phases 3 and 5 from June to October 2021 in DRC’s target locations in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States. In regards to the targeted locations for this project, projections show Madagali having a population of 66,076, Michika 60,744, MMC 325,000 and Jere 187,332 falling between IPC Phase 3 and 5 between June and August 2021 during the lean season . Despite significant achievements by the humanitarian community, millions of affected people are still facing hunger; lack protection; have limited or no access to health facilities or to water, sanitation and hygiene; or live in overcrowded camp conditions, with some of them still sleeping in the open. In various areas across the three states, levels of acute malnutrition for children under five are above emergency thresholds. Stressed to emergency malnutrition thresholds was witnessed in several LGAs of these states (Adamawa, Borno and Yobe) during the current period. Stressed threshold GAM rate of 7.5% was reported in LGAs of Adamawa (North) State. In Borno (North) and Yobe (Central) crisis threshold GAM rate of 14.5% and 14.7% was witnessed; while in Yobe (central) GAM rate is under the emergency threshold of 15.8%. The impact of the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic, rising insecurity and funding shortfalls still pose challenges to aid organisations providing urgent assistance. Though food commodities are available, food prices have continued to rise significantly across Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states. In Borno and Yobe states, prices of key staples saw a seasonal spike due to decreased availability of market stocks since July, ascribed to the lean season, underlying high inflation and the Eid celebration in late July. Though security is still a challenge, markets remain generally accessible as most people can physically reach the markets anytime to perform their economic activities without barriers. The protracted crisis has disrupted livelihoods and agricultural activities, with many conflict-affected families relying on humanitarian aid and market purchases to meet their daily food needs.

2 - Activities

Food security remains a pressing issue for conflict-affected households across the Northeast. DRC would seek to increase purchasing power of households 3,060 households (representing 18,360 individuals) in Maiduguri, Jere, Michika, Madagali, Gujba and Gulani LGAs, who have limited access to food commodities available in the local markets through unconditional food assistance. DRC will utilize electronic cash as the main cash delivery mechanism through RedRose and UBA for cash and voucher assistance as well as pilot bank transfer modalities where possible. DRC will provide 3,717 NGN per individual/capita based on the guidance note from the Food Security Sector (FSS) on the food basket transfer value. Additionally, each household will also receive NGN 2,000 to address cooking fuel costs. On average, each household would receive the value of 24,300 NGN (approximately 63 USD ) per month based on the FSS recommended per capita method of calculation for an average household size of 6 (subject to fluctuation during implementation based on actual HH size). The suggested intervention would mainstream nutrition through active screening for malnutrition cases and reinforce the existing referral system of malnutrition cases to the relative health/stabilization centers in the operational areas to complement in achievement of the unconditional food assistance objectives. DRC will complement the above mentioned through providing agricultural production trainings to 950 households (representing 5700 individuals) and will provide agricultural resilience support through agricultural inputs (crop and livestock) to 450 farmers (representing 2700 individuals). DRC anticipates to secure funding from the USAID's Bureau for Humanitarian Assistance (BHA) and the European Union to fund the proposed activities.
