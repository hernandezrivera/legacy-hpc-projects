Project Summary

Although the formation of transitional government of national unity in February 2020 resulted in improved security however the subsequent shocks including COVID-19, flooding, inter-communal violence and political conflict in part of country continue to exacerbate the already fragile humanitarian context in South Sudan. The Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2020 estimate that 8.3 million people in South Sudan estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2021 including 5.2 million in need of health assistance.
Under this project, Concern ensures provision of the Ministry of Health (MoH’s) Basic Package of Health Services (BPHS) to communities that are not covered by Health Pooled Fund (HPF) partners in North Bahr al Ghazal, and recently identified unmet needs in Kajo-Keji in Central Equatoria. Concern will support mobile sites that will provide primary health care for outpatient treatment of common illnesses including malaria, ARTIs and diarrhea and coordinate with partners to ensure Concern is targeting those locations where there are critical gaps in provision of basic services. Concern will also support the SMoH and CHDs to provide the EPI services in Health facilities not covered under HPF 3 in Aweil west and North county through seconding one EPI Worker in those health facilities, to reduce the risks of communicable diseases. Concern’s mobile teams will focus on providing services in hard-to-reach areas. In NBeG, these are generally remote areas that are more than 10km from fixed health facilities, where the mobile team will conduct weekly outreach services to 10 locations (Aweil west: 05, Aweil North:05). A mobile team comprised of Clinical Officers, Nurses, Midwives and EPI worker. In Kajo-Keji the approach will be to similarly offer services in 5 hard-to-reach locations where limited services are being offered, either by the Ministry of Health or other Health partners, however this team will work within the framework of existing facilities where possible. In Kajo-Kaji based on coordination with County Health Department (CHD), Concern has recently conducted an assessment of Primary Health Care Centre (PHCC) for repair and rehabilitation, thus the intervention will also conduct Renovation and rehabilitation of one PHCC and support Ministry of Health (MoH) national staff in health service provision in PHCC.
In Kajo Keji, despite a number of Health and Nutrition implementing Partners on ground, there are 2 functioning PHCCs and no Hospital in the whole county, while in some areas IPs deploy mobile teams on a biweekly basis. There are many areas for both host communities and IDPs not covered by mobile teams yet, there’s no functioning Health Facility and the number of returnees is increasing every month. KK is in IPC emergency phase in projection for coming period, the WASH sector severity mapping highlighted limited access to WASH and sanitation that further contribute to water born diseases.

1 - Needs

The formation of transitional government of national unity (TGoNU) in February 2020 provided some hope in term of political stability and reduced conflict related incidents however, the overall progress on implementation of peace agreement remained slow with no progress on the critical area of security sector reform. According to the Humanitarian Need Overview (HNO)-2020, 8.3 million people in South Sudan estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2021. According to HNO the weakened health system compounded by multiple shocks will impact the health and wellbeing of an estimated 4.9 million South Sudanese in 2021. This is a 36 per cent increase from the 3.6 million people in 2020. South Sudan’s healthcare system is one of the weakest in the world and limited availability and a lack of access to health services has contributed to South Sudan having one of the highest maternal and under-five mortality rates in the world, at 789 per 100,000 live births and 90.7 per 1,000 live births respectively. The limited access to healthcare facilities and medical supplies results in a high number of preventable deaths. Among the top causes of preventable deaths are severe malnutrition and maternal mortality. Additionally, there are high rates of tuberculosis, HIV/AIDS, malaria and diarrheal diseases in the country. UNICEF reports that more than 92% of births occur without the presence of a skilled professional and child mortality rate of 96 deaths per 1,000 live births. Around 75 per cent of all child deaths in South Sudan are due to preventable diseases, such as diarrhoea, malaria and pneumonia. This contributes to South Sudan having the fifth highest maternal mortality in the world. Years of conflict and economic decline have eroded the Government’s ability to provide basic services. South Sudan has a critical shortage of trained health personnel, with only an estimated 47.6 nurses and midwives per 39,088 persons and one physician per 65,574 persons, while the coverage of essential health services is only 30%. Due to the lack of financial support from the South Sudanese government, international organizations have become the major providers of healthcare in South Sudan. Concern currently supports five mobile clinics in NBeG and includes full integration of nutrition services. Considering the needs mobile clinics will be increased from 5 to 10. There are 43 health facilities in Aweil West and 36 in Aweil North. HealthNet TPO, the lead health partner in Aweil West and North Counties, offers health services in only 21 health facilities in Aweil West and 20 health facilities in Aweil North. Hence, 22 health facilities in Aweil West and 16 health facilities in Aweil North are not covered by HPF with shortage of staff and medicines for most of the time. In Kajo-Keji the vast majority of health facilities have been vandalized and abandoned. The CHD reports that there are currently 16 functioning facilities, but only 7 of which are supported by implementing partners. Of the five “functioning” facilities which were assessed by Concern in March 2020, two do not have any clinical staff and no facility has more than 3 clinical staff. All facilities lack essential drugs and materials. Considering the limited government capacity, on-going health crisis inflicted by COVID-19 and already frail health system in South Sudan, there is greater need for continuation and expansion of health services to most affected population. Malnutrition, food insecurity, limited access to WASH services and disruption to vaccination efforts and to the surveillance of vaccine-preventable diseases due to COVID-19 may further deteriorate the health outcomes for thousands of households in the country.

2 - Project activities

SO 1: Concern will provide primary health care services through mobile clinics in Aweil West and North counties in NBeG and Kajo-Keji Country in Central Equatoria. Concern will continue to meet the transport cost for patients referred with medical complication to the next level of management. In Kajo-Keji Concern also support Ministry of Health in health service provision in Lire Primary Health Care Centre (PHCC). Concern will support the CHD to provide EPI services at mobile sites and campaigns. Concern will continue to coordinate with other agencies in the identification and response to communicable disease outbreaks. As the lead health partner is not delivering vaccinations in PHCUs, it is therefore important that Concern shall fill the gap by supporting vaccination in PHCUs not supported by HPF as well as provision of EPI services in mobile sites.The COVID-19 prevention messages are integrated in Risk Communication and Community engagement activities to sensitise the community about increased awareness and prevention measures on risk of infection. Concern will also continue supporting communications and information outreach activities to encourage behavioral change to contain the spread of the virus. Concern will support health facilities with improved water sources, construction of latrines, waste management infrastructure and provision of hygiene materials to improve IPC.SO 2: To improve timely access and scale-up of health response Concern will provide primary health care services through mobile clinics with focus on providing services in hard-to-reach areas. In NBeG, these are generally remote areas that are more than 10km from fixed health facilities, where the mobile team will conduct weekly outreach services to 5 locations. Mobile teams shall work in each of the 10 locations once a week and they will provide OPD consultation. In Kajo-Keji the approach will be to similarly offer services in 4 hard-to-reach locations where limited services are being offered, either by the Ministry of Health or other Health partners, however the team will work within the framework of existing facilities where possible. In Kajo Keji new areas reported or identified with high numbers of new IDPs and returnees will also be targeted for mobile health services. Based on recent assessment and coordination with CHD) Concern will also support ministry of health in Kajo-Keji for provision of health services in one static site (PHCC).To decrease morbidity and mortality from communicable diseases, Concern will concentrate on improving the quality of curative and preventative services for malaria, diarrhoea, acute respiratory tract infections, and other communicable diseases. All mobile clinics will follow relevant MoH diagnosis, treatment and prescription protocols. Concern will also ensure that targeted beneficiaries have access to antenatal and postnatal care. Reproductive health will be provided at mobile clinics to ensure attendance by pregnant mothers in remote communities at ANC, skilled birth attendant deliveries and attendance at post-natal care (PNC).SO 3: Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled, mental health disorders and the elderlyAlthough Concern does not implement programmes providing specific services for survivors of SGBV and mental illness, it will continue to respond to scale-up a health response that will be integrated with its nutrition response, Concern has a referral system in its mobile clinics and CRM to identify and refer such cases to other Health and Protection partners. In Kajo Keji, despite a number of Health Partners there are only 2 functioning PHCCs and no Hospital in the whole county, the number of returnees is increasing every month. Concern will coordinate with cluster, fill the gap, rehabilitate static health unit and work toward capacity building of government health staff.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Concern has a Code of Conduct and its associated policies including Program Participant Protection Policy (P4), Child Safeguarding, and Anti-Trafficking in-person Policy. Concern staff and any one engaged by Concern including consultants, contractors, partners and visitor are required to understand and comply with Concern safeguarding policies. Concern’s Code of Conduct and Associated Policy is consistent with the six core principles of IASC Task Force on PSEA in Humanitarian Crises. During project inception and regular consultations with the beneficiaries and relevant stake holders, Concern informs them about right to assistance, scope of services, right to appropriate behavior including Concern’s commitment to zero tolerance on Sexual exploitation and abuse by humanitarian workers. The beneficiaries will be informed about the Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) to raise their voice in case of any issue pertaining to PSEA. The monitoring mechanism will ensure that the beneficiaries are aware about PSEA and how to complaint in case of any exploitation and abuse. Concern will ensure that health clinic staff is gender balance. Concern regularly coordinate with protection partners and safeguarding/protection issues are referred the Protection partner. In all locations, Concern has a Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) where beneficiaries can register complaints about gender related or protection concerns. All recruitments of staff and trainings ensure that there is equal access to women and men to ensure balanced representation. Monitoring will collect data on any information on safety and protection concerns and to improve future programme designs.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Concern understands different vulnerabilities within target communities that may exclude target beneficiaries in their access to feedback and complaint mechanism. Therefore, Concern will consult with the target community, the local chief and state authorities to understand the overall target population profile, access to communication channel and nature of vulnerabilities. While establishing the Feedback and Complaint mechanism, Concern will consider such vulnerabilities and access constrained therefore the target population will be consulted on their preferred mode of complaint/feedback channel. Different mode of feedback such as complaint boxes, phone numbers, email, verbal feedback/complaint during Concern monitoring visits, in-person to Concern office and Help desk at health sites will be used to avoid exclusion of access to feedback mechanism. Concern will use both pictorial, written and verbal channels in local languages to communicate the feedback mechanism to target beneficiaries. The individuals living with disabilities, illiterate, women and girls can also share their complaint/feedback through their local representative and relatives. In addition, during monitoring and support visits FGDs will be conducted separately with women and men to seek feedback and provide opportunity to discuss any concern/issues or complaint.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Prior to the initiating project activities and delivery of assistance, Concern conduct an inception meeting with the target beneficiaries and stakeholders to inform them about the project scope, selection criteria for mobile sites and entitlements to receive health services. Similarly, during the inception meetings and during regular consultations with the community, Concern will inform the beneficiaries about the feedback mechanism, their right to assistance and complaints. Depending on type of assistance, context and target population profile, Concern ensure that the banners with information on complaint channel and feedback mechanism are displayed broadly in the target areas and at the health clinics delivery points.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Concern engage with the target community and relevant state authorities during the needs assessment, project design and monitoring to seek their feedback and adapt project assistance/delivery accordingly. The Boma health committees will be formed to ensure consultation with target community and their involvement in program implementation. Caregivers attending mobile sites and health facilities will be asked to provide feedback on quality of the services and Concern will improve the service delivery according to the feedback from target community. Concern will consult with target community, local representative and relevant government authorities in selection of sites for mobile clinics. The Complaint and Feedback Mechanism and regular consultation with beneficiaries during monitoring will provide opportunity to beneficiaries to share their feedback which will be considered in adapting the program accordingly. Concern conduct project end-line survey to review the progress on project outcomes and seek feedback for future improvements. The lesson will be documented and shared with cluster and other relevant stakeholders.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

During assessments, Concern will coordinate with Protection partners and identify safety, security and protection risks and findings will be used to adapt the programme. Although NBeG is relatively stable, there is still a high risk of GBV. In Central Equatoria, due to on-going conflict there are considerable protection risks to women and girls including sexual and gender base violence and men and boys including forced recruitment into armed groups. The locations of mobile clinics will be identified in consultation with communities to ensure they are in safe areas. The timing of mobile sites and service provision consider that the beneficiates can reach back to their home during day light. Concern will sensitize the community and make sure that the target population is aware about free health service provision, scope of program and staff is gender balance as for as possible. Concern will incorporate conflict sensitivity to ensure that the programme does not have negative impacts on conflict or social dynamics. Consultation with communities will be conducted during the programme and sensitisations on targeting being solely based on medical needs and entitlements to health assistance will be done. This will reduce misinterpretations of the programme and provision of health services. Referrals will be made of protection cases identified during mobile sites or the CRM. Concern safeguarding policies require all staff and any one engaged in program to comply with code of conduct and reporting of breaches. The beneficiaries will be sensitised on CRM and their right to share feedback and complaint. Concern will ensure that the staff were trained on GBV, aware about referral pathways and sensitise the beneficiaries on reporting and use of GBV pathways.
