Project Summary

FIVDB will undertake a comprehensive livelihood program comprising certain agricultural inputs, vocational & skill training programs, awareness sessions, and CFW programs under this project aiming to promote self-reliance of men & women in Rohingya refugees and enhance the livelihoods and resilience of vulnerable host communities intending to reduce different identified vulnerabilities I.e lack of skill & livelihood opportunities, food insecurity, lower participation of women in training and other IGA activities. Rain water harvesting, DRR training and Reforestation programs will be undertaken as an initiative to strengthen Natural Resource Management. This is going to be based on the experience and learning from some existing programs funded by DCA and FCDO. Inclusion of households with disabled person, female headed, orphan hosting, elderly person headed, large sized households and other vulnerable groups will be prioritized. Center based Training programs on agricultural and nonagricultural skills with input distribution will be provided considering SAAD format of reporting. Capacity building of Community Resource Persons (CRP) in both the camps and the host communities and providing technical support to beneficiaries under previous projects will be included. Participants will be chosen from Camp 14, 21,23, 10,2E and KRC , Halidiapalong ,palangkhali , Jaliapalong , Hnila and Whykong. All cross cutting issues will be prioritized. Inclusion of overall 60% female , 40 % male and 7% households with disabilities targeting aged 18 to 60 will be ensured. Participants will be provided a freedom of choice in training category selection. Meaningful access, maximum participation of women, inclusion of protection principles will be ensured through community consultation. Local authorities & elites, community people in camp & HC will be engaged while designing the project implementation strategy. Establishment of Complain & feedback mechanism and satisfaction survey conduction will be ensured including necessary GBV referrals. All participants will receive further input & other technical support to put their training into practice.
Community engagement and participatory planning & monitoring is the key strategy for the program. Community Resource Persons drawn from the locality & incapacitating them to be able to support the community is the key for sustainability of the program. The CRPs will provide door to door support to the program participants. To avoid the risk of COVID transmission safety protocols will be strictly followed and the Project will also undertake awareness raising on DRR and training on life skills for all the participants.
There will be a baseline and end-line for all the participants households and OECD criteria will be used for program evaluation.

1 - Identified Needs

"Following the influx in 2017, Over 0.85 million FDMNs are residing in 34 congested camps in Bangladesh and livelihoods of local community have been affected (REVA, April 2020, JRP 2021). Subjected to several vulnerabilities despite various assistance provided by the humanitarian organizations and government, all refugees and 330,401 population in host community including men, women & children are reported to be in need of livelihood & support and cash assistance (JRP, 2021). 15%, 50% and 35% households were reported to have poor, borderline and acceptable food consumption score respectively in refugee community and 8%, 49% and 43% households were reported to have poor, borderline and acceptable food consumption score respectively in host community. Households reported to have reduced food expenditure and limited access to food as an impact of COVID 19 in refugee community (JMSNA, 2020 preliminary findings).40% of population consisting of men & women in Ukhiya and Teknaf are reported to be vulnerable to shocks (REVA, December 2019), 58% men & women were subjected to poor FCS in May from borderline (WFP, May 2020). Skilled labor & livelihood opportunities: More general improvement is hindered due to lack of livelihood opportunities despite 70% refugee men & women are receiving E voucher assistance. 46% population consisting men & women is adopting negative coping strategies instead of optimizing assistance (REVA, April 2020). Diminished income due to COVID 19 was reported by 93% households (JMSNA, August 2020. It is recommended to provide informal workers with income support to help the effect of food price hike (FAO, June 2020).55% population in Teknaf & Ukhiya consisting of men & women is engaged in the agricultural sector and 85% of households reported to be engaged in labor (ACAPS 2020). The agricultural intervention coverage is reported to be lower in various camps i.e KRC, 2E, 22, 23, 10 etc. (Cox’sbazar FSS, Feb 2019-March 2020). Engagement in income activities is lower in camp and Host communities (50% & 54%) (WFP, August 2020) and access to IGA was demanded by men & women from both communities (JMSNA, preliminary finding 2020). Engagement of adolescent girls & boys and youth male & female are comparatively lower in different IGA activities, Education programs and livelihood training due to lack of opportunities (NRC, December 2019). Skill based training, different CFW and IGA activities for men, women and youth male & female may help in reducing the vulnerabilities. Lower scopes for Female : Females are reported to have lower income indicating a level of discrimination in labor market favoring male workers followed by lower working opportunities (JSMNA,2020, REVA ,April 2020).Vulnerabilities are reported to be less among households engaged in different cash for work programs (REVA, April 2020). Women participation is reported to be less in different education programs or IGA due to lack of freedom of movement by family & community pressure in camps (NRC, December 2019).Most vulnerable groups affected by COVID 19 are reported to be female headed households, households with disabled persons, including large size, elderly headed households (JMSNA 2020 preliminary finding, REVA 2020). Inclusion of maximum female and vulnerable households & youth male and female may reduce the vulnerabilities. Lack of water to meet domestic needs was reported by refugee and host community (JMSNA, preliminary findings 2020). According to the report of JMSNA 2019 Nhila, Haldiapalang, Jaliapalang unions face high water scarcity. Following the influx damages of cultivable lands and deforestation have been a huge crisis in many areas and unions of Cox's Bazar. Rain water harvesting in Refugee camps and Host community may enable the Men & women in both communities to have access to clean water to meet domestic needs."

2 - Activities or outputs

1. This Project will select a total of 4185 program participants for vocational training skill , counseling on IGA agricultural inputs, subsistent support through CfW. This includes continuation of support to existing beneficiaries and inclusion of new beneficiaries from RRs & HCs. We focus on adolescents girls and boys from ultra poor families including HHs headed by women, HHs with impaired persons, HHs hosting orphans and with other form of vulnerabilities. Selection of program participants are of a participatory approach and community led. Participants database will maintain following SADD standard. 2. Capacity building of 54 Community Resource Person (CRP): CRPs will be selected from RR (24) and HC (30) from the age group ( 18-60) from the targeted camps and HCs and trained to provide door to door support to the program participants to ensure trust building and avoid COVID transmission risks. 3. Development of Content for Training : We conducted a market survey jointly with DCA, which has been presented to the sector. The findings of the study will be shared with the program participants, , trades and contents will be selected according to their opinion after analysis of the findings and their experience of the market . Local resource persons will be selected for the training as much as possible and local resources and experiences will be incorporated in the training contents. At least 5 training modules will be developed. 4. 890 RRs ( 80% Female) & 1145 HC ( 50% Female) will receive training on agriculture and 360 RR ( All female) and 699 HC (75% Female) will receive non agricultural non agricultural training. 720 RR ( 75% female) and 420 HC beneficiaries (60% Female) would receive cash and kind inputs for agricultural and non agricultural activities. Only new beneficiaries are provided inputs, while existing beneficiaries are provided followup support only. 5. 1,300 ( 50% Female ) will be enrolled in Cash for work . CfW activities for women are to be chosen on the basis of consultation with the community, considering the cultural norms. Men will be engaged in the care of trees planted already and new plantation, and other activities in the camps and HC areas on the basis of community need with a focus on waste management and environment preservation, production of organic manures etc. 6. At least 11 rain water harvesting plants will be established on a pilot basis. FIVDB Engineers will work closely with the selected program participants for designing a locally suitable of easily replicable model for rain water harvesting plant7. All the program participants will take part on awareness sessions on DRR, Nutrition, Hygiene Practice, Life Skills etc, CRPs will take lead on those sessions. Peer learning will be emphasised for ensuring community participation. A strong monitoring mechanism will be established using an efficient CRM and help desk in the communities. Regular Post activity surveys and sharing of the survey reports with the community is an integral part of the monitoring mechanism. Participants data will be maintained using SADD format and women empowerment, protection and inclusion are considered in the implementation strategy.
