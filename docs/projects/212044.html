Project Summary

This project seeks to improve the livelihood situation for the olive farmers in Idleb countryside three sub-districts of Armanaz, Jisr Ash-shughur, and Azaz, through conducting the integrated olive management . where the general character of the targeted area is characterized by olive cultivation as a main source of income.
The project will target 2,500 olive farmers within one season all of them are from small ownership between 3 -4 donum.
The project will provide the targeted beneficiaries (2,500 olive farmers) with the inputs, intercropping kit, and trainings. with estimated budget 985,000 $, knowing the secured fund is approx 350,000 $.
However, the project concentrates on five main activities:
- Creating agricultural cooperatives for integrated management of olive trees.
- Providing the agricultural inputs (fertilizers, harvesting tools, pruning tools).
- Providing legume seeds for the intercropping between olive trees.
- Providing the agricultural services of the olive trees (tillage, harvesting service, pruning service).
- Providing the agricultural training beside the agricultural guidance for the olive farmers.
By providing inputs, services and trainings, farmers are enabled to increase the volume of their olive production. The application of more sustainable agricultural practices not only benefits the soil fertility but also improves the adaptation to changing climatic conditions, working towards sustainable production in the long term. The establishment and support to a farmers’ cooperative with productive assets supports farmers with a collaborative processing of their olive harvest to enhance the quality of produced olive oil and additional by-products. Despite the existence of traders, consumers, and an active market, the olive value chain remains weak and requires revitalization through higher added value and raising the quality of the olive products. Through strengthening the farmers’ cooperative, a higher product quality reached, and opportunities to process by-products will be ensured to generate additional revenue.
The capacities of local olive farmers in ecologic and economic sustainable olive production are strengthened. The project aims to enhance the resilience of farmers and driving change to reduce reliance on free support, which often leads to prolonged dependency on foreign aids. Farmers' usual practices are harvesting, pruning, tillage, fertilization, etc. This project aims to empower self-reliance, development, and production of the targeted farmers in A’zaz by providing specific agricultural inputs, tools, and services for up to 3 donums per farmer. This support aims to enhance both the quantity and quality of olive and olive oil production. Additionally, the project will conduct theoretical and practical training sessions on Good Agricultural Practices in olive orchards. The project also will support this group with a productive asset that would be used for utilization of olive by-products and improving the quality of produced olive oil.
The project adopts a coaching methodology where the technical advisors will accompany the farmers and the cooperative members through introducing new techniques through comprehensive training sessions. Distribution of tools, inputs, and services will align with the new techniques. Support, coaching, and meticulous supervision will be provided during the application phase to ensure the effective implementation of the new practices.
This comprehensive approach aims not only to empower the participating farmers but also to establish a self-sustaining cooperative that can efficiently manage the olive sector in the community and beyond. By promoting sustainable practices and reducing dependency.
The total direct beneficiaries of the project are 6,250 HHs distributing as following; 2,500 HHs of olive farmer, 2,500 HHs of unskilled worker in harvesting with total workdays 25,000, and 1,250 HHs of skilled worker in olive tree pruning with total workdays 25,000.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Amal Organization, in collaboration with local councils, will launch a project announcement campaign in targeted areas, unveiling its comprehensive activities aimed at supporting olive farmers. Beneficiaries will be carefully selected based on project eligibility criteria, with a priority given to the most vulnerable households. The project will foster coordination and consultation with local councils and farmers to establish agricultural associations. These associations will facilitate cooperation among farmers, enhancing production management and marketing strategies to optimize efficiency and increase profits.A key aspect of the project involves providing essential agricultural inputs, including fertilizers, harvesting tools, and pruning tools, to targeted beneficiaries. This initiative aims to improve the overall condition and productivity of olive trees.Furthermore, The project will insert additional component (the intercropping "faba bean seeds, local variety winter season, sterilized with fungicide, germination percentage 95%, purity 85%") between the olive trees in the aim of improving the soil fertility and rise the income of olive farmer, resulted from the intercropping, where the legume seeds will be distributed to the olive farmer beneficiaries to grow.The project will provide the agricultural services of the olive trees (tillage, harvesting service, pruning service) and to do that will recruit the unskilled and skilled worker to do these activities where 2500 unskilled worker and 1250 skilled workers will be recruited to do these services. In addition to practical support, the project emphasizes sustainability through agricultural training and guidance for olive farmer beneficiaries. This approach not only enhances participants' knowledge but also fosters knowledge exchange and collaboration among farmers. Amal Organization coordinated with GIZ in order to increase the efficiency of the internal structure of cooperative societies and improve their performance. It will also coordinate with ACU to conduct soil analysis tests for farmers before starting intercropping. Importantly, all project interventions adhere to the 'do no harm' approach, ensuring that activities promote positive outcomes while mitigating potential risks or harm to communities.
