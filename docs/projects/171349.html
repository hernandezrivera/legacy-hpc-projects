Project Summary

Malnutrition remained persistently high in Somalia, at the national level, the median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) has remained Serious (10–14.9%) over the past three seasons (11.8% in 2020 Gu, 13.1% in 2019 Deyr and 13.8% in 2019 Gu ). According to the Nutrition cluster, an estimated 962 885 children under the age of five years (total acute malnutrition burden), who will likely face acute malnutrition through December 2021, including 162, 000 who are likely to be severely malnourished, 54% are girls. In addition to children, under five years of age, a further 571,159 pregnant and lactating women are estimated to be in need of either preventive or curative emergency nutrition assistance. Save the Children proposes a multi-sectoral, integrated health and nutrition response to respond to vulnerable, IDPs, and marginalized communities in Somalia. Save the Children will work with MOH to improve access to quality lifesaving nutrition services in static health and outreach sites to deliver an integrated package of promotive , preventive and curative maternal, nutrition services. SC will build the local capacity by providing training and technical support to local government, health facility staff, the Community Nutrition volunteers , representatives of local communities to raise awareness, promote nutrition, and strengthen linkages between the community and health facilities.
The nutrition program will be based on the Community-Based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) approach so as to maximize the coverage of, and access to, life-saving child and maternal focused nutrition services for children under five years and PLW. The CMAM approach empowers community volunteers to screen, identify and refer children and PLW with acute malnutrition for treatment before they become seriously ill, and ensure proper treatment by conducting frequent home visits of children admitted in program. In addition to the CMAM service and Infant and young child feeding (IYCF-E) services will be integrated with the CMAM. Trained IYCF-E counsellors will provide support to PLWs and care givers of young children with difficulties in adopting the optimal infant and young child feeding practices recommended by WHO/UNICEF. In addition, SC will support to increase awareness and sensitization on COVID-19 amongst vulnerable populations in target areas using the RCCE strategy. The CMAM and IYCF services will be adopted for the COVID-19 infection prevention as per nation and nutrition cluster guideline, and especially all IYCF counselling card will be adopted on addressing breastfeeding in COVID-19 context. Health and nutrition staff will be trained on mainstreaming of COVID-19 interventions in regular service delivery. Provide training on COVID19 adopted IMAM and IYCF guideline including COVID19 infection prevention. Mitigation Measures

o The project’s action will be implemented in selected locations from targeted districts and beneficiaries identified based on the analysis of vulnerabilities and stresses among crisis affected communitieso Local authorities and community representatives (community education committees) will be involved in the project from design through to exit o Save the children will consider the do-no harm principle in its operations throughout the project implementation to minimize causing further suffering to the supported communities/beneficiaries. All activities will prioritize the safety and dignity of the beneficiaries and in particular the ones living with specific and intersecting vulnerabilities such as; gender, age, disability and marginalized clans• Due to cultural sensitivities and gender dynamics, women and girls will be seen by practioners of the same sex and consultations will be at a time and place of the beneficiaries convenience and safety Protection/ Child-safeguarding (CSG): As this project focuses on improving nutritional out come and IYCF among the most vulnerable populations, children are direct beneficiaries of the project and will be direct contact with SCI staff, partners, MOH staff, as well as contractors and community members/volunteers, which increases their exposure to risks. However, SCI a has robust CSG reporting mechanisms in place. All staff and partners (CCBRS) are required to complete CSG training as part of induction and refresher training. There is a CSG champion in each area office to monitor and provide support as needed. Project specific CSG risks and mitigation measures are identified during project kick off and reviewed during regular review meetings. All staff are vetted before recruitment and trained, oriented to child safe guarding and protection in order for them to be able to implement safe programming and provide safe and dignified services to the beneficiaries.
