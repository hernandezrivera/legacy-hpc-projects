Project Summary

CAFAD proposes a three pronged strategic program aimed at emergency livelihood support, food assistance and multi-sector response to support protection, nutrition, re-integration and resettlement as there is high potential increased returnees into South Sudan in 2020 as the peace process progresses and alignment towards resilience and capacity building.
The first approach will be blanket assistance to the highly vulnerable individuals (5040 Women, 600 girls, 3060 men and 300 boys) classified in Emergency IPC 4, 5 and some population in IPC 3 with main and dry season crop and vegetable seeds along with fishing equipment. Safe use and basic guidelines on agriculture inputs and tools will be conducted during distribution. With additional funding, livestock disease surveillance, vaccinations and treatments in conjunction with the County Ministry of animal resources and Fisheries. Female headed households will be targeted with small stalk livestock (goats and chicken) in-kind.
For the communities affected by flooding in Nyirol, Uror and Fangak Counties, resilience initiatives as an alternate solution to the emergency will include community sensitization about appropriate locations for planting and cultivation rather than relying on food rations for subsistence.
Secondly, in collaboration with WFP, CAFAD will give conditional food assistance to population in IPC 3 (540 women, 480 men and 780 school going girls and boys) through Food For Asset (FFA) targeting rehabilitation of roads and dykes and Food For Education (FFE) modalities oriented towards school going children (especially girls) in conjunction with UNICEF and the Ministry of General Education and Science.
Thirdly the program will focus on capacity building through basic agronomic trainings (crop and vegetable production) and fisheries training targeting fisher folks. CAFAD plans to train 522 women, 576 men, 120 men and 60 boys. Also 120 CABHWs are going to be trained on animal vaccinations, treatments and disease surveillance. To build local procurement of seeds and cereals, CAFAD will conduct Inputs Trade Fairs (ITFs) targeting 3000 local producers and marketers of locally grown crops and vegetables.

1 - Needs

By January 2020, South Sudan is estimated to have 1.2 million people in IPC 4 and another 4.3 million in Crisis (IPC 3) with the highest concentration of acute food insecurity in Jonglei, Uppernile and Unity States. Of these food insecure households, more than 60% are women headed necessitating interventions targeting girls and women. The high dependency on markets in times of seasonal depletion of harvests (lean) makes most of the households stressed due to high food prices in low income environments (IPC September, 2019 Update)Initial Rapid Needs Assessment (IRNA) recently (October, 2019) conducted by CAFAD and other partners in Nyirol in response to flooding incidence identified Lankien, Majok, Thol, Dengdorl and Wechdeng Bomas and payams to be the ones heavily affected. About 7056 IDPs are affected from Key Informant (KI) interviews. The worse affected are women and children. Houses are submerged with heavy losses of household items (NFIs) and lack of basic services (health and nutrition facilities). Cases of gender based violence and revenge killings are rampant in the assessment areas. According to UNOCHA and IOM DTM (September, 2019) there are 1.5 million IDPs in South Sudan displaced by the conflict, natural disaster (flooding) and diseases.Most of the rural populace have no access to clean timely, safe water for drinking predisposing them to water-borne diseases (REACH-WASH Assessment June, 2019).A recent report by UNFAO predicts increase in rainfall between October and December 2019 with increased incidences of livestock and crop pests and diseases thus affecting productivity and in the field thus recommends enhanced continuous monitoring and early planting for next season. Vaccinations against the common cattle diseases such as Lumpy Skin Disease (LSD) and Haemorrhagic Syndrome (HS) are essential at these period to minimize livestock losses (UNFAO GHACOF 53, August 2019) FEWS NET report (September 2019) suggests that the cessation of hostilities is likely to result to better crop production compared to 2018 but most households having lost key livelihood assets and copying strategy as a result of the protracted conflict is likely to slow down the recovery and may slip into famine warnings.FSNMS R24 data showed persisting high levels of Acute Malnutrition with Nyirol, Uror, Canal and Fangak being classified as “critical” and “Serious” (GAM WHZ 10.0-14.9 percent) indicating IPC 3 and 4 respectively. The informal taxes and price fluctuations continue to negatively affect the market dependent poor population in peri-urban and urban settling. Many of the households that were interviewed anticipate that harvests in 2019 will be lower than last year due to pests and diseases and flood-related destruction of crops from July to September; this is especially true in Fangak, Uror and Nyirol Counties. Assessment by REACH-IMPACT on March in Kuriwai Payam of Canal/Pigi County found 21% of the interviewed households to be “very severe” on Household Hunger Scale (HHS) contributed by limited food availability, lack of staple food in the markets and crop loss. Families resort to borrowing from relatives, consuming wild fruits and leaves.CFSAM report, 2019 notes that the displacement of farming households to neighboring countries and fear of rapes, reprisal attacks, kidnappings in Jonglei has led to shrinked areas under cultivation with women opting to plan around homesteads where they feel safe. Over 60% of South Sudanese population is livestock rearing dependent underscoring the important role in food security and income generation. Vaccination and treatment campaigns remains critical to safeguard livestock against most prevalent diseases including Hemorrhagic Septicemia (HS), Peste Des Petits Ruminats (PPR) and Foot and Mouth Disease (FMD)

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Emergency livelihood support provided to most food insecure 9,000 women, men and youths in Uror, Nyirol, Fangak, Lafon and Kapoeta EastMain activitiesActivity 1.1: Main Season agriculture inputs are provided to vulnerable households in Uror, Nyirol Fangak, Lafon and Kapoeta East 9, 000 Crop kits; quality crop seeds of sorghum, maize, cowpeas and hand tools distributed to increase food production. Activity 1.2: 9,000 Vulnerable women, girls, men and boys are given 7 variety of fast maturing vegetable seeds and agriculture tools distribution in Uror, Nyirol, Lafon,Canal and Kapoeta East.Time lines: Purchase or Preposition and distribution of vegetable and fishing kits will be done in conjunction with FAO 2020. Distribution will be done in early in the day (08:00 am 17:00 pm) and in an open area to ensure safety and protection for women and girls against sexual exploitation. A gender specialist from CAFAD will be deployed to ensure that women and girl rights are catered for during distribution.Activity 1.3: Fishing kits distribution to fisher forks in Uror, Fangak, Nyirol and Canal 9,000 Fishing Kits; 2 fishing twines, a box of hooks, monofilament and tarpaulin per beneficiary. Women voice, rights and needs will be taken into account all through during targeting (at least 50% women and girls) and distribution. Activity 2: Livestock vaccination and treatment services are provided to vulnerable agro pastoral communities in Uror, Nyirol, Canal, Fangak and Kapoeta East. Main activity Livestock treatment and vaccination campaign; 132230 livestock heads targeted, 60 CAHWs (re)trained. Time lines: Targeting for mass treatment, vaccination campaigns will be maximized during the dry season window of opportunity from January-March and October-December 2020 when the cattle are easily accessible near the homesteads. Refresher training of CAHW teams will be planned in the dry season in order not to disrupt their agricultural activities at the onset of rains in May.The “one health” approach will be used in the treatment and awareness campaigns especially when dealing with zoonotic diseases in the target locations Activity 3: Conditional food assistance through food for assets and Food for Education; 3, 000 IPC 3 and 4 population in the target locations of Uror, Nyirol, and Kapoeta East are targeted with sufficient labour. This is to build resilience and assets targeting at least 50% women, girls and school going children.Main ActivityActivity 3.1: Community participatory identification of productive assets for creation/rehabilitationActivity 3.2: Distribution of food ration to 3, 000 vulnerable households (860 women, 320 men and 1820 school going children)Rationale: Emphasis will be given to households and members who are actively engaged in set out task and complete within the stipulated time. Activity 4: Support to 5000 households through Input Trade Fair (ITFs) where locally sourced seeds will be distributed in Kapoeta East and Uror Counties.Rationale: Voucher system will be employed for seeds procurement. Accountability with complaint and feedback mechanisms will ensure inclusion of vulnerable target groups. Done through Community Committees for beneficiary selection for transparency, accountability and fairness.Activity5: Training/capacity building for marginalized populations to enhance household income, livelihoods, and/or food sources in Uror, Nyirol, Fangak, Canal, Lafon and Kapoeta EastMain activitiesActivity 5.1: Resilient building in the communities in emergency by establishment of vegetable and crops farmer field schools mentor programs for the farmers.Activity 5.2: Fish production (net making, fish line) post-harvest training, value addition and marketing techniques for the fisher folkActivity 5.3: Public health training for the pastoralists for milk production, safe handling of milk and preservation techniques

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

CAFAD takes account that beneficiaries are human being with rights and are offered assistance with due dignity and are entitled to information regarding the humanitarian aid. This is done through consultative meetings through representatives where their voices and concerns are recorded. Any mistreatment or deeds by CAFAD staff against a beneficiary is given the highest priority and whenever the case are prosecutable the due procedures according to the South Sudanese law is taken.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes. The project aims to reach at least 50% of the target beneficiaries being IDPs who have not been integrated within the host communities and those affected by floods in the recent months (less than 3 months). These persons often lack means of survival in their new areas of settlement and the provision of lifesaving agriculture and fishing kits will go a long way in ensuring that they are food sufficient.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes. The project is linked to line ministries of animal resources and fisheries and agriculture of South Sudan providing networks and capacity building for the County line ministries for smooth takeover when the conditions stabilizes in all area of operation and the government is in capacity to offer the much needed support.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is linked to IASC Framework on durable solutions for displaced persons thus ensuring adequate standards of living at minimum ensuring access to quality and nutritious food and restoration of livelihoods and ownership of assets (farming inputs, shoats and chicken for women)

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

With the implementation of the peace agreement signed by the parties to the conflict in South Sudan, the conditions are expected to improve into 2020 though at a slower pace. The security situation is expected to improve into 2020 though the effects will be felt on the long run as basic social services will be provided for by the government.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes. CAFAD will work hand in hand with the staff in line ministries of agriculture, livestock and fisheries during induction, implementation and at the end of the project so that they can take over their specific responsibilities when conditions are stable.It is envisaged that the training to be carried out in the field will have carry-over effect where one farmer can reach at least ten (10) of his/her friends and teach them best practices on agronomic practices or fisheries.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

CAFAD is committed to providing a high quality, accountable and dignified response, which takes account of the needs and capacities of children, women, men and vulnerable groups. Together with the international aid and humanitarian community, CAFAD has adopted the seven (7) Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations, which means that CAFAD. meaningfully engages affected people at every key stage of their work (assessment, design, implementation and monitoring). These includes strengthening leadership and governance, transparent and routine two way communication practice, complaints mechanisms and prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). CAFAD will give pregnant and lactating women, the elderly, the disabled and the chronically ill priority at the time of distribution and ensuring that they are accorded the necessary assistance. CAFAD also recognizes that women and girls have right to own assets hence the aim to target 68% of the total beneficiaries being women, girls, lactating mothers and pregnant women ( Female headed HHs). CAFAD will utilize the position of male counterparts in the communities as champions of change and perception against stereotyping women and girls.
