Project Summary

The proposed interventions will look at addressing immediate food needs, livelihoods recovery and protection needs, promotion of alternative livelihoods opportunities. The project will deliver the food security and livelihood services equally to boys, girls and women identified with food insecurity issues. To ease the food insecurity situation among the most vulnerable, unconditional cash transfers will be provided to approximately 14,545 HHs food insecure HH focusing on women and child headed households, cash for work will be provided to 2,194 HHs, and conditional cash grants will be provided to 1039 skilled women and farmers to restock vegetable seeds and 4,139 HH to restock animals and 1725 HH to start small businesses. Different vulnerabilities of women, girls, boys and men related to their different food and capacity building requirements will be considered. Both women and men will be participants of the project community-based committees formed to assist in the effective implementation of the project activities in the project area. However, culturally men are usually overriding women when working as jointly in committees in some public functions that require equal gender participation. The project will make efforts to change such negative attitude towards increased participation and effective decision-making of women in the community-based project committees. The issue of involvement of community women is a challenges due to cultural barriers, however through awareness to community leaders we can make change hence we are continuing to sensitize the community on this matter. Under CfW activity, for women-headed and labour-poor households, work will be identified with the communities which is suited to cultural practices or physical abilities, such as conducting awareness sessions amongst the community, childcare for others participating in the project, tree nurseries, school vegetable gardens and demonstration plots. The need to segregate working groups for activities by gender will also be respected as will the need to adjust working hours around domestic and childcare duties. If a household meets the targeting criteria but has no member above 18 years who is able to engage with the cash for work projects, an unconditional cash payment will be made to the household. Given the existing vulnerabilities and limited income opportunities, the CfW activities will target households with much needed income to meet their food and non-food needs. At the same time this will inject cash into the local economy while contributing to community resiliency and improved agricultural production.

1 - Needs

Ongoing conflict, protracted population displacement, climatic events, economic crisis, and political tension in Sudan has impacted food security and livelihoods, and impeded access to natural resources and basic services, causing malnutrition, high morbidity rates, and disease outbreaks. Conflict and food insecurity in neighboring South Sudan continues to fuel an influx of South Sudanese refugees into Sudan, placing further constraints on government and host community resources.According to HRP, Sudan remains a large protracted humanitarian crisis and the severity and scale of needs remain far reaching. About 1.86 million live in protracted displacement. About 27,000 people were newly displaced in areas experiencing localised armed conflict such as Jebel Marra, Blue Nile and South Kordofan – 19,000 people were mostly displaced in 2018 in Jebel Marra alone. The food security and nutrition situation remains fragile and many affected populations remain dependent on assistance to meet their basic food needs. According to the Government’s Food Security Technical Secretariat, 5.7 million people were estimated to be in Crisis - Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) Phase 3 - or Emergency (IPC Phase 4) levels of food insecurity - ten out of 18 states have people in IPC phase 4. People in emergency and crisis levels are only able to meet minimum food needs with irreversible coping strategies that involve liquidating livelihood assets or diverting expenses from essential non-food items. Some are facing extreme food consumption gaps that could result in high levels of acute malnutrition, already above the emergency threshold especially for children under the age of 5.FEWSNET Sep 2019 indicated that IDPs and poor households in SPLM-N-controlled areas of South Kordofan and Jebel Marra in Darfur continue to face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) food security outcomes during the lean season in September. Harvests starting in October should improve food security outcomes in these areas, which will be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Stressed (IPC Phase 2), while most other areas will be in Minimal (IPC Phase 1) or Stressed (IPC Phase 2) through at least January 2020. FEWSNET reported that overall, the food security situation started to improve seasonally during October 2019 relative to the peak lean season period of September 2019 due to the increased availability of own households' production of earlier maturing crops from the ongoing 2019/20 summer season, in-kind payments from agricultural labor and market purchase with relative stability and/or decline of staple food prices. However, the persistent poor macroeconomic conditions and continued above-average prices of essential food and non-food items during this year are driving worse food security outcomes than are typical during the start of the harvest period. Additionally, the high prices of non-food commodities and services including agricultural inputs, transportation and medicine have limited poor households’ ability to maintain their typical livelihoods. Therefore, Stressed (IPC Phase 2) outcomes are prevalent in many areas while Crisis (IPC Phase 3) food insecurity outcomes exist in conflict-affected areas and areas hosting IDPs in Jebel Marra; SPLM-N areas of South Kordofan and southern Blue Nile; in addition to the northern parts of north Darfur, parts of North Kordofan, northern Kassala, and Red Sea states.The continued local currency depreciation and increased shortage of hard currency continues reducing the ability of both the private sector and the government to import required essential food and non-food items including wheat flour, fuel, and medicine. The government plans to end all subsidies on food and fuel by the coming few years. As a direct consequences, price of goods specially food will increase due to high transportation cost which will have more implications and impact in the ecomony of households with severe effect on poor populations.

1. Provide agricultural inputs and training to boost crop production under traditional rainfed cultivation. This includes provision of improved seeds of major food and cash crops grown in the area; it also includes provision of a set of basic hand tools as well as conduction of trainings and extension services for the farmers on the appropriate cultural practices in planting. Target 28,168 2. Support women and women groups to establish Kitchen-garden. This introduction of rain-fed vegetable production, usually grown in the house yard, includes provision of vegetable seeds as well as training and extension package. This activity is targeting women who will be selected by the committees. SC will also procure the vegetable seeds from the specialized companies. Extension agents from the horticulture department of the state Ministry of agriculture will provide the training. Target is 6,209 3. Provision of small ruminants to most vulnerable households. The project will also focus on income generation projects such as availing small ruminants for the vulnerable households who lost the stocks during the conflict. The most vulnerable and food insecure households will be targeted who will be identified by the committees. Each household will be provided with 3 heads either goats or sheep depending on the circumstances. Animals vaccination will be provided. Target is 24,835 4. The committee will also select and organize the beneficiaries (preference given to women). For each group start-up capital will be provided to run the business. Target is 10,348 5. 5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

A key tenet of Save the Children’s work is mainstreaming environmental considerations into all aspects of project design and implementation, as it is well recognized that environmental degradation can have major impacts on a country’s future development. The environmental impact of the project will be fully analyzed prior to the provision of cash grant for IGAs. SC is also committed in every sector of the project focused on construction or rehabilitation to use locally procured renewable resources. This not only lessens the impact on the environment, but also serves to make structures as contextually appropriate as possible. Tree planting will also be promoted as well as use of Fuel Efficient Stoves among participating women. FSL activities take the environment into consideration. To avoid clearing of vegetation cover when machines are used under mechanized crop production, this project will promote farmers using small hand tools. The irrigated vegetable production will be implemented in already open areas to avoid the need to clear more bushes and shrubs. The small ruminant restocking activity will not result in big increase in number of animals that adversely affects the environment, as generally small ruminants are less hazardous to the environment compared to cattle and camels.

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Under this project all proposed interventions are designed to protect children from harm including exploitation, violence and abuse. Save the Children will ensure all staff implementing this proposed project including facility and implementing partner staff have the awareness and capacity to identify, support and refer children facing protection concerns, such as family separation. Children with medical conditions will be supported to access required health services that can save their lives. Planned vaccination and treatment activities will be implemented following safety procedures as per protocols. SC staff are accountable to follow the child safeguarding policy and code of conduct. The staff internal policy strives to prevent any harm to children resulting from our programs, including sexual exploitation and abuse.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Accountability to affected communities - SC currently has a functional Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) which collects feedback through FGDs, individual interviews and a dedicated call in number. Complaints/feedback are captured and shared on a regular basis through a CRM database. In accordance with SC protocols complaints are grouped in 6 categories captured which are - request for information, request for assistance, minor dissatisfaction with activities, major dissatisfaction with activities, breaches of Save the Children's Code of Conduct and/or Child Safeguarding Policy and Allegations of child abuse or sexual exploitation of beneficiaries by non-Save the Children staff or representatives. Depending with the type of complaint some will immediately be closed through sharing required information, whilst for some an investigation will be undertaken to verify the facts before a concrete decision is made. Strict confidentiality principles will be adhered too. Based on investigation findings cases will be concluded accordingly and feedback provided to beneficiaries requiring it.
