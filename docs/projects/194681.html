Project Summary

According to the South Sudan HNO 2022, it is estimated that 6.1 million people will be impacted by inadequate or lack of access to safe water and improved sanitation. Presently, only 39% of the total population of South Sudan have reported to meet their water needs – a figure which drops to 34% in rural areas. Statistics which are expected to significantly impact standards of living and wellbeing.
In line with the exacerbated WASH needs with the COVID-19 pandemic, ACTED will implement a variety of WASH activities in 2022 including WASH infrastructure and rehabilitation, hygiene promotion activities, and supporting communities in prevention against diseases such as COVID-19 and Ebola. The response will be in line with the following cluster objectives:
CO 2: Provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties
CO 3: WASH specific activities for GBV mitigation
CO 4: Community members exposure to environmental hazards is reduced through the construction of disaster-resistant structures and infrastructure

1 - Needs

South Sudan is characterized by climate change-related hazard, dry spells and seasonal floods, leading to chronic food insecurity and stress over resources. The latest South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022 clearly states high needs for WASH assistance since more that more than half of the population have inadequate or lack of access to safe water and sanitation facilities, representing 6.1 million people, out of which 50% are women, 54% of children and 15% persons with disability. Presently, only 39% of the total population of South Sudan have reported to meet their water needs – a figure that drops to 34% in rural areas, in majority due to lack of access to water, forcing households to rely on surface water as main sources of water, which means people consume mostly untreated water from rivers and puddles, increasing their exposure to water diseases. This situation, along with the lack of sanitation facilities all across the country, rises the public health risks. Indeed, it is commonly observed that, when sanitation facilities such as lack of clean water and soap, or when latrines pits seep into waterways, population have to resort to negative coping mechanisms, such as open defecation and poor hygiene habits, resulting in exacerbated sanitary risks and spread of diseases, in particular the ongoing outbreaks of Covid-19 and Ebola. The recent flooding observed across South Sudan between August and October 2022 have severely impacted the WASH services: infrastructures can be damaged, submerged or destroyed by the floods, while floods also cause important disruptions of livelihoods and limit humanitarian access to remote regions of the country with high WASH needs. Likewise, flooding will also compel movements of displaced families into already WASH-strained populated areas, further exacerbating the situation by raising prices of WASH goods and significantly increasing demand without the ability to improve supply and services. The limited access to WASH services renders all segments of the population, including internally displaced persons (IDPs), host community members, returnees from internal displacement and refugees, all particularly vulnerable to increased sanitary and protection risks. In several target states such as Jonglei and Upper Nile, 33% of households travel for more than 30 minutes to collect water, women and girls reported to be particularly exposed to sexual and gender-based violence risks: 12% of women and girls assessed have reported avoided collecting water by themselves at water points due to fear of such risks.Thus, in line with the worsen WASH needs combined with the COVID-19 pandemic and the recent Ebola cases reported at the border with Uganda, ACTED will implement a variety of WASH activities in 2023 including WASH infrastructures and rehabilitation, hygiene promotion activities, and supporting public health risks prevention, including a mobile response in case of massive displacements, as per the following cluster’s objectives: CO 2: Provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 CountiesCO 3: WASH specific activities for GBV mitigationCO 4: Community members exposure to environmental hazards is reduced through the construction of disaster-resistant structures and infrastructure

2 - Project activities

ACTED will implement the following activities to increase vulnerable populations’ access to WASH services:- Construction of boreholes - Rehabilitation of non-functional boreholes and solar upgrading- Rehabilitation/repair of handpumps- Rehabilitation of water points (quick impact projects)- Emergency latrine construction and decommissioning- Distribution of core WASH items- Construction of haffirs for water storage- Hygiene promotion sessions/campaigns including COVID-19 awareness- Formation and training of COVID-19 committees- Conducting safety audit and risk mitigation assessments to identify the key WASH resources, facilities, needs and service provision gaps- Installation of handwashing points- Establishing COVID-19 tracking system and service monitoring mechanism- Establishing and training Water Committees Users- Provision of Solid Waste Management Assistance - Construction of shallow wells- Rehabilitation and construction of spring wells

3 - Project indicators

ACTED will utilize the following WASH cluster indicators to monitor the progress of the project:- Number of beneficiaries at risk of WASH-related diseases reached with safe access to semi- or permanent water- Number of beneficiaries at risk of WASH-related diseases reached with hygiene promotion- Number of beneficiaries reached with safe access to temporary or semi-permanent water- Number of beneficiaries reached with safe access to temporary or semi-permanent sanitation- Number of beneficiaries reached with hygiene promotion- Number of beneficiaries in communities showing high prevalence of GAM and high WASH vulnerabilities reached with safe access to (semi-)permanent water- Number of beneficiaries in communities showing high prevalence of GAM and high WASH vulnerabilities reached with hygiene promotion

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

As per its standard operating procedures for CRM, throughout the project, ACTED will ensure that multiple channels of communication (e.g.: help desk, staff registering complaints, hotline etc,) are available for ACTED to receive feedback from communities including the most vulnerable members such as individuals living with disabilities and individuals who are not literate. Furthermore, ACTED will have at least one women staff member in help desks to encourage women to register their feedback to ACTED. In compliance with the CRPD, a consistent and systematic approach to disability inclusion will be ensured at each stage of the project management cycle. Disability and inclusion will be mainstreamed across the programming to prevent and mitigate barriers to PwD and other individuals with mobility concerns into participating to the activities. As such, specific consultations with the abovementioned groups will be organized to ensure their capacity to raise their views and globally enhance their inclusion. Accessibility will be assessed and monitored and awareness of PwDs rights will be promoted through the protection awareness campaigns. The protection case management will additionally allow partners to respond to their specific needs as appropriate, on a case-by-case basis and with referrals to specialized service providers.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

As part of its on-going efforts to improve accountability to beneficiaries, ACTED will establish a complaint and response mechanism (CRM), allowing beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback and receive a response in a timely manner. At the beginning and during the implementation of the project, targeted communities will be sensitized on the CRM. A range of relevant communication modalities will be made available to the targeted communities to ensure the mechanism is accessible, taking into account the specificities of the local context and culture, as well as the project resources and beneficiaries’ vulnerabilities and preferences. The most commonly used means include complaints desks and boxes, community-based committees and submission of a complaint directly to the project staff in the field. All accountability staff will also be thoroughly trained in terms of referral pathways for protection cases and a strict – but interactive – distinction between protection and accountability services will be maintained throughout the intervention. Complaints will be confidentially recorded and compiled by the CRM focal points in the field and reported each month at a country-level. ACTED will ensure that follow up is conducted by the relevant departments. Standardized CRM tools, including set SOPs and sensitivity levels will be utilized. Beneficiaries’ feedback will continuously inform the partners actions throughout the entire intervention, allowing to adjust and adapt the intervention according to the needs and priorities of beneficiaries. The CRM will also ensure accountability to beneficiaries and build trust between the partners and the community, as well as improve the quality of the programmes. Moreover, the implementation will rely on community participation with continuous engagement of all its member. ACTED will pay particular attention to the specific needs of vulnerable groups, including women and adolescent girls, elderly and persons with disabilities as they tend to be disproportionately exposed to additional protection risks. These groups also tend to be excluded from decision making and have limited access to services due to deeply rooted gender imbalances. Similarly, the specific needs of people with disabilities and elderly are often overlooked making them more vulnerable and exposed to protection risks when disaster or displacement occur as they may face mobility difficulties.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

During sensitization of beneficiaries on CRM mechanism, ACTED informs beneficiaries about PSEA and provides clear instructions on how to report such cases utilizing CRM present at the site of implementation. ACTED explains how such sensitive cases are handled to encourage reporting.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

ACTED’s independent AMEU will continuously register the complaints and feedback of the target population and insert them into ACTED South Sudan’s learning database. The findings will be discussed at least on a monthly basis with the project implementation and project development teams to ensure that the feedback is incorporated into the ongoing project as well as for the design of upcoming projects.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

ACTED counts on a robust internal mechanism to combat and prevent child safeguarding and PSEA. All ACTED staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct which includes ACTED’s Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse Policy and Child Protection Policy. The staff will receive refresher trainings on these policies on a regular basis. ACTED will take the necessary measures to ensure that the communities receiving WASH items will have safe access to distribution sites by selecting an accessible location. Vulnerable and/or marginalized groups will be included in community consultations to ensure they are included in decision-making processes and receive the assistance provided by ACTED. ACTED will conduct inception meetings with external stakeholders including local authorities to ensure that the scope of the project and any selection criteria is widely understood.

12 - Contribution to Collective Outcomes
12.1. Does your project contribute to any of the three Collective Outcomes, i.e. Improved Food security; Enhanced support for prevention and response to needs of survivors of Gender-Based Violence; and Flood displacement?

Yes

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

Climate change is one of the major issues in South-Sudan as flooding massively affect populations. Floods exacerbate the vulnerability of communities and force people to flee their house, increasing their basic needs. In view of the climatic situation, ACTED will ensure in each of its intervention to strengthen the resilience and self-reliance of communities in order to reduce the impacts of recurrent shocks. As an example in this project, ACTED will strengthen the communities to adopt strategies to increase their resilience to climate and disaster related shocks/stressors, included flooding, through participatory, community-driven planning and resource management.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

In its approach and protection mainstreaming activities, ACTED ensures that prevention and mitigation of risks related to GBV and gender inequalities are integrated into each intervention and focuses on empowering affected populations, particularly women. As part of the preventative measures and to reduce the exposure to risks adolescent girls may face, ACTED will distribute hygiene kits to targeted vulnerable and at-risk women and girls. The female hygiene kits aim at supporting women and girls in maintaining their dignity, psychosocial and physical wellbeing, which in return supports their ability to cope in stressful and potentially overwhelming humanitarian situations. The access to hygiene and sanitary materials directly impacts not only their dignity and mobility but also their health, their access to education, their community involvement, family functioning, and their security. Moreover, to mitigate the protection risks which, arise from inadequate and insufficient access to basic WASH services, ACTED proposes will construct latrines that will be gender-responsive; taking the needs of women and girls into account. Rehabilitating waterpoint and allowing safe access to these services within communities will also support in reducing the exposure to protection-related risks notably for women and girls and ensure all community members live in safer and more sanitary environments

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

ACTED will ensure that vulnerable populations have equitable access equitable access to WASH basic services at all stages of the project and will improve their overall health and hygiene conditions, with the aim to increase community resilience in the targeted locations. The project activities will be planned to ensure the needs of different groups – particularly those most in need, including women, children, people with disabilities, and the elderly to make sure that their needs are taken into consideration while reducing the impact of future recurrent shock. These vulnerable populations will also be included in community consultations to promote sustainable solutions that specifically address the concerns of these vulnerable groups. The purpose of this is both to normalize the inclusion of vulnerable voices and to specifically target the groups that will help reduce the needs in priorities areas.
