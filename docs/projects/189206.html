Project Summary

Medair will continue multi-sectoral intervention in its proposed catchment area and ERT intervention. The total targets for 2022 based on Medair’s mapped catchment populations and at least 60% coverage in all areas will be 3545 SAM admissions, 8787 U5 MAM, and 8213 PLWs. Medair will continue to integrate MIYCN at both community and health and nutrition site levels and will support VAS in Medair’s catchment areas when no other actors are conducting. The proposed interventions in the plan align with the strategic and sector objectives and contribute to increasing equitable access and utilization of quality preventative, lifesaving, early detection and treatment of malnutrition and program monitoring, analysis, and utilization of nutrition information.
In Renk, there has been a consistent nutritional emergency for many years with the last GAM measured in June 2019 alarmingly high at 32.1%. Medair is the only nutritional actor in the county and has been doing multisectoral programming—health, nutrition and WASH. After seven months of program suspension due to insecurity, Medair abled to re-enter to Renk since December 2021 and on process of initiating multisectoral intervention in selected areas. As effort of re-entry Medair conducted pre-implementation MUAC screening in five selected sites (Saraya, Anbayok, Geiger, Shemedi and Jelhak) in December 2021 and found a proxy GAM rate of 14.4% for U5 and 21.4% for PLW. Medair is hoping to start at small scale and monitor the situation for possible scale up depending upon access and security and to implement over at least a 1-year period.
The 2022 FSNMS+ estimates GAM rate in Leer County as 19.2% (SAM 6.1%). But, in the mass MUAC screening conducted in August 2021, the proxy GAM rate was 7.9% (SAM 0.6%). Medair is the major nutrition actor covering a catchment population of 41,200 in Leer County (6) and 1 site Luol in Mayendit County. Medair is continuing multisectoral programming—community health, nutrition—CMAM and MIYCN via Care Groups (CG) WASH, and MHPSS to address some of the underlying factors.
In the recent IPC classification (HNO 2022) Pibor was classified as IPC 5, “famine likely” with extremely critical levels of acute malnutrition. In 2022 nutrition cluster prioritized Pibor as one of the neediest areas with no significant improvement from last year situation. Medair continues to run the SC and set up multisectoral programming in Gumuruk and Lekuangole (est population of 55,126 (Feb 2021 OCV Campaign). OTP and TSFP services will continue be covered by Plan and JAM. As part of Medair’s health and WASH programming Medair will set up a community CG network which will cover MIYCN and other public health topics as well as ensure Mother’s MUAC is established and well linked to Plan and JAM’s services through referrals. Health facilities will also conduct nutritional screening and referral of U5 and PLWs as part of BPHNS. As no actor is addressing at risk infants <6 mo, Medair will continue exploring integrating MAMI with the health services in 2 PHCUs. To avoid duplication, Medair will continue to coordinate activities with JAM/PLAN to manage all the coexisting illness referrals. Medair will continue supporting minimum WASH packages and child friendly spaces and early child development activities will be promoted.
ERT continues to respond to emergency levels of acute malnutrition by establishing CMAM services to address the most lifesaving needs, in areas with high GAM and low program coverage. The RRTF in collaboration with the NC gives input on priority areas and intervention modalities in scenarios characterized by agreed triggers where other actors are absent or unable to fully intervene. The team will work with the partner on the ground to identify areas for capacity building for quality service delivery. The team will provide nutrition emergency services for up to 3 months and thereafter handover services to an identified partner before exiting.

1 - Needs

All Medair’s projected intervention sites are located in states classified as high priority areas by the nutrition cluster with emergency levels of GAM. There have been emergency levels in Renk County since 2014 and with the recent suspension of programming since October 2020. Due to insecurities the situation is likely to be much worse. The most recent exhaustive mass MUAC screening (March 2021) done by Medair, during the failed attempt to restart programming, highlighted the need for more decentralized coverage of nutrition services, as the coverage rate ranged between 40% and 60% for acute malnutrition. Currently, Medair is in the process of re-starting services following the well-documented suspensions of activities from October 2020. Medair conducted pre-implementation MUAC screening in 5 selected areas to understand the malnutrition status and found a GAM rate 14.1% for under five and 21.8% for PLW. As discussions are ongoing with local authorities and other implementing partners, Medair has started intervention in a few selected areas with emergency approach at fixed and outreach locations. Medair will need to conduct a SMART survey to measure GAM this year when access permits and it can be conducted safely with COVID-19 measures. In Leer and Mayendit, Medair currently runs nutrition services in 7 sites covering a portion of Leer County and 1 location in Mayendit. Nutrition partner UNIDOR is also present in Leer town and Mayendit running OTP/TFSP sites. Medair covers a catchment population of 41,200 in Padeah, Dindin, Gandor, Guat, Adok, Lual and Bow through a blend of mobile outreaches and static sites that fluctuate based on the security situation. The population has experienced recurrent displacement and disruption of services due to flooding and repeated waves of conflict. A mass MUAC screening in July, 2021 revealed a ‘proxy GAM’ of 7.9%. Medair continues to provide nutrition, WASH, community health, and mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS). MIYCN is integrated at nutrition sites and via the Care Group network. Medair-led household surveys, as in other locations, show a need for improved complementary feeding and dietary diversity in IYCF. Medair will continue providing lifesaving services in nutrition, health, WASH and MHPSS in up to 7 sites. Medair will need to conduct a SMART survey to measure GAM this year when access permits and it can be conducted safely with COVID-19 transmission. Pibor has experienced multiple emergency humanitarian situations due to floods and intercommunal violence for the past 2 years with significant impact on health and nutrition services. Throughout Jonglei State, the insufficient number of health facilities and staff is further aggravated by limited road access during rainy season, recurrent flooding, drought and outbreaks, and continued violence. The HNO 2022 report classified Greater Pibor Administrative Area as IPC 5, “famine likely”. There are high levels of morbidity and mortality in children U5, low vaccination coverage, poor IYCF and health seeking behaviors. As noted above Plan and JAM have been providing nutrition services in the area and there were no geographic areas needing outpatient nutritional services to be covered by Medair. Medair will be providing multi sectoral services--health, WASH and integrated nutrition in the communities around the supported Gumuruk and Lekuangole PHCUs while exploring opportunities for addressing the needs of at risk <6 mo children linked to RH services. Medair will also continue to run the SC in Pibor to treat SAM cases with medical complications and support a referral network with Plan and JAM. The Emergency Response Team (ERT) will be deployed for a period of up to 3 months to respond to nutritional emergencies through helping integrate nutrition services in health facilities, building nutrition capacity of local partners or scaling up mobile responses in remote areas with nutrition service coverage gaps.

2 - Project activities

Infant Young Child Feeding and Behaviour Change: In Renk, Leer, and Pibor, Medair will use the National MIYCN Guidelines to govern the dissemination of IYCF messaging. This will be integrated into each level of contact beneficiaries have with staff throughout the CMAM programmes as well as Care Groups to provide sufficient opportunities for knowledge acquisition and to promote behaviour change. Child friendly spaces, will be used as an opportunity to support mothers, in particular in the violence-affected areas of Leer, and Pibor. Management of Acute Malnutrition: Medair proposes to continue treating children under 5 years for SAM and MAM, through the provision of the CMAM programme (static, mobile and outreach sites) in up to 38 locations including up to 5 in Renk including 1 SC, up to 7 in Leer, 1 SC in Pibor, and 25 in ERT. Medair will use the 2016 National Ministry of Health approved CMAM Guidelines to govern implementation of the Nutrition Programme. As per standard protocol, all children with complicated SAM will be referred to an SC run by Medair (i.e. Renk and Pibor) or another implementing agency. OTP and TSFP will either be established alongside health activities being implemented by other actors (as is the case across all sites in Leer and Mayendit) or will consist of screening in supported health facilities and referring children and U5 and PLWs to the nutritional actors in the area (Lekuangole and Gumuruk). Malnourished children that require coexisting illness will be treatedat the PHCUs. The community mobilisation activities will rely on the development of networks of Community Nutrition Volunteers/Workers (CNVs and CNWs) in Renk, Leer and ERT as well as Care/Cascade Groups in Pibor, and Leer. Preventative measures to mitigate the risk of developing acute malnutrition will be put in place and will include activities of behavior change communication targeting maternal, infant and young child feeding practices (noted above), participation in the National Immunization Days (NIDs), as well as periodic campaigns of mass distribution of Vitamin A (children aged 6-59 months) and deworming with albendazole (children aged 12-59 months) when COVID-19 transmission permits and other actors are not conducting. as explained above, the minimal WASH package, health package (RDT and measles vaccination; provision of amoxicillin, and deworming according to the CMAM protocol, management of co-existing illnesses; referral to the stabilisation centre); FSL linkage and ECD activities will be implemented in the patient’s best interest and according to the directives of the South Sudan Nutrition Cluster. The Emergency Response Team (ERT) will aim to provide the full CMAM package in all emergency interventions as needed. The actual response will depend on the needs and gaps identified in the Needs Assessment. Emergency nutrition interventions will be rolled out in the following cluster rapid response phased model depending on security and accessibility of target areas:•Phase 1: High insecurity location with limited accessibility, day trips: MUAC screening at a central location and BP5 distribution •Phase 2: High GAM/immediate need to respond, limited access and absence /lack of capacity of nutrition actor: MUAC screening, provision of 2-week ration for MAM and SAM with deworming, key MIYCN/HHP messaging and follow-up visits as security permits•Phase 3: Secure location with access and absence /lack of capacity of nutrition actor: establish full CMAM programme, identify or capacity building of long-term partners for handover

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

A database is used to capture beneficiary feedback received through the various channels and response to beneficiary feedback will be processed depending on the nature of the feedback. It will be responded to either through Medair staff or a community meeting, care group meetings or discussions with key informants. Community chiefs and local authorities are regularly updated regarding any anticipated changes in programme activities and the Medair community liaison officers provide an on-going contact point for beneficiaries and other community members to provide feedback. Feedback can be used to improve on the service delivery to the community.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Through a functional beneficiary feedback system, beneficiaries will be able to provide feedback through direct contact with Medair staff, feedback boxes in all clinics and or a dedicated phone line. To ensure the project provides feedback to the most vulnerable members in the community existing communication channels e.g. community leaders, holding community meetings, care group meetings, key informant will be used to deliver messages.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

All Medair staff receive training on Protection against Sexual Exploitation and abuse and will provide information regularly

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Medair implements mother-to-mother peer-support ‘Care/Cascade Groups’ in all static locations, in which information is cascaded through communities, and feedback is gathered. . In addition, Medair consults regularly with community leaders and other key stakeholders to update them regarding the initiation of or new developments within interventions. Medair also keeps a suggestion box at all static sites to collect feedback/compliant from beneficiaries that will be analysed and responded to through the mechanisms mentioned above. Through a network of community nutrition volunteers/workers who do house-to-house outreach, communities are regularly informed about existing services (Renk, Leer and ERT). When the emergency response team deploys for an assessment, they initially meet with community leaders to inform them about the objectives of the assessment. Based on the needs found, , Medair will decide in collaboration with the cluster whether an intervention is appropriate. Local authorities and community leaders will be informed about the outcome. When an emergency intervention is conducted, continuous messaging through a variety of channels as mentioned above will ensure the communities are well informed—including through the use of CNVs going door-to-door.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Medair coordinates with protection partners to conduct in-depth protection assessments as needed. Mobile nutrition sites reduce the protection risks faced by women and children who otherwise would have to travel to distant facilities. Nutrition facility locations are informed by protection-related considerations. All Medair staff receive training in Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, and relocatable staff are trained in protection-sensitive programming as part of Medair’s standard briefing procedures. Medair has strong policy and inbuilt mechanisms to prevent and combat child safeguarding and PSEA. Through Care Group and Cascade Groups (specifically in Leer), women are made aware of existing pathways and service available to address protection incidents. Barriers to access to services are identified via FGD and mother-to-mother Care/Cascade group meetings. Furthermore, regular mass MUAC screening provides information on the coverage of nutrition services. If coverage is limited, the community outreach component will be strengthened. Mobile nutrition sites facilitate access to services where beneficiaries otherwise might be unable to access services. Medair uses a combination of complaint and feedback mechanisms mentioned previously that aim to maximize access for beneficiaries to provide feedback. Medair provides data management training including confidentiality guidelines.; Medair will always abide by confidentiality best practice with beneficiary data confidential unless requested by law. Impartiality/neutrality is Medair’s core value, Medair will always try to work closely with all stakeholders bwithout compromising impartiliaty mandates or negatively affect social cohesion orspark intercommunity conflict
