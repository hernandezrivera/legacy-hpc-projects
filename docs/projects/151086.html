Project Summary

The Project is aimed at life-saving humanitarian response by reducing household food consumption gaps in drought- and flood- affected districts of Somalia with life-saving multi-purpose cash. Additionally, ACTED will ensure provision of livelihood-specific and seasonally appropriate inputs for both rural and urban populations, including through formation of Agro Pastoral Field Schools, livestock restocking and support to community animal health workers, as well as cash grants to support Village Savings and Loan Associations. ACTED will also promote restoration of community productive assets and disaster preparedness. This intervention will result in improved household income, assets and community resilience and risk mitigation capacity.

Needs

Despite improvements following the 2018 Gu season, significant food consumption gaps and unsustainable coping strategies remain problematic in Somalia, where 1.5 million people still face acute food security Crisis or worse (FSNAU September 2018). Main needs to be addressed include: emergency support to improve access to food and basic items, tailored support for IDPs and recovery interventions to support longer term resilience to shocks.Immediate access to food and nutrition: Among the households surveyed through the Somalia Joint Multi Custer Needs Assessment (REACH, August 2018), nearly half (55%) reported inadequate access to food. Although representing an improvement over the 2017 JMCNA findings, still 32% of assessed households are categorised as having a poor Food Consumption Score (FCS), with Nugaal and Banadir showing the highest proportion (50% and 45% of assessed HHs respectively). Emergency interventions need to be sustained in 2019 in order to address the food needs of these populations.Lack of access to food and basic needs for IDPs: With limited livelihood and coping options exacerbated by recent large scale displacement due to a combination of conflict and natural hazards, food security and nutrition outcomes across most of the 13 main settlements for internally displaced persons (IDPs) have been classified as Crisis (IPC Phase 3). Emergency livelihood support: Poor households in the Northern Agropastoral livelihood zone, affected by erratic rainfall, and some parts of southern Somalia (riverine affected by flooding in April-May 2018) currently have little or no food stocks and limited farm labor opportunities due to low production (FSNAU September 2018). As a result, in the absence of humanitarian assistance, food security outcomes are expected to deteriorate to Crisis (IPC Phase 3) in these areas and will need urgent livelihood support in 2019.

Activities

Short term access to food for vulnerable IDP and rural communities: This will be done through provision of both conditional (9,250 households) and unconditional (13,800 households) cash transfers, selected through inclusive community-based targeting. CfW activities will be identified in consultation with participants to ensure nature of work is selected in consideration of specific gender needs. Local authorities will be consulted as well as the relevant partners and clusters. ACTED intends to target populations in IPC3 and 4 especially the newly displaced and worst affected agro-pastoralist communities. Payments will be done through mobile cash transfers, equivalent to 100% of the food component of the Minimum Expenditure Basket. Provision of livelihood specific and seasonally appropriate inputs for vulnerable households: Early recovery will be supported through the provision of livelihood specific inputs to both rural and urban populations. For rural beneficiaries, activities will include formation of 30 Agro Pastoral Field Schools, fodder provision for agro-pastoral communities, livestock restocking and support to 25 community animal health workers. For urban populations, activities will include provision of cash grants to support 150 IGA and VSLA groups and support to 10 farmers market association groups. Restoration of community productive assets and disaster preparedness: This will be done through rehabilitation of priority community assets which will be identified by the community through an inclusive process of consultations with Village Relief Committees, the local community and authorities. Rehabilitation of assets will be done via the CfW modality to ensure that beneficiaries earn an income from rehabilitating these assets. ACTED will also intend to support disaster preparedness through facilitating the formation of community disaster management committees (CDMCs) in target community to undertake a hazard vulnerability and capacity analysis.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Cash transfers provide temporary relief to food insecure populations. However, by doing so they do contribute to reduction in use of negative coping strategies such as sale of livelihood assets. They also help in restoring livelihood strategies. The multi-purpose cash provided is focused on life saving needs and concentrate on addressing the immediate humanitarian challenges faced by the affected population, however ACTED is cognizant of the longer term goal of building a cash delivery system that can eventually be adapted as a social safety net system inherited by the government to implement core-resilience and social protection strategies. ACTED aims at contributing to this goal through ensuring synergies with ongoing drought-response programmes, including the Somalia Cash Consortium, the STREAM consortium and BRiCS. To build ownership and connection, ACTED will ensure strong coordination with authorities to feed into and support the design and implementation of projects on the ground. ACTED will also support national government and Ministries in their efforts to build and expand their specific strategy and vision for Somalia, and support critical reflection on best practice through demonstration of impact and workable solutions in Somalia. At the local authority level, prior to the implementation of any work, ACTED will meet with the respective local administrators to ensure their support and approval. They can also support in identifying the most vulnerable populations and guide us to locations where other actors are not operating. Throughout project implementation, ACTED will ensure the transfer of skills tools and of ownership to the relevant community governance structures to ensure sustainability after the completion of the project. In this way, we will meaningfully contribute to both immediate needs in Somalia through cash based programming but also build capacity necessary to address strategic long-term priorities. Further the activities proposed will contribute to building the resilience of targeted communities.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Risk: Assistance and services do not reach the most vulnerable categories Likelihood: Low Impact: High Mitigation measures: ACTED strictly applies the 4 core humanitarian principles plus Do Not Harm in all its interventions. Through its regular physical presence in the target areas with FSL team, ACTED will build strong relationships with the affected communities. Inclusive community structures representative of the all the different component of the affected population will help in identification of most vulnerable and more in need households to be targeted with the interventions. A Complaint and Response Mechanism (CRM) will be in place and give the chance to every individual to present complaints and share feedback, including on beneficiaries’ selections and verification processes, which will be used to inform programming.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Risk: Age- and gender-specific needs are not taken into account throughout the proposed intervention. Likelihood: Low Impact: High Mitigation measures: Through Inclusive Community Governance structures representative of the all the different component of the affected population, ACTED will pay particular attention to gender and age representation. Involvement and consultation of all categories and layers of the affected population will help in identifying and responding to the food security needs through participatory assessments. ACTED will particularly pay attention to the protection component of the intervention through working closely with protection partners and ensure that livelihoods programmes are designed to protect and support household caring responsibilities, child protection and to promote family unity. At the same time, ACTED will ensure that both men and women have equal and fair access to work opportunities, micro-credit, cash transfers, agricultural inputs and skills training and receive the same benefits for their input and work.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Risk: Volatile security environment hampers project’s activities Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation measures: ACTED closely monitors the security dynamics liaising with the local communities including traditional elders, local authorities and regional state government representatives. Over time, ACTED has developed a low profile access strategy with solid security measures. A participatory approach is used to update security risk analysis and triangulated with other sources to provide regular security briefings to staff. Working closely with targeted communities, engagement with local partners and prioritizing community sensitization and participation, ACTED and partners build acceptance of activities from the bottom-up. In all its project, ACTED ensures that FSL staff and relief committees are representative of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, disability), to ensure inclusion and mitigate risks of conflict over resources.
