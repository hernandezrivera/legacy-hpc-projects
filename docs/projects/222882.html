Project Summary

This project will provide essential ES/NFI assistance, transitional shelter assistance and basic shelter repairs including capacity building to conduct shelter repairs and upgrades to 45,170 conflict affected populations. Based on identified needs, CARE proposes to provide comprehensive shelter support packaged to conflict affected populations in displacement sites, returnees, repairs which will include repair, rehabilitation and capacity building for vulnerable local populations across Macomia, Quissanga, Meluco, Chiúre, Muidumbe and Nangade.
19,684 vulnerable conflict affected households will be assisted through basic domestic items in displacement sites/resettlement sites, shelter assistance will be provided in areas of return or integration in places of origin or within host communities. CARE also proposes to distribute ES/NFI to 5,000 conflict affected households through partnership with the Shelter Cluster pipeline. Based on identified needs in areas of return and integration, CARE will provide repair and rehabilitation support for 1,700 shelters and provide technical supervision for community lead construction of 500 transitional shelters. Resilient shelter construction trainings will be provided to 900 local artisans/ members of shelter construction committee.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

This project will provide essential ES/NFI assistance comprised of standard shelter cluster compliant family kits sourced through in-kind donations or from the Shelter cluster pipeline to provide first line ES/NFI assistance to 5,000 conflict affected displaced families - prioritization for women representatives of households will be undertaken during assistance selection process. ES/NFI assistance will target primarily women who are the main users of household NFIs. To ensure this, CARE aims to conduct gender disaggregated needs assessments and promote registration of women as primary beneficiaries of aid with the aim to mitigate exclusion errors. The family kit is composed of 1 tarpaulin, 2 blankets, 2 mosquito nets, 2 sleeping mats, 1 solar lamp, 1 kitchen set, 1 IEC with instructions for the installation of tarpaulins on an emergency shelter and an ES/NFI bag. As part of ongoing activities CARE proposes to continue promoting recycling and re-use of single use plastics and related waste produced from purchased ES/NFIs and related relief supplies. CARE is already engaged in community led recycling projects such as tarpaulin wrappings to make sleeping mats, wooden pallets to construct drying racks for dishes, nylon ropes to construct fishing nets among others. Through transitional shelter assistance CARE aims to promote community led construction through provision of technical supervision for the construction of 500 transitional shelters. The transitional shelter model will be test-constructed within the targeted community and amendments will be made in accordance to community needs and feedback. This aims to ensure its adaptability to the needs of users in each community. CARE will utilize a recently constructed transitional shelter model utilized for returnee families in Quissanga and Meluco district as a starting point and build upon the design to suit the needs of each targeted community. During recent assessments conducted by CARE (October 2024) in Northern districts; the assessment team identified significant destruction of shelters across communities in Macomia, Quissanga and Meluco district and these shelters are in dire need of repair to host returning households. CARE proposes to support community led repair and rehabilitation through the provision of capacity building on undertaking repairs and rehabilitation, provision of standard repair materials for each family, provision of technical supervision to ensure shelters are rehabilitated or repaired to standard. Shelter repairs will also be utilized by CARE to bridge the gap and reduce tensions between host communities and integrated IDPs or returnee households – by targeting a percentage of vulnerable host/local community families whose shelters will also be repaired and rehabilitated as part of the project. Resilient shelter construction trainings will be provided to 1,700 households on shelter construction techniques – the training is aimed at equipping households with the know-how to maintain and undertake small repairs of houses in their community. Training participants will be provided with certificates at the end of the training with the aim of boosting opportunities to conduct cash for work within their community. CARE will ensure 70% of training participants are women who have shown interest and commitment to rebuilding their community. As the primary user of the shelter, the inclusion of women will be important in identifying and modifying shelter construction approaches to best suit users. CARE will promote equal participation by including, girls, boys, women and men as part of the consultation forum and to ensure that shelter designs are adequate for intended users. CARE will mobilize communities and ensure at least 70% of shelter committees are comprised of women heads of household.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

• Conduct rapid needs assessment to ascertain accuracy of needs in selected communities in Quissanga, Macomia, Meluco, Chiúre, Muidumbe and Nangade. • Conduct verification and registration of 45,170 conflict affected populations across the 6 districts (focusing on the registration of women as representatives of their respective households). • Distribute life-saving ES/NFIs to 2,320 (11,600 ind.) vulnerable conflict affected HHs in Quissanga, Macomia, Meluco, Chiúre, Muidumbe and Nangade districts. • Select and train community construction/rehabilitation committees in the 6 mentioned distrits (ensuring at least 70% of the committee is comprised of women). • Undertake repair and rehabilitation of 1,700 shelters in areas of return, in host communities and resettlement sites. • With the support of locally recruited and CARE trained committees composed of 70% women, support construction of 500 transitional shelters across areas of return and host communities in Macomia, Meluco and Muidumbe district.• Conduct training/capacity building for 4,500 heads of household on resilient shelter construction across the 6 districts (ensuring participation of at least 70% women).• Promote single use plastic recycling and recycling of locally available construction materials to mitigate environmental impact.• Establish accountability committee in targeted sites to ensure adequate collection of feedback and complaints.• Conduct PDM to collect feedback on the assistance provided and adequacy of intervention in targeted sites.• Conduct PSEA briefs in targeted communities and spread key messaging around SEA through established accountability committees.• Mainstream protection and gender, through equal participation by ensuring participation of girls and women in communities where shelter construction and rehabilitation will take place (through consultation. and inclusion of women in construction committees)

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The proposed project will adhere to the Do No Harm principle. This begins at the beginning of the project cycle, with project design being informed by various analyses conducted by CARE including a recent Conflict-Sensitive Gender Analysis, a district level needs assessment and community level needs assessment to be conducted at the beginning of the project to ensure a relevant and appropriate response and identify potential risks. CARE will regularly collect data through our Feedback and Accountability Mechanisms (FAMs) managed by the Accountability officer, as well as compliment them with secondary data, to best examine how intervention may interact with the conflict and social context. CAREs 5 FAM entry points include Accountability officer, Accountability committee, Linha Verde, Complaint Boxes, Beneficiary Registration/Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDM) and directly with project staff during activities. The results will inform any adaptation of activities to prevent negative impacts. Finally, CARE has robust safeguarding policy in place, with accountability holders throughout the organizations, ensuring safety and dignity are prioritized. In the proposed project, CARE plans to conduct a training on PSEA to all staff and key community stakeholders so they can identify, respond to and prevent/mitigate protection risks.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

CARE will utilize its established Feedback and Accountability Mechanisms (FAM) managed by the Accountability officer. The CFM which will be implemented through tracking from the Accountability officer and equipping of Accountability committees at field level, allows all groups including men, women, boys and girls of different ages, illiteracy abilities and other vulnerabilities to provide feedback at different points during implementation. The 5 CFM entry points include Accountability officer, Accountability committees, Linha Verde, Complaint Boxes, Beneficiary Registration/Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDM) and directly through project staff at distribution exit points. CARE will be using all 5 channels noted above but will consider any communicated preference by the target population through deployment of the Accountability preference survey at the start of activities. The availability of these CFMs ensures at least one accessible option for all. For those who cannot access the hotline or are illiterate, exit points, the accountability committee, PDM teams and direct communication with CARE staff provide an appropriate option. CARE has experience operating in the target districts and is currently (October 2024) undertaking need assessments in Meluco, Quissanga and Macomia which includes data collection from participants of different ages, sex and ability.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

During the needs assessment planned at the beginning of activities in each site, CARE will be able to identify and assess the needs of People with Disabilities (PwDs). This will allow CARE to consult PwDs to determine how assistance can be delivered in an appropriate and accessible manner. During distributions, CARE will accommodate PwDs to remove barriers to assistance. This includes a bypass system for the queue, registering an “alternative collector” to pick-up items and adapting the distribution site where possible to ensure they’re safe (e.g. removing tripping hazards, etc). By conducting PSEA training, CARE will highlight the specific protection risks PwDs face and how they can be responded to and prevented. Finally, CARE having Feedback and Accountability Mechanisms (FAMs), information dissemination and awareness raising will come in a variety of formats, to account for different disabilities (radio, use of images, speakerphones, etc.).

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Overall, the project will have a minimal impact on the environment. As this project focuses on material support through a variety of packages tailored to different vulnerable groups, aid packaging waste will not have a negative impact as nearly all materials/packaging will be re-used by beneficiaries as documented through CARE’s previous ES/NFI projects. For example, at the CARE warehouse, pallets are being reused to rehabilitate school desks and make furniture as part of IGA for communities in Quissanga, plastic straps are donated to local artisans for the production of baskets metal pallets and wooden pallets have been used as part of hygiene promotion to construct drying racks, nylon ropes have been used to build fishing nets and tarp packaging have also been used to construct latrine blocks. Bales packaging can be used to demarcate spaces in agricultural plots and home gardens (as a type of fencing) so that chickens and other animals do not invade the area. This will prevent beneficiaries from using mosquito nets for this purpose, as seen in the past. Resourcefulness of the recipients of aid and CARE’s own reuse of material/packaging in its own warehouse, promotes reduction of negative impact of distributed materials on the environment.
