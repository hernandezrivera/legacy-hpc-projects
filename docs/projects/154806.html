Project Summary

The proposed action’s main objective is to restore basic water and sanitation services to vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities in old Mosul city, Sinjar city, Sinuni city and surrounding villages, as well as to promote good hygiene practices among targeted beneficiaries. Ninawa governorate ranks the highest in severity of living conditions of return for livelihoods, basic services and social cohesion, with hotspots concentrating in Sinjar district and Mosul City.
The Water, Sanitation and Health Directorates are overstretched and under-resourced. They struggle to cover the urgent critical needs and are not in capacity to mitigate a sudden deterioration of the epidemiological situation is case of waterborne diseases outbreaks. French Red Cross (FRC) and IRCS (Iraqi Red Crescent Society)’s experience in the area and analysis of the situation led designing this project that aims to fill part of the gaps of the basic WaSH services and ensures a safe hygiene environment.
For water supply service, the FRC/IRCS propose to rehabilitate damaged boreholes in Sinjar District, secondary water network in Old Mosul and support vulnerable households to connect to the network. The action will extend the current support to the municipalities in maintaining solid waste collection service, complemented by waste collection support in the communities. Hygiene promotion campaigns focusing on personal hygiene, safe water storage and waste management will be rolled out for all beneficiaries with a strong focus on menstrual hygiene management. School WaSH infrastructures will be rehabilitated and complemented by a participative hygiene training for school aged children using the CHAST (Children Hygiene and Sanitation Training) approach adapted to Iraqi context.

Needs

The Iraqi Civil War (2014-2017), left Iraqi populations particularly vulnerable and exposed -amongst others- to chronical shortages of safe water, lack of equitable access to safe water, non-functioning sanitation facilities and difficulties to getting essential hygiene products. This situation has thus been negatively impacting the country’s public health and the dignity of people across Iraq. Ninewa Governorate concentrates the highest number of Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) (584 520 IDPs, DTM IOM, 31 Oct 2018) and Returnees (1,591,000 Returnees, DTM IOM, 31 Oct 2018). In this area, there have been improvements concerning rehabilitation of water treatment plants, access to safe water through trucking, solid waste management, operations and maintenance of latrines and showers, hygiene promotion. An important push is required to reach humanitarian standards for the most vulnerable population and an immense effort is needed to restore services as they were before the crisis. Restoring WASH services, education, and livelihood is seen by IDPs as an essential criteria for considering returning permanently to their original area. Currently, returnees to newly liberated areas have little or no access to clean water and WaSH services. Children, in particular, have been developing skin problems due to lack of hygiene and playing in areas where they are exposed to sewage water spilled due to burst pipes. Systematic lack of access to adequate and sufficient WaSH services exacerbate the risk of water borne epidemics. In 2015, the acute watery diarrhoea outbreak has affected most of the country and similar events are still feared.Cities such as Mosul and Sinjar still have huge WaSH needs and public actors lack the financial means and tools to be able to address those needs. As of September 2018, Ninewa governorate ranks the highest in severity of living conditions of return in terms of livelihoods, basic services and social cohesion, with hotspots located in Sinjar district and Mosul City. (Return Index Findings Round 1, Iraq, IOM, September 2018).Mosul, has a high vulnerable population density due to massive displacement (380,328 IDPs, DTM IOM, 31Oct 2018) and returnees (944,718 Returnees, DTM IOM, 31 Oct 2018). With the amount of returns, occurring nearly one year after the war was declared over, the additional pressure on already insufficient WaSH services has consequences in degrading the population’s health and weakening social cohesion between the IDP, Host and returnees groups. West Mosul, almost completely destroyed has a high level of vulnerability. Large areas of damaged water pipelines and networks remain to be rehabilitated, requiring organization to continue water trucking operations. At present, although rehabilitation of city water network and treatment plants are underway by the Directorate of Water and WaSH partners, daily availability of potable water remains insufficient. The water supply could not reach households due to the lack of functioning secondary water network. The municipality of Mosul faces shortages of fuel and trucks for waste collection. Waste management budget and focus have not been prioritised by the municipality in the efforts of reconstruction. Assessments by the IRCS volunteers have highlighted that needs of population are high in terms of sensitization and information on household safe water storage to avoid the spread of water borne diseases. Delivering personal hygiene awareness sessions to the population will also help protecting themselves from these risks.In Sinjar District, the number of IDPs is 24,024 (DTM IOM, 31 Oct 2018) and Returnees are 56,418 (DTM IOM, 31 Oct 2018). The needs in terms of access to safe water are important, with the additional constraint of salty pockets of ground water. Based on recent assessments conducted by the FRC/IRCS in Sinjar District, several water networks are facing challenges to supply safe water to the population, some are in need of rehabilitation of boreholes of the water sources, some are facing operation and maintenance challenges. In Sinjar and Sinuni waste collection services need more staff and vehicles in order to collect waste, household garbage bins are still lacking in a lot of cases, this increase the risk of disposal in public areas therefore raise the sanitation and health risks to the community In the aftermath of the conflicts, schools have restarted to function. However, the quality of education environment of functioning schools in Sinjar district and Mosul city are still facing great challenge to accommodate increasing school aged children with the movement of return in the area due to lack of space, equipment and water sanitation facilities.

Activities

Coordination with other actors:The FRC/IRCS will be in close collaboration with other actors in the targeted areas via cluster meetings, technical working groups, general coordination meetings as well as ad hoc bilateral meetings. Such coordination will on the one hand avoid overlap in the activities, on the other hand learn from the best practices in the specific sectors. Thanks to the IRCS, relations with local authorities will be facilitated, constant consultation with respective local authorities such as the DoW, the DoE, the municipalities will be maintained trough out the project. Output 1 The access to safe water in Sinjar, Sinuni and Old Mosul is extended and improved for vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities Activity 1.1 Rehabilitation of water supply facilitiesBased on recent field assessment and consultation with the DoW of Sinjar and Mosul, the FRC and IRCS propose to rehabilitate 5 boreholes in Sinjar city and Sinuni city to enhance the water supply capacity. The project will also rehabilitate the Khasrech and Khazrac secondary water network and ensure the connection to water network for extreme vulnerable households in Mosul Al Qadima neighbourhood.Activity 1.2 Provision of integrated WaSH intervention in schoolsIn collaboration with the Directorate of Education, the FRC and the IRCS propose to rehabilitate or construct water supply and distribution system, latrines for boys and girls, hand washing facilities in8 schools during the project.Output 2 The access to solid waste management services in Sinjar, Sinuni and Old Mosul is improved for vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities Activity 2.1 Support to Municipalities in improving solid waste collectionThe FRC/IRCS have been supporting Sinuni and Mosul municipalities since 2018. The FRC/IRCS propose to support Sinjar, Sinuni and Mosul municipalities to restore and expand solid waste collection in neighbourhoods or villages for the vulnerable population in need.Activity 2.2 Support vulnerable households in solid waste collection and promotion of safe environment In order to achieve a successful and efficient solid waste management, material and orientation on municipal solid waste system will be given at the household level. The FRC/IRCS propose to support waste collection at the household level by distributing garbage bins and by organising cleaning campaigns. This activity will be complemented by the public hygiene promotion campaign of activity 3.1.Output 3 Good hygiene practices in Sinjar District and Mosul City are improvedActivity 3.1 Public hygiene promotion campaign Public hygiene promotion campaign will be organized promoting better household practices of water storage and use, waste disposal and personal hygiene. For women and girls a focus will be made on menstrual hygiene management, and information will be given to boys and men.Activity 3.2 Children’s Hygiene and Sanitation Training (CHAST) activities in SchoolsThe rehabilitation of WaSH facilities in targeted schools will be complemented by the Children’s Hygiene and Sanitation Trainings (CHAST) with school children. The CHAST method has been proven efficient in improving hygiene practices of school children. The FRC and the IRCS are currently adapting the CHAST methodology to the Iraqi context based on the FRC experience in other countries. IRCS master trainers will organize teachers and IRCS volunteer trainings with context specified materials to implement this activity. Teachers training will be organised to ensure the continuity of the activity and longer term impact. Protection Risk Analysis

The action planned is taking the following risks into consideration: 1- Beneficiaries not able to participate in the training sessions or awareness raising campaigns due to unrest, curfews or other restrictions on their movement inside the neighbourhoods. 2- Access to the most vulnerable communities (IDPs, Returnees, Host Communities) restricted due to prevailing security and/or military circumstances.3- Authorities interfering in ways that put the activities on hold.4- The project is subjected to corruption on a significant scale. 5- Staff insecurity has increased due to political tensions and military operations in target areas.6- The French Red Cross needs to withdraw from Iraq, as a result of increased political tensions and insecure situation.

Mitigation Measures

1- No mitigation measures possible, because this is an exogenous issue that the organization cannot have control upon.2- No mitigation measures possible, because this is an exogenous issue directly linked to the political climate that the organization cannot have control upon.3- Mitigation measures possible: try to maintain permanent contact with local authorities and provide explanations to all stakeholders at all stages of the proposed and planned action. 4- Mitigation measures possible: maintain strong internal control procedures and involve all stakeholders and local authorities at each stage of the proposed action. 5- Mitigation measures possible: keep close and continuous monitoring of the context. 6- Mitigation measures possible: make sure that FRC national staff and IRCS volunteers are involved in the proposed action in order to ensure the continuity of actions, in case any unlikely event would happen, forcing withdrawal of expatriates from Iraq.

How is your project accountable to affected population

The FRC/IRCS works for improving transparent communication, feedback and complaints mechanisms which invite people to share concerns regarding the assistance provided. Information provision: To be accountable to the targeted communities and donors, project activity information will be shared with beneficiary communities. Participation: Discussions with beneficiaries will be done during project monitoring and evaluation to make sure that their voices and feedback are heard. Regular meetings with the community leaders and authorities will be organized, and especially with the Mokthars representative of their community.Feedback/Complaint: After the completion of the activities, a post-distribution monitoring will be conducted to collect further feedback from beneficiaries. Once complaints are received, the FRC/IRCS will look for the most appropriate way to respond to the complaints. The principle of accountability will be widely based on the sanitation and hygiene committee. It is a strategic choice to hire the technicians and the hygiene promoters among the targeted population. It should strengthen the acceptance and the confidence from the community. To mitigate the associated risk of arrangement and connections, they will be deeply trained to the humanitarian principles and communication with beneficiaries and the supervisors will come from outside the area.
