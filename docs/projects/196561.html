Project Summary

Over 2023 and 2024 ,the action seeks to deliver formal and non-formal education to 5434 IDP out of school children (OOSC) :2717 girls, 2717 boys aged 6-18 in 11 camp schools intents and in buildings built with bricks and metal roofs in the camps themselves. The proposed project contributes to the HRP Objectives through: -Increase safe and equitable access to formal education for 5434 crisis affected students -Improve the quality of formal education for 5434 crisis affected students: 2717 girls,2717 boys. The camps are located in rural Idleb, Harim district. The action will focus on access and quality.Each camp school will have around 494 children from both genders, in additionto155 teachers(35 Pupil Teacher Ratio), 11 head teachers, 11 cleaners and 11 guards who will be recruited based on a fair hiring criteria. Teachers will be paid monthly incentives according to days worked as tracked by timesheets. As a plan A, Syria Relief will deliver physical education based on the EDs decisions taking into consideration the hygiene practices for children and staff.However, home-based education will be as a plan B if an emergency situation occurred, where teachers will deliver lessons to children in groups in safe camps if ,for instance, if one of the camps was shelled or evacuated or flooded The plan C can be applied through the distance learning in which the children can attend online sessions over the smart devices. SR will provide contextualized and adaptable capacity building through trainings and improved learning circles to school staff as relevant and needed and further coaching and mentoring to improve professional development of teachers within each school, with the collaboration of teachers, school management and relevant education authorities .For formal education, the project will use the Syrian Interim Government (SIG)curriculum.For non-formal education, a BLN curriculum developed by SCI and IRC will be used. In the school year, provide inclusive formal education in the targeted locations: in case schools were closed due to any pandemic or emergency , the formal curriculum education will be provided through online learning for children with access to smart devices, or through SLP work papers for children with no access to smart devices. SR will ensure equal access of siblings to smart phones, through mobilizing parents and communities and close monitoring and follow up. SR will provide remedial classes in the summer season in order to help children who are falling behind their peers at the class (due to school suspension) in addition to SEL and PSS Recreational activities/sessions. In case school suspension continues, SR will provide 5 remedial classes (2 Arabic, 2 Math and 1English) in collaboration with the ED, in addition to at least 1 recreational PSS session and1 SEL skill session that will be done either online using online platforms for children with access to electronic devices, or through SLP for children with no access to electronic devices. BTL campaigns will target the 11 camps for key messages on the importance of learning.There will be classroom Observation for overseeing the teachers and support them.Teachers will form Teachers Learning circles in each school for exchanging the experiences and teaching CWD will have an access to education through including them in the schools among their peers. There will be ramps and reels for them in addition to special WASH gender-sensitive facilities.

3 - Needs

According to HNO 2023, ''75,726 children aged 6-59 months who suffer from severe acute malnutrition (SAM), one in four children under the age of five in the north-west and north-east Syria who are stunted, 2.4 million children who do not go to school, 12.1 million who are food insecure, and 92 per cent of people above 59 years reporting living with a disability. In 1,017 assessed camp sites in northwest, there are only 196 schools''. Syria Relief conducted a needs assessment in Idleb governorate prioritizing 11camps in Idleb which have IDP out of school children who fl ed recently from southern Idleb, Hama, rural Aleppo or when the conflict erupted from all over Syria. The 11 camps have 5434 children (3260 girls, 2172boys )aged 6-15 (including 19 children with disabilities).Children are out of school for 9 months at minimum. The assessment revealed that there is an urgent need to support the IDP camps aged 6-15 by providingformal and non-formal education as well PSS activities as well as emergency food kits in which SR will cover as much of those emergency needs from other donors.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks:•Learning facilities could be attacked: airstrikes, bombs,shelling, fighting/gunshots, arson/fire, looting/theft; kidnapping/arrest of a teacher or students, direct attack on teacher or student, intimidation of school staff / students or military use of the school.•Risk of explosives, UXOs exploitations, harassment that students encounter on their ways to and from schools•Students and education personnel may encounter UXO, crossfire, exploitations, harassment, arbitrary arrest and detainment, physical or verbal threats, etc. when they travel to and travel from learning facilitiesMitigation:• Reduce time/distance travelled by decentralizing learning facilities. • Consider sunrise and sunset times when setting learning hours. • Provide students, their caregivers and school personnel information on safer routes/times to travel. • Provide students, their caregivers and school personnel information on what to do and what not to do if they face different types of threats. • If appropriate, possible and practical promote caregivers accompanying students to/from school. • Engage community leaders/members and change makers to support awareness Risks:Teachers or other education personnel participate in physical punishment, act violently, bully and neglect students. Students participate in bullying or act violently towards other students and/or to teachers. Mitigation:Enforced CoC,training teachers


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Aid workers or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they should be serving. Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policies should be in place, practiced and monitored. Polices should cover standards of behavior for the organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and include accountability measures. Polices should address risks at the organizational level and the program level. The targeted communities and learning facilities should be aware of the organization’s policies and procedures. The organization’s staff and targeted communities should have access to safe and effective reporting systems including knowing when they will get a response to their report and having the right to report anonymously. Organizations should ensure a survivor centered approach that ensures the safety and confidentiality of the affected person. The survivor should have a say in the processes. A poor handling of allegations of sexual explanation and abuse can put the accuser/survivor, alleged perpetrator and others at risk. If you need support in dealing with PSEA issues, please contact OCHA who has technical PSEA experts.
