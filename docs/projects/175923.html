Project Summary

The “Increasing awareness on explosive ordnance risk education to enhance safety and security for at-risk populations of Magwi, Terekeka, Juba, Yei and Morobo Counties of South Sudan” project aims to contribute to provide safe access in areas with high rates of returns in Magwi, Terekeka, Juba, Yei and Morobo Counties. Support for Peace and Education Development Programme (SPEDP) has worked extensively through Central and Eastern Equatoria, throughout its history in South Sudan. SPEDP has been implementing Dan Church Aid funded mine action related project titled “Saving lives and building safer communities through sustainable risk education, livelihood and cash intervention in Magwi County Eastern Equatoria State of South Sudan” since 2020.
In alignment with the Humanitarian Response Plan 2021, the SPEDP Strategy 2021 – 2025, and United Nations Strategy on Mine Action 2019-2023, SPEDP will mobilise Explosive Ordnance Risk Education Officer to conduct Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) in project locations. The project targets Magwi, Terekeka, Juba, Yei and Morobo Counties, reflecting the needs and severity in line with Mine Action Sub Cluster, and aligned to SPEDP’s area of existing operations to benefit from logistical and structural support. SPEDP maintains GBV, Mine Action, Education, Health, and FSL in these areas for a multi-sectoral life-saving intervention. In line with the Protection Cluster Objective 2 (prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness), the project seeks to provide lifesaving EORE.
The project will train Community Liaison Team (CLT) who can provide safety briefings to returnees and provide explosive ordnance risk education and deploy them to Magwi, Terekeka, Juba, Yei and Morobo Counties where a large numbers of internally displaced people (IDP) and refugee returnees who are likely to return to their home villages. The project will deliver EORE sessions to 56,000 beneficiaries disaggregated as follows 13,500 boys, 15,200 girls, 14,400 women and 12,900 men. The total allocated budget for Mine Action will be US$ 600,000. The affected population at the county level was further disaggregated into the following three groups: IDP, refugee returnees and host community.
The project will implement the following EORE activities: Training of EORE teams; EORE assessment; community engagement / mobilization of women, girls, boys, and men; leaflet provision of integrated EORE and community liaison, linked with child protection and education to particularly reach girls and boys in and out of school; use of approved / standard EORE materials and age-appropriate/gender-sensitive means of communication; and advocacy to greater prioritize EORE as a life-saving humanitarian intervention, especially for children. EORE provides community members with the means to keep themselves and their families safe and to report potentially hazardous items to keep their communities safe. Training to CLT members gives the communities the means to continue to spread safe behavior messages even after the project is over. Through this integrated approach of EORE, and training CLT members, the project can sustainably prevent and mitigate protection risks.

1 - Needs

An estimated 8 million people live in counties impacted by landmines and Explosives Remnants of War (ERW). Over 79km2 of land is known to be contaminated, with shifting frontlines and historical conflict making it problematic for IDP, returnees and host community members to be aware of mined areas. The areas of greatest contamination stem across the southern area of the Greater Equatoria region, which accounts for many of the primary return routes for expected South Sudanese refugees in Uganda and DRC. 5 counties are classified as catastrophic (severity 5) which are: Juba, Canal/Pigi, Magwi, Terekeka and Torit. 6 counties are classified as extreme (severity 4), 19 counties are classified as severe (severity 3) , 11 counties are classified as Stress (severity 2) while remaining 37 were not classified due to the specifity of Mine Action (Mine Action Sub-Cluster 2021). However, the total extent of contamination across South Sudan is unknown and particularly in areas ongoing conflict as illustrated in the Greater Equatoria Region. These explosive hazards threaten the lives of civilians, particularly those fleeing active armed clashes. Because of this threat to physical safety, explosive hazards also reduce the ability of community members to access land, services such as schools and clinics, and natural resources such as natural water sources, wood for charcoal, and land for grazing. Since 2013, the largest proportion of incidents have occurred as a result of play/recreation, and more specifically has impacted children. The 2018 Mine Action Review conducted finds that South Sudan is not on track to realize Article 5 of the Mine Ban Convention, and recommends: South Sudan should make every effort to minimise the risk to civilians from mines and UXOs; Continued efforts should be made to ensure accurate reporting by operators of mine action data and recording according to IMAS; Greater assistance from the government and international partners should be provided to strengthen capacity to develop and implement effective policies and programmes. As of October 2019, there are approximately 467 hazardous areas recorded in the IMSMA database, which are either suspected or confirmed to be contaminated by landmines or ERW. In total, there have been a recorded 5,071 injuries and deaths from mines and ERW, according to IMSMA, as of September 2019. The presence of explosive hazards present barriers to freedom of movement, access to resources and services these communities need, and the delivery of humanitarian aid. Explosive hazards affect all members of the community: women, girls, boys, and men. However, men and boys are at an elevated risk of being harmed by mines and explosive remnants of war (ERW), making up 74% of known accidents in the IMSMA database between the year 2000 and June 2019 (data provided by UNMAS; casualties nation-wide: 74% male, 16% female, 10% not specified or unknown). EORE will ensure that communities and individuals are able to develop strategies and coping mechanisms to reduce the threat of landmines and ERW. However, men often are more difficult to reach with EORE: in many communities, young men may be away from their villages with their cattle, in other communities they may be focused on farming activities and may be reluctant to take time away from their farming activities to attend risk education sessions. The project will educate displaced persons, returnees, and host community members about the dangers of and safe behaviors to adopt when encountering explosive hazards. In addition, the project will train CLT members who can provide safety briefings to returnees, while also increasing the number of women in leadership positions in the community.

2 - Project activities

Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) is a life-saving intervention, especially for children. The dangers posed by remnants of war to children has prompted SPEDP to develop specifically targeted EORE interventions. Since the first quarter of 2020, SPEDP has undertaken EORE in heavily affected counties of Magwi, Terekeka, Juba, Yei and Morobo. The main objectives are to: advocate and communicate life-saving skills and education; and, promote safe behaviour among women, girls, boys, and men, to reduce the risk of injuries and death caused by mines. Explosive Ordnance Risk Education Officer and Community Liaison Teams (CLTs) will provide EORE tailored to the needs of the audience (IDP, returnees, host communities, children, men and women to receive targeted messaging and teaching methods). SPEDP will deploy CLTs to conduct EORE, with particular attention paid to targeting recent returnees and currently displaced persons who intend to return to their homes, by targeting areas with high concentrations of IDP and returnees. SPEDP aims to provide EORE to 13,500 boys, 15,200 girls, 14,400 women and 12,900 men for IDP, returnees and host communities in Magwi, Terekeka, Juba, Yei and Morobo Counties. SPEDP will reach all members of the community with EORE, however, SPEDP will give special attention to identifying the best way to reach men, boys, returnees and displaced persons likely to return, in order to make sure that they are aware of the nature of the contamination and understand the mitigation measures that they can use to reduce the physical threats to themselves. SPEDP sets targets for women CLTs and puts greater emphasis in encouraging community leaders to nominate women to be CLTs and encouraging women to take such roles. The project will conduct the following activities: Activity 1: Conduct EORE in schools and other community based centers for children and community members living in high mine risk areas; Activity 2: Provide community based psychosocial support activities and case management for girls and boys in extremely vulnerable situations; Activity 3: Develop and disseminate EORE learning materials; Activity 4: Formation of community liaison teams (CLTs); Activity 5: Mobilise CLTs to conduct focus group discussions with community leaders, youth, and women to determine what types of contamination different members of the community encounter; andActivity 6: Training to 100 CLTs to reinforce EORE messages and provide safety briefings to returnees and newcomers to the community.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project has been designed based on the protection and GBV need analysis. During the IRNA, emphasis, were put on; SGBV survivors, adolescent girls, vulnerable women, men, boys, and elderly citizens. The project will target both sexes (male and female) with gender and age disaggregation. It will integrate Age Gender Diversity Mainstreaming (AGDM) tool into programming and implementation. In line with the Secretary General’s bulleting of 57/306 (15 April 2003), SPEDP is committed to Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). The project will provide trainings and orientations to project staff on Programme, Participation, Protection, and Policy (P4) and PSEA policy. It will focus on the five working principles: 1) rights of all, 2) participation and access to decision-making, 3) non-discrimination and equal access, 4) accountability and access to rule of law and 5) transparency and access to information. The other factors such as cultural, gender norms, values and beliefs will take into account while developing behavioral change communication messages.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) will be set up and made functional at community levels. The CFRM will deliver a mechanism that enables the beneficiaries and community members to provide feedback and seek responses in relation to activities of this response and other development actors in their communities, in a manner that is safe, non-threatening and accessible. The initiative will promote beneficiary accountability and feedback and response mechanism to other development actors. The project will measure the adequacy of interventions and address concerns and complaints of most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls). Access barriers could be at following level i.e. within IDP, refugee returnees and host communities and age and sex.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

A complaint and feedback mechanism will be established in both Juba and field offices in order to register complaints from community and give immediate feedback to the complainers if it is a case that can be handled at field level but if not, the case will be referred to Juba. The project will utilize Community Based Response Mechanism (CBRM) as a community management mechanism that delivers collaborative livelihoods and income generating opportunities as a means of building peace and stability. The CBRM will address issues that may arise in a credible manner. CBRM will also facilitate community-based dispute resolution process to receive feedback, deal with complaints or disputes. The project is expected to receive and proactively respond to issues with the potential to impact community relationships. It will increase transparency and accountability of local authorities for fair, inclusive and quality service delivery to the affected populations. The project will obtain informed consent from participants and ensure confidentiality for project activities that involve any probing into participants’ experiences in project activities.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Views and feedback will be collected from the Community Liaison Teams (CLTs) from the start (design) of the intervention through key information interviews. The key informant interviews will conducted among the local leaders and community leaders over the period. All stakeholders will be involved in all assessments including assessment among IDP, host community and other interest groups. SPEDP will approach the principle of participation from equity perspective and advocate for equal participation of different groups. Based on the cluster reporting system, the project will put in place and implement and monitoring and reporting system, progress, best practices and lessons learned will be presented in cluster meetings based on the 5Ws formats developed by the cluster.
