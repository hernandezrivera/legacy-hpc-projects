Project Summary

In order to continue to address the burden of vector-borne diseases and the leishmaniasis epidemic across northern Syria in particular, The MENTOR Initiative proposes to continue partnering with stakeholders and communities to ensure that the most vulnerable populations, such as IDPs and those with poor access to healthcare services, receive:
1. Prevention of leishmaniasis and other vector-borne disease transmission within their homes. MENTOR will provide protection to 555,602 households through an Indoor Residual Spraying (IRS) campaign northern Syria of Idleb, Aleppo, Al-Hasakeh, Ar-Raqqa and Deir-ez-Zor governorates.
2. Raising awareness of leishmaniasis, other vector-borne and infectious diseases (such as cholera) and their transmission and prevention. MENTOR will run combined Information, Education Communication as well as Behaviour Change Communication (IEC/BCC) campaigns to ensure that households are empowered to prevent disease transmission in their communities. This will be achieved through leveraging the access that the IRS campaign provides and various other channels of communication, such as dedicated IEC workers, focus sessions and training key community representatives. This strategy will maximize the acceptance and uptake of IRS and sustainably increase knowledge and the capacity to self-protect in vulnerable communities, as well as improving healthcare seeking behaviour, which will in turn also improve disease surveillance.

3 - Needs

Leishmaniasis is a neglected tropical disease and an estimated 1.5–2.0 million new cases per year are reported from nearly 100 endemic countries. It is transmitted by the bite of a sand fly, which breed in burrows of rodents, uncollected waste, cracks, crevices and in the rubble of destroyed infrastructure created by conflict and displacement. The most common species of the leishmania parasite in Syria, L. major and L. tropica, use host reservoirs such as humans, rodents, and dogs. In 2022, until the end of October, MENTOR diagnosed and treated 51,080 cases of Cutaneous Leishmaniasis (CL) in northern Syria, and conducted 512,342 treatment consultations. Of the overall cases, 48% were female, 52% were males, 64% were children and 18% were children <5 years. Furthermore, MENTOR diagnosed and treated 59 Visceral Leishmaniasis (VL), of which all were children. A year earlier, MENTOR-supported health facilities and MENTOR-run mobile clinics diagnosed and treated over 79,000 cases of CL, which represents 85% of all CL cases diagnosed in 2021. MENTOR’s project directly addresses this need by conducting prevention activities to provide protection to the most vulnerable people in need and at danger of contracting leishmaniasis and other vector-borne diseases in accessible areas of Idleb, Aleppo, Al-Hasakeh, Ar-Raqqa and Deir-ez-Zor governorates.

4 - Implementation of activities

The IRS campaign will be conducted prior to or just inside the disease transmission season, which starts in March and lasts until October, with peak sand fly numbers occurring in September of each year. As IRS lasts about six to seven months, depending on the wall timing ensures maximum benefit and protects beneficiaries throughout the entire high-transmission season. The residents also perceive the benefits of IRS through a reduction of nuisance pests such as cockroaches, bed bugs, flies and mosquitos, which also transmit diseases, such as flies acting as mechanical vectors for cholera, which also presents an added health benefit. MENTOR proposes to use the WHO-approved insecticides ICON & FICAM for IRS. The campaign will also include animal houses and outbuildings, where sand flies and other vectors also thrive. In line with WHO’s latest IRS guidelines, a minimum coverage of 85% of all households in targeted areas will be achieved to ensure maximum protection. Effective awareness campaigns, the employment of local workers, the recruitment of female workers to facilitate access to homes, and printed IEC/BCC materials accompanied by verbal explanation during the house-to-house visits increase IRS acceptance. The selection of target areas will be guided by the latest leishmaniasis incidence numbers. Success is measured through IRS evaluations surveys during and after the campaign, as well as data analysis and recommendations after the campaign has been completed.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Throughout the entire period of any grant MENTOR ensures that the safety of beneficiaries and staff is addressed in an inclusive manner, i.e. beneficiaries and staff are informed and are given the opportunity to discuss safety and the reasoning behind safety decisions. MENTOR provides a thorough briefing to beneficiaries prior to the start of an IRS campaign, such as avoiding contact with the walls for two hours after the spraying, covering items that should not be sprayed, potential side effects of the insecticides and where to seek help in the rare case of side effects. The design of MENTOR prevention campaigns also reduces insecurity as prevention workers (male and female) are recruited locally - regardless of gender, ethnic origins and religious beliefs to provide protection within their own communities and increase trust and access. MENTOR also applies a mitigation strategy to ensure that neighbouring sub-districts not being targeted by the campaign are informed about proposed activities and the reasons for their non-inclusion. Explaining to these neighbourhood sub-district local councils why they are not being targeted has proven to reduce inter-community tensions. During the IRS and IEC campaign, MENTOR teams also make sure that no staff is alone with a beneficiary at any point to avoid protection issues and instil trust and a sense of safety in the beneficiaries. A tried and tested Complaint Response Mechanisms accompanies all of MENTOR’s activities.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

MENTOR has a Safeguarding Policy which incorporates a Code of Conduct (COC), which all personnel associated with the organisation, including programme teams and field workers are required to sign upon joining the programme. These policies outline the principles under which MENTOR operates, and incorporate the Inter-Agency Standing Committee principles on Protection Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, wider safeguarding principles, supporting policies and procedures including the Whistle Blowing Policy, and how these should be implemented in practice. As part of our accountability to beneficiaries in line with the Humanitarian Accountability Partnership, we notify all communities in which we work that our staff operate under a strict COC, the standard of behaviour that should be expected, and the reporting mechanisms available to communities and beneficiaries if the standards are not followed. The importance of adherence to the Safeguarding Policy and the COC will be reiterated at several points in time during the training of MENTOR staff (be it permanent or temporary staff). Our teams are also trained to note all attacks against civilians by armed groups or criminals, destruction or theft of property or sexual violence that they witness and to report these immediately through to the Country Director. MENTOR also ensures that no staff enter situations where protection issues could arise, such as being alone with a beneficiary, in particular women and children.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The main risks to the programme are access restrictions due to volatile security or uncooperative local authorities, safety of staff, logistical issues and wrong perceptions about the activities. MENTOR maintains and regularly updates security operating procedures that have been tailored to the operating environment. The security situation is reviewed and assessed on a daily basis by the Country Director. Likewise, all movements and operations are subject to a security assessment and a permissive operating environment. MENTOR will not place staff or assets at risk and takes all measures to distance personnel from potential threats. MENTOR security staff inside Syria continuously analyse the security situation working in tandem with the Country Director. MENTOR also works closely with the local authorities and communities, only operating in a given area when permitted to do so by the relevant authority. Inter-agency security awareness information exchange and sharing of the current best practices are also MENTOR priorities. To avoid issues with procurement, all items with long lead times and procured internationally will be purchased well in advance of project implementation. Suspicions within the communities about the use of insecticides will be addressed by transparent implementation of the campaign and explaining in detail how IRS works and what it aims to achieve. MENTOR has also been able to build trust over the many years that it has been operating in Syria.
