Project Summary

The International Organization for Migration (IOM) Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) is used to track the number, locations and multi-sectoral needs of internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and migrants throughout the country. This information is critical to inform humanitarian responses and development assistance. As of Site Assessment round 27 and Village Assessment Survey round 10, DTM has identified a total of 4.2 million IDPs and 1.5 million individuals returning IDPs nationwide. DTM also tracks migration flows and trends at 5 Flow Monitoring Points at key border points in Ethiopia.

1. - Projects activities

Site Assessments (SA): Site Assessments will be conducted nationwide in accessible sites that host a reported 20 IDP households or more through focus group discussions (FGDs) with IDPs. The assessment tool will capture information on demographic breakdowns, locations, vulnerabilities and multi-sectoral needs data and will be used to inform the Government of Ethiopia (GoE), United Nations agencies, cluster coordinators and other partners. SA are conducted on a quarterly basis.ii) Village Assessment Surveys (VAS): Village Assessment Surveys will be conducted in accessible villages nationwide hosting a reported 20 returning IDP households or more. The VAS will conduct focus group discussions (FGDs) with local key informants. The assessment includes the mapping of infrastructure and services to assess the socio-economic status of returning IDPs and to evaluate the absorption capacity of villages. VAS is conducted on a quarterly basis.iii) Flow Monitoring: FM is established across five strategic Flow Monitoring Points with high mobility in an effort to gather information on mobility trends. FM data is captured daily and presented in monthly dashboards. It captures quantitative as well as qualitative information on the number of movements, place of intended destination, place of origin, demographics and reason for travel.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

DTM works with local government counter parts at local level to ensure collection of quality data through the engagement of relevant government technical experts for the design of tools, site assessment, and data quality monitoring. Local government bureaus and civil society organizations (CSO) monitor data collection and data verification stating at woreda level up to the national level. Once data is collated and analyzed, the National Disaster Risk Management Commission (NDRMC) has the role of endorsing all DTM products for publication. The Government of Ethiopia also uses DTM data for planning purposes, advocacy, and resource mobilization.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

DTM deploys trained enumerators with the required local language skills. The enumerators are trained in data collection, data protection and accountability to affected populations, protection and are also bound to the PSEA rules and policies. At the commencement of every data collection activity, community members are always briefed on the purpose, methodology, and expected results of the data collections process. DTM also ensures the participation of various vulnerable groups such persons with disability, female-headed households, elderly persons etc. as key informants in data collection. Participation is ensured through focus group discussions to allow them to express their views and concerns. Beneficiaries also validate the collected data. There are also regular consultations with IDP committee members to gather their feedback. In camp setting where IOM CCCM is active, beneficiaries can also use the Complaint Feedback Mechanism (CFM). Further, DTM has a Disability Inclusion Advisor.
