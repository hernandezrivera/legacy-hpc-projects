Project Summary

This project is going to improve the access of 24400 children(13000 boys & 11400 girls) in 47 schools , 35 of them are in Idleb (25 camps &10 off camps) and 12 schools in Aleppo to quality formal and non-formal education through conducting back to learning campaigns ,provision of Social protection services , rehabilitation of classrooms and wash facilities , delivery of education supplies , provision of non-formal education, train teachers and education personnel and provide stipends/incentives to teacher as well as providing PSS and recreational materials and activities.

Needs

Basing on the NHO of education 2019 , The protracted crisis in Syria, now in its eighth year, has forced 2.1 million, over one third of children, out-of-school and has put a further 1.3 million children at risk of dropping out. Over 5.8 million school-aged children (including more than 100,000 Palestine refugee children) and about 121,972 education personnel are in need of education assistance inside Syria, 61 per cent of whom are in acute and immediate need. The provision of education services inside Syria is limited. Hostilities have resulted in over one in three schools being damaged, destroyed, no longer accessible or occupied for shelter and other purposes. Schools are reported as the most common building type used for IDP sites in Syria, accounting for an estimated 32 per cent of collective centers. Due to a shortage of functional classrooms, classes are overcrowded and over one million children required to attend double-shift schools. An extreme shortage and under payment of qualified teachers also limits the quality and provision of education. Children who are out of school face increased protection risks including child marriage and the worst forms of child labor. Saed Charity Association conducted modern needs assessment on 47 schools in Maaret Anuman , Idleb city and in the camps of Al-Dana and Harim in Idleb and in Afrin , Al-Bab and Jarablus in Aleppo and the key findings of needs assessment show that:1- 90% of the schools assessed aren't supported with basic education supplies and teaching materials for children and teachers , teaching aids , textbooks , social protection services and recreational materials for children and teachers .2- 78% of teachers and supporting education staff in the assessed schools didn’t receive any stipends or incentives and need training on CP , classroom management , EIE and active learning .3- 45 % of classrooms and wash facilities in the assessed schools need maintenance and rehabilitation and new furniture. 4- 28% of children in the assessed camps are dropped out of schools and not receiving education. 5- 85% of assessed schools don't have safety and security plans6- All the classrooms and wash facilities in the assessed schools lack the ramps for the children with disabilities

Implementation of activities

Back to learning campaigns : There will be 94 campaigns in the project , one campaign in each semester targeting the community or camps around each school. The campaigns will focus on raising community awareness on the importance of education and the education services that are available in the community. The campaigns will put special focus on female children and children with mental and physical disabilities. Key issues on child protection, health and hygiene will also be promoted. Meetings and information sessions will be carried out. Teachers, local leaders and others will meet with parents. Information will be shared through discussions, sessions and signs and posters. Formal and non-formal educationFormal education will cover 24,400 students in 47 schools will be taught the Syrian Interim Government (SIG) curriculum.. One teacher will be assigned to each classroom. The higher grades will also have subject-specialist teachers for specific subjects. The Principal of school will manage the school with assistant and the other education personnel, including the psycho-social support (PSS) workers, cleaner and guard in addition to three education supervisors(1 supervisor for each 5 schools) to provide the daily guidance and coaching to teachers and principals of schools Non-formal education will be provided to 12,200 students in the 47 schools for 7 months through remedial and catch-up,. The classes will focus on the basic subjects of Arabic, English and Math. The project will focus on getting children up to their expected learning levels and ideally lead to pathways to formal education. One Psychosocial worker will work in each school to provide protection services and insure protection mainstreaming, S/he will receive training on Protection Principles, Inclusive Education, PFA and case management training. S/he will provide PSS and inclusion activities through group activities and one-on-one support as well as apply referral pathways to link the beneficiaries with specialized services as needed in coordination with protection active members. Repairs Repairs are limited to light repair in 750 classrooms, common spaces and 47 WASH facilities. Repairs will be done with gender and disability needs in mind. The chairs, tables, boards and school equipment will be repaired. Provision of teaching and learning materials SCA will provide stationary for 24,400 students and 811 teachers twice during the school year (at the beginning of each semester), 24,400 students will also receive schools bags as well as text books. In addition to that, the schools will receive teaching aids in the 47 schools and non-formal education centers.SCA will conduct FGDs with the boys and girls in the schools to take their suggestions into consideration when procuring their stationary and school bags in terms of color and model. Operational costs The schools will be provided with drinkable water during the school year as well as with fuel in winter. To ensure safety of the students, all the schools will be provided with first aid kits, fire extinguishers, and anti-blast film Capacity building As the conflict progresses, it is increasingly difficult to find qualified teachers and teachers who have the skills to teach in an ever increasingly difficult teaching environment. During the project , 811 teachers in 47 schools will receive training on the following packages : Education in emergency standards and guides (INEE, EIE)  PFA, PSS and case management  Classroom management  Active learning and teaching literacy and numeracy Protection principles. Safety and Security The security and safety of all project staff and targeted beneficiaries is one of our Organizations' top priorities. Therefore, the project aims to ensure that the children are studying in safe and protective environment. To achieve that, SCA will provide the following in each school:  First aid kits,  Fire extinguishers,

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Child safety incidents in school due to bullying and overburdened teachersMitigation : Training teachers ,partners and local teams on Child safety, development of schools specific child safety policies; establishment of complaint mechanisms in schools Risk 2: Risk of children dropping out of formal education to attend non-formal education Mitigation: Direct meetings will be held with the parents and caregivers clarifying the importance of formal education and the role of non-formal education activities. Risk 3: Resistance to gender mainstreaming from male leadership structures. Mitigation : The project will benefit all members of the community and will always seek prior agreement and active involvement of the traditional leadership structure. Risk 4: Tension/competition over salary/incentive due to different scales, interruption of payments, etc. Mitigation : Standardize teachers incentives

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Changes in the security situation, particularly in project target areas, affects the safety/mobility/ displacement of beneficiaries/staff and results in being unable to participate in project. This includes the risk of targeted attacks and bombing of schools/ communities and the risks of mines, airstrikes and cross fire. Mitigation : Closely monitor the security situation in coordination with EDs and relevant stakeholders and conduct a security assessments. They will ensure that staff members and teachers are trained in safety and security best practices, including mine risk education. Risk 2 : Attacks on schools Mitigation : Alternative locations (community centers, homes, etc…) and methodologies of education should be considered to avoid attack-related risks. Risk 3: Interference of armed groups, including confiscation of sensitive materials (laptops, phones); Mitigation :In order to ensure that armed groups do not get involved in programming, education partners will engage with relevant stakeholders and make sure that the local councils and local authorities are aware of the projects and have provided adequate access and space to conduct activities.
