Project Summary

According to Human Development Reports (undp.org) The population of Somalia consistently experience some of the worst health outcome indicators in the world, the average life expectancy is estimated to be 51.5 years (UNDP’s Human Development Reports) addtionally Somalia is among the countries with the highest rankings in infant and child mortality rates of, respectively, 76.6 and 121.5 per 1,000 live births. The maternal mortality rate (692 deaths per 100,000 live births) and the fertility rate (average of 6.9 children per woman) are also among the highest in the world, 79 per cent of all births are home delivered without skilled assistance. Overall, 99 per cent of Somali women have undergone female genital mutilation, 64 per cent of these having suffered the Pharaonic type. Overall, only 11 per cent of children aged 12-23 months are fully vaccinated, around 19 per cent of children in urban areas have received all basic vaccinations, while this is the case for less than 1 per cent of children in nomadic areas (FINAL SHDS Report 2020). The inadequate comprehensive and reliable epidemiological and disease information from all districts makes it difficult to ascertain the extent of gaps in service provision and burden of disease across the country and effectively plan for response among actors. To estimate the health needs indicators, a combination of health status, health resources, and contextual factors were considered.
For People living with disabilities (PLWD) not only is COVID-19 a significant direct risk to health, but it has added to the mental suffering of the population, causing anxiety, fear and driving stigmatization. The nature of the pandemic and some of the measures used to control it have increased vulnerability especially to gender-based and interpersonal violence for persons living with disabilities (PLWD) in their access to care. Those most vulnerable to health risks are PLWDs and those with mental health disorders. Particularly People living with disabilities (PLWD) become more vulnerable when healthcare is inaccessible and of poor quality.
In the proposed Project TASS will respond to the most urgent needs for 25,000 beneficiaries including People living with disabilities (PLWD), The services including emergency clinical service provision, community health care and maternity (supporting ANC, PNC, normal delivery, Basic emergency obstetric and newborn care (BEmONC) services and newborn care, outbreak investigation and response for epidemic prone diseases and the provision of essential medical supplies. SGBV, protection services to support MHPSS services and CMR in the health facilities through case management, training staff on MHPSS and CMR and conducting Community dialogue on the prevention of GBV and Harmful social norms
Minority groups and People with diability, who are often omitted from humanitarian assistance, will be participate at all phases of the project cycle. The project will also strive to include all age and gender groups. To ensure staff have capacity to promote these while ensuring protection, all staff will be trained in PSEA, social mobilization and disability inclusion in programs. Strong monitoring will ensure realization of this with all data disaggregated by age and gender Gender will be mainstreamed throughout the project: data will be disaggregated by sex and age whenever possible and activities will be tailored to respond to the specific needs of women, girls, men and boys affected by crisis in three targeted Districts of Sool region. The needs of girls and women in conflict will be taken into account by tailoring specific training to the female health workers and by strengthening the referral mechanism to assist women and girls who have been subjected to sexual gender-based violence. The access to integrated lifesaving emergency health services will be particularly ensured for pregnant and lactating women, U5 children and PLWDs.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

There will be a deliberate effort by TASS to ensure accountability to the target population through open, transparent and inclusive project implementation and management processes. The target beneficiaries particularly marginalized and people with disability will be highly involved in the planning, implementation and monitoring of the project activities. In addition, proper feedback mechanisms will be set in place to continuously improve on the quality of programming and ensure that TASS is responsive to the community needs. A Complaints Response and Feedback mechanism with a hotline is already available for use and is designed through multiple channels to be accessible to all groups, including women, girls, IDPs, and people with disabilities. The use of this system will be enhanced by making beneficiaries more aware of its existence in the proposed project locations. Furthermore practical measures will be taken in the activity design to minimize risk of exposure to violence as a result of distance to medical services. Most notably, mobile clinics to remote areas will be prioritized as a modality that reduces the need for women to travel to reach high quality maternal and child health, SRH and GBV/CMR services. The health workers running the mobile clinics will receive training on GBV and mental health topics in order to better address protection needs that are identified in the rural communities. TASS will ensure that activities being implemented will not lead to or perpetuate discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect or exploitation. All activities will be tailored to ensure that they promote and respect human rights and enhance protection of women, girls, men and boys including children with disability. Specifically, this action will mainstream protection by ensuring that from the outset, it will deploy female and male local health workers. In addition, this action will also ensure that survivors of sexual and gender-based violence when encountered by the mobile clinics and CHWs will be referred to TASS supported health facilities where they will be able to access medical and psycho social support. In addition, the community members will be informed of the nearest health facilities where SGBV are available.
