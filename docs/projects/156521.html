Project Summary

BINAA will provide humanitarian life-saving shelter and NFI assistance to IDPs and host community HHs in Afrin, Al Bab, A'zaz, Jebel Saman, Al Ma'ra, Ariha, Harim, Idleb, and Jisr-Ash-Shugur Districts. The locations are prioritized as per the HNO 2019 data "draft" and according to prioritization of BINAA to the location as per the field team’s communication with the local councils and their field visits to some villages. BINAA will provide shelter repair and functional upgrades valued at an average of $600 for 1300 HHs (7,800 individuals) (1673 Men, 1759 Women, 2143 Boy, 2225 Girl). The repair works will enable the target communities to use local materials and work through local contractors to improve their living conditions, fostering a sense of ownership, responsibility, and resilience while supporting local markets. We will also rehabilitate 25 collective centers accommodated by 416 HHs (2,500 individuals) (537 Men, 564 Women, 687 Boy, 713 Girl) and providing safe and dignified housing conditions. valued at an average of $10,000 for each CC
BINAA will provide emergency winter NFI assistance (distribution of fuel and stoves) to 2000 HHs (12,000 individuals) (2574 Men, 2706 Women, 3297 Boy, 3423 Girl) in the targeted locations, and (distribution of NFI kits) to 6000 HHs (36,000 individuals) (7722 Men, 8118 Women, 9891 Boy, 10269 Girl) in the targeted locations, and In line with the Do-No-Harm principle, BINAA will include IDPs and host community HHs as beneficiaries where and when applicable.

Needs

With continuing conflict creating repeated displacements and reducing coping mechanisms the shelter needs are great. The HNO 2019 states that there are now 4.7 million people are in need of shelter support in Syria due to inadequate conditions in existing shelters, and the overall lack of available, adequate and affordable shelters. Over the course of the crisis, shelter and infrastructure damage has occurred on a massive scale, with the scale of rebuilding and reconstruction needed which goes beyond the capacity and remit of the SNFI sector and the humanitarian response in general. Against the backdrop of increased self-organized returnees in 2018, there are increased needs related to the repair and rehabilitation of damaged shelters and infrastructure as people seek more durable solutions. It is likely that these needs will further increase in 2019 at the same time, of the 6.2 million long-term IDPs in Syria, many continue to face a range of barriers preventing return. Significant proportion of population are living with at least one shelter adequacy issue. Similarly, significant proportion of population living in rented accommodation have been unable to afford their rent in the last 3 months. This issue disproportionately affects IDPs, of whom 64 per cent are renting. In addition, host communities face significant pressures in terms of shelter capacity and adequacy.According to BINAA field surveys, and consultations with local committees and beneficiaries who received previous shelter assistance through projects are being implemented by BINAA such as shelter housing units rehabilitation (HFP) for 1000 HHs and 4 CCs, and shelter housing units rehabilitation for International Organization for Migration (IOM) covering 420 HHs, also for a shelter rehabilitation project implementing by BINAA for KSRELIEF covering rehabilitation of 1535 shelter units, still there many IDPs live in unfinished or overcrowded buildings which do not protect them from weather conditions or do not provide privacy to the residents and this increase the protection risks for the residents especially for women and girls.Thus, needs are related to improving protection from the weather conditions and enhancing privacy and dignity (particularly for women and girls) through rehabilitation of the damaged houses and CCs in Al-Bab, Aza’z, Ariha and Jisr-Ash-Shugur districts and distribution of NFIs and heating kits in Afrin, Al Bab, A'zaz, Jebel Saman, Al Ma'ra, Ariha, Harim, Idleb, and Jisr-Ash-Shugur Districts.Furthermore, as the targeted locations received a huge number of IDPs as per the SNFI fact sheets and BINAA reports, BINAA will respond to gender and age-specific needs by promoting lifesaving NFI and shelter rehabilitation services to the most vulnerable IDPs and host communities’ households.

Implementation of activities

1.2 Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs and shelter assistance (e.g. winter clothing, fuel, winter-specific shelter upgrades; including cash/voucher for these items)Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs and shelter assistance (2,000 fuel kit), BINAA will take into consideration the coldest area and targeted it based on SNFI priority, BINAA will prioritize as per vulnerability criteria include length of displacement/hosting period, with families displaced/hosting for a longer period of time receiving priority; number of times of displacement; households with extremely vulnerable family members (disabled, elderly, mobility-impaired etc.); female or child-headed households; households with more than 3 children under the age of 5 years, or more than 6 total family members; and households who have not received prior NFI assistance in the previous 6 months. 1.3 Provision of emergency shelter (e.g. tent installation in camps, transit sites and spontaneous settlements with associated infrastructure, distribution of emergency shelter materials and kits; rental assistance, including cash/voucher for these items).Provision of emergency shelter (6,000 NFI), BINAA will prioritize as per vulnerability criteria include length of displacement/hosting period, with families displaced/hosting for a longer period of time receiving priority; number of times of displacement; households with extremely vulnerable family members (disabled, elderly, mobility-impaired etc.); female or child-headed households; households with more than 3 children under the age of 5 years, or more than 6 total family members; and households who have not received prior NFI assistance in the previous 6 months. 1.4 Rehabilitation of collective centers and transitional shelters (including: in-kind, cash, voucher, physical repair, etc.)Rehabilitation for 25 collective centers, BINAA will prioritize the overcrowded centers and unfinished buildings with the intervention. Collective center repairs vary between replacement of doors and windows, installation of partitions to allow families privacy, sealing or repairing holes and cracks in walls and roofs, repair of WASH facilities within the center, and preparing another part (unfinished) of the building to expand the capacity of the overcrowded center. The works will include providing disability friendly entrance and toilet facilities.for this intervention, BINAA will ensure that HLP guidance are considered and utilized. 2.1 Support to sustainably repair/rehabilitate housing and related community/public infrastructure and facilities to owners/tenants/host families (can include: materials, cash, voucher, cash-for-work, labour, etc.)Support to sustainably repair/rehabilitate housing for 1,300 HHs will be for IDP and host community families living in an inadequate shelter that does not meet minimum SPHERE minimum standards for square meters of covered living space per person and has inadequate privacy or protection from the elements, settlement planning and WASH facilities. We will target HHs that have light to medium damage but remain structurally sound. Works include doors and windows repair or replacement, partition installation, recaulking and sealing cracks and holes in walls, and WASH facilities repairs. For HHs with disabled persons, additional works will be included to respond to their needs by including disability friendly entrance and toilet facilities.Beneficiary HHs will be selected and prioritized based on the standard vulnerability criteria prioritizing respectively; 1) IDP HHs share hosting communities HHs same damaged shelter; 2) IDP HHs hosted by hosting communities HHs in another damaged shelter for free; 3) IDP HHs renting damaged shelter; and 4) host community HHs live in damaged shelter.for this intervention, BINAA will ensure that HLP guidance are considered and utilized.
