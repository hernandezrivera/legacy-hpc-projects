Project Summary

The project will provide immediate access to safe drinking water to 15,000 vulnerable population boys,girls,women and men affected by the drought and other natural disaster, OPRD intends to rehabilitates 15 shallow wells benefiting 15,000 men, women and children to enable enhanced the community access to safe drinking water. The project will also provide the target beneficiaries with hygiene kits as well as provide hygiene promotion to ensure community is aware of the water born risk as well as sanitation and other vector born disease. Through this OPRD will ensure to establish 19 water management committee at the villages that includes men,women and youths and rehabilitate 19 water sources to enable ease the water related protection risk among the community in the target location. OPRD will construct both IDPs latrines as well as health facilities and schools to ensure proper sanitation in the target areas. Through this project OPRD will ensure the project is coordinated across other sector as well as state leadership to enable have better and well managed program. At the Community Level OPRD will implement Community Led Total Sanitation to achieve the desired latrine coverage and hygiene practices using locally available materials through formation of Village Sanitation Committees made up of natural leaders. To achieve OPRD’s nutrition outcomes (15,000 households gaining access to nutrition services and a minimum of 10,,000 children between 6 and 23 months receiving a minimal acceptable diet), we will support and work through local organizations and clinics already active in the agricultural and health/nutrition sectors (community savings groups, farmer groups, community care groups) in each both Counties, prioritizing locations with high percentages of malnourished or stunted children, as well as where there are opportunities to link with other donor -funded health and agriculture programming. We will also hold gender equality mainstreaming workshops, and develop action plans to integrate gender equality mainstreaming into OPRD policies, practices, and procedures. Safe drinking water, adequate sanitation, and proper hygiene will prevent undernutrition and stunting in children by inhibiting environmental enteropathy and diarrheal disease, and
reduce child deaths. To address issues of Gender Based Violence in WASH, OPRD will ensure the rehabilitated wells are safe, well-sited , well-lit for women- and in schools provide the girl-friendly sanitation facilities with provision for Menstrual Hygiene Management

Needs

According South Sudan humanitarian needs overview (WASH cluster) a total of 2.9 M people are in needs need urgent WASH assistance in South Sudan in 2019, if these 1.5 M are in cattle camps WHILE 1.4M are in non cattle camps and host communities.2.1m women,children boys and girls are in need of protection at water point Unreliable access to a temporary water source and/or unprotected water is still an important feature of the WASH humanitarian landscape in South Sudan. Nationwide, access to an improved water source remains below 40%, with large variations from one state to another, however, Karukomuge and Paringa payams below 25% each. In most urban and sub-urban hubs and cattle camps settlements, access to an adequate quantity of water is often granted but water is unsafe and often microbiologically contaminated due to direct collection from the Sources such as wells and water pans for the animals. More than half of the population lack of access to adequate sanitation. The situation is particularly critical in Karukomuge and Jie payams below 25% and in cattle camps settlements in Kapoeta North and Kapoeta East Counties, Hand washing with soap at critical moment is practiced by only a third of the population as access to soap and availability to a functional hand washing point is limited. This percentage drops to below 25% in Jie, despite that these two payams were badly affected by AWD/Cholera recently. Heavy rain falls affected more than 700,000 people in 2018 affecting WASH infrastructures in more than 25 Bomas. Most of these infrastructures have not been repaired yet. Because of the floods and the chronical lack of investment in WASH infrastructure, South Sudan continued to suffer from AWD/Cholera in 2017 and risk factors for an outbreak persist in several hotspots in Kapoeta North and Kapoeta East.OPRD will continue to focus our activities on to most vulnerable populations in hard to reach locations; IDPs, people at risk of AWD /cholera, acutely malnourished and people in drought-affected areas in two Counties.

Activities

1.1: Provide safe and clean water to 2,000 drought/floods affected households (15,000 persons). Every household will receive 45 liter/day as SPHERE standard for 60 days. The selection of the beneficiaries will be done with the consultation of community leader1.2: Rehabilitate 15 shallow wells (10 with hand pump and 5 with animal trough) benefiting around 15000 men, women and children. The community leaders including women groups will assign the design and the location of the shallow wells. 1.3: Rehabilitate 4 water pans benefiting 4,000 men, women and children including the limited saved animals from the drought. The action will include: de-silting, increasing the volume of the water pan and protecting its areas from animals and human, in order to avoid the contamination of the water pan. 1.4: Provide water treatment, chlorination and routine water quality testing programme, in the 19 water points (systematic) and household level. Safe water and hygiene kit distribution as an incentive for participation in promotion of WASH behaviors such as owning latrines and practicing hand washing 1.5: Establish and train: (a) 19 water management committees (WMCs) at community and cattle camps level from the rehabilitated 19 water sources, composed of 133 members shared equally by men and women (76 men and 57 women) on water source management and water source maintenance to keep the sustainability of the water source. (b) 38 well operators shared equally men and women (19 men and 19 women) on operation and maintenance (O&M), chlorination of water sources and collection of revenues. (c) 100 community members from community leaders, community elders, women groups, and youth shared equally by men and women (50 men and 50 women) on Disaster and Risk Reduction mechanisms (DRR) and conflict resolution mechanisms. .1: Rehabilitate/Construct 200 gender-sensitive and flood proof IDP latrines. The action includes: desludging of the filled septic tank, repairing of broken floors, replacing the damaged walls and doors. 2.2: construct 150 gender-sensitive, desludging and flood proof IDP latrines with hand washing facilities.3.0. Implement Community Led Total Sanitation in villages around rehabilitated shallow wells to increase latrine coverage and sustainability of sanitation activities in both counties4.0. Conducting nutrition based workshops and nutrition assessments, counseling, and support services for pregnant, post-natal, and lactating women to optimally impact the 1,000-day window as a part of HIV/AIDS and prevention of mother-to-child transmission programming. Children under 5's and lactating mothers to be targeted 5.- . A gender analysis to inform specific design of and identify Sanitation needs of women, girls, men, boys, the disabled and persons with disabilities.6.0. 100 male and female community hygiene promoters trained and organizing hygiene promotion sessions and campaigns for dissemination of key hygiene messages to male and female IDPs.7. Branding of WASH facilities with hygiene promotion and gender sensitive messages 8. Implement Sanitation in the wider community using Community Led Total Sanitation to improve households access to basic sanitation through use of locally available materials and emergence of natural leaders 9. Promotion of Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) among women and young girls to enhance their dignity

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

The project will provide beneficiaries with information on what is the intention of the project, who are being targeted by the project in the community, how are they going to benefit from the project, what assumptions have been made prior to meeting the beneficiaries, how does the beneficiary benefit after the project closure, how can the beneficiaries own the project, what rights do the beneficiaries have on the project, who are the actors that form the community referral network that will support the activities post the project life. Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

OPRD will use innovative and experiential behavior change communication (BCC) approaches to promote essential WASH and nutrition actions for women and children and to stimulate demand for improved water and sanitation. We will engage women as partners in planning and monitoring to ensure that OPRD meets household and community needs, tailor interventions to engage women, and look for opportunities to further advance women as entrepreneurs and WASH leaders. We will Integrate nutrition and WASH in training, job aids, communication, and outreach Establish demonstration sites for hands-on WASH and nutrition near rehabilitated Shallow wells and in schools that will be targeted. OPRD will engage households that have already invested in sanitation improvements to pinpoint the messages, tools, available services, and channels of communication and means of triggering that will be most effective in motivating their investment. We will use these findings to stimulate demand with households that have similar demographics and identify appropriate technical solutions in sanitation that meet their need. The messages will be tailored for specific age groups and gender by defining clearly the target behaviours in WASH we want to address. Secondly, we will discuss who the critical target groups are, keeping in mind the chosen behaviours. Thirdly, we will analyse the potential barriers and drivers for the target groups to change their behavior. Fourthly, we will develop key messages to each target group based on their barriers and drivers. Finally, we will select the communication strategies and identified tools to communicate effectively.The messages will be used for motivating people to operate and maintain water supply schemes through the rehabilitation of shallow wells.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project is based a protection risk analysis generated from the assessment report that was conducted by WFP IPC earlier this year during the assessment. We analysed the protection risks women and girls face when they are involved in their daily chores that include fetching water and firewood in the bushes and even when they go open defecation. This was basically among the Jie IDPs and the host community in Kapoeta East and Kapoeta South Counties. The worst of it all is when they have to gather wild fruits in deep forests and bushes. The communities have some boys concerned with community security who patrol the outskirts of their villages waiting for attackers. OPRD will work with security agencies and constant consultations will be done about the security situations of the project area. An effective humanitarian response to such situations will be informed by an analysis of the threats and risks that people face. Appropriate steps will be taken to minimize those threats and risks, and ensure full respect of the rights of all people affected by disaster or armed conflict. OPRD will ensure our project activities do not lead to or perpetuate discrimination,abuse, neglect and violence.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Dignity of the beneficiaries is well catered for throughout the project from the distribution of the supplies to the distribution monitoring visits and interviews. Sex disaggregated data will always be produced to help monitoring support to persons of different genders to reduced Gender Based Violence at water points .The project will measure the changes in girls’ confidence, self-efficacy and school attendance when it is safer, easier, and more dignified for them to use the toilet and address their Menstrual Hygiene Management needs. it will also measure the reduction of women’s time spent on caretaking responsibilities as a result of the decreased burden of childhood disease, leading to more time for other opportunities.
