Project Summary

The Danish Refugee Council is dedicated to preventing and responding to Gender-Based Violence (GBV) by addressing its root causes and providing comprehensive support to survivors. DRC’s approach combines specialized GBV case management with non-specialized psychosocial support, promoting community engagement in accountable practices, and making referrals to specialized services. This includes initiatives like distributing dignity kits and offering regular skills training for GBV case workers to understand risks and manage cases effectively. Additionally, non-protection staff receive training to mainstream GBV considerations, recognize potential cases, and make appropriate referrals.
DRC’s work challenges harmful attitudes and practices that contribute to GBV, with a focus on sustainable change through community involvement. Collaborating with the GBV Sub-Sector and other protection sectors, including child protection and general protection, DRC builds community resilience against GBV. Active participation in the GBV Sub-Sector’s safety audits ensures that DRC’s programs align with guiding GBV principles and remain relevant, safe, and accessible. Through protection monitoring and quarterly reporting, DRC documents key protection risks, with a focus on GBV, allowing for the disaggregation of data by age, gender, and diversity. This Age, Gender, and Diversity (AGD) approach ensures that DRC meets the needs of all survivors, including those from marginalized communities, such as the hijra population and individuals with disabilities.
In the areas ,Camp 6, Camp 10,Camp 26 camp 27 and Palongkhali, Rajapalong—DRC provides life-saving case management services for women, girls, men, boys, gender-diverse populations, and children of all abilities. DRC has set up safe spaces , GBV risk mitigation activities and community centers that serve as hubs for psychosocial support, sessions, and essential services. Through the Engaging Men in Accountable Practices (EMAP) activities, DRC raises awareness among men and boys, while also empowering women and gender-diverse communities.
Recognizing that sustainable change requires community-wide involvement, DRC actively includes beneficiaries in every stage of planning and implementation. Local authorities and NGOs receive capacity-building support tailored to their community needs, ensuring training initiatives are impactful. DRC places significant emphasis on the Protection from Sexual Exploitation, Abuse (PSEA) mechanism, reinforcing it through volunteer training and awareness sessions.
Aligned with Inter-Sector Coordination Group (ISCG) strategies, DRC’s activities are informed by GBV-Sub-Sector mapping to maximize the impact of its interventions.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

In this context, there are urgent protection needs driven by a complex mix of poverty, displacement, and socio-cultural factors that increase vulnerabilities, particularly for women, girls, and gender-diverse communities. Gender-based violence (GBV) remains prevalent, with women and girls facing high risks of domestic violence, sexual harassment, and exploitation. Gender norms within these communities often restrict women’s and girls’ access to education, healthcare, and livelihood opportunities, limiting their ability to achieve self-reliance. The GBVIMS factsheet indicates that physical, emotional, and sexual violence were the most frequently reported incidents in the last quarter. The Q2 2024 protection monitoring report similarly highlights physical assault as the most reported incident, with an observed increase in sexual violence cases within camps. Adolescent girls, young women, female-headed households, and persons with disabilities have been identified as particularly vulnerable. Furthermore, gender-diverse communities, including hijra populations, face significant discrimination, leading to exclusion from basic services and employment. Persons with disabilities, especially women and girls, encounter additional barriers to accessing safe spaces, healthcare, and protection services, making them more vulnerable to violence and exploitation.Youth aged 15-24, particularly adolescent girls, lack safe spaces where they can seek support, engage in recreational activities, and participate in awareness programs. These populations face challenges in recovering from trauma and building resilience. Economic vulnerability, exacerbated by displacement and lack of livelihood options, has been identified as a key driver of exploitation and abuse, with many individuals resorting to negative coping mechanisms to survive. The Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment identifies economic vulnerability as a key driver of exploitation and abuse.AGD considerations are central to DRC’s approach, recognizing that effective intervention requires an understanding of the different risks and barriers faced by women, men, boys, girls, and gender-diverse populations. DRC incorporates an Age, Gender, and Diversity (AGD) approach to identify and address the specific needs of each group, ensuring inclusivity across all activities. To support women and girls, DRC has established safe spaces that provide essential case management and psychosocial support, offering a secure environment for GBV survivors. Additionally, DRC conducts community-based GBV prevention programs targeting men and boys to foster accountability and reduce harmful gender norms. Gender-diverse communities are provided with tailored support, such as referrals to specialized services and inclusion in awareness activities to reduce stigma and promote acceptance.Cash assistance will be provided to those with urgent financial needs, with a focus on at-risk groups. By embedding gender considerations into all program components, DRC aims to create a more equitable environment where everyone, regardless of gender or background, has the opportunity to access essential services.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

DRC’s project prioritizes protection mainstreaming by integrating safety, dignity, and accessibility across all activities to ensure inclusive, protective, and responsive services for vulnerable groups, including women, children, persons with disabilities, and gender-diverse communities. Safe spaces and community centers are designed to be accessible to all, providing essential services like psychosocial support and case management within safe and supportive environments. DRC fosters accountability and transparency through active beneficiary involvement in planning and feedback mechanisms, enabling community members to voice concerns and suggest improvements. Staff and volunteers receive continuous training on protection principles, confidentiality, and safe referrals to maintain a survivor-centered approach. GBV team provides support to other sectors in DRC and non protection in the camps and host locations where we work to do Protection mainstreaming. following relevant IASC guidances Through these efforts, DRC ensures that protection considerations are central to supporting vulnerable populations with dignity and respect.

2.3 - Age & Disability

DRC implements an Age, Gender, and Diversity Mainstreaming (AGDM) approach to ensure inclusivity across all program activities. With a dedicated AGDM policy and ongoing AGDM assessments, DRC prioritizes collecting disaggregated data by age, gender, and diversity profiles. This focus enables the organization to address the specific needs of marginalized groups, including persons with disabilities and gender-diverse communities such as the hijra community. Informed by unique insights gained through our work with gender minorities and gender-based violence (GBV) programming, we establish safe spaces, community centers, and provide tailored psychosocial support to ensure equitable access to life-saving services for people of all abilities and ages. Aligned with DRC’s five-year strategy, which includes a key performance indicator (KPI) on AGD inclusion, our country operation has appointed a dedicated AGD focal point. This focal point, with technical support from DRC headquarters, drives the implementation of AGD principles on the ground. Additionally, DRC conducts regular AGD mainstreaming training for staff and volunteers, bolstering the team's capacity to deliver inclusive, responsive services to vulnerable populations.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

DRC is working towards ensuring the participation of affected population in the planning and implementation of all project activities. DRC has established Feedback mechanism that enable beneficiaries to express their needs and concerns, which are integrated into the program cycle to align interventions with community expectations. Feedback is gathered through complaint boxes, CFRM desks , safe spaces and community centers where regular sessions encourage open engagement and foster transparency.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

To create a safe and respectful environment for all beneficiaries, DRC embeds PSEA principles across all program activities, prioritizing the dignity, rights, and well-being of those we serve. Key activities include regular volunteer training on PSEA to reinforce safe practices and enable volunteers to recognize and address risks in the community, as well as community awareness sessions to aware beneficiaries on PSEA standards, reporting mechanisms, and available protections.DRC PSEA policy respects survivor centred approach and follows it to ensure service and victim assistance. DRC has designated PSEA focal points in all operational areas, with a country-level focal point providing technical support and guidance. Technical expertise is further strengthened through support from DRC’s headquarters, ensuring alignment with best practices. As an active member of the PSEA Network, DRC is dedicated to upholding the highest standards of protection and accountability.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

DRC is committed to reducing its environmental impact and mainstreaming climate adaptation into all aspects of its programming. This commitment centers on three key components: (1) minimizing the environmental footprint of program interventions by prioritizing renewable resources and nature-based solutions; (2) integrating climate adaptation strategies across sectoral approaches to enhance community resilience; and (3) establishing a carbon baseline to monitor and reduce DRC's carbon footprint. To drive these efforts, DRC is setting up a dedicated "Go Green" team to oversee climate initiatives and ensure sustainable practices. The country office will collaborate with regional and headquarters teams to explore and implement climate pilot projects, both within and beyond Cox’s Bazar, contributing to a more sustainable and environmentally conscious response model.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

DRC has embedded comprehensive emergency preparedness and response (EPR) measures within our project to ensure continuity and resilience in times of crisis. Business continuity plans, contingency funds, and emergency response protocols enable us to respond swiftly to emerging risks, such as natural disasters, conflicts, and other emergencies. Staff receive regular training in emergency response procedures, ensuring that essential services, including GBV case management and psychosocial support, remain accessible to affected populations during crises. Additionally, DRC staff actively participate in the PERU team, strengthening our collective response capacity during emergencies.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

"DRC actively engages youth aged 15-24 in GBV awareness and prevention activities, recognizing their potential as agents of change within the community. DRC’s GBV programming includes dedicated prevention activities specifically designed for adolescent girls and boys, while response services address the needs of adolescent girls. Comprehensive case management services are provided by trained case workers who address the unique needs of youth. Female youth form the majority of returning participants in Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSSs) and are regularly consulted in program design, ensuring their perspectives shape the interventions. Male youth are also foregrounded as allies in various events and campaigns organized by DRC.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

DRC’s GBV project in the camps promotes self-reliance and resilience among refugees by integrating GBV prevention with psychosocial support, awareness-raising, and capacity-building activities. Through safe spaces, skill-building sessions, and psychosocial support, we empower women, girls, and gender-diverse individuals to build resilience, while also providing essential case management services for survivors. Our approach fosters individual and community resilience, equipping participants with tools to address GBV risks and enabling them to engage in positive coping mechanisms and supportive networks.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

Aligned with Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 5 on gender equality, the Danish Refugee Council (DRC) is implementing targeted interventions in Cox's Bazar to empower women and girls, reduce inequalities, and promote inclusive development. In this region, particularly in Ukhiya, restrictive social norms, limited institutional access, and the heightened vulnerability of marginalized areas to natural and human-induced hazards compound existing socioeconomic challenges.
