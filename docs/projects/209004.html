Project Summary

Since the outbreak of fighting in Sudan on April 15, 2023, between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Paramilitary Rapid Support Forces (RSF), ongoing hostilities and violence have extended to various parts of the country, including the Darfur Region. The humanitarian situation in Sudan has sharply deteriorated, leading to shortages of essential resources such as food, water, fuel, cash, limited communication, and healthcare services. In Central Darfur state alone, approximately 192,430 individuals have been displaced, primarily from other areas within the state, with smaller groups displaced from South and North Darfur. The displaced individuals are mainly seeking refuge with relatives in Gharb Jabal Marrah, Shamal Jabal Marrah, Um Dukhun, Wadi Saleh, and Zalingei localities. White Nile state has accommodated IDPs from various regions of the country; Khartoum, Darfur region –North, South and Central states. The lack of a resolution to the conflict has further exacerbated the humanitarian crisis in Central Darfur and White Nile, with disrupted market systems impacting the prices of basic food commodities and limiting access to transportation for those seeking safe havens in less affected areas. Women and children in Central Darfur state and White Nile state face day to day risk increment on Child Protection such as gender-based violence (GBV), UXOs/explosion abandoned in the streets and other child rights violations.
The localities are facing severe challenges, particularly in WASH (Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene) services, healthcare, child protection and food supply shortages. This has led to starvation, especially among children, and a lack of access to safe drinking water and sanitation facilities. Safety and security concerns within IDP camps are prominent, with diseases like malaria and diarrhea spreading. Lack of save friendly space and psychosocial support for children in majority of this localities. Open defecation has become widespread due to inadequate latrines, disproportionately affecting children and women. And thousands of UXO’s/explosive abandoned in the streets of this localities especially in Zalingei post danger to children in the IDPs and schools.
The project will focus on addressing multiple vulnerabilities during the crisis, lifesaving services including specialized services, Community-Based Protection - service providers, government officials and other duty bearers, community representatives and humanitarian staff, trained in protection issues and safe effective and inclusive service delivery, community reached through information, awareness raising and community outreach activities on general protection including civil documentation, peaceful co-existence, conducting protection need assessment at the community level, provision of Cash Grants/ in kind to prevent, mitigate or response to protection needs, establish a number of protection desks to address issues of GBV/PSEA in the community, and provision of wash (water, sanitation and hygiene) services to the communities in line with sector guidance. The project mainly targets the locality of zalingei, Mukjar, wadi salih (villages; Kordol, Amer Jedit, Baga, Damber, zalingei, Korley and Kuturi) in White Nile; Kosti and Tendalti.
To attain sustainability, the program will support interventions that are relevance to needs and context, promoting ownership by community and local government by leveraging community resources and structures.
JMCO aim to establish Community-Based Child Protection Networks (CBCPN) with more focuses on inclusive youth/adolescent groups; sustainability of the child protection interventions, enhancing local government and promoting community engagement for strengthening child protection and PSEA activities to provide support on family tracing and reunification (FTR, Referral pathways, and PSS, GBV) process.
