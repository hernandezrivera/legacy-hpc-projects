Project Summary

Peace Corps proposes an integrated emergency education response programme focused on provision of life saving safe and quality protective education interventions to school going children(3-17 years) in counties of Western Bahr el Ghazal, South Sudan. This project target 9,010 beneﬁciaries of which 8,540 will be school going children aged 3-17 years (3,416 Boys, 5,124 Girls), and 470 adults (259Men, 211Women) who will include 250 teachers, 190 PTAs/SMCs and 30 caretakers, out of these beneficiaries, 65% will be host community, 25% IDPs and 10% returnees. This project will run for a period of twelve months covering the three terms and the target will be 20 schools in selected counties. This education project will incorporate and complement life-saving components such as psychosocial support, positive disciplining to prevent corporal punishment, referral mechanism, provision of school feeding and availability of gender-sensitive WASH facilities to deliver a comprehensive package of services.
Key activities of this project will include setting up/rehabilitation of temporary learning spaces (TLSs), providing psychosocial support, providing learning materials, providing WASH facilities, training PTA members and teachers on psychosocial support, provision of ECD services to 3-5 years children, school feeding, teachers training on EiE, basic pedagogy and learner-cantered methodologies, training of community education committee members and Local Education oﬃcials in school management, monitoring and record keeping and providing, training of youth and adolescents (14-17 years) in life- skills and basic vocational courses and teachers’ incentives.
PCO will collaborate with other clusters like WASH, Protection, Nutrition and Health and stakeholders to ensure coherent, coordinated and integrated responses driven by the assessed education needs of affected populations. PCO will liaise with the nutrition partners on ground in providing Nutrition Education to teachers who will then cascade the knowledge and awareness to learners in classroom set-up as a move to improve on the general nutrition status of learners by promoting a balanced diet in every home. Also, PCO will synergize with health and nutrition cluster partners who manage the health facilities and OTPs centres in the counties of operation, by offering outreach services to supported schools as catchment areas for service provision on cases of acute malnutrition and health challenges to aid in referral and support services.
PCO will also set-up a simple, safe and accessible Community Based Complaints Mechanism (CBCM) for SEA and protection of school children to add eﬃciency to strengthen reporting mechanism.
The organisation will involve SMCs, PTAs and Community leaders in community sensitisation efforts focusing on access to education services, school WASH and protection messages, and prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) with the aim to increase access to education for boys and girls as well as preventing and reducing protection risks for children on their way to and from school. Community members will also be involved in the design of TLS, identiﬁcation of a safe location for construction of TLS and monitoring teachers and pupils’ attendance through the PTA.
The project will also mainstream gender, GBV, conﬂict sensitivity and protection with hopes of contributing towards protection of children from risks, GBV and other protection challenges that have a potential of disrupting their education, enhance their psychosocial development, improve hygiene and sanitation and alleviate strains on their academic performance.
PCO is currently implementing education project in 10 schools in Wau and Raja counties supported by SSHF SA2 running upto May 2020 and so is familiar with WBeG State. In addition, PCO is the education cluster Co-Lead of WBeG alongside Unicef and has established presence across the entire State so is best placed to implement.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian crisis, displacement, protracted conflict emergency, poverty, and livelihood insecurity are the main drivers leading to interruption of education and absence of protection for 3-17 years old children those are accounting 3 million. Considering these challenging predicaments in the operating environment and limited access to education for the targeted host community children and their demand for education, the majority of these children are out of schools due to insecure stances and destruction of schools. In addition, the recent influx of spontaneous refugee returnees from the neighboring countries, and the returning of IDPs to their original residential areas are demanding educational access and protection of their children that has put pressure on the education sector. The prolonged economic and social crises have also affected teachers directly through lack of payment of incentives. All the years of conflict, famine, and general instability have taken their toll on South Sudan’s education system. Many schools have been destroyed/ stopped functioning due to conflicts/ unfavourable conditions and or stand empty because children and teachers are too scared to attend, while some parents are no longer able to pay for uniforms and books as they deal with unemployment and the effects of a chronically devalued currency.68% of schools in South Sudan lack proper WASH facilities according to Nation Wide Education Cluster Assessment (November 2018), lack of proper WASH in schools exposes learners to risks of diseases, coupled with high level of malnutrition, and weak immune system. The HNO 2019 report affirms that at least 1 out of 3 schools have been damaged, destroyed, occupied or closed since 2013 war affecting about 70% of children who should have been attending classes. Another challenge affecting resumption of normalcy in education is the continuous violation of the right of children. A total of 2700 incidents of grave violations affecting 81,000 children and 2300 GBV cases reported to service providers by the UN since 2014According to the joint assessment mission in Baggari in June 2019 in which PCO was involved, there were 17 functional schools with a population of 5505 pupils (2963 boys, 2542 girls) and 121(99 male, 22 female) teachers. Many trained and qualified teachers fled during the years of hostilities, many of whom have not yet returned and the existing teachers came forward as volunteers from within their communities. Top of the concerns include lack of clean water and latrines in most of these operational schools in greater Baggari, no school is benefiting from WFP school feeding programme. A number of community members mentioned the need for school girls’ dignity kits, which has prevented girls from at times attending classes.

• Project Inception workshops in selected counties to share project document with stakeholders. This will be conducted to gather perspectives from various stakeholders on the roll out modalities on ground and as well agree on the schools to support. • Setting up/rehabilitation of 30 temporary learning spaces (TLSs). In selected schools where learning structures were/have been destroyed as a result of conﬂict, TLSs will be rehabilitated to offer protective learning environment to the pupils. • Providing psychosocial support in 20 schools • Providing learning materials and supplies pupils to 8540 pupils. This will include pupils learning materials like school-in a box and also teachers training guidebooks • Providing gender-sensitive WASH facilities. These will include both hardware and software components of WASH and will majorly be WASH in schools. This will include things like disability and gender sensitive 10 latrines construction/rehabilitation, 40 hand washing facilities installation,8 boreholes repairs and hygiene and sanitation awareness and messaging etc. • Training of 190 PTA /SMCs members and teachers on psychosocial support. This will be trained to offer psychosocial support and care which will be focused on core aspects such as skills and knowledge, emotional well-being and social wellbeing. These trained members will in-turn be of support to the pupils • Provision of ECD services to 540 children of 3-5 years children. This will where appropriate be set-up to cater for this age group • Provision of school feeding to children in 20 selected schools. This will be implemented where school meals will take two phases, ﬁrst phase will be high energy biscuits for two weeks and later onsite cooking of meals. HEB will be provided in a period when schools infrastructural preparations for onsite cooking are underway. • 250 Teachers training on EiE, basic pedagogy and learner-cantered methodologies • Training of community education committee members and Local Education oﬃcials in school management, monitoring and record keeping and providing • Training of youth and adolescents (14-17 years) in life-skills and basic vocational courses. This will be delivered as a form of informal learning and basically courses will be market demand driven so as to produce graduands who are relevant to the prevailing labour market • payment of teachers’ incentives where necessary • Conduct 9 awareness raising on school-related gender-based violence(SRGBV) among students, teachers, communities and local oﬃcials about sexual & physical violence and implementing prevention strategies • Conduct routine monitoring of project activities and reporting. There will be routine monitoring of all supported schools and the entire project by both PCO and also externally by stakeholders to ensure activities are in line with the project design document. 9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will establish a range of Beneficiary Communication and Accountability mechanisms to strengthen community engagement and feedback throughout the lifecycle of the project. This will be developed during the project’s inception phase, and will be the primary responsibility of Project Management Committee comprises of diverse representatives such as pupils representative, PTA/SMC, Teachers and project staff to implement, with support from project staff. A core component of this will be the regular dissemination of project-related information and updates to community members (beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries). Communities will be encouraged to identify multiple information dissemination channels that leverage, where relevant and appropriate, multimedia platforms including radio and SMS. Low literacy options will be included, and women specifically consulted to identify gender-responsive content and channels. The CFM will also outline strategies for soliciting regular feedback from communities (beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries). This will be done through monthly community consultation meetings facilitated by PMCs. Information will be fed back to Project Management Team who will be responsible for adapting and iterating implementation strategies and workplans accordingly. Information relating to the project’s response to feedback will be fed back to communities by the PMC via scheduled consultation meetings, ensuring ongoing two-way communication. PMCs will also publicise and manage a complaints and response mechanism which will allow communities to provide feedback between scheduled community consultations, and anonymously report grievances and complaints.
