Project Summary

Bihndumlem Humanitarian Association of Peace and Hope (BIHAPH) is a local, non-profit grass root humanitarian Organization with profound understanding of local realities, with more than 9 years of experience, has gained the trust and confidence of the stakeholders, and has access to the rural communities not often accessible to many other organizations.
BIHAPH works towards conflict prevention, Humanitarian Assistance, Livelihood, Peacebuilding , fight against GBV and actively works with community support groups to ensure Child protection. The Organization supports vulnerable women, girls, men, and boys including persons living with disabilities, especially those affected by wars, conflict, and diverse disasters. BIHAPH provides support in healthcare, Child protection, PSS to GBV survivors, education, capacity building, and entrepreneurship to ensure long-term economic development within impoverished rural communities in Cameroon. BIHAPH has within the past 5 years touched and transformed the lives of more than 12,000 young women and girls living in poor rural communities in the Northwest Region through sponsorship programs, education, health provision, and vocational training in partnerships with UNHCR and UNDP.
The main goal of this project is to reduce Gender-Based Violence (GBV) amongst adolescents in the school environment in two communities: Wum and Bafmeng, Menchum Division, Northwest Region. The interventions and Needs assessment conducted revealed that 200 persons including young women and girls suffered from GBV in 2023 especially in the school environment as reported by school gender clubs and community structures like Local Dialogue Committees, and more than 250 lacked civil documentation including birth certificates, ID cards, and marriage certificates for GBV. The project will ensure that the 10 schools in the two communities are educated on sexual exploitation, abuse, and gender-based violence-related issues; strengthen the capacities of Community-Based Organizations (CBOs) enhance and ensure functionality of 10 Gender Clubs in the schools, sensitize teachers and administrators, enhance the services of structures such as health care workers to fight Gender-Based Violence (GBV); do effective referrals and provide comprehensive case management including conference case management and a child-friendly space to children and teenage mothers who are survivors of GBV. The project will utilize a community-centered approach to sensitize 10,000 persons and provide comprehensive case management to 1234 survivors of GBV including conference case management for pertinent cases where stakeholders such as the security, judiciary, and social affairs will be brought to deliberate on the way forward. Furthermore, the project will ensure that over 2,000 children [and caregivers] access mental health or psychosocial support. The survivor centered approach will be prioritized in all the stages of case management. This will ensure the dignity and safety of the survivors. The project therefore stands to mitigate the effects of GBV and ensure that young women and adolescent girls who are survivors of GBV as well as persons living with disability benefit from comprehensive case management. The project also aims to support 100 children through sports, music and MHPSS.
BIHAPH is soliciting the sum of 128000 US dollars for 12 months . The activities planned will greatly alleviate GBV amongst adolescent girls in 10 schools.
This is the ideal time that rural communities like Wum and Bafmeng need more intervention in fighting GBV through schools. Living conditions are deplorable, exposing adolescent girls to all form of SGBV either sexual exploitation, denial of opportunity, force marriage, or rape.
BIHAPH therefore considers this the best moment to address this critical issue and give adolescent girls dignity and respect in the communities,

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

In a Global Ministerial Conference on ending violence against children held in Bogata Columbia on the 7-8/11/2024, the very first of its kind, the latest Global data shows that every 4 minutes, a child is killed somewhere in the world by an act of violence, nearly 50 million adolescent girls aged 15 to 19 have been victims of physical or sexual assault in the past year, violence takes the lives of about 130,000 children and adolescents under 20 each year on average. It was also revealed that in fragile settings like the North West Region especially Menchum Division with a severity score of 3.0, girls face an even greater risk with the prevalence of rape and sexual assault. The rates of depression, anxiety disorders, unplanned pregnancies, sexually transmitted infections and HIV are higher amongst adolescents who have experienced violence compared to those who have not, as well as many other health, psychological and economic problems that can last even after the violence has ended. The effects of violence, are felt at the individual, family, and community levels. Consequences of violence include increased risk for suicide, high school dropout rate, alcohol abuse, as well as negative impacts on human development outcomes. Gender-based violence has dire economic consequences, costing an estimated 1.2%-3.7% of GDP in some countries due to lost productivity, equivalent to the average spending of low and middle-income countries on primary education.Decreasing GBV in schools requires a community-based, multi-pronged approach, and sustained engagement with multiple stakeholders. The most effective initiatives address underlying risk factors for violence, including social norms regarding gender roles and the acceptability of violence. In Cameroon, 56.4% of women have suffered emotional and/or sexual violence (OCHA, 2023). The situation is worse in the rural areas, especially with the advent of the Anglophone crisis which started in 2016 and escalated into a full-blown armed conflict with insecurity, unprecedented killings, and kidnappings. More than one million people have been displaced and a major humanitarian crisis has been unleashed, with two million people in need of assistance (A more generous embrace, 2023). The conflict has affected adolescent girls and young women differently than men and boys. They all face specific risks. They also have vulnerabilities, needs and coping mechanisms. The people of Wum and Bafmeng in the Menchum division in particular and the Northwest Region of Cameroon in general face a slump in economic activities. The poverty level of the people has increased resulting in a high dependency rate forcing young girls into early marriage. The crime wave has also surged and all forms of violence against girls and young women have become commonplace. The shutdown of the majority of schools has increased the rate of gender-based violence. This is compounded by existing laws and customs of the people with male dominance with girls and women exempted from traditional law-making and decision-making bodies. BIHAPH has offered psychosocial support to over 500 survivors of SGBV and sensitized over 10,000 inhabitants of Wum and Bafmeng on the effects of GBV yet the unavailability of a source of likelihood such as no farming tools, no source of economic empowerment resulting from the conflict which forced many women and children to flee rendering them powerless in the hands of perpetrators. Survival of the fittest is rising, some of the girls as young as 13 have resorted to sex for petit cash as a means of survival. Interviews with queen mothers revealed that majority of women and girls are exposed to sexual exploitation and abuse because they are idle. Living conditions are deplorable, at least 2 in every household have suffered a form of SGBV either sexual exploitation.BIHPAH referred 194 cases to a partner organization for civil documentation, but only 14 were treated.
