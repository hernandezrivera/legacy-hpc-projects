Project Summary

The project envisages the distribution of cash assistance for food as well as in-kind food distribution for vulnerable Palestine Refugees. Food assistance is a critical component of UNRWA’s emergency response in Syria, as food insecurity continues to negatively impact the Palestine refugees who have become increasingly dependent on UNRWA to meet their minimum needs. UNRWA provides two types of food assistance to up to 418,000 Palestine Refugees: 1) in kind food baskets and 2) cash assistance for food. The interventions were designed to be complementary in addressing the food insecurity of Palestine refugees.
UNRWA estimates that 418,000 out of 445,000 Palestine refugees in Syria will continue to require humanitarian assistance in 2019. However, UNRWA will be working towards a targeted approach of assistance that prioritizes the most vulnerable Palestine refugees. A vulnerability assessment was conducted in 2018 to identify the most vulnerable Palestine refugees, who will be prioritized for assistance.
In this sense, UNRWA is planning to adopt a two-tier approach for humanitarian food assistance in 2019. The focus will be on four main groups that can be identified through UNRWA’s registration system. These categories are: families headed by a person older than 60 years; families headed by a person with disability, and people with disabilities; female-headed families; and orphans. UNRWA estimates that 126,000 Palestine Refugees fall under the identified categories and will accordingly provide them with cash for food assistance amounting to USD 17.5 per person per month, and in-kind food assistance. Meanwhile, UNRWA will provide the remaining 292,000 Palestine Refugees in need of assistance with cash for food equivalent to USD 8.75 per person per month to mitigate food insecurity among them and prevent them from falling into further poverty.

Needs

Eight years of conflict in Syria continue to have dramatic consequences for the people living in the country, including Palestine refugees. Violence, economic disruption and a contracting job market as well as a decrease in provision of public services and social security programmes have created and compounded vulnerabilities within the population. In the absence of a durable political settlement, intense and widespread hostilities have resulted in deaths and injuries, continued internal displacement, large-scale migration to other countries, lost livelihoods, mounting humanitarian needs and access restrictions hindering the emergency response, including in UNRWA areas of operation. In 2018, UNRWA estimates that 438,000 Palestine refugees remain in the country (the number is expected to increase in 2019 to 445,000 due to returns, mainly from Lebanon and Jordan ) out of 560,000 individuals registered with the Agency in Syria. Displacement, loss of assets and livelihood and high levels of inflation have contributed to the increased vulnerability of civilians, including the Palestine refugee population. Many families have lost their main breadwinner, whether through death, incapacitation, detention or departure to another country in order to seek protection and asylum. Almost 60 per cent of Palestine refugees have been displaced at least once since the beginning of the conflict and, according to UNRWA estimates, more than 180,000 have had their homes destroyed or severely damaged due to hostilities. Yarmouk, Dera’a, and Ein El-Tal (Aleppo) camps, previously home to more than 30 per cent of the Palestine refugee population in Syria, have been almost completely destroyed. In December 2017, UNRWA carried out a representative sample survey to estimate social and economic hardship among Palestine refugees currently residing in Syria and receiving emergency assistance from UNRWA covering a total population of 418,000 individuals. The survey revealed that 90% of Palestine refugees are in absolute poverty (less than US$2 US a day) while 80% are estimated to live in extreme poverty (less than US$1.5 a day). Post UNRWA cash assistance, those numbers decrease respectively from 90% to 74% and from 80% to 50% demonstrating the necessity of the Agency’s lifeline response. Based on the analysis, four main groups were identified to be more likely to face high vulnerability. These are: (a) families headed by a person older than 60 years; (b) families headed by a person with disability, and people with disabilities; (c) families headed by a female; and (d) orphans. The four groups will receive USD 17.5 per person per month and at the same time will continue receiving in-kind food assistance through 2019. In order to ensure that most vulnerable Palestine refugees meet their basic food needs, UNRWA will provide USD 17.25 per person per month to 126,000 Palestine refugees who fall within one of the four vulnerable categories stated above. The remaining caseload of Palestine refugees will continue to receive USD 8.75 per person per month for food to support vulnerable families to enhance their resilience. These amounts are subject to funding availability.

Implementation of activities

The project has two main components that will be implemented as illustrated below:‎A)‎ Cash assistance for food: UNRWA has been providing life-saving humanitarian assistances to Palestinian Refugees ‎since the beginning of the crisis in Syria. The cash assistance programme remains the most critical form of UNRWA ‎emergency support to Palestine refugees in Syria, helping them to meet part of their essential humanitarian and ‎survival needs that provides flexibility and dignity of choice. The cash assistance programme is considered as a ‎flagship programme of UNRWA’s humanitarian operations. It remains the primary form of assistance for 418,000 ‎conflict-affected and vulnerable Palestine refugees living in Syria. Currently, UNRWA distributes cash through three ‎partners (Al Baraka Bank, BBSF Bank and Al Haram Company) in addition to its own cash distribution centre located ‎at filed office. UNRWA works with the three partners to ensure smooth implementation of cash assistance activities ‎within, the capacity of each partner, the location of the outlet and the address of the beneficiaries. As part of the ‎planning phase, UNRWA generates a list of all eligible Palestine refugees and provide the list of beneficiaries to the ‎partner ahead of the starting date. Once the local currency is availed in the outlets and following intensive coordination ‎with the partners, UNRWA sends SMS to each beneficiary communicating the amount, location and time. UNRWA ‎assigns dedicated technical UNRWA staff to each of the cash outlets, allowing the Agency to ensure strict compliance ‎with financial rules and regulations for distribution of cash and also for verification.‎B)‎ In-kind food assistance: UNRWA distributes in-kind food baskets to vulnerable Palestine refugees through 15 ‎distribution centres and two mobile teams. Families who fall under any of the specified categories receive food ‎baskets to meet one third of their caloric needs. The food basket content is aligned to food sector food baskets as ‎much as possible. Following competitive international tendering and bidding, UNRWA procures the food ‎commodities. The commodities are then packed into food baskets by the packing team and shipped to areas where ‎UNRWA has distribution centres. Beneficiaries are notified of the date and content of food basket through SMS and ‎other media channels. UNRWA also uses mobile teams for food distribution in remote areas to avoid the beneficiaries ‎the high cost of transportation and mitigate protection risks. ‎

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Improved access with localized conflict in certain areas of the country: Overall, humanitarian access is expected to improve with fewer Palestine refugees in hard-to-reach areas (13,500 as of end October 2018). At the same time, hostilities may continue in certain areas of the country. Should safe access resume for civilians in areas like Yarmouk and Dera’a camps, the Agency will focus on resuming its basic education services in these areas. Most Palestine Refugees in Syria will remain displaced: It is anticipated that the levels of displacement will remain high among Palestine refugees, as the housing stock in Yarmouk unofficial camp suffered significant destruction during the conflict, most recently in May 2018. The majority of the 160,000 former residents of Yarmouk are therefore expected to remain in displacement, often paying high rents, which further increases their vulnerability. A similar situation is expected in Dera’a and Ein El Tal camps which have sustained major destruction; these three camps used to provide housing for more than 30 per cent of Palestine refugees in Syria. Increase in spontaneous returns as more areas become accessible: As new areas become accessible to civilians, as observed for Sbeineh and Khan Eshieh camps in 2018, additional small scale spontaneous returns are anticipated, provided that safe access is granted for civilians and basic public services (water, electricity) are restored by the authorities. UNRWA will therefore seek to resume its education services in defined locations where feasible, to ensure that Palestine refugee school children are served. While a small number of spontaneous returns of PRS (Palestine refugees from Syria) from outside Syria were observed in 2017 and 2018 (about 2,000) and are expected to continue, most returns are expected to involve Palestine refugees displaced inside Syria. UNRWA continue to raise sufficient funds to sustain its operations in Syria: UNRWA will continue to mobilize resources and to advocate for the needs of Palestine refugees among donors, partners, and other relevant stakeholders. UNRWA will need an estimated US$ 220 million to respond to the emergency needs (health, education, protection, humanitarian assistance, WASH) of 445,000 Palestine refugees in Syria in 2019. UNRWA will mitigate the risk of limited funding by prioritizing key projects and interventions, and focusing on the needs of the most vulnerable.
