Project Summary

Médecins du Monde – France (MdM) is operating in Gaza since 2002, working mainly in the fields of emergency preparedness and response, mental health and psychosocial support, and Sexual and Reproductive Health. Gaza is suffering from protracted crises for more than 14 years now. The COVID-19 pandemic has been an additional burden on the already existing economic and humanitarian crises. Moreover, the last attack on Gaza in May caused an aggravated negative impact on the already fragile health system and infrastructure.

MdM is an active member of both the Health/Nutrition and Protection clusters. In the past years, MdM contributed to the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) and, in this case, more specifically to the HCT Response Plan and responded to needs to be highlighted in the different reserve allocations funded by the HF.
This proposed intervention is contributing to the health cluster objective 1: Ensure the availability of acceptable and quality essential healthcare services to vulnerable communities in oPt that is related to the strategic objective 2. MdM-F is focusing its intervention on strengthening the capacities of the health system and the community to deal with emergencies, aiming at enhancing the mental health and medical emergency services at the hospitals and community levels.
At the hospital level, the intervention will aim to increase the capacity of the health staff in 5 emergency departments on the mental health and medical flow chart that was designed and validated by the Ministry of Health (MOH) in the previous MdM interventions. This intervention is informed by the success of the MdM pilot project in Al Aqsa hospital where mental health was integrated into the emergency department as part of the MOH strategy. MdM will duplicate the same approach to the emergency departments of Indonesian and Naser hospitals in developing mental health units at both hospitals. At the community level, MdM will work with local partners to ensure the delivery of MHPSS services to the communities and increase their basic life support skills in Beit Hanoun and Khan Younis governorates through the provision of technical support, taking into consideration COVID-19 adaptations, needs integration and mainstreaming in all levels of interventions.
MdM is working with both the MoH and Health cluster to coordinate its intervention and to ensure the effectiveness of its actions as well as to avoid duplication. This intervention complements the Palestinian Ministry of Health (MoH) national strategy as the main provider of health services in Gaza that is responsible for the supervision, regulation, and control of all health services. Finally, MdM has involved the MoH in the designing phase of the project and has also coordinated this proposal with other relevant health actors such as MdM- Switzerland, MdM-Spain, MAP UK to avoid any duplication.

1 - Needs

Gaza is in a complex emergency, characterized by various risk factors, central of which is a continued risk of armed escalations which can compound and exacerbate preexisting crises in social service provisions, such as health care services. Based on the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2022, the already challenged healthcare system in the oPt is experiencing added strain from the COVID-19 outbreak. The current surge takes place against a backdrop of an overall extremely precarious humanitarian situation in the Gaza strip. The health care system in Gaza is suffering from a chronic shortage of drugs and disposables. The allocation of health staff to respond to COVID-19 needs has a negative impact on the day-to-day provision of medical services, especially in the emergency departments. The MoH has expressed the need for continuous improvement in the quality of medical emergency services through the dissemination of medical and mental health emergency flow charts in the main five emergency departments in the Gaza strip, and the training of dedicated staff for its application. The charts, developed by MdM in a previous project, were accepted and validated by the MoH. This proposed intervention is a response to the request of the MoH to continue the dissemination and capacity building of relevant health care staff on the validated medical and MH flow charts. Through this, MdM will contribute to the enhancement of medical emergency services and enable staff to access flow chart tools through an online training platform. Moreover, this project aims to contribute to addressing the priority needs concerning emergency and trauma care and crosscutting the MHPPS needs to be emphasized in the HNO 2022. Specific for Gaza, the following priority needs are what this project hopes to address: early access to emergency medical services; establishment of a national adopted triage system, provision of drugs, supplies, including PPEs, and equipment, including prepositioning of supplies; and capacity building for clinical and management skills. It was highlighted in the HNO 2022 the need for training among health care workers and community members on disaster response. In Gaza, previous hostile incidents have left 370 people needing limb reconstruction and 600 in need of rehabilitation, while 7 MoH hospitals need support in improving their emergency response and preparedness level. This proposed intervention targets vulnerable groups, specifically admitted cases to Emergency Department, acute emergency cases in need of immediate medical intervention, Persons with Disabilities in need of health care intervention; trauma injured people, HWs (frontline HWs), and people in need of specialized MH services (especially women and girls with MH needs to be exposed to GBV, and children and adolescents with MH needs). The recent escalation in May and the sustained COVID-19 pandemic have aggravated the MH problem existing in Gaza as well as stretching further the fragile health care system, where at least 1 member of surveyed household experiences psychological distress (MSNA, 2021). Although MHPSS has affected both genders, women are more disproportionately affected than men (UN Women Gender Alert, 2021). COVID-19 has also caused further psychological distress among adolescents (WHO and Juzoor, 2021). The HNO 2022 also identified 345,000 boys and 332,200 girls who have MHPSS needs across oPt, where 53% of these children are from Gaza. Current and previous engagements of MdM with the MoH, local partners, and community members allow this intervention to be informed of the current needs in the targeted project areas. Through the establishment of the MH unit in Al Aqsa hospital, data collected showed that out of the 7,738 individuals who were provided MH services, 68% were diagnosed as suffering from anxiety related to a health condition, 18% from acute stress, and the remaining were either agitation/psychoses, conversion disorder, depression, panic, suicidal thoughts, and substance abuse. Out of the 700 cases that were referred to specialized services, 62% were female adults which reflects the high vulnerability and need for women. MdM’s intervention aims to strengthen the medical and mental health capacity of the public health hospitals in Gaza and reinforce the link between community referrals and hospital uptake to launch an effective and quality response during emergencies.

2 - Activities

Activities under this intervention, which are directly aligned with the Health sector priorities, aim to contribute to increasing mental health and emergency preparedness response, considering gendered needs and priorities and including COVID-19 and protection mainstreaming at community level and secondary level hospitals. The logic of the intervention is outlined by the following: Outcome 1: Delivered essential elective surgery, emergency and trauma care at all levels of the pathway, through life and limb-saving training, triage and injury protocols, deployment of emergency medical teams, timely data, and multidisciplinary rehabilitation Output 1.1.: The capacities of MoH staff at emergency department of five hospitals have been strengthened Activities related to outcome 1: 1.1.1 Strengthen the capacity of the MOH staff of 5 Emergency Departments on the medical and mental health flowcharts prepared by MdM and approved by the MoH. The training will be conducted by MdM Technical team and the national committee that validated the flowcharts. The training will target 200 staff members working in the main emergency departments in Gaza strip (Shifa, Indonesian hospital, Al Aqsa, Naser and Abousef Al Naiar) 1.1.2 Formation of a technical supervisory committee, with the MoH, composed of MoH consultants and MdM staff to supervise the quality of the flowchart implementation. 1.1.3 Provision of needed mental health and medical emergency-related drugs and disposables to 5 Emergency Departments of 5 hospitals Outcome 2: Improved MHPSS services for moderate and severe cases ensuring inclusion of marginalized people and those exposed to traumatic events through integration of MH into all health facilities Output 2.1: The mental health and psychosocial services provided at Indonesian and Naser hospitals are enhanced and strengthened through the establishment of functional Mental Health Unit Activities related to output 2.1 2.1.1 Conduct MHGAP training for at least 150 medical staff at Indonesian and Naser hospitals 2.1.2 Provision of financial incentives to the volunteers at Indonesian and Naser mental health units (12 psychologist volunteers + 2 liaisons), ensuring functionality of MH unit with needed human resources2.1.3 Provision of technical support and trainings to 12 Psychologist volunteers to ensure quality provision of services, focusing on COVID-19 mental health-related topics2.1.4 Strengthening the capacities of the Mental Health unit to detect and manage mental health cases and refer them to more specialized services, if needed, considering the special needs of people with disabilities and GBV survivors 2.1.5 Establishment of feedback and complaint mechanism, integrating PSEA, within the MH unit Output 2.2: Established MHPSS services at community level, ensuring inclusion of marginalized people, provision of psychological first aid to those exposed to traumatic events and strengthened referral pathways. Activities related to output 2.2 2.2.1 2.2.1 Support to Al Atta association in Beit Hanoun and CFTA in Khan Younis by increasing their staff capacity in terms of quality and quantity.2.2.2 Enhance the capacities of the partners in the provision of face-to-face and remote service through logistics support. 2.2.3 Provision of technical and emotional support and trainings to local partners to ensure the quality provision of services, with focus on COVID-19 mental health-related topics. 2.2.4 Conduct BLS and PFA trainings to prepare vulnerable communities in developing positive coping mechanisms towards current and potential man-made emergencies; enhancing their skills to deal with emotional distress during and post-emergency. The BLS training will be conducted as a Training of Trainers (ToT) so the new trainers will be able to roll out the training to other community volunteers.2.2.5 Strengthen the capacities of the local partners (Al Atta and CFTA) to detect, manage and refer GBV cases, considering special needs of people with disabilities. 2.2.6 Strengthen the feedback and complaint mechanisms of partners (Al Atta and CFTA), integrating PSEA. This intervention has considered the exit strategy when designing the project. As the period of the project is 12 months, MdM has involved the MH directorate in the design of the structure and objectives of the intervention and will continue to work closely in the implementation as part of the on-job capacity building, ensuring sustainability of technical capacities after the end of the project. MdM will ensure the utilization of available resources at the MoH to ensure the continuation of service provision. The safety and dignity of the beneficiaries are considered in the design of the activities. Trainings and supervision planned under this intervention ensure that service providers do no harm when providing services. The MH unit will be established in a way that privacy and confidentiality of the beneficiaries will be guaranteed (e.g. rooms have privacy, data are coded with full confidentiality, providers are skilled to provide dignified services). Working with local partners and CBOs where services are closer to communities also contributes to addressing access barrier experienced mostly by vulnerable groups in the communities. PSEA is built-in the project activities through integration of PSEA into MdM’s and partner’s feedback and complaint mechanism; training partners and MdM staff on PSEA policy and receipt of complaints in the beginning of the project; identification of PSEA focal point in the PHCC and partner clinics, ensuring that PSEA awareness messaging is integrated in community outreach programs.

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

MdM accountability system is based on sharing information in a regular and timely way with the different actors involved in the project. The presence of MdM teams in the field makes this flow of information continuous and bidirectional, establishing a system that allows the participation of the health staff at MoH in the definition and adaptation of MdM activities. In the same way, regular communication is kept with the International Cooperation Department (ICD), PHC, and Hospital Directorates of the MoH to ensure full information sharing, participation, and feedback on MdM interventions. Part of the MdM intervention is to support the MH unit at the two targeted hospitals. MdM will work closely with the unit to establish a feedback mechanism. At partner level, MdM will ensure to strengthen the partners’ existing complaint and feedback mechanism. When feedback or complaints are received through any of the mechanisms in place, an immediate reply will be provided to the user with a reference number so the user can follow-up and track the process. The complaint will be processed first by prior identified focal point per the organization who will categorize the feedback into 2 main categories: regular or sensitive feedback /complaint. Regular complaint is a non-sensitive complaint which relates to programmatic issues. A sensitive complaint includes issues related to abuse /inappropriate behavior with beneficiaries involving verbal abuse/physical assault, discrimination. Based on the details of the complaint, the organization will take up the feedback to the local partner. The committee will gather information and conduct hearings and visits. Users who provided feedback will be continuously updated about the process through email or phone, and final decisions and actions will be officially communicated through official mechanisms. All complaints will be registered anonymously by the focal point in a specific data base and analysis will be done regularly.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

MdM is currently using its Feedback and Complaint Mechanism (FCM) with all its activities beneficiaries (MoH Staff), local partners, and suppliers through the distributed FCM card. The FCM cards highlight that feedback, whether positive or negative, will all be taken into consideration and addressed by MdM independently from project team and be dealt with respect for the principles of confidentiality and privacy, without affecting the possibility to receive assistance in the future. The cards include a unique mobile and WhatsApp number to receive the feedback calls or text messages, the working days and hours to receive the feedbacks, a unique email address to receive electronic feedback and all of this information is clearly written in Arabic language. Beneficiaries can also share their feedback/complaint via in-person. All of these modalities are in place to allow safe access to the FCM to different groups regardless of sex, age, disability status, literacy, etc.MdM teams distributed and explained the FCM cards to the beneficiaries during their presence in the field, In addition to that, MdM logistic department distributed and explained the FCM card to all suppliers involved within the project. A beneficiary satisfaction survey is developed for each activity and introduced the satisfaction survey to the targeted beneficiaries and obtain their consent to be contacted after the end of the intervention. MdM MEAL team are usually analyzing the data and feedback and share it on a quarterly basis with the technical and operational managers, to adapt and improve the activities accordingly.At local partner level, as a best practice, MdM will ensure to dedicate a phone number for feedback, complaints, and suggestions to the patients receiving the MHPSS support, the information coming through this number will be discussed amongst a committee from MoH and MdM to be addressed, the committee will work according to a TOR that will be developed.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The form of humanitarian assistance that MdM is providing does not further expose people to physical hazards, violence, or abuse. One of the components of the present proposal aims to increase access to quality services taking into consideration age and gender and safety for healthcare providers. MdM is taking into consideration the specificity of the current health situation with the presence of COVID-19 and the mental health wellbeing of the patients. Within the proposed intervention, MdM from a human rights perspective is combating the stigmatization of Mental health and COVID-19 through an awareness campaign. Within its intervention, MdM is following specific rules for the data collection, gathering, and publishing based on MdM GUIDELINES “Ethics in the Field”. As a rule, all MdM teams signed the MdM confidentiality charter, MdM is keeping safe storage of the beneficiaries’ data and information. When providing feedback to communities and stakeholders, maintaining confidentiality is critical for MdM and its team. The interest of beneficiaries is always protected, and individual names are not revealed in public or group meetings. Medical and mental health teams from MoH and the local partners who are providing medical and MHPSS services to vulnerable community members will be trained, supervised, and gender-balanced and it will be ensured that they are updated about the cultural and religious contexts. The project will also ensure that the communities are aware of the protection and complaints mechanisms in place. MdM interventions at the community level are provided by Al Atta and CFTA association team through outreach to the households which reduce the exposure of communities to harm and ensure equal access, including easier access for people with disabilities.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Impartiality, non-discrimination, gender, and inclusion are adopted by MdM within its intervention in Palestine. During MdM’s intervention in 2020 and 2021, MdM reinforced the capacity and supervision of MdM field staff on gender, GBV, and inclusion of PWD. MdM is addressing the needs of the local community based upon gender mainstreaming approach (gender-specific health), for instance, developing different IECs for women, men, and children, developing different protocols based on gender-based various manifestations, and also addressing all the barriers preventing PWD to access to services. With its outreach program, MdM can reach most of the victims affected by the critical events, with the support provided by a well-prepared Logistics department. MdM MEAL process is taken into consideration in the monitoring and documentation of needs per age, gender, vulnerabilities including people with disabilities, and targeted locations. PWDs are taken into consideration in all of MdM’s monitoring, evaluation and reporting processes. Gender mainstreaming is a very important consideration for all the MdM projects, following the MdM gender policy. Gender analysis is integrated into all the phases of project management such as diagnostic elaboration, objectives, results, activities and indicators design, implementation, and evaluation based on MdM gender policy and PSEA policy. For MdM, gender equality is one of the primary conditions for social sustainability in development and emergency interventions. Project activities encourage women to take on decision-making roles and ensure they are participating in project activities. As per the nature of the intervention, the provision of drugs and disposables will be delivered to MoH central drug stores and distributed to the facilities and beneficiaries based on the health needs. For MHPSS support, the specific needs per gender will be taken into consideration with the provision of services, a female provider will be available to service patients. Age and gender of children also will be taken into consideration while providing the service to their caregivers.
