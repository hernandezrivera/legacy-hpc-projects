Project Summary

The objective of the intervention is that vulnerable internally displaced households receive timely humanitarian assistance to meet their needs through the provision of shelter and non-food items (NFIs). Through this project NRC targets 10,000 households i.e., 50,000 individuals through the provision of emergency shelter kits, family tents and non- food items.
The intervention geographical coverage in Sudan will be in the following states – Gedaref, Al Jazirah, White Nile, West Darfur, and South Kordofan States. These 5 states have been prioritized because they have been increasingly affected by current Sudan armed conflict coupled with inter-communal violence in recent months and suffer from critical gaps in humanitarian response.
The proposed intervention will provide humanitarian assistance to individuals negatively impacted by conflict and displacement dynamics, a rapid onset natural disaster, within the first 14 days after the shock as part of the first line response. The target population will undergo Rapid Needs and Household level assessments that will further determine whether they meet the set vulnerability criteria and are selected. The main target populations will include:
• Individuals that have been directly affected by a conflict, either displaced or unable to flee.
• Individuals that have been directly impacted by rapid onset natural disasters.
• Vulnerable host populations (in displacement contexts).
Inclusion of vulnerable groups such as women, people with disabilities, and the elderly will be considered during the response to ensure they have access to services.

1 - Needs

Sudan has been affected by myriads of emerging and cyclical, man-made and natural disasters for the last decades, against an unraveling political backdrop. The humanitarian needs overview indicates that for 2023, the four most significant risks identified are conflict, disasters associated with natural hazards, disease outbreaks, and economic deterioration. Seasonal flooding continues to affect communities across Sudan causing devastation to housing, infrastructure, services, agriculture, livestock, and various other livelihood assets (food stocks, NFIs, etc.) Environmental degradation and drought continue to cause shifts in migration patterns, pushing many nomadic groups to transition from pastoralism to a semi-sedentary lifestyle, forcibly occupying the land and property of others in the process. While political issues fuel tensions throughout the country, competition for land and resources add to the mix as one of the major sources of conflict. On 15 April 2023, a violent power struggle broke out in Sudan’s capital of Khartoum between the two main factions of the ruling military regime: the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF), which acts as the official Sudanese army, and a paramilitary force, the Rapid Support Forces (RSF). Heavy gunfire and explosions were heard from several locations in Khartoum whilst significant clashes were reported throughout other parts of the country, including in El Fasher in North Darfur, El Obeid in North Kordofan, Nyala in South Darfur, Zalingei in Central Darfur, Kassala in Kassala State, and in West Darfur State. The conflict has had a severe impact on the civilians with at least 705 fatalities reported and 5,287 injured, according to the Federal Ministry of Health (FMoH), as of 16 May 2023. Over 1 million people have been displaced since the violent conflict began on 15 April, fleeing to safer locations inside and outside Sudan. Over 843,100 are internally displaced within Sudan, according to the International Organization for Migration’s Displacement Tracking Matrix (IOM DTM) as of 16 May. The states hosting the highest numbers of newly displaced people are White Nile (which hosts 25.2 per cent of all displaced people), West Darfur (18.6 per cent), River Nile (13.8 per cent) and Northern (13.34 per cent) states. In addition, over 248,000 people have crossed borders into neighbouring countries—Central African Republic, Chad, Egypt, Ethiopia and South Sudan—, according to the UN Refugee Agency (UNHCR). The violent conflict has had an impact on the displaced population and the population that remains in conflict affected areas who now require humanitarian and protection assistance to meet their basic needs. Affected populations are facing critical shortages of food, water, and basic supplies like fuel, particularly in dense urban areas like Khartoum, or in point of transit/reception (like Madani or Gedaref) as well as in greater Darfur (which hosts a large share of displaced and acutely food insecure people). People in need of shelter and NFI assistance are estimated to rise from 3 million to 5.7 million, while the target will see a more modest increase from 1.5 million to 1.9 million due to challenges with access, fuel, market functionality and banking services. At the onset of the crisis, Shelter and Non-Food Item (NFI) needs were already acute, exacerbated- by the significant underfunding of the cluster. The intensity of the new clashes and their indiscriminate nature is now causing widespread devastation to housing, household assets and public infrastructure, prompting many to leave their homes. The Immediate provision of shelter will be critical to ensure privacy and safety, mitigate GBV risks, and safeguard displaced populations from harsh environmental conditions to preserve their health amid the upcoming rainy season. Provision of essential household items will be crucial for restoring basic daily activities.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The objective of the intervention is that vulnerable internally displaced households receive timely humanitarian assistance to meet their needs through the provision of shelter and non-food items (NFIs). The intervention will target to reach a total number of 10,000 households (50,000 individuals) through the following proposed activities: Distribution of 10,000 non-food items (NFI) kits to vulnerable households: the kit will be based on the shelter and NFI cluster standards and will include the following items, 2 mosquito nets, 3 blankets, two jerrycans, 1 solar lamp, 3 sleeping mats, 2 plastic sheet and kitchen set Emergency 2,500 emergency shelter kit (ESK): Through the action, NRC will support affected households through the distribution of emergency shelter kits based on the shelter cluster standards i.e., 2 tarpaulins and 1 rope and tools Distribution of 1 000 family tents: Family tents will be distributed to affected communities mainly targeting PSNs and other vulnerable groups. The proposed intervention will provide humanitarian assistance to individuals negatively impacted by conflict and displacement dynamics and a rapid onset natural disaster. The target population will undergo Rapid Needs and Household level assessments that will further determine whether they meet the set vulnerability criteria and are selected. The main target populations will include: • Newly displaced people in open spaces, public buildings and camps, as well as those residing with friends or relatives, including populations in transit at border points or within the country. • Vulnerable host communities and residents in hotspot areas, utilizing community-based structures, and those returning to areas where safety has been restored• Flood-impacted communities.Inclusion of vulnerable groups such as women, people with disabilities, and the elderly will be considered during the response to ensure they have access to services.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Through the intervention, NRC will prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects which can increase people’s vulnerability to both physical and psycho-social risks. In this respect, NRC will prioritize safety and dignity of the beneficiaries and uphold the do no harm principle. This will be done by ensuring during the needs assessment, protection issues around selection of distribution sites, feelings of safety are analysed clearly. NRC will invest in conflict sensitivity especially for IDP groups and host community to ensure that we do not cause tension between the two groups. Meaningful access by beneficiaries will also be considered during the intervention. NRC will pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services such as women, the PSNs and people living with disabilities. These will be prioritized when arranging distribution points, during registration and ensuring they are included in decision making. NRC will also ensure there is meaningful community engagement and accountability to the targeted communities. This will be achieved through ensuring the presence of functional and accessible systems of providing feedback such as the hotline, suggestion boxes and the help desk during distributions. NRC will ensure that communities are able to use the feedback mechanisms and they also receive feedback for any concerns raised. Post distribution will also be used as a way to get feedback from the communities and will also inform how NRC will improve its operations.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

NRC is a member of the shelter and NFI cluster, as such through participation in the cluster meetings, NRC will be able to interact and receive feedback from other cluster members on the needs, feedback and lessons learned during relevant responses. Where possible NRC will participate in inter agency needs assessments to inform its programming. NRC will also ensure that its staff are aware of the existing referral pathways so as to ensure that any feedback from beneficiaries which NRC cannot address will be referred to the relevant agency and vice versa. Cases of SEA and abuse that will be received through NRC channels of feedback will be directed to the relevant contacts in the organization.
