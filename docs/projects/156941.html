Project Summary

POINT aims to reduce IDPs vulnerability in 8 camps during the 2019 winter by improving the infrastructure, providing fire extinguishers, improving the WASH facilities. Moreover, Point aims to alleviate the pressures and tensions caused by the mass displacement of civilians, combined with a shortage of basic services by: encouraging entrepreneurship and improving access to the local labour market by establishing new business which will lead to IDPs leaving the camps.
The planned intervention falls under the frameworks related to the CCCM Cluster strategic priorities. The project is designed in line with the following main activities in these 8 camps:
1- Improve the infrastructure of the IDPs camps against winter by a) gravelling the internal roads and pathways to latrines and WASH facilities. b) digging trenches to redirect the raining water and ultimately avoid floods.
2- Winterization of WASH facilities through upgrading the WASH facilities male/female segregation to ensure privacy by a) maintaining the buildings in the targeted camps b) lighting the facilities by the solar energy system to ensure the privacy and protection; c) providing cost-effective boiler “solar heater” to make certain that hot water is available.
3- Encourage community participation and ownership in the camps through: a) activating the community participation and volunteering committees in the targeted camps; b) training these committees to manage the WASH public facilities c) providing volunteering intensives.
4- Improve Fire Safety measures in Formal and Informal Settlements in the camps by: a) conducting Contingency Planning, first aid and how to use Fire Extinguishers trainings. These trainings will have a practical part through coaching sessions and provision of fire extinguishers. b) distributing guidelines and awareness materials for Fire Safety measures.
5- Distributing awareness materials for the hygiene concept and its practice in the camps and schools established in these camps.
6- Strengthening household and communal coping mechanisms in IDP sites and developing exit strategies through: a) Vocational Training: POINT will organize vocational trainings workshops for 200 participants. Each training will last for one month. The training workshops will be on different topics like: maintenance of public infrastructure (water and electricity systems); alternative sources of power, maintenance of house electrical devices and maintenance of Motorcycles… b) Mini Grants: Exceptional trainees (best 50 percent which equals 100 beneficiaries) will have the opportunity to attend business training and learn how to develop project proposals and receive mini grants to implement their projects. 50 individual beneficiaries will be awarded a mini grant of up to 800 USD. per beneficiary.
However, it’s worth to mention that these planned activities will not be implemented as one block for all mentioned camp, but rather, activities in each camp will be tailored according to each camps’ needs which will not be repeated for the same previously targeted camps.

Needs

According to OCHA report for north-west Syria in January 2019, The North-west is marked by ongoing conflict and witnessed hostilities throughout 2018. This has resulted in civilian casualties, damage and destruction of civilian infrastructure, and waves of mass displacement that continue to have significant impact on needs and vulnerabilities.There are some 2.8 million people in need including some 1.9 million IDPs, many of whom have displaced multiple times and are particularly vulnerable.Moreover, as for HNO 2018: In addition to general issues of over-crowding and cleanliness of sanitation facilities, assessments confirmed that protection issues, such as the lack of door locks and lights, lack of gender segregated facilities, long distance to the facilities as well as the lack of privacy and harassment on the way to facilities are considerable concerns for women and girls. Based on the CCCM cluster 2019 HRP results framework, POINT developed an assessment tool consistent with the cluster objective and after that, Point team assessed the current situation in 8 camps ( Khaled Bin Al Waleed, Al Imdad, Ghetaa Al Rahma2 (Al Kamouneh), Al Forkan, basmat amal, Al Wafa (Sarmada), Reef Aleppo Al Janobi, Blouzia) in Idleb governorate, Harim district, Dana subdistrict, Sarmada community in 29th January 2019; the following are the assessment’s findings:1- Public WASH facilities: All camps have public toilet blocks. 44% of these blocks are not effective at all, while 56% of them considered as effective.2- All toilet blocks need rehabilitation (installing water tanks and pipelines, rehabilitation of sewage systems, cleaning toilet, taps, solar system for hot water, lighting, desludging, doors and walls repairing and installing wastebaskets). 3- In 56% of the assessed camps, there are separated toilets for women. Only 32% of the camps have toilet for people with disabilities.4- Bathroom accessibility: camps' population considered the bathrooms and toilets “not safe” especially at night because of darkness, animals (mainly stray dogs) and soil roads.5- 56% of the roads in the camps are soil which turn into mud when the rain fall. While 19% of it stones and 25% graveled. In addition, no lighting in all camps roads.6- All camps don't have water trenches to avoid the rain flooding, so the rain water go through the tents and roads.7- Only 7% of the camp population have the necessary tools for winter weather related issues, while 93% of them don`t have these tools.8- 63% of camps have community participation in camp managements as committees. Their role is to support the distribution of relief parcels, solve problems, consultation. While 37% of camps don’t have this committees.9- 81% of the camps don't have contingency plan, or fire extinguishers. While 19% of camps have contingency plans and fire extinguishers and we trained the IDP's about the contingency planning and fire extinguishing. Moreover, the above-mentioned assessment revealed an inadequate advocacy on community participation which led to poor awareness about IDPs’ role in decision-making to encourage exiting camps.Only 25% of the assessed camps has been supported with vocational trainings since 2011 and no one were supported with grants in all camps. Most IDPs relying on basic assistance provided by organizations to receive foods and NFI basic assistance in form of monthly food baskets and water trucking.IDPs living in camps have very limited income that is based on daily labour mostly, where around 60% of IDPs in camps have almost zero income per month. Moreover, dependency ratio of HHs living in camps is very high, it is averaging on 7 to 1, meaning that each household includes one person generating income, verses 7 that are not. finally, most respondents provided positive feedback to support beneficiaries’ livelihoods to build their resilience and encourage them to exit camp.

Implementation of activities

POINT through this project plans to intervene in three components in 8 assessed camps in Idleb governorate, Harim district, Dana subdistrict, Sarmada community that are compatible with the HRP & CCCM objectives for 2019 and targeting about 15977 IDPs (Adult male 2754, Adult female 3726, Boys 4642, Girls 4855) living in these camps. First component is tailored to meet priorities and directly linked to CCCM cluster first objective and allocation strategy “ Providing streamlined life-saving, humanitarian multi-sectoral assistance and strengthening the basic infrastructure support in IDP sites”. In this component, Point plans to:  Minimizing the bad effects of harsh weather on IDPs living in the above-mentioned camps in Idleb area by establishing several winterization related activities that aims at enhancing IDPs abilities to adapt and have better response to the coming winter by conducting the following activities:1. Improving the infrastructure of the IDPs sites against winter based on community by:• Digging trenches to redirect the raining water and avoid floods.• Graveling internal roads and pathway to latrines and shower-baths facilities.2. Improving the fire safety measures in the targeted camps by:• Establishing contingency planning, first aid and fire reduction trainings to previously established community’s committees with both gender representation (males and females).• Establishing fire points in the targeted camps that contains firefighting tools and fire extinguishers.• Establishing awareness campaigns targeting all IDPs in the site and distributing Fire safety flyers.3. Improving the hygiene levels and hygiene awareness among IDPs in camps by:• Rehabilitating the public wash facilities and segregate them for both gender (males & females) which will lead to better protection especially for women and girls.• Establishing wash facilities maintenance mechanism and forming community’s committees to follow up with this mechanism.• Conducting wash management training to the formed committees for both male and females.• Conducting hygiene promotion campaigns to IDPs in camps and distributing flyers that promote for maintaining cleanliness of the camp that targets both genders and all ages especially children. The second planned component is linked to the CCCM cluster second objective that aims at “improving the management quality and accountability of IDP sites” by:  Encouraging community participation and mobilization among IDPs in camps by:• Forming communities’ committees with both genders’ representation according to a previously developed criteria and in coordination with all actors in the camp.• Building the capacity for the formed committees and provide them with trainings about the concept of camps managements’ approaches and mechanism. The third planned component is directly linked to third CCCM objective and allocation strategy related to “Strengthening household and communal coping mechanisms in IDP sites and developing exit strategies” in the targeted areas. The project is in line with CCCM and HPF priorities and as this project is going to strengthen the CCCM camp-exit and decongestion program by Community participation through vocational capacity initiatives, apprenticeships (post vocational training for some careers that need practical experience), grants to selected beneficiaries and business coaching and support by:  Establishing camps’ exit strategy and decongestion related activities:• Support community mobilization by forming one community committees in each of the targeted camps.• Improving livelihoods opportunity for male and female IDPs through provision of vocational trainings.• Provide beneficiaries with business training and develop their business plans.• Support best projects with mini grants to start their own business.• Select training participants for apprenticeships opportunities to link them with labour market outside camps and support them with incentives.

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk:Women and girls not able to participate in the contingency planning trainings due to community or local authorities’ restriction for direct intermingling between males and females.Likelihood: High. Impact: Medium.Mitigating measures: if the mentioned risk happened, point will conduct separated trainings for males and females. Protection risk:Rehabilitation of infrastructure activities might harden the accessibility for roads in the camp.Likelihood: medium. Impact: High.Mitigating measures: Point will be aware for safety consideration and follow safety procedures. Moreover, Point field staff will make sure to redirect people to alternative roads after informing the IDPs for these activities. Protection risk:The public WASH facilities might get partially unaccusable during the rehabilitation period.Likelihood: High. Impact: High.Mitigating measures: Point will implement the rehabilitation related activities on several stages to avoid the total closure of the facility for the rehabilitation period. Moreover, the community committees will play a significant role in managing the accessibility for these WASH facilities. Protection risk:Increased risk of inter-community tension due to varied assistance grants among beneficiaries.Likelihood: High. Impact: High.Mitigating measures: Point will share the developed selection criteria for participant and the grants mechanism including the proposed projects’ assessment criteria.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

 Assumptions: - Cooperation from other NGOs in working together to improve the infrastructure in the rest camps. Risks: - Rain full that will affect the solid which will affect the rehabilitation work.Mitigation Plan:POINT plans to conduct the related activities in summer season where the weather is stable.  Assumptions:- contribution from white helmet (Syrian Civil defense) in training the IDPs committees- Supporting from trained committees in distributing the fire extinguishers Risks: - Cross boarding problem from Turkey to Syria for shipping the fire extinguishers from Turkey to Syria.Mitigation Plan:POINT will secure the items from inside Syria from suppliers inside Syria.  Assumptions:- based on the training that we will provide it to committees, we assume for more community participation in implementing the activities Risks:- Weather conditions that will affect the project’s activities.- Destroying public WASH facilities by vandal.Mitigation Plan:By POINT trained and experienced staff we will provide training for Communities on how to manage the WASH facilities in Camps, Also we will rehabilitate and maintain the WASH facilities as preparation for winter by volunteers from community and committees with volunteers intensive, who will be trained for promoting public facilities owner ship and follow up with facilities affairs.  Assumptions:collaboration from camps management to support this initiative community initiatives Risk: Lack of responsiveness from IDPs in camps to interact with the newly established initiatives.Mitigation: to announce, introduce, and simplify the idea of these initiative to IDPs and promote for community participation. Risk: little number of female applicants, Acceptance by private sector representatives mitigation: From inception of operational activities the project will be implemented in consultation with local stakeholders, including authorities, relevant NGOs and private sector partners to ensure meaningful engagement in the project planning and coordination. Risk: Volatile economic conditions could impact decisions of local businessMitigation: Career coach who can provide counseling and employer mobilization to support remaining participants not chosen for apprenticeships.
