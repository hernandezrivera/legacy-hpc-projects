Project Summary

Nkafamiya Rescue Mission (NRM) undertakes around 3 projects per year supporting nearly 300,000 people across Adamawa and Borno states. NRM has been present in Adamawa since September 2019, and has developed a good expertise to address the needs of crisis-affected populations through a community based comprehensive integrated response. NRM seeks to implement live-saving activities, strengthen the capacities of the locals, provide an appropriate response and give a primary place to the accountability to affected population in all implemented programs. Given the persisting humanitarian needs within the BAY States, and the increased security threats towards the civilian resulting in constant attack by the NSAGs, Madagali and Michika LGAs, the proposed project aims to improve access to basic services through an integrated WASH and nutrition response for conflict affected populations, based on a community approach.

1 - Needs

The armed conflict in North east Nigeria has continued through the year 2021 with Adamawa state being among the worst hit. The people who are in need of humanitarian assistance has risen from 7.9 million in the beginning of the year 2021 to 10.6 million people towards the end; 80% of this vulnerable population are women and children. It has been estimated that over 300,000 children are acutely malnourished across the BAY states. Also, over 40% of the health facilities have been destroyed in the region. The nutrition situation has significantly deteriorated in towards the end of the year 2020, owing to the lean season (rising food prices and low harvest) complicated by disease outbreaks and continued insecurity situation. Madagali and Michika have continued to report arrival of returnee’s people from neighboring communities where they have been staying during the insurgent’s attacks. In Madagali in 2021 about 240 households arrived the community between January to October 2021 alone (NRM, need assessment report 2021). These new arrivals depend on the existing health and nutrition facilities their communities, adding to the needs to be addressed. Michika LGA has continued to witness increased humanitarian needs due to the conflict's prolonged nature and OAGs territorial extension, especially ISWAP around this area. The overall humanitarian crisis further deteriorated due to the adverse effects of the COVID-19 pandemic and related restrictive measures. Although strict lockdown measures were not implemented in Madagali and Michika, the impact was significantly felt since interstate movements was imposed that lasted till June 2020. During this period, a 30% increase has been reported on admission caseload for nutrition treatment in the BAY region. In September 2020, UNICEF conducted a sentinel survey following multiple nutrition alerts from various LGAs including Adamawa, where the survey by MUAC revealed an increase in the GAM rate by 13.9% in the second and third quartile of the year, with GAM rate as high as 15.38% among children aged 6-23 months in the state.

2 - Activities

NRM nutrition intervention strategy will continue to adopt evidence based integrated multi sectoral approach including WASH, FSL and protection towards addressing the nutrition gaps in Adamawa state. NRM plans to reach both host population and IDPs including returnees in Madagali and Michika LGAs. To ensure relevant impact on reducing malnutrition with in the community, NRM delivers integrated Nutrition package of CMAM and IYCF. In addition, NRM is planning to start targeted supplementary feeding program in Madagali and Michika LGAs using locally sourced nutrition rich foods such as Tom-brown to address MAM. IYCF interventions with focus on preventive Social Behavior Change, Communication through a network of community outreach volunteers and Mother Care Support Groups as well as skilled based counseling of IYCF at health facilities mainly critical contact points in the OTP, SC and TSFP services. Alongside with IYCF messaging, NRM also target MNP distributions for 6-23 months’ children to improve dietary fortification of essential micronutrients. CMAM approach for timely identification and treatment of MAM in line with WHO and MoH guidelines while applying new sustainable innovations through the provision of therapeutic supplies for management of malnutrition, and extending support to MAM cases in the Stabilization Center as well as empowering the community to address malnutrition using locally sourced nutritious foods. To reduce exposure of COVID-19, delivery of nutrition activities at the community level will be an important step to ensure affected population has access to the services and implement COVID-19 infection prevention and control measures. NRM will adopt the nutrition sector proposed strategies to mitigate the outbreak such as implementation of Mother-MUAC approach for continuation of screening, ensuring of protective measures are applied by Health care providers and volunteers and sensitization of the beneficiary communities on the preventive measures of Covid-19. To ensure continued screening, identification and referral of acute malnutrition to facilities, the mother-MUAC approach will be continued through training of the mothers of children <5years on how to asses’ acute malnutrition using MUAC screening and oedema identification and referral of identified SAM cases to the nearest service providers. The approach significantly reduces exposure between the nutrition service providers and beneficiaries by avoiding gatherings for screening at the community. The mothers trained on the Mother-MUAC approach will perform MUAC screening for their children and will refer for confirmation and treatment of the identified SAM children to the nearest OTP. In the context of restricted gathering during screening in the context of Covid-19 outbreak, this will give mothers ability to identify the earliest signs of malnutrition. All NRM and MoH staff working in PUI supported health facilities and in the community will be trained on the prevention measures of the COVID-19 infection to protect themselves beneficiaries. Meanwhile all the facilities will adopt the infection prevention measures through applying hand washing at entrances of health facilities, social distancing of 2m between service providers and between beneficiaries and establishing screenings sites at the gates of all NRM activity sites.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The Nutrition program, especially through the IYCF component directly empowers women and mothers in the community through: Identification of mothers to take part in the Mother Care Groups activities to raise awareness on IYCF key messages. Through community consultation at the beneficiary selection phase, NRM will ensure that the community is aware of and in support of the beneficiary selection criteria to reduce risk of tension arising from the assistance and that the most vulnerable populations are selected for the intervention; Creating strong community linkages between women and mothers in the community through MCGs Promoting the election of Lead Mothers within each MCGs. Through lessons learnt from previous programming, targeted populations have provided valuable feedback into this component. Among those are creating additional MCGs as many women within the community had growing interest, better integration into NRM protection, WASH and Food Security programming, and the need for identification and treatment of MAM. Moreover, all Nutrition outreach workers are provided with protection mainstreaming trainings in addition of induction sessions on the safe identification and referral of protection issues to relevant protection officers internally, in addition to training on NRM’s code of conduct and humanitarian principles.

NRM has a strong mechanism in place that relies on various channels. Feedbacks are mainly captured through: a. Community Committees Meetings that are organized once a month with community leaders, elders, women and NRM program staff to discuss project activities, gaps and needs; b. Community Volunteers Network which comprised all outreach workers who directly link NRM to targeted communities; and c. LABDI Complaint Mechanism which consist of helpdesks in the community managed by dedicated trained staff.
