Project Summary

This intervention links to the strategic objectives outlined in the 2024 HRP by providing a life-saving humanitarian intervention through an integrated health, nutrition and WASH package for conflict affected and vulnerable displaced population, their host communities, returnees and other vulnerable groups in West Darfur, South Kordofan, West Kordofan and Red Sea State.
The total direct target for this integrated services pack is 385, 000 beneficiaries across all targeted states: West Darfur, South Kordofan, West Kordofan and Red Sea States.
The project will focus on addressing the most critical, life-saving humanitarian needs for conflict affected, as well as protracted vulnerable population in health, nutrition and institution WASH services through static and mobile outreach services in the targeted localities,. Concern’s operating in the target localities and in 2023 scaled up in Red Sea State in response to displacement as the result of the current fighting in Sudan.
The proposed operational and financial plans are based on projections through BHA, EU, Irish Aid, UN (UNICEF, WFP, SHF, IOM) and other private Funding. The 2024 programming targets to support 83 facilities across 4 states: 30 facilities in West Darfur, and 59 facilities in Kordofan and 4 health facilities in Red Sea State.
The intervention will increase access to essential lifesaving primary health care (PHC) , including provision of pharmaceuticals, consumables, and LLINs maternal, reproductive health, and child health and community education for vulnerable populations (pregnant and lactating women, and children under 5) will be provided. In Line with health sector objectives, Concern will focus on key humanitarian interventions including strengthening existing emergency obstetric and new-born care facilities, provision of Antenatal care, skilled delivery and postnatal, immunization service, curative consultation and support the referral system including covering operational costs, fuel and WASH support in health facilities. In addition, reactivating the disease surveillance system and reporting in both government and partner supported facilities and supplying the static vaccination centres with the needed supplies will be a priority.
The need for increased access to essential, lifesaving, and quality nutrition services and community sensitization for the management of moderate to severe acute malnutrition of the vulnerable population (pregnant and lactating women, and children under 5) will also be addressed. In line with the nutrition sector objectives, integrated with the health interventions , Concern will scale up maternal and young child nutrition services. The priority will be the , early detection and treatment of severe and moderate acute malnutrition among under-five children, pregnant and lactating women, preventive nutrition interventions, and micronutrient supplementation among children under age 5 and PLW to prevent further deterioration of nutrition situation. Mother and child nutrition care will focus on improving IYCF practices, and improve dietary practices through community engagement and Mother Support Groups .
Finally, improvement and maintenance of WASH services within health facilities, and hygiene-related community sensitization for vulnerable population (pregnant and lactating women, and children under 5), including distribution of essential dignity kits, will be guaranteed. In line with the revised HRP and at targeted facility levels, Concern will support provision or rehab of latrines, incinerators, and handwashing points depending on needs assessments; water trucking; distribution of essential WASH supplies and NFI emergency sanitation; and extensive hygiene promotion especially for those reliant on surface/river

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Concern’s protection strategy is guided by Code of Conduct and associated policies. Concern’s Code of Conduct sets out all steps to protect programme beneficiaries. Concern’s approach to protection is based on the key concept of analysis and management of risks: i) the risks faced by the communities, including refugees, we assist (e.g. risk on the way to access health care services); iii) the risks our programme may create if it is not properly designed (e.g. not considering host communities only conflict affected IDPs for services delivery). Concern also updated its Child Safeguarding and Child Protection Policy that will be applied for this intervention. Among other vulnerable individuals, refugee mothers and their children from the host communities who walk many hours to reach the centres, as well as for distributions, will be oriented about ways to protect themselves on the way. Each HF will refer to, will maintain the availability of a protection focal person and the communities will be informed about the role of this person. Traditional healers will be targeted by behaviour change activities at community level, as well as Lead Mothers in the Mother support groups. Focal persons in each HF (one male and one female) will be responsible for dealing with GBV cases and related protection issues. In the community where new IDPs settled, there will be also protection focal points (possibly one female, and one male) for dealing with sensitive issues. Children will be in focus, with field staff duly reporting on safeguarding. Beneficiaries will be oriented about their rights regarding the project objectives (e.g. access to information, access to services). In order to increase equal access for the most vulnerable from different ethical groups and backgrounds (IDPs, refugees, and host communities), information sharing about project implementation, achievements, selection of beneficiaries, will be provided continuously through a selected focal person in each group.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

Concern's CRM system that was updated in 2022 will play an important role: the programme will work with CRM officers across all operation areas, and we will activate the CRM reporting system including sensitive and no-sensitive reporting. Each health facility that we are targeted will serve as a CRM hub where one male and one female person will be identified and oriented on how to receive report and manage individual cases. Post-distribution monitoring will be organized after in-kind distribution such as hygiene kit, and mosquito nets (max. two weeks) that will be a support and opportunity for participants to feedback about the services received. PDM feedbacks will inform the programme team about the next step for the activity (including future programming), and also will provide support in case finding (both sensitive and non-sensitive) and address the complaints and feedbacks.. Feedback will also help the programme adapt the quality of services delivery e.g. health treatment, (like/dislike) choices will support programme team to improve the services.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Concern’s protection strategy is guided by two central documents, notably the organisation’s Programme Participant Protection Policy and Code of Conduct; and the 2010 Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP) Standard in Accountability and Quality Management. Concern’s Programme Participant Protection Policy sets out all steps that are reasonably possible to protect programme beneficiaries – children, women and men. Concern’s approach to protection is based on the key concept of analysis and management of risks: i) the risks faced by the communities we assist; ii) iii) the risks our programme may create; and reducing vulnerabilities as a consequence. As certified member of HAP, Concern is committed to implementing the HAP Standard related to different aspects of beneficiaries’ accountability. Three of these aspects are particularly relevant to conflict sensitivity and protection: beneficiaries’ access to information; beneficiaries’ participation in all the stages of the programme; and establishing a Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM). As of August 2022, Concern Sudan’s Complaint and Response Mechanism (CRM) was updated and aligned with overall staff and communities’ expectations as well as ground realities through a consultative exercise undertaken with the aim to adjust Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs).Concern has a Safeguarding and Accountability Coordinator who will work with the CRM Team to get the feedback from the project participants e.g. Every 6 months Accountability team will conduct FGDs with programme participants, as well as interviews with other stakeholders related to effectiveness of CRM system. At the time of conclusion of a complaint, each complainant will be asked for their feedback using a Satisfaction and Consultation form. Based on data received from these two sources, Concern will regularly evaluate and re-design the CRM system to improve its accessibility, responsiveness, efficiency and anonymity. Accountability towards affected populations as well as awareness raising is ensured through information materials as brochures, posters, in Arabic, and pictures for those are not able to read and write. . All these items will be distributed to programme participants and will be posted/available wherever Concern Sudan work to ensure transparent information sharing.
