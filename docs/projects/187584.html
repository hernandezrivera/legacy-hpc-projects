Project Summary

The public health system in Syria has been degraded by a decade of conflict and remains extremely vulnerable as a result of the displacement and migration of qualified professionals and the widespread destruction and damage to health facilities. The COVID-19 pandemic has further strained existing capacity. Therefore, Palestine refugees will continue to rely heavily on the Agency for health care.
UNRWA's 25 health facilities in Syria (including 23 health centres and health points located in Palestine refugee camps and two mobile clinics) provide a range of primary health services, including out-patient consultations, immunization services, infant and child growth monitoring, dispensing of medications, gynaecology consultations, laboratory services, NCD care, maternal and child health care, dental and MHPSS services. The two mobile clinics will be deployed to areas affected by conflict or localized tensions where refugees are facing movement restrictions and to locations where Agency health facilities have been damaged or destroyed during the conflict.
UNRWA will continue to subsidize access to secondary and tertiary care (including COVID-19 treatment) for around 9,000 refugee cases (400 COVID-19 cases and 8,600 non-COVID-19 cases) through a network of partnered hospitals and private laboratories. This intervention has become increasingly critical as families’ ability to afford hospitalization services continues to decrease. The support provided by UNRWA will cover between 75 and 95 per cent of the costs, depending on the level of vulnerability and availability of funds.
Dedicated support will be provided to persons with disabilities through the provision of prosthetic devices, including hearing aids for the elderly. Persons with long-term disabilities as a result of conflict-related injuries and in need of physiotherapy will be referred to external partners in community-based organizations. It is estimated that 8,000 physiotherapy sessions will be required.
In 2022, UNRWA will also conduct minor repairs to some of its health facilities that require urgent maintenance. This does not include facilities covered under the Emergency Repair, Rehabilitation and Maintenance of UNRWA Installations section in this appeal.
Under this appeal, the Agency will also continue to respond to the COVID-19 emergency through subsidizing COVID-19 testing for 2,400 Palestine refugees. As in 2021, COVID-19 testing will be extended to UNRWA health and non-health staff to contain the spread of COVID-19 and ensure the safety of Agency staff and the refugees they serve. In addition, UNRWA will continue to ensure that health centres are equipped with PPE to protect patients and frontline staff, and that disinfectants and sanitization materials are also available at all facilities
.
Since May 2021, UNRWA has been responsible for administering COVID-19 vaccinations for all United Nations (UN) staff and partner NGOs in Syria as part of the UN vaccination campaign, which has contributed significantly to the inter-agency COVID-19 response. UNRWA is also planning to vaccinate Palestine refugees if government approval is secured. This appeal will support the recruitment of five additional staff for the vaccination campaign and the establishment of a cold room in Damascus to store the vaccines.

4 - Needs

The public health system in Syria has been degraded by a decade of conflict and remains extremely vulnerable as a result of the displacement and migration of qualified professionals and the widespread destruction and damage to health facilities. The COVID-19 pandemic has further strained existing capacity. In 2021, UNRWA’s health services in Syria faced increasing pressures due to the socio-economic crisis in the country and the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic. Therefore, the 438,000 Palestine refugees living in Syria are in acute need. In the context of the socio-economic crisis and pressure on the public health system due to COVID-19, Palestine refugees increasingly rely on humanitarian assistance to respond to their basic health needs. This includes 6,275 persons with disabilities, who are especially vulnerable. More than 600 Palestine refugee families returned to Yarmouk and Ein El Tal refugee camps and are particularly in need for support. Due to the heavy damages of UNRWA health facilities during the conflict, Palestine refugees in these camps currently depend entirely on mobile health services.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

(1) Exposure to Explosive remnants of war as Palestine refugees access UNRWA Health centres. Mitigating measures: 1.Training and awareness-sessions to UNRWA staff and community members is conducted; large-scale distribution of ERW awareness raising fliers is organized to enhance awareness among the community, particularly in areas with high levels of destruction. 2. Mobile health clinics and health points have been established, in close proximity to the PR communities, minimizing refugees’ movement to access the health services; immunization campaigns limit unnecessary movement. 3. Telehealth services are provided for patients which helps limit their exposure. 4. UNRWA delivers regular medication of NCD patients to their homes and can also provide them by delegation through relatives or friends. (2) Safety, dignity and privacy are eroded for GBV survivors and vulnerable groups when attending health clinics due to overcrowding. Mitigation measures: 1. UNRWA is using telemedicine to reduce the number of in-person consultations at the health clinics. 2. NCD patients are receiving long term supplies of their medication to reduce the number of visits made to the health clinics. 3..Ensure numbering system and prioritization of most vulnerable; 4.Ensure private spaces are made available where possible 5.Train staff on GBV.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Discrimination of vulnerable groups and risk of SEA in the context of provision of health services Likelihood: medium Impact: high Mitigation Measures:- 1. UNRWA staff prioritize vulnerable groups such as women, older persons and persons with disabilities in order to reduce their waiting time.- 2. UNRWA provides training on protection mainstreaming and disability inclusion. - 3. UNRWA is conducting protection audits on a biennial basis to get feedback from the staff and the community on how to improve its services and how to optimize its compliance to the main protection principles.- 4. All UNRWA staff sign the code of conduct on commencement of their contracts.- 5. Training to staff on the code of conduct and PSEA and regular reinforcement of the zero-tolerance policy as well as UNRWA staff standards of conduct, reporting obligations and channels for SEA.- 6. Reinforced messaging to community members on the importance of reporting SEA and ensure mechanisms are in place to ensure confidentiality and set up accessible and efficient complaint mechanisms. - 7. UNRWA is enhancing focus on community engagement and participation, and makes sure feedback is responded to.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: 1. Humanitarian needs of of PRs in Syria remain relatively stable 2. Sustained commitment of donors to support UNRWA financially 3. Security situation and active violence do not deteriorate any further. 4. COVID-19 pandemic challenges remain within the scope of the Agency's capacity to respond financially and operationally. Risk UNRWA is unable to continue providing services and respond to PR needs because of lack of funding or due to increased humanitarian needs. Last year, UNRWA received only around 30% of its requirements for Syria. Mitigation: 1. continue to monitor the situation and to adjust plans prioritizing most pressing needs 2. Regularly engage with donors and partners to leverage funding opportunities 3. Ensure robust financial, planning and management systems 4. Maintain communication with stakeholders on status of funding. 5. Continue to monitor its interventions through a results-based monitoring framework. Risk UNRWA staff and PR are exposed to violence and security threats. Mitigation: 1. Monitor security updates and conduct security assessments for facilities and missions 2. Train staff and beneficiaries on ERW, and personal safety. Risk: COVID-19 pandemic situation persists/worsens, leading to further operational challenges. Mitigation: 1. UNRWA will adapt its programme modality to ensure sustained delivery of services while prioritizing the safety of staff and beneficiaries 2. PPEs and sanitization materials will be provided to frontline staff
