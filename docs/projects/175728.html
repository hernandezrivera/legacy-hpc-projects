Project Summary

The emergency nutrition response is targeting children under five years and pregnant and lactating mothers from host communities and Internally displaced persons in flood and conflict affected counties of Tonj South, Tonj North and Tonj East Counties (Warrap state) and Ulang County (Upper Nile state) with chronic emergency nutrition needs.
The planned nutrition activities will as well support health, WASH and GBV prevention interventions by creating synergies with the health, GBV and WASH clusters through Health, GBV and WASH implementing partners so as to reduce nutrition related morbidity and mortality by reducing the prevalence of global acute malnutrition in children under five years and pregnant and lactating mothers (PLWs) in Tonj North, Tonj South and Tonj East Counties (Wrrap state) and Ulang county (Upper Nile state). Therefore, the proposed project activities and targets include early detection of malnutrition through nutrition screening at the nutrition site and community level and treatment of 2800 girls and boys under five years of age and 1350 pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition though establishment of eight (8) nutrition sites, 2 sites per county, micronutrient supplementation to 5200 under five children with vitamin A, provide behavioral change messages to 3700 care takers of malnourished children with maternal infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN) counselling to improve nutrition at household level, conduct 32 cooking demonstrations to the beneficiaries so as to improve on the nutrition diversity and nutrition status at household level, carry out deworming for 6200 children 12-59 months with albendazole tablets to prevent iron deficiency anemia due to worm infestation, conduct monitoring visits to the nutrition sites to make sure nutrition services offered as per the sphere standards, conduct testing of malaria for 1000 severe acute malnourished children under five years so as to reduce the prevalence of malaria among malnourished children, provide WASH kits to the caregivers of 1000 severe acute malnourished (SAM) children to improve on their hygiene and sanitation conditions at household level to reduce the prevalence of diarrheal diseases among these children, establish a kitchen gardens at every nutrition site for demonstration to the mothers and care takers of malnourished children so as to improve the availability of kitchen gardens at household level hence improving food security and dietary diversity, stimulate play for acutely malnourished children by provision of child play materials at every nutrition site, conduct Gender based violence safety audits at every nutrition site to make sure child protection and gender-based violence mitigation measures are guaranteed. The project intends to support nutrition needs of 2,1300 beneficiaries from most vulnerable food insecure host communities and internally displaced persons (IDPs) beneficiaries of Tonj North ( 2250 boys, 2800 girls, 1540 women), Tonj South (1600 boys, 2150 girls, 1150 women) and Tonj East Counties (1950 boys,1750 girls,1400 women) of Warrap state and Ulang county ( 1950 boys, 1750 girls, 1010 women) in Upper Nile State, mostly children under five years with acute malnutrition and malnourished pregnant and lactating mothers. This project comes at a time when these Counties have been affected by floods causing heavy displacements and continuously experience intercommunal conflicts worsening the vulnerability evidenced by high rates of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) due food insecurity caused by poor harvests and death of livestock, limited time for cultivation due to displacements and high food prices due to poor access to markets and inflation. The situation has impacted negatively on the already limited basic services in these locations. Therefore, this project will address the needs of the most vulnerable women, boys and girls affected by the conflict and malnutrition integrated with GBV risks mitigation.

1 - Needs

According to South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) report 2021, intermittent communal conflicts and natural disasters like floods coupled with the resent COVID-19 pandemic in South Sudan has deepened the country into humanitarian crisis with 8.3 million people in South Sudan estimated to be in dire need of humanitarian assistance in 2021. The communities of Tonj East, Tonj North and Tonj East Counties of Warrap state and Ulang County of Upper Nile state have experienced unprecedented humanitarian crisis which has been heightened by the recent heavy floods that has seen more than 50% of the households displaced, loss of harvests, destruction of farmlands and livestock that has plugged these counties into severe food insecurity that has led to high prevalence of malnutrition. According to the IPC report published in October 2020 on the food insecure counties, 5% of the population in Tonj East and Tonj South appeared in IPC phase 5 (catastrophe) and the situation is projected to worsen in the first and second projection while 10% of the population in Tonj North was in IPC phase 5 and is projected to remain like that or worsen in the first and second projection. According to the same report, the drivers of this crisis is food insecurity due to destruction of farm land and harvests, death of livestock and lack of access to markets leading to increased food prices, communal conflicts that have led to displacements majorly Tonj North and Tonj East with poor access to food and COVID-19 pandemic that has increased travel restrictions, transportation of food commodities to the markets hence increased food prices and scaled down nutrition and food security interventions and poor WASH facilities hence unsafe water consumption by the households and inadequate water storage containers that lead to inadequate water supply and this exposes children to frequent episodes of diarrhoeal diseases hence malnutrition. According to Ulang county UN-OCHA lead IRNA report October 2020 which TADO participated, Ulang County is faced with severe food insecurity due to flooding resulting from heavy rains and bursting of the river banks of River Sobat that caused severe displacement of more than 50% of the households, destroyed farmlands, harvests and live stocks. This coupled with communal conflicts, COVID-19 restrictions, inadequate health and nutrition services has plugged the county into high prevalence of malnutrition with high GAM rates way above the WHO minimum standard of 15%. TADO proposes to bridge this gap by responding to nutrition needs of 21,300 beneficiaries in Ulang county (Upper Nile state) and Tonj East, Tonj North and Tonj South counties (Warrap State) by establishing 8 nutrition sites in these locations mostly in the payams with high population of IDPs and inadequate health and nutrition services where people walk long distances to receive nutrition services. TADO will utilize the operational and static presence in Tonj South, Tonj East and Ulang with field offices where we are implementing health projects currently in the three counties to make sure equitable access to quality preventive and curative nutrition services to children and women are offered to the vulnerable population. The synergy with other existing health, Nutrition, WASH and protection interventions by other implementing partners in these counties will easily contribute improve cluster objectives by reducing acute suffering and prevalence of global acute malnutrition. The 6 health facilities and mobile clinics operated by TADO with no active nutrition services will easily integrate nutrition services including other payams which urgently need nutrition intervention. Due to TADO’s operational and static presence in Gorgrial East, Tonj South and Tonj East, it will be easy for TADO to access Tonj North in terms of proximity and easy coordination at the County and state level hence quality service delivery.

2 - Project activities

TADO will address the nutritional challenges of the vulnerable population in Tonj South, Tonj North, Tonj East and Ulang counties though the following activities:1. Conduct early detection of malnutrition through nutrition screening at the nutrition site and community level and treatment of girls and boys under five years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition though establishment of two nutrition sites per county.2. Provide micronutrient supplementation to the under five children (Vitamin A) and pregnant women (Iron and folic acid) at every nutrition site during nutrition visits and growth monitoring.3. Provide behavioral change messages to the mothers and care takers of malnourished children with maternal infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN) counselling to improve the nutrition at household level.4. Conduct cooking demonstrations to the beneficiaries so as to improve on the nutrition diversity and nutrition status at household level.5. Conduct deworming for children 12-59 months with albendazole tablets to prevent iron deficiency anemia due to worm infestation. 6. Conduct monitoring visits to the nutrition sites to make sure nutrition services offered are of quality following the ministry of nutrition guidelines.7. Conduct testing and treatment of malaria for acute malnourished children under five years so as to reduce the prevalence of malaria among malnourished children.8. Distribute WASH kits to the caregivers of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) children to improve on their hygiene and sanitation conditions at household level to reduce the prevalence of diarrheal diseases among these children.9. Establish kitchen gardens at every nutrition site for demonstration to the mothers and care takers of malnourished children so as to improve the availability of kitchen gardens at household level hence improving food security and dietary diversity.10. Stimulate play for acutely malnourished children by provision of child play materials at every nutrition site.11. Conduct Gender based violence safety audits at every nutrition site to make sure child protection and gender-based violence mitigation measures are guaranteed.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

TADO has protection officer that will constantly sensitize the beneficiaries on the protection against sexual exploitation and abuse from the inception of the project and during project implementation. The local authorities shall be oriented on protection against sexual exploitation and abuse so that they can sensitize the beneficiaries in their respective meetings. 7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The people living with disability shall be sensitized on the avenues of feed back so that their opinions can count at every level of implementation. TADO shall involve women, girls and people with disability at every level of the project implementation and this will help on improving the avenues of feedback from these vulnerable groups.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

TADO always provides information to the beneficiaries about the projects and creates feed back mechanisms for the beneficiaries. 7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

8.11. Mitigation Measures

1. Risks that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services. This is a medium risk and therefore TADO shall make sure safety audits are conducted at every nutrition site for women and girls so that protection risks are identified and dealt with. TADO shall also involve the local authorities to make sure women and girls are protected from violence along the way.2. Risks of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance. This a low risk and TADO shall make sure that the beneficiaries especially women and girls are sensitized from the inception of the project on protection of sexual exploitation and abuse and their rights. Women leaders shall be involved at every stage of the project implementation so that they are sensitized on PSEA and continue to sensitize the women and girls. A PSEA focal person shall be appointed and introduced to the community so that any early warning signs of sexual exploitation and abuse can be reported to the responsible authorities.3. Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.). This is a medium risk since the protection services at community level are limited. TADO will include protection awareness in our routine programming so that vulnerable beneficiaries especially women, girls and children shall be sensitized on the possible protection issues and prevention measures. TADO shall work with the protection partners in the project locations so that the referral pathway is established and active to support the survivors of GBV and child protection issues among others.4. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and Older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. This is a medium risk and TADO shall make sure that marginalized groups like persons with disability, older persons and women are involved in the decision making process at every level of project implementation.
