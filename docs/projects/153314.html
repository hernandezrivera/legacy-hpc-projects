Project Summary

Following the recent revitalization of peace, much more efforts is still needed to rebuild and restructure the health system in South Sudan after the civil conflict. High number of IDPs are still in the different exiting POC sites and also greater number of returners are expected to resettle in the different Counties accross the Country.
Cordaid intend to provide lifesaving emergency healthcare access to vulnerable populations focusing on the major causes of morbidity and mortality in both primary health care units and hospitals to bridge the unmet health gaps that was compounded by the long period of conflict in the Country. The project will target the IDPs, returnees and the host population in the Counties of Fashoda, Manyo, Raja, Budi and Torit. A total of 5,495 male population and 4,505 female population is expected to be reached with essential health care services in 5 Counties by the year 2019.
In Fashoda and Manyo Counties, the project will be implemented in 2 PHCUs and 2 PHCCs while in Raja, Budi and Torit, the project will be implemented in Raja hospital, Chukudum and Torit hospital. Cordaid has existing health grand in all the location of intend but due to increasing need and demand for health services, the supplementary support under this grand will health to close the gap and focus will be directed towards Improving access and scale-up responsiveness to integrated essential healthcare needs of the vulnerable populations focusing on major causes of morbidity and mortality, Prevent, detect and respond to epidemic prone disease outbreaks and Increase access to essential clinical health services among vulnerable populations targeting SGBV, NCDs, SAM with Medical Complication which will not be fully covered the existing funds.

Needs

Due to sporadic conflict in Fashoda, Manyo, Raja, Budi and Torit County, there has been massive displacement of community and rampant movement of population and settlements in different part of the Counties where community feel it was safe to settle in. Amidst conflict, the health care system was compromised in most health facilities as medical equipment’s and even structure destroyed, and staffs fled out from health facilities in fear of their lives. The host community and IDPs from the neighboring Counties who never went to the POC where left minus any health care services. Most parts of the Country were inaccessible, community component to create a linkage with the health facilities were hampered and no tract of mortality and morbidity cases recorded from the community.Fashoda, Manyo, Raga Budi and Torit Counties are one geographical locations Cordaid is implemention health care services as per of the Health pool fund and Rapid Result health project (RRHP) to both the Host community, IDPs and returnees. However, the high disease burden has been observed from the increasing number of consultation within July to august from 2670 to 2947 cases within one month period and malaria among U5 years increasing from 121 to 195 cases registered from Fashoda County. (DHIS Report 2018). The Current developmental funds are over stressed to meet the hugehealth demand for both the IDps, returnees and the population in Fashoda, Manyo, Raja, Budi and Torit County. Cordaid intend to supplement primary health care support in Kodok PHCC, Oriny PHCC, Kaka PHCC and Lul PHCU that has the greater number of returnees and the host community. Also essential health support will be rendered in Raja, Budi and Torit hospitals with focus on management of comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (CeMONC), minor trauma and surgery, Scale up TB/HIV/AIDS/Disability care, Scale up integrated response to SAM with medical complications and procurement of emergency health requirements. The primary health care service will be implemented in already exiting health facilities to target and serve the IDPs who moved and settlement outside the POC. The supplementary support under this grand will be directed towards reinstating a few essential medical equipment’s, recruitment and filling of human resource gaps which will not be fully covered RRHP and HPF funds.
