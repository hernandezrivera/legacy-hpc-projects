Project Summary

Palestine Save the Children Foundation (PSCF) in partnership with Union of Health Care Committees (UHCC) seek to respond to the urgent need for lifesaving essential healthcare services for elder people suffer from chronic diseases in particular hypertension and diabetes. The project aims at promoting a community based response to basic health and rehabilitation needs of the elder people and people with disabilities living in the most vulnerable areas in Gaza Strip and can’t reach the clinics and hospitals due to the spread of COVID-19 and lockdown imposed on Gaza. The project will take place in 5 areas in north and Gaza Governorates. In specific, the project will target 300 elder patients including people with disabilities living in Beit Lahia, Beit Hanoun, Al Shajaiya, Al Zaitoon, and Johr el Deek. The activities that are planned to be implemented include the following:
• Conducting need assessment visits to identify the target beneficiaries, and screen existing elder patients and disabled cases suffer from hypertension and diabetes that need medical, health and rehabilitation intervention.
• Launching a mobile clinic consists of a doctor, a nurse, a physiotherapist, an occupational therapist and a psychologist to conduct outreach visits to provide health, medical and rehabilitation services to those most in need. The main services that will be provided through the mobile clinic include making general check-ups for blood pressure, sugar..etc, nursing care, making periodic laboratory tests, and individual psychological support sessions.
• Providing physical and occupational therapy sessions for patients and people with disabilities need rehabilitation intervention.
• Collaborate with key health actors in MoH to improve referral pathways coordination in order to facilitate the process of providing electives surgeries for patients.
• Provision of assistive devices and disposables such as “wheelchairs, toilet wheelchairs, air bed mattresses, walkers, diapers and hygiene kits. The assistive devices will support the functional independency of target beneficiaries and enhance their participation in the community.
• Conduction of health prevention and promotion awareness sessions about most common health problems targeting the project beneficiaries and their caregivers to enhance their access to quality basic health services as well as specialized rehabilitation services through following up the rehab exercises and home programs.
• Providing training courses on inclusive health services for the health workers working in clinics and hospitals.

1 - Needs

The Gaza Strip has been under a health blockade since June 2006. Its health services is severely crippled in the longer term under restrictions of movement and access to resources. The health situation in Gaza Strip has been worsening dramatically than before due to the spread of COVID-19 which has negative effects on old patients and persons with disabilities suffering from Non-communicable diseases (NCD) and in particular hypertension and diabetes. The NCDs patients and in specific the older people are at highest risk from COVID-19, so they are advised to stay at home. Most of them haven’t the ability to access the health care centers to receive medical and rehabilitation services. The HNO report (2020) showed that there is a delay and interruption in the treatment of the chronic diseases due to the lack of medical supplies, including diagnostics and therapeutics, and referral restrictions. The report assured that untreated NCDs can lead to serious complications, such as renal failure, disability, and amputations; in Gaza, treatment interruptions are common, with 61 per cent of medicines depleted on average in 2018, and 65 per cent depleted in the first six months of 2019. Diseases in which treatment interruption causes rapid decompensations, like insulin dependent diabetic patients, patients with severe hypertension, in addition to those on renal dialysis, are considered to be at highest risk of complications and death. The elderly are particular vulnerable to NCDs which may develop impairments and lead to disabilities. At the same time, people with disabilities are more vulnerable to NCDs, because of their underlying health condition, and vulnerability to poverty and exclusion from healthcare services.PSCF has a long experience in working with people with disabilities and elder patients by providing them with comprehensive rehabilitation and health services and supporting their integration into the community. PSCF has a physiotherapy unit that is placed in Maghazi camp and provides center-based rehabilitation services and out-reach activities for elder patients and people with disabilities who can’t reach the unit. Throughout the periodic home visits conducted, the project team pointed out that people with disabilities including the older people suffer from lack of rehabilitation services especially in the most vulnerable areas due to several barriers include; the insufficient rehabilitation services of MoH in Gaza strip, weakness of referral processing in the local referral network system, the poor experience of the caregiver's, lack of financial resources, lack of accessible infrastructure, inequality between males with disability and females with disability in accessing rehabilitation services. On the other hand, the team reported that there is a big gap in comprehensive rehabilitation services using multidisciplinary approach and inclusive services. Most the target populations in this project like most Palestinian populations have a lack of knowledge and awareness about disability issues and health education due to a historically and culturally grounded prejudice that people with disabilities are passive members of the society who cannot play an active role or get involved in their community problems. Furthermore, the capacity and knowledge of health workers in the domain of inclusive health services need to be improved.All of these limitations increase the bad impact on people with disabilities, their families and community at large at the health, physical, psychological and social levels. This project proposes to intervene by providing assistive devices for people with disabilities, physiotherapy, occupational therapy and psycho-social as well as awareness activities to prevent more health problems for disabled people.

2 - Activities

Through out this project, the following activities will be implemented: - Coordinate with MoH to identify the people with disabilities and elder patients suffer from hypertension or diabetes need elective surgical intervention.- Refer 40 patients at risk to hospital to conduct elective surgery that was postponed due to COVID-19. - Equip 2 mobile clinics with medical equipment, laboratory supplies, medical disposables and drugs to conduct community based healthcare and rehabilitation interventions and awareness raising in the target areas.- Conduct need assessment visits to identify the curative and rehabilitation needs of the target beneficiaries and prepare the intervention plan.- Providing basic health/medical and nursing care through door-to-door home visits for 300 elder patients and people with disabilities in the most vulnerable areas of east Gaza.- Provision outreach sessions of physiotherapy, and occupational therapy and psychological support for 300 older people and people with disabilities based on the need assessment survey - Distribution of the medical supplies devices and disposables including (20 wheelchairs, 10 toilet wheelchairs, 15 air bed mattresses, 30 walkers, 200 optical eye glasses, 20 hearing aids, 1000 packs of diapers, 300 hygiene kits)- Conduct 2 training courses (30 hrs per each course) on inclusive health services and positive practices to deal with disability targeting 40 health workers.- Conduction of home-based awareness education health sessions targeting 600 family members and patients'' caregivers on social factors, lifestyle habits and anthropometric measurements according to hypertension and diabetes diseases. - Implementation of 150 sessions on health education/awareness component, that will target the disability issue from community perspective via educating families and community members about their personal hygiene and health needs, disability prevention information sessions, right to health and access to services, supporting positive participation on relief and preparedness to emergencies.

3 - Indicators

For PSCF, measurable impact is one of its key program principles. It seeks to demonstrate lasting positive impact on the lives of target beneficiaries by regularly assessing program quality. Monitoring and evaluation is a very crucial element in project management because it measures the effectiveness, and identify the most valuable and efficient use of resources. Within this project, PSCF will conduct its independent review and evaluation at any point during any project life cycle. Monitoring activities will depend to a considerable degree on monthly reporting by staff. Documentary review will be supplemented by regular discussions with staff and site visits to beneficiaries. Financial monitoring will be accomplished in similar manner; the Accountant’s monthly statements will meet the requirements established by the Project Manager and be suitable for synthesis into periodic financial reports of PSCF to the donor. The Project Manger will coordinate closely with the Project Coordinator and other key staff when needed to collect accurate information and create an information system of documentation to fulfill the grant reporting compliance. The Project Coordinator will be responsible of handling all regular activity reports submitted by project employees, provide the latter with the necessary forms, synthesize all information into an English report and submit it to the Project Manager who in his turn will revise it and make it fit to the donor requirement. Such a management structure builds on the strengths of existing PSCF staff. PSCF will be ready to submit to donor any type of reports at any stage of the project.The following indicators/milestones shall be monitored: - 40 patients at risk referred to hospital to conduct elective surgery that was postponed due to COVID-19. - 300 older patients and persons with disabilities received health and comprehensive rehabilitation services through outreach visits. - # of medical supplies devices and disposables distributed to older patients and PWDs. - # of health workers received training courses on inclusive health services and and positive practices to deal with disability/- # awareness sessions conducted for community members on health education.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

PSCF has a series of policies that ensure the protection of beneficiaries through the implementation of the project activities. To safeguard PSCF’s beneficiaries, there are strict policies developed such as Child Protection Policy, Code of Conduct, and the Anti-Fraud, Corruption and Bribery Policy that the staff should read and sign on. These various PSCF institutional policies define conduct deemed to be forms of unacceptable behavior and, if confirmed, subject to action. To achieve protection mainstreaming and safeguarding mechanisms during the implementation of the project activities, the capacities of PSCF’s team will be built on knowledge and good practices related to beneficiaries’ protection and safeguarding. PSCF will recruit male and female employees to avoid gender-sensitivity. All the staff will sign on the code of conduct and commit to it. During implementation, the staff should follow all the safety procedures including physical distancing and hygiene promotion through providing services for beneficiaries. Also, PSCF will focus on preventing and minimizing as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the project, which can increase people's vulnerability. This can be achieved by providing assistance for the elder people and people with disabilities without discrimination. Upon such term, there will be a regular meetings with the project target beneficiaries and their caregivers, so that the project-team be acknowledged with recent recommendations, preferences or any complaints. Also, regular visits will be conducted to the target beneficiaries. The staff will communicate with them to ensure that they are satisfied with the services provided and they haven’t any complain. They also will be oriented with the principles, policies, reporting and monitoring frameworks of protection mechanisms that assist them to claim their rights.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Throughout the project, the project team will be empowered to effectively respond to the health needs of the vulnerable target older patients including children with disabilities through communication and learning methods. The project psychosocial counselors will be supported to respond to the protection and psychosocial needs of patients. Also, The project will significantly contribute to gender equality. PSCF believes that social inequalities between men and women are an obstacle to community development and promotes the elimination of those obstacles through undertaking the following actions: 1) In the process of identifying the target beneficiaries, the priority will be given to families who includes widows, divorcees and single women. 2) To ensure good participation of women in all the project cycle, two local committees will be formed in the two target governorates taking in consideration that at least 50% of the members will be women. The local committees will participate in planning, implementing, and monitoring and evaluating the project activities.
