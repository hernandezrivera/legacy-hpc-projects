Project Summary

The project aims to deliver sustained assistance to the most chronically food insecure. It will focus primarily in underserved areas, informal IDP settlements in multiple governorates while also increasing the provision of assistance to conflict affected areas along the Euphrates River in Deir-ez-Zor Governorate. This will be done in coordination with other food security partners in NES. The project will consist primarily of two main modalities of food security assistance dry food rations and food vouchers and will also include a complimentary activity comprising the provision of agricultural vouchers. A small stock of RTEs will also be pre-positioned in case of any new displacements or emergencies.

4 - Needs

The collapse of the Syrian pound combined with the secondary and tertiary impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic has had a devastating impact on households across Syria with many struggling to meet their basic needs in turn leading to continuous displacement trends as households move to camps and sites due to the inability to pay rent in urban centres. Levels of food insecurity in NES have reached a critical level with underserved and hard-to-reach communities most severely impacted 84% of IDP households assessed by HNAP across NES reported that monthly income was insufficient to meet basic needs with households increasingly reliant on harmful negative coping mechanisms to survive. REACH reports that the average daily labourer would have to work 84 days to afford the SMEB. In this context of deepening poverty, the NGO will provide food assistance through a voucher modality, in-kind support and agricultural inputs to support vulnerable households to rebuild livelihoods.

5 - Implementation of activities

Improving food security for vulnerable households and IDPs through two food assistance modalities: food vouchers and general food rations (GFRs). The NGO will also distribute to multi-purpose cash (MPC) to acutely vulnerable households with inter-sectoral needs. In addition, agricultural inputs will be provided via a voucher modality to support vulnerable rural households reliant on agriculture. The NGO will provide food rations to newly-displaced IDPs and those residing far from a functioning market. We will aim to support IDPs in informal settlements for 8 months, while newly displaced IDPs who are still moving will receive the same for one month. IDP and returnee households in areas with access to functional markets will be supported with a minimum of eight months’ food voucher distributions. This intervention will primarily be implemented in Deir-Ez-Zor, Aleppo and Ar-Raqqa Governorates. We will target IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities using our extensively tested food voucher household assessment tool. The NGO uses a multi-vulnerability assessment, in line with cluster guidance.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Based on the analysis of the FSS sector of the numerous risks and potential threats linked to the implementation of the sector strategy, the NGO similarly identifies two key protection risks around distributions themselves and the possibility of causing tension between IDP and host communities. Mitigation measures are spelled out below: In order to mitigate the security/protection risks for all beneficiaries during distribution, they will be carried out early in the day to ensure beneficiaries can come and go with daylight. Women and men have separate lines at distribution points that are staffed by male and female field workers. In camp settings, multiple distribution points are established to help keep the number of people at one point to a minimum and to reduce waiting times. For out of camp distributions, the duration between assessment and distribution is kept to a minimum to avoid large distributions and to help deliver assistance to beneficiaries as soon as possible. Voucher distributions take place by mixed-gender teams at the household level. Whenever possible and appropriate staff from the area will carry out activities to ensure all staff have strong local knowledge, extensive informational networks and good community acceptance. It ensures conflict sensitivity as our staff have an awareness to the social, cultural and political dynamics of the area and are representative of the program participants. The NGO also holds consultations between host and IDP communities


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The project prioritises the importance of PSEA by explicitly addressing the issue in its Programme Participant Protection Policy and staff Code of Conduct, which together define the project's expectations of staff and partners, their responsibilities as well as a mandatory duty to report in cases of potential abuse. These policies are backed up by a Community Response Mechanism open to and accessibly to staff, beneficiaries and the general public to provide multiple means of reporting. In the specific case of Syria, The project is committed to supporting and engaging with the current Inter-Agency Standing Committee PSEA Expansion Plan for the Whole of Syria - to date three staff have been trained as internal focal points for PSEA and are involved on rolling out PSEA training and awareness sessions for the rest of The project's staff.
