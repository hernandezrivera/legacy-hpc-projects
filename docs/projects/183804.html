Project Summary

The aim of the project is to enhance the capacity of health facilities and health screening at points of entries (PoEs) to enhance emergency preparedness and response and address the protracted essential health needs for the most vulnerable displaced people and local communities affected by inter-communal conflicts, disease outbreak and natural emergencies. The response plans under the project are in line with Health Cluster Objective 2022: Objective 1- Support essential public health functions with focus strong primary health care, Objective 2- Strengthen emergency preparedness, response, and all hazards emergency risk management. IOM will focus on 17 localities and this includes: Wad Al Mahi (Blue Nile), Yassin (East Darfur), Al Fao, Galabat Ash-Shargiah, Basundah (Gedaref), Reifi Khashm Elgirba (Kassala), Al Fasher, Tawilla (North Darfur), Buram, Gereida, Rehaid Albirdi, Tulus, Um Dafoug (South Darfur), Delami (South Kordofan), Ag Geneina, Jebel Moon, Sirba (West Darfur). High rates of internal displacement or return have been reported in these localities. The vulnerable people, such as women of reproductive age, pregnant and lactating women (PLW) and children under-five (U5) living inside or outside of IDPs camps as well as their host communities are at high risk of morbidity and mortality due to unavailability and inaccessibility of sustained health services in these localities. IOM will support the health facilities in rural areas to strengthen emergency preparedness and response and address the protracted health needs of the most vulnerable groups of IDPs, returnees and other affected migrants and host communities through enhanced a basic package of primary health services (BPPHS). The BPPHS will include: 1) outpatient consultation and treatment for communicable diseases and non-communicable diseases including support for a prevailing disease surveillance, early warning and response system; 2) supporting reproductive health services; 3) child health services with a focus on immunizations; 4) supporting mental and psychological consultation; 5) environmental health, including waste management and vector control; 6) facilitated referrals and mobile services; 7) health and nutrition promotion services.
IOM will promote the continuity of BPPHS ensuring a quality of essential and timely life-saving health services on prevention, enhancing risk mitigation means and close monitoring of outbreak disease trend and strengthen community health services. This will be achieved through strengthening capacity of health-care service providers including community health-care workers (CHWs) and Community Midwives (CMs). The COVID-19 mitigation measures will be integrated within the planned capacity building activities with emphasis on strengthening surveillance and reporting systems, infection prevention and control and risk communication and community engagement (RCCE).
The planned capacity building activities will be extended to support the state Ministry of Health (MoH) and border authorities to enhance disease prevention and response measures at prioritized PoEs. Some of the targeted localities are subject to diverse human mobility patterns including, Sudanese returnees, refugees, and irregular migrants, due to its geographic location at cross-border movements across states and with neighboring countries. IOM will enhance health screening services to mitigate the risk of transmission of communicable diseases including COVID-19 at the prioritized PoEs and this will be made through building capacity of key stakeholders such as the State MoH, border officials, front-line services workers including health service providers. The support will include IPC measures by installing and updating water and sanitation facilities and provision of other IPC supplies and equipment, and the delivery of hygiene and health promotion campaign enhancing community engagement in the response to disease outbreak including the COVID-19 pandemic.

1 - Needs

The health needs continue to be significant, especially among the most vulnerable displaced populations such as IDPs, returnees and hosting communities due to inter-communal conflicts, the worsening economic crisis, floods and disease outbreaks that has disrupted the ability of the public health sector to provide adequate healthcare. The escalation of communal conflicts was reported in 2021 and, are leading to new displacement. The humanitarian situation is notably worse for those who were newly or secondary displaced populations, especially among women and youth for whom a lack of access to primary health services. A seasonal flood damages health facility and disrupts the delivery of medical supplies and limit physical access to health facility. In 2021, seasonal rain resulted in floods was the main factor creating displacement and hazards that affected hundreds of thousands of people who were suffering from water-related diseases. Chance of disease outbreaks was increased due to the limited access to clean and safe water, lacked vector control strategies, low coverage of vaccines and unavailability of essential medicines. Over 3.1 million people will be at risk of water related disease and 11 million people are living in high risk areas of vector-borne diseases, especially malaria (National Multi-Hazard Health Emergency Preparedness Plan 2021). Sudan has been affected by the COVID-19 pandemic that further strained the access to essential health services. As of September 2021, a total of 37,873 case were reported across the country with 7.5 fatality rate while the COVID-19 vaccination coverage still remains at 20 percent. The COVID-19 along with the deteriorating economy has a significant impact on the fragile health systems’ capacity to respond to the needs of the people-especially childcare. Children U5 are exposed to vaccine -preventable diseases due to reduced coverage of the expanded program of immunization (EPI). The coverage of measles vaccination was declined at 67 % within 29 localities and this was reflected on the recent measles outbreak in some states. During 2021, the availability of emergency medicines declined at 43 percent compared to 57 percent in 2020. The shortage of essential medicine is limiting the access to BPPHS as the most health facilities are only partially functional and many are in remote areas and can only provide limited services due to lack of supplies and dilapidated infrastructure. Their capacities concerning surveillance and early warning systems are very limited and there are very few or no community-based surveillance officers trained. Among crisis-affected populations, women and children are especially at risk of malnutrition, and gender-based violence (GBV) and high maternal mortality rates due to limited supports on maternal and child health. Hence, resources need to be allocated to establish functional health facilities that offer BPPHS, referral systems and support health worker training. As part of strengthening emergency preparedness and responses, there is also an urgent need to provide enhanced health screening services at PoEs. The geographic locations of IDPs and returnees in the target localities often close to internal and external cross borders, and the transmission of diseases is a critical dimension of human mobility. Given the volume of cross-border human movement in the target localities, the risk of disease transmission may be high. Therefore, disease surveillance is significant and crucial role in state and local-level early warning and response. Yet there is poor health screening service at PoEs with little documentation to support emergency preparedness and responses. The low capacity of the health screening services at PoE may results in the delay of early detection of disease outbreaks. Hence, the health, border, and other front-line workers are continuously in need of training to assist enhanced emergency preparedness and responses.

2 - Activities/Outputs

1. Provide the BPPHS at the targeted health facilities (one health facility per locality) and catchment areas encompassing. 1) Outpatient consultation and treatment for communicable diseases and non-communicable diseases including support for a prevailing disease surveillance, early warning and response system.2) Reproductive health services with enhancing a quality of prenatal and postnatal care and increasing delivery services by a qualified health person. 3) Child health services with a focus on immunizations. 4) Mental and psychological consultation. 5) Environmental health, including waste management and vector control. 6) Referral and mobile services: IOM will establish a mobile medical teams (at least 7) to increase access to health services for the mobile groups and local communities who are facing limited physical access to health facility.7) Heath and nutrition promotion services.2. Organize and conduct capacity building activities: 1) Train health-care staff at the target health facilities to enhance their skills and knowledge to provide a quality of the BPPHS. The COVID-19 mitigation measures will be integrated within the capacity building activities with emphasis on strengthening surveillance and reporting systems, IPC and RCCE.2) Capacity building workshop for CHWs on community- based health and nutrition promotion services. The training will be designed as training of trainers aimed at building their capacity to support community members. The health and nutrition promotion activities will be delivered by encouraging a direct participation of community members to tackle issues of malnutrition and negative health seeking behaviour and practices (e.g. an establishment of community health management committees and mother to mother support groups). RCCE will be emphasized in an effort to understand risks, perceptions, behaviours and knowledge gaps, misinformation associated with the inflection diseases and provide accurate information related to common infection disease including COVID-19. Advocacy efforts on migrant inclusive services will be emphasized and this will address a right of displaced people such as refugees, IDPs and returnees to receive essential services including testing and the COVID-19 vaccine.3. Refurbishment of health facilities including installation of hand washing facilities and screening points to prepare future infection disease outbreak and control. 4. Strengthening health screening at PoEsThe activities will focus on capacity building for key stakeholders on health screening and IPCs at the prioritized PoEs. The following components may be addressed: 1) basic health screening skills to mitigate the risk of transmission of communicable diseases; 2) risk criteria consisting of potential for exposure (e.g. caring for ill persons, attending the funeral for a person with possible COVID-19) and self-reported or observed signs and symptoms; 3) IPC measures including the implementation of protocols for handwashing, disinfection and waste disposal; and 5) facilitation of referral. RCCE will be conducted at the focused PoEs. This includes the promotion of appropriate health and hygiene practices to combat communicable diseases, advocacy for COVID-19 vaccines, dissemination of information, education, and communication (IEC) materials to address the misconceptions such as COVID-19 stigma. IOM will further procure and provide a minimum set of medical equipment, supplies, furniture for the implementation of health screening and installing and updating water and sanitation facilities. 3) Conduct a regular monitoring and post assessmentIOM will contribute to organizing and conducting the sector joint monitoring and assessment to assess health situation and identify urgent needs and gaps among the most vulnerable groups of IDPs, returnees and hosting communities. IOM will also ensure a regular attendance in the sector meetings to share key outputs and challenges for future programming.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project will address the protection needs of IDPs, women, and other vulnerable groups such as IDPs, returnees and other affected communities through strengthening the capacity of community to create protective environment. The project will pay a special attention to women of reproductive age, PLW, U5 children and persons with disabilities who do not have adequate access to essential health services and vulnerable to large scale of epidemiological event and disease outbreaks. The planned interventions will focus on the development of women’s capacities, strengthening women’s participation and direct interaction with communities as well as health providers and addressing negative health seeking behaviours to promote gender equality and reproductive health rights. Protection against gender-based violence such as harassment sexual exploitation and abuse, targeted women, girls and persons with disabilities will be strengthened.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The project will embrace accountability to affected populations, including host communities in all processes of project design and implementation. Vulnerabilities will be taken into consideration starting from the first phase -immediate assessment of critical/humanitarian needs of affected migrants as well as local communities to the implementation of the interventions. Efforts will be made to engage in particular women, PLWs, children and youth. The project will empower these groups by encouraging direct participation in community- based health awareness activities. As part of capacity development, the peer learning groups/mother to mother support groups will be formed and trained as mentor and key messenger addressing negative traditional practice as well as protection threats such as harassment sexual exploitation and abuse, targeted women, girls and persons with disabilities. Accountability will also be promoted through the capacity development of CHWs and health providers as well as NGOs/CSOs in the health sectors as duty bearer to inform to the sector partners in health and protection if protection threats were reported. Applying IOM's experiences enhancing the protection of migrants through the provision of direct assistance services, IOM will provide direct assistance through psycho social support, counselling and medical assistance to victim in strong coordination with the sector partners as needed.
