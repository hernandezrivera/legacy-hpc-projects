Needs

100% of refugees rely on food assistance for their survival to sustain the minimum daily required kilocalories. Dietary diversity is a gap, and common request by refugees, yet only 87,000 refugees are receiving fresh food vouchers and other interventions from WV and other agencies to address this gap. Moreover, a significant funding shortfall in this sector threatens sustainable assistance. Cash for Work is putting conditional cash in the pockets of some households (HH), but 40% of HHs are selling relief items to generate cash to buy diversified and nutritious food, among other HH needs . In addition, provision of cooking fuel, especially environmentally sustainable types like LPG, remains an unmet need, hence the need for energy savings centralised cooking facilities through Community Kitchen model The host communities and refugees have been engaged in less intensive community disaster mitigation activities and compensated through the Cash for Work/Assets, this has further strengthening the community resilience to identified hazards. The refugees and host community also need continued to be supported with cash grants for Income Generating Activities and other livelihoods inputs. The assistance has also been given following capacity building sessions to enhance the usage of the livelihoods resources distributed. Lack of access to basic services and self-reliance opportunities exposes refugees to protection and potentially harmful coping mechanisms. There is also a need to improve the entrepreneurial capacity of the host communities through promoting savings (through VSLAs) and increasing community access to credit to diversify and sustain their livelihoods.In terms of the gender and protection, In terms of gender and protection, World Vision will encourage women to be the primary recipients of the fresh food vouchers on behalf of the households. Men and women will be engaged in VSLA and IGAs. The Community Kitchen participants will mostly target the women as participants; however, this will benefit all members of the households. In terms of Cash for Work/Assets community disaster mitigation labour work, men and women will be engaged, women will be assigned tasks that are lighter. The program will also engage the host community (men and women) to participate in homestead gardening. The safety of the participants when travelling to the Cash for Work/Cash for Assets including IGA/VSLA training center will be a major consideration when choosing the project sites in the camps and host community. Effort will be made to ensure that Cash Distribution Points are closer to the beneficiaries’ camp sites for security of beneficiaries to and from the cash distribution site. All targeting will be based on vulnerability and the selection criteria will be well-communicated to the Refugees/Host Community Food Management Committee. The selection process will be facilitated and monitored by World Vision Staff. Staff supervising project activities in the field will be sensitized to ensure they are able to monitor and report any cases immediately to management for action

Activities or outputs

Output 1.1; 5, 000 households in refugee camp are supported with complimentary fresh food vouchers distributed to improve dietary diversity.Output 1.2; 1, 150 refugees households provided access to community kitchens for preparation of household daily meals, promoting use of user -friendly fuels that reduce the environmental impactOutput 2.1; 4, 500 host community and refugees households are engaged in in identified Cash for Work/Assets (pathway levelling, drainage clearance, rehabilitation of bridges, rehabilitation of access roads)Output 2.2; 4, 000 host community and refugees households are supported with agricultural of inputs/IGA cash grants and technical trainings to stablish VSLA/alternative livelihood skills and opportunities.
