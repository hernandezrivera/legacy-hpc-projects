1 - Needs

Sudan faces Three major overlapping humanitarian challenges: one triggered by conflict leading to wide-scale population displacement and another due to climatic and socio-cultural conditions leading to crisis levels, and the third one in a number of refugees from, ( Ethiopia , Eretria, South Sudan, DRC, CHAD Refugees) all of them are share the local resource such (Food, Education Services, and Health facilities), which lead to food insecurity and malnutrition among the hosts communities and IDPs. The scale and long-term nature of refugees, especially in Khartoum, which has not been matched by economic opportunities, has exposed refugees to hardship and uncertainty about their future. There are over 1.86 million refugees both protracted and new displacement in the hot spots of neighbors spicily Khartoum, Blue Nile Darfur and the Kordofans, in addition to Sudanese refugees in neighboring countries, mainly in Chad and South Sudan. The number of registered refugees originating from Sudan reached 724,800 by the end of 2018. Chad continued to host the largest Sudanese refugee population with 336,700, while 269,900 Sudanese refugees were living in South Sudan. This is putting an additional strain on the 3.6 million people currently suffering from food insecurity, and the 2.2 million children suffering from acute malnutrition. Refugees and asylum seekers living in both emergency and protracted situations remain largely dependent on humanitarian assistance, with very limited access to livelihood opportunities. Khartoum as one of the most popular area for the new arrivals refugees, is facing various challenge to control and serve the new arrivals and protected Refugees, more than 409,126 living in Khartoum state according to the allocation paper of 2020. In this allocation SHF 2020 HRP the targeted refugees by the RCF are 292,779. Alshrooq as one of the active members of the RCF and Refugees Working Group RWG is planet to contribute in develop the situation of the urban refugees in Khartoum State targeting Khartoum, Khartoum North and Omdurman in Khartoum State. Including the area of (El Geraf , Al Duom . Umm Badda, Um Durman, Jabal Aulia , by supporting Resilience program and Livelihood opportunity for some of 500 HH.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Women and girls everywhere are disadvantaged in terms of social power and influence, control of resources, control of their bodies and participation in public life all that is a result of socially determined gender roles and relations. Gender-based violence against women and girls occurs in the context of this imbalance. AOSCD will support protection actors to analyses different gendered vulnerabilities that may put men, women, boys and girls at risk of violence.Alshrooq will work to ensure that especially care and support for all survivors, special attention will give to females due to their documented greater vulnerabilities to GBV, the overarching discrimination they experience, and their lack of safe and equitable access to humanitarian assistance.AOSCD will involve the completion of a successful entrepreneur in the implementation of the activities business in high professionalism, of the GBV and Child Protection staff how attend the training curse and have enough Experience to implement the GBV activities, In order to avoid any errors or difficulties that will backfire during the implementation .> This project is designed to create equal opportunities among all groups, and give people with disabilities and the elderly a priority in the construction of self-resilience. This project also takes into account the participation of women in training to chive the outputs of this project, especially with regard to the capacity building of the livelihood activity

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

On Accountability to Affected Populations: AOSCD shall work to ensure accountability to the affected population (AAP) especially through participatory approaches and routinely consult and engage with the communities. Whilst monitoring will be progressive, evaluation will be mid-term and final and accountability reports (both financial and narrative) will be generated and shared as appropriate to UNHCR office Both in field level and at the HQ. Some specific measures/activities undertaken/planned to integrate Accountability to Men, Women, Boys and Girls among Affected People in the project design, implementation and monitoring shall include the following:Our presence in South and East Darfur as one of the leading NNGO , we got acquainted with local culture and community mentalities in conflict situations , that will add value to practice and ensure i) Consultation, and participation of, beneficiaries during needs assessments (including, for example, the baseline surveys);ii) Reimplementation stakeholder workshops and county level cluster meetings;iii) Case study profiling and documentation of learning themes;iv) Use of community based structures e.g. Self Help Groups, Youth and Women Associations, School Clubs, and Per Groups. v) Active collaboration with local authorities especially County Gender Departments and Minster of Social Afters (e.g. on selection of case managers, volunteers, community based psychosocial group’s staff selection for competency strengthening…); vi) Community based complaints mechanisms such as use of local leaders like chiefs ( Shakhs), county authorities, etc..; vii) Community led campaigns and advocacy initiatives; viii) Use of dedicated local staff (knowledge of cultures, knowledge of local language, local contexts and operational environment, cost effectiveness, sustainability…);
