Project Summary

This project aims to strengthen the protection and social safety net and resilience of children, caregivers, and communities in the face of chronic exposure to conflict-related violence by: (1) providing community-based and specialized child protection case management and psychosocial and mental health services to children and their families, (2) providing specialized package of services to GBV survivors and girls and women at risk, and (3) addressing IHL and IHRL violations through advocacy initiatives targeting all duty bearers and based on documentation of grave violations. This project targets Palestinian children directly and indirectly exposed to violence and abuse related to the Israeli Occupation, including settler violence, unsafe school environments, home and school demolitions, and displacement. War Child Holland (WCH) and partners - the Psycho-Social Counseling Center for Women (PSCCW), the Palestinian Counselling Center (PCC), and Defence for Children International Palestine (DCIP) - will be targeting acutely affected children and families living in the most marginalized areas of Area C in the West Bank including Bedouin Communities located in the below:
*Area C and Bedouin communities of the Jordan Valley under Tubas and Jericho governorates, including Jiftlik and Ash-Shuneh, Gharb al Muthallath, Abu al Ajaj, Fasayel including Fasayil al Wasta and Fasayil Al Fauqa, Marj Naji, Zbeidat, and Al Auja with focus on Ras Ein al ‘Auja. Al Ka’abenh , Alshallal , Arab Najadeh
*Hebron H2, Area C and Bedouin communities of Hebron including MasaferYatta, Fawwar and Al Aroub, Yatta and Al-Karmel
*Area C and Bedouin communities of Bethlehem Governorate, including: Beit Skaria , AlTaquo, Rashaydeh and Kisan, AlMaasara, Al Walajeh, Za’tara, Battir, Beit Fajjar, Whawawreh, Janata
*Area C of Nablus governorate covering Duma, Qaryout, Beita communities
*Salfit Area: Rafat, Hares and Kufer Al Deek
*Qalqilya area including Jayous and Qalqilya
The project partners will target 23 CBOs distributed among the seven above-mentioned Governorates. The areas were selected based on previous experience of consortium partners in those areas and needs identified as well as on the OCHA Vulnerability Profile of Palestinian Communities in Area C, and WCH’s recent Rapid Needs Assessment of the West Bank following the May 2021 escalations.
In addition to specific Protection interventions, War Child and partners will mainstream PSEA, disability inclusion, and Protection in Covid19 response efforts in line with local infection and prevention control (IPC) measures and global guidance notes produced by the Alliance for Child Protection in Humanitarian Action (e.g. CPHA Note on the Protection of Children during Covid 19 outbreak and annexes), and IASC PSEA during COVID-19 Response Technical Guidance Note.
The project will be implemented in coordination with MOSD and active members of CPAOR and GBV Sub-Cluster providing CP, MHPSS, and GBV services in Area C and Hebron H2. WCH and partners are currently active members of the CP/MHPSS WG, GBV sub-cluster, Protection Cluster. WCH caseworkers closely coordinate with MOSD child protection officers, and regularly attend coordination meetings in the field, including the CPN monthly meetings at governorate level. Child Protection and GBV Cases detected and managed will also be reported to the OCHA online system.
The project interventions are comprehensive and will be implemented according to the humanitarian standards such as CPMS, ISAC and GBV standards to ensure safety of the project participants and do no harm.

1 - Needs

This project addresses MHPSS, GBV and protection risks stemming from occupation-related violence and complements and fills gaps in community-based CP, GBV and MHPSS interventions in Area C. The gaps and needs were identified through analysis of complementary assessments including: 2022 HNO, MSNA Protection Analysis, WCH’s 2021 rapid MHPSS, CP, and Education needs assessment in the West Bank following the May 2021 escalations, and 2020 COVID-19 Needs Assessment in Area C. The project design was informed by consultations with the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD), Ministry of Women Affairs (MoWA), CP/MHPSS and GBV sub-cluster partners, and direct input from communities. The Area C communities included in this project are some of the highest ranked vulnerable communities according to the Vulnerability Profile Project (OCHA) and Protection Vulnerability Index. Many were greatly impacted by the April-May 2021 escalations, and all face regular incidents of settler violence, military drills, home raids, and demolition and displacement orders- which according to War Child’s assessments are the main causes of distress for children. According to the HNO 2022 (draft) and Child Protection Area of Responsibility (CP AoR), and in line with the UNCRC (including Articles 19, 24, 27, 28, 31, 34, 39) there are considerable needs for MHPSS interventions including specialized case management, and approaches targeting adolescents and caregivers. An estimated 678,000 children (345,800 boys ad 332,200 girls) across the oPt (in the West Bank 12% of all children) are in need of child protection and MHPSS services, including 122,000 in need of focused non-specialized support or specialized services. WCH’s 2021 Rapid Needs assessment of West Bank communities during the April-May 2021 escalations confirmed the high needs due to the increased violence, compounded also by the COVID-19 pandemic: *75% of respondents said that they are not safe or protected in their current place of residence; main reason were physical attacks by Israeli forces or settlers (nearly 40 percent of respondents). *86% of all respondents said there was no safe place for children in their communities to play. *Majority of respondents (73%) had noticed recent signs of distress or other changes in their own mood and behavior (increased fear, stress, anxiety) *62% of caregiver respondents said they had observed signs of distress and observable changes in one or more of their children’s behaviors (hypervigilance, including excessive fear, overreactions, and inability to calm down). Meanwhile, only 34% of respondents said they were able to attend to their children’s needs. *Despite high reporting rates of mental health needs, only 10% of respondents reported that a family member had already received some kind of MHPSS support. *Knowledge of organizations which provide MHPSS to children and families was correspondingly low, with only 19% of respondents saying that they knew of any such groups or institutions. The 2022 HNO also highlights the inter-related factors of increased violence and pandemic conditions contributing to children’s vulnerability, resulting in need to continue to strengthen children’s access to protection and MHPSS services, and prioritize multi-sector approaches. According to War Child Case Management database, in Area C of the Jordan Valley, Hebron, and Bethlehem Governorate, in 2021, out of 100 cases (53 boys, 47 girls), 43 were cases of neglect, 42 of emotional abuse, 14 of physical violence, and 1 of exploitation. The majority of the cases are between the age of 6-11. While boys and men tend to be more directly exposed to occupation- related violence, girls and women are also affected. According to the 2022 HNO, women and girls with disabilities, Bedouin women, internally displaced women and women and girls living in refugee camps are particularly at risk of violence. The HNO also confirms that the GBV system is not sufficient in meeting the needs of GBV survivors. The system is fragmented with different quality and services depending on different areas. Among the most pressing issues are the lack of available and accessible services, lack of quality GBV care and lack of functioning referral system. While the need for child protection, GBV and psychosocial support is increasing, the current care system is overstretched and cannot respond to the protection and psychosocial needs of girls, boys and their caregivers. The MOSD has expressed to War Child the need for more support needed for the CPN in the field, as the CPN only responds to high-risk cases and do not have the capacity to follow-up on low-medium risk cases. Access to the remote villages and Bedouin communities targeted under this project is also a challenge due to the MOSD’s capacity. Through this project, War Child will identify community case workers within targeted CBOs to increase surge preparedness at community level to detect children at risk, provide basic assessments and safely refer. WCH will provide case management services and referrals for low and medium risk cases, which are currently not covered by the MOSD or other organizations, preventing them from becoming high-risk cases. In addition, the project will strengthen the capacity of CBOs and PSCCW to provide community-based preventative CP and PSS interventions for children and families. DCIP will also closely monitor, document, and rapidly report grave violations against children, for greater accountability on IHL and IHRL by duty bearers. War Child and partners will also support urgent efforts required to scale up current services to prevent GBV and support its survivors. PSCCW and PCC will use their specialized expertise to provide an integrated package of services to survivors of GBV and awareness raising activities within communities on availability of services and referrals, and PCC will provide MHPSS services for children and families.

2 - Activities

HRP Objective 1: Increased respect for IHL and IHRL and accountability for violations - Monitoring and documentation of IHL and IHRL violations and related trends. Capitalizing on their cohesive network of sources including local media, health workers, CBOs, and community leaders and members, DCIP field workers will closely monitor, document, and rapidly report grave violations against children, including child killings and injuries; settler violence; and recruitment. HRP Objective 2: Protection response mechanisms are in place - CHILD PROTECTION and MHPSS: Provision of emergency child protection responses, including case management, psychosocial support and programs: (1) Case management services for 120 boys and girls. WCH community caseworkers in Area C will provide basic counseling, psychosocial support and individual case management for low to medium risk cases, and refer high-risk cases to the CPN. Detected children will receive integrated quality services, through appropriate safe referral to specialized mental health services provided by PCC under this project and external mental health and other service providers, in coordination with CPWG and the CPN at governorate level. Children and families will also be supported with other sectoral needs such as medical, education, etc through a provision managed by the case workers. Case Management services are implemented according to the case management interagency guidelines and the CPMS, and in line with the national referral system/MOSD Index of Networking and Referral of Child Protection against Violence, Neglect, and Abuse. We will ensure that the data protection protocol is in place as well as the information sharing agreement to ensure privacy and confidentiality of the data collected (storage, use of data). THe service directory will also be regularly updated; (2) Protection groups for 200 children to establish children advisory committee age (13-16) to participate in the planning and implementation of the activities in the targeted areas (3) Structured community based PSS sessions using WCH’s TeamUP methodology for 300 children. HRP Standard Activity : CHILD PROTECTION: capacity building for CP non-child protection professionals: (1) WCH will capitalize on its internal expertise and previous capacity building work to build the capacity of 10 PSCCW social workers on information management, and provide coaching and supervision for the social workers working in case management. (2) PCC will train 10 CBO counselors on their community-based Child Protection methodology, and on identification and referral of children at risk. (3) ToT for 10 CBO counselors and 5 PSCCW counselors in remote Psychological First Aid (PFA) to build their capacity to be qualified trainers; HRP Standard Activity: CHILD PROTECTION: Awareness raising for vulnerable communities on CP issues: WCH, PCC and PSCCW, through 23 CBOs, will disseminate information on CP and MHPSS service provision and availability, ensuring CP/GBV mapping and directories are up to date. PCC will produce 2 publications , a children's magazine and 3 CP animation videos for children and their parents on child protection, and will co-create episodes with children on child protection and rights. HRP Standard Activity MHPSS: Provision of MHPSS for adults and youth: (1) Positive Discipline in Everyday Parenting training for 300 parents of children affected by crisis. PCC will complement the 10 PDEP sessions per group with WCH’S Caregivers Support intervention (9 sessions per group), which has been adapted to remote modality and includes further stress release tips for caregivers. (2) PCC counselors will provide debriefing sessions to 50 families affected by violence to help them deal with stress and possible stigmatization. (3) PCC will provide individual counseling to adults and youth who demonstrate indicators of psychological distress including as a result of GBV risks. (4) WCH will train PSCCW social workers on its PSS - Caregiver Support Intervention to support their GBV case management work. HRP Standard Activity: GBV: Mental health and psychosocial services for GBV survivors, in close coordination with the GBV sub cluster and according to the GBV minimum standards: (1) Group counselling for 180 GBV survivors. The groups will include 10% of women and girls with disability. Should access be restricted due to Covid19, PSCCW will deliver these sessions through mobile teams thus ensuring continuity of service provisions throughout the project. HRP Standard Activity: GBV: Legal aid (1) PSCCW will provide 30 GBV survivors with legal aid before courts, including 5% of women with disability. Standard Activity : GBV: Awareness raising for persons at risk of GBV: PSCCW will implement 45 awareness raising workshops on women’s rights as stated in national and international legislative frameworks and how to access legal and protection services, and PSEA reaching 450 women and girls at risk of GBV. Standard Activity : GBV: Economic enablement: (1) PSCCW will provide 66 women (10% women with disabilities), with in-job training and vocational training to increase employment opportunities and enter the job market. Selection criteria will include: young women head of households, GBV survivor, living in marginalized areas, lack of income generation/livelihood. Standard GBV: Capacity building for GBV service providers (1) PCC will continue to train 8 PSCCW counsellors on MHPSS techniques for GBV survivors , focusing on counselling and intervention skills , expressive therapy intervention, and telecounslling. Selfcare for service providers: PCC will provide monthly professional supervision for 10 CBO counselors working with children and parents, 8 PSCCW counselors and 3 WCH case managers, including training on self care and stress management. Protection Mainstreaming - All project staff will be trained in PSEA and complaints/referral pathways by WCH's PSEA trainers and on disability inclusion.

3 - Indicators

War Child has an established global monitoring and reporting framework and uses standardized and globally validated means of verification to assess program effects on the wellbeing of participating children and families, and participant satisfaction. Through WCH’s monitoring and evaluation methodology, quality implementation of programming and delivery of care will be ensured. Consortium partners will integrate monitoring at all levels in their daily work and mechanisms will be put in place to facilitate early detection of any negative unintended results of the project. Regular field visits to and supervision of all activity sites will be conducted, not only for accountability purposes but will also serve learning and support adaptive programming. Activities and interventions will be further refined and adapted to meet the specific needs of the communities through engagement of CBOs and community facilitators who will regularly assess needs and participant satisfaction through quarterly focus group discussions and using WCH tools for measuring participant satisfaction and through the establishment of a complaints and feedback mechanism with support from WCH. Routine project progress meetings and reviews will be held to ensure that the project is reaching its intended targets, address any unexpected results, reﬂect new priorities, or changes to the context. Learning will be embedded in the project at multiple levels: at the project level, the monitoring system including the participatory feedback mechanism - will gather evidence against the indicators in the results framework and will inform project level decision-making and adaptation. Progress and lessons learnt will be shared on a regular basis with other stakeholders including government officials and Cluster partners. An end-of-project evaluation with participants through focus group discussions will also give children, caregivers, and community volunteers the opportunity to evaluate the various activities. Globally validated tools including the short Warwick Edinburgh Mental Wellbeing Scale are used to measure this indicator. The items cover the feeling and functioning dimensions of mental wellbeing. 2. % of caregivers with improved mental wellbeing score. The short Warwick Edinburgh Mental Wellbeing Scale is used to measure this indicator 3. Participant satisfaction - Children, young people and adults including those with disability have a right to provide feedback about how satisfied they are with program activities. The group exercise gathers both qualitative and quantitative information.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

War Child considers the specific needs and vulnerabilities of boys and girls of varying ages, as well of men and women, recognizing their roles in the communities, while trying to empower those who tend to be left out of power. War Child aims at being accountable to the children and families of this project and has taken a systematic approach to ensure that Accountability to Affected Population, Protection, Conflict Sensitivity, Do No Harm and GBV are mainstreamed into War Child’s project implementation. Practical applications of the Do No Harm in the proposed project will encompass: *accountability measures in line with War Child Integrity Framework, including community-based and child-friendly complaints and feedback mechanisms for all services, and participants’ satisfaction assessments using WCH tools and FGD with women, men, girls and boys • Identifying vulnerable groups across communities to foster social stability, avoid exacerbating tensions within the community and promote the equitable distribution of services across identity groups; • Reaching out and including vulnerable children within War Child intervention as per locally contextualized vulnerability criteria; • Analyzing existing community power dynamics to ensure that the needs of individuals and groups at risk of marginalization are voiced (also through the feedback mechanism); • Do No Harm principles included as a component of child protection and child safeguarding competency development of partners and communities; • A strong focus and commitment on child safeguarding and confidentiality in all interventions • With regards to case management services, case workers consult with the child and caregiver as to where they feel most comfortable to receive the service – for some they prefer in their homes, for others they prefer in the case worker’s office • Timely referral to specialized referrals to MHPSS, CP, and GBV professionals *Meaningful Access through outreach to children and caregivers with disability; *promoting participation and empowerment of boys and girls, and men and women; *applying a gender age marker in the design and monitoring of activities. Furthermore, as the project includes provision of case management services, War Child ensures adherence to information management and data protection protocols that uphold the privacy and confidentiality of cases. Access to reports and case files are kept limited to the professionals working on the cases and other professional organizations whose work require seeing these reports. War Child will ensure confidentiality through safety measures outlined in the information sharing agreement that will be developed and signed by the project partners and signed data protection protocol starting from the beginning of the project. Informed consent/assent of children and parents is also integrated at all stages of case management- at the beginning when they register, on the case plan/services to be provided, and at case closure.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

To ensure meaningful access and participation, vulnerable children and caregivers (including people with disabilities) will be identified through outreach (including home visits), awareness raising sessions in the community, structured PSS programs, then referred to case management for assessment. The case workers are based in the community where we work, making it easier to identify vulnerable children and caregivers. Following a comprehensive assessment, case workers will refer children who are under 18 years old and their caregivers to the needed internal services or external services. Access to external MHPSS services in the targeted communities are limited, therefore requiring children and their caregivers to travel to the nearest city which may be several hours away. As the travel and transportation costs may at times be a barrier to access, War Child in these instances will provide transportation support and case workers will accompany the children (including children with disabilities) to the specialized services to ensure access to care. In addition, given the overall and high need of children and families affected by conflict- related violence, selection of participants in community-based PSS activities and case management services will be based on specific vulnerability criteria (such as being acutely affected by or at highrisk of conflict- related violence, family or community violence/abuse/neglect, disabilities, child labor, COVID-19,etc), which will also be well publicized through community meetings. PSCCW will guarantee outreach to women and girls with disability in their GBV activities. CBOs and community facilitators will regularly engage women, men, girls and boys, including with disabilities, to tailor the community-based PSS and CP interventions specifically to their needs. Also recognizing that the participation of children with disabilities can be a challenge due to social-cultural norms, the project will actively identify person with disabilities through awareness raising in the community and coordinating with the CPN network and Protection Cluster partners such as the PRCS and YMCA who provide services for children with disabilities in the targeted areas if needed. CP Case workers will also coordinate with the CPN officers in their governorates who oversee protection cases for children with disabilities to ensure that children with disabilities are referred to relevant PSS activities as needed and coordination will be closely done with the available services provided by Protection Cluster actors such as the YMCA and the PRCS for children with disabilities. The non- specialized activities are provided by community members themselves, trained and supported as part of the project. Activities and interventions will be further refined and adapted to meet the specific needs of the communities through engagement of CBOs and community facilitators who will regularly engage women, men, girls and boys (including persons with disabilities) in defining their needs and tailoring the community-based PSS interventions. Case workers and community facilitators will also receive an orientation training by War Child staff on inclusion principles in order to skillfully adapt PSS interventions to ensure inclusivity, including for children with disabilities. Given the overall and high need of children and families affected by conflict- related violence, selection of participants in community-based PSS activities will be based on specific vulnerability criteria, which will also be well-publicized through community meetings. Communities including children will have the opportunity to help define the selection criteria, including adding or removing criteria. The non-specialized activities are provided by community members themselves, trained and supported as part of the project. Community-based and child-friendly complaints and feedback mechanisms will also be established for all services, and participant satisfaction.
