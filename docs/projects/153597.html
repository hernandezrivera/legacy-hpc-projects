Project Summary

CCO will build upon existing efforts and target vulnerable children in Guit county of Unity State. The following payams shall be targeted: Kuach, Kuerguine, Kedat, Nimini and Wathyotna all within Guit county. Approach will be integrated and aligned with the protection cluster SOs for 2019 which seek to: 1. Address high priority protection needs in hard to reach areas, 2. Promote preparedness by strengthening resilience of vulnerable children and 3. Target IDPs with assorted but durable protection solutions. Activities will be tailored to maximize reach and strengthen community level effectiveness in addressing identified vulnerabilities through direct support as well as advocacy.
Objective: To contribute to improved key indicators for child protection and reduced cases of rights abuse inside Unity State, South Sudan.
Target population: Vulnerable children inside Unity’s Guit County will be targeted as follows:
1. Conflict affected & displaced children and adolescent girls and boys (age 7-18).
2. Girls and boys (age 7-18) in host community in need of CP based on RNA recent reports
3. IDP women and men especially those identified as caregivers for complementary CP support and advocacy.
Projected number of beneficiaries:
Children targeted with comprehensive CP package will be 74,960 (G-37,900 and G-37,060) within the five payams of Guit County.
Intervention: The project will identify and train 20 case corkers based on case management principles and PSS priorities inside Guit county. The case workers will work hand in hand with already established community based CP systems to strengthen methodologies which have shown results. 6 new CFSs shall be established.

Needs

Empirical evidence shows strong direct correlation between economic stress and human rights violations affecting women and children in South Sudan (SIDA SS 2017 report). Cultural practices which place families’ daily survival burden disproportionately on women have aggravated household susceptibilities to rights abuses and as well as gender and age based trauma. There is a significant proportion of child-led households inside Unity state due to conflict related parental loss and family separations. Although there is no official data confirming their exact proportion, such households remain severely vulnerable. Most households inside Unity State like most other rural South Sudan are experiencing extreme social and economic stress due to years of conflict and associated disruptions in family livelihoods. The situation has been aggravated by reduced agricultural activity, increased monetization of rural economies and over-dependency on imports for essential supplies (FAO report, 2018). IPC phase classification forecasts that between January-March 2019, at least 150,000 individuals will drift into emergency (phase 4) and catastrophic (phase 5) food insecurity conditions. Another 465,000 will experience IPC crisis level classification (phase 3) over the same period. The result is that children will be severely affected in the country with over 1.2 million children under 5 registering GAM of 20%, way above the WHO threshold of 15% (Joint FAO/UNICEF/WFP news Release-September 2018). This figure is expected to go up later in the year if effective interventions are not put in place. Currently, Children Charity Organization (CCO) has an active caseload of 560 children receiving case management services (PSS, FTR and MRE). Given the immense pending caseload for case management with renewed hostilities in Guit and Rubkona counties the need for urgent continuity and scale-up of comprehensive case management cannot wait.

Activities

Activities will include1. Recruitment and training of additional 24 case workers to boost case management reach(SO1)2. Establishment of additional 10 CFS and 2 mobile units(SO1 & SO2) for enhanced PSS coverage3. Strengthening of community PSS initiatives through continuous mentorship of case workers and caregivers.To avoid duplication and overlap with other ongoing similar programs inside the County, CCO will be part of the c ounty coordination forum on Protection. There will also be regular consultation with the Protection cluster leads within the county, the State as well as the National forum.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

A bottom-up participatory approach will be used to mobilize, organize and sensitize community about their role in enhancing reach and effectiveness of the project as well as define the scope in terms of eligibility and access criteria. While such criteria shall be set by community, CCO will ensure they conform to the overall principles and centrality of Protection Details of available assistance will be communicated and fully discussed with beneficiaries (and their caregivers where applicable) to avoid cases of over-expectation and increase effectiveness of uptake of existing assistance. Selected community representatives and local authorities shall serve as M&E liaison focal persons to ensure assistance data is relayed back to community The project through established CFSs will put in place feedback and opinion boxes as a way of gathering uninfluenced data from beneficiaries. Community case support workers will also be provided with feedback tools to double as M&E agents for the project ensuring information flows both ways promptly..

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

All messages will be designed with sensitivity to gender and age. Adults will only be mixed with children whenever mutual and family consultations will be necessary.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Yes. CCO will work with community to identify trusted caregivers who will have the ear of the beneficiaries and who will then relay any messages of complaint or new ideas from beneficiaries. CCO will also keep an eye on local news channels for any messages whose implications may affect the project.

Community Engagement?

There is also provision for minor alterations in the activity schedules as and when members of the community may feel so without disrupting the entire calendar.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Briefly describe how

Yes. The project relied on two key mechanisms to establish protection risk profiles of targeted population.1. Directly reported cases from police files, hospital records and church reports,.2. Needs analysis building on ongoing UNICEF project.Having worked with communities inside Unity State, CCO has picked crucial lessons. The risk profile of different population segments whose data are already available with us will inform the implementation of this project. Hierarchically therefore, children (girls & boys) are at the top in our priority list, followed by women.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Past Protection reports indicate statistically significant cultural barriers against access to protection by the vulnerable in many clans within Guit County. The project intends to further widen the community dialogue forums around key issues regarding access to protection. Community rights champions and dialogue leaders will be engaged and trained to sustain advocacy against any threats to access that may arise. As an ongoing effort, other partners implementing projects within the county shall be engaged doe enhance synergy.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes. The project shall at all times during activity roll out maintain frank and unhindered discussions around key issues affecting members of different gender. Local authorities will be part and parcel of the roll out as much as possible to minimize response time.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project intends to apply consultative, community driven approach in addressing past inequalities or threats observed during past programming as follows:1. CCO will employ the philosophy of cultural neutrality when addressing extreme & outstanding threats against protection especially those affecting girl-child and married women. This approach will widen the environment of honest dialoguing between the different segments of community and enhance consensus in identifying those issues. Solutions will then be sought jointly and everyone will gradually feel part of it. .CCO recognizes the inseparability between breach in protection and peace. The project will exploit the on-going National peace-pact and work with partners implementing peace programs to advance messages of social cohesion through community forums. The project will identify peace champions within community and deploy them as volunteers to promote peaceful coexistence between different communities living inside Unity state.

Yes. Beneficiary safety is central to the success of protection project. Monitoring will be tailored to obtain perception of threat or danger by beneficiaries. This shall then be analysed and reported as a way of tracking impact.

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes. The project will develop and avail to beneficiaries feedback tools to gather data whose analysis will provide an indication of the 'feeling' effect for each beneficiary. This tool will be administered by trained community based volunteer enumerators and literate caregivers.
