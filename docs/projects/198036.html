Project Summary

Female genital mutilation (FGM) comprises all procedures involving partial or total removal of the external female genitalia or other injury to the female genital organs for non-medical reasons (WHO, UNICEF, UNFPA, 1997). Due to its large population, Nigeria has the third highest absolute number of women and girls, approximately 19.9 million, who have undergone FGM . FGM is a violation of girls’ and women’s human rights and is condemned by many international treaties and conventions, as well as by national legislation in many countries, including in Nigeria. Yet, where it is practiced, FGM is performed in line with tradition and social norms to ensure that girls are socially accepted and marriageable, and to uphold their status and honour and that of the entire family.
The prevalence of FGM actually increased from 19% in 2003 to 25% in 2013 in Nigeria . Although female genital mutilation is illegal in Nigeria, it remained practiced among commoners as well as health workers.
The project will aim at institutionalizing system of accountability, surveillance, monitoring, and evaluation of local actions to end FGM in five local governments in Borno State. The LGAs are MMC, Jere, Bama, Mafa and Damboa.
The project output are;
1. Enhanced capacity of Youths to use Digital platforms to sustain FGM education, report perpetrators to disciplinary bodies for penalties, and document in 5 LGAs most affected by FGM.
2. 45,000 young people mostly girls and women reached with information on FGM issue.
3. Community acceptance and support to provide evidenced-based accounts of real-time results and data of end FGM report actions and achievements.
4. Support the establishment of a system for regular documentation, dissemination, and easy access to information on FGM by 2017.
5. Institutions, agencies, and community stakeholders’ data contributing to the overall national information management system.

1 - Needs

Women and girl children suffer different forms of distress when subdued to FGM. The procedures can cause severe bleeding and problems urinating, and later cysts, infections, infertility as well as complications in childbirth and increased risk of newborn and maternal deaths (Plan International on FGM). At least 200 million girls and women alive today have undergone a form of female genital mutilation (FGM). An additional 2 million girls could undergo FGM by 2030 as a result of COVID-19, on top of the 68 million cases which had previously been anticipated.Various forms of advocacies have been conducted to stop and institutionalize the practice of FGM in Borno State, however, because of long-aged practice and perceived benefits, FGM has become difficult to abolish as stakeholders such as the traditional leaders have not shown the magnitude of commitment needed to bring about the change in the cultural norm.Through a desk review of the different needs, women and girls are faced with by being vulnerable to FGM, the following were captured;1. Need for respect for women despite their femineity 2. Their right to choose what happens to their body is denied, and hence the need for their rights to be protected.3. They also have need to be protected from bad cultural practices.The intervention will hence be addressing these needs from a community-based system-strengthening approach. This approach will ensure that youths are empowered to champion advocacies to implement the rights of the women at the community levelThis will give rise to the needed platform for women and girls to feel protected in their families and communities.
