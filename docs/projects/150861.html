Project Summary

The project aims to increase food availability and food access for drought affected communities in Mogadishu and Lower Shabelle IDPs. This will be done through commodity food voucher distribution to the affected households. Voucher distributions are the most appropriate modality in all the targeted areas, because there are functional markets. Low purchasing power of vulnerable local inhabitants dampens market incentives and limits the incentive of traders and wholesalers to expand markets. Through the provision of food vouchers, this project will assist in stimulating these markets. Using vouchers is also preferential as there would be cost saving rather than cash transfers which is costly due to higher transfer costs charged by money vendors. Food vouchers will be provided on a monthly basis to 1100 targeted households in Daynile, Kahda and Dharkenley IDPs in Mogadishu and Afgooye corridor IDPs of Lower Shabelle regions for a period of 5 months; unconditional vouchers will be supplied to vulnerable HH (estimated 10%). This assistance will address acute food needs by bolstering the purchasing power of targeted households through the projected lean season. The proposed voucher will provide access to a food basket consisting of national rice/Wheat flour/Pasta, beans/lentils/Tuna fish , and vegetable oil) supplying 80% of daily caloric needs of a family of 5-7 members. Conditionality is subject to completion of a variety of infrastructure and environmental rehabilitation projects aimed to improve local food security, environmental degradation and market access conditions. The monthly voucher value will be based on the FSNAU MEB for Somalia. The community members will identify and select assets that are not in proper working condition and those that have been damaged by human activities or natural calamities for rehabilitation/restoration. Possible cash for work activities will be bush clearing, erosion control, digging latrine pits, road work to improve access to markets, basic services and other work to preserve community assets and recover from effects of drought. Women and men will be equally involved in determining the final gender appropriate cash for work activities. Care will be taken to select activities that allow a high percentage of women to participate. Work norms will be established to ensure a reasonable quantity of work but allow women time to carry out their family responsibilities.
There will be regular coordination meetings at the field level between Food security and other relevant clusters to foster synergy, avoid duplication of efforts/ resources and share information on response efforts. SOYDA will keep the food security cluster, Ministry of Agriculture and UNOCHA regularly informed on progress of activities through sharing of progress reports and also share any challenges encountered for rapid assistance.

Needs

Despite recent improvement, an estimated 1.5 million people in Somalia are expected to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse through December according to findings from the post-Gu seasonal assessment conducted in June and July 2018. The food security situation in Lower Shabelle remains worrying. Over 15 percent or 182,000 people of the 1.2 million total population for Lower Shabelle are classified as falling in crisis and emergency phase and require interventions aimed at reducing food consumption gaps, reducing acute malnutrition, saving lives and protecting and saving livelihoods, while 315,000 are stress phase and require livelihood interventions. Majority of those in crisis and emergency are among the internally displaced people while the rest are in rural areas and likely to be displaced if the drought continues to worsen. Mogadishu corridor IDPs became a place of accommodating for many of Southern Somalia’s internally displaced people. To date the town has become home for many displaced persons from rural Lower Shabelle, with some also arriving from Bay and Bakol districts.Those facing acute food security Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse urgently need humanitarian assistance and livelihood support through December 2018 to prevent food security deterioration. The DTM rapid assessment conducted in June 2018 indicates that over 80 percent of the newly arrived IDPs as well as about 30 percent of the protracted IDPs have no access to adequate food and nutrition in Mogadishu and Lower Shabelle IDPs. Access to safe water and sanitation facilities and knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices is below 25 percent on average. Scaled up humanitarian assistance must be sustained in order to prevent further deterioration of food security and nutrition situation of the affected population. SOYDA is going to address above situations in our target areas of Benadir and Lower Shabele region.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

During displacement in emergency period there are many risk related to exclusion based on the displacement such risk factor are, child labor, sexual exploitation, discrimination and poor documentation on the specific needs of the people at risk.To mitigate this, SOYDA will however, put in place strict policy that will protect the right of women and children in emergency and conflict status, it will ensure all its staff are capacity build on child right, women, vulnerable groups and protection mainstreaming in its livelihood and food security activities, it shall also advocate community awareness in its implementation of livelihood through equal representation of community relief committee and village elders responsible for selecting the most vulnerable household as well ensure no harm policy within its implementation of the program.SOYDA will strive to adopt most reasonable project implementation methodology such as mobile outreach registration of beneficiaries in the far village to enable take services to the most vulnerable with less mobility status, through this, SOYDA will achieved handsomely on wider coverage to its target population.SOYDA will ensure to protects its beneficiary information and as well adopt reasonable program location that will ensure access and safety of the beneficiary.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The following are some of the risk factors facing the target population such factors are, discrimination against race,sex or gender employment opportunity, sexual exploitation in exchange of relief food as well as poor documentation, monitoring and reporting within the program implementation.SOYDA will however, mitigated such factors without hinderance,it intends to provide equal opportunity for employment to all these qualified ad within the local areas as first priority and based on their skills, it shall also ensure both men and women boys and girls have equal access to project information and services in the target areas, through this program SOYDA will ensure its staff and community members were capacity built on “DO NO HARM” principle of neutrality as well as disseminates project information to the community members through consultative forums and in particular religious institution such as mosque. This will facilitate more inclusive of both gender and various community members hence create an atmosphere of more responsive coverage among the target group. SOYDA will ensure the safety and dignity of its beneficiaries by providing regular monitoring and reporting of the new challenges and opportunity in consultation and coordination with other stakeholders and community key point

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The following are main risk factors that might impact negatively on the beneficiaries during implementation such factors are poor timing during distribution of foods or agricultural input to the beneficiary which may fall late in the day hence susceptible for theft, cross fire, intimidation as well as sexual exploitation among poor women and girls beneficiaries, it may also risk of beneficiary by registering one main clan against other dwellers in settlement through collusion with project staff hence this might create community tension in this case conflict might a raise thus dangering the safety of these vulnerable population.Also leaking of vital beneficiary data might be risk associated with arm conflict and violence this is because in the instance militia groups get the beneficiary details they might intimidate and force to surrender the little foods provided thus risking the life of the vulnerable women, men, boys and girls population.In mitigating the above SOYDA intends to establish strict beneficiary data protection for safety purpose as well as facilitates equal representation among the target community in selection of community village committee that will directly work with SOYDA food security teams. Staff and community capacity shall be build and ensure staff signed code of conduct
