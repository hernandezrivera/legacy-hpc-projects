Project Summary

13 years of conﬂict in Syria has not only led to destruction and reduced functionality of infrastructure, but also affected socio-economic patterns, and diminished Syria’s population’s ability to sustain living standards, to beneﬁt from decent and gainful livelihood opportunities and to access essential services. Consequently, the protracted nature of the crisis combining with 2023 earthquake have put an additional strain on social cohesion, community security and peace building.
Needs assessments through various sources indicate that current PIN for ERL Support increased from 13.8 M to 15.4 M, which means the growing need for job opportunities after the massive loss of livelihoods and increased pressure on essential services and infrastructure.
Based on that, the ERL sector’s objectives have been categorized under the three interrelated components: a) Support the maintenance and improve equitable access to basic services, focusing on electricity; b) Increasing the job opportunities, providing vocational skills; and c) Strengthen social cohesion by empowering communities to lead the recovery process, focus on joint context analysis with communities, build their capacity and facilitate inclusive community led and participatory early recovery plans and priorities’ areas.
Through the area-based and community engagement approach, considering the context sensitivity in participatory planning and designing with community, UN-Habitat aims to boost sustainable access to basic and social services to displaced, host and returnee populations in selected neighbourhoods in (Rural Damascus (Arbin, Nashabiyeh, and Maliha), Lattakia (Jableh), and Dar’a (Dar’a and Izra’).
Access to electricity is one of the most critical service underpinnings to address the wider sector needs. By doing so, the project will achieve an immediate response to vulnerability communities and can be effective in moving communities along a continuum from dependance to resilience and recovery.
In practice, the approach consists of the rehabilitation of public and open spaces, roads, sidewalks, providing solar lighting. It also includes neighbourhoods’ cleanings and community-based initiatives to build the awareness and social cohesion within damage-affected neighbourhoods through ‘Neighbourhood Recovery Action Plans', which have been developed through a municipality and community-engaged program supported by UN-Habitat.
The restoration of urban service and infrastructure (e.g. restoration of market area and its surrounding roads) will contribute to enhance self-reliance of communities and lead to economic recovery. The rehabilitated infrastructures will revive the value chain and urban-rural linkages. Through this process the livelihoods opportunity for urban and rural communities will also increase.
The selection of targeted locations is based on:
- Being in line with the ERL Sector and HRP 2024 strategic priorities and objectives, as well as severity of needs
- Being priority interventions and location identiﬁed through area-based recovery plan with detailed ﬁeld assessments and consultation with local communities and municipalities
- Complementarity with ongoing projects already through UN-Habitat resources and donor commitments including UN-Habitat projects under Shelter and WASH sectors.
- High numbers of people in need, vulnerable and returnees targeted by the sector 28,000 in these locations
- Guided by the participatory recovery plans supported by UN-Habitat.
The proposed intervention recognizes the critical importance of addressing and combating Gender-Based Violence (GBV) and Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) within community's public and open spaces rehabilitation projects. These issues pose significant risks to vulnerable populations, particularly women and girls, and require a comprehensive response.
However, the risks of GBV and PSEA risks and risks’ mitigation are explained clearly in the proposal.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Acknowledging the core ERL objectives, it is in critical need to provide essential services and to facilitate socio-economic recovery not only for 4.6 million internally displaced persons, but also for host communities and returnees as well.Livelihood: According to sector estimates, 48% support to livelihood alone has proven insufficient, as well as the provision of quality services alone. Therefore, only a multi-livelihood and services approach can provide the needed support. Besides to the lack of getting access to employment, the high likelihood of youth to adopt negative coping mechanisms such as joining extremist organizations, crime, traﬃcking etc. is an important consideration.Social cohesion and community security: Prolonged conﬂict and the challenges of COVID-19 and the results of 6 Feb earthquake have weakened the social fabric in ways which equally affects host communities and IDPs. This situation is further pressured by returnee dynamics, adding strain to already overburdened systems. In the selected locations, the project will implement the following activities:I- Updating/or creating the recovery action plan using the area-based approach, engaging target communities in the context analysis to identify the real needs and priorities.II- Restoration of and access to basic social services: 1. Cleaning the neighbourhood from waste and sterilizing the roads and streets. 2. Rehabilitation of market area, public/open spaces, roads, and footpaths and provide street solar lighting.3. Creating a gender friendly learning open spaces for women, youth and children to conduct awareness campaigns and sessionsIII- Improve effective local public services delivery:1. Provision of required training to engage the communities in monitoring the humanitarian support being provided. 2. Create a competition among neighbourhoods by implementing the initiative of “cleanest neighbourhood initiative”. Following the respective ministries’ procedural requirements, projects will be implemented through local resources – communities, municipal technical departments, private sector entities, and workforce engaged from affected populations, in particular unemployed youth will be an important to provide short-term employment opportunities.
