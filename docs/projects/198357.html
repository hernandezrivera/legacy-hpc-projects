Project Summary

The project aims to: 1) strengthen IDP and host communities resilience through diversified livelihoods and improved access to food. 
2) prepare target communities for external shocks with developed coping strategies.
3) support women, youth and children to thrive in peaceful and safe environments that promote their participation in decision-making processes.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project focuses on increasing agricultural production and livelihood diversification, increasing food consumption and diversity of food sources in 6 communities of Chiure and Montepuez districts in Cabo Delgado province.In resettlement areas, project activities combine a series of elements covering farm and non-farm activities, income generation and capacity building of partners and beneficiaries, engaging IDPs and host communities. The project promotes Climate Smart Agriculture (CSA) technologies including conservation agriculture, and agroforestry grounded in healthy ecosystems, stable and enduring institutions and sustainable financing approach. SC established saving groups using a well-established VSLA methodology within the target communities, in which each VSLA member saves, gives and takes out loans, repays their loans with interest, and individually invests this money into small businesses. This provides further opportunities for communities to increase income generation and enhance their resilience in the face of future shocks and stresses, as well as providing women with access to social and economic capital and increasing their self-confidence. 2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

1. Increased agricultural production: support target IDPs and affected host communities with greater focus on female-headed households (through a targeting criterion developed closely with local social protection institutions) to increase production through the provision of agricultural tools and assets, as well as training to improve practices. The project works in close coordination with the District Services for Economic Activities (SDAE) to identify the best approach to promoting economic development based on the proposed assessment results, observing the best practices in places and in line with socio-economic needs. The interventions promote Climate Smart Agriculture (CSA) technologies including conservation agriculture, and agroforestry grounded in healthy ecosystems, stable and enduring institutions and sustainable financing approach. 2. Diversified livelihoods, vocational training and establishment of VSLA groups: For those households that are not in a position to access land, we will provide opportunities for alternative livelihoods by distributing inputs, along with associated vocational training and coaching. A greater focus will be given to female headed households (through developed targeting criteria). 30 VSLA groups are formed and started saving activities.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The project is informed by recent and/or ongoing assessments carried out in CD, including a protection assessment; a Rapid GBV assessment; a protection mapping; and a protection audit. The key gender aspects informing this project, which are informed by experience and up-to-date research, include analysing the specific stresses that host and IDP women are facing in this specific crisis, and ensuring that the project does not do harm in placing disproportionate responsibilities on women in livelihood activities on top of their care and domestic work, nor in exacerbating vulnerability to SGBV. A Gender Marker will be used in combination with SC gender-sensitive MEAL approach which collects sex and age disaggregated data, implements a comprehensive Feedback and Complaints Mechanism and monitors indicators focussed on gender roles and relations.At the beginning of the project, SC completed a Conflict & Gender-Sensitive Crisis/Risk Analysis to support the identification of protection threats against, and vulnerabilities of, specific groups in the current crisis context in CD. Findings from this analysis informed an appropriate and relevant protection strategy to advise project implementation. One of the broader project outcomes focuses specifically on strengthening the protection and participation of the most vulnerable women, children and youth, contributing to inclusive, peaceful and safe environments in Cabo Delgado.Save the Children adheres to the principles and has specific policies in place for Do-No-Harm and PSEA. Additionally, SC rolls out regular training on PSEA for all staff, and particularly field staff/ community volunteers. The adoption of the PSEA policy by programme staff ensure that both staff and community members are aware of how to report concerns. Using the SC Child Safe Programming Guidance, Safeguarding staff provide PSEA and child safeguarding induction, awareness raising sessions, and training to staff, suppliers, volunteers and all who come into contact with children during the project. Feedback mechanisms are in place to ensure that community members are able to identify and report any protection issues arising. Additionally, PSEA focal points attend trainings on GBV/CP case referral for SEA cases to improve access to survivor support. In Cabo Delgado, SC is co-leading the PSEA network, ensuring coordination and providing oversight on activities related to protection from sexual exploitation and abuse by international and national staff. SC’s co-lead on the PSEA network also contributes to rolling out training for other PSEA focal points which in turn is rolled out to field staff, and ensures an effective response to SEA cases that are reported and improves awareness of PSEA in the province. Close coordination is maintained with the Protection Cluster for additional assistance.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

SC adheres to the Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS) in making sure that the response plan is implemented in a way that ensures participation of people, complaints are welcome and addressed and interventions capture learnings and lessons and improve on a continual basis. To achieve this, an accountability system is in place to fulfil the three key dimensions - information sharing, participation and feedback mechanisms.• Information will be shared with the beneficiaries mainly through community meetings and IEC material with key information.• Participation will be ensured through community meetings, operational surveys, FGD with adults and children as well as child consultation using friendly mechanisms.• During distributions and other community-based activities, apart from verbal contact with beneficiaries, the project will use the Linha Verde hotline and Complaint Boxes for them to raise their concerns and feedback about the project. Appropriate actions will be taken to address feedback and complaints routinely.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Key inclusivity aspects include a social group-sensitive contextual analysis, gender and disability-sensitive procedures and criteria in beneficiary selection, accessible physical infrastructure and inclusive access to services. SC will ensure that women, youth, and children take leading roles in community-based structures and decision-making processes to create more cohesive, inclusive, and equitable communities. The elderly and people with disability (PWD) in this context may already have impairments that affect their daily functioning, and the “triple crisis” of displacement, disease and natural disaster recovery can overwhelm their capacity to cope. The project will ensure all activities at the community level are accessible and communicated in advance. Additionally, the project considers the needs of the elderly and PWD in the construction of resilient infrastructure; depending on the location of these sites, ensuring accessibility will be a priority. The creation of a peaceful, inclusive society requires the engagement and support of these groups and awareness of the specific challenges they face. Community dialogues and community-based protection monitoring will be cognizant of the needs and protection risks of PWD and the elderly. The project will also empower them by giving them a voice to influence project effectiveness and to be heard by different stakeholders, including duty-bearers and influential local-level decision-makers.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The CO is in the process of developing a strategy to reduce our environmental impact, primarily through two approaches: Maximising the use of sustainable / natural resources, and Reducing waste, CO2 emissions, carbon footprintExamples, for example, are: To work as locally as possible – limit flights from Maputo to Pemba and instead, when possible, work with staff in Pemba office. All construction projects will be preceded by environmental assessments to understand the impacts that the project could have and mitigating measures for risks that are identified. Where possible, building materials will be procured through sustainable means and we will work with supply chain with framework agreements with suppliers that prioritize reducing their environmental impact, and the use of more environmentally friendly materials for the building will be prioritized.Minimise as much as possible the use of plastics in offices, meetings and while on trips, as well as use of wrappings, packaging, plastic bags etc. The project will promote Climate Smart Agriculture (CSA) technologies including conservation agriculture, and agroforestry grounded in healthy ecosystems, stable and enduring institutions and a sustainable financing approach. Including CSA in this curriculum could have lasting effects on how communities perceive the impact of climate change, and continue to think about new climate resilient techniques to reduce food insecurity while minimizing the impact on the environment around them.
