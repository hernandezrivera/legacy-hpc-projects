Project Summary

This project aims to provide Rohingya refugee children with education support (early learning and basic education). Adults from the refugee community receive in turn life-skill support through community resource centers. This project also targets host community and provides quality development of secondary schools and establish multi-purpose community learning centers for adults.

Objectives

Objective 1. Expand and strengthen immediate access to equitable learning opportunities, in a safe and protective environment, for crisis-affected refugee and host community children and youth (4-24 years) Objective 2. Improve the quality of teaching and learning for refugee and host community children and youth, aligned with Education Sector standards, and increase teaching-related professional development opportunities Objective 3. Increase refugee and host community participation and engagement in the education of children and youth

Needs

For Rohingya Community:Over 332,000 Rohingya children and youth (3-24 years) are still not accessing any learning opportunities, of these, 48 per cent are children from 3-14 years. The focus will be to provide these children and youth with opportunities to learn and develop skills. Over 2,000 learning facilities are needed to meet the existing needs of pre-primary and primary aged children 3-14 (Sector Gap Analysis/UNHCR MSNA). Over 26 camps (out of 34) have less than half of the learning facilities required to cover out-of-school children. Learning facilities have suffered from a lack of standardized and relevant teaching and learning materials, due to the sensitivities associated with Rohingya refugees having access to the national formal and non-formal curriculum. Recruitment of sufficiently educated Rohingya learning facilitators remains a challenge.The provision of learning and skills opportunities for adolescents and youth requires urgent focus in 2019. To date, 97 percent of refugee adolescents and youth do not have access to any learning opportunities. For the host community:Cox’s Bazar has been one of the lowest performing districts in Bangladesh against many of its education indicators. Cox’s Bazar net intake rate for the first grade of primary school is the lowest in the country at 72.6% for boys and 69.1% for girls, compared to the national average of 98%. Similarly, the dropout rate in primary education is the highest in the country—39.6% for boys and 22.8% for girls, compared to the national average of 22.3% and 16.1% respectively.

Activities or outputs

Objective 1.a. Provision of early learning and basic education for Rohingya and host community children and youth (4-24 years, at least 50% girls).b. Provision of life-skills supports through engaging youth in Rohingya and host community (10-24 years, at least 50% girls).c. Capacity development of mainstream schools in host community through engagement with teachers, students and community (at least 50% girls). Objective 2.a. Arrange learning material to Rohingya and host community children, adolescents and youth (4-24 years, 50% girls). b. Provision of teaching-learning material to the teacher of the host community and Rohingya community. c. Organize capacity development initiatives (training, mentoring, coaching etc.) for teachers to delivery the learning competency framework and approach (LCFA) at respective levels. Objective 3.a. Organize forums and committee to ensure participation, ownership, and the improvement of practices on girls education, child protection, caregiving, life skills, disabilities, social cohesion, disaster risk reduction (DRR). b. Coordinate with other sectors (WASH, Child Protection, Protection, Shelter, Food Security etc) and agencies to ensure effective and efficient implementation of activities.

Key project/performance indicators

- Number of safe, protective, and equipped learning centers and facilities established with standardized educational materials.- Number of children, adolescents, and youth enrolled and continuing in early Learning, non-formal basic education facilities, participate in Community Resource centers, multi-purpose community learning centers (MCLC) and trained through subject based and leadership training in secondary schools. (considering at least 50% girls). - Number of male and female teachers, language instructors and facilitators from Rohingya and host community-recruited and trained on teaching-learning process, life skills, safety and other child-related issues like gender, child rights, protection etc.- Number of Center/School Management Committees established, trained in learning facility/school management, Disaster Risk Reduction, and the participatory engagement of community
