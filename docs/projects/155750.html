Project Summary

The proposed project is aimed to fill the critical gaps identified during FATA Vulnerability assessment 2017 and HeRAMS study jointly conducted by UN Agencies and DoH FATA in October 2017. According to the study a total number of 1.6 Million displaced people has returned to their native areas. Access to health care in the return areas is poor due to multiple reasons. The study revealed that 24% of women who gave birth in the past three years did not seek medical attention during their pregnancy, and utilization of Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI) is also very low. This low coverage is mainly attributed to damaged infrastructure, non-availability of skilled Human resources and essential medical supplies. The Agencies most affected in terms of infrastructure are North, South Waziristan, Orakzai and Mohmand Agency. The study revealed that the most affected areas of FATA with respect to non-availability of skilled human resources are North & South Waziristan, Orakzai and Mohmand Agency. Similarly, availability of equipment and required medical supplies is a challenge in North & South Waziristan and Kurram Agency. The tribal districts is also facing the challenge of low capacity of available skilled human resources. The purpose of the proposed project is to renovate/refurbish health facilities, provide skilled human resources for the specified time, ensure availability of equipment and required medical supplies and enhance capacity of available human resources. The proposed project will ensure efficient provision of health services with the support of DoH including but not limited PHC, MNCH, EPI and psycho-social support to the population segments who are in critical need. Training and capacity building of DoH staff will be conducted on standard protocols such as MISP, IMNCI, ENC and impactful supervision and monitoring to improve quality of Health care in the targeted areas. Similarly, impact-full social mobilization will be focused to address the promotive and preventive aspects of controlling common diseases. Functional referral pathways will be established among available health components and levels of health care facilities to maximize outputs/results. The proposed project will target all population segments (Men, Women, Boys and Girls) with special focus on children under 5, pregnant lactating women, adolescents and people with special needs (elderly, disabilities, genetic disorders, chronic diseases, etc).

Findings of FATA Vulnerability assessment 2017: The average distance to the nearest health facility in tribal districts of KP is 11 KM, thus negatively affecting the reach of population segments particularly females, young children, elderly and special people to receive required health services. Approximately 24% mothers having children in the last 3 years reported that they didn’t seek any medical help or services during their pregnancy. Fourty percent women reported deliveries at home. Around 20% of the households from FATA and 15% from FR areas reported that their eligible children were not vaccinated during routine EPI. Around half of the respondents (51% in FATA and 46% in FR areas) reported that during the last three months, they were unable to seek medical help even it was needed. Key reasons include the unavailability of relevant staff, service and financial constraint. Over 80% of health infrastructures in return areas are severely damaged by the crisis and remain non-functional. Many of the health staff who were displaced especially the female health workers have not resumed work. In addition, only 11% and 22% respectively of responders in FATA and FR Areas have ever been visited by Lady Health Workers during the last three months. Access to safe water and sanitation is also poor with 45% of the population in return areas relying on unsafe drinking water. 36% Returnees have no toilets and practice open defecation. The presence of water-borne diseases is prevalent with 33% household reported diarrhea in FATA. Mental health problem is a big issue with [16-39%] particularly females reported mental health and psychosocial issues (depression, anxiety post-traumatic stress disorder).

1 - Ensure returnees’ and displaced populations’ continued access to emergency essentialhealth care, especially those who live far from health facilities in all KP Tribal Districts andin displacement2 - Operationalize non-functional health facilities for integrated primary health care,maternal and child health (MNCH) and reproductive health services3 - Strengthen disease surveillance and response to outbreaks of communicable diseases4 - Prevent and treat pneumonia and diarrhea, the leading causes of morbidity in FATA5 - Increase access to routine immunization, particularly for children and women6 - Improve access to quality health care services by strengthening the supply chain formedicines, medical supplies, and the rehabilitation and repair of health facilities
