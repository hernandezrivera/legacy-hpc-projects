Needs

Living conditions for the Rohingya community are very poor and sccess to basic services such as water, health, sanitation and shelter is insufficient, and levels of malnutrition remain high, with the most recent SMART (May 2018) survey indicating a global acute malnutrition (GAM) rate of 12.0%. The World Food Programme (WFP) Refugee Influx Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA) report, (Aug2018) found that only 7 refugee households out of 10 had access to an ‘acceptable’ diet. It notes that food assistance plays a key role in ensuring access to an acceptable diet among refugees: 55% of those that did not receive assistance had ‘poor’ or ‘borderline’ diets, compared to 31% of those that did receive food assistance. This trend was particularly pronounced among new arrivals: only 27% of new arrivals not receiving food assistance had acceptable food consumption. Measures of dietary diversity also demonstrated inadequacy, with only 35% of refugees having access to a minimum acceptable dietary diversity. Given constraints such as lack of land for own food production, limited opportunity for income generation (47% of refugee households do not participate in any income generating activities (WFP, 2018)), movement restrictions, and limited access to cooking fuel and energy sources, the refugee population remains highly dependent on food assistance but this is not a sustainable solution and interventions reducing dependency and promote self-reliance are critical. These interventions must account for the constraints faced by Rohingya women, whose access to income generating activities is particularly restricted as Rohingya cultural norms typically require that women tend exclusively to household-level work. Yet female participants in FGDs conduct by ACF, SCI and Oxfam in Aug 2018 indicated an interest in activities such as tailoring, sewing, handicrafts and cooking. Protection risks must be mitigated by ensuring wider consultation with family members and community gatekeepers during project design and implementation when promoting self-reliance among women, particularly in the case of female-headed households, which account for around 12% of the total households (Joint Agency Research Report, August 2018). The Rohingya refugee influx has placed significant pressures upon the host communities in the Ukhiya and Teknaf unions, straining resources, infrastructure, public services and the local economy. While access to basic services was already limited, and patterns of poor food consumption were already much higher than the national average, the dramatic growth in the population has led to rising prices for food, firewood and transport, increased competition for jobs, and placed significant pressure on water, basic services and the environment. Ultimately, in a region where the population remains highly vulnerable to natural disasters and hazards such as tropical cyclones, tidal surges, flash floods, deforestation and landslides, the ongoing crisis has undermined food security and livelihoods and exacerbated the level of vulnerability. While not as pronounced as within the refugee community, disparities also exists in terms of the participation of host community men and women in income generating activities. Significantly, host community households headed by women are more vulnerable to food insecurity, with 45% of such households being “vulnerable” or “highly vulnerable”, compared to 35% among male-headed households, according to the REVA report. Efforts to promote engagement in income generating activities must therefore be designed based on consultation with women to meet their needs.

Activities or outputs

Sector Objective 1: • Complementary Food Assistance (Voucher)o Complementary food assistance vouchers will be provided on a blanket basis in camp 20-E, which has a population of 1,003 households in camp 20-E, which is currently not served by complementary food assistance agencies, and in the planned “Choukali” camp, where 970 households will be relocated. Monthly, targeted households will be provided with unconditional value vouchers to the value of 830 BDT, or 1,170 BDT in the case of large (8+) families. These values correspond to the food security minimum expenditure basket calculation also used in WFP’s e-voucher project (excluding staples such as rice, vegetable oil and lentils) and the range of items purchasable will be restricted in order to encourage consumption of fresh food items • Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance for Host Community householdso Concern will provide 2,736 households in Ukhiya and 1,662 households in Teknaf with multi-purpose cash assistance on a monthly basis over a period of four months. Each transfer will be to the value of BDT 5,500. These cash transfers will be provided as a subsistence allowance aiming to prevent the need to rely on negative coping strategies. In order to avoid dependence and promote household-level income generation, Concern will seek to include targeted households within the livelihoods-oriented activities outlined under Objective 2. Sector Objective 2: • Promoting Self-Reliance through Refugees’ Skills Trainingo Concern will establish one multipurpose training centre, where skills trainings will be provided to 30 participants on a monthly basis for 10 months, reaching 300 individuals, with a focus on ensuring the participation of women and youth/young adults (age 18-25). Given cultural restrictions which prevent the movement of women within the camp, alternative training modalities will also be implemented, including a house-to-house master trainer approach. It is envisaged that a house-to-house approach, through which 55 master trainers will provide skills training at the household level, an additional 440 households can be reached each month, or 5,280 over 12 months. Skills training will also be provided to 258 refugees living within the host community. • Livelihoods Resilience Training and Grantso 25 Livelihoods Groups of 30 individuals will be formed and provided with training (750 households). The groups will be formed on a thematic basis (e.g. agriculture, fishing, crop production, livestock/poultry rearing, etc.) and will provide a platform for addressing shared challenges and sharing information. The groups will also be provided with training regarding the respective livelihoods practices, with a focus on improving practices and promoting resilience. After completion of the training, and upon development of business plans, participants will be provided with a grant of BDT 20,000 to invest in their enterprises. Vulnerable households will be prioritized, with a focus on women and youth-headed households. Furthermore, livelihoods skills training will be provided to 2,268 host community members. Upon completion of the training these participants will be provided with in-kind inputs or cash grants to support their engagement in income generating activities. Sector Objective 3: • Community-Based Disaster Planning (CBDP) and Disaster Risk Reductiono In 2 unions in Teknaf and/or Ukhiya, Concern will implement the national CBDP institutional model, adapted to support the capacity development of the Upazilla and Union DMCs to identify and mitigate disaster risks in the post-FDMN influx context. Through the capacity building of union and upazilla government departments and disaster management committees on the CBDP approach and implementation of community risk assessment processes, the capacity to anticipate shocks, plan risk mitigation strategies, and develop disaster contingency plans will be strengthened, in relation to food security.
