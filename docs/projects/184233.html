Project Summary

In Somalia, an estimated 3 million school aged children are out of school with the majority of these being in the South and Central Somalia. Education in Somalia is negatively affected by drought, floods and conflict, contributing to food insecurity and depriving children and youth of access to quality education. The Education for all project will focus on: Objective 1: Ensure emergency and crises-affected children and youth have access to a safe and protective learning environment; Objective 2: Ensure vulnerable children and youth are engaged in lifesaving learning that promotes personal well-being and social cohesion; and Objective 3: Strengthened capacity to deliver effective and coordinated education in emergencies preparedness and response within the education system.
Africa Educational Trust (AET) will collaborate with schools, Community Education Committees (CECs), State Ministries of Education (MOE), Regional Educational Officers(REOs) and host communities to target 59,000 primary age children and out-of-school youth, across 30 primary schools, especially IDPs and returnee communities promoting equitable educational opportunities for girls and inclusion for children with disabilities. Besides construction of disability friendly classrooms and WASH facilities, children with disabilities will receive assistive devices and adapted learning materials to enhance their participation in education. Through community engagement sessions, the project will sensitize duty bearers on their roles and responsibilities to protect and support children. The project will increase access to primary education and alternative basic education(ABE) through the rehabilitation of existing learning spaces and the provision of gender-sensitive latrines. Teachers will be trained in inclusive, protective and social-emotional teaching and learning and psycho-social support, improving the quality of teaching and learning outcomes for primary students as well as training in ABE for out-of-school youth. During this training, there will be a specific focus placed on positive behavioral management in order to equip teachers with the skills to deliver lessons in a child friendly and conducive manner. Teachers and Head Teachers will be incentivized to promote flexible, quality and inclusive learning practices which can maximize access to education for our target communities and utilize existing school structures efficiently through double shift teaching schedules. Training and support for CECs will build on students’ safety and school development through the development of school improvement plans which are community driven and sustainable. As part of this, we will strengthen existing referral systems both at community and school level so any protection cases can be responded to. Emergency school feeding to increase enrollment, retention and learning for learners will be managed by the CECs. The project will also provide clean drinking water through water trucks, donkey carts, and permanent connections to water systems. In the long run, this is expected to impact the performance of the students in class and improve their overall health. The project will also support out-of-school youth and primary children through cash transfers direct to parents/caregivers. Eligibility will be determined through criteria set by the community in liaison with the State MOE. Primary and ABE students will receive teaching and learning materials to improve the quality of teaching and learning. 25 Education cluster partners and Ministry of Education (MOE) staff will be trained in emergency preparedness and response planning and coordination in order to strengthen their capacity to deliver effective and coordinated education programes.
AET works in Somalia, South Sudan, Uganda and Kenya to find innovative ways to provide quality formal and non-formal education for disadvantaged children and young people, including girls, pastoralists, street children and rural communities

1. - Projects activities

Objective 1: Ensure emergency and crises-affected children and youth have access to a safe and protective learning environment.Objective 1 Activities:1.1: Construct new or rehabilitate dilapidated classrooms with child-friendly, accessible and gender-sensitive WASH facilities;1.2: Sensitize duty bearers at all levels on their roles and responsibilities to protect and support children;1.3: Provide Cash in education in emergencies assistance for out-of-school youth in rural areas and IDP children (costs include student transport, stationery, school bag and uniform);1.4: Strengthen school and community level referral mechanisms, which will include appointing child protection focal points at school;1.5: Conduct community sensitization sessions so the community are sensitized on children's rights and the importance of education for all;1.6: Provision of specialised support for particularly marginalised groups of children, such as girls and CWDs, in order to ensure they can access an education (such as provision of MHM kits, assistive devices, adapted learning materials etc). Objective 2: Ensure vulnerable children and youth are engaged in lifesaving learning that promotes personal well-being and social cohesion.Objective 2 Activities:2.1: Provision of teaching and learning materials (TLM) for emergency-affected learners;2.2: Pay incentives to teachers in emergency-affected schools;2.3: Provision of safe drinking water and hygiene awareness for crisis-affected children;2.1.4: Provision of school feeding to crisis-affected children. Objective 3: Strengthened capacity to deliver effective and coordinated education-in-emergencies preparedness and response within the education system.Objective 3 Activities:3.1: Emergency education teachers training in inclusive, protective and social-emotional teaching and learning and psychosocial support;3.2: CECs training in school management, child protection and school safety;3.3: Education clusters and MOE staff training in emergency preparedness and response planning and coordination. AET recognizes that in cases where parents are faced with financial constraints to educate or feed their children, most parents opt for allowing children to remain at home. This is one area that the project through community engagement will ensure children are enrolled and retained in schools with the support of parents and religious leaders and trained school Community Education Committees. AET will build the capacity of CECs to assess families in need of cash transfers to support financing of their children’s education, particularly through scholarships to enable access to education for families in poverty. The intervention would be guided by Somali Federal Government and State Ministries of Education and the community through engagement and participation and would seek to build on already developed programmes and materials and infuse AET experience and expertise in rolling out programmes that would be of quality and benefit to the marginalized learners in the target regions AET will seek to build partnerships with other implementing partners and EiE cluster members working in the target regions to ensure project activities are not duplicated and to share lessons learnt during implementation. Networking through social media and face to face meetings will also be exploited to maximize efficiency, minimize the risk of duplication and reduce program transaction costs

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

4.1 - The project will involve all targeted communities in project implementation right from the onset. Community education committees and local leaders will be involved in mobilizing all the target communities. During co-creation and project start-up phase, consultation meetings will be held with stakeholders to ensure the views of minority groups feed into the design. Project review meetings will be held quarterly in the course of project implementation to ensure continuous collection of feedback from all stakeholders. This will ensure that all minority clans/ethnic groups are included in our project. AET will seek the guidance of the communities in developing activity work plans to ensure communities are aware of and take responsibility of the process while ensuring prudent utilization of the available resources. The community will be part of the implementing team and will be charged with the responsibility of developing accountability mechanisms for the resources through consultation with AET and steering committees 4.2 - AET will work closely with other actors, local government authorities, local leaders and human rights organizations to guard against sexual exploitation of women and girls. All the project staff, teachers and CECs will undergo safeguarding and GESI training so they are able to prevent, respond to and refer any cases that they may identify. Referral mechanisms will be established and existing systems will be strengthened so that girls and women are aware of how they can report any incidences that may occur. A child protection focal point person will be appointed at each school so they can monitor and follow up on any issues that may be identified during this project. Additionally, education is key to reduce gendered forms of abuse, violence and exploitation. We will therefore incorporate sessions during which we explore this topic in the curriculum with children so they are aware of their rights. Alongside this, we will host community sensitisation sessions with key community members so that they are informed about children's rights and are able to identify, respond to and refer any forms of abuse that may occur in the community.
