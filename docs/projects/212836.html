Project Summary

The assessment report conducted by the Camp Coordination and Management Cluster revealed that there are 87 registered camps under the Camp Coordination and Management Cluster in Jisr al-Shughur, accommodating 51,668 individuals (comprising 11,682 families), including 4,158 individuals with special needs. This evaluation spanned from the eleventh month of 2023 to the first month of 2024.
During this period, the provision of non-food services in the Jisr al-Shughur area did not surpass a 50% coverage rate. Specifically, in the Badama district and Jisr al-Shughur district (consisting of 39 camps), the coverage rate for non-food items did not exceed 21%.
Similarly, in Azaz, there are 91 camps registered with the sector, hosting 223,497 individuals (belonging to 42,928 families), with 10,060 individuals having special needs. A year ago, the distribution of non-food items in the Azaz region did not exceed 35%. For instance, there was a three-month period where no non-food items were distributed concurrently, affecting five camps (458 families).
In general, distributions of non-food items are positive in the Al-Bab region, reaching 65% in the first month of 2024. The number of camps registered with the sector in the city of Al-Bab is 39 camps, hosting 54,419 people (from 10,671 families), including 1,812 individuals with special needs.
However, despite distributions of non-food items at the regional level in Al-Bab, these distributions are almost non-existent or very weak in some villages such as Al-Burj, Al-Hadath, and Sheikh Alwan, which collectively host 4 camps (1,507 families).
This project aims to improve the lives of vulnerable individuals and internally displaced persons (IDPs) by providing them with essential resources and improving their living conditions. To achieve this:
1. Winter assistance:
winterization voucher: $180 per household will be distributed through a voucher system to purchase winter necessities like heating fuel, stoves, clothing, and other non-food items (NFIs). This will directly benefit 20,000 individuals (4,000 households).
2. Shelter:
Construction: 500 temporary shelters will be built, providing homes for 1,800 individuals (500 families).
Minor Repairs: 400 homes will receive light repairs, benefiting 2,500 individuals (400 families).
Rehabilitation: 400 partially built or damaged homes will be rehabilitated, providing shelter for an additional 2,500 individuals (400 families).
This project prioritizes "Do No Harm" and gender sensitivity. We'll consult the community and hold FGDs with women & girls to address the specific needs of all (men, women, boys, girls) throughout the project cycle.
This ensures their voices are heard and fosters a safe, inclusive environment.

Likelihood: Medium
Impact: Medium
Mitigation Measures:
Conduct a thorough needs assessment and pre-implementation planning.
Develop contingency plans to address potential logistical challenges and security threats.
Maintain open communication with local authorities and stakeholders.
Build strong partnerships with local organizations to ensure project sustainability.
Risk 2: GBV risk, due to the project's focus on vulnerable populations, potential power imbalances, and limited access to support services in the target area.
Likelihood: Medium
Impact: High
Mitigation Measures:
Implement a GBV risk assessment to identify potential vulnerabilities and risks throughout the project cycle.
Develop and disseminate a GBV prevention policy outlining zero tolerance for GBV, reporting mechanisms, and support services available.
Train staff and beneficiaries on GBV prevention and response strategies.
Segregate genders during project activities when necessary and ensure adequate lighting and security in distribution locations.
Partner with local GBV service providers to ensure referrals and support for survivors.
