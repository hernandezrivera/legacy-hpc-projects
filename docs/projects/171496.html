Project Summary

In response to the displacements in the Hiraan region due to flooding, conflict and expecting drought, HIWA is proposing response with mobile teams providing child Protection support services to both IDPs and host communities. In this project, HIWA will provide emergency child protection services to vulnerable, newly displaced boys and girls in Beletweyne and Buula Burte districts and Kahda and Deynille districts of Banadir in both IDP and host communities.
Somalia continues to face a dire humanitarian situation, with an estimated 5.2 million people in need of humanitarian assistance. This is attributed to cumulative shocks, including erratic 2020 Gu season, widespread and severe seasonal flooding, desert locust infestation, socio-economic impacts of COVID-19 and protracted conflict -and flood-related displacement.
To ensure the most vulnerable are reached, HIWA will target 20,541 newly displaced women, men, girls and boys from both IDPs and host communities. This project will fill gaps in service provision where government and humanitarian support is currently insufficient. Provision of services in general and child protection services, in particular, have been significantly challenged by the limited access. As a result, HIWA aims to support the current situation in Beletweyne while preparing for the possible influx of people following the expected rain.
HIWA is proposing the following interventions to expand lifesaving child protection services to vulnerable populations in Hiraan Region. The complexity of the operating environment requires improved approaches that are tailored to each location including:
1. Child Protection interventions in the target districts the child protection intervention has been specifically designed to be integrated within HIWA’s existing protection program in Hiran region, which are aimed at strengthening community¬ based protection mechanisms and facilitating access to information and appropriate services.
• Through existing, Community Protection Committees, provide a platform to provide family tracing and reunification, alternative family care and arrangements including community-based support to identified UASC.
2.Community outreach and engagement activities to disseminate key information on Child protection, including information on the availability of services, and promote positive social norms to improve the safety and well-being of boys and girls
3. Increasing the capacity of community-based child protection mechanisms (CBCPM) to prevent and mitigate Child Protection risks through multi-layered, community based psychosocial support interventions to reduce vulnerabilities and risks to children by building a protective environment at family and community levels.
4. Psycho social support through temporary existing friendly spaces providing psycho social support. Community-based child protection committees to support community based psychosocial support interventions to reduce vulnerabilities and risks to children by building a protective environment at family and community levels.
5. In close collaboration with the Child protection sub-cluster, Safe walks and Site Safety audits will be conducted to assess the safety risk for children and population in the camps and host community and examine aspects pertaining to protection and safeguarding, to ensure the safety of men, women, boys, and girls, including people with disabilities.
6.Conduct Rapid Protection Assessments to identify the most urgent needs after new displacements
7. Provide specialized Child protection case management to at-risk boys and girls. The guiding strategy will be twofold: combining collection, analysis and dissemination of relevant information through community outreaches; and direct support of identified cases through comprehensive case management (including case management for boys and girls and psychosocial support and referral to other specialized care and ensuring appropriate follow up and care where necessary

Project Activities

1.Awareness raising activities targeting community wide on prevention messages and awareness sessions on recruitment/CAAFAG, SGBV, harmful practice, family separation, and Children’s rights.2.Community Based Child protection Groups will be trained on how best to support the identification, documentation, tracing and reunification of unaccompanied and separated children including ensuring appropriate follow up and providing alternative care where necessary. 3.Protection and support to children at risk and survivors including unaccompanied and separated through providing case management services that meets their unique needs - Identification and documentation. The survivors will receive Medical appointment, Lab test, medicine, Ambulance service /Transport cost to and from the Hospital.4.Children identified Documented by case workers and Community Based Child Protection Groups will be traced and reunified with their families through community based child support groups established.5.Facilitation of nutritional support to foster care families for the child being placed under a foster family, this goes into reducing economic burden to the foster family for absorbing extra person to feed during the family tracing process. 6.Safety walks and safety audits: to identify the safety risk for children and population in the camps and host community. HIWA will conduct safety audits in camps to ascertain protection risks that can be used to inform and advocate for actions to address those risks. 7.Child Protection Rapid assessments. HIWA will conduct rapid child protection assessments in target location to identify and document urgent child protection risks and needs faced by girls and boys, including CP related issues, as well as grave violations of human rights especially of children, in line with the Rapid Protection Assessment (RPA) mechanism
