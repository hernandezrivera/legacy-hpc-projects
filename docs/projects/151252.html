Project Summary

The project provides daily information and programming with and for all Somali communities through the IMS specialist humanitarian broadcaster Radio Ergo, airing nationally on shortwave and local FM radios. Programming targets women, men, girls and boys in all regions, and has a particular focus on gender equality and social inclusion for women, minorities, IDPs, disabled, youth, children, and other affected groups. The purpose of Radio Ergo is two-fold: to amplify the voices of local Somali communities across the country to inform the humanitarian response; and to provide access to timely reliable information to save lives and enable Somali communities to make informed decisions on key issues.

Needs

Radio is the primary source of information for the majority of Somalis across all regions of the country. Research conducted by IMS and others shows that radio is usually the only source of news in rural and remote areas. In urban locations, radio is also popular whilst TV and social media are accessed by those who can afford such connectivity. However, even with over 90 Somali FM radios across the south, no local radio has national coverage. There is also a poverty of content, with local radio and TV stations focused largely on politics, current affairs and security. There is little issue-based or programming apart from externally mastered or sponsored content. Radio Ergo is the only provider of regular humanitarian information and uses a strategy of direct shortwave broadcasts combined with rebroadcasts via a network of FM partners to reach the entire country. An independently conducted audience survey in 2017 showed that 58% of the population – roughly six out of 10 – regularly listen to Radio Ergo, and 30% listen daily. The listening figure rises to seven out of 10 in the rural areas, where the number of female listeners is also higher than in urban areas. Communities most vulnerable to drought, floods, conflict, and other seasonal crises rely on Radio Ergo shortwave broadcasts for information on disaster preparedness and mitigation, protection, health, nutrition, food security and livelihoods. Other gaps in Somali media include extremely low levels of audience participation and inclusivity in content due to a prevalence of top down approaches, and a lack of women’s and other socially excluded groups’ voices. In this project IMS addresses the gap between communities and humanitarian agencies by sharing the unique feedback Radio Ergo collects from rural listeners with the Clusters in order to drive response planning and decision-making. Clusters utilize this feedback data to identify information gaps, emerging beneficiary issues, and community perceptions.

Activities

The project has two main activity components. First, the production and broadcast across Somalia of daily humanitarian programming for radio, plus content on interlinked social media platforms, focusing on disaster risk, humanitarian and resilience topics; and second, the collecting of independent audience feedback from radio listeners in communities across the country, and sharing of data with Clusters for accountability and action. Indicative activities include the following:-Gathering programme material using Radio Ergo's network of locally based stringers in all the regions,-Development of informative programme content on disaster risk, health, nutrition, food security, protection, livelihoods, and more with input from Clusters;-Broadcast of daily one hour humanitarian programme on shortwave nationally with local FM rebroadcasts, involving news, messaging, features, interviews, discussion, debate, drama;-Dissemination of humanitarian content in appropriate formats on social media platforms,-Co-Production with local radio partners of special programming including radio dramas targeting women, youth, children, disability groups, and other marginalised communities;-Engaging with local community groups and CSOs including women's and youth groups to develop themes for further programming content;-Collecting of feedback using toll-free mobile Freedom Fone service across the regions, on local needs, views, and comments; analysing the data for regular sharing with the Clusters;-Engaging regularly with Clusters on followup messaging and information for broadcast;-Conducting focus group discussions and audience surveys to assess impact of programming.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

1. The strategy and flexibility of Radio Ergo will enable the production and broadcast of timely and relevant lifesaving information to help people to prepare for and survive crises and emergencies, including drought, floods, disease outbreaks and others (such as cholera awareness messages, vaccination campaigns, and flood warnings); and as appropriate to produce and air longer term schedules of radio programming with a durable solutions/resilience approach across Education, WASH, Nutrition, Health, Child Protection, Food Security, Shelter, and CCCM sectors. IMS-Ergo experience shows that Somali communities become most engaged in radio content when their own voices and stories are part of an interactive discussion, as opposed to simple top down messaging and advice. Such programming also provides valuable learning and insights into cultural beliefs and social or other barriers or boundaries that can guide further ongoing programming, as well as more effective interventions. Ergo’s wide local reporter network and its rural audience feedback service will be instrumental in engaging with local communities on key issues around Awareness; Prevention and/or immediate needs and treatment; Solutions to the problems, by empowering communities to address longer-term resilience building through empowering the population on their needs and prioritization. Do no harm principles guide Radio Ergo's editorial policies and programming is designed in close consultation with a wide range of partners and agencies to ensure relevance and suitability. IMS experience and learning though the Radio Ergo project shows that Somali radio listeners are hungry for information to assist them out of a trapped cycle of disaster and emergency, towards recovery and development. 2. Radio Ergo links to a broader long term media development programme for Somalia run by IMS in collaboration with partner NGO, Fojo Media Institute. This means that the project works to strengthen local Somali journalists and media to produce more effective and community-oriented programming, and has long term media and CSO partners. In addition, Radio Ergo uses government officials in disaster management authorities and ministries as expert interviewees and sources in its programming.3.Sustainabilty is built in to the project through IMS partnerships with local radios, media associations, journalist networks, and community groups. The project seeks to empower communities to speak and thereby institutionalize media content that prioritizes community needs; and to strengthen the quality and relevance of local radio programming through co-production activities and mentoring. Radio Ergo also actively raises the bar for other media to follow and expand their content to include humanitarian and resilience oriented programming.4. IMS works through its media development programme to promote freedom of expression and the right to access information in Somalia

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Information and communication is critical to enable communities to share their needs and vulnerabilities, and be able to access vital services as a result. It is also indispensable as a means of resolving challenges and gaining acceptance of proposed solutions within society. This project builds on pioneering work by Radio Ergo to open up on-air dialogue between displaced, host communities and local authorities, with a view to influencing attitudes, understanding and actions towards finding long term solutions to the problems of displacement. Radio Ergo's local reporters focus intensively on coverage of the challenges and problems facing families living in IDP camps, as well as the successes or motivational stories of displaced women and men who have embarked on new livelihoods, ventured into business, and put their children in school. Ergo's approach is to ensure that the voices of displaced men, women and young people are heard despite the fact that they have been uprooted from their homes and often live without basic services and are unaware of their basic rights. In 2018, Radio Ergo has worked with local radio partners in Mogadishu and Baidoa to develop half hour weekly programmes on the challenges and solutions of local displacement. IDPs and local residents took part in interviews and discussions on key issues, including live phone in segments to their local radio. In this project, Radio Ergo will continue to provide a humanitarian and disaster risk information service to IDPs through direct and co-produced programming, and additionally to facilitate dialogue and discussion between IDP and host communities and government over the long term solutions to displacement in particular regions.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Women, youth and children are among the least heard voices in Somalia. They also have specific informational needs, and potential to act positively based on information if they are able to access it. Radio Ergo's gender policy applies an internal quota to all programming seeking to reach a minimum of one third women's voices across the output. Production staff have received gender and social inclusion training and are encouraged to ensure that conducive conditions are created for women and girls to contribute to interviews and programmes. The gender policy also pays attention to women's portrayal in the media. Ergo reports aim to avoid representation of women as purely victims. They are asked to speak about their needs, views and decision making processes in in the family or in other contexts. Radio Ergo has special programming targeting women, covering such issues as health, nutrition, small business, education. Motivational programmes show women making a positive difference as role models in their own community. The shortwave radio broadcasts are scheduled during the afternoon to attract a female audience. Children are even more rarely heard or targeted in the Somali media. In July 2018 IMS initiated an innovative radio project with World Vision Somalia to engage children through a radio drama series on child protection. The series will air from November and includes child actors. The aim is to reach children with information about hygiene, health, nutrition, girls education, and other protection themes and to institutionalize a new young generation of Somalis able to advocate for themselves. For older youth, who are vulnerable to exclusion and despondency associated with unemployment and lack of future, IMS strategy is to give them a voice through targeted content on radio and in particular on popular social media forums including Facebook, Twitter and You Tube.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The challenges posed by lack of access clearly limit the work of humanitarian actors in certain regions. IMS is nevertheless able to deliver critical information to communities affected by armed conflict and insecurity using Radio Ergo's shortwave service. Feedback messages from listeners trapped in conflict areas demonstrate the value of the humanitarian information such communities are able to receive and the opportunity they have to get their own messages out. This project also focuses on reporting on the needs of those displaced by conflict (as well as by other causes). Ergo's coverage of the challenges facing IDPs has revealed gaps in aid provision. At the same time the radio broadcasts support and amplify the cluster interventions by informing IDPs about access to their rights and to critical services. In addition, it is important to note that the Somali media has often been used to incite conflict. A recent example is the negative use of radio and social media spaces to fan flames over the contested area of Tukaraq. In such cases Radio Ergo provides an independent and non-political source of information that may defuse tensions by focusing on the common denominator facts of the humanitarian consequences of conflict. Radio Ergo also plays a significant role in reporting on other forms of violence notably gender-based violence and rape, in order to catalyse public discussion over preventing such violence and to encourage legal redress. Ergo has worked with the GBV sub-cluster to inform women and girls about available services and legal support.
