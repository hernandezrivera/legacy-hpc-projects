Project Summary

Cox's Bazar is one of the most economically vulnerable districts in Bangladesh. The impact of the COVID-19 pandemic has plunged households that were already relying on low income or wages from the informal sector into an even greater crisis. The project will support these extremely vulnerable households to rebuild their livelihoods to enable them to meet basic household needs such as food and medicine in the short and medium term. In doing so, extremely vulnerable households, with a special focus on single mothers, are identified and supported. The vulnerable households will be selected as per predefined household vulnerability assessment criteria. The criteria will be fixed consultation with Livelihoods and Skills Development Sector and Community Consultation Meeting. Both male-female will be the participants of conditional cash grant support. Coordination will take place with local government bodies and livelihoods and skills development sector to avoid duplication of the assistance. After the final selection of the households, they will receive a one-time cash payment of BDT 25,000 on the condition that they attend a training course that provides basic skills in micro-enterprise management. Discussion will take place with the participants which income base should be (re)established and how the money can be invested wisely. During the training, the participants will draw up a business plan, among other things. Factors such as local demand, personal preferences and skills play a role in the planning. Examples of possible micro-businesses would be market gardening, poultry farming, running a market or tea stall, sewing, etc.). Although the households are free to use the money as they see fit, the conditional cash assistance or participation in the training is intended to show them that if they invest wisely, the households can cover basic daily needs not only once, but in the short and medium term. After the distribution of the conditional cash assistance, post-distribution monitoring is carried out in order to be able to prove the use of the cash assistance. With the project, host communities are provided with additional income generation opportunities with livelihoods training and resources to start income generating activities. The project will also targeted to train host community leaders in human rights and conflict mitigation that will help peaceful co-existence of host community people and Rohingya People

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Besides the refugees, there are also urgent needs in the local population. Cox's Bazar is one of the poorest regions in Bangladesh and providing for almost one million refugees puts a heavy strain on local resources. The refugee crisis has led to price increases for food and daily necessities, as well as lower salaries in the informal sector such as for day labourers engaged in agriculture. This was further exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic and associated lockdowns. In 2019, 41% of host community households were classified as moderately to highly vulnerable. In 2020, this figure increased to 51% and remained comparatively high at 52% in 2021. Added to this is the worrying global trend of rising inflation, which has been exacerbated by the outbreak of the Ukraine crisis. Food prices as well as the cost of petrol, building materials and other NFIs have risen sharply in recent months. Therefore, the local population also urgently needs support to improve their living conditions, not least to reduce negative tensions between the host community and the Rohingyas. In the host community, an average of 65 per cent of household income was spent on food in 2021. The local population earns its livelihood mainly in the informal sector as day labourers, through agriculture, livestock and fishing. Poor infrastructure, low connectivity to national markets, low access to services from financial institutions and advancing climate change and the COVID-19 pandemic have acutely worsened the overall situation and living conditions of the host community. Many households have lost their sources of income due to the restrictions imposed by the government. At the same time, the evaluation conducted in December 2021 highlighted that shelter repair and strengthening, and thus improved protection from environmental hazards, are also a priority for the local population.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Caritas Bangladesh (CB) is one of the first responders and has been working in the camps and surrounding host community following the Rohingya influx in Bangladesh since 2017. CB is trying to provide quality services both at camps and host community by effective communication and collaboration. CB regularly communicate and coordinate with camp level sector and CiC office and UNO to avoid the duplication and sattled the issue in mutual understanding. CB has been the part of JRP for Shelter, Site Improvement & NFI, WASH, Protection, Child protection, GBV from the begening. CB is the active member of PSEA Network and have PSEA Focal, Safeguarding Focal within the organization. The proposed intervention is an integrated part of overall response in Cox’s Bazar. Caritas Bangladesh is already working both in Rohingya and host community through different interventions. The intervention will implement near to the camp location and will implement by operating through camps that will reduces the relevant operational cost. As part of project implementation, Caritas will ensure that project staff and volunteers are trained in the basic standards of humanitarian assistance, particularly with regard to the "do-no-harm" principle.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Caritas concerns with the unique and immense risks of the safety and dignity of project participants, especially those who have elevated vulnerability like women, girls, boys, persons with disabilities, elderly persons, etc. PSEA and Safeguarding is the major concern areas of humanitarian response.The project team will organize separate community meetings and train ings facilitated by female staff to allow women to speak more freely. In addition, CB is working toward the goal that Protection staff will be present in every community meeting to ensure proper communication and look after power dynamics. Community Committees (CCs) will have equal representation and will be elected by the community

3.2 - Gender

CB follows comprehensive Gender Policy in operation of humanitarian responses and gender is mainstreamed here. In the proposed project, it will continue services to ensure female participation in all committees and decision making process, trained equally both female and male staff. All project staff will be trained and equipped on gender sensitive programming.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

CB also follows a comprehensive Child and Vulnerable Adult Safeguarding Policy to this humanitarian context. CB has Safeguarding Committee and one dedicated Safeguarding Focal in all offices, including Cox’s Bazar office. Staff, volunteers are trained and Safeguarding, Code of Conduct, PSEA orientation organize each year for all. Staff and volunteers must sign a safeguarding, Code of Conduct and self-declaration form during joining and reference checked before offering job. Beneficiaries are oriented on safeguarding policy through providing orientation before any service delivery, distribution. Agenda on safeguarding keeps in all meeting, training and workshops. Bengali version of Safeguarding policy and one pager distributed among the staff and host volunteers. All visitors, advisors and vendors read and sign the safeguarding form before visiting the host and camps.

3.4 - Diversity

Caritas Bangladesh seeks the active participation of all members of the refugee and host community in the design of project activities. This explicitly includes women, persons with disabilities, the elderly and other extremely vulnerable persons. In particular, Caritas Bangladesh will seek opportunities to involve women and persons with reduced mobility in active work processes. Community involvement will be intensive in identifying the concerns and needs of vulnerable groups. In the host communities, the selection of the most vulnerable households is discussed together with the local government and the affected communities and decisions are made based on clear vulnerability criteria. Households headed by women are to be given special consideration in the interventions, as they are more often affected by food insecurity, exploitation, gender-based and other forms of violence

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

For AAP Effective complain and feedback mechanism is in place through setting Opinion & Complaint box in all offices, camp facilities, distribution points and majhi blocks, dedicated Hotline and e-mail account. CB conducts PDM and beneficiaries feedback survey regularly. These feedbacks are processed, and regularly analysed by the Safeguarding committee and M&E team and shared with higher authority to action & program team to re-design the programming.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The programme will not have environmental issues but will ensure special attention to upholding the environmental sustainability. Community Committee (CC) will be formed and their members are trained on Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) approaches to mitigate future environmental risks through this activity. The community people will also be motivated to protect the soil erosion through roadside plantation, keep their surrounding clean with waste management and reduce use of single use plastic for their daily chores. Due to creation of alternative income sources, the project participants will be able to withstand weather related incidents and they will also be able to enhance their financial capacity to reduce the risk of incidents. They will also be made knowledgeable and motivated not to cut trees indiscriminately and to plant trees at their homesteads.
