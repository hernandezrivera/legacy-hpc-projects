Project Summary

The project aims to ensure that approximately 11, 500 most vulnerable and food insecure households (57,500 people) are supported to meet their basic food and non-food needs and to protect their livelihoods from multiple shocks. This will be done through emergency cash-based assistance combined with livelihood strengthening. The targets are broken down by intervention as follows:
cash transfers for 6 months - 1,000 HHs; Horticulture inputs – 6500HHs; Seeds and fertilizers – 10,000 HHs; Small livestock - 2000HHs; Additional trainings on climate smart agriculture - 2,000 HHs.

1 - Activities

- Cash transfers to 1,000 most food insecure HHs during the peak lean seasons ( Jan- March and Oct - Dec 2021)- Provide horticulture seeds to 6,500HH ( 1,300 HH per district to enhance the nutritional status of acutely food insecure population). The package comprises leaf vegetables (Covo, tsunga etc), legumes (peas, sugar beans etc), root vegetables (carrots, beetroots etc) and a variety of available herbs.- Provide drought-tolerant seeds for cereals and fertilizers 10,000HH, that is, 2,000 HHs per district (1,900 for seeds + 100 contact farmers) having access to minimum 0.5 hactares of arable land. The package includes Sorghum/Maize, Cowpeas and Compound D. The distribution will be done in August to October 2021.- Provide support - transport and allowances to training, extension and advisory services to enable them to support farmers focusing on good agriculture practices with emphasis on climate-smart agriculture.- Provide 10,000HHs with equipment and training for crop pest management, post-harvest handling, storage and processing. - Restocking of small livestock production through distribution of chickens and/or goats to 1,900HHs. Trainings and distributions to be conducted in May-July 2021.- There will be overlaps in targeting for some interventions with somehouseholds benefiting from more than one intervention hence the overall target of 11,500HH.

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

Accountability to Affected populations stands at the core of Christian Aid's work. Throughout this response we will ensure that communities can provide input, suggestions, feedback and complaints through formal and informal feedback and complaints handling mechanisms. Christian Ai and itspartners will ensure follow up on feedback, close the feedback loop and track feedback. Information will also be shared in the form of banners, written in the local language with contextually appropriate pictures, on who is providing assistance, the principles we adhere to, the expected behaviour of staff, the assistance that people will receive and how to give feedback. All feedback and complaints, that is received will be recorded by the partner in a database and resolved or referred where appropriate. Multiple feedback mechanisms will be established – based on what the affected community want. Africa AHEAD will keep a log of all feedback and complaints received and any resolution actions taken using a feedback tracker which will be shared with CA. Safeguarding: The prevailing COVID-19 has increased safeguarding risks in vulnerable communities including increased cases GBV cases. CA has conducted SG training for its staff , partners and volunteers. ALL staff have signed CoCs and will ensure that all volunteers sign against a coontextualized CoC to ensure adequate awreness of expected code of behaviour. COMPASS, our digital feedback system has an inbuilt escalation feature for senstive complaints including SG, fraud, corruption and financial crime which will be sent to relevant staff for prompt action. CA will also roll out contextualized/translated IEC materials to increase awareness on expected staff behaviour including what constitutes abuse or breach of expected behaviour. Emphasis will be placed on the fact that giving feedback or raising a complaint will not affect their eligibility for receiving aid and none retaliation by staff, safety of beneficiaries will be assured as way of building trust.CA's digital feedback system will capture real time feedback dissagregated by gender, age and disability. The system analyses feedback and generates dashbaords and reports that will be dissagregated in gender, age and disability enabling learning and adaptation of the response in real time. The feedback dashboards will shared with staff and partners on a regular basis to ensure timely action in adaptation is taken where applicable. All data collected for distributions will be age and gender seggregated. Accountability to affected populations- CA will use the verbal feedback form to share critical project related information including: Rights and entitlements, peoject timelines, selection criteria, activities and expected staff. Expected staff behaviour including prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) will be shared using already developed, IEC materials translated into local language. This will also include contacts and how to report breach of code of conduct by staff, partner and volunteers. CA will also review its referral maps to better coordinate with other stakeholders on issues that are outside the scope of the project including GBV for timely referral and management of reports registered.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

CA and its partners are committed to the protection of the environment by integrating environmental considerations into the planning and implementation of all development initiatives, regardless of their sector of focus. Effects of social and environmental vulnerabilities will be reduced through building capacity of communities and institutions to prepare and respond to disasters through effective civil protection capacities from government. The response will identify possible remedies to environmental rehabilitation and will ensure that no negative environmental impacts will emerge due to the project interventions The project will promotes care and awareness of environmental issues. Awareness campaigns will be given on environmental conservation, use of fuel-efficient stoves, solar power for drawing water and food preservation and use of good agricultural practices.
