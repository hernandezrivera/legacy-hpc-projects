Project Summary

Since the beginning of its presence in the Country, in 2006, Armadilla has worked in the field of health assistance, with a specific focus on children and youngsters with disabilities. The Syrian crisis in March 2011, has dramatically worsened the situation, especially for the displaced people that are leaving behind the little they owned. Armadilla has been supporting the work of both the local partner Zahret Al Madan (ZAM) in implementing rehabilitation activities for children with disabilities (both physical and cognitive), through the provision of specific training to the local health workers with the aim of increasing their professional skills and capacity of attendance. The Zahret Al Madan Centre, originally located in Hajar al Aswaad, indeed was born with the specific task of assisting vulnerable families, in which there is a presence of minors with disability both physical and/or cognitive. The main goal of Armadilla and Zahret Al Madan is the implementation of rehabilitation paths with the aim of developing the skills of minors with disabilities and favouring their social inclusion through a wide range of activities. Due to the conflict, the further lack of resources of the families of coming from Hajar Al Aswad and Midan (host community) does not allow them in access quality services for their relatives. The reinforcement of the technical skills of volunteers and workers of the ZAM association, as well as of the other associations newly involved in the activities, is the first answer to the increasing needs of the population of Damascus and Rural Damascus. The project foresees the strengthening of capacity of ZAM volunteers and workers in the field of assistance to minors with disabilities, as well as the training of other 4 associations of operating in Damascus and Rural Damascus in the field of assistance and care of children and youngsters with disability. Armadilla and Zahret Al Madan has been working since last October in identifying the other 4 associations, among a range of the ones working with minors with disabilities; the NGOs have been identified, but process of MoU drafting is still ongoing. Beneficiaries of the activities will be both volunteers and workers of the associations, as well as children and youngsters with disabilities, through the development and implementation of individual assistance plans.
Moreover, a component of psychosocial assistance addressing relatives and family of minors with disability, will be realized in order to support the wellbeing of the families, as well as the development of community activities with the aim of strengthening the awareness around the people with disability theme.
The project is integrated with activities implemented by Armadilla in Damascus and Rural Damascus under the umbrella of health topics, which purpose is the support of specialised health centres in delivering quality assistance to patients.

4 - Needs

In Damascus and rural Damascus areas, such as the ones proposed in the present project, despite the reduction of hostilities civilians are suffering the effects of over seven years of conflict, starting from the disintegration of community structures, safety nets and high levels of trauma and psychological distress. All the beneficiaries of the proposed intervention should be considered extremely vulnerable, due to their conditions of disability. The initial conditions of poverty, low income, scarce access to basic services have been further aggravated by the negative impact of the conflict, especially as a consequence of displacement from their home places (more than 90% of targeted beneficiaries come from the areas of Hajar Al Aswad, Yarmouk camp, Sbeneh, Set Zeinab, Babila, Yalda). All the families subject to the intervention see the presence of one or more minors with disability, and their lack of resources, aggravate the physical and cognitive status of the minors, as well as the emotional and psychological burden of the families, especially for the women who traditionally are the ones in charge of the wellness of children and youngsters, as well as of elderly. It should be noted that as per the survey conducted by Armadilla and ZAM, 74% of the families interested by this proposal are led by breadwinner women, due to the absence of husbands as a consequence of the conflict. The proposed intervention is part of the multi-year project realized by Armadilla and ZAM, where children and youngsters with disability are assisted, as well as their families, through an intensive therapeutic programme delivered by the ZAM workers and volunteers. According to the continuous analysis realized by the I-NGO with the local partner and the Ministry of Social Affairs, there is a continuous need to increase the capacity of the workers, as well as the quantity of volunteers and workers in order to match the increased quantity of beneficiaries which see the ZAM Centre as the most

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will be implemented in three locations: 1) Midan, at the Zahret Al Madan (ZAM) Centre. Since more than 10 years, the Centre work in the field of assistance to minors with disability, in strict collaboration with Armadilla and the CPR of the Hospital Ibn-Al Nafis of Damascus (partner of Armadilla in health sector projects). Project activities will be implemented by Armadilla and ZAM staff. ZAM volunteers will be coordinated by specialized health workers (physical therapists, pediatrics, physiatrists) of the CPR. Recreational days involving both minors with disabilities and peers without disabilities will be coordinated by volunteers of ZAM and the other NGOs involved in the project. 2) Al Tall (Rural Damascus), in which Zahret Al Madan staff and volunteers on bi-weekly basis will perform activities based on the experience of the ZAM Centre, delivering assistance to children with disabilities and, at the same time, training local associations volunteers and relatives of children with disabilities.3) Yarmouk Camp, where a team composed by professionals (psychologist, doctor, nurses) and ZAM workers and volunteers will establish a consultant point for pregnant women.List of activities to be implemented:Therapeutic (cognitive and physical) paths for children with disabilities, including assessment of their conditions held in ZAM Centre;PSS sessions addressing relatives and families of minors with disabilities;Training for volunteers and workers of ZAM and other local associations located in Al Tall, as per the learning by doing approach.Consultation for mothers of children with disabilities and pregnant women at the established assistance point in Yarmouk camp.Recreational days for children with disabilities and children (peers activities).Supply of equipment (assistive aids and educational toys for children with disabilities) to the associations. Awareness community sessions on children with disability topics.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Access to the Area of the activities The access to the area of activities, both in Damascus and Rural Damascus, does not seem problematic nowadays. However, the Organisation will be in continuous communication with the relevant security actors in order to organize activities and movements only after consultation with them. Monitoring activities will be realized previously informing the Ministry of Social Affairs (MoSA), who has specifically approved the activities of the present project.Lack of permission to enter in Syria for expatriate staff: while there are no problems at all in obtaining visa for professionals blem, there is the possibility that some Armadilla’s management staff may be face difficulties in obtaining the visas to come in the Country (e.g. for internal monitoring work). Visas issue will be tackled by advocating with the MoSA and MoH in supporting the Organisation. This method worked just fine in recent years. However, training sessions may be delivered by remote methodology, and activities on the field will be guaranteed by the Local partner and local staff of the Organisation. Availability of goods (assistive aids and toys for children disabilities): Goods will be purchased on the local market, looking for the best vendor in terms of economy and capacity of guarantee the provision of the service. A back-up list of potential suppliers to contact will be drafted, in order to avoid any delay. In case there will be the need to purchase items abroad, the MoSA will facilitate the import of goods.
