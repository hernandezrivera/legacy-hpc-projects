Project Summary

The project aims to distribute bread daily to about 4,800 displaced families in more than 10 camps in Maarat Tamsrin, and Harim, especially the newly displaced families who have been displaced from the Maarat al-Numan countryside, Saraqib and the surrounding areas and the western countryside of Aleppo.
The flour is provided free of charge by the Turkish Religion Endowment, while Al Deya Association secures the operating cost of baking flour and distributing free bread to beneficiaries at a package price for each family, taking into account the most vulnerable groups, such as people with special needs, the elderly, and families headed by a woman or child. Each ton of flour produces 1150 packages of bread weight (1 kg), containing (10 loaves per package).
Around 4,800 bread packages will be distributed daily to 4,800 displaced families on weekdays for one year, except Friday. The project started on 1/2/22020 and will end on 01/31/2021.
The main objectives of the project:
1- Improving the food security situation for people who are food insecure in northwestern Syria.
2- Providing life-saving food assistance and preserving the lives of the internally displaced in the camps.
3- By providing the assistance mentioned above, IDPs will be less vulnerable to the negative coping mechanisms that result from insecurity about the near future, income and social instability.

4 - Needs

Based on the results of the household assessment nationwide, it is estimated that 7.9 million people in Syria are food insecure and an additional 1.9 million people are at risk of food insecurity. About70percent of the households assessed reported that food represented more than 65 percent of their expenditures. Over 37 per cent of food-insecure households reported outstanding debt and 57 per cent of food-insecure people adopted negative coping strategies. Among the displaced, more than 760,889 people living in last resort camps are a particular concern for the sector as they have limited access to a varied diet or income opportunities. With at least 69 per person living under $1.90 a day, more than twice the amount in 2011, and with a cumulative GDP loss of $ 226 billion between 2011 and 2016, the relationship between poverty and food security remains particularly strong . Wheat production is estimated at 2.2 million tons, which is still below the pre-crisis level of 4.1 million tons (2002-2011) and local requirements(food use)of 3.4 million metric tons. Humanitarian needs in Syria are expected to remain high in 2020, and multiple measures are needed to ensure that all four pillars of food security related to access, availability, use, and stability of food are adequately addressed through life saving, sustainable life, appropriate resilience, and early recovery interventions.On May 1 1 2020, the Al-Diaa Humanitarian Society team conducted a needs assessment for 4,800 highness(22,850 individuals)in the Ma`rat Misr and Harem camps. According to needs estimates: 76% of the breadwinners are male while 12% of the breadwinners are female, of whom 81% are widows. 75% of His Royal Highnesses were displaced in less than 4 months. 92% of Excellencies stated that their most important need is bread, while2% say the food basket is a priority for them. 99% of the households surveyed stated that they needed to buy bread per day, and the bread package cost between 250 to 400 SYP.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: No more IDPs displaced Danger: An increase in displacement waves. Mitigation: updating beneficiary lists regularly and increasing the amount of bread. Assumption: The distribution runs smoothly. Danger: A conflict between the displaced and the host community, because the host community will not receive humanitarian assistance. Mitigation: Explain clearly the mechanism for selecting a list of beneficiaries, and we will rely on local authorities and civil society leaders to explain the selection criteria and resolve any dispute. Provide written, verbal and clear information such as banner ads, patron cards, etc. to inform people who are eligible for assistance. Assumption: The price of fuel is stable. The risk: high fuel prices due to the deteriorating security. Mitigation: 1- Sign a contract with the bakery based on fixed prices throughout the project, and ask the bakery to purchase and store fuel for this project. 2- Reducing the number of baking packages produced from flour or reducing the weight of baking packages to be appropriate to compensate for the loss resulting from high fuel prices. Assumption: Beneficiaries will use bread as intended. The Risk: Beneficiaries who sell bread. Mitigation: application of the distribution criteria (bread packaging) for each family. Assumption: The security situation is stable at project sites. Danger: The area is vulnerable to a security or military crisis. Mitigation: The area in which we will work on the project is considered safe and did not encounter any major security problems. However, we will closely monitor the situation to secure our team and the materials planned for distribution. In the event of emerging security problems, the location of the operation will be changed after consulting the donor. 6- Assumption: Flour is available. Danger: Lack of Florida. Dilution: flour storage in our warehouse. The Turkish Religious Endowment is provided through the cross-border portal if suspended, to find local source
