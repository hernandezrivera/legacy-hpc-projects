Project Summary

The project aims to provide lifesaving and life-sustaining coordinated, equitable health services to address the dire need for health services for all age and sex groups; boys, girls, men, and women, with attention to people with special needs; physical disability, mental disorders, and psychosocial problems, without any sexual discrimination, among IDPs and host communities in northern Syria.
SEMA will prioritize the reduction of morbidity and mortality through improving the access to quality health care (primary and secondary, specialized services, Cholera response, community health, and risk communication) in 26 health facilities.
SEMA will provide the following services at the primary healthcare level: RH services (antenatal care, post-natal care, family planning, normal deliveries, gynecological consultations, and clinical management of rape), Integrated management for child illness, EPI, communicable and non-communicable diseases, Nutrition, MHPSS, health awareness and basic laboratory and radiology services.
Services at the secondary healthcare level will include gynecologic surgeries and cesarean sections, trauma management, major surgeries, hospitalization services, blood transfusion, hemodialysis, and ICUs, in addition to Cholera response in 4 CTCs in include (Case Management for children and adults, providing ORS) and providing PPE/IPC at facilities.
This program also includes MHPSS, MH gap intervention, and focused-non-specialized services by PSWs within SEMA health facilities, also at the community levels.
SEMA's planned activities will meet specific needs to enable access for people with disabilities to health services under their specific situation.

SEMA will ensure privacy, equity, and easy access to the services as there will be women waiting for areas within the hospital and having available female staff to deal with women and children smoothly.
SEMA will provide quantitative and qualitative reports that include disaggregated information about cases/age, sex, gender, and disability. including reporting to DHIS.
SEMA will ensure following the protocols and guidelines according to WHO eight pillars. SEMA health facilities will be provided with the required PPEs to keep the frontline staff safe and keep the health facilities running as well. Furthermore, IPC measures and tools will be secured and followed to ensure adherence to the WHO standards and guidelines and prevent the health facility from being a potential infection source.

4 - Implementation of activities

SEMA will ensure providing integrated health services of primary, secondary, and specialized health services with nutrition and protection ones. This will be implemented in all levels of the supported health facilities; 26 health facilities; 3 general hospitals, 4 CTCs, 5 CemONC facilities, 12 PHCs (3 are mobile) and two dialysis centers. All supported health facilities will provide free of charge preventive and curative services. SEMA will ensure providing primary health care services according to the WHO essential Primary Health Care Package which includes six pillars: child health, reproductive health, communicable disease, NCD, MHPSS and nutrition. While in secondary health care services including specialized reproductive health care services BEmONC and CEmONC services; the services will be provided through ER, OPD, IPD, and departments in addition to radiology and laboratory. Moreover, neonatal, vaccination, reproductive health care including care during pregnancy and childbirth, and conducting normal and C-section deliveries will be provided in the maternity and RH facilities. CTCs will be supported by SEMA to provide case management of Cholera cases, according to WHO. SEMA-targeted facilities will receive support in the form of paying staff salaries (medical and non-medical), monthly operational costs, provision of pharmaceuticals, and other medical and non-medical commodities.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project will mainstream protection principles which include making sure that all beneficiaries have purposeful access to services prioritizing their safety and dignity, ensuring non-discrimination and 'do-no-harm' in the delivery of the services, pursuing a meaningful engagement of the stakeholders, and ensure that confidential reporting mechanisms are in place to address any potential abuse.This project is designed to boost the dignity and living conditions of the targeted beneficiaries including the most vulnerable populations.The following consideration will be taken into account in all activities:Having a trained and qualified team that is committed to the code of conduct and PSEA principles. The team works on not to violate these principles knowing that there are disciplinary actions in place.Care is taken to ensure that the specific requirements of the beneficiaries are taken into account, especially for people and children with disabilities.SEMA will hire localized and gender-balanced staff, and a supervision mechanism will be put in place to enhance the quality of services.SEMA will actively look to reach children who are at-risk through the identification of the IDP population located in areas such as collective centers, including children with disabilities.The project will advocate an indiscriminate approach, equality, and protection against gender-based violence. The project tries to avoid exposing beneficiaries to further physical and psychological harm


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SEMA ensures the privacy of the beneficiaries and vulnerable groups through adjusting and keeping the data of the beneficiaries secured, being responsible for the principle “Do-no-Harm” to the beneficiaries, and keeping their dignity. There is effective feedback and complaint mechanism, and beneficiaries are made aware of how to access it. This mechanism includes developed practices and channels for reporting Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. The concept of PSEA is also considered in the awareness-raising topics offered for children and adults to raise their awareness on their rights and safety and the safety measure to report such misconduct. IEC materials, banners, and posters will be available in all facilities and distribution points. Staff will be oriented in the contracting stage on SEA issues and will sign the CoC. Refresher training for the staff will be conducted on a regular basis. All complaints will be taken into consideration and will be investigated following SEMA’s investigation policies, Disciplinary measures will be taken against any breach of CoC.
