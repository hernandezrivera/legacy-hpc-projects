Project Summary

The overall estimated Sector PIN for 2022 – of food insecure and those at risk of food insecurity – is estimated at 13.9 million, with 12 million facing acute food insecurity and 1.9 million people at risk of sliding into food insecurity. The acute food insecure figure includes 1.9 million people living in camps and deemed to be 100 percent food insecure. The water crisis in Syria has resulted in a deterioration of the food security situation for thousands of people, most notably farmers – further reducing primary food production. The continuity of socioeconomic difficulties has driven more people into food insecurity. The 2020/2021 rainfall season was significantly poor and erratic, in terms of temporal and spatial distribution, combined with a significant delay in the onset of rainfall. This was further worsened by several successive seasonal dry spells and an early cessation of rainfall, with the last rains being received in mid-April 2021. This significantly affected critical crop growth and development, especially grain filling. The seasonal constraints were also coupled with significant shortages and high cost of agriculture inputs, including diesel fuel, which resulted in limited access to water and ability to provide supplementary irrigation.
In this context, FAO has designed a project to assist 228,571 vulnerable crisis-affected food insecure farming households (equivalent to 1,371,429 people) in Syria. The objective of the proposed action is to improve access to staple foods for vulnerable farmers through increased cereal and pulse production.
The project outputs include:
Output 1: Access to agricultural inputs to facilitate production of cereals, pulses and vegetables for 225,571 households increased.
The response package under this output will be in line with sector recommendations for provision of 200 kg of wheat/barley seeds (adequate to plant 1 ha), 15kg NPK 10:10:10 (adequate for 0.5 ha). Or 25 to 30 kg of various legume seeds (adequate to plant approximately 0.4 0.5 ha) or 15-20 kg per dunum for vegetable will be provided per household.
Output 2: Skills for cereal and pulses production of 228,571 targeted households enhanced.
This output aims at building the capacity of the 228,571 households that will benefit from cereal and pulses production inputs. Beneficiaries will trains to support use of the inputs and the training programme will be built on the following FAO concepts: “Save & Grow”,

4 - Needs

Overall, the Food Security Sector (FSS) estimates that 12 million people in Syria are facing acute food insecurity. The FSS provided record levels of assistance in Syria in 2021, the outlook for the year ahead remains dire since well over 50% of Syrians remain food insecure. Significant challenges continue to impact local food production in Syria and this pillar remains under-funded. Scaling up local food production, improved food systems and agriculture-based livelihoods, especially in rural areas, will be fundamental to sustainably reduce the humanitarian caseload and high food insecurity in Syria. The water crisis and economic difficulties have had a particularly devastating impact on all Syrian governorates. Furthermore, the harvesting season there is still a significant gap between available wheat and the national consumption needs. The macroeconomic conditions show no signs of improvement and it is likely that high food and agricultural input prices will persist. This will drive more people into food insecurity in the coming months.Based on the special report published by FAO in December 2021, there is an anticipated shortage of wheat seed for the upcoming season. Farmers continue to experience challenges in accessing high quality and locally adapted seeds. As a result, the quality of seeds continues to degenerate, affecting production and productivity.
