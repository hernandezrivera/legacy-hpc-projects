Project Summary

The now decade-long Boko Haram insurgency has exacerbated an already fragile socio-economic and livelihood system in North East Nigeria, with Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states the most affected. The conflict has negatively affected livelihoods, social cohesion, human security, the natural environment, and the overall growth prospects of people in the region. The result has been the loss of lives, high levels of food insecurity and malnutrition, the depletion of livelihoods and high levels of displacement.
Findings of the October 2019 Cadre Harmonisé analysis highlight that, between October and December 2019, approximately three million people in the BAY states will face acute levels of food insecurity (Phase 3 and above) and will be in urgent need of assistance.
The interventions will involve livestock restocking, value chain development and veterinary support. Vulnerable households will be targeted with bulls, ewes/rams, goats or poultry interventions. Targeted populations will include IDPs, returnees and agro-pastoralist communities with minimal assets. Through this intervention, FAO will build the capacity of state veterinary services and community-based animal health workers in the region. These groups will provide animal health services to project beneficiaries and to other vulnerable livestock owners.
The crisis has also severely affected households who depend on the livestock value chain and has had a significant impact on the availability of highly nutritious food such as meat, milk and eggs. Many IDPs whose livelihoods relied solely on animal production have had their animals rustled or killed. There is, therefore, a crucial need to restock key income generating livestock assets such as goats, rams, sheep, bulls and poultry. This will enable households, particularly women and youth IDPs, to revive and boost their livestock production. This intervention is also aimed at supporting the targeted beneficiaries especially youth and women with bulls as an alternative source of livelihood opportunities and income generation from the sale of bulls after fattening.

1 - Needs

Agriculture remains the key livelihood activity of the affected population, with approximately 80 percent engaged in agriculture-related livelihoods to meet their basic needs. In support of the government’s recovery plan, FAO has been supporting rural livelihoods in agriculture and pastoralism as a means by which to restore and protect vulnerable livelihoods. However, substantial gaps remain in the assistance needed to protect livelihoods and build the resilience of those currently reliant on livestock as a major source of livelihood. Driven by increased consumer demand, the livestock sector has been growing at a rate of 12.7% , higher than the agricultural growth rate of 6.8%. The sub-sector is vital to the socio-economic development and key for nutritional security, providing 36.5% of the total protein intake of Nigerians (ASL 2050 Report, 2019). The majority of the Nigerian livestock owners are the rural poor, and a significant proportion of the urban poor as well. In many communities, women have specific family responsibilities and ownership of livestock and access to livestock products. As such, targeting this households with livestock assistance offers a unique pathway to improve their income and nutrition, particularly women. In this way, livestock provides increased economic stability to farm households, acting as a cash buffer (small stock), a capital reserve (large animals) and as a hedge against inflation. In mixed farming systems, livestock reduce risk through diversification of production and income sources and thereby provide safety net to deal with seasonal crop failures and other natural calamities.

2 - Activities

FAO will collaborate with relevant government institutions, the state ministry of agriculture and livestock, Veterinary services and community based animal health workers to support 20000 households . The beneficiaries will include a minimum of 35% inclusion of women and at least a 60% inclusion of youth, throughout all activities. Marginalized groups including people will disabilities will be identified and targeted through community base approach. Their specific needs in terms of training, resources, communication with communities and all aspects related to their successful participation in the project will be considered. The interventions will involve livestock restocking, value chain development and veterinary support. Vulnerable households will be targeted with bulls, ewes/rams, goats or poultry interventions. Targeted populations will include IDPs, returnees and agro-pastoralist communities with minimal assets. Through this intervention, FAO will build the capacity of state veterinary services and community-based animal health workers in the region. These groups will provide animal health services to project beneficiaries and to other vulnerable livestock owners. The specific activities includes; • Provision of bulls, rams and ewes, goats, poultry kits and feed to households with vulnerable women and youth. • Delivery of training to beneficiaries on good livestock husbandry, livestock management practices and animal feed production through Agro-Pastoral Field Schools. • Vaccination and medication of livestock in the BAY states against endemic animal diseases. • Training and equipping community-based animal health workers and supporting the rehabilitation of veterinary diagnostic labs, abattoirs and slaughterhouses. • Training farmers in handling and processing animal products for value addition and to promote food safety and animal welfare. • Provision of support to youth cooperatives toward development of fodder production livelihoods FAO has developed a rigid protection sensitive strategy in the northeast, based on a do no harm, approach, boosting meaningful access and integrating principles of the accountability of affected populations and communications with communities to enhance participation. The goal of FAO’s approach is to boost participation in the project cycle, curb SEA and GBV. FAO implements feedback mechanisms including toll free numbers, suggestion boxes, focus group discussions. In the M&E of the project, FAO will undertake yield assessments & PDM which will collect beneficiary feedback concerning acceptability, timeliness, transparency of the selection process etc. Women, men and youth’s feedback will be deliberately targeted as part of the above processes, in consultation with local authorities and interest groups such as women and youth cooperatives. FAO undertook a broad scale protection risk analysis to understand the risks of specific groups across agricultural value chains in the northeast and is well placed to design projects which consider the specific risks facing at-risk groups. FAO has field experts including female field assistants and staff who speak the local languages and are frequently deployed during trainings, distributions, monitoring and planning missions.To greater understand the protection risks faced by HHs, FAO will undertake a protection risk assessment prior to the implementation of the activity. The Organization will also reinforce training to staff on GBV, PSEA, AAP and CwC. By adopting a CBA, ensuring beneficiary participation in decision-making, and involving local and traditional authorities, the project will promote accountability, capacity building & empowerment. Five percent of the budget will be allocated for the recruitment of a protection expert, training of stakeholders communication and advocacy.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

FAO will ensure that feedback mechanisms such as toll free numbers, complaint boxes, complaint desks are available throughout the life cycle of the project and that vulnerable community members have access to these mechanisms. The organization will also ensure that the views and experiences of the most vulnerable are integrated in post-activity monitoring and reporting. FAO will engage a wide cross section of stakeholders to identify the most-vulnerable and those least likely to utilize feedback mechanisms . These individuals will be identified and reached in person where possible and their specific requirements and needs will be taken into account in project design, implementation and appraisal.
