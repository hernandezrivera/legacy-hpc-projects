Project Summary

Climatic extremes, which have in the recent past been characterized by unprecedented flooding, coupled with increasing waves of inter and intra communal violence, bureaucratic impediments including instances of youth aggression towards humanitarian actors, and economic implications of the COVID 19 pandemic that have continued to negatively impact vulnerable boys, girls, men and women, including people with disabilities in Unity, Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Central Equatoria among other States in the country. These have had consequent implications on increased livestock disease outbreaks and mortalities in addition to submerging of agricultural land contributing to unprecedented food insecurity levels, projected to further increase in 2023 with at least 7.76 million people projected to face severe food insecurity by April –July 2023 (IPC November 2022).
Many vulnerable host, returnee and displaced families, especially women, widowed women, child headed households, the elderly, boys and girls including people with disabilities (who have impaired access to livelihoods); solely depend on livestock, fishing, vegetable and crop farming among other coping strategies. VSF Suisse 27 years of experience in the targeted states, presence on the ground and proven capacity will deliver high quality programming by leveraging on strong grass root networks, local knowledge, understanding of seasonal and location specific contexts.
Proposed actions will target 187,000 beneficiaries (30000 men, 30000 women, 63500 girls, 63500 boys) who shall include elderly and people living with disabilities. The action will be aimed at ensuring cost efficiency through complementarities by linkages with FAO pipeline for livestock vaccines prepositioning and agricultural livelihood kits - crops, vegetables and fishing kits in order to promote preparedness, household resilience and self-reliance through protection of livelihood opportunities.

Proposed actions are in line and correspond with the FSL Cluster 2023 HRP Cluster Objectives Supporting CO2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock; and Supporting CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks & contextual changes. Proposed actions will establish a nexus between humanitarian response, peace building (conflict sensitivity) and resilience programming. By supporting famine clusters in the catchment areas of health and nutrition facilities and in communities with or without functional boreholes; in addition to the establishment of demonstration sites for vegetable gardening support at nutrition facilities coupled with agronomic training and on-going extension advice and guidance at both main Season farming Response and Dry Season Response that shall include conduct nutrition sensitive feeding and cooking practices.
The project will ensure a multi-sectoral approach through inter-cluster synergies, quality programming, cost efficiency in addition to mainstreaming cross-cutting programmatic approaches that include application of Accountability to Affected Populations principles, protection including Gender Based Violence mitigation measures, Do No Harm, conflict context analysis, gender equity, HIV/AIDS awareness and prevention, environmental conservation and sustainability.

1 - Needs

Climatic extremes, which have in the recent past been characterized by unprecedented flooding, coupled with increasing waves of inter and intra communal violence, bureaucratic impediments including instances of youth aggression towards humanitarian actors, and economic implications of the COVID 19 pandemic have continued to negatively impact vulnerable boys, girls, men and women, including people with disabilities in Unity, Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Central Equatoria among other States in the country (Flood Situation Reports 2022; IPC November 2022). Flood waters have persisted with a projected above normal rainfall patterns in 2022 (2022 Global Climate Risk Index), which have consequently led to submerging of farming lands in addition to limited grazing land and pastures; with household resorting to distressed sale of livestock for fear of disease outbreaks and decimation (Unity and Northern Bahr el Ghazal recorded highest losses in crop and livestock production (FSNMS 2022; IPC November 2022). Flooding has in addition contributed towards prolonged congregation of livestock in marsh lands, a key precursor for livestock disease outbreaks, drowning and mortalities. 2022 VSF livestock flood assessments have indicated at least 220,000 heads of livestock have died between May and December 2022 in Unity and Jonglei and increased livestock disease incidences like Contagious Bovine Pleuropneumonia, Black Quarter, Foot‑and-Mouth Disease, foot rot, increased internal parasites, anthrax, Trypanosomiasis, haemorrhagic septicaemia among others) (VSF Livestock Disease Investigation Reports 2022; REACH Updates 2022). With disrupted flood induced traditional migration routes, agro-pastoral communities have resorted into changing grazing areas, which has further elicited resource based tensions and increased risks to cattle raids and looting including cross border clashes with migrant pastoralists in northern Bahr el Ghazal and northern Unity states (OCHA, 2022 Updates). Above events, have been further contributed to unprecedented food insecurity levels, projected to further increase in 2023 (at least 7.76 million people are projected to face severe food insecurity by April –July 2023) (IPC November, 2022). Many vulnerable host, returnee and displaced families, especially women, widowed women, child headed households, the elderly, boys and girls including people with disabilities (who have impaired access to livelihoods) solely depend on livestock, fishing, vegetable and crop farming among other coping strategies. VSF Suisse 27 years of experience in the targeted states, presence on the ground and proven capacity will deliver high quality programming by leveraging on strong grass root networks, local knowledge, understanding of seasonal and location specific contexts. The project will be aimed at ensuring cost efficiency through complementarities by linkages with FAO pipeline for livestock vaccines prepositioning and agricultural livelihood kits - crops, vegetables and fishing kits and veterinary supplies in order to promote preparedness, household resilience and self-reliance through protection of livelihood opportunities. The project will ensure a multi-sectoral approach through inter-cluster synergies, quality programming, cost efficiency in addition to mainstreaming cross-cutting programmatic approaches that include application of Accountability to Affected Populations principles, protection including Gender Based Violence mitigation measures, Do No Harm, conflict context analysis, gender equity, HIV/AIDS awareness and prevention, environmental conservation and sustainability.

2 - Project activities

• Prepositioning, transport and distribution of crops, fishing kits and vegetable kits among187,000 beneficiaries (11,000 HHs) – comprised of 30000 men, 30000 women, 63500 girls, 63500 boys) who shall include elderly and people living with disabilities.• Set up at least 60 vegetable demonstration plots/ kitchen garden demonstration sites at nutrition facilities and conduct nutrition sensitive feeding and cooking practices and agronomic trainings. • Conduct agronomic trainings and postharvest preservation techniques training for 11,000 household heads while observing COVID 19 mitigation measures in line with Ministry of Health/ WHO Guidelines • Training/ Refresher training for 400 Community Based Animal Health Workers/ Stock Persons (100 female, 300 male) • Conduct refresher training of 40 Stock Persons and Animal Health Auxilliaries on cold chain management and monitoring to support safe storage of veterinary vaccines/ integrity. • Install and maintain 20 solar vaccine fridges for livestock veterinary vaccine handling in Central Equatoria, Unity, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Jonglei. • Carry out livestock disease investigation outbreaks in collaboration with line ministries, FAO among other key stakeholders. • Carry out livestock treatment and vaccination campaigns targeting 1,000,000 heads of livestock against endemic livestock diseases in Unity, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Central Equatoria and Jonglei. • Support 5000 households through input trade fairs using locally sourced seed inputs in Northern Bahr el Ghazal.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Most vulnerable members who shall include people with special needs, illiterate, women and girls will have an equal opportunity in active participation throughout the project cycle. These will be engaged and be part of implementation committees including ensuring assistance is offered including priority given to people with disabilities who cannot walk and ensure services are provided at their residences or ensuring activities are implemented close to their homesteads.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

VSF Suisse will adopt a consultative project implementation approach; by including equitable representation of male and female, youth, boys and girls, women and men (including disabled and elderly, illiterate); local community leaders, youth and women representatives among other relevant authorities in decision making as well and also ensuring their full participation in project planning, selection, verification, implementation, monitoring/evaluation. VSF Suisse field teams will also conduct weekly/monthly project management meetings to review and consider any issues emerging that may impact on the project implementation, in addition to mainstreaming of cross cutting issues such as gender, disability inclusion, protection, HIV/AIDS and accountability etc. Activities including livelihood kits distributions and animal health campaigns etc will be conducted in presence of beneficiary trustees such as local community leaders, RRC and youth/ women representatives and facilitated by representatives of specific beneficiary groups (eg., those with disabilities who will be supported); including inviting the Cluster in implementation and monitoring. Beneficiary distribution lists will be developed, verified and endorsed by beneficiary representatives and filed for records / references. Men, women, boys and girls are consulted jointly and separately including making arrangements to engage people with special needs such as disabled. Sufficient information on the project, including sharing relevant documents with the local leadership, local State/County ministries will be shared to enable them understand and influence key decisions in the project including putting in place a feedback and complaints desk at Activity/ distribution sites and appropriate contacts.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

In line with VSF Suisse PSEA policy, the organization will sensitize targeted communities (including those with disabilities) on the principles of Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and harassment including communities having key contact information they can confidentially report. Beneficiaries will as well be empowered through sensitizations and information dissemination including ensuring their meaningful access to proposed services/facilities and cater for specific needs of girls, women, youth and men including the elderly and disabled. Child/ women/ widowed/ elderly/ disabled headed and those with pregnant and lactating mothers will be prioritized and identified as primary beneficiaries to ensure their dignity and safety is guaranteed including advocacy against Gender Based Violence/ Sexual Exploitation and Abuse.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

VSF Suisse will set up community engagement and feedback desks at targeted sites, which will be manned by male and female beneficiary representatives to collect feedback that shall be reviewed on a weekly basis including holding consultations and discussions on issues raised and way forward. These will then inform programming including relevant adjustments upon consultations with other key stakeholders including line ministries.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Key risks such as insecurity and inter/ intra communal violence including congregation of beneficiaries during distribution, long distances covered especially by women/ girls to access services including PSEA; will be mitigated through conflict sensitive programming. VSF Suisse actions will ensure appropriate conflict sensitivity including dissemination of peace messaging to de-escalate tensions and working closely with key stakeholders on ground including relevant line ministries to ensure safety and access guarantees’. VSF Suisse will conduct routine gender and protection risk assessments and risk monitoring to understand and adapt to the evolving protection concerns during implementation. This is in addition to the project prioritizing beneficiary safety and dignity in consideration with the principles of ‘Do noharm; non-discrimination, equitable, gender segregated and meaningful access to proposed services/facilities and cater for specific needs of girls, women, youth and men including the elderly and disabled. Communities will also be engaged among other relevant stakeholders, including awareness and training on basic human rights, UXOs while implementing various activities. For instance, the primary beneficiaries for this project are identified as host, returnee and IDP communities. This is designed to deliver services in a non-discriminatory manner for all those who are most vulnerable, and to ensure any tensions between host communities and IDPs over access to facilities and services is avoided. The bulk of the program activities are planned to be delivered during dry periods, to ensure communities are not distracted from the core farming activities with potential to harm their productive livelihood engagements. Child/women/widowed/elderly/disabled headed and those with pregnant and lactating mothers will be prioritized and identified as primary beneficiaries of livelihood kits to ensure their dignity and safety is guaranteed including advocacy against Gender Based Violence/ Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Beneficiary information dissemination on specific support livelihood kits packages by the field teams will be done, in addition to instituting a “feedback and complaint mechanism”. Contacts ofkey project VSF Suisse will in addition monitor security trends and ensure conflict sensitive programming and linkages with other security focal points such as UN Department of Safety and Security Contingency Plans for South Sudan, South Sudan NGO Forum Security Working Group and short message alerts and coordination with UN OCHA and UN HAS emergency relocation and evacuation plans. As cases of COVID 19 continue to spread, VSF Suisse will ensure implementation adheres to key Ministry of Health/ WHO Guidelines including provision of protective gears among frontline staff, ensuring social distancing and messaging on key preventative efforts throughoutimplementation.
