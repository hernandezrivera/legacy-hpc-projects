Project Summary

The project aims to support vulnerable children in elementary grades from two to six by providing remedial classes on two core subjects of Arabic Language and Mathematics. This project will contribute to the 2021 HRP Education Objective 2: “Increase equitable safe access to inclusive, quality educational services for vulnerable children”
The remedial classes will assist the low achieving children to master the foundational numerical and reading skills. According to the 2021 HNO, a total of 18,989 are in need of remedial education in the same year. It is anticipated that the COVID-19 outbreak which resulted in school closures of the second semester of the academic year 2019-2020 from March 2020 will further increase the number of boys and girls in need of remedial education, especially amongst the vulnerable poor children without internet and necessary devices to access distance learning opportunities, since schools have been re-opened using a blended approach that combines distance and face to face learning.
UNICEF will support 5,000 vulnerable school children to improve their learning achievements through the provision of remedial education lessons in Arabic Language and Mathematics. It is expected that improving their grades in these core subjects will reduce their risk of dropping out of school. According to UNICEF’s Out of School Report (2018), low achievement is given as one of the main reasons for dropping out of school.
The intervention has been developed in consultation with relevant stakeholders, targeted teachers, students and parents. All of them expressed a great need for this intervention as it reduces the risk of school dropout. Under this project, 5,000 children will be supported with remedial education by 220 newly hired teachers (50% females) from university graduates who will receive training on remedial education teaching methods and formative assessments in classrooms and during self-study at home in case of re-closure of schools due to the COVID-19 pandemic. Teachers will also be trained on how to provide psychosocial support and outreach for children who do not return to classrooms, in addition to UNICEF course on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Parents will be oriented about remedial education, health protocol for safe school operations, and how to be engaged to support their children’s learning during their self-study and remote learning at home. The project will also contribute to the reduction of the high unemployment rate among youth in Gaza (60% according to World Bank report 2014) since it provides working opportunities for the university graduates and facilitates their future recruitment by the relevant education authorities.
Target children will be selected through the examination of the learning achievement records in most vulnerable schools and from those who have shown continuous or rapid drop in learning achievement over the past five months. The project will focus on foundational skills in the Arabic language and Mathematics as key priorities among core subjects where students have been falling behind. The project will target particularly those who are failing to meet minimal standards in the subjects. Therefore, special customized tests will determine which students are in need of support.
The project will build on catch-up/remedial programmes implemented by UNICEF between 2015 and 2019 and will benefit from materials developed and tested in partnership with relevant stakeholders. These materials considered the recent changes in the curriculum and have been completely updated as of 2019. UNICEF will use methodology already proven effective through the on-going and previous interventions implemented with relevant stakeholders. Children will be instructed in each school by three teachers, one teacher being from the targeted school itself and the other two support teachers who are university graduates who obtained high scores in the final university exams.

1 - Needs

The Gaza Strip faces a chronic humanitarian crisis, impacting the livelihoods and access to essential services by its two million residents. This crisis has been driven by over 13 years of closure and an unsolved internal Palestinian political divide. The dire socio-economic situation has been worsened by the COVID-19 emergency which has further exacerbated vulnerabilities. According to the recent statistics revealed in the “Gender Alert” report from UNWOMEN published in August 2020 that 66 per cent of the beneficiaries received support from Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) are from Gaza and half of the population in Gaza are living under poverty line and roughly 33 per cent of them are below the extreme poverty line. School boys, girls and their teachers in schools from the poorest communities in Gaza Strip are amongst those particularly hard-hit by the COVID-19 pandemic. These children do not have access to the internet or the required devices to access the remote learning opportunities that are currently being used by the education system to provide education during the COVID-19 pandemic. The education system’s capacity in providing quality education to all children remains a major challenge especially for the children in Gaza. The deteriorating living conditions, teachers receiving partial salaries 421 public schools (serving 50 per cent of children in Gaza) lacking operational budgets and shortage of teachers are some of the factors compromising the provision of education in Gaza according to the 2021 HNO. With the COVID-19 pandemic, more families have been pushed below the poverty line undermining their capacity to afford for basic education supplies, transportation costs, adequate internet or IT equipment for distance learning and increasing the risk of school dropout. Only 62 per cent of individuals use internet compared to 74 per cent in West Bank according to the PCBS 2020 report on Sustainable Development Goals.The reopening dates of the new school year 2020-2021 in Gaza continued to change following the resurgence of COVID-19 cases and its community transmission in Gaza. The current disruption of education affects negatively the learning process for all school children, particularly those in the primary schools as they learn better through interactive methods. Children not only learn from interactions with their teachers and peers, but also develop their social and intellectual skills at school. The blended learning approach that combines distance learning and face to face approach adopted by the Ministry of Education (MoE) to provide continuity of learning has its own challenges in that not all children are able to benefit from the learning opportunities. A study by the MoE, 2020 found that only 30 per cent of school children continued their study through the distance learning, implying that 70 per cent students lost their learning time. If not mitigated with remedial education, these children are at high risk of dropping out of school. The education cluster estimated that 162,122 children in Gaza are in need of Education in Emergencies. Of these, 18,089 (Male: 9,513; Female: 9,476) are in need of remedial education.

2 - Activities

The interventions listed are linked to the following Strategic Objectives and activities of the Education cluster and aligns with the contingency plan of relevant education authorities. The below listed activities will be provided to 5,000 school children aged 8-12 years with 40% girls for remedial classes in Gaza. 1. Recruitment of 220 temporary teachers (50% females) from university graduates and teachers to provide remedial education to the children in collaboration with relevant authorities and the implementing partner; 2. Conduct pre- and post-tests to diagnose the knowledge and skills due to their loss of learning or their weak performance;3. Preparation of learning materials based on the identified learning gaps from the pre-test. This will include learning sheets for children and a schedule for the remedial classes; 4. Training of 220 teachers on how to provide to provide remedial education, psychosocial support and outreach to children who do not return to classroom;5. Provision of stationery and other learning and teaching materials teachers and learners including learning sheets to children to ensure quality remedial education is provided; 6. Prepare analytical report from children performance on Math and Arabic in the pre- and post-tests to monitor progress; and,7. Conduct awareness sessions for around 3000 parents 70% females on the remedial education to enhance their capacity to participate in their children’s learning. The orientation will also include health protocols on the protective measures for safe schools and homes. During the remedial education classes, teachers will be able to assess children’s learning performance as they will have been trained on the two types of learning assessment in classrooms and virtually in case of remote learning. At the end of the project, children will sit again for the post-test and will answer the same questions on Arabic Language and Mathematics. The scores of the pre- and post-tests will be analysed by experts and a report will be provided to UNICEF with the result.
