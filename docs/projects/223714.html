Project Summary

Introduction: This project aims to address the critical lack of access to essential primary healthcare services in 10 of the 25 primary healthcare units not supported by the HSTP project, and to strengthen Bentiu State Hospital to provide secondary (surgical) emergency lifesaving services, particularly for women with pregnancy-related complications among the 179,242 displaced communities in Rubkona and Guit Counties.
Background and Rationale: Conflicts in South Sudan (2013, 2016) and Sudan (since April 2023), along with unprecedented flooding, have displaced at least 335,000 people from Guit and neighboring counties, who are now living in IDP camps in Rubkona and Bentiu towns. This population movement substantially increased the catchment population of Bentiu State Hospital (BSH) in recent years, straining the already meager resources such as medical staff, medical supplies, and the hospital’s infrastructure. For instance, the hospital’s sewage system is now broken, forcing admitted patients, including bedridden individuals, to bathe outside the hospital wards. This situation has also led to open defecation within the hospital premises. The septic tank now requires emptying every 2 to 3 weeks, compared to the previous quarterly schedule.
Although HSTP support is available, the resource envelope is small and supports only 8 health facilities (3 out of 23 facilities in Rubkona and 5 out of 10 facilities in Guit County). An additional 3 health facilities are supported under the SSHF, leaving at least 20 health facilities in Rubkona and Guit Counties unsupported and with very limited access to vaccines, essential medicines, and health staff, relying solely on volunteers. Populations surrounding these facilities face significant health challenges and lack access to lifesaving essential health services, including immunization. The risk of delayed epidemic detection and response in these communities is high, and if an epidemic occurs, mortality and morbidity rates can be very high.
Therefore, Cordaid is seeking funding to support at least 10 health facilities of the 20 unsupported facilities to provide basic life-saving essential health services to the surrounding populations. Reopening and strengthening these facilities will not only reduce pressure on BSH and the MSF Camp hospital but also extend life-saving healthcare closer to the beneficiaries. Including these facilities in the weekly integrated disease surveillance and reporting system will also facilitate early warning and response in case of an epidemic.
This funding will also be used to strengthen the capacity of BSH to provide emergency life-saving services, particularly emergency obstetric care. Additional support will be utilized to strengthen the hospital’s capacity to respond to the cholera epidemic, with at least 86 cases reported in Rubkona County as of December 2nd 2024. Cordaid has already set up a Cholera Treatment Unit in BSH and Rubkona PHCC.
Principal Objective: To reduce excess mortality and morbidity among the most vulnerable persons living in Rubkona and Guit Counties by providing essential healthcare services through:
• Strengthening the capacity of BSH to serve as a referral point for 24/7 emergency life-saving obstetric care services
• Supporting 10 selected facilities to provide basic essential healthcare services, community outreach, and weekly IDSR reporting

1. Needs

Rubkona and Guit Counties, located in Unity State, continue to face significant public challenges due to natural disasters, primarily flooding, and violent conflicts that occurred between 2013 and 2016.Rubkona County currently hosts about 330,000 Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs). Among these, about 205,000 were displaced by severe floods, and an additional 100,000 were displaced by violent conflicts. Additionally, the Rotriak IDP camp in Rubkona County accommodates at least 30,000 returnees, Sudanese refugees, and flood-displaced individuals.On the other hand, Guit County, with an estimated population of 63,869, experienced severe flooding that displaced most of its inhabitants, as well as health facilities and the County Health Department, to Rubkona County.This population displacement described above created unique public health challenges for Rubkona County. For instance, the substantial increase in the catchment population of Bentiu State Hospital (BSH) has continued to strain the availability of medical supplies and has adversely affected the hospital’s infrastructure and, consequently, the quality of healthcare services provided. The hospital’s sewage system is now broken, forcing admitted patients, including bedridden individuals, to bathe outside the hospital wards. This situation has also led to open defecation within the hospital premises. The septic tank now requires emptying every 2 to 3 weeks, compared to the previous quarterly schedule. Additionally, BSH lacks a mortuary, resulting in deceased individuals being placed on the floor of a three-square-meter room and necessitating removal within 24 hours. Additionally, there are several gaps, especially a lack of trained personnel to provide Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) services. Despite these challenges, BSH and the MSF facility in Rubkona remain the nearest well-functioning health facilities for the population in Rotriak, about 40 to 45 kilometers away.Together, Rubkona and Guit Counties have a total of 33 health facilities (23 in Rubkona and 10 in Guit), but only 8 are supported by the HSTP project and about 3 by SSHF. The 23 unsupported facilities have very limited access to vaccines, essential medicines, and health staff, relying solely on volunteers, and support from SSHF. This lack of support further heightens the risk of delayed epidemic detection and response, potentially leading to increased mortality and morbidity.There are three water purification and pumping stations in Rubkona and Bentiu towns; however, they are currently nonfunctional due to a lack of supplies. Consequently, IDPs collect stagnant floodwater for domestic use. Considering the high level of open defecation, and the inconsistent desludging of latrines, fecal waste flows into communities whenever it rains. The Hepatitis E outbreak since 2018 and the current cholera outbreaks in Rubkona are therefore not surprising. As of 2 December, a total of 86 cases of cholera had been identified in Rubkona County, with 2 deaths (which is above the expected threshold of 1%); this is a serious health risk. If left unabated, the cholera outbreak cases will eventually overwhelm Bentiu State Hospital and Rubkona PHCC treatment units.An integrated public health assessment in July 2024 revealed additional public health crises in IDP settlements in Bentiu Town, Bentiu IDP Camp, and Rotriak. These included: • 63% of households experienced food consumption gaps due to rising food prices; • Poor sanitation conditions, with 90% of households practicing open defecation.• Overcrowding, forcing families to share shelters or settle in WASH corridors, compromising privacy, safety, and health, and creating a fertile environment for rape and gender-based violence. According to UNMISS, about 150 women and girls sought medical treatment after having been raped or sexually assaulted along roads near Nhialdiu and Guit on their way to Bentiu.

2. Project activities

Cordaid will deliver low-cost, high-impact essential health services in 1 hospital and 10 HSTP unsupported primary healthcare units in Rubkona and Guit Counties through static facilities, mobile teams, and community health outreaches. The proposed activities will target a population of 179,242 IDPs, Sudanese refugees, and the host community.The package of essential health services and scope of activities to be implemented in the 11 health facilities include:• Infection prevention and control (IPC): Training all front-line clinical staff and cleaners on IPC, printing and distributing IPC Standard Operating Procedures to health facilities, and ensuring they are displayed in every ward, outpatient department, and other facility departments.• Child health services: Health education, routine immunization, measles vaccination, integrated management of neonatal and childhood illnesses, rapid response and referral for complicated cases, distribution of long-lasting insecticide-treated nets, vitamin A supplementation, promotion of adequate infant and young child feeding behaviors, nutrition screening, and referral to nutrition therapeutic programs.• Care for disabled persons: Providing rehabilitation services with data on cases treated disaggregated by age, sex, and type of disability• Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) services: Ensuring the availability of mental health information, education, and communication (IEC) materials and guidelines, having at least one staff member trained on the Mental Health Gap Action Programme in each of the 11 facilities, scheduling clinic days for mental health cases at BSH, and ensuring the availability of carbamazepine, and amitriptyline tablets. Data on MHPSS cases treated will be disaggregated by age, sex, and type of disability.• Maternal and neonatal health services: Providing health education, antenatal care, skilled delivery, postnatal care of mothers and newborns, family planning, and referral to secondary health services, as well as basic and comprehensive emergency obstetric and newborn care• Sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) services: Identification, counseling, management, and proper referral for victims of SGBV, including rape victims• Treatment of malaria, respiratory tract infections, and diarrhea: Focusing on these major causes of morbidity and mortality, especially among children.• Distribution of essential medicines and supplies to supported health facilities.• Emergency preparedness and response: Building the capacity of the 11 health facilities, the County Health Department (CHD), and local partners to detect, assess, and respond to health needs in the event of emergencies and disease outbreaks.• Disease surveillance and outbreak response: Aligning with the nationwide Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR) and Early Warning Alert and Response System (EWARS)• Quality improvement and supervision: Conducting in-service training, continuous quality improvement activities, supportive supervision, quarterly reviews, regular monitoring visits, and promoting proper waste management and disposal• Community outreach activities: Including vaccination, screening and treatment of infections such as TB and HIV, health education, and behavioral change communication•Community feedback: Regular needs assessments and surveys, including interviews and community meetings, will be used to gather input and feedback. Feedback mechanisms such as suggestion boxes and hotlines will collect continuous feedback. • Coordination meetings: Actively participating in TWG and Health Cluster meetings at the state and national levels. For Rubkona and Guit Counties, where Cordaid is the HSTP lead, we will organize weekly health cluster meetings in collaboration with the respective CHDs, as well as participate meetings or in collaboration with the respective CHDs, as well as participate meetings organized by other closely related clusters such as nutrition and WASH.
