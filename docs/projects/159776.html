Project Summary

The project aims to mitigate, prevent and respond to Gender Based Violence (GBV),
supporting GBV survivors and people in risk of GBV living in the eastern area of Khan
Younis (including ARA). Increase in tension, violence and casualties related with Great
March Return (GMR), serious deterioration of socioeconomic situation and the _nancial
crisis, in addition to the lack of GBV services, aggravate the existing fragile living
conditions of the target population and increase on GBV cases. This project will be
implemented on a complementary and coordinated partnership between APS and UHWC
both with strong expertise in GVB and solid mutual collaboration. Humanity Inclusion will
be a strategic partner to ensure the disability mainstreaming compo-nent throughout the
project. The proposal organises around 3 main components: 1) Providing access to
coordinated, safe and con_dential GBV multisectoral services in the new “One Stop
Center” of Khan Younis, and through a mobile clinic and home visits in the East of Khan
Younis (including ARA). Provision of case management and GBV multisectoral response
includes: Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) services as a needed entry-point for GBV
survivors, psycho-social support (PSS) services, emergency health care, SGBV case
management, referral, specialized legal assistance and economic enablement (the
economic empowerment component will be worked only with a selected group of GBV
survivors). Furthermore, the response is complemented with the distribution of dignity kits.
2) GBV awareness-raising workshops and campaigns will be conducted for GBV survivors
and people at risk of GBV, CBOs and vari-ous social services platforms (kindergartens,
schools, etc.) in East Khan Younis (including ARA). 3) Trainings will be carried out for GBV
non-specialized humanitarian actors on GBV detection, _rst support and referral. The
workshop is designed to cover all non-specialized actors working in the Khan Younis area,
contributing to the enhancement of community ca-pacities in responding to GBV. This
training aims at contributing to the integration of GBV in a more holistic concept of
Protection and to ensure those cases detected by CBOs, LNGOs and other non-specialist
actors reach the GBV specialized pathways. The project will provide GBV multisectorial
services and awareness-raising sessions predominantly to GBV survivors and people at
risk of GBV. This selection criteria will include the following target groups: women and girls impacted by GMR (including women whose husbands have been killed or injured,
mothers with injured children and girls who lost a father or who are expected to have a
father or caregivers with disability), refugee women and girls, female head households,
widows, women and girls with disability(ies) and wom-en cancer survivors. Through the
intervention, these women and girls will be able to strengthen their ability to cope in a
more positive way, not only with GBV, but also with protracted threats and shocks related
to the heavily and permanent exposure to policies and practices related to the blockade
and the occupation. The project is guided by community-based protection approach,
survivor-centred approach, right-based approach and do-not-harm approach. It has been
designed and formulated mainstreaming gender and diversity (focused on disability). At
the beginning of the implementation, Humanity Inclusion will lead workshops on disability
and GBV to the project staff in the One Stop Center and the staff of the Health and
Community Center in Khan Younis followed by monitoring at mid-term and evaluation at
end of the project. The project is aligned with the priorities set by HNO 2018, speci_cally in
relation to gender and protection concerns. Outputs are designed to meet the three HRP
2018-2020 strategic objectives. It has also been designed in line with IASC standards and
the Minimum Standards for Prevention and Response to Gender-Based Violence in
Emergencies from UNFPA.

1 - Needs

GBV is a key protection concern in Gaza. It is recognized that GBV in humanitarian settings affects men and women, boys and girls, with and without disabilities differently. The impact of the humanitarian crisis in Gaza has exacerbated GBV in all its forms (especially domestic violence, psychological/emotional and physical abuse, sexual assault, denial of resources, opportunities and services and forced marriage).In March and October 2018, ApS and UHWC conducted GBV rapids need assessments (RNA) in Gaza´s middle area and in Bani Suhaila (Khan Younis). The RNA carried out 4 FGD with 43 women, men, girls and boys, with and without disabilities, interviews with CBOs and with relevant stakeholders in Khan Younis. The age distribution of the participants was 15 Women (24-60 yrs), 12 men (34-59), 10 girls (14-18 years), 8 boys and young men (14-22). Participants stressed lack of information on their rights, GBV and the nearest GBV specialized service. Adults highlighted the fragile economic situation and consequent increase in psychological stress as the main threats to family stability as the central cause in both negative changes in family dynamics and increase of GBV. A few men and women identiﬁed forced marriage (including child marriage) as a type of GBV, but most of them perceive it as a protection mechanism, especially in cases of food insecurity. On the contrary, FGD carried out with boys and girls highlighted forced marriage as a main cause of GBV. Women emphasized domestic and psychosocial violence, along with physical assaults were the most common types of GBV, related mostly with their intimate partner; pointing out economic dependence as one of the main reasons why women cannot leave the circle of violence. In addition, some women express their concern regarding widowed women (by GMR) forcefully married to their husband´s brothers. Girls emphasized sexual assault in and outside families, behaviour-controlling and rights, opportunities, resources and services denial. Regarding perception of GBV causes, girls mentioned patriarchal culture and lack of electricity, while both adults and boys pointed out occupation, economic situation and high unemployment rate as the main cause. Furthermore, men identiﬁed only sexual assault and rape outside the family as a type of GBV. Common views of all participants were the lack of information on their rights, GBV or the nearest GBV specialized services available. Key actors interviewed believed VAW cases are increasing as a result of emotional and psychological abuse, sexual assault, physical abuse, forced marriage and denial of resources and opportunities. In relation to GBV and disability, the specialized actors point out that women and girls with disabilities in Gaza experience double the discrimination, which places them at a higher risk of GBV, sexual abuse, being neglected, exploitation and harm. Lack of services in eastern area Khan Younis and nearby ARA were raised in reference to prevention or response to GBV. While GBV non-specialized actors interviewed (CBO, LNGO…) often come across GBV cases (including on women and girls with disabilities), they stressed being unable to provide support as they lack expertise in dealing with such cases and do not know how and which specialized service the person needs to be referred. The main activities included in this proposal are grounded in the expertise that both Alianza and UHWC have through the implementation of previous interventions. Our teams, and the target group, often refer to the acute need of mobile teams to reach the most remote areas. Furthermore, our experience shows that health (sexual and reproductive services) are a safe entry point for GBV survivors and that the community approach, both in awareness and with the training for non specialized actors, are important to ensure protection mainstreaming.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

The needs assessment used for the design of the project has been done through participatory methodologies such as focus discussion: including the affected populations, women and men separately, CBOs, and other actors/ responsibilities holders, such as the GBV service providers teams, and interviews with relevant stakeholders.A monitoring system will be put in place to measure satisfaction of beneficiaries and also to do adjustments in case of need in order to improve the efficiency, the efficacy and the impact of the implementation. Thus, feedback from the beneficiaries will be monthly collected by Alianza´s staff who will visit the beneficiaries to monitor the status and degree of implementation of the on-going activities. Satisfaction surveys will be also provided to the beneficiaries. Furthermore, a formal process of complaints will be developed to register and identify any gap, challenge or deficiency that may happen and to solve it. All the information will be used for quality improvement, programming and advocacy. M&E methodologies include Focus Group Discussions and interviews to be conducted during the implementation providing the beneficiaries with information on project activities as an accountability tool, this way not only project staff aims to obtain information from beneficiaries, but also beneficiaries will have a space where they can understand what has been done, what has been achieved and how services are planned and improved during the intervention. The accountability tools need to be implemented ensuring confidentiality and safety of SGBV survivors so no disclosure of cases information will be done during these sessions.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

This proposal has been designed based on a Protection Mainstreaming approach and is in compliance with the Minimun Standards for Protection Mainstreaming. ApS works closely with key implementing partners that have a strong presence in the field and in the targetted communities. Prior to every intervention there is an in-depth analysis including: gender and power relations and threats and risks (contextual and programmatic) the conclussions of this analysis are used to guide the projet design. Through this intervention, we will ensure that targetted people are not exposed to any further hazards or abuses or any more Human Rights violations. This issue is particularly relevant when providing services to GBV survivors. All staff (mixed teams male and female to respond to particular sensitivities) will be trained on the International Guidelines and follow up the UNFPA- IASC standards. The service provision is under the regular quality check up to ensure safety&confidentiality, respect, non-discrimination, survivor-centered approach, community and Human Rights based approach. All information related to the cases is managed ensuring confidentiality and the security of both the beneficiaries and the service providers.The above-mentioned identified protection risks are taken into consideration in all phases of the project, and specific complains and feedback mechanisms will be put in place to ensure that we integrate lessons learned and any corrective meassure to better respond to the protection needs. All phases of the project promote the dignity, inclusive participation and diversity of the target group. With a strong focus on the access and participation of People With Disability, the project aims at, not only ensuring that the center and the services are accessible, but also that the specialized service providers are trained to respond to specific needs and to better respond to cases with any type of disability.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

This proposal is drafted based on the non-discrimantion principle ensuring equal and impartial access to the services provided and reaching the most vulnerable population, including groups with special needs. The activities are conducted by a field team that have an extensive experience in the community, therefore, is easy to tailor the activities and services to the calendar and specific times when is possible for the target group to attend. There is an important work on awareness raising and community and men engaging to ensure that women are not prevented from accessing the services and the activities and to avoid revictimization for those attending the GBV specialized services. All phases of the project promote the inclusive participation of People With Disability, the project aims at, not only ensuring that the center and the services are accessible, but also that the specialized service providers are trained to respond to specific needs and to better respond to cases with any type of disability.The response is based on a Human Right based approach and GBV survivors are active agents in the design of their recovery process. Every person receiving specialized services agrees with the case manager on the identified needs and the required services, as well as on the security plan and the intervention designed by the professional. The multisectoral approach includes legal information and support. All people receiving the services are informed about their rights and how to provide feedback and the complain mechanism in place.
