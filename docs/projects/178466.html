Project Summary

SP in NES is currently supporting two primary health care clinics (Tel Hajar, Hasakeh City, and Serekanyie camp), to provide a comprehensive package of free primary healthcare services to vulnerable in and out of camp populations where there are significant gaps in health services. SP has prepared this response alongside KRC, based on gaps expressed by the NES NGO Forum and HAO and has actively coordinated with WG leads to determine a harmonized response. By strengthening these clinics SP is increasing the quality of clinical data to inform future responses. Both clinics address non-emergent and urgent cases at the outpatient level, which will improve health outcomes in the local community by ensuring earlier access to free quality primary healthcare by IDPs. This approach also ensures strengthened local pathways and bottleneck relief on hospitals, resulting in a more evenly distributed burden on PHCs. The project will provide supportive referral and coordination structures alongside critical inputs, capacity building support and clinical staffing models to improve quality of care and adherence to Sphere Core Humanitarian Standards. SP will set-up a referral system (which currently does not exist in DeZ) to support referrals from DeZ to Hassakeh, where more robust facilities exist. Tel-Hajar clinic currently services approximately 150-175 beneficiaries per day. Of these, approx. 90% are unique every month. The clinic in Serekaniye (the only fixed, full time clinic in the camp with services offered 24/7 per the HWG guidance) serves up to 2,162 households/11,163 individuals (camp coordination meeting, April 7th) and an average of 120 patients per day, leading to decreased morbidity amongst the host community and IDP population, and increased capacity amongst national healthcare personnel. SP is also creating effective CHW programs in these facilities as well as in other regions to provide education and compliance regarding COVID-19 IPC and COVID-19 screenings. SP also works with the DeZ Health Committee to provide COVID support in DeZ, focusing on the recruitment, training and education, deployment of community health workers in the region who will be able to increase suspected COVID-19 case identification and encourage IPC compliance among health care workers. SP supports 4 full-time Respiratory Response Teams (RRTs), 30 people in total, through December 31, 2021. SP supports the teams with 4 rented vehicles with drivers. SP directly provides to the RRT teams monthly provisions of PPE as outlined. SP provides trainings to the RRT's on a monthly or bi-monthly basis and ensure staff are adequately prepared and trained for their positions. RRTs are a link between the healthcare system and communities providing COVID-19 testing, sampling, case management, advice, homecare, and referral support, in the referral pathway from the community to COVID-19 departments and in case management. CHWs will raise awareness among the population, provide related campaigns, and will support in the development of a referral pathway from the community to the health center. RRTs have an important role to play in highlighting the number of suspected/confirmed cases, determining the path of COVID-19 in the region. Employees will be local residents. More checklists for services will be developed in order to monitor workflow remotely in the event of the inability to travel for security reasons. We plan to establish an operations room to manage referrals in the DeZ area and coordinate all referrals by communicating with Qamishli Operations Room and COVID-19 Hospital in Washokani, and in same time the RRT team leaders will be in contact with DEZ-OD for internal referrals. There is a critical need to ensure that localized PHC capacity is bolstered and that support is provided locally to ensure stress is relieved on Hassakeh National and Al Hikma hospitals, which currently share referrals.

4 - Needs

Based on feedback from both the NGO Forum and the Health Working Group (HWG) there is a critical need to ensure that localized Primary Health Care (PHC) capacities are bolstered and that support is provided locally to ensure stress is relieved on Hassakeh National and Al Hikma hospitals, which currently share referrals (a third referral sharing hospital in Ras-Al-Ayn was destroyed). It is critical that patients' needs can be met by local providers who can ensure quality and continuity of care. The SP-supported clinic, Tel Hajar, is the only free clinic serving both IDPs and the host community in Hasakeh City, drawing people in need from residences, temporary settlements, and camps, with the IDP population projected to increase in 2021 (especially given Serekaniye' s projected growth - Serekaniye Camp Meeting). Given the vulnerability of patients in the Tel Hajar catchment, many would be unable to seek timely medical care without these services offered. Without the early intervention that these primary care services provide, patients would deteriorate to the point that hospitalization would be necessary, burdening the only two hospitals in Hasakeh which have already taken increased referrals from camps. The camp's growing population also illustrates a need for community based support to PHCs to relieve the stressors on hospitals. In Serekaniye camp, projected growth put the camp at a maximum capacity of 16,200 individuals (the camp population currently stands at 11,163 individuals as of 7 April 21). The key drivers for relocating to the camps include deteriorating socio-economic conditions and host community fatigue ongoing stressors expected to last through the duration of the program.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

SP and KRC are consulting with local populations to create safe strategies for access and equitability and to mitigate protection risks through health programming as well as support to remote teams in Deir-Al-Zour. Support is provided to 4 RRTs from DeZ. Protection risks identified for this project include a. Staff or patients assaulted en route to/from clinic b. Beneficiaries or staff exploited by staff members c. Lack of monitoring of remote teams leads to lack of accountability. All staff involved in the project are trained in managing and/or referring specific gender and protection concerns and have a good operational awareness of protection risks/issues. A safeguarding focal point is assigned to this program and the complaints/feedback mechanism means that feedback is captured from remote/off site locations and acted upon. SP will assess the contextualized assistance needs of other vulnerable groups including PWDs and ensure their equitable participation and inclusion in the project. 369 persons of the 11,163 residents (2,162 HH) in Serekaniye camp have disabilities (around 17% of HH). Of these, 143 are under the age of 18. Across NES, the numbers of disabled people are quite high – HNAP’s 2021 report puts the number at 37% of the population. To support the needs of PWDs and others in camp settings, transport to and from the clinic will be provided to vulnerable groups, including PWDs, PLWs etc to ensure that they do not need to walk to the clinic during hours or darkness. Vulnerability groups with limited mobility will be supported via SP and camp management coordination on their specific needs and if needed, household visits will be carried out for PWDs who struggle to access services.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Security situation or border restrictions cause the team to lose access to NES for a period of time. This is mitigated through KRC staff and SP national staff being able to continue to operate the clinics in the interim period. More COVID-19 cases amongst health worker staff, either SP or partner. This is mitigated through continued training and enforcement of IPC protocols. Pockets of higher risk area due to tribal tension and ISIS sleeper cell activity. Mitigated through security assessment of area to identify processes and protocols, and coordination with local community actors and members. Our supply chain has remained relatively stable as we procure all medications and consumables locally within NES. Our most significant barrier to consistent medication and consumable supply have been road closures between Hassakeh, Damascus, Quamishli, and the KRI border as a result of lockdowns. We have been able to successfully mitigate medication and supply shortages resulting from supply chain instability by setting up contracted agreements for the supply of 6-9 months’ worth of medication from several local vendors. This medication supply is delivered to the clinics monthly or bimonthly, which allows vendors time to plan well in advance, taking advantage of times the roads are open, to have adequate stock in their warehouse to continue to supply our clinics.
