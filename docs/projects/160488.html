Project Summary

Oxfam’s immediate objectives are to save lives, meet the immediate food needs and protect the livelihoods of the most vulnerable groups of women, men, boys and girls in the targeted counties. Extreme food insecurity persists throughout South Sudan and increased humanitarian assistance is needed to prevent the loss of lives and their livelihoods. An estimated 1.7 million people remain internally displaced in South Sudan, many of whom have been displaced more than once. Another 2.5 million people have fled South Sudan to neighboring countries since December 2013. A collapse in national economy, reduced crop production and dependence on imports seriously deteriorate people’s ability towards food security round the year, putting millions of lives at risk.
In August 2019, an estimated 6.35 million people (54% of the population) are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, among whom an estimated 1.7 million people are facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and 10,000 people are in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5). Compared to the same period in 2018, there is a slight reduction in the proportion of people facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity by an estimated 5%. However, high levels of acute food insecurity still persist in the country.
The acute food insecurity persists, driven by localized conflicts, climatic shocks, pests and diseases, slow recovery from prolonged asset depletion, poor macro-economic conditions and sub-optimal market functionality. The slight improvement in the food security situation in 2019 lean season compared to the same period in 2018 is attributed to reduced insecurity after the signing of the Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in South Sudan in September 2018 along with humanitarian support.
Oxfam has been working in Jonglei, Upper Nile, Western Bahr el Ghazal and Lake states for long time. Food Assistance Project in Nothern Jonglei, Humanitarian & Resilience in South Sudan (HARISS) program, seeds and tools distribution project, and Fortifying Equity and Economic Diversity projects have been implemented in the proposed locations.
A total of 55000 people will be targeted through different means of Food Security & Livelihood interventions in Jonglei (Nyirol, Akobo, Pibor, Uror and Twic East), and Western Bahr el Ghazal (Wau), Upper Nile (Malakal) and Lake states (Rumbek East, Rumbek North, Yirol East and Yirol West). These interventions are aligned with Food Security & Livelihood Cluster’s Objectives (CO1, CO2 & CO3). Most of these counties are under IPC phase 4 where Wau, Malakal, Uror, Twic East & Rumbek Center are under IPC phase 3. The overall nutrition situation during the lean season of 2019 is Critical and worse compared to same period in 2018. Most counties in Jonglei, Upper Nile & Lakes are in critical levels of acute malnutrition. The affected population are Internally Displaced People (IDPs), Returnees and Host Communities.
The targeting will be accomplished using gender lens, mainstreaming of addressing protection and gender-based violence and ensuring accountability to the most vulnerable people to improve their immediate food security. The program implementation will ensure that “Do No Harm principle” is adhered at the center of beneficiary targeting and provision of support. Oxfam will use a zero-tolerance policy towards Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) and any kind of harassment during implementation. Multiple and complementary feedback and complaints collection mechanisms will be considered to avail safe and convenient options to the community.

1 - Needs

The food security situation in the targeted counties are very critical in terms of food consumption, dietary diversity, reduced coping strategies, level of hunger and livelihood coping strategies. Under Jonglei state; Nyirol, Akobo and Pibor are under IPC phase 4 (Emergency phase) where General food distribution along with other immediate support and its continuation are necessary; Uror and Twic East are under IPC phase 3 but still these are in crisis state and need immediate food security support to prevent slipping into IPC phase 4 in near future. The GAM rate for Akobo and Uror is 26.7, for Nyirol is 25.5, for Pibor is 14 and for Twic East is 21.4%.The need in Lake state is also critical as Rumbek East, Rumbek North, Yirol East, Yirol West are in IPC phase 4 (Emergency phase) and immediate food security and livelihood support are essential there. Rumbek Center is under IPC phase 3 and is demanding humanitarian support as well. The GAM rate for Rumbek East, Rumbek North and Rumbek centre is 12.2, for Yirol East is 8.1Though Wau and Malakal are IPC phase 3 and because of previous conflicts the population lost their productive assets. They were unable to grow their own food and earn income to survive. The GAM rate for Wau is 13.1% and for Malakal is 19.4%. According to the gender analysis conducted in Pibor education based 2018 and in Wau, men, boys, women and girls have been suffering from high number of gender and protection concerns, high rate of food insecurity, lack of access to WASH facilities and gender inequality.Food support, seed & tools for agricultural, capacity building for women in order to increase their confidence and participation in leadership, economic empowerment for women, massive awareness to break all the social cultural norms are required at the ground level. Strengthening the existing community structures which support in communication and information channels are vital to addressing the harmful traditional norms and practices as well.Oxfam has been implementing Conditional Food Distribution together with nutrition supplement in Nyirol, Akobo and Uror since 2014 and still there are needs to continue supporting to meet their immediate food needs. Cash Based Interventions (CBI) are also on going in Akobo and Nyirol counties to create short term employment and income. However; because of conflict for long run earlier, Climatic issue, pest and disease; they are still food insecure.In lake state, Oxfam has been implementing short term livelihood projects in Rumbek East with 5000 Households, and in Rumbek North with 4000 Households. As the proposed counties (Rumek East, Rumbek North, Yirol East and Yirol West) are mostly under IPC phase 4; therefore, a huge humanitarian needs are still required in the proposed counties.In Malakal and Twic East; there is a need of restoration of livelihood and there may be a risk of returnee’s influx which require appropriate recovery support. Oxfam’s has been supporting livelihood and resilience program with a crisis modifier component through Humanitarian and Resilience Program in South Sudan (HARISS) Program.

2 - Activities

The following are the proposed activities in the targeted counties from 1st January 2020 – 31st December 2020: Activity 1: Food Assistance (40% of the targeted population) – Provision of food to the most vulnerable people where it will cover both unconditional and conditional General Food Distribution (GFD). Oxfam has been implementing General Food Distribution in Uror, Nyrol and Akobo counties from 2014.Activity 2: Provision of food assistance to people through Cash Programming, cash voucher or commodity voucher food distribution (10% of the targeted population where 70% conditional and 30% unconditional). Unconditional transfers will be targeted at vulnerable households such as the elderly, child-headed households, chronically ill, disabled, or pregnant women.Activity 3: Cash for Work/Food for Assets/ Food for Literacy work (10% of the targeted population) will aim to develop community assets, e.g secondary dyke rehabilitation to control flood events with the environmental objective to combat land degradation and promotion of agricultural activities. Cash for Work will be linked with WASH component to re-rehabilitative local WASH infrastructures for making accessibility of water. Activity 4: Livelihood promotion for on farm & off farm production (40% of the targeted population) - Promote cereal production, livestock and fishery for improved household own food productivity which is linked to improved household food consumption and/or sale of surplus.• Strengthening existing grassroots formal and informal extension service agents.• Fishery training & support - Distribution of fishing nets, hooks and fish drying ovens to support people to support market-led fishery. • Horticultural training & support – Distribution of nutritious and high value vegetable crop seeds and farm tools to target people and support market-led vegetable production.• Cereal training & support – Distribution of cereal seeds and farm tools to people and support short-term cereal production• Promotion of women friendly off-farm income generating activities.• Establish market linkage between producer’s groups and traders for buying and selling their products. Across the activities 50% women's engagement will be ensured to meet their specific needs and rights. A bottom up community participation approach will be followed with a view to beneficiary section, program implementation, monitoring & evaluation as well.Gender Action Learning (GALs) dialogue at the producer’s level where the spouse will be part of the discussion to make change of gender role at the community level.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

The project will consciously be adopting the services and entitlements which are agreed by the Strategic Advisory Group (SAG) of Food Security Cluster and general members of the clusters. These all are legally recognized by all relevant stakeholders. The Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS), Humanitarian principle and Code of Conduct will be applied through out the project period to ensure the rights of affected people. The protection principles (Enhance people’s safety, dignity and rights and avoid exposing them to further harm; Ensure people’s access to impartial assistance, according to need and without discrimination; Assist people to recover from the physical and psychological effects of threatened or actual violence, coercion or deliberate deprivation, help people to claim their rights ) will be followed through the project period.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes; the displaced people have additional challenges not having access to land for production, security and social cohesion are the key issues to be considered in the planning phase of the project. Their interventions will be based on their particular needs, accessibility and interest to overcome their challenges. Due to conflict, if population are further displaced and this will affect the accessibility of the project target groups towards short – long term interventions. The displaced people may not be able to attend the project intervention as per plan. For these sorts of displacement; some immediate support may be required to meet their immediate needs and then some transitional interventions can be designed for them based on the feasibility analysis. Protection of the displaced people in this context will be considered including Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) through Community Protection Committee and its mechanism at the ground level.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, the project will be linked with local community level structures, civil societies, community organisations and local government agencies and line departments. The community level Protection Committee and Project Implementation Committee will be engaged in the whole project cycle to get actual needs, priorities and actions for a long-term durable solution.Capacity building of producer groups on good agronomic practices, post-harvest handling and management will be provided by the project. The beneficiaries will be trained on improved farming practices at two levels; crop agronomic training and post-harvest training. The training will be conducted by the trained extension agents of the county department of agriculture with support from the Oxfam project staff. The beneficiaries will be required to participate in all the trainings of the agriculture to increase their knowledge and practical skills of the good agricultural practices (GAPs). The capacity building will be complemented with exposure visits to best performing groups & lead farmers. These improved agriculture practices will lead towards improved agriculture production in a joint effort by Producers. This approach will be ensuring sustainability of the extension services to the community and enjoyment of social proximity and acceptance of the extension agents within their community. Deliberate efforts will be made to encourage women extension agents to enable for full participation of women counterparts and to break the legacy of men dominance of extension service delivery network. The extension agents will be directly incentivized by the project and no through a third-party arrangement to ensure the incentives is used for facilitation of the extension agents. Market linkage will be established between producers and buyers at the local level for buying and selling of essential commodities.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP 2020) is obviously aligned with national objectives of Food Security & Livelihood to prevent the potential famine and increase food security and livelihood situation at the country level. The project will be based on the following Food Security & Livelihood Cluster objectives: Cluster Objective 1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable population.Cluster Objective 2: Enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable, crop seed, fishing and livestock support.Cluster Objective 3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen to household’s ability to absorb shocks.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

During the last six months the on-going conflicts are not occurring remarkably and that is allowing community people to be able for their movement in their locality. If this will continue then this is the positive sign for them to be engaged in long term interventions; engaging in agriculture production and earning income; not only depending on humanitarian assistance. The different sort of interventions will be targeted for IDPs, Host community and Returnees based on their local context, availability, access to resources and potentiality. The implementation process of peace agreement is due and in mid-November 2019 there is a schedule for formation of transition government. This issue has a significant impact of country level safety, security and stability. The number of returnees of IDPs and Refugees will be directed by the country level socio-political situation in the coming months. This will also be applicable for country level market functionality as well for establishing durable solution.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The sustainability is essentially concerned with creating proper understanding of ultimate objectives of the project by target beneficiaries which would be accomplished from the very beginning of the project, the project will emphasize building ownership of the project by the community and local authorities to run the interventions for long run even after phasing out of the project. Co-existence and cohesion among the target groups through institutional capacity building will lead towards sustainability and that would be followed in exiting from the project. The project will ensure conflict sensitivity, Do No Harm, redress of existing threats through the risk reduction approach, strengthening community-based structures, information dissemination as well as through protection advocacy and influencing duty bearers for responsibility of protection of civilians.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Raise awareness on available services using the service mapping available from the protection team within Oxfam or from protection agencies within area. A protection risk analysis over land control, utilization and ownership to inform conflict sensitive programming to be done for the proposed locations. Oxfam project will benefit from humanitarian and protection access negotiation meetings for safe registration, distribution, arms free humanitarian space for the project. Humanitarian assistance will be delivered in a safe, context specific, timely and appropriate manner which does not cause harm to the beneficiaries going to, during and soon after receiving assistance. Oxfam will mainstream gender throughout the project period. Project team will disseminate information and conduct awareness raising on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. The project team will go through training on PSEA and humanitarian code of conduct. Regular protection and gender analysis in the project locations will assist in implementing gender sensitive project activities in targeted locations. To reduce exposure of displaced women and girls to sexual violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements, the project team together with protection and gender team will disseminate information and raise awareness on protection, GBV, rights, entitlements, trafficking in persons, and etc. More so, where feasible with the protection team and gender team will collect case studies for advocacy on protection issues. Gender mainstreaming, protection mainstreaming & inclusive community engagement to be followed across the project locations. Appropriate context specific messaging, IEC materials, and complaints feedback mechanisms will be established. Project Management Committee, Community Protection Committee and other personnel’s will be trained on accountability issues.
