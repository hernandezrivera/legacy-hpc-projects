Project Summary

With the existing health worldwide crisis of COVID19 and its long term impacts, the number of women in need for GBV services is increasing. In Palestine, the heightened political situation is exacerbating the situation mainly in areas like East Jerusalem and area C, especially the Jordan valley and northern district of the West Bank. In addition, the approaching threat of annexation and its impact on Government services in Area C further compounds the situation. Based on the needs identified through the HNO process, and several analysis of the humanitarian situation specifically in the aforementioned areas, the project and its interventions will aim to strengthen and empower women exposed to GBV. The project will also enhance the approach of remote service provision through hotlines and other means in case the current situation of COVID 19 persists, also there will also be readiness to provide face to face services anytime feasible. During 2020, UNFPA has already conducted a series of online capacity building workshops on providing services using hotlines. Those workshops targeted a number of GBV services providers including governmental partners; this experience will be advanced during 2021
On the other hand, the project will target the GBV survivors as well the providers to match the scaling up of emergency GBV services with building the capacity of government and non-government actors to provide quality survivor-centred GBV services. the project will continue to use the multi-sectoral approach in providing the GBV services and build on past UNFPA achievements.

1 - Needs

Based on the PiN analysis in the HNO 2021 process, the total number of People in Need for the GBV sector is 277,749, with 135,495 women in the Gaza Strip and 142,254 in the West Bank. 30% of GBV cases (around 42,000 cases) are located in East Jerusalem and 26% in Area C (around 37,000). Those figures if related to the severity ranking in the HNO 2021 process show that GBV cases residing in area C and East Jerusalem are considered to be under “stress” or “severe stress” due to the fact that 55 to 75% of them do not know about existing protection services. In addition, following the halt in PA coordination with Israeli Authorities, the resulting absence of Government protection services in Area C was considered an aggravating factor in this area which led to the conclusion that all GBV cases in this area are under increased stress, therefore ranking them as “severe”. In another household survey of 2019 conducted by Birzeit university analyzing needs of Palestinian households in area C, it was indicated that women in South West Bank Area C communities suffer the greatest domestic abuse compared to women in other regions, with 51% suffering any instance of any abuse, 51% of them suffering from psychological abuse and 15% having suffered any instance of physical abuse over the past year. Physical abuse is lower in the other regions.Age also seems to be a factor, with 41% of young women 16-29 years old reporting having been exposed to any type or instance of domestic violence compared to 38% among those 30-49 and 29% among those 50 years or more. In these communities, younger women are at greater risk (which is consistent with the finding on greater psychological abuse experienced by single women).In East Jerusalem, UNFPA has established three safe spaces and two counselling rooms in Al-Maqassed and Augusta Victoria hospitals where GBV survivors and women at risk is receiving health, psychosocial and legal support. However, certain areas are still lacking effective GBV multispectral response to GBV survivors, and functional referral mechanism due to the absence of authority, and inability of local NGOs to protect and provide full support to women and girls in areas under full Israeli control and hence investing in those spaces is of utmost importance. The lockdown imposed on Jerusalem led to a deterioration in accessing GBV services especially for those women who hold a West Bank ID and live inside the Jerusalem barrier. Additionally, the Separation Barrier, severing off Jerusalem from the West Bank, has resulted in jurisdictional ambiguity between the obligations of the Palestinian National Authorities (PNA) and the Israeli Police, further fragmenting the vulnerable communities in East Jerusalem.Moreover, COVID-19 has caused a socio-economic depression especially for women who are already suffering from vulnerability and domestic violence. The pandemic crisis has affected new women and contributed to a behavioural dynamic of men being more frustrated, unable to fulfil their expected role in patriarchal society, mainly their responsibility to provide and protect. Some women who had a stable situation before the crisis have become GBV victims due to economic deterioration as the loss of livelihoods due to the lockdown. Many of them do not have the savings and capabilities to cope with the change, and are now at high risk of violence. All the above mentioned findings indicate the necessity of mental health and psychosocial structured interventions (MHPSS) to support women and their families. Holistic approach of providing services will be considered through this project, mainly to consider the survivor center approach and to look at the women as the core of support and address all needs comprehensively.

2 - Activities

1. Multispectral services to GBV survivorsa. Develop counseling services through investment in the already established GBV rooms at Augusta Victoria and Almakassed hospitals, more training to the teams in those rooms, disseminating awareness messages to women on the existing GBV services as well as general prevention messages. PPE materials will be delivered to ensure full protection of service providers and women beneficiaries alike. GBV rooms in hospitals will follow the whole sterilization and disinfection procedures.b. Through UNFPA supported safe spaces, provide case management and SRH services and counselling, to 1000 GBV cases including women with disabilities identiﬁed and treated through the face-to face and virtual services provision modality to respond to COVID-19 crisis. UNFPA will ensure that all safe spaces have health protocol to follow in case of any COVID-19 suspected cases.c. Provide support for 500 women GBV survivors and women at risk, 200 in Jordan Valley, 200 in Qalqelia, and 100 in Jerusalem to start small businesses to reduce vulnerability of GBV survivors. GBV survivors will be equipped with vocational and life skills, to improve household basic needs as part of the multi- sectorial service package taking into account NO HARM and RIGHTS approaches. d. Provide MHPSS and referral services for 1500 women and family members at risk through networking with HEMAYA Network in East Jerusalem, and through active CBOs and service providers in Qalqeelia and Jordan valley and through UNFPA partners who are active in Area C, e. Provide immediate protection and dignity kits for 3000 survivors of GBV targeted through the safe spaces and the CBOs. This assistance will be part of Cash and Voucher transfer modality in partnership with World Food Programme. f. Establish and strengthen the referral pathway for HEMAYA network in Jerusalem to effectively respond to GBV risks in Jerusalem.g. Carry out 10 self-help support groups for GBV survivors (50 women per group, 25 sessions). Provide protection services and reintegration to women survivors of GBV at west bank shelters. and provide needed supplies as PPE, food items, and communication equipment laptops, mobiles.h. As for virtual services, dissemination of hotline numbers will occur simultaneously with receiving the service through face to face modality, to ensure women are aware of those services in case of lockdown.2. capacity buildinga. Train medical staff at Jerusalem hospitals, local CBOs in Jordan valley and Qalqelia and other service providers, mainly on detection and referral for GBV survivors while following all protection measures including physical distancing and wearing mask during training. b. Build the capacity of health and social providers on Clinical management of rape (CMR) to be fully acquainted also with the nature of forensic services in Palestine, how to refer and when.c. Strengthen the capacity of 8 CBOs working in Qalqelia, Jordan Valley and Jerusalem to provide multi sectoral remote quality services.to communicate, monitor cases and document progress d. Strengthen the capacity of local organizations on PSEA by conducting PSEA training, strengthen complaint mechanisms and monitoring capacities and increase awareness on PSEA among communities through media campaigns, animation videos, social media, and publications. 3. Enhanced the knowledge and awareness of vulnerable groups of women and girls, in the affected communities on GBV, a. Scale and develop up the GBV mobile application to include more features such as online counselling, connect to helpline.b. Conduct 150 awareness-raising sessions on GBV and available GBV services among 3,000 women and girls at high risk of GBV. c. Produce series of Videos to be broadcasted in the social media related to the legal and psychosocial and mental health to GBV survivors. d. Disseminate SMS messages that contains tips that support GBV survivors and women at risk and contribute to their wellbeing

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

All UNFPA partners are required to follow the GBV Survivor CenteredApproach and the ‘GBV Minimum Standards in Emergencies’ thereby prioritizingsafety, respect, confidentially and do no harm, providing meaning full access toservices, ensuring accountability mechanisms are in place as well as participation andempowerment of GBV survivors. UNFPA will continue to provide capacitybuilding to partners on these principles including on GBV Minimum Standards andsafe GBV remote service delivery.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project will ensure needs of marginalized groups are taken into consideration through providing training for providers on how to consider needs of for example people with disability, through incorporating sign language within any awareness messages for example. also an assessment was done by UNFPA in 2019 to assess safe spaces for GBV survivors and how it responds to the protections needs of PwD among other groups. recommendations of this assessment will be considered in all upcoming projects.
