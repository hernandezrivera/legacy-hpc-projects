Needs

The WASH Sector Gap Analysis for the camps on September 11, 2018, shows the gaps and actual needs. For example, 76% families are having a shortage of batching cubicles in Camp-15. Also, in camp-19, 53% lacks inclusive latrines and 66% needs bathing cubicles. However, in camp-18 this gap is 7% for latrines and 65% for bathing cubicles. In camp 14, 13 and 12 the overall gap identified for bathing cubicles as 72%, 79%, and 56% respectively. Christian Aid and its partners intend to cover part of the WASH service gaps in Camp 14 and 13 (as this is not covered by any other WASH partners of the cluster) and host communities in Palongkhali union. Christian Aid is the site management agency for camp 14 and planned multi-sectoral interventions in camp 13 through DFID and other donor-funded projects. As part of the WaSH JRP 2019. CA will focus on decommissioning of latrines in Camp 13 and 14, and will construct 220 new inclusive latrines 220 in Camp 13 and will dislodge 3,119 of latrines. In camp 14, we will provide faecal sludge management supports. Furthermore, CA acknowledges the need to support the host community in line with the localization strategy, there is a gap, but no survey data is available. CA proposes to address the WASH needs of the host community at Palonkhali, Bacharchara Union in Ukhia and Teknaf by providing latrines, bathing cubicles, and hygiene promotion activities. In the initial emergency phase, many agencies in camp 13 and 14 have installed emergency and semi-permanent latrines but the current needs have shifted towards decommissioning, or improvement of these latrines. The decommissioned latrines need to be replaced by new latrines for beneficiaries those to be accessible for women and PWDs. However, the existing gap for bathing cubicles especially for women is huge. Therefore, CA will install or improve some bathing cubicles. There is also a gap in hygiene promotion activities for improving hygiene practices among the Rohingya community and host community as well. According to a survey conducted by REACH in July 2018 covering the camps, there is a lack of soap (35% of interviewed households did not have soap in their homes) for the Rohingya population, which is resulting weak hygiene practices. In the host community, the situation is almost same for hygiene practices. We propose to continue hygiene promotion activities and provision of top-up or full hygiene promotion kits for the community. Waste management system has been set up but will require scaling up and continuation of current activities as initiated by Christian Aid in camp 15. We propose to create a similar system in camp 14 and camp 13 under this response plan.
