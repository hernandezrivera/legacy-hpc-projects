Project Summary

The education of children of Tribal District South Waziristan has been disrupted due to displacement which deprived thousands of children to resume their education, In order to assist the returnee children to avail the educational facilities, PADO will be working on two-pronged strategy of demand and supply. On the demand side of education services, a comprehensive community mobilization strategy along with teachers and Taleemi Islahi Jirgas (TIJs)/School management committee capacity enhancement will be launched in targeted areas and in schools. On supply side PADO will rehabilitate damaged institutions with latrines and water facilities along with provision of recreation materials for psychosocial support, Cash stipend/incentive to encourage enrolment, provision of school supplies, hiring of teachers on contract basis and for for over-age dropped out children accelerated Learning Programme will be initiated. PADO through this project will target 3,000 returnee children (age group 3-18 years, 55% girls), 300 TIJ Members and 60 teachers (50% Women) in educational activities in order to ensure that the returnee population has access to safe, well equipped, and quality learning opportunities.

Needs

As per UNHCR (dated 31 October, 2018) all of 79,723 displaced families (100% of total displacement) successfully returned to their area of origin in Tribal District South Waziristan when their area was de-notified by FATA Secretariat and after being cleared from the miscreants. The prolonged displacement of more than 6 years have left devastating effects on mental and physical health of returning families specifically women and children. The displacement has badly affected the morale of students and teachers. Female teachers in SW have mobility issues. Necessary educational supplies are non existent in return areas. There is still a substantial number of children who are out of school and need support from the organization, community and parents to resume their education. As per the FATA Vulnerability Assessment (August-September 2017) report around half of the respondents expressed dissatisfaction with the quality of education due to lack of basic facilities, lack of teachers, teaching materials and damage to school infrastructure. In terms of damaged infrastructure, the RRU data shows that 943 educational institutions were partially and fully damaged in five tribal districts. The Education Cluster in its meeting on 20 November, 2018, has agreed on a case-load of 100,000 children (50% girls) to be supported in 2019 for continuation of education. There is a need to initiate conditional cash transfer schemes to enroll and retain children, particularly girl students in schools. The experiences shared from field underlined the need for ensuring provision of water, and sanitation facilities in schools. There is also a need for initiating a fast track or second-chance condensed education programme to support and mainstream over-age children, particularly girls, complete their primary/secondary education. The restoration of Education services is one of the priorities and interventions are, therefore, critical in areas of return because these services are an integral part of the early recovery needs of the affected populations. There is a great need to make these areas of return conducive for IDPs by improving their basic living conditions.

Activities

• Provision of safe and child-and adolescent-friendly temporary school facilities through rehabilitation of 5 damaged institutions and establishment of 5 pre-fab schools with latrines and water facilities that are gender and age sensitive;• Provision of recreation materials in 30 schools for psycho-social support to raise children’s self-esteem, reinforce resilience and provide an opportunity for self-expression, including early child development materials;• Cash stipend/incentive to encourage enrolment and retention of children at primary level, and for girls at secondary level.• Coordination with government for provision of textbooks.• Provision of school supplies (school-in-a-box), recreation kits, school bags and stationery among 30 schools.• Hiring of teachers on contract basis to reopen closed schools, if needed• Formation of school management committees for improved school-community linkages.• Accelerated Learning Programme for over-age dropped out children.
