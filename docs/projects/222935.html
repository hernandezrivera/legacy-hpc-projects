Project Summary

The AMAN Shelter/NFI Project for 2025 is a comprehensive initiative aimed at improving living conditions for Rohingya refugees in Cox's Bazar through an integrated approach under the Shelter-Camp Coordination and Camp Management (SCCCM) framework. The project focuses on constructing 300 Mid-Term Shelters (MTS) to accommodate 1,443 refugees and supporting Non-Food Item (NFI) to 4,815 individuals. Special attention is given to Extremely Vulnerable Households (EVHHs), with provisions such as solar energy systems, porter support for 200 households, and culturally appropriate NFIs, including gender-sensitive and disability-inclusive designs. 4 capacity building session in 04 camp to ensure AAP for 4815 participants and 30% contingency for emergency preparedness
In Camp-8W, 70 MTS shelters will be built across Blocks C, D, E, and F, with an average of 17 shelters per block. Similarly, in Camp-15, 75 MTS shelters will be distributed across six blocks: C, D, E, F, G, and H, at an average of 13 shelters per block. Camp-16 will see 75 MTS shelters constructed across Blocks A, B, C, and D, with an average of 18 shelters per block. Finally, Camp-17 will receive the highest number, with 80 MTS shelters built across Blocks A, B, and C, at approximately 26 shelters per block.
The project goes beyond physical infrastructure by empowering communities through capacity-building initiatives, particularly targeting women, adolescents, and elderly individuals. Training sessions will address Gender-Based Violence (GBV), social cohesion, and rights awareness. Refugee participation will be integral, with community feedback mechanisms ensuring their involvement from planning to implementation. The project also ensures accessibility for persons with disabilities, following Protection Mainstreaming and Safeguarding/PSEA guidelines.
With an estimated budget of $344,608, the project aligns with the 2025 Joint Response Plan (JRP) and sector coordination efforts, promoting a holistic response to shelter and NFI needs. AMAN emphasizes protection through safe living environments, inclusivity, and gender-responsive designs, while ensuring accountability and adaptability through continuous monitoring and collaboration with CiCs and key stakeholders. These efforts reaffirm AMAN’s commitment to fostering resilience and stability for displaced communities.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The refugee camps in Cox’s Bazar face urgent shelter and non-food item (NFI) needs, which are critical to ensuring the safety, dignity, and well-being of Rohingya refugees and the host community. One of the most pressing issues is the significant damage to shelters, with the 2024 Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) revealing that 72% of households report shelter damage, and 41% are unable to make repairs due to lack of resources and humanitarian support. These damaged shelters expose refugees, particularly women, children, and elderly individuals, to protection risks such as GBV, exposure to the elements, and physical harm. Immediate repairs are needed to address this gap, while long-term solutions, including more weather- and fire-resistant shelters, need to be scaled up, especially in high-risk areas prone to landslides and natural disasters. Moreover, the 39,000 refugees living in landslide-prone areas require specialized mitigation measures and shelter designs that can withstand extreme weather conditions. It is essential that shelter designs incorporate gender-sensitive features, such as privacy and safety, to mitigate the heightened vulnerabilities of women, girls, and persons with disabilities (PWD), who are disproportionately affected by both shelter damage and natural hazards.In addition to shelter needs, there is a critical gap in the provision of essential NFIs, such as blankets, mosquito nets, clothing, and solar lamps. These basic items are often inaccessible to vulnerable populations, leaving them exposed to harsh conditions and further increasing their vulnerability to GBV and exploitation. For example, solar lamps are especially important to enhance safety after dark, reducing the risk of violence and allowing women and girls to move about more freely and safely in their shelters. Similarly, hygiene kits need to be gender- and age-appropriate, including essential items such as sanitary pads for women and girls, and assistive devices for people with disabilities. Vulnerable groups, including single-headed households, elderly individuals, and those with physical impairments, often struggle to access or use essential NFIs without tailored support, and as such, these populations must be prioritized in distribution plans.Furthermore, site planning and infrastructure improvements are crucial to addressing the overall overcrowding and space constraints within the camps. Overcrowded shelters and communal spaces not only compromise the privacy and security of refugees but also increase the risks of GBV. The ongoing advocacy for two-story facility structures and the consolidation of sector services into multi-functional hubs can help maximize space, improve accessibility, and provide critical services more efficiently. These facility hubs could integrate health, nutrition, protection, and education services under one roof, significantly improving service delivery. However, these solutions must also consider the unique needs of women, girls, and persons with disabilities, ensuring that safe spaces are provided for women's health services, privacy in shelters, and appropriate accommodations for persons with special needs. AMAN’s focus on protection mainstreaming, community engagement, and the collection of disaggregated data on gender, age, and disability will ensure that the most marginalized and vulnerable populations are included in the design, implementation, and monitoring of all shelter and NFI interventions. By prioritizing these gender-responsive and inclusive approaches, AMAN seeks to provide safe, dignified living conditions and protection for all refugees fostering social cohesion and long-term resilience in the camps.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

For the 2025 Joint Response Plan (JRP), protection mainstreaming will remain a cornerstone of AMAN's Shelter and NFI response. The project will prioritize vulnerable groups such as women, children, persons with disabilities (PWDs), and elderly refugees to ensure that all activities are gender-sensitive, age-appropriate, and disability-inclusive. Gender and age considerations will be embedded into shelter designs, ensuring privacy, security, and safety, particularly for female-headed households (FHHs). Special attention will be given to gender-specific NFI distributions, such as sanitary items for women and assistive devices for PWDs and the elderly. Training on gender-based violence (GBV) and child protection will empower women and adolescent girls, reducing vulnerabilities and raising awareness of available services. In terms of disability inclusion, shelters will be designed with universal accessibility, including ramps and wider doors, while assistive devices like wheelchairs and hearing aids will be provided. The project will ensure that persons with disabilities and elderly refugees can access services and participate in community life.Community participation is central, with consultations and feedback mechanisms to ensure the response is responsive to the needs of all affected groups. AMAN will collect disaggregated data on gender, age, and disability to ensure interventions are tailored appropriately.Finally, coordination with CiCs, the Shelter/NFI sector, and PSEA networks will ensure a coherent and accountable response, including safe spaces and confidential support for survivors of GBV and SEA.

2.3 - Age & Disability

AMAN’s SCCCM project prioritizes the cross-cutting issues of age and disability, ensuring full alignment with the 2025 JRP Guidance Note. The project ensures the active participation of adolescents, elderly individuals, and persons with disabilities (PWDs) in site planning and camp management. At least 3% of camp facilities, including community centers and latrines, will be made accessible for PWDs, with special attention to the LGBTQI community. AMAN’s approach incorporates safeguarding and PSEA policies, protecting vulnerable groups, especially children, from GBV and exploitation. The project also emphasizes safe spaces, confidential reporting mechanisms, and collaboration with site management and camp stakeholders to ensure the safety of all residents. In addition, the project integrates health, hygiene, and MHPSS services to support vulnerable groups, particularly PWDs and the LGBTQI community, enhancing their empowerment and inclusion within camp life. Through these efforts, AMAN ensures that the needs of all age and ability groups are met, fostering a safe, inclusive environment in the camps.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

AMAN’s SCCCM project prioritizes Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP), fully aligned with the 2025 JRP Guidance Note.Information, Communication, and Transparency: AMAN ensures regular information dissemination to stakeholders, including camp residents, site management, and local partners. Clear communication channels will be maintained, with transparency around feedback and complaints procedures.Participation, Engagement, and Inclusion: The project actively involves refugees in camp management using the Do No Harm framework, fostering an inclusive and participatory environment for all community members, including vulnerable groups.Complaint and Feedback Mechanism: AMAN will establish clear and accessible feedback and complaint mechanisms, ensuring that concerns are addressed quickly and transparently.Learning and Adaptation: The project emphasizes continuous learning, incorporating feedback to adapt and improve camp management practices to better serve the affected populations.In summary, AMAN’s SCCCM project fosters transparency, inclusive participation, and responsive feedback mechanisms to strengthen accountability and ensure the needs of the affected population are met.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

AMAN’s SCCCM project prioritizes the safety, dignity, and well-being of beneficiaries by embedding Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) principles into all aspects of camp management. The project aligns with the 2025 JRP Guidance Note on PSEA mainstreaming and takes the following key actions: Policies and Guidelines: Adheres to PSEA policies and participates actively in PSEA coordination meetings. Awareness and Prevention: Conducts awareness-raising for stakeholders to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse within the camp. Complaint Mechanisms: Provides accessible, confidential reporting channels for beneficiaries, ensuring timely responses to PSEA concerns. Risk Assessment and Mitigation: Regular risk assessments inform proactive measures to reduce vulnerabilities to abuse. Community Involvement: Engages community leaders, volunteers, and residents in PSEA efforts, ensuring active participation in protection activities. Accountability and Reporting: Establishes transparent reporting systems to ensure accountability and monitor PSEA practices. Feedback and Learning: Continuously improves PSEA practices based on feedback from beneficiaries and the community. PSEA as an Acceleration Tool:PSEA is not only a cross-cutting issue but a key tool for enhancing the effectiveness of SCCCM. By integrating PSEA into project design and implementation, AMAN strengthens community trust and creates a safer, more supportive environment for all.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

AMAN’s SCCCM project actively incorporates climate action through Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and Disaster Risk Management (DRM), aiming to build climate resilience in refugees of Bangladesh. The project promotes eco-friendly camp infrastructure, utilizing sustainable building materials and designs that minimize environmental impacts. Climate-responsive strategies such as early warning systems, emergency preparedness, and resilient evacuation routes are integrated into camp management to address climate-related risks like floods and storms.In addition, AMAN emphasizes environmental awareness and education within the camp, encouraging waste management, water conservation, and the use of renewable energy. Community involvement is central to this approach, with both camp residents and host communities participating in climate adaptation efforts such as tree planting and sustainable resource management. The project also incorporates climate-resilient livelihood training, equipping individuals with skills in eco-friendly industries like green energy, sustainable agriculture, and waste recycling, helping communities adapt to climate change while fostering long-term sustainability and resilience.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

The Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) plan for SCCCM focuses on safeguarding camp residents, particularly vulnerable groups, through proactive risk assessments, training, and resource readiness. The plan identifies key hazards and ensures targeted support for high-risk individuals, including children, women, and persons with disabilities. Training is provided to staff, camp management, and community members on evacuation and first aid. Emergency supplies, such as first aid kits and necessary materials, are pre-stocked. The plan includes clear evacuation routes, safe shelters, and a reliable communication system with stakeholders, including residents and external partners. It integrates child safeguarding, gender-sensitive support, and mental health care to ensure the safety and well-being of all camp residents. Following emergencies, rapid assessments and the establishment of temporary shelters ensure contingency of 30% essential services in emergency response. Regular updates and monitoring ensure the plan remains effective, fostering a resilient and safe camp environment.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

The Youth Mainstreaming Plan for SCCCM focuses on empowering young people within the Rohingya Refugee/FDMN community by involving them in decision-making, skill-building, and leadership roles. Youth representatives will actively participate in camp management and advisory groups, providing valuable input on shelter and community services. They will also undergo training in leadership, vocational skills, and life skills, preparing them to serve as peer mentors and community facilitators. The plan emphasizes youth protection, incorporating safeguarding policies, mental health support, and the creation of safe spaces. Through collaboration with community leaders and stakeholders, the project promotes youth inclusion, resilience, and empowerment, ensuring that young people play an active role in shaping the camp environment.
