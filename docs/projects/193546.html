Project Summary

The proposed action is designed to contribute to the recovery of long-stay patients admitted at the advanced surgical departments, including limb reconstruction centre (LRC), of the Nasser medical complex (NMC) in Khan Younis, and to alleviate the socioeconomic hardship of the most vulnerable families who have one of their family members in the unit. It also aims at ensuring a safer and healthier environment for the meals preparation for patients in the NMC kitchen. Besides, it will contribute to more adapted housing conditions for persons with disabilities resulting from their injuries.
In full coordination with MoH and WHO, PUI will provide high-protein balanced meals to the long-stay patients admitted at the LRC and other orthopaedic/advanced surgery departments. PUI will cover 100 beds with 3 high-protein balanced meals/patient/day for one year (365 days). The daily cost per bed will be around 10 $. The number of the targeted patients is estimated at 2,000. PUI will also target 300 most vulnerable patients’ households through the provision of tailored unconditional cash assistance for transportation and medication costs. The assistance will enable the households, especially those who come from Gaza and North Gaza, visiting their family members in covering the cost of essential medicines without bearing further economic burdens. The average transfer value per household is of 300 USD. This amount should be able to cover the cost of transportation and medicines for a period of three months based on calculations done by PUI and its partner, Disability Representative Bodies Network (DRBN).
Besides, PUI will select 100 acutely vulnerable patients from the targeted departments with permanent disability to benefit from critical housing adaptations. This activity aims at improving accessibility and ensuing protection, safety and dignity for the patients inside their houses.
Moreover, PUI will carry out essential rehabilitation works at the central kitchen of Nasser Medical Complex (NMC) to improve the hygienic conditions and ensure healthier environment for cooking the meals delivered to the patients. It should be noted that this action and the proposed activities build on a previous project implemented by PUI in the NMC/LRC. Between January 2021 and March 2022, PUI, in close coordination with WHO and the MoH, provided 71,628 meals to more than 3,000 long-stay patients at LRC and other orthopaedic/advanced surgery departments in Nasser Medical Complex.
The intervention contributes to the achievement of the HRP2023’s 2nd strategic objective as well as the 1st health cluster’s objective (Ensure the availability of acceptable and quality essential healthcare services to vulnerable communities in the oPt). Finally, PUI will continue its close collaboration with the DRBN in raising awareness of PwDs on their rights and in referring cases when needed to other services. Continuous coordination with MoH, WHO, and the health cluster will be a key factor for the successful implementation of the planned action.
The project will take into consideration the different needs of the different age, gender and diversity (AGD) groups, in terms of nutrition (e.g. pregnant and lactating women, older persons, etc.) and in terms of economic capacities. The selection process for the aforementioned activities will be based on vulnerability, which will include reflections around gender, age, and diversity (including disability).

1 - Needs

According to the 2022 MSNA findings, among the 89.6% of households that reported accessing healthcare services in the 3 months prior to data collection, 99.7% reported encountering barriers when trying to access healthcare services (76.8% due to the high cost of services). A parallel factor aggravating these barriers in access to healthcare is the inability of the Gaza health system to support the hospitalized patients with food, notably due to the financial crisis impacting the national authorities. This is the case for Nasser Medical Complex (NMC): the Responsible of the Nutrition Department at NMC has confirmed that the hospital will not be in position to provide any kind of food for the inpatient departments, including limb-reconstruction (LR) patients. Yet, for those patients in particular, the access to nutrient ingredients is essential to the healing process. According to the NMC Nutrition Department, this is linked to the following factors: - LR patients are suffering from severe soft and bone tissue loss. The regeneration of tissue requires large amounts of nutrient substances, in regular provision; - Bone tissue is the slowest growing tissue in the human body; no more than 1mm per month, in optimal nutritional conditions, for non-smoking patients. Insufficiency of adequate proteins and calcium has a direct impact on this growth process. - Health experts highlighted the importance of balanced diet before and after surgery and it is vital that LR patients have sufficient calcium, vitamin C, vitamin D, and protein in daily diet for their recovery. - For an optimal patients’ care, balanced feeding is of paramount importance to the healing process. Standardization of nutrition secures faster recovery and decreases possible complications. Consequently, each LR patient requires 3 high-protein meals on a daily basis with an average cost of 3 meals / day / patient of around 10 $US. Adding up to those demanding medical needs, specific socio-economic factors further exacerbate the vulnerability of those patients and their households: - Families and relatives are generally taking on the responsibility to provide food for the patients. The provision of the food itself, with its nutritional requirements, represents a significant economic burden, especially when the patients are often the main bread-winner for the family (notably for those injured while working in the ARA). - According to the hospital’s registries, most of the LR patients are originated from Gaza city and the north of the Gaza Strip. Families and relatives live far away, and transport fees to reach Khan Younis can average 20 NIS (5USD)/visit, making regular visits to the patients a continuous challenge. Transportation might also represent an additional challenge for women, girls and PwD, due to social norms and lack of adapted services. - The average Length of Stay (LOS) for LR patients and other surgical care patients is longer compared to the LOS of other departments’ patients, adding a disproportionate burden of care to the patients’ families. While the provision of the food to the patients would lighten the economic burden, those families still need to be able to come to visit the patients regularly, to support them and provide them with various basic items. Moreover, the time dedicated to hospital visits further restricts access to livelihood opportunities for household members able to work. The most vulnerable families are therefore in need of cash assistance to meet the additional transportation and medical expenses. During the needs assessment phase, the Ministry of Health and NMC Nutrition Department highlighted the urgent need to also include in the assistance other patients through the provision of meals, notably in orthopedic and advanced surgical care departments at the NMC, as those face the same type of needs and challenges as the LR patients. This was also confirmed during the HNO/HRP2023 workshops. Moreover, many of the recovered patients of the LRC and other surgical departments may have remanent disability from their injuries. Upon leaving the hospital, they will need to adapt to their new capacities, and resume their lives in their households, so critical housing adaptations are direly needed to ensure safe and easy access to different facilities inside and outside the house. Finally, during the field visits carried out PUI’s team in its previous project, it was noticed that the structure, infrastructure, and equipment at the central kitchen of NMC are very old and worn-out. PUI, in consultation with the NMC engineering department, managed to carry out a few urgent repairs in the framework of its previous project. However, critical rehabilitation works still need to be carried out at the kitchen to improve the its conditions in terms of health and safety for cooking patients’ meals.

2 - Activities

PUI will collaborate with Disabilities Representative Bodies Network (DRBN) to implement the activities. DRBN was involved in the design of the action, and will have a direct role in implementing, monitoring, and evaluating the planned activities. The intervention contributes to the achievement of the HRP 2023 2nd strategic objective, as well as the 1st health cluster’s objective. • Activity 1: Provision of high-protein balanced meals to the patients admitted at the LRC and other advanced surgery departments PUI will provide high-protein balanced meals to patients admitted at the LRC and advanced surgery departments in NMC. The activity aims at ensuring a healthy diet that contributes to speeding up the recovery of the patients after the advanced surgical operations. Through this activity, PUI will target 100 beds (around 2,000 patients per year, disaggregated by gender) for a period of 1 year (365 days) with the daily cost per patient/bed is 10 USD. The targeted patients will receive three (3) daily balanced meals. The meals will be cooked in the central kitchen of NMC according to the feeding plan adopted by MoH’s central nutrition committee. PUI will sign a contract with a supplier to deliver daily the needed raw materials to MoH’s central warehouses. Implementing partner: DRBN, working under the umbrella of the As Salam Sport Club for PwDs. Intervention areas: All GS governorates. • Activity 2: Provision of tailored cash assistance for transportation and medicines PUI and its partner (DRBN) will target the most vulnerable households who have long-stay patients at the LRC and other advanced surgical care departments in NMC through a tailored cash assistance for transportation and medications. 300 households (1,650 individuals; 813 females, 836 males, 71 PwDs) will benefit from the intended support. Each targeted household will receive unconditional cash assistance totaling to 300 USD, divided into three monthly payments, to cover critical health-related needs, and better cope with the situation resulting from the injury (including temporary or permanent disability). The assistance will also enable the vulnerable HHs (especially those who live far away from the NMC) to visit the patients during their hospitalization period through covering the transportation costs. The most vulnerable households will be identified by PUI and DRBN using the unified vulnerability assessment tool (UVAT), prioritizing the following groups: one HH member admitted at LRC and/or other advanced surgical care departments; no regular source of income; large HHs with high dependency ratio; HHs with PwDs or chronically-ill persons; HHs living in substandard housing units; and female-headed HHs and child-headed HHs. In addition, PUI will coordinate and share the initial lists of beneficiaries with all the relevant key actors working in the health, food security, and cash sectors in order to ensure that there is no overlapping or duplication in the provision of assistance before issuing the list of beneficiaries. Once the final list of BNFs is issued, PUI will disburse the payments to the beneficiary HHs through either Bank of Palestine cheques or PALPAY (cashing out from 350 accredited agents using PIN code). The selection of the most appropriate payment mechanism will be mainly based on the size of the caseload to be supported. Following the provision of assistance, PUI’s MEAL officer will conduct a Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) with a representative sample of to capture utilization patterns of the cash assistance and to measure the effectiveness of the cash distribution process. Intervention areas: All GS governorates (with a focus on North Gaza, Gaza, and Deir Al Balah) • Activity 3: Critical housing adaptations for PwDs PUI and DRBN will target 100 households (550 individuals; 271females, 279 males, 100 PwDs) of LRC/NMC patients with disabilities due their injuries through carrying out critical rehabilitation and adaptation works at their housing units. This will ensure an adapted, safe, dignified and healthy environment for those individuals. PwDs will be targeted with a special focus on the patients with new disabilities who are still receiving ambulatory medical treatment at the LRC. The housing adaptations will be implemented through a contractor under the direct supervision of the technical staff from PUI and its partner. The average cost of proposed adaptions is 2,000 USD per beneficiary knowing that these adaptions include but are not limited to installing handrails/grab rails, constructing small ramps, widening doors, replacing a bath with a level access shower, fitting lower work surfaces to make kitchen easier to use, and other internal modifications. It is worth mentioning that the BoQs for the housing adaptations will be prepared in full consultation with the persons with disabilities and their families. Continuous coordination and consultation will also be made with the shelter cluster, and Humanity and Inclusion (HI) to ensure that the adaptation works are implemented in accordance with standard and specifications. Intervention areas: All GS governorates. • Activity 4: Essential rehabilitation of the central kitchen at NMC PUI, in full coordination with the engineering department at NMC, will carry out essential rehabilitation and maintenance works in the central kitchen of the complex. The average cost of the proposed rehabilitation works is estimated at 25,000 USD. The activity aims at ensuing a safer and healthier environment for cooking the meals for the patients at NMC and especially those who are admitted at LRC and advanced surgical care departments. Thousands of patients will benefit from the intended rehabilitation works. PUI will identify a contracting company to implement the rehabilitation works according the bill of quantities and technical specifications agreed-upon by PUI’s technical team and the representatives of the engineering department.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

• In-kind food distribution:The in-kind support is done through contracting a supplier selected by a committee composed of PUI, WHO and MoH to deliver the raw materials to the central kitchen of NMC. The decision to use in-kind was taken in 2019 in close collaboration with the LRC and other partners. Cooking the meals at NMC allows the hospital nutritionist to closely supervise the quality and ensure a balanced diet. • Cash: Cash modality is recommended by the affected population and the humanitarian actors as long as the markets in the affected areas are functioning, the financial service providers are operating with an adequate capacity, and goods are available. According to MSNA (July, 2022), 91.8% of households reported a preference for cash and voucher assistance in any modality. In Gaza, goods are available in the local market, but the affected population has lost the means to buy them. Cash transfers can be securely provided to households. Evidences showed that cash transfers, in GS and other contexts, are often more efficient and effective than other forms of aid. It provides affected people with the means and flexibility to decide and prioritize. Moreover, it also supports the local markets and contribute to boosting local economy. Cash transfers are effective in addressing negative coping strategies such as poor diets and debts. PUI is focusing in its assistance to enable patients’ families paying the transport fees to visit the patients during hospitalization period, the cash assistance will be also used cover other urgent health and non-health needs. • Service delivery (implementing rehabilitation works through contractor): Based on PUI’s experience during the last 12 years, contractor-driven approach is the most appropriate option for implementing the rehabilitation works at the institutions and/or the houses of PwDs as it allows for more control on the quality of the implemented works.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

PUI has started working in the Hospital in 2019, in close collaboration with the medical staff. The Action’s primary goal is to respond to the affected population’s needs, expressed and identified during the needs assessment phase. Feedbacks received from the patients and the partners in 2021 were a great source of information; e.g: the idea of cooking directly in the hospital kitchen instead of doing food delivery improved the quality control during the meals preparation phase. This modification suggested by the nutritionist and PUI MEAL staff had a direct impact on the patients’ satisfaction. All relevant feedback and recommendations will be directly shared with the project manager and relevant staff for improvement. Feedback received through the FRM will be documented and shared in writing, via email, between the MEAL Officer and the program team (in a secured and confidential manner). Decisions and steps taken will be duly reported as well, until the closure of the case. More informal feedback received by any PUI staff in the field will be dealt with depending on its nature: either through oral response to complainants, or through the opening of dedicated case file, as described above. PUI MEAL team will pay attention that feedback, whether positive or negative, is collected and followed up on by PUI representatives independent from project team and be dealt with respect for the principles of confidentiality and privacy, without affecting the possibility to receive assistance in the future. Monthly internal reporting will include analysis of the received feedbacks, as well as during the yearly strategy exercises. Project modalities or objectives will be discussed with the program and technical team, in order to better respond to the needs and concerns of the served population.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

PUI uses various two-way communication channels: oral, written, easy to read, face to face, via email and through meetings; to ensure that feedback and complaints mechanism is easily accessible by all beneficiaries including people with specific needs such as PwDs and children. These communication channels include (but are not limited to) feedback boxes, hotline number, email, and the pages of the organization on social media. PUI designed a kids-friendly and interactive hand-out card including the hotline number, drawings, and painting exercises to increase their awareness regarding their rights, and to encourage them in complaining in case they faced a misconduct/misbehavior from humanitarian workers or the community. Additional publications were designed to the same purpose i.e. posters and leaflets. During all the beneficiary selection processes, orientation meetings with the beneficiaries, visits to individual houses in the field, and visits to the LRC/NMC, PUI’s team systematically provides detailed information on the feedback and complaints mechanism, sharing the details of the contact person FRM leaflets are handed out and the process is also explained orally, to account for those who cannot read. In the context of this project, PUI will disseminate the information on the FRM through the NMC hospital management and medical teams as well, briefing them on its existence and functioning and advising them to inform patients. PUI team will be handing out cards, posters, and other means of information dissemination regarding PSEAH and the FRM mechanism, ensuring cultural appropriateness and inclusivity. Meanwhile, feedbacks may also be provided verbally in the field, through text messages, email or phone (using the dedicated address and phone number provided on information cards). The MEAL team will be providing an initial feedback in less than 24 hrs.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

PUI has a country level Protection and Gender Mainstreaming strategy as well as a global PUI Protection Mainstreaming framework to reflect on the type of actions required on each stage of the project cycle to ensure protection and gender principles are adequately mainstreamed. Based on this strategy and on the planned community consultations, a project specific Protection and Gender Mainstreaming action plan will be developed once the project started. A protection and gender risk and mitigation measures analysis will be conducted for the project. The analysis will be further refined and triangulated with the solicitation of direct feedback from the target community, regarding the perceived protection risks, needs and capacity. The analysis will have special focus on the risks of gender-based violence (GBV) and challenges for people with disability. According to the outcomes of this initial Protection and Gender risk analysis, project specific and nuanced mitigation measures will be adopted to respond to the identified risks and gaps. Project beneficiaries in need of more specialized protection or psychosocial support (PSS) services will be provided with safe and timely referrals to other dedicated service providers. In order to ensure safe protection referrals, a protection and PSS service mapping for the area of operation will be accessed or drafted. At the same time, PUI staff members will be trained on “Safe Protection Referrals” training module, to include “Communicating and responding to GBV disclosures”. To ensure “Do-no-harm”, PUI will first keep on consulting any available studies on gender and power dynamics within the communities or facilities it is working in. Then, PUI makes an effort to consult groups that are traditionally excluded from social dialog about the safest way to include them. Finally, PUI ensures the promotion of key human rights, the rights of people with disabilities and of the humanitarian mandate messages to the targeted communities. PUI cares for the safety of the location of the implementation/distribution and ensure the distribution point/implementation premises is accessible for different groups of people and make special arrangement for people with specific vulnerabilities (e.g. Providing transportation, home distribution, etc.) and follow the safety procedures concerning COVID-19 and provides personal protective equipment and considers precautionary safety measures, including physical distancing and hygiene promotion.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Through the use of interviews, focus groups and surveys, PUI aims to involve vulnerable groups and people with specific needs in all aspects of the project cycle and promote the general concept of age and gender diversity (AGD) in selecting the beneficiaries (BNF). PUI will also ensure assessment methodology complies to the standards and includes protection considerations; put additional effort to include representative from all different groups within the targeted community; ensure the assessment tools are accessible for different groups of people; conduct vulnerability mapping and socioeconomic survey to all the potential BNFs, and besides promotes the Disability Mainstreaming through the project’s cycle. Moreover, PUI has a specific budget and human resources dedicated to protection mainstreaming, and thus is able to ensure support for different people and to offer them equal access to our services. Accessibility is not only physical but psychological as well, thus, all PUI staff received several trainings on different disciplines (such as Protection Mainstreaming, PSEAH, safe inter-sectorial referral, child safeguarding and child protection) in order to ensure that they can promote and defend inclusion in our day-to-day implementation. Regarding PSEAH specifically, PUI has strengthened its SEAH reporting mechanism with the opening of our whistleblowing mechanism to others and different channels for beneficiaries, staff, and other stakeholders. Accessible and clear awareness raising and key messaging on PSEAH to project beneficiaries come to complete the scheme. Besides, PUI staff members will also be further trained on age, gender and diversity (AGD) mainstreaming, psychological first aid, safe referrals, GBV and inclusion of people with disabilities.
