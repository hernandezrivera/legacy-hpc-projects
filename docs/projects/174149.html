Project Summary

In 2021, Mercy Corps will continue to implement a comprehensive WASH intervention in LGAs of operations in Borno state, Nigeria – covering five sub sectors (Water Supply, Sanitation, Hygiene promotion, WASH NFIs and Environmental Health) and COVID-19 mitigation measures/risk reduction. Under the sanitation component, Mercy Corps aims to ensure that the affected communities in new displacement sites and host communities have full access to improved sanitation facilities that are secure, sanitary, user friendly, and gender-appropriate, Mercy Corps will ensure providing Safe clean water, Promotion of sustainable Hygiene best practices and behavior change, Environmental management, vector control, capacity building and good governance. Furthermore, to mitigate the depletion of the water table in the areas of operation, Mercy Corps will build on the existing groundwater monitoring in other camps and other LGAs and strengthen the local capacity to monitor the groundwater level through the installation of data loggers in hybrid water supply systems.
The Primary purpose of Mercy Corps WASH intervention is to reduce morbidity, mortality and malnutrition from Water- borne diseases through safe and appropriate access to clean drinking water, hygiene promotion, Waste management, distribution WASH NFI’s and appropriate access to sanitation infrastructure and facilities and.
Mercy Corps WASH intervention primarily targets the affected population of Internally Displaces Persons (IDP’s), returnee and Host communities

1 - Needs

The continuation of conflict in Northeast Nigeria has created a complex humanitarian crisis and has created a great shocks and high level of displaces of IDPs in to the camps. The congested high population and limited WASH services have dramatically affected access to key water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services in Northeast Nigeria. Supply of safe water and provision of improved sanitation facilities in IDP camps and host communities in the areas of our operation is still a challenge due to high demand of WASH services by existing residents (IDP’s & Host communities), putting greater pressure on already limited water points within the IDP camps and host communities. Because of high demand and limited WASH facilities/services, Women and girls still spend a long-time queuing at the water point, the sphere standard of 15 liters of water per person per day are not yet met in most of LGA’s of our operation, the number of individuals sharing a drop-hole latrine in camps (exceeding the minimum standard of 50 people per drop-hole latrine). Poor hygiene practices couples with outbreak of Covid- 19 has created another dimension of acute humanitarian needs. In addition, the critical nutrition needs serve as an additional indicator for the urgent need to improve WASH conditions. This has led to high morbidity, mortality and malnutrition rates among the affected communities

2 - Activities

MC WASH activities shall be addressing five key thematic areas of: 1) Supply of Safe clean water, 2) Provision of improved sanitation facilities and 3) Hygiene promotion&COVID-19 mitigation and risk reduction 4) Environmental Health/ Solid waste management and Ground water monitoring 1) Supply of safe clean water: i) Mercy Corps will rehabilitate or construct water points to meet the needs of IDPs and other vulnerable populations in Ngala, Damboa, Gwoza,Bama and Dikwa LGAs. These activities will take place in both IDP camps and host communities through contract driven approach. ii) Formations and training of WASHCOMs for sustainability: Sustainable water sources access will not be possible unless communities themselves take the responsibility for the operation and maintenance of the rehabilitated/constructed water points. Mercy Corps, will ensure the sustainability of the intervention by reviving and/or selecting and training members of Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene Committees (WASHCOMs), pump mechanics and pump attendants from within the affected communities. iii) Water Quality Measures: MC will ensure that the water provided for communities meets acceptable WHO drinking water standards of zero E.Coli contamination per 100 ml of water sample. MCs’ WASH technical team will take water samples from various water sources, household water containers, water vendors (donkey carts and water trucks) for water quality testing. The key parameters to be monitored are (E-coli form, PH, Turbidity, Free residual chlorine, Total chlorine, temperature and other dissolved chemical parameters. iv) Safe clean Water trucking for new arrivals and IDP’s; MC will conduct water trucking for the new arrivals to support the already stressed existing water points to try meet the high demand of safe water for the new arrivals. Water trucking will be conducted through contract driven approach. MC WASH technical team will ensure that the water supply through trucking is of good quality and will monitor the quality of water (Water treatment and monitoring the free chlorine residual) 2) Access to improved sanitation facilities . To address identified sanitation gaps in IDP Camps in Dikwa, Ngala, Bama, Damboa and Gwoza LGAs, Mercy Corps will ensure that the affected communities have full access to improved sanitation facilities that are secure, sanitary, user friendly, and gender appropriate in all the targeted IDP camps and host communities by supporting the construction &rehabilitation of latrines. For host community settings, Mercy Corps will construct single stance latrines that are to be shared by three households each. In camp and camp-like settings, Mercy Corps will construct blocks of emergency communal latrines (VIP latrines), and shower shelters through contract driven approach. 3) Hygiene promotion: To deliver the hygiene messaging, MC will make use of hygiene promoters conducting hygiene campaigns in both IDP camps and host communities in the targeted LGAs of Gwoza, Dikwa, Ngala, Bama and Damboa. The CHP's will be selected in the target locations and receive a thorough five-day training at the beginning of the program. The trained CHP will conduct door-to-door hygiene sensitization in both IDP camps and host communities. ii) /COVID-19 & Cholera preparedness response plan, prevention intervention and awareness: Mercy Corps intervention will be cholera preparedness, COVID-19 mitigation& Risk reduction, respond plan; community awareness, prevention intervention that will contribute to Health and WASH cluster objectives by including activities related to surveillance, case management, capacity building, infection prevention and control. To promote environmental Health, Mercy Corps will form Sanitation Teams to clear the drainage channels from debris and accumulated waste-materials blocking them, thus eliminating both potential and actual persistent standing water sites. MC will conduct groundwater monitoring in its area of operations.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Mercy Corps will ensure that protection is mainstreamed, that there is gender equity, and inclusion of people living with disabilities and older people throughout WASH facilities design and construction activities. Mercy Corps is prepared to build ramps at the blocks latrines to help facilitate the movement of people in wheelchairs/hand-pedaled bicycle. All water facility designs will allow easy access of persons with disabilities and older people. For example, water taps stand will have ramps. Women and girls often have to walk long distances to fetch water, or wait a long time in queues to receive water, increasing their time poverty and exposure to sexual violence and abuse. To minimize these risks, Mercy Corps will involve women, girls, boys and men in all consultations and assessments to identify safety and security risks, and to identify locations for water points and sanitation facilities that are accessible, nearer and low risk of harassment and abuse while taking into consideration the safest pathways.
