Project Summary

The project will undertake preventative and responsive activities to provide services to 7,500 direct beneficiaries households and an indirect population of estimated 45,000(average HH sizes of 6 persons). The project will also seek to support 200 GBV/other protection cases and form 20 Community Working Groups of 12 persons each in order to strengthen community resilience. The main focus of the response is on GBV and Child Protection targeting the needs of women, girls, boys and men of Internally Displace Persons, poor host community and the Somali Voluntary Returnees from the region (Kenya, Ethiopia, Djibouti and Yemen) in Kismayu, Baidao and Mogadishu areas. The project will seek to reduce the incidences of GBV predominantly reported in IDP sites throughout the country. GBV, particularly sexual violence, is a key protection concern for women and girls in Somalia. The project will also reduce cases of child abuse especially forced recruitment of children into armed groups, abduction of children by armed non state actors. Therefore, the project will establish child protection structures in target districts to improve case management and psychosocial support which is required for the resilience of the children. Due to continuous displacement, the project will support family tracing for unaccompanied children, case identification, referral services and protection messaging. Through coordination with other actors, the project seeks to create a protection conducive environment with increased accountability to Affected Populations and Protection Mainstreaming across all sectors.

Needs

The absence of protection environment in Somalia remain the greatest humanitarian need throughout 2018. Lack or limited governmental authority and near continuous conflict throughout many parts of the country, coupled with rampant impunity and recurrent draughts has resulted in mass displacement of people both within Somalia and to the neighboring countries. Central Somalia remains vulnerable to conflict and displacements and the prevalence of protection violations is high, this includes forced displacement, gender-based violence, child rights violations, arbitrary detention, forced recruitment particularly of children, unlawful deportation, forced evictions, forced relocation, and family separation. Women, children and minority groups are the most vulnerable and they do not always have equal access to humanitarian assistance. The IDPs who live in the vast unprotected and unregulated IDP camps across the country face the greatest risk. According to our assessments in Kismayo, Galkayo and Baidoa Gender based violence affecting women and girls remains prevalent yet under reported. GBV cases related to intimate partner violence, especially domestic violence and deprivation of resources, continue to be the most reported type of GBV. Under reporting for fear of retribution, stigmatization is an issue. Gaps exist in basic service provision for survivors, including post rape care, psychosocial support, and comprehensive survivor centered care, including case management. Child abuse cases have been reported to increase during difficult transitional periods such as displacement movements due drought and conflict. These children estimated to be making over 60% of the displaced populations continue to face child protection risks unless supported. The risks include neglect, exploitation including recruitment into armed groups and early marriages as well as physical violence are sometimes perpetrated by people known to these children.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The project design takes into account that durable solutions are not able to be achieved for all protracted situations, hence the need to start re-building the resilience of affected communities. Mercy Corps through this project target to strengthen the resilience of affected groups such as children and women through the preventive approach. It also complements the other more resilience oriented projects implemented by Mercy Corps in targeted districts.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The project will directly target areas receiving or hosting displaced households in order to mitigate the risk of exclusion based on displacement. The project seeks to address the needs of the households displaced and the selection criteria will target those households displaced. To respond to the needs of migrating vulnerable populations, the project is designed in a flexible manner to meet their protection needs. During the targeting process local authority might influence on the selection of locations due to clan nepotism or particular interest and this way risk of exclusion of selection of the most disaster/displacement affected locations may be experienced. To mitigate this risk, Mercy Corps will adopt vulnerability-based targeting with community input approach. In addition, at the onset of the project a comprehensive community mobilization and sensitization activities will be undertaken focusing on the three pillars of; Information sharing, participation and complaint & response mechanism. This process will enable all community members including displaced communities to be fully aware of the project which will be implemented in their community. During targeting priority will be given to displaced women, children and girls.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Mercy Corps will ensure the process of targeting and selection of beneficiaries will ensure that marginalized groups are adequately represented in selection committees that follow some agreed vulnerability criteria. Following the HCT’s centrality of protection strategy, targeting of settlements and selection of beneficiaries will especially look out for, and aim to include women, girls and children who are the most vulnerable. This includes having them represented in beneficiary (or settlements’) representation committees that make decisions on behalf of beneficiaries IDPs in protracted situations, ensuring the involvement of women, children and girls in project activities. Confidentiality remains central for the survivor ours of GBV and abused children.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The target populations remain concerned with insecurity which caused their displacement and it continue to affect them in different ways including the risk of exclusion from accessing humanitarian assistance due to limited access and perceived risk by aid agencies. To mitigate this, Mercy Corps has updated security and safety plans in place to prevent and respond to threats. Mercy Corps works closely with UN agencies, local partner organizations, and stakeholders to share security information, coordination, and joint security emergency response including evacuation and relocation. Furthermore, Mercy Corps has strong community networks at the local level and in their respective work areas. Mercy Corps also has a well-established field office and a strong community network across the regions for many years which can be instrumental in ensuring the safety and security of staff and project assets as well as the overall coordination hub for activities. Additionally, Mercy Corps will instruct field staff to take the requisite field security trainings. This project will put deliberate efforts to increase most vulnerable groups’ participation and influence in contexts of peace, conflicts and other humanitarian crisis. To mitigate impact of armed conflict and violence particularly on the most vulnerable groups (women, children, girls etc.) the project will seek to collaborate with communities to foster peaceful co-existence and advocate for behavior change during community gatherings.
