Project Summary

"AAR Japan and NGO Forum for Public Health will continue to provide its protection services based on the achievements and learning of the existing intervention focusing on Child protection facilities. There are two child friendly space, one in Camp-25, Alikhali, and one in camp-27, Jadimura, that are run by AAR Japan and NGO Forum for Public Health since December, 2018. These CFSs will continue to operate offering various recreational activities, PFA, Psychosocial support, counseling services, non-formal education and sports amenities for children in order to recover from trauma or to avoid protection risk of the children. The facilities will also work to prioritize children’s voice and empowerment at the center of the CP interventions by forming Child safeguarding committee and assigned child safeguarding representative to each center to assure children’s participation in this committee and to facilitate spaces where children can contribute with their opinions and feedbacks. The facilities will continue activities like parents meeting, CBCPC meetings where the parents of the children or members of the community can present their feedbacks and expectations from the CFS. A separate Complaint and response mechanism with a hotline number (free) has been already running since June, 2019 for complaints and feedback receiving on protection services along with awareness on the CRM facility to the children, their parents and community members. AAR Japan and NGO Forum for Public Health are committed to oblige Child safeguarding and PSEA policy and practice and it will reflect through maintaining the CRM and assurance of community participation. AAR Japan and NGO Forum for Public Health have been continuing their Coordination with Child protection sub-sector, GBV sub-sector and protection working group. In the camp level as well, NGO Forum is the GBV Focal for Camp-25 and coordination with other child protection intervention organizations, site management, GoB and other stakeholders are well maintained in camp-25 and camp-27. The coordination with other child protection intervention organizations are regularly maintained so the PSS support of all facilities can continue to utilize the referral pathway in case of requiring professional care that are not available in the facilities. A total of 200 registered refugee children will be supported in the CFS while all children within the catchment area of the CFS are allowed to visit and receive all kind of support available in the CFS, including the children of host community. The project aims for community capacity building through facilitating awareness sessions within the center and outreach for the parents and community, through sports competitions, dramas, CBCPC meetings and parents meetings. Gender and inclusion is mainstreamed in the organizational policies of AAR Japan and NGO Forum for public health, which indicates that all the activities of this project will have a mainstreamed gender and inclusion approach. The project will also focus on sustainability, DRR, capacity building of the staffs, community engagement and aims to ensure that by the end of the project, the refugee community in the target area have access to adequate child protection services as well as the host community has access to services like WASH and livelihood.
Purpose: To facilitate dignified child protection services for crisis affected Rohingya refugee communities.
Total beneficiary will be 9259 including 200 registered children.
"

Needs

"For decades, the Rohingyas from Myanmar have sought protection from Bangladesh in reaction to different waves of violence at home. On 25th August 2017, due to renewed violence which erupted in rakhine state, there was again a massive influx of people including women, elderly, PWDs and children fleeing and crossing the border to seek safety and shelter in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh. Now, Bangladesh hosts about 913,988 (UNHCR, 2019) individual Rohingyas from Myanmar who are living in 32 congested camps of Teknaf and Ukhiya of Cox’s bazar district. Most of the new arrivals sought shelter in makeshift settlements and host communities creating new spontaneous settlements around the existing camps. The government has given additional land to accommodate most if not all the refugees in the extension areas of existing camps, collectively considered as the world’s largest refugee camp, where many of the displaced people depend entirely on assistance for basic needs. The scale and speed of the displacement and the intense vulnerability of those displaced has created one of the most urgent, critical, challenging and complex humanitarian crises globally. With no legal status, the Rohingya population is one of the most vulnerable groups in Bangladesh particularly exposed to violence and denied fulfillment of basic needs. A high proportion of Rohingya refugees remain in a chronic emergency situation due to long term challenges to find a durable solution to their status in Myanmar and Bangladesh and therefore require continued humanitarian assistance. The refugee population including the children has faced and borne witness to physical beatings, torture, rape and other forms of violence. Violence and displacement exacerbate personal challenges and compound social, economic and environmental barriers to access basic services. While discussing these needs, it is also crucial to consider different vulnerabilities including female-headed HH, children, and elderly, PWDs and/or young males and single females. Existing support network and coping mechanisms formed by vulnerable populations of Rohingya community were already disrupted due to the crisis, intensifying feelings of disempowerment, dependency and distress, potentially amplifying situations of disadvantage and exclusion. Amongst this population, 9497 individual Rohingyas are living in camp 25 and 14,269 in camp 27 of Teknaf upazila. In Teknaf upazila Rohingya refugees are living amongst the host community which in recent days creates further protection risk. The majority of Rohingya children need Child protection services, especially due to the emergence of new protection risks. There is a need to scale up specialized mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) for those most severely affected remains urgent. Refugee children report of feeling unsafe and vulnerable to violence, including sexual violence. Based on result of different studies, approximately 5-10% of Rohingya households engage in child marriage and two-thirds of the household consider it as a normal practice and one-third use it as a coping strategy to either protect girls or reduce economic burdens on the household. The perceived risk of trafficking and abduction remains high including the child labor, involving children as young as 7 years old. By Sept 2019, 8,596 unaccompanied and separated children were identified and are deemed to be at risk of multiple CP risks. Access to education for adolescent boys and girls remain a challenge, with only 13% of boys and 2% of girls aged 15-18 attending learning centers; out of school children may also be exposed to further risks such as exploitative labor, drugs and criminal activities. All these issues mentioned above have a huge impact on the mental health and psychosocial well-being of the children and creates the need for MHPSS and CP support. "

Activities or outputs

Operation of Child friendly spaces (CFS) located in camp 25 and camp 27 are the major programmatic operations under this objective. A total of 200 registered refugee children will be supported through these facilities. The selection of the children for registration is based on prioritization of different vulnerability issues. The CFSs also include accessibility features for PWDs to ensure a dignified participation of all children. In CFSs, activities include a number of awareness raising on protection issues in the current context of the camp through child-friendly facilitation, dramas and other cultural activities, psychosocial support including counseling based on needs and observations mainly targeting children and adolescents will be provided. Psychosocial support in CFS includes counseling, PFA, play therapy, Cognitive behavior therapy, stress management that are designed for children and provided by expert and trained PSS counselors. The counselor will follow the procedure as per the requirement and under strong supervision and coaching of Child protection and MHPSS officer of NGO Forum for Public Health. Additionally, referral will be made following the ISCG MHPSS provided referral pathway for particular camps, in cases of specialized support required beyond CFS’s capacity. Both CFSs provides medical, GBV and Legal referral services by following the referral pathways provided by ISCG and through regularly coordinating with other Child protection agencies in the camp. The operation of CFSs maintains a safe programming approach to ensure prioritized child safeguarding and “Do no Harm”. The CFSs will continue to be operated in line with ‘Standard Operating Procedures’ created for the CP intervention of AAR Japan and NGO Forum. The regular CFS operation also includes the activity of staff capacity building on the SOP guidelines, child safeguarding and PSEA policy of AAR Japan and NGO Forum, CHS etc. AAR Japan and NGO Forum have designed regular coaching, supervision and mentoring tools for ensuring quality that are undertaken every three months. To ensure children’s leadership, a child safeguard committee has been formatted in the CFSs to ensure children’s participation in child protection related discussions. Participation of children through child safeguarding committee, risk mapping, child friendly FGDs and IDIs, from the need analysis, to project design, to operation and monitoring, has been ensured and reflected in program design as children are in the center of the program design. The monthly meeting of CBCPC committee will be held on regular basis. The CBCPC committee includes local leaders such as Majhis, Imams, Govt Authority such as CIC, parents of the children and children representative selected through the children of the CFS to ensure promotion of accountability through the members of CBCPC community in the areas they serve. Group discussions on protection awareness will be conducted in separated group sessions for male and female, with a priority to include different vulnerable groups. The issues also include DRR, awareness on environmental pollution, social cohesion. The community engagement through awareness raising on child protection issues and DRR will also be conducted in the host community including 60 females and 60 male participants from the Teknaf host community area, where AAR Japan plans to offer skill development courses in 2020. One mainstreamed activity for all the working component of AAR Japan and NGO Forum, including Child protection is ensuring a safe and accessible Complaint and response mechanisms to ensure meaningful participation of the community. NGO Forum and AAR Japan have a free hotline number and regularly maintained complaint boxes in all the facilities. AAR Japan and NGO Forum have a designated PSEA committee who are in charge of handling PSEA complaints and are mandated to maintain confidentiality and full support to the survivor.
