Project Summary

In 2024, UN estimates that nearly 9 Million people in South Sudan need Humanitarian assistance. This is due to continued sub national conflicts and devastating effects of floods arising from the above average rainfall experienced in South Sudan and an increased water flow along the River Nile from Uganda after a controlled release of Water from L. Victoria. These man-made and natural disasters have caused immeasurable loss of livelihoods such as destruction of crops causing poor harvest. Compounded with the economic hardships arising from increased market prices in 2024 across the Country, the Country has seen unspeakable human suffering due to food insecurity.
In 2025, HealthNet TPO (HNTPO) intends to implement the HRP in Aweil North, Aweil West and Raja Counties which have been classified as categories 4, 3 and 3 respectively by the IPC and health cluster. These are Counties where HNTPO is currently implementing the Health Sector Transformation project (HSTP) where only about 68% of the health facilities are supported and therefore leaving a projected population of 182,419 in 2025 as vulnerable with limited access to health services and therefore needing humanitarian assistance with essential life-saving intervention.
In meeting the needs of the vulnerable populations, HNTPO through an integrated live-saving health interventions will target a total of 98,817 vulnerable people across the 3 Counties: 40,389 in Aweil North, 48,005 in Aweil West and 10,423 in Raja with a 4-pronged approach. 1) HNTPO will support the functionality and provision of services in the Health facilities not supported through HSTP in each of the 3 Counties, 2) We will establish Mobile clinics to communities which are cut-off and/ or displaced by floods therefore taking services to them and 3) supporting emergency referral services within the counties such that severe cases can access appropriate services in the hierarchies of health service provision and 4) establishing a trained, competent and facilitated Emergency preparedness Response (EPR) team in each of the 3 Counties, with capability to identify, Investigate and respond to any epidemic outbreaks.
This intervention therefore intends to increase access to equitable, live-saving and essential health interventions to the vulnerable populations in the target Counties through an integrated package consisting of curative and preventive approaches including maternal, neonatal, child health and nutrition, sexual and reproductive health, Mental health and Psychosocial support, and care for survivors of sexual Gender Based Violence with an inclusive approach without discrimination to sex, age, disability status or any social ascriptions within the affected communities. These interventions will contribute to achieving the health cluster’s 2025 objective that vulnerable crises-affected people in South Sudan have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable, safe and dignified access to life-saving assistance to meet their needs.

1. Needs

South Sudan in 2024 is challenged with a multifaceted crisis characterized by conflict, climate-related disasters (floods and droughts), social and political instability, significant food insecurity, further exacerbated by inflation and various outbreaks of diseases of epidemic potential including Cholera, Polio, Measles, Meningitis, Hepatitis E, and yellow fever. Approximately 9 million people, representing 75% of the population, require humanitarian assistance compounded by the ongoing conflict in Sudan that triggered an influx of over 700,000 refugees, thereby intensifying needs within already vulnerable host communities, which are also home to 2 million internally displaced persons (IDPs).Catastrophic food insecurity (IPC Phase 5) is projected in Aweil North County while Aweil West and Raja County Counties are projected to be IPC 3 respectively leading to alarmingly high Malnutrition rates among women and children, with Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels exceeding the World Health Organization's (WHO) 15% threshold principally due to food insecurity, inadequate water and sanitation, high child morbidity, gender-based violence (GBV), and poor child feeding and caring practices. These conditions are expected to remain or deteriorate in 2025 particularly among affected communities including displaced people, refugees, and returnees. Summary of Risks/Hazards1. Public Health RisksIn the 3 counties, only about 68% of the total 59 Health facilities are supported and functional leaving a population of about 43% not accessing health facilities-based care within one hour of walking. The conflicts, flooding and limited access to health care services has caused many indicators Iin the health sector wanting. Specifically, Measles (72%) and Penta 3 (73%) coverages are below the average 80% expected by the national Ministry of Health. Malaria remains endemic and the leading cause of morbidity and mortality, especially amongst children under 5 years of age, often exacerbated by frequent flooding events. Women and girls in remote and crisis-affected areas face additional challenges due to, limited trained personnel, recurrent medical supply chain breaks affecting family planning commodities and essential drugs and supplies for maternal and child health services including antenatal and postnatal care, skilled delivery assistance and treatment of sexually transmitted infections and immunisation. The GBV health analysis revealed only 37.5% of supported health facilities under HSTP in the 3 Counties provide GBV care and services. Lower-level facilities struggle with staffing and infrastructure, limiting early intervention to referrals.2. Scenario Overview in 2025Climate predictions indicate that the current situation South Sudan or its effects are likely to flip into 2025 with Counties in IPC phase 5 (Aweil North) remaining in their situation while those in Phase 3 (Aweil West and Raja) probably deteriorating into IPC Phase 4 or worsening into Phase 5 as continued flooding can lead to significant displacement and food insecurity, undermining infrastructure, livelihoods, and access to essential services. Vulnerable groups such as women, children, and the elderly face heightened risks of GBV and health issues arising from unsanitary conditions. The overall humanitarian situation may further be compounded by economic challenges and the increasing number of refugees from Sudan, which strain already limited resources.In addressing the identified needs of the vulnerable populations, HNTPO intends to respond with an integrated live-saving health interventions through a 4-pronged approach. 1) HNTPO will support the functionality and provision of services in the health facilities not supported through HSTP in each of the 3 Counties, 2) We will establish Mobile clinics in communities which are cut-off and/ or displaced by floods therefore taking services to them and 3) supporting emergency referral services within the counties

2. Project activities

This intervention will contribute to reduction of excess morbidity and mortality in the targeted vulnerable communities, thereby complementing and filling gaps in the regular developmental HSTP Program. Activities will broadly fall into the priority activities of the sector, and they will be a combination of health promotion, health prevention and treatment of common conditions in the targeted communities. Specifically, we shall provide life-saving and essential healthcare services including management of non-communicable diseases in health facilities not supported through regular programs (HSTP). In addition, we intend to communities which are very far, displaced or cut out from accessing services in the supported health facilities through integrated outreaches. To support this, HNTPO will procure essential medical kits and collaborate with WHO and UNFPA for emergency kits to be distributed to affected communities. Other aspects of the program will include; training of health workers on epidemic disease surveillance, prevention and control including outbreak detection, investigation and response; Community health service provision linking with facility-based services using initiatives such as Boma Health Initiatives; IPC in supported health facilities through regular assessment using score cards; provision of supplies and protocols and continuous monitoring of WAS; enhancing referral pathways between the communities and health facilities for critical conditions. The project will specifically: 1). 10 PHCUs (5 in Aweil North, 3 in Aweil West and 2 in Raja) will be supported to provide essential services to hardly inaccessible communities especially those displaced by floods and in Payams where health facilities are non-functional. 2). Establish 3 outreach teams (1 in Aweil North, 1 in Aweil West and 1 in Raja) to conduct integrated outreaches to mobile and displaced communities and those due to floods, they cannot access nearest health facilities. An integrated services package including services in antenatal, facility delivery, postnatal, family planning, immunization, curative consultations and growth monitoring, nutrition screening and health education will be provided as well as MHPSS and care for victims of Sexual gender-based violence in supported health facilities and outreaches. Linkages will be established with the nearest PHCCs and hospitals where severe cases will be referred for further management and appropriate care. Women in labor with emergencies will be referred to CEmONC center supported in Gok Machar PHCC, Nyamlel Hospital and Raja Hospital respectively depending on the location of the patient. These services will be provided at no cost to all age groups. 3) At the community level, Boma Health Workers will be helpful in community nutrition screening, immunizations’ defaulter tracing, health promotion and prevention and referral to the nearest health facilities.Using the essential medicines list of South Sudan, a list of essential commodities including psychotropics will be procured following the procurement policies of the donor in consideration with Organisational policies. Mechanisms for rational use will be instituted and stock closely monitored to ensure that stock ruptures are minimized during the project period. 4) Medical staff in supported facilities and outreaches within the targeted communities will be trained and refresher trained on various health topics including, Clinical management of rape and mhGAP in MHPSS. Beyond trainings, mentorship, coaching and supportive supervision will further be used to enhance staff capacity. 5) During outreaches, health education will be provided daily on various topics. 6) Surveillance mechanisms will also be instituted to monitor the trend of epidemic prone diseases. Friendly and private spaces will be created within the supported PHCUS to provide care and counselling support for victims of sexual violence.
