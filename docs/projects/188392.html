Project Summary

The activities listed under this plan are aiming to improve living conditions for IDPs in Atmeh Camp through water supply, solid waste management, sanitation system supply, and hygiene promotion activities. Under this plan, GC will be able to respond to the essential WASH services for the anticipated camp population of 240,000 people due to the ongoing conflict.
The activities also are aiming to improve living conditions for host communities and IDPs through sanitation system rehabilitation for the new informal settlements in Northern Maaret Masrin and Azaz area, where the plan is to assist 110,000 people, where the sewage from several villages flowed into this natural drainage because of the lack of sewage drainage system. The sewage was able to penetrate into the soil and the natural drainage (river) was usually dry in the summer. Now with the size of newly established settlements and the surrounding population, the flow into the natural drainage has increased and it is wet year-round.

4 - Needs

According to WASH assessments, access to sufficient and affordable safe water, adequate sanitation, solid waste management and/or hygiene supplies remain a challenge for newly displaced populations, IDPs living in IDP sites, as well as other most vulnerable households with limited livelihood opportunities and access to basic WASH services. Newly displaced populations require a timely and quality response based on comprehensive preparedness. IDPs in formal sites and informal settlements are highly dependent on continuous humanitarian assistance. GC has been working in Atmeh since early 2016. Our staff continue to monitor displacement trends and humanitarian access, as well as assessing needs on the ground as part of ongoing programming; the approximately 175,000 camp residents - a population that could grow to nearly 240,000 with the unstable military situation in NWS. The camp population rely on GC for essential WASH services. Our field teams in Idleb and Northern Aleppo also conducted a rapid assessment, and we found the need for implementing the sanitation infrastructure, especially, for the newly established informal settlements Northern Idleb and in Azaz. Under this plan, we will be able to extending a sewage line for villages (Al Dana ,Sarmada, Batabo, Teermanen Batabo camps), of a length of 4 km, as the line is exposed and causes diseases in the village of Batabo and the surrounding camps.

5 - Implementation of activities

From our office in Gaziantep and sub-office in Qah, GC will be responsible for overall program strategy and design, reporting, grant administration and compliance, procurement, vendor and sub-award payments, monitoring and evaluation (M&E) and quality assurance/quality control (QA/QC), and capacity building of staff. Program staff will oversee direct implementation of all proposed activities. Our Syrian staff will enter Syria as appropriate to monitor program implementation. The activities are including the water trucking, water pumping, de-sludging, solid waste management, hygiene promotion, and the WASH NFIs distribution (in kind/voucher). Out of Atmeh Camp activities will include; 1.Community engagement: collecting detailed information about the specific sanitation infrastructures require rehabilitation, through FGDs, KIIs, Observations with all the stakeholders. 2.Topographic surveys for the selected locations, developing the BoQs and the technical specification. 3.Procurement process: through transparent procurement process, advertising and receiving offers from the bidders, opening bids, TEC, and signing the contract with the selected contractor. 4.Implementation for the sanitation systems rehabilitation and construction, and supervising the implementation of the rehabilitation and construction works. Along the implementation of the activities we will have an ongoing monitoring for the implementation.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Consistent with our standard approach to humanitarian programming, the Program will follow Sphere Guidelines in implementing activities. The Program is based on the "Do No Harm" principle, with activities designed to be culturally appropriate and tailored to the specific context while preventing competition over resources. While implementing the proposed activities, there will be ahigh probability for the following risks; Movements of water/ desludging/garbage trucks in congested, camp like settings; Quality and/or quantity not adequate as per humanitarian standards; Water distribution not safe or accessible, especially for women, girls (exposure to GBV risk) and children elderly and people with disabilities; inequality in distribution; Toilets present GBV and/or health, safety risk; Access to toilet is not available to all 24/7; Health risk associated with garbage removal; Children below 18 years old conduct works on behalf of humanitarian organisation, its partners or contractors, the following measures are considerd for mitigating the risks impact; Consulting with the community (separately women and girls) regarding location of water distribution points/ desludging services modality/ SWM services modality, delivery routes and its schedule; Avoid location of water points in narrow streets, congested areas; Establish clear routes and schedule for water trucking that use wider access routes, avoiding school areas during the time children travel to/from school; Conduct safety


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy
