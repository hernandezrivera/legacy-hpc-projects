Project Summary

The Strengthen Nutrition Intervention to reduce Malnutrition in Magwi, Aweil South and Terekeka Counties of South Sudan project will contribute to 2021 South Sudan Humanitarian Response Plan by increasing equitable access and utilization of quality preventative and curative nutrition-specific services for children, and women in Magwi, Aweil South and Terekeka Counties, hence contributing to the overarching strategic objective of the humanitarian community.
Aweil South County has been categorized under IPC Phase 5 with Proxy GAM rate of 15.2% (Nutrition data from SMART surveys, RRMs and FSNMS 2020)
R14 conducted after September 2014 , meaning the county is faced with “catastrophe” food insecurity according to the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) analysis released on 18 December 2020 which also projects the same by mid-2021. Terekeka classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) with GAM rate of 22% (Nutrition data from SMART surveys, RRMs and FSNMS 2020) and According to the Projected IPC Acute Malnutrition Situation For May-August 2020 projection analysis it was reported that the acute malnutrition situation of Magwi County is likely to deteriorate during the lean season and the GAM rate is report at 6.7% (Nutrition data from SMART surveys, RRMs and FSNMS 2020).
Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) analysis released on 18 December 2020 estimated 7.24 million people or 60 per cent of the South Sudanese population will face severe acute food insecurity and will need urgent assistance of which many of the families have exhausted their emergency coping strategies.
In order to encounter the emergency Nutrition needs of 74665 individuals (2417 SAM children, (1184 Boys 1233 Girls), 5,815 MAM children (2849 Boys 2966 Girl). 3332 PLW, VAS 24403, (11957Boys, 12445 Girls), Deworming 21868, (10715 Boys and 11153 Girls) 25% from Magwi, 35% from Terekeka and 40% Aweil South Counties, Impact Health Organization proposes project will focus on meeting the nutrition cluster’s strategic plan and response objectives by; Prevention of malnutrition through provision of specialized food, Prevention of malnutrition through behavior change communication, Prevention of micronutrient deficiencies; Early detection and treatment of girls and boys under 5 years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition, Monitoring visit, Promotion of community participation for a responsive nutrition service delivery, Support planning and roll out of population-based surveys, Improve facility-based data collection and management; Prevention, testing and treating for malaria among children under five years with acute malnutrition; Provision of WASH kits to children with SAM in nutrition sites; Establishing or maintaining demonstration kitchen garden at nutrition sites for or in the vicinity; Integration of child stimulation and ECD in the provision of care for children with SAM and MAM; Mainstreaming of child protection and GBV risk mitigation at nutrition sites.
IHO will work closely with communities to identify the problem affecting them and the community will greatly participate development and implementation of activities and community shall provide feedback on the progress of the interventions. The project will build capacities of the communities through training and awareness campaigns designed to halt the spread of communicable disease. IHO will address unique protection needs of women and girls, particularly respond to GBV, WASH and protection by working closely with the line clusters.

1 - Needs

Two years after the signing of the revitalized peace agreement, its implementation has not reduced the humanitarian needs of the South Sudanese people. Lack of durable peace and limited investment in basic services are holding people back from stability and sustainable development. In 2020, communities were hit hard by the triple shock of intensified conflict and sub-national violence, a second consecutive year of major flooding, and the impacts of COVID-19. Some 1.6 million people remained internally displaced and another 2.2 million as refugees in the region (South Sudan HNO January 2021). Findings from Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) released on 18 December 2020 classified Aweil South County of Northern Bahr el Ghazal as catastrophe phase (levels 5) with Global Acute Malnutrition above the WHO minimum of 15%. The worsened food insecurity in Aweil South is primarily driven by conflict, destruction of crops by floods, insufficient crop production and limited movement due to Covid-19. As a result livelihoods have been disrupted and resulted into macroeconomic deterioration.Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) analysis released on 18 December 2020 estimated 7.24 million people or 60 per cent of the South Sudanese population will face severe acute food insecurity and will need urgent assistance of which many of the families have exhausted their emergency coping strategies. Aweil South County has been categorized under IPC Phase 5 with GAM rate of 15.2% (Nutrition data from SMART surveys, RRMs and FSNMS 2020), meaning the county is faced with “catastrophe” food insecurity according to the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) analysis released on 18 December 2020 which also projects the same by mid-2021. Terekeka classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) with GAM rate of 22% (Nutrition data from SMART surveys, RRMs and FSNMS 2020) and According to the Projected IPC Acute Malnutrition Situation For May-August 2020 projection analysis it was reported that the acute malnutrition situation Magwi is likely to deteriorate during the lean season and the GAM rate is report at 6.7% (Nutrition data from SMART surveys, RRMs and FSNMS 2020). The high levels of acute food insecurity in the country are driven by flooding, insecurity, population displacements, declined crop production, diseases and pests, the economic crisis, the effects of COVID-19, limited access to basic services, and the cumulative effects of prolonged years of asset depletion and loss of livelihoods. The Terekeka Inter- Agencies Rapid Need Assessment Report on flooding in Terekeka County/ CES 4th to 6th of November 2020 (RRC, IHO AFOD, Plan international, Plan, SNCDI, SSRC, ACSO, SAADO, HealthNetTPO, TAYA, NPA), reported that 2020 floods has affected Terekeka county central Equatoria state severely and left women, children and elderly people more affected and displaced, exposing them to health risk conditions, malnutrition, food insecurity and improper shelter. The finding consistent with IHO July 2019 Aweil South Assessment. The inter-agency assessment (CINA, RRC, AVSI, IOM, SPEDP, SCI, SSRC, HLSS, ST. MONICA, ICAP, STERWARD WOMEN, FARMSTEW, GIZ, IHO, PLAN international, GLOBAL ARM, LWF, IMA GLOBAL WORLD HEALTH) conducted in Magwi January 2021, indicated that over 598 individuals were displaced from Torit County into Magwi County due inter-community fighting in areas of Magwi, this coupled with the influx of returnees estimated at 11000 in the last 12 months ( AVSI 2020 Magwi-multispectral survey report). This possess a great gap in terms of nutritional services.In order to prevent further more escalation of food insecurity and acute malnutrition the project will support 74665 individuals (2417 SAM children, (1184 Boys 1233 Girls), 5,815 MAM children (2849 Boys 2966 Girl). 3332 PLW, VAS 24403, (11957Boys, 12445 Girls), Deworming 21868, (10715 Boys and 11153 Girls) 25% from Magwi, 35% from Terekeka and 40% Aweil South Counties.

2 - Project activities

IHO will align the project Activities in line to the nutrition cluster 2021 and these will be as follows:  Prevent malnutrition through provision of specialized food. The project will provide under-five girls, boys and PLWs provided with specialized nutritious foods though the OTP services and Outreaches  Prevent of malnutrition through behavior change communication The project will target primary caregivers of children aged 0-23 months and provide them received Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition counselling, during the OTP days, community outreach, through mother support ground and nutrition volunteers. The project will also promote cooking demonstrations sessions and cooking demonstrations sessions Prevention of micronutrient deficiencies; The project will provide girls and boys aged 6-59 months with vitamin A supplement and Albendazole tablets by working closely with WHO, UNICEF and MOH.  Ensure early detection and treatment of girls and boys under 5 years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition. This will include; admission of girls and boys aged 6-59 months with SAM for treatment; admission of boys, girls aged 6-59 months and Pregnant and lactating women with MAM for treatment;  Monitor quality of services by local leaders and key groups in the community through quarterly supervision vision both at service delivery points and at community level (household).  Promote of community participation for a responsive nutrition service delivery. The project conduct community meeting which include community leaders and as well conduct interviews using feedback questionnaires to ascertain the quality of services provide. The project will also provide suggestion boxes at service delivery points.  Prevent, test and treat for malaria among children under five years with acute malnutrition: The activities will be integrated in OTP, TSFP, Stabilization Centre and outreaches.  Provide WASH kits to children with SAM in nutrition sites and well conduct assessment to establish WASH needs linked to NFI. The project will also educate mothers and caregivers on proper sanitation and hygiene. Establish kitchen garden at nutrition sites where they don’t exist and maintain existing one to work as demonstration to promote dietary diversity by using locally available foods. Integrate child stimulation and ECD in the provision of care for children with SAM and MAM, in this regard the project will, undertake outreach sessions in schools on ECD, Establish safe spaces at Nutrition site for children 0-5 years, their primary caregivers and pregnant/lactating women, Train nutrition/health staff on Care for Child Development and similar types of packages, Provide developmentally appropriate play materials for health/nutrition spaces (i.e. ECD kit or similar) and relevant health/nutrition materials and Integrate early stimulation and play in nutrition/health spaces where children and parents can use developmentally appropriate toys.  Mainstream of child protection and GBV risk mitigation at nutrition sites. In this regard, the project will undertake Gener Audits, Assess community perceptions, norms and practices linked to nutrition that may contribute to GBV (e.g. gender dynamics in food consumption; obstacles to nutritional assistance for at-risk groups; etc.), Prepare and provide training for nutrition staff and community nutrition groups on the safe design and implementation of nutrition programmes that mitigate the risk of GBV, Implement strategies that increase the safety, availability and accessibility of nutrition services for women, girls and other at-risk groups (e.g. locate services in safe areas; establish supplemental feeding schedules in collaboration with women, girls and other at-risk groups; consider the need to bring feeding supplements to GBV survivors and their children in safe shelters; etc.) and undertake GBV awareness-raising.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

IHO has a policy of zero tolerance of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) by IHO staff members and the employees or any other persons engaged and controlled by IHO Contractors. To provide beneficiaries with information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), IHO has PSEA focal person, who will plan and ensure communities are reached with PSEA information, in addition, IHO will conduct PSEA training with beneficiaries. IHO will Conduct Awareness Raising in the community on causes of SEA, how beneficiaries can report SEA, how to handle and direct complaints and receiving and responding to Complaints. This will be communicated during trainings, OTP/TSFP sessions, Community meeting, outreach, radio talks and use of IEC materials.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

IHO will facilitate the provision of feedback from the most vulnerable people on the services provided through feedback meeting, consultations, feedback questionnaires conducted on quarterly and monthly basis. In addition the project will undertake a baseline study to establish the needs of vulnerable members and end line study to understand the how the Vulnerable members benefited from the project.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project frontlines beneficiaries as prayers for the project, to facilitate the feedback mechanism, IHO will hold quarterly feedback meeting through will which beneficiaries’ men, women, boys and girls will understand the project operation and achievement and share way to have better outcomes.The project provide will also provide feedback during the monitoring visits by local leaders and key groups in the community.IHO will administer a perception feedback questionnaire to beneficiaries with an aim give feedback on the services received and suggest way to improve where necessary.Finally at the end of the project, IHO will also conduct evaluation survey involving men, women, girls and boys and local leaders. The feedback Information will be available to local communities in local languages were possible.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

IHO will use beneficiary feedback mechanisms to ensure greater accountability, hence IHO will be accountable to the beneficiaries, hence will ensure that projects indicators and targets are met, the beneficiary feedback will help to priorities the community needs and align the project centering on people’s needs. Beneficiary feedback will evaluate the project performance in terms of relevant to the context in which you are operating.
