Project Summary

The Project will provide multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) to 2,500 acutely vulnerable households to allow them to meet their basic needs. Both emergency and regular MPCA will be provided depending on the needs and context. Regular MPCA comprising six rounds will be provided to 2,000 acutely vulnerable households -12,000 individuals, that are food insecure and are also unable to meet their priority basic needs, especially in underserved communities targeting IDPs, returnees, and host communities (severely food insecure) where there is no regular food assistance support.
The team will carry out rapid needs assessments across different sectors, along with a quick evaluation of markets in the target area. The latter will be used to determine the functionality, availability, and quality of commodities, as well as the overall market capacity regarding the planned MPCA project needs. The rapid market assessment will form the basis for a market impact assessment, including trend analysis on vendor capacity, prioritizing environmentally friendly businesses, and analyzing commodity supply and pricing. This is to ensure that MPCA does not have any negative impacts on the local economy. Additionally, the JMMI reach report will be considered.
Emergency MPCA will be provided at the onset of an emergency or shock comprising of 1 to a maximum of 3 rounds to 500 vulnerable households- 3,000 individuals to meet their most urgent needs immediately after a shock.
Across NES, deteriorating socio-economic conditions have drastically increased humanitarian needs with many households struggling to meet their basic needs. The cumulative impact of these factors has led to a high prevalence of acutely vulnerable households that the project will aim to specifically target with MPCA assistance to allow these households to meet their basic needs. Acutely vulnerable households will be identified through a multi-sectoral vulnerability assessment with targeted questions to identify whether cash is the most appropriate form of assistance by looking at beneficiary preference, market accessibility and safety. This follows guidance produced by the Cash Working Group (CWG). Post-distribution monitoring will also be conducted to ensure beneficiaries preferred cash over other types of assistance and did not face any barriers or protection risks as a result of receiving cash assistance. To ensure that cash transfers do not have any negative impact on the local economy, we will conduct market impact assessments. These assessments will monitor the availability and quality of core staple items in the local markets of intervention. Furthermore, To ensure an efficient and streamlined response, we will be coordinating the project with CWG and other key stakeholders. This collaborative effort will prevent any unnecessary duplication and enable us to deliver the best possible outcome.
Cash will be distributed in USD which is considered to be highly stable in NES when compared to the Syrian Pound
and equivalent to 120 USD per transfer in line with the CWG recommendations. CWW staff will facilitate the MPCA distributions
including selecting the distribution site, beneficiary sensitization about the cash distribution plan, beneficiary verification and ensuring
beneficiaries sign on the distribution lists as an acknowledgement for cash receipt,CWW will ensure distributions meet protection principles including promoting meaningful access, safety and dignity, accountability and participation.
The project will target the following acutely vulnerable households: (i) Head of household: Female, child, elderly or disabled, (ii) Households with two or more persons with a disability or chronic illness, (iii) Food security profile: severely food insecure, (iv) Income profile: Households with low to very low income, (v) Livelihoods coping strategies: Crisis or emergency
