Project Summary

This proposed project for Bama, Dikwa, magumeri, Maiduguri, Ngala and Konduga LGAs will lead to improved Health and Nutrition care amongst under 5, pregnant and lactating women and the most vulnerable persons in the proposed locations. This intervention will support primary health care service delivery hampered by the damaged health facility infrastructures and attacks by the insurgents on the functioning health facilities. In addition to the high risk of epidemic outbreaks especially Diphtheria recorded in the state. This intervention will also support referral to secondary health care and improve health care access in remote areas is where there are significantly limitation. The intervention will also address the serious shortage of skilled health care workers, particularly doctors, nurses and midwives, many of whom remain reluctant to work in inaccessible areas because of ongoing conflict and also support on the job training for MOH staff. Population displacements and influx of returnees and/or refugees due to camp closure and economic fortunes related to inflation has put strain on already existing facilities. Flooding in over 27 LGAs and displacement many households which counting still continue as we have recorded over 60,000 affected individuals. Some of the settlements and communities are not accessible at the moment due to flooding. The aftermath effect of flooding will be spilling over to the year 2023 and these need to be estimated for and planned for as well.
Worsening malnutrition trends in NE Nigeria is expected to worsen in the coming year due to food insecurity linked to armed conflict, flooding, inflation and climate change. The project will aim to identify children with acute malnutrition promptly and refer them to OTP/TSFP sites for treatment. During the treatment, the care givers will receive optimal education on infant and young child feeding(IYCF) and health messaging. The latter will allow them to seek appropriate care where necessary should the child fall ill.

1 - Needs

Based on the HNO 2023, an estimated 8.3 million women, men and children need some form of humanitarian and protection assistance in the BAY states this year. Over 2 million people have been forced to flee their homes due to persistent violence and remain internally displaced. There are nearly 1.5million returnees who lack essential services and livelihoods, and 4.8 million people in host communities or communities that have otherwise been affected by the conflict.Borno state remains the worst affected state amongst other states, and the last IPC (May 2023) predicted a worsening of nutrition status in the lean season of 2024. According to IPC 2023, 10 LGAs in Borno State were predicted to be in IPC phase 3. Monguno a LGAs in Northern Borno was classed under IPC phase 3 while 14 LGAs were predicted to be in IPC class 2, Ngala also in central Borno remains in IPC class 2The percentage of children 0-5 months exclusively breastfed (EBF) was 44.9% in Borno while the percentage of children who continued breastfeeding at age 12-23 months currently being breastfed was 55.1%. According to Health sector Humanitarian Response plan 2024, Borno State, has a population of 6,180,077.00 with a People in Need (PIN) of 2,724,244.00, and a target of 2,176,229.00. Concerning epidemics, cholera has particularly affected Nigeria this year. In Borno, as of November 2023 and according to the health sector, zero cases of Cholera were recorded in the state. Measles and Diphtheria epidemics was amongst the contributing cause of death amongst Children U5. Diphtheria: From the Health sector, a total of 822 Diphtheria cases were reported in Borno state, from week 1 to week 46. Maiduguri, Jere, and Biu remain the worst affected of the 16 LGAs. INTERSOS will build on these previous achievements, incorporate the lessons learned, and exercise a high level of flexibility and modifications in responding to the rapidly evolving needs of the vulnerable beneficiaries and local security contexts so that we are able to continue bridging prevailing gaps in unmet needs through USAID/BHA funding.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

All groups including women, children and elderly are engaged in gathering data on their specific needs. Concerns of all affected people, including vulnerable groups are factored in to the programme design ensuring that activities proposed are specifically designed to meet the needs of different age and gender groups. The programmes include estimated sex and age disaggregated data for the target groups based on the findings of the need’s assessment. For latrines, gender segregated sanitation location, design, safety and appropriateness and convenience of facilities will be decided in consultation with the users, particularly women, adolescent girls and any persons with disabilities to ensure the protection of affected people. All sanitation facilities, including latrines, will be constructed near residential units to ensure the privacy and security of women and children and easy access for elderly. Project will ensure that separate lockable, well-lit latrines are constructed for women and children and the criteria and details of activities will be explained in the community for better understanding. Project will ensure women's participation while selecting the sites for sanitation facilities, water points and timing of hygiene promotion sessions and WASH committee meetings. A close supervision that is also adaptable to the evolving needs of the target beneficiaries will be carried out by INTERSOS to support the community members to maximize their knowledge and promote their engagement to prevent and address child protection concerns. First, the training modules will be adapted to the needs of the audience and will use interactive activities, visual material and as far as possible key actors working with children will be invited to stimulate beneficiaries’ understanding, participation and interests. Before and after each session group play activities will test participants’ understanding on specific issues, if needed, topics will be re discussed. After the training cycle is completed, INTERSOS staffers initially will facilitate regular meetings with the community members and the subsequent design of an action work plan and afterwards to follow up on the action plan and its results. INTERSOS has a strong reputation on protection and GBV prevention and risk mitigation, as supported by the international protection coordinator and GBV/PSS specialist. In addition, INTERSOS protection staff work alongside the health, WASH and nutrition teams to ensure mainstreaming of protection and GBV issues across all activities. Furthermore, all INTERSOS staff is bounded to INTERSOS Code of Ethics, an official document that sets the rights, duties and responsibilities for all INTERSOS operators, collaborators, partners, advisers and members of the statutory bodies to which it is addressed. It also reaffirms the observance of values and ethical principles common to every human being, such as honesty, loyalty, probity and those pertaining to its nature as a humanitarian organization: humanitarian relief, neutrality, impartiality, independence, non-discrimination, transparency. Moreover, protection will be mainstreamed in this project as per the following consolidated procedures: - Prioritizing safety and dignity, and avoiding causing harm, by preventing and minimizing any unintended negative effects of activities; - Ensuring access, by arranging for beneficiaries’ easy access to assistance and services, and paying special attention to individuals who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services; - Participation and empowerment, by supporting the development of self-protection capacities and assisting beneficiaries to claim their rights through their full involvement in activities.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

INTERSOS embraces participatory community-led approaches, that involve target beneficiaries, Ward Development Committees (WDCs), and key local leaders and influential persons to understand the project targeting criteria as well as expected contributions from the community, for instance social capital and indigenous knowledge. Community entry messaging include information like source of funding (donor), selection criteria, targeting criteria and expected humanitarian support among others. As part of the CHS commitments, INTERSOS establishes functional complaint and feedback mechanisms in areas of project interventions. A compliant help desks are set up in INTERSOS supported safe space (funded by USAID-BHA) where people can report their complaints or give feedback about the project. Beneficiaries feedback will be gathered through several sources, including: face to face with field staff, WhatsApp, suggestion boxes, help desk, and email. Feedback received from adults and children in the community is gathered and organized by project and sector. This information is sent to the INTERSOS office in Maiduguri, where it is added to the Complaints Response Mechanism Register and referred to the most relevant person. In addition to the aforementioned set-up, the community-based approach, through active involvement of men, women, boys and girls, is at the base of the project design. Since the onset of INTERSOS action in the region, it has become well-known and respected within the targeted communities, positively engaging with both the hosting and IDP communities. INTERSOS conducts regular focus group discussions with target beneficiaries to improve its programming, taking into consideration the suggestions coming directly from the affected communities.
