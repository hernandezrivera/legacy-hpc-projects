Project Summary

n Chad the health situation is characterized by the prevalence of diseases with epidemic potential, such as cholera and Hepatisi E, exacerbated by the weak health system, poor hygiene practices, poor access to water and sanitation. In 2017 Chad was confronted to 2 major outbreak with cholera cases in Sila and Salamat regions (more than 1,000) as well as Hepatisis in Salamat. Transhumance and population movements are risk factors that spread epidemics. Chad is also at risk in the border regions of Nigeria, Soudan and Cameroon when outbreak are reported, creating a risk of spread in Chad. The Lake and Mayo Kebbi region are known to be risky areas.
As a priority, there is a need to improve the supply and quality of primary health services, especially in areas favorable to epidemics and other serious diseases in emergency contexts, and address the structural causes of these epidemics.
Particular attention must be paid to improve prevention and preparedness against the spread of epidemics (pre positioning of cholera kits in high risk areas). Strengthening access to drinking water and sanitation (only 46% of the population has access to drinking water and 10% to sanitation services, 65% of population are practising open defection, data from the last JMP), the management of water services and hygiene promotion campaign, particularly in urbain areas and IDP’s sites because of great human concentration. It is also essential to work to strengthen the community based surveillance and early warning system to improve outbreak detection and to be able to respond quickly. We will also work at local level in order to reinforce the preparation and response in foor regions at risk (Hadjer Lamis, Mayo Kebbi East, oust and Lake Chad) regarding cholera
During 2018, Chad didn’t notify cholera cases. But borders countries such as Cameroon, Nigeria and Niger have been notifying cholera cases since may 2018. It means that Lake Chad and Mayo Kebbi regions are known to be risky areas..
