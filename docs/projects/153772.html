Project Summary

Despite the signing of the revitalized peace agreement South Sudan still faces one of the world’s most dire humanitarian crises as a result of the 5 years brutal war that has left 7.5 million food insecure and over 2.3 million displaced.Moreover,an estimated 5,712,000 women, men and children will not have access to adequate water, sanitation and hygiene in 2019. The number of people requiring emergency WASH services in 2019 increased by 7 per cent from the previous year, explained by the expansion of WASH data with a higher reliability and more detailed analysis. Only 29 per cent of the population have access to a borehole, tap stand or water yard within a maximum 30-minute distance without facing protection concerns. The remaining two thirds of the population are thus required to travel greater lengths to access water or are reliant on surface water. SAADO's intervention is intended to focus primarily on life saving WASH activities that will include provision of emergency water facilities for women and girls with safe water and access in secure location agreed after a GBV risk analysis, distribution of hygiene/sanitary kits, provision of women and girls with safe sanitation and hygiene facilities access in secure location,provision of safe drinking/chlorinated water to WASH related diseases population to reduce the risks of WASH related morbidity and mortality.This project is going be implemented in selected counties of Terekeka and Talli where SAADO is currently implementing a school feeding programme targeting 19 schools and already has an established presence with well qualified local staff to ensure timely delivery of services with good standards. The proposed project will target women, children, boys, girls and men including IDPs, returnees and most vulnerable host populations. Inadequate WASH services in these two counties contribute not only to disease outbreak, but also to increased malnutrition. There is lack of improved sanitation and limited knowledge of hygiene practices specifically for those communities displaced by current conflicts into Gemeiza highlands and the host communities including in the schools. To respond to the negative WASH impact in the mentioned areas SAADO has currently set up static and mobile teams that are responding to the WASH needs in the targeted areas and schools.The proposed intervention will reduce the vulnerability of those communities who do not have access to safe water supplies and proper sanitation and hygiene in Terekeka and Talli counties through provision of improved water, sanitation and hygiene services and increased knowledge and coping capacity of affected communities to prevent outbreak of WASH related diseases such as cholera and diarrhea.
SAADO's proposed intervention is designed to align with the WASH cluster's objective of Mitigating WASH related GBV as well as objective 4 of WASH in health for outbreak control at wider community level.
SAADO’s proposed project will integrate with the nutrition and food security clusters through referrals to specialized partners with OTP centres since it is widely noted that nutritional status is greatly impacted by diarrheal and other WASH related diseases/ illnesses which largely affect children under 5 years of age which is a key focus of the WASH cluster. SAADO will encourage active participation of the local community including women and children in the implementation of the proposed project through consultation with local authorities at all levels, women, men, boys and girls in the selection of the project payams within the county based on the identified needs. The local authorities, host community and IDPs men, women, girls and boys will be involved in the design of the community and institutional sanitation facilities putting into consideration the need for privacy for females for the case of latrines and bath room’s construction.

Needs

Despite the signing of the revitalized Peace Agreement,an estimated 5,712,000 women, men and children will not have access to adequate water, sanitation and hygiene in 2019 as the number of people requiring emergency WASH services in 2019 increased by 7 per cent from the previous year, explained by the expansion of WASH data with a higher reliability and more detailed analysis across the country. Only 29 per cent of the population has access to a borehole, tap stand or water yard within a maximum 30-minute distance without facing protection concerns. The remaining two thirds of the population are thus required to travel greater distances to access water or are reliant on surface water or unprotected water sources. In addition,more than 90 per cent of the population practices open defecation, either because they do not have access to or are not accustomed to using a basic sanitation facility with only 13 per cent of the population having access to WASH NFIs, such as jerry cans, soap and mosquito nets. This poor access to WASH services and goods combined with high levels of food insecurity has a detrimental impact on the health of the most vulnerable, as seen through the high prevalence of malnutrition and water-borne diseases. For example, 75 per cent of the population reported households with members that had been self-diagnosed with a water-borne illness in the previous two-week period. The protracted conflict has continued to drive people’s WASH needs, from blocking or destruction of WASH infrastructure to limiting WASH commodities in the market. Insecurity at times also prevents humanitarian actors from accessing areas that have high WASH needs, while the presence of armed groups can deter civilians from accessing existing WASH facilities.In Central Equatoria,the overall situation of water supply and sanitation in Terekeka is one of the poorest with 68% not having access to improved sanitation and nearly 45% still using unimproved water sources as a result of localized intensifying conflicts, repeated natural disasters and poor operation and maintenance which is a huge burden on WASH infrastructure. Moreover,unplanned and continued arrival of migrating cattle keepers from the neighbouring counties is putting additional pressure on already limited and dilapidated infrastructure. Likewise, much lower percentage of returnees have access to water within the compound (4%) compared to conflict displaced people. This means, both the host cattle keepers and migrating populations face the burden of traveling long distances to fetch clean water. Such conditions are pushing affected population (including host communities) to share their limited water sources and often seek refuge to unimproved water sources and practice risky behaviours like open defecation. Female headed households are more likely to opt for open defecation compared to male headed, presumably because of their inability to build pit latrines. Such conditions are conducive for manifestation of diarrhoea: Attention needs to be paid to female headed families, as these families are less likely to have access to the means of hygiene (access to soap by 43% male headed families compared to 35% by female headed) and sanitation. The condition of poor WASH compromise the dignity of women and girls, put them at risk of harassment, and expose people to life-threatening diseases including outbreaks of acute diarrhoea, cholera, ARI and measles, especially to young children,the sick and elderly people. SAADO’s proposed interventions will be targeting cattle keepers, both male and female headed households without pit latrines, communities without protected bore holes that use unprotected water sources and communities displaced by ongoing conflict. The target population needs more community awareness on cholera and other diarrhoea diseases and more access to safe sanitation and water.

Activities

1.Gender appropriate response starting from a risk assessment, for secure access, location and design of water points, toilets and showers. 2.Provide safe water and access in secure location to women and girls with after appropriate risk analysis 3.Provide safe sanitation and hygiene facilities to women and girls with after appropriate risk analysis 4.Provide a timely WASH minimum package of interventions on an ad-hoc basis, adapted to the specifics of the related-outbreaks, at an institution level as well as for the wider community 5. Set-up of WASH services, training of health workers and pupils as ambassadors for appropriate hygiene practices within handwashing with soap at critical times at their household level 6.Mobilization and training of 100 hygiene promoters. Hygiene promoters will sensitize the IDPS about the use of latrines and host communities, water sources, importance of cleaning water containers, solid waste management, the importance of hand washing at critical times and the importance of personal hygiene among the affected community. 7.Conduct daily hygiene campaign/awareness among the internally displaced persons in the highlands of gemeiza. Ensure all users in particular women and girls participate in identification of risks hygiene practices and coordination.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

This project will ensure that the affected populations in the targeted areas have a genuine voice during activity design and decision-making process that inform the proposed projects's priorities and modalities. As part of their orientation training and continual development, project personnel will receive instruction on why information sharing is prioritized, how it is operationalized, and what their roles and responsibilities are in making it work. In each target area SAADO will initiate an open dialogue with the community and key stakeholders. We will ensure that communication and messaging is timely, accurate, appropriate,effective and understood. Project parameters such as site and beneficiary selection will be openly discussed with beneficiaries using a highly transparentmethodology that is grounded in community participation. Information sharing will also be a key feature of the proposed project to help in avoiding overlaps between different actors in the selected counties of Terekeka & Talli of Central Equatoria State as well as building collaborations between humanitarian agencies and affected populations.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages will be contextualised to speak to different age groups and gender.The project will achieve this through establishment of Project Management Committees (PMCs)composed of 60% women and 40% men which will enhance community participation, ownership, and satisfaction inSAADO’s proposed intervention. Furthermore,the PMCs will include a membership that represents all subsets of society including marginalised groups, and will be responsible for implementing a two-way BCA strategies identified in the project’s Beneficiary Communication and Accountability Plan.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project will ensure that beneficiary sites and training centres are safe spaces for both genders and those women with children have access to child spaces where they can play safely while their mothers attend trainings. During community consultations,meetings and training sessions or any gatherings on this project, SAADO gender and protection staff will address the people we beneficiaries on issues around prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Where ever such issues are raised SAADO will link the survivors with protection implementing partners as part of strengthening the referral pathways.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Tailored transparency and feedback mechanisms such as open meetings,verbal, written and pictorial notifications of decisions and actions; consultations with women and men (separately where necessary to ensure distinct gendered voices are heard), consultations with different age groups and persons withspecial needs, and confidential complaints mechanisms (such as a complaints box) will be used in this proposed project.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Our main approach in achieving this project project is through community engagement. This entails participatory planning and action enhancing creativity and innovation at all levels of community engagement. This will entail setting up a clear monitoring and evaluation framework in discussion with women and men separately and in groups, to be monitored by implementing field teams, which will include monthly monitoring reports with clear action points. We will also ensure that a gender-sensitive community feedback mechanism (for consultation, feedback and complaints) is established to inform and change programming, including the establishment of accountability groups.
