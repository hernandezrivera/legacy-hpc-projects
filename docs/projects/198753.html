Project Summary

Ariel proposes a project that is grounded on community health care service delivery platforms to provide an integrated and inclusive care package that responds to the specific health needs of IDPs while establishing a strong referral system to ensure the provision of complementary and/or specialized services as needed. Moreover, the project will reinforce the quality of health care service delivery, targeted at IDPs in stable zones HFs, which are overloaded due to elevated population influx.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

This project leverages activities implemented in 04 target districts in the previous year by expanding support to 04 more districts (Ancuabe, Chiúre, Metuge e Montepuez) to support MCH, HIV and TB services. Ariel will provide an integrated package of essential services (preventable communicable diseases, malaria, malnutrition, MCH, SRH, and mental HS) through a variety of facility, mobile and community platforms to secure access to primary health care and continuity of comprehensive, HIV and TB prevention and treatment interventions for populations residing in high-risk zones, IDP and host communities in 07 districts. Support will be provided to HFs; remote communities will be reached through MB and a network of community actors will be established to provide assistance to the affected population with particular emphasis on the most vulnerable groups: children under five (CU5), women in childbearing age, adolescents girls and young women (10-24 yrs), youth, adults, elderly people and people with disabilities. Project activities will be integrated into a broader network of protective services addressing the rights, material and psycho-social needs of affected populations, collaborating with humanitarian response service providers and linking vulnerable people to protection services.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

1: Primary Health care, MCH and sexual and reproductive health Modalities -HF, THC, MHB, CHP, campaigns: Service delivery modality will depend on district stability. Host communities and resettlement sites in conflict/at-risk zones will be serviced by a community system through community cadres (APEs, Mentor mothers, activists and community leaders, CBOs, FBOs and health and co-management committees, that will provide basic health care and referral to mobile brigades and HF for further care as needed. A robust communication package will be developed to broadcast on community radios; community leaders will be involved to disseminate information regarding, access to HS, disease risk and prevention (cholera, malaria, measles and covid-19), and human rights. In stable zones, health service delivery packages will be grounded on reinforcement of HR in HF to respond to demand caused by the increase in patient volume. At the community level, health services will be provided through mobile brigades complemented by service delivered by community cadres hired and trained by the project. 2. (MHPSS, PWD): Mental HS will be integrated into the PHC package using the CETA package. Trained community cadres will screen and provide PSS for post-traumatic stress disorders, psychosomatic illness, depression and anxiety, substance abuse, aggression, and higher levels of sexual and domestic violence. A strong referral system will be established between community cadres and other humanitarian partners to refer complex cases to HF or MB and reinforcement of monthly coordination meetings at the district level between all partners.3. In partnership with the IOM and in collaboration with INS disease observatory Department, the project will support the operationalization of the IDSR by strengthening community-based surveillance; strengthening the surveillance capacity of local health authorities, and integrating surveillance into the monitoring systems of the COE-P, INS and provincial surveillance cluster for rapid analysis/use. Community cadres (activists and APES) will collect basic key surveillance indicators (HIV, TB, malaria, cholera and measles and other preventable diseases cases, deaths) and report on a weekly basis. Monthly project reports that include the 5W matrix and but are not limited to the 12HRP indicators will be shared with partners, donors and government, as well as submitted to DHIS-2.The target population calculated based on IOM and SPS data: 157,178 (IDP+Host)Target Districts: Nangade, Mueda, Muidumbe, Chiure, Metuge, Ancuabe and Montepuez

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Conflicts and resulting population displacement increase the vulnerability of the affected population in general and exacerbate the vulnerability of an already vulnerable group of the population such as women, girls and children. In this project community cadres and actors will be trained to ensure the identification of vulnerable populations and the risk of vulnerability in communities. Furthermore, mass campaigns will consider the transformation of gender norms and tackle existing myths and taboos. Constant data collection and review will allow continuous situation analysis and adaptation of strategies to ensure assistance to all population groups. Community/key informants will be strategically used, coupled with data analysis to assess the specific needs of different population groups. Based on data collected from districts affected by the conflict, risk zones and stable zones in CD, young girls, women and children are mostly affected and more vulnerable compared to men. Our project is designed to equally target males and females, through interventions and modalities that target both women and men, adolescents girls and boys as well as youth and children. Our interventions focus on MCH care for women and children, SSRH, including GBV response. Women in childbearing age have different needs and the intervention we are proposing are targeting the different phases of life a woman is facing, from adolescence to motherhood, including SSR education, FP methods, ANC and post-natal care and care for children <5yrs.Males who are not accessing HF or MB are also targeted through male champions in the community to mobilize them and refer them for care. Community actors in our project (MM, Peer Educators, APEs, traditional birth attendants and activists) will be the ‘voice’ of the population they serve through project activities and a community participatory mechanism will be established.To strengthen the community health service delivery platform, Ariel will ensure the utilization of CBCM and collaborate with IOM to ensure a culturally and gender-sensitive community-Based Complaint Mechanism. The project will ensure the prevention of SEA by training Ariel Staff involved with a humanitarian response as well as community cadres and community leaders. The project will also promote the utilization of a hotline so that complaints related to the delivery of humanitarian response assistance are routed. Through mass campaigns, distribution of IEC material and various community actors the project will increase awareness of SEA as well as reporting mechanisms of SEA incidents. The project will collaborate with not only other humanitarian response partners but also with other service providers in target districts to ensure cooperation and complementary referral procedures. Furthermore, the project will collaborate with CBCM to report SEA incidents to allow for timely adjustments.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

As vulnerable populations need more than health services, this project will link vulnerable high-risk populations such as OVC, elderly people, young women and disabled individuals to specific services such as legal services, social services, education services and CBOs that work with this specific population for long term assistance, as needed.As the health implementing partner, Ariel works closely with MOH at the central level, and DPS, SPS and SDSMAS at the provincial and district levels, to ensure the sustainability of project activities and that those activities are appropriately tailored to context. Leveraging on the existing collaboration between Ariel and IOM, the project will routinely engage IDPs and host communities to assess and respond to needs and refer them for urgent and immediate services, such as GBV. In fact, Ariel is part of the technical working group at the national level and was involved in the development of the GBV package for emergency contexts and this will be presented in January 2023. Furthermore, Ariel developed TORs for GBV care on Mobile brigades. Ariel coordinates the GBV cluster in CD where all aspects related to prevention and care are prioritized. Community actors will be trained to conduct active case finding and referrals to ensure a continuum of care and effective utilization of available health, social and humanitarian services.The project will provide support to the Camp Coordination and Camp Management CCCM teams that operate Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms (CFM) and Community Based Complaint Mechanisms (CBCM), to ensure that emerging issues are promptly addressed thus demonstrating the project´s responsiveness to IDPs and host communities.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Project staff and community cadres will be trained to identify persons with disabilities and barriers to accessing services and assistance. The project will work with CBOs and FBOs to continuously carry out a situation analysis to identify and analyze the situation of persons with disabilities within the community, their needs, and physical, attitudinal and institutional barriers to service access. Work with persons with disabilities to engage them in project activities and monitoring. To ensure inclusion monitoring, data collected for key project indicators will also be disaggregated by disability to ensure continuous improvement in the engagement of people with disabilities, through gap-filling and project adaptation/adjustment. Ariel will map local institutions that provide specialized services to disabled individuals and ensure referral for further support as needed.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

This project does not anticipate any direct impact on the environment but Mozambique is at high risk from all three climate hazards: drought, floods and coastal storms that may negatively impact the project’s intended outputs and outcomes. This project is grounded on the community subsystem (community cadres and MB) for health service delivery, which allows for adjustment flexibility and rapid response. Furthermore, Ariel abides by the existing Cabo Delgado contingency plan in the course of the project to ensure the continuity of health service delivery in situations of risks or an emergency scenario.
