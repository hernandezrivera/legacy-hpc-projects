Project Summary

This project will aim to specifically identify and address protection needs such as monitoring, risk prevention, mitigation and assessments including severe mental distress caused by cycle of war and violence in South Sudan to internally displaced persons and host community in the proposed project locations leaving children and women vulnerable to sexual abuses and psychological distress, livelihoods disrupted, separation of children, child recruitment and households looted. Despite efforts made by the warring parties to silencing the guns, however, human right violations are still being perpetrated by the same parties and most of violation and abuses went unreported especially in the hard-to-reach areas. This project aims to prevent and mitigate protection risks by working together with community-based protection mechanism and protection monitoring in hard to reach areas targeting 8,600 (2,000 girls, 1,200 boys, 3,200 women, and 2,200 men). Gender-based violence (GBV) remains under-reported due to barriers to reporting. Through UNCDR experiences in the protection field, monitoring, risk prevention, mitigation and assessment will be in place. Over the last two years 30 GBV related incidence including the six underage girls were recorded in Ayod and Maiwut alone. That indicates that the level of GBV is high amongst the community of South Sudan especially in hard-to-reach areas. Women and girls being exposed to rape forced marriages and other forms of GBV due to the fact that displacement makes women and girls particularly vulnerable to exploitation and abuse due to their social and economic status. Women have been reported exposure to the sexual assault in several locations across South Sudan including Ayod, Maiwut and Panyijiar counties when it comes to accessing services and assistance. They face similar risks when walking long distances to collect water and firewood. According to the report and individual/group focus discussion during UNCDR Assessment in March 2019 in Ayod as mentioned above, UNCDR wanted women and girls to have safe spaces for sharing their experiences and accessing support, link them with services providers and dissemination of life-saving messages through awareness raising/community mobilization. UNCDR has both static and mobile presence in Ayod and Maiwut, as protection partner dealing with protection monitoring, risk prevention, mitigation and assessment, we had conducted an assessment between 26-30 of August 2019 in Maiwut and 18-22 in Ayod including surrounding villages and interviewed a good number of boys, girls, and women who expressed their basic needs and protection as well. Based on the discussion and observation, It was clear stated that more efforts are needed to ensure that most vulnerable and persons with specific need must be reached wherever they are. People had to walk a long distances about 3 - 4 hrs in order to access humanitarian assistance.
Following were discussed to have been the major challenges.
1. Most vulnerable IDPs and host community have very little chance or no chance at all to express their need if they are not reached by humanitarian agencies in the hard-to-reach villages including Island of Ayod County (Haat, Menime, Wan Machar and Nyawit), this is due to accessibility issue where they are not able to reach the services and humanitarian assistance. 2. Protection monitoring and assessment to identify the basic need and protection related concern amongst IDPs is lacking. 3. Awareness raising to sensitize the community on harmful practices, negative coping mechanism, their rights, their responsibilities within the community, where they should access services and humanitarian assistance is lacking due limited partners accessing hard-to-reach locations due to insecurity and the distances. 4. There is also a need to strengthen the community based-protection networks because communities are the primary support mechanism for women, men, girls, and boys affected by the conflict.

1 - Needs

South Sudan got its independence from Sudan on 09th July 2011 after a decade long conflict with North Sudan that claimed thousands of lives of innocent children, women and men. Many people lived in IDPs camps and refugee camps in the neighboring countries. The independence was a moment of joy and happiness to the society and great hope for the future dreams of all people in South Sudan. When the sudden crises of December 2013 came, and repeated in July 2016 during the implementation of 2015 peace accord it was unexpected shock to the South Sudanese society and claimed many lives and devastated lives of the remaining people. UNCDR already had both static and mobile presence in Ayod and Maiwut counties since 2017, but this year in March 2019 UNCDR had expanded its operation to filling in uncovered general protection gaps by 2020. We established new field office in Panyijiar county with a team of 5 staffs who had already been deployed (2 females and 3 males staffs). As protection partner dealing with protection monitoring, risk prevention, mitigation and assessment, we had conducted an assessment between 26-30 of August 2019 in Maiwut and 18-22 in Ayod including surrounding villages and interviewed a good number of boys, girls, and women who expressed their basic needs and protection as well. Based on the discussion and observation, It was clear stated that more efforts are needed to ensure that most vulnerable and persons with specific need must be reached wherever they are. People had to walk a long distances about 3 - 4 hrs in order to access humanitarian assistance. This project will intend to carry out housing, land and property (HLP) activities to promote and claim the rights of women and group at risk, also facilitate free legal assistance. However UNCDR will therefore arrange and encourage broad-based community dialogue regarding HLP among women and men.Following were discussed to have been the major challenges. 1. Most vulnerable IDPs and host community have very little chance or no chance at all to express their need if they are not reached by humanitarian agencies in the hard-to-reach villages including Island of Ayod County (Haat, Menime, Wan-machar and Nyawit), this is due to accessibility issue where they are not able to reach the services and humanitarian assistance. 2. Protection monitoring and assessment to identify the basic need and protection related concern amongst IDPs is lacking. 3. Awareness raising to sensitize the community on harmful practices, negative coping mechanism, their rights, their responsibilities within the community, where they should access services and humanitarian assistance is lacking due limited partners accessing hard-to-reach locations due to insecurity and the distances. 4. There is also a need to strengthen the community based-protection networks because communities are the primary support mechanism for women, men, girls, and boys affected by the conflict. Through our current programming in protection monitoring, UNCDR is working with the community-based protection networks in hard-to-reach areas of Ayod, Maiwut and Panyijiar building on existing community structures and networks because they are also best placed for identifying vulnerable children, GBV survivors and can greatly reduce the risk of exploitation, violence, and abuse of children. The same need to be done in proposed project sites. 5. The community-based protection mechanism is weak and needs to be strengthened and supported. 6. Community lacking the information on where they should access services. 7. Promote registration of HLP right for women and group at risk. 8. Awareness raising on how they should claim their rights and facilitate free legal assistance. 9. Arrange and encourage broad-based community dialogue regarding HLP among women and men 10. Introduce a complain mechanism within the community and in the organization level where HLP related concern can be reported.

2 - Activities

1. To minimize exposure to further risks, UNCDR aims to advocate on behave of the beneficiaries in the hard-to-reach areas/proposed project locations so that humanitarian assistance and other services can be delivered to their locations instead of long-distance walk to access services and humanitarian assistance. 2. To conduct weekly, protection monitoring and assessment within the targeted locations to identify the trend, gaps and related protection and basic needs and the information and finding will be shared with partners on the ground and protection cluster for a further decision to be made for possible intervention. 3. Community mobilization and awareness raising on harmful practices such child early marriage, negative coping mechanism targeting common public places, like churches, schools local authority and community leaders. 4. Dissemination of lives saving messages, where beneficiaries can access services, humanitarian assistance, their rights, and their responsibilities within the community especially for community leaders and local authorities. 5. Establish or support the existing community-based protection mechanism, train and support them so that they will be able to help in identification of the protection-related concerns and to be able to cope up with protection related risks that are affecting them. 6. Construction of the 3 IDPs leaders meeting places in Ayod, Maiwut and Panyijiar 7. To link the beneficiaries with the services providers in various need and address the protection need of individual or group. 8. Conduct 8 capacity building training for Community leaders/community Based-Protection Network in Ayod, Maiwut and Panyijiar counties. The training will cover basic human right, prevention of GBV, forced/early child marriage and conflict mitigation between IDPs, returnees and the host community. 9. Conduct monthly advocacy meeting with IDPs, returnees and host community Leaders to convey the advocacy messages and to get feedback from the community on the project activities and share the planned activities to increase their participation in the project implementation. 10. To conduct services mapping and coordinates with other protection agencies on the ground to avoid duplication of services. 11. UNCDR will engage and advocate for women, adolescents boys and girls to be included in the leadership of area land board committee in the target areas. 12. Conduct training for local authority, customary/traditional leaders that involved in the traditional/rule of law. 13. Promote registration of HLP right for women and group at risk. 14. Awareness raising on how they should claim their rights and facilitate free legal assistance. 15. Arrange and encourage broad-based community dialogue regarding HLP among women and men 16. Introduce a complain mechanism within the community and in the organization level where HLP related concern can be reported. UNCDR has assessed the level of risk on how the services can be delivered to the beneficiaries in each location. There was no significant risk that can prevent project activities because this is going to be a field-based intervention where the staff will be locally recruited and are able to access these areas free because they are part of the communities. On another hand, UNCDR will include the advocacy for the services to deliver where they are needed so that beneficiaries are not subjected to further risk when accessing services from far distribution points. The management has already established a good relationship with local authorities where UNCDR has no issues in relation to access to the project sites.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Members of marginalized community’s/minority groups are not included in consultation and/or excluded from humanitarian services/assistance. UNCDR will carry out sensitization among its own team members to raise their awareness about the need to engage members from all segments in the consultations/project implementation. UNCDR will also carry out home to home visits to ensure the inclusion of community members who may be unable to leave their household due to physical or cultural/social restrictions. Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance. As observed in Ayod, Maiwut and Panyijiar counties of Jonglei, Upper Nile and Unity States. Women and girls face an increased risk of sexual violence when accessing humanitarian assistance. Considering this, UNCDR will seek to reduce/mitigate protection risks that may be incurred during the course of the project. UNCDR includes engagement of beneficiary members around selection of the locations for latrine construction and screening of locations with a GBV risk assessment. Risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure in targeted project areas. The conflict has seen a rise in the incidents of housing, land, and property (HLP) disputes with the land of displaced households often resettled in their absence. The situation is particularly challenging for female headed households, who have land rights under the constitution and national framework, but often experience discrimination in fulfilling these rights at the local level due restrictive social and cultural norms at the household level. Considering these challenges, UNCDR will carry out community sensitization and awareness raising on HLP issues.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

UNCDR adopts a community-based approach, where beneficiaries and local authorities are involved in the prioritizing, planning and implementation of activities as an important measure of rehabilitation and capacity building. UNCDR will a certify communities on accountability through information, involvement, participation and adequate handling of complaints. UNCDR will also deploy beneficiary feedback and complaints mechanism and operates complaints desks to enable community members to flag concerns or complaints in person or anonymously, improving participation and accountability for all affected populations. Through its activities, UNCDR will ensure confidentiality of sensitive and personal information, while also ensuring data protection mechanisms are in place. Informed consent and people centred-approaches are sought throughout the activities, this is measured through information management tools, consent forms, and information sharing protocols. Complaints and feedback submitted to the complains box will be reviewed to identify any issues in implementation of the project.
