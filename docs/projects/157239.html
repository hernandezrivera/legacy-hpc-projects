Project Summary

The main objective of this project is to strengthen the critical interface between humanitarian action and economic development while building the resilience and self-reliance of vulnerable communities by delivering sustainable, development focused agricultural and livestock interventions. The project also aims to mitigate the risk of food insecurity and malnutrition among vulnerable communities through food processing activities that promote the synergy between agricultural production and nutrition requirements. The interventions will target the most vulnerable small scale farmers, IDPs, returnees and local communities. The project will focus on seven localities where high rates of displacement or return have been reported and this will include: Umm Dukhun locality, Central Darfur, El Fasher locality, North Darfur, Rahad El Berdi and Um Dafog localities, South Darfur, Talodi locality, South Kordofan, Ghubaysh locality, West Kordofan State and Dourdeb locality, Red Sea State. These localities have been considered as one of the most vulnerable areas to basic services and livelihood opportunities while there are little humanitarian supports. Local communities in these localities are vulnerable to livelihood opportunities as the results of growing constraints on access to sustainable income generating activities and loss of their assets and rights including access to farm land from last displacement. The majority of migrants and communities in the selected localities are suffering from food insecurity, chronic and severe malnutrition, indeed, the target localities were all classified in Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) phase 3 (Crisis), and the four of them (Doudeb, Umm Dukhun, Talodi, Ghubaysh localities ) were suffered from the high prevalence in IPC phase 3. In addition to this, 21, 247 people from El Fasher, 1,510 from Ghubaysh and 7,909 from Talodi were estimated to be in IPC phase 4 (Emergency). Hence, timely and adequate livelihood interventions to address food insecurity is required while strengthening resilience of community to protect their live and initiate a long term solution for economic growth.
A community based participatory approach will be used as an important strategy to encourage communities to take responsibility to participate in identified agricultural and livelihood interventions. The interventions will include: (1) community based agricultural training and practice by increasing knowledge and skills for better agricultural production, addressing climate adaptation technique: (2) provision of improved seeds and farming tools to restore agriculture productive capacity: (3) support sustainable livestock husbandry practice and veterinary services: (4) food processing activities based on local agricultural production. In addition, community-based agricultural training will be designed and implemented through cooperation with diaspora. A Sudanese agricultural expert from abroad will carry out the training to transfer his knowledge and skills in agriculture training to promote sustainable natural resource management.

Needs

The ongoing economic crisis along with continued drought and environmental degradation has exacerbated the livelihood situation of vulnerable communities in Sudan. The high price of agricultural inputs and food has undermined food security. This is particularly relevant to small scale farmers, IDPs, returnees, refugees and local communities affected by human displacement. Poor food security and livelihood opportunities, together with cultural practices and inappropriate feeding practices drive acute and chronic malnutrition among pregnant and lactating women and their children, especially those in displaced situations. In the target localities of States of Darfur, Kordofan and Red Sea, the majority of migrants suffer from chronic and severe malnutrition and disruption of livelihoods sectors as the results of loss of their assets and rights including access to farm land. Local communities are also vulnerable due to growing constraints on access to sustainable income generating activities and returnee and IDP movements, season migrants, scarce resources and the difficulty of accessing these areas have added to the high-level of vulnerability in the targeted localities. Additionally, efforts to reduce severe malnutrition in these areas is impeded by the communities’ minimal or absent awareness of the importance of food nutrition. The lack of knowledge on nutrition has led to severe and chronic malnutrition, particularly among the most vulnerable group such as women and children. IOM began working in return and displacement areas of Umm Dukhun, El Fasher, Um Dafug and Dourdeb localities. The communities in these areas highlighted that whilst basic social services are a priority, the majority of the communities rely heavily on agriculture and livestock as a source of food and income, identifying agriculture and livestock as live saving assistance and the most sustainable strategy. Nevertheless, despite this self-identified importance, local production of crops and livestock remain low and limited to self-subsistence due to relatively low rainfall, deteriorating grazing lands and poor farming and livestock practice that do not meet the local market demands. Existing practices are also limited in their ability to mitigate the risks of climate change. Communities’ awareness regarding an importance of food nutrition is limited low and this is a factor for malnutrition. The project will respond to the needs by delivering immediate agricultural assistance while ensuring sustainable agricultural practices through community and resilience-based agricultural and livestock interventions. To enhance skills and knowledge for better agricultural production and climate adaptation technique, agricultural training will be designed and implemented by transferring knowledge and skills of Sudanese agricultural expert from abroad. The project will also tackle food insecurity and malnutrition by designing nutrition-sensitive food processing business for vulnerable women

Activities

Output 1: 21,140 individuals received agricultural extension supports1) Conduct field assessments in collaboration with Sudanese diaspora and AOAD to identify the locations and the type of agricultural supports.2) Set-up a community agricultural committee for each locality (7 committees: 20 members for each committee).3) Organize and conduct Training of Trainers: Training the selected committee members by the Sudanese agricultural expert. 4) Distribute improved seeds and farming tools for a total 21,000 beneficiaries (600 HHs from each locality) and other agricultural inputs, e.g. small water pumps for irrigation farm. 5) Conduct agricultural training for the selected beneficiaries by the trained committee members. 6) Install an irrigation system for the selected 3 farms. Output 2. 7,070 individuals from local communities supported with livestock supports1) Provide upgraded veterinary care equipment to a total 70 Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) (10 CAHWs from each locality). 2) Conduct refresher training on veterinary services for CAHWs. 3) Provide livestock to the vulnerable households from local communities: 1,400 HHs-200 HHs from each locality/a total 7,000 individuals, together with vaccination campaign and training on better husbandry practice and livestock health. Output 3. 10,500 women individuals benefited from food processing interventions strengthening a synergy between agricultural production and nutrition requirements 1) Conduct assessments on local market demands and nutritional situation for women and children.2) Select 2,100 HHs/10,500 individuals from vulnerable female headed households with children (300 HHs from each locality). 3) Provide food processing materials and training on food processing based on local agricultural productions and addressing key components of nutritional care for lactating and pregnant women and their children. 4) Organizing food fair and introducing the beneficiaries’ food products
