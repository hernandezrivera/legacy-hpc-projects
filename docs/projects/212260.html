Project Summary

The prevalence of GBV increased due to widespread displacement and the breakdown of social structures in 2023, with the main risk factors being gender norms, social factors, the economic crisis, heightened insecurity, overcrowded living condition, access to arms and drugs, and reduction in GBV response funds (Voices from Syria 2024). Case management data from Al Hol, Washokani, and Jdidet Bikara PUs indicate that from April to November 2023, emotional and psychological abuse are the most common types of abuse experienced by GBV survivors, followed by physical assault, early/forced marriage, denial of resources, opportunities, or services, and sexual assault. A total of 99% of the participants in PSS assessments displayed medium to high symptoms of anxiety, primarily caused by stressful life events followed by relationship challenges.
In line with the HRP 2024’s strategic objectives of saving lives and increasing access to services, the organization will provide GBV prevention and response services alongside empowerment activities through both as a standalone and integrated approach with health as an entry point. Distinct focus will be placed on persons with disability, widowed women, women and girls headed household, adolescent girls and older women among others. One women and girls' safe spaces (WGSSs) (Kisreh Sub-district, Deir-ez-Zor [DeZ] governorate), and three Protection Units (PU) (Washokani\Tweineh, Al Hol camp and Basira – covering the communities of Jdidet Bikara) will be supported. Referral for women and girls at risk between health and GBV services will be strengthened either by establishing a WGSS in close proximity with supported health facilities (HFs) or through deploying GBV team in the HF to provide prevention and response services in line with the GBV Minimum Standards in Emergencies.
The services provided within the WGSS will continue to include case management in line with the GBV case management Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) developed by the GBV Sub-Cluster (SC), community based and focused PSS services including psychoeducation sessions, psychological first aid (PFA), awareness raising, and empowerment activities (skills development and life skills sessions). In addition, the organization will conduct safety audits and risk mapping, integrate awareness raising and psychoeducation sessions for men and boys, and provide psychosocial workshops to address behavioral problems, such as anger and violence, to enhance engagement in GBV programming. Hotline services will be used to provide online information sharing, PFA, individual counselling and case management for women and girls who do not have access to the center. Emergency Cash funds will also be integrated, in accordance with the Emergency Funds SOPs within the GBV case management framework, when relevant in the case action plan to complement the protection response. Cash support will be available for GBV survivors and women and girls at-risk in all the static services or upon referral from social workers and assistants in mobile units or HFs, as well as from external referrals.
Societal norms and cultural barriers might impede the integration of specific social groups into programs/ community initiatives. To mitigate this risk, the organization will conduct information sessions to build on peer support networks and reach more socially isolated groups, particularly women and youth, implement activities with separate sessions for men and women, engage community throughout the entire project cycle to foster local ownership, and adapt GBV messaging to ensure sensitivity to the context. In addition, lack of quality services may expose survivors to harm, enhancing risk of stigmatization, community and family ostracization and further trauma. Therefore, only trained personnel will conduct case management with survivors and referral pathways will continue to be strengthened.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

In addition, the aim is to improve soft skills and positive coping mechanisms of targeted communities to manage stress and anxiety, reduce tensions and create more inclusive communities. This objective is driven by findings from the Protection Monitoring and Returnee Assessments, revealing that returnees experience prejudice and limited job options as a result of their late return, while host communities (HC) feel neglected in comparison to the assistance provided to internally displaced persons (IDP), leading to negative coping mechanisms such as child labor, begging, smuggling, theft, and recruitment of youth by armed groups. In response, WGSSs and Men Youth Centers will operate as inclusive community hubs fostering social cohesion and conduct group PSS sessions ensuring the participation of individuals from a diverse mix of backgrounds, with a particular focus on improving mutual understanding of challenges faced by returnees, HC and IDPs alike, and supporting youth to create social enterprises that will deliver market demanded products and services to maximize profits and build youth economic self-reliance. The Syria Management Team provides technical support to the overall Syria response, led by the Country Director. Protection services will be provided by PSS and Social Assistants, supervised by a GBV Officer and a Protection Program Manager with advisory support from the Whole of Syria Protection Advisor. MEAL Field assistants and Program Officers in Syria will be responsible for the day-to-day and regular monitoring. Moreover, Third Party Monitoring will evaluate end of project achievements. Furthermore, the established feedback, response and complaint mechanism will be monitored by the MEAL and program team. Finance and HR staff will and conduct key organizational policy awareness sessions, such as Code of Conduct, ethics, security incident reporting and management, safeguarding, combating human trafficking, conflict of interest. Safeguarding incidents could increase as living conditions deteriorate, and victims may not disclose SEA incidents due to mistrust in the system/fear of stigmatization. The organization will raise awareness on PSEA and other types of exploitation and abuse by mainstreaming protection across all program activities, assure that service-users/staff are aware of what constitutes exploitation/abuse and how to report safeguarding incidents/violations of Code of Conduct provisions, maintain an accessible and confidential compliant and feedback systems, and enforce disciplinary procedures for individuals found guilty of SEA. Volatile security situation in NES can disrupt project implementation, jeopardize the safety of service users and hinder their access to areas of operations. Therefore, the organization will conduct comprehensive risk assessments and monitor the security situation, engage with key stakeholders, adapt from static HFs to Mobile Medical Units and uphold neutrality to guarantee access.
