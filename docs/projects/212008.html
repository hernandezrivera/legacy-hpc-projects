Project Summary

IR Syria's initiative aims to address critical food security needs in northern Syria, particularly among vulnerable populations affected by conflict and displacement. By providing essential food items, agricultural support, and livelihood assistance, the project seeks to mitigate escalating rates of malnutrition and food insecurity.
Key activities of the project include:
1. Provision of Daily Bread Bags:
Each day, 10,000 households receive bread bags weighing 750g. These are distributed to every 3 individuals in the family, with larger families receiving additional bags. Distribution occurs on 26 days per month.
2.Monthly Food Parcel Distribution:
10,000 households receive monthly food parcels.
The parcels contains:
- Rice, 1 kg pack
- Bulgur, 1 kg pack
- Red lentils, 1 kg pack
- Sugar, 1 kg pack
- IODIZED SALT, 1 kg pack
- Sunflower Cooking Oil, 1 Litre PET bottle
- Tomato Paste, 830 grams Tin
- Spaghetti, 400 grams
- Bottles of Margarine (Vegetable ghee), 750g
- Green lentils, 1 kg pack
3.Supply of Canned Meat and Adahi Meat:
27,378 households benefit from the supply of canned meat and Adahi meat.
4.Distribution of Agricultural Kits:
3,000 households receive agricultural kits tailored to support wheat cultivation, olive trees, and summer vegetables cultivation.
5.Livestock Income Generation Support:
Veterinary services and support for livestock income generation are provided to 5,000 households.
The agricultural component includes inputs tailored for three types of crops:
1.Wheat Cultivation: Inputs include wheat seed, urea fertilizers, herbicide, insecticide, foliar fertilizer, and harvest bags, sufficient for cultivating 10,000 m2 of wheat.
2.Olive Trees: Inputs include compound fertilizers NPK, insecticide, foliar fertilizer, and harvest bags.
3.Summer Vegetables Cultivation: Inputs include urea fertilizers, balanced fertilizer NPK, high phosphorus, high potash, foliar fertilizers (Boron and Amino acids), plastic packaging bags, and an agriculture voucher for redeeming pesticides and fungicides.
This intervention directly addresses urgent humanitarian needs highlighted in the 2024 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), By providing immediate food assistance and supporting livelihood opportunities.
Needs:
Food insecurity in Syria is exacerbated by economic deterioration and prolonged conflict, necessitating urgent humanitarian support.
Intervention and Implementation of Activities:
The project will execute a comprehensive response strategy in coordination with local stakeholders and humanitarian partners. Activities include the distribution of essential food items, provision of agricultural support, and livelihood assistance to vulnerable households. Targeted assessments and community engagement will ensure effective implementation and mitigate potential tensions.
Bread Quality Control and Monitoring:
Dedicated monitoring staff oversee bread preparation, loading, distribution, and household delivery. Daily workers at bakeries provide reports on baking activities and bread quality. Additionally, daily monitoring in communities gathers feedback for prompt issue resolution.
Protection Risk Analysis:
Mitigation measures are in place for potential risks such as beneficiary selection, community tensions, and sexual exploitation and abuse. Transparent processes, community engagement, and complaint mechanisms prioritize beneficiary safety and dignity.
PSEA Strategy:
Beneficiary feedback and complaints are collected through multiple channels to address concerns promptly. High-risk complaints related to sexual exploitation and abuse are prioritized and addressed in line with safeguarding policies.
Risks, Assumptions, and Mitigating Measures:
Potential risks, including conflict and security threats, are identified, and mitigation measures are implemented. Transparent beneficiary selection, risk assessments, and contingency plans enable adaptive management and timely response to challenges.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

In response to the food security crisis highlighted in the FSA 2024 HNO, IR Syria's project aims to alleviate suffering in northern Syria, specifically the Idlib governorate. The complex drivers and economic challenges have left 16.7 million people in need of humanitarian assistance, with 5.5 million displaced, including 2.2 million in last-resort sites. Food insecurity is contributing to rising mortality and morbidity, stressing global acute malnutrition levels in Idleb. The project strategically intervenes through a multi-faceted approach, targeting 10,000 households. Daily provision of free bread, monthly food parcels, canned meat, Adahi meat, agricultural kits, and livestock income generation activities address immediate needs while fostering long-term resilience. These interventions are tailored for vulnerable families, whether displaced or residing in camps, unfinished buildings, or informal settlements. Implementation involves close coordination with the FSL Cluster, local councils, camp management, and other humanitarian organizations. Mapping areas with high numbers of new IDP families guide the provision of daily baked bread and monthly food parcels. Agricultural vouchers, training, and market linkages empower families for sustained income. Veterinary support enhances livestock health, contributing to economic stability. The Protection Risk Analysis identifies potential community tensions arising from beneficiary selection, emphasizing the importance of transparent communication of criteria. Local engagement, equitable representation in selection committees, and adherence to guidelines minimize risks of violence and SEA. A robust complaint mechanism ensures effective response to misconduct and conflicts among beneficiaries. The PSEA strategy is comprehensive, collecting beneficiary complaints through various channels. High-risk complaints, especially related to PSEA, child protection, or corruption, are prioritized and managed promptly. IR upholds confidentiality and data protection, providing accessible feedback options for marginalized groups. Proactively managing risks, IR Syria's enterprise risk management policy addresses potential conflicts, truck takeovers, checkpoint limitations, and border closures. Clear beneficiary selection criteria, continuous security assessments, and alternative plans for border crossings ensure project resilience. IR's commitment to addressing and monitoring risks underscores effective project implementation and impact in alleviating food insecurity in northern Syria.
