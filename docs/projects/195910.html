Project Summary

SOP intended by this project is to respond to the core needs of the BNFs in the targeted areas by providing (Core NFI Kits / Kitchen Kits / Winter clothing kits for both adults and Kids). It comprises essential NFI items. In case of new displacement, to meet the basic and NFI-related needs of a total 13.800 vulnerable Syrian IDPs families in Idlib (Ad Dana sub-district ) through targeted 2,000 families (10.000 for NFI and 3800 for Winter Kits) .
Winter Kits: Targeting 2000 Children and 1800 Adults / Adult kit estimated cost 33$ Children's Kit 28$
Initially, Space of peace (SOP) will start this project by conducting needs assessments in the targeted locations in coordination with the SNFI cluster to identify the needs, gaps, risk factors, and capacities of the targeted populations, then submitting the needs assessment reports to donors. Based on the findings of the conducted needs, SOP will allocate final quantities of Kits to be distributed in the targeted location aiming to partially meet the pressing needs. Afterward, the allocated quantities of the kits will be transported gradually to SOP’s warehouses in the targeted locations, which are already identified, and well prepared for storing the NFI kits until all the quantities are distributed to beneficiaries.
SOP already has full access to targeted locations and coordinates regularly with local authorities on the ground; nevertheless, SOP will inform the local stakeholders of the proposed project in order to ensure a smooth implementation of the project and better identification of the most vulnerable groups who will be targeted with the assistance.
SOP usually conducts any distribution following a specific cycle that starts with SOP’s M&E teams visiting the targeted location (neighborhood), preparing initial beneficiaries lists aligned with donor vulnerability criteria, and sharing it with the SOP program team to be endorsed. Then, SOP distributes receipt vouchers, to the identified beneficiaries, which clarify the type of emergency kit\s that they will receive the contents of each kits (materials and quantities), the beneficiary’s ID number, and the signature of the field project manager. Afterward, SOP hands over the kit\s to the beneficiaries who have previously received receipt vouchers and asks them to sign the receipt vouchers, which will be restored by SOP to be documented later.
Finally, and along with this process, SOP uses also the beneficiaries’ distribution lists during the distribution that are used to collect beneficiaries' HHs data (Age, ID number, family members according to age and sex, vulnerability status, contents of the emergency kits, and signature of beneficiaries and field project manager).
Putting protection mainstreaming in place, SOP recruits female and male teams whenever it carries out any kind of distributions, where these teams distribute the kits for women and men separately in two locations in order to avoid exposing women and girls to any potential assault or harassment. Additionally, SOP sets up appropriate beneficiary feedback mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints.
In coordination with donor, SOP will start distributing the NFI kits, Kitchen kit and winter clothing kits, and to the identified beneficiaries. The type of kit\s that will be distributed to each HHs will depend on the beneficiary's HHs’ needs.
During the distribution SOP’s M&E team will conduct regular distribution monitoring and PDMs and will submit them to donor on monthly basis, as well as, SOP’s project manager will send donor narrative reports, beneficiaries lists, human interest stories HISs, and media documentation including videos and photographs of the whole project stages

3 - Needs

The overall humanitarian situation remains dire in northwest Syria (NWS), with the population reaching approximately 4.4 million people as of June 2022. Humanitarian needs continue to be driven by the impacts of over a decade of hostilities exacerbated by economic deterioration and multiple displacements.More than 2.8 million are Internally Displaced People (IDPs), many of whom are living in overcrowded locations with limited access to essential services. As of June 2022, 1.8 million IDPs were living in 1,434 camps or informal sites. Women and children represent 79 percent of this caseload. Access to and availability of Food, Non-Food Items (NFIs) and protection remain limited. Through its in-kind and voucher distributions, SoP has been addressing the crucial needs for (Food, NFIs items) of IDPs in Idlib since 2016 As the number of Syrian IDPs in area continues to grow and further needs are being identified by SoP teams through periodic field assessments and post-distribution monitoring, SoP now sets the goal to continue the targeted cash assistance to Syrian IDPs in the targeted area and reduce negative coping strategies. To Assess the situation in Northwest of Syria. 76% stated that they are unable to meet their basic NFI needs due to a variety of reasons the most significant reasons were (Lack of income and unaffordable items).Based on the severity/PiN, a total of 337,960 are in need for the need of NFI out of more than 1M residing in the Dana Sub-District.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

A lack of food and NFIs, WASH facilities and shelter create a myriad of protection concerns, especially for vulnerable groups, such as the elderly, women and adolescents. SoP will continue to monitor protection risks, working with protection colleagues if specific issues arise – and will ensure that any activities including targeting, distribution and monitoring do not increase these risks. Due to security threats from the conflict, such as aerial bombing, most relief assistance is being delivered in a low-profile manner, through the door-to-door processor in public buildings where IDPs currently reside. This distribution modality helps to limit the exposure of beneficiaries and the local association members. Furthermore, contextual and historical knowledge of the local associations assists in mitigating the risk in terms of on-going security threats. Where it is deemed appropriate by the local associations, there are distribution points where beneficiaries convene to collect distributions. In some areas, public distributions are unavoidable, but care is taken to arrange them at what is considered the safest possible location and time of day. When distributions are done in a more centralized manner, utilizing information solicited from beneficiary communities, SoP will ensure that they are done at times and in places that are safe and accessible to women and girls, as well as the elderly and people with disabilities and that any barriers to access as a result of gender norms


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information are linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups. SOP will provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA Policy, and will conduct induction sessions for new staff to ensure that they fully understand the content of CoC and PSEA policy. Strict disciplinary measures will be applied on any breach of the CoC. Affected population will be consulted on the convenient reporting channels to ensure effective and timely response for any compliance. SOP is a member of PSEA network (Adopting network's CoC and Policy) and regularly coordinating on the relevant issues and disseminating the central hotline numbers during the provided activities along with the organizational own reporting channels and hotlines. SOP periodically assessing the targeted communities on the suitable reporting channels for them and feed the information into threatening the existing CRM and deploying new channels when needed.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions & Risks Assumption 1: The conducted needs assessment reports reflect an immediate need for NFI and winterization assistance among vulnerable groups of IDPs, HCs in the targeted locations. Assumption 1: Covering the most vulnerable populations with the needed NFI and winterization assistance. Risk 1: Conflict among population results during the distributions in the targeted location. Risk 2: changing SOP’s access status to the targeted locations. Risk Mitigation: Mitigation 1: SOP has previous experience in implementing such activities and has good relationships with the targeted population. However, SOP will inform the beneficiaries when delivering the receipt voucher about the distribution date and then will carry out the distributions on another day in a specific location to avoid any potential conflicts taking into consideration protection mainstreaming guidelines. Mitigation 2: SOP will clarify the beneficiaries’ selection criteria among the targeted communities to avoid any related complaints or conflicts. Mitigation 3: SOP is coordinating closely with stockholders on the ground and will inform the donor directly of any changes, challenges, or difficulties related to access and security situation.
