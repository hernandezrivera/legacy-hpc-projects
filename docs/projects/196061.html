Project Summary

This project supports children in Idleb and Aleppo from the host community and IDPs, which includes the most vulnerable groups (children under 12 years, IDP children, and children with disabilities to provide equitable access to quality learning opportunities for girls and boys in both primary and secondary school level in both formal and non-formal settings. Violet currently implements this project in partnership with SCHF, focusing on adopting the education program in Violet to ensure the continuity of education and provide access to learning opportunities throughout three modalities, mainly physical and applying blended or distance modalities if needed due to the COVID-19 pandemic. The project design complies with INEE minimum standards and safe-programming principles for children to ensure that children have access to inclusive and equitable quality education that provides lifelong learning opportunities.
The project's targeted locations (Aleppo: Jebel Saman, Idelb: Harim, Jisr-Ash-Shugur, Idleb, Ariha) with pre-identified needs (high numbers of IDPs or out-of-school children according to HNO and HRP 2022-2023). 17% of benefices under education severity 3, 77% under severity 4, 10% under severity 5.
Violet aims to achieve the overall change by improving student resilience and learning outcomes – specifically, improved resilience for children with a particular focus on girls and CWDs achieving improved primary learning outcomes, improving school safety and security, enhancing parents and caregiver and community participation through:
- The provision of quality and inclusive education in and out of camps ensures the continuity of education and all groups of children have access to quality education in all context settings with prompting safeguarding in learning facilities. Violet will provide 1) Formal education based on the SIG curriculum for 12,298 students during the academic year 49 schools (shifts) and 2) Non-formal education programs, including remedial classes, Accelerated Learning Programs (ALP), catch-up classes, literacy and numeracy classes, Self-Learning Programs (SLP) for 5,023 children based on their academic needs in the same communities where Violet supports 49 schools during the summer program
- Violet will hire 609 school personnel (49 headteachers, 462 teachers, 49 guards, and 49 cleaners) and ensure their development to deliver quality education to the enrolled students in the target schools during the academic year. In the summer program, Violet will target all communities by opening one school to target 5025 children in need of non-formal education and hire 262 school personnel (34 headteachers, 160 teachers, 34 guards, 34 cleaners)

3 - Needs

In Syria, based on HNO 2022, 14.6 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 1.2 million from 2021. Syria still remains one of the largest humanitarian responses in the world, with assistance delivered to 6.8 million people per month in the past year. 6.6M PiN for education, 47% female (3.1 M), 53% male (3.5M), 97% CHILDREN (3-17) 6.4M, 3% ADULTS (18-59) 0.2M, 7% WITH DISABILITY 0.5M. The protracted nature of the hostilities, economic duress, and the COVID-19 pandemic, continue to impact overstretched education services. With 25 verified attacks,7 military use of schools in nine months, attacks affecting schools remain high. At the national level, the data reveals that 18% are out of school and half of the children who are not in school have never enrolled. Attendance rates between boys and girls were similar but older children (12-17 years) were less likely to attend than younger children (5-11 years)The most dominant form of learning (95%) is in-person and formal. Most affected population groups: out-of-school children 18%, Young learners (only 11% 4-year,30% 5-year attend learning), Lack of access to secondary education, 36 % IDP children attend learning,50% children with a reported health condition, school personnel. Idleb has a critical confluence of negative factors: low attendance rates, the highest classroom-to-school-aged population ratio, the only governorate with triple and quadruple shifts, nearly half the schools are non-operational

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Risk of discrimination or perceptions of discrimination if the education service is provided to a specific group, e.g. IDPLikelihood: Medium; Impact: High Before implementation, Violet conducts assessments and selects most vulnerable communities based on severity; selection criteria (eligibility/vulnerability) transparently communicated through the AAP team. AAP team has access to service mapping to communicate informed with communities about alternative services. 2) Children with special needs might be excluded from education and PSS. Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High Ensure facilities and services are suitable for special needs, e.g. mobility or learning materials, conduct community events that help understanding the importance of participating in education/PSS, consultation with local stakeholders to identify excluded beneficiaries, training teachers on conflict sensitive teaching. 3) Child labor, early marriage, recruitment into armed groups. Likelihood: High; Impact: High Conduct awareness to parents (PTA) on importance of education, complementary with other assistance (cash, food baskets, NFI) to offer support to the families as positive coping mechanism. 4) Overcrowded physical infrastructure as a risk to children’s safety including war remedies Likelihood: Medium; Impact: Medium Conduct necessary rehabilitation (incl. gender sensitive WASH facilities and mobility) for a safe learning environment and mine action awareness and safety training.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Violet targets PSEA risk through a) training on PSEA for staff, BNFs, vendors; b) integrating PSEA in AAP with safe and accessible complaints mechanism; c) appointment of PSEA focal points in each location to handle reports of misconduct, d) support to survivors through case management, e) regular information of affected community on their entitlements. Potential risks:1) Misconduct of education staff against child safeguarding principlesMitigation: initial and refresher training on CSG responsibilities, behavior training for teachers, assign focal point for CSG, child-friendly AAP sessions including CSG information and how to raise complaints, zero tolerance on misconducts and rapid action after investigation.2) SEA misconduct from education staff to parents or childrenMitigation: provide information sessions to parents (sex-segregated if preferred) on expected behavior from teachers and humanitarian workers, explanation of complaints mechanism, availability of AAP staff face-to-face for sensitive complaints. 3) Request of SEA for selecting BNFs to the servicesMitigation: clear information on the provided services in terms of quality and quantity and that nothing is expected in return in any case, emphasise on complaints mechanisms, provision of multiple channels to raise complaints, referral to inter-agency hotlines as alternative.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Risk; Inaccessibility to target areas due to insecurity, large-scale military offensives, or change in territorial control with safety and security of staff/beneficiaries being compromised. Mitigation; Violet would triangulate information with available sources, use rigorous monitoring mechanism, suspend; relocate activities to areas where access is upheld; shift program resources to other areas; switch to distance learning, distribution the SLP materials. 2. Risk; Insufficient stipends continue to result in teachers and education personnel absenteeism due to additional employment and/or leaving their jobs to seek higher-paying work. Mitigation; A standardized and harmonized stipend scale that ensures adequate remuneration should reduce turnover, absenteeism, support teachers by other sectors and resources. 3. Risk: Increased COVID 19 and cholera cases in the targeted location, all project stakeholders and activities will be affected, and the staff will face many obstacles to conduct face-to-face activities. Mitigation: Protection Violet staff will take into consideration all Donor, Partner, Clusters, and Local Authority's recommendations in link with WHO and Health sector guidelines. The number of beneficiaries per session will be decreased, also using online modality to conduct the activities after discussion and coordination with donor and partner. Violet covers internet charge to families. 4. Risk; students dropping out of schools. M. Provide PSS get PTAs support
