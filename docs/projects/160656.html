Project Summary

The project aims to provide life-saving and recovery food security and livelihoods support to women, men girls and boys affected by displacement in South Sudan. The intervention will contribute to life-saving and livelihoods protection efforts for internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees and host communities in order to enable them meet their basic food and other household needs, while protecting their assets and livelihoods and resilience to shocks and stresses. Target groups will include the most vulnerable households in locations that were projected by the integrated food security and nutrition phase classification (IPC) of August 2019 to be in phase 4 (emergency) and 5 (catastrophe). A highlight for 2020 will be the default preference for using cash programming unless where it has been deemed not feasible. This decision will contribute towards revitalization of local markets as they play a very important role in building livelihoods to areas affected by displacement. With the emergency of new companies offering cash transfer services in South Sudan (e.g. MGurush and NilePay), NRC will engage with them to provide opportunities of using their services to improve cash transfer for both safety nets and livelihoods activities. With the recorded reduction in conflicts across the country in 2019 NRC will continue setting up static field offices in areas of high humanitarian need which used to be saved by rapid response mobile teams, while keeping the rapid response capacity in areas that still have access challenges. With its static presence in Jonglei, Unity, Twic, northern Bahr El Ghazal and Western Bahr El Ghazal, the intervention will provide both life saving food security, emergency and recovery livelihoods support improving food production and income generation for target groups. Community consultations using assessments and community based participatory planning will be used to enable communities identify and prioritize their needs and response strategies and their decisions regarding targeting will be sought in order for the project to reach the most vulnerable groups. Women and youth will be specifically targeted as they make up some of the most vulnerable groups that have great potential to change their situation around and help in bringing peace to their communities. The intervention will ensure good understanding of the gender and power dynamics in the target communities with the aim of avoiding doing harm or perpetuating the imbalances. Gender and age dis-aggregated information will be collected in preparation for, during implementation of activities and monitoring and evaluation of the activities. This information will be used in designing and implementation of activities in order to provide equitable and relevant support to the different target groups. The intervention will incorporate safe programming principles in order to protect the target groups and communities from protection concerns that are related to implementation of the activities in conflict situations. This will include a good understanding of the protection risks related to the intervention and identifying the mitigation measures that should be part of the intervention. These will be monitored throughout the implementation phase. Efforts towards integrated approach both targeting same communities and target groups with a variety of food security and livelihoods interventions and with other sectors will be done to strengthen the holistic approach and enhance household and community capacities to anticipate, cope, adapt and recover from effects of conflict and environmental shocks.

1 - Needs

Young children, adolescence, youth (male and female), middle aged adults (women and men) and elderly (male and female) have specific needs related to food security and livelihoods due to their physiological needs, socio-economic issues that surround societal expectations of their gender and age. The minimum food needs of children below the age of 5 years, pregnant and lactating women and the elderly have specific dietary needs that require special attention during humanitarian responses. This project understands the intra-household food consumption dynamics through previous interventions and continued interactions with communities. The recent IPC report (August 2019) estimated that 6.35 million people (54% of the population) were classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, among whom an estimated 1.7 million people were facing emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and 10,000 people were in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). However, high levels of food insecurity still persist in the country. Former Jonglei State has the highest number of people estimated to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity with 1.18 million people. In post-harvest period of January to April 2020, the food security situation will deteriorate as household food stocks start depleting and an estimated 5.5 million people (47% of the population) are likely to face crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. Climate change induced weather has seen most parts of the country receiving above normal rains in 2019, causing floods that continued after the August IPC assessment affecting general livelihoods of people as they were displaced and their shelters, crops and livestock were damaged.The acute food insecurity drivers in 2020 will include localized conflicts, climate shocks, pests and diseases, slow recovery from prolonged asset depletion, poor macro-economic conditions and sub-optimal market functionality. Food access will remain a big challenge which has always been exacerbated by the harsh economic conditions as a result of high inflation currently being experienced in the country. Food security and livelihood support will remain critical for most vulnerable populations to prevent asset depletion and reduce their adoption of negative coping mechanisms as well as reduce the number of people relying on food assistance for their survival in 2020. Household members, especially women, are exposed to negative coping strategies as they search for food and try to meet basic household needs. Chronic pattern of food insecurity has eroded livelihoods, disrupted social safety-nets and negatively affected the resilience of affected populations. This is compounded by already existing factors of low seed and tools access, mono-culture, flooding, dry spells, low area under production, lack of land use planning, weak state institutions and poor governance. As a way to cope, households (mainly female-headed households) engage in negative strategies such as reducing number of meals, eating less preferred foods, sending children to beg and selling livelihood assets. Income generating options are scarce and unprofitable due to inflation and most vulnerable households engage in casual jobs, selling firewood/charcoal and petty trade. With projections of more returns of both IDPs and refugees in 2020, if the peace agreement holds, the country needs to prepare for increased humanitarian needs in order to support the returnees. Their needs therefore will range from immediate food/cash needs to support to livelihoods establishment. Areas of settlement will require increased social and economic services in order to meet the increased needs. Collaboration with the government ministries will be high as the resources will be stretched (both human and economically).

2 - Activities

Safety net activities will be implemented to enable target households access food, diversify diet, and reduce negative coping strategies. Market based programming will be used to identify relevant modalities which will include food distributions, cash transfers and a mix of the two. The intervention will contribute and utilize the monthly market monitoring information coordinated by REACH using its joint market monitoring initiative (JMMI). This information will be used to adjust the modalities and value of transfers in order to prevent negative impacts on the local markets. Awareness creation on the use of cash and household gender dynamics will be addressed through consultative focus group discussions before and after distributions. This will be complimented by post distribution monitoring and establishment of a complaints and feedback mechanism. The activity will target recently displaced populations, food insecure households and vulnerable groups during the lean season to cover a percentage of their annual household food gap. These activities will prioritize counties in IPC level 4, 5 and some 3. Complementary emergency food production: The intervention will support the target communities with inputs and kits to complement dietary needs and act as a transition towards self-reliance activities by providing them with access to vegetable and crop production inputs, fishing kits and and livestock support. Market based approaches will be used to identify the most relevant method of accessing agricultural inputs and fishing kits for the target groups. A high percentage of beneficiaries will be women especially beneficiaries of gardening activities while men will be targeted for fishing activities. Households who are willing and able to participate in asset creation and public works will be targeted for conditional food and cash for asset interventions. These complimentary livelihoods activities will be preceded by basic knowledge and information regarding correct use of the inputs and kits. This will be done in order to promote correct usage of the inputs to increase production. Post distribution monitoring will be conducted complimented by participation in country wide assessments led by Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) i.e Seed Security Assessment and Post-Harvest Assessments. This activity will cover locations in IPC 5, 4 and 3 as per Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) objectives. The same locations will be targeted for activities that reduce dependency on food and emergency livelihoods inputs by engaging in activities that build the target households' capacity and strengthen their ability to produce food and earn income that will enable them to continue with their livelihoods. Support to value chain addition e.g milk, groundnuts and fish, preservation and marketing. Training on farm and off farm activities such as small business management skills, life skills and vocational training, small livestock fairs, seed and input fairs. Diversification of restoration of livelihoods, small grants for petty trade, village saving and loan scheme. NRC will be responsible for community engagement and sensitization with consecutive meetings with local authority to ensure they take ownership of the projects for sustainability. The implementation methodology will be based on group formation for value chain activities and seed multiplication. The project will promote the use of supply markets for agricultural inputs and services. This will be complimented by intensive skills training and knowledge transfer for relevant on and off farm topics. The project target women and will also have a specific focus on youth to reduce their engagement in negative activities. NRC’s main role will be to facilitation a linkage with private actors and beneficiary communities for sustainable transformation. All activities will be implemented through a gender and protection lenses.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

In collaboration with NRC's information, counselling and legal assistance (ICLA) core competence, the project will inform the displacement affected people about their rights through information sessions and providing case by case support to those that require legal support to claim their rights. NRC's uses advocacy to in influencing larger cases of rights abuse and works in collaboration with the protection cluster to ensure such issues receive the much needed support. As a co-lead in the protection cluster, NRC is in a strategic position to further bring beneficiary rights issues to the relevant stakeholders for constructive dialogue and mitigation.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The appropriateness of the activities to each target group will be assessed during the Community Based Project Planning (CBPP). NRC has experience conducting CBPP and all people affected by displacement (IDPs, returnees and host communities) are represented during this process in Project Management committees. Moreover, as outlined in the M&E section NRC will set up a complaints and feedback mechanism where complaints and feedback from affected populations will be captured and addressed using various mechanisms. NRC plans to strengthen internal capacities for the collection and analysis of protection data so as to be able to share information on observed trends with greater confidence, as well as to refer and follow-up on individual cases with specific protection actors.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

NRC has on-going projects in target locations which will enable the planned project to benefit from progress already made especially in relation to linkages and collaboration with government and local authorities. Work with state ministry of agriculture in these locations presents the current project with an added advantage to utilize government extension capacity and other resources provided or supported by previous and current projects. The community leadership is an important stakeholder in this and other projects as it provides much needed political will to implementation of humanitarian activities. The leadership also supports in providing security and other requirements for a conducive environment for project implementation.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is aligned to the Comprehensive Agricultural Management Plan (CAMP) in its objectives to support target groups to increase food production and general food security and livelihoods. The project will contribute to the reduction of community conflicts which are sometimes results of fighting over scarce resources and lack of livelihoods opportunities. By engaging with the youth in economic activities, the project will take them away from participating in conflicts by so doing contributing towards the peace agreement. The project will contribute towards reduction of gender-based violence by its provision of safe methods to get access to food and income for women, girls, boys and men. It lessens the burden on members of the household that are responsible for sourcing food and income especially during the lean season.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The protection environment is not sufficiently safe or stable. The project has therefore taken into consideration risk analysis and mitigation measures in order to reduce the impact. The target communities were involved in identification of protection and security risks in their locations and the analysis has been captured in NRC's area and country strategies. These risk assessments remain living documents as they are continuously updated during specific periods of the year and when certain events take place in an area. NRC practices safe programming across all its projects through training of project staff to be able to engage with communities in identifying protection issues for the different gender and age groups. Awareness raising of communities is carried out in order to enable them to understand protection issues that can affect the different people by gender, age and displacement status. NRC protection officers trained all casual laborers, incentive staff, project management committee members in PSEA, and all NRC staff have been previously trained in PSEA and prevention of gender-based violence. Basic do no harm principles and accountability to affected populations will be upheld ensuring all aspects of the project take into consideration the needs of the elderly, the sick, people with disabilities, women, youth men and children. Throughout the project, NRC protection officers will receive support and mentoring from the Protection and Gender Adviser.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The project through its cash and food for assets strategy, will allow communities to identify community assets that will support food production, disaster risk management and other livelihoods beyond the project life. Communities will be tasked to identify maintenance plans for the community assets that will be rehabilitated by the project in order to enable maintenance to continue after project closure. The safety net activities will be linked with other on-going recovery and projects aiming at building household self-sufficiency. This linkage will allow target households to move out of their dependency on relief towards self-sufficiency. By using market based programming strategies the project will contribute towards recovery of local markets which are an important system in recovery and development of communities affected by displacement. All agricultural activities will be implemented in collaboration with the state ministries of agriculture in order to ensure that government extension workers benefit from and also contribute to the technical components that will be offered by the project by so doing building capacities that will continue to be utilized after project closure. Community based extension workers such as lead farmers will be trained to continue supporting other farmers during and after project closure. Community based participatory planning approach builds the capacity of communities to identify their needs and plan on mitigation which is a skill that will remain in the community as it promotes dialogue and sharpens problem-solving skills of the community members which are very essential characteristics of a community moving towards development. Community project management committees will also be additional structures that have potential to exist beyond the project life as members are exposed to management skills for public projects.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Lack of awareness or access to GBV services - the project will collaborate with the protection cluster by highlighting such locations and lobby for coverage. The complaints and feedback handling mechanism will enable target communities raise the issues that will affect their access to humanitarian support and issues of PSEA will be dealt with in ways that will protect the victims.Housing land and property awareness sessions will aim to improve women's access and ownership of land. The project will be integrated with other core competences of NRC such as youth program, information counselling and legal assistance (ICLA), protection where peaceful co-existence initiatives will be promoted through collaborative dispute resolution between host communities, IDPs and returnees. through collaboration with NRC ICLA core competency department who will be operating in the same locations conducting group and individual information sessions, community outreach (mobile clinics, monitoring et al), sessions on securing land tenure through legal documentation, advice clients on available options, case management (mediation, accompaniment, representation) and legal assistance (documentation, business registration etc.).
