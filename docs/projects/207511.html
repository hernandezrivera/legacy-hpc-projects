Project Summary

To address the identified needs based on JMSNA-23 for Rohingya refugees mostly required the WASH infrastructure repair and maintenance, and rehabilitation of existing infrastructure. For promoting their hygiene practices including behavioural changes with gender-sensitive hygiene promotion round off the year of the NRC WASH program and contingency stock is available to support in emergency response.
NRC worked closely with WASH Sector in last couple of weeks on rationalization exercise and agreed to continue WASH intervention in 3 blocks of Camp 19. NRC will provide support with water supply, sanitation and Hygiene promotion and awareness campaign for community people's behavioural changes. Awareness sessions will be conducted through local staff or trained volunteers and WASH sector guidelines, modality, and approach NRC will follow. WASH sector-developed IEC materials will be use among the BNF. In the host community NRC will do close collaborations with UNO, DPHE, PIO, chairman and host WASH focal and other stakeholders. Minimum requirement of ISCG WASH Sector standards, NRC will conduct this intervention following the latest Covid-19 guidelines without any distributions. NRC have a CFM team for receiving community complaints and feedback for program quality improvement and quality service delivery. A PDM will be conducted for comparing the stratus with the baseline. Outcome minoring will let NRC the impactful result and BNF satisfaction service from NRC.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Rationalization aims to ensure that all Rohingya refugees in Bangladesh have equitable access to all basic services in a predictable, efficient, and timely manner, and that the humanitarian community is more transparent and accountable. NRC will work closely with the CiC office, site management, and protection focal points, camp and area focal alone with other relevant stakeholders so that services can be tailored to reflect camp needs, in order to avoid duplications and create synergies, including the continuous development of efficient and effective referral mechanisms. NRC programs are predicted to the stable operations and improving the relationship and social cohesion among refuges, host and government. In addition, NRC`s presence and provision of services would enable to understand the WASH ground needs and gaps, improve access to facilities, consider the protection risks and vulnerability. NRC will do prior consultation with sector, twig and ISCG for initiative activities in align with the approved modalities and guideline with ensure minimum and basic requirements to address BNF critical needs based on needs and gaps. NRC will do follow the sector objective, guidelines and direction that adhere to NRC policies and minimum standards while serving the BNF. NRC have the established CFM desk where BNF can share their complaints and feedback for improving the program. NRC always focus on BNF real time needs and BNF oriented service to quality delivery with a transparent and equitable manner with adapted and integrated approach. BNF engagement and participation are ensured for decision making while initiative the activities in terms of location, contribution and promote ownership. Moreover, NRC will make available its global expertise in WASH and experience gained in Bangladesh context in WASH response on these thematic areas.

3.3 - Age & Disability

The context presents specific risks for women, adolescence girls, persons with special needs, and gender-diverse populations, who continue to encounter heightened barriers in accessing WASH services. To address this issue, meaningful participation of project participants is crucial. The project aims to enhance the involvement of these diverse groups and NRC is committed to ensure inclusivity throughout the project. NRC recognizes the gender impacts, distinct needs, and vulnerabilities of women, men, boys, and girls .The organization acknowledges that women and adolescence girls, face socio-cultural barriers limiting their access to service provided for WASH intervention. Additionally, special attention is given to the elderly and persons with special needs, ensuring their specific vulnerabilities are taken into account through tailored assistance and accessibility measures. This holistic approach acknowledges that different age groups have distinct requirements, and the program is structured to cater to the specific needs of each demographic, fostering inclusivity and resilience within the community. To mitigate the risks of delivering inequitable assistance or reinforcing existing inequalities, NRC will implement community-led creative interventions, fostering meaningful participation of beneficiaries in decision-making processes. During distributions and training, NRC will create designated area for different age and gender group. Volunteers comprehensive training in child safeguarding, child protection, PSEA, and a code of conduct to minimize risks. The project aims to improve access to services for persons with special needs through activities and referrals, with coordination maintained through meetings and capacity building.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Climate change has an immense negative impact on WASH services. People affected by displacement can be impacted through water in many ways as it is the primary medium through which we feel the effects of climate change. Water scarcity can affect health, food security, economic activity, environmental wellbeing, and create conflict. Managing water resources effectively is necessary for ensuring the supply of water on which people’s health and livelihoods depend – now and in the longer term. Below are described some of the climate change mitigation and adaptation measures and initiative that NRC will implement at country office level: - NRC will prioritise solar-powered water supply systems, where technically and economically feasible- NRC will improve its environmental performance through the application of Environmental Minimum Standards (EMS) and the ‘NEAT+’ tool. - Advocacy will support communities to have access to and influence with higher-level institutions and decision-makers. Global initiatives will be developed to support country offices to achieve this including capacity building.- Reduce NRC WASH CC carbon print and other negative environmental impact of our responses. The project has number of DRM elements to address the needs of population affected by climate-driven disasters which includes, emergency WASH/hygiene kits distribution, affected infrastructure rehabilitation, raised awareness through session and campaign and will create community leadership for key messages sensitisation. NRC also will do some early action with the information that will help the community to be prepared for monsoon or disaster through the community volunteers. Also, number of capacity building training will be conducted for emergency response roster and community volunteers as a partner organisational readiness to respond to an emergency.
