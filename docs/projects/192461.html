Project Summary

Lebanon Needs (LN) is an apolitical and non-sectarian nonprofit organization founded in 2019 to deliver sustainable healthcare solutions for underprivileged patients and underserved healthcare institutions in Lebanon. LN’s goal is to bridge identified needs and gaps to help as many people as possible while supporting the Lebanese healthcare ecosystem. We are on a mission to fight medical exodus from the Patient, the Professional and Healthcare Institution points of view.
To date, we have executed an acute disaster response plan. It consisted of procuring medical necessities to cover the Beirut Blast aftermath era, the Akkar Tleil explosion, COVID-19 and the national crisis. We answered patient medical and testing needs, fulfilled hospitals’ and healthcare institutions’ requests, and established relationships of trust with the affected people. This has also allowed us to successfully conduct COVID-19 vaccination campaigns in rural areas. Our goal has always been to ensure the creation of long-term projects aimed at supporting as many individuals as possible, in the most personalized, evidence-based and scientific ways. In order to better respond to daily needs, we had to be present on the ground in a decentralized fashion. To date, we have established 10 Task Forces of healthcare professionals (nurses and medical doctors), who have been recruited and trained in 10 underprivileged areas across Lebanon: Abey, Bcharre, Berkayel, Cheikh Taba, Choueifat, Deir El Ahmar, Kafra, Lala, Majdl Selem, Saadnayel. By doing so we answer our main objectives of fighting patient neglect, healthcare exodus, and institutional decay.
Since December 2019, Lebanon Needs has been an active partner in healthcare service delivery country-wide. We executed a disaster response plan to support chronic disease patients in different areas of Lebanon with medication throughout the shortage crisis. LN also recruited, trained and deployed 10 task forces comprising of nurses and MDs in 10 underprivileged villages across the country to serve respective communities and support patients in different health sectors: COVID-19, breastfeeding, disease awareness, prevention, management, and medication. By supporting the communities and local healthcare institutions we were also able to prevent the exodus of medical professionals from these areas of interest and further root our 20+ women nurses in their villages with strength, pride, independence and hope of a better and more stable tomorrow. Respective to their areas of operation, we are able to mobilize LN crisis cells to support PHCs of interest in order to serve the target community. Furthermore and selectively, we were able to support healthcare staff at the level of the PHC by equipment and expertise support.
In Akkar, we have built 2 task forces of 5 nurses and 2 medical doctors in Berkayel and Cheikh Taba that followed up on COVID-19 patients, acutely answered the Akkar Tleil Explosion with specialized wound care and urgent medication, surveyed and selected 150 candidates for chronic disease intervention and follow-up. As a result of these efforts, Lebanon Needs has developed strong and trusting relationships with people and organizations in Akkar, working closely with local authorities such as the Akkar Network for Development (AND) NGO while developing strong on-going relationships with community nurses and PHCs, notably in Halba (Remedy Medical Center) and Berkayel (Al Amir Medical Center).
We have deployed similar task forces in the 8 remaining areas of deployment (Abey, Bcharre, Deir El Ahmar, Kafra, Lala, Majdl Selem, Saadnayel, Beirut) and wish to expand their involvement and reach within the community - to sustain the task forces first, expand our healthcare support beyond COVID-19 and reach the various subjects of non-communicable diseases (NCDs), and Women’s Health including Sexually Transmitted Diseases .

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Access to healthcare services is our primary concern. We aim to address gaps in the access and affordability of care for patients. Our goal is to prevent, manage and control NCDs in their communities through reinforcing LN’s Nurses Task Forces (NTF) deployed in underserved areas. These task forces are made up of local RNs who have been working with us for over a year. As such, this project would be supporting nurses, preventing their exodus and keeping them active in their communities.Our activities include-In coordination with MOPH, continuously training NTFs in NCDs management and additional training as required, including ANC/PNC and SRH; -Reinforcing early detection and management of NCDs and relieving potential patients from long and costly battles with pathologies and treatment;-Assisting patients to navigate the healthcare system and decreasing their healthcare bills via local partners and partial coverage and subsidizing patient transport to PHCs whenever possible;-Supporting patients with needed medical support, awareness and targeted interventions, including screenings and advanced diagnostic testing; -Subsidizing patients’ NCD-related and non-covid hospitalization costs, specifically for life-saving general surgical procedures up to 400 USD each per year; -Covering doctor’s consultations at PHCs at the rate set by MOPH which include PHCs’ overheads;-Donating needed chronic disease medications to PHCs when possible according to PHC and MOPH needs

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Main marginalized groups we serve are people with low socio-economic status and living in rural areas. 17 of the 22 RNs in our NTFs are women. Our beneficiaries are patients of any age, gender, disability status or sexual orientation who struggles from NCDs, and we aim for all of our efforts to be delivered without discrimination. To protect both our NTFs and beneficiaries: -All LN members will be trained in prevention against different forms of exploitation, discrimination and abuse including PSEA;-All beneficiaries will be made aware of a complaints mechanism to ensure our services are delivered at the highest quality of care;-Further trainings and programmatic efforts will be made to remain open and inclusive of LGBTQIA and Persons with Disabilities in our services. We are aware of issues related to violence against healthcare providers, though this has been rare in our own experience. The protection of our NTFs is of utmost importance and a protection plan will be developed

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

LN follows a grounds-up participatory approach with local RNs in-tune with their communities needs and complaints. They work closely with patients to improve health and wellbeing and safe and trusted relationships between them are at the core of our program’s success. The following accountability mechanisms are in place: -Beneficiaries will be made aware of a feedback and complaint mechanism to a FP outside the NTF for feedback, complaints or report of discrimination and abuse. The FP will work closely with the NTF to respond to feedback, rectify complaints and address reports of discrimination and abuse.-Monitoring plan: All activities are systematically monitored and evaluated. Nurses report on each patients’ needs, follow-ups, referrals, services provided, progress in behavioral changes, and any barriers arising. Patients will be asked to fill satisfaction surveys at each in-person interaction. Monthly M&E meetings between LN and each NTF for systematic issues problem solving
