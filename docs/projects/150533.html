Needs

The Health and Nutrition Cluster partners estimated the number of people in need of humanitarian health/nutrition interventions across the oPt to be 1.2 million, of whom almost 900,000 are in Gaza (HNO, 2019). The enormous rise in casualties is overwhelming Gaza’s fragile health sector, already buffeted by the 11-year blockade, the deepening intra-Palestinian political divide, an energy crisis, inconsistent payment of medical personnel, and shortages in medicines and disposables. The Gaza Central Drug Store reported that at the end of September 2018, 245 essential medicines out of the total 516 essential medicines list (47%) were at less than one month’s supply and 206 essential medicines (40%) were completely depleted at the MoH store in Gaza. In addition, 253 essential disposables out of the total 853 essential disposables list (30%) were at less than one month’s supply (WHO/Health Cluster Situation Report, 9 October 2018). As of September 2018, the prolonged electricity cuts have left major hospitals in the Gaza Strip with 4-6 hours of electricity supply per day. Gaza’s 14 public hospitals rely on donated fuel to run generators during the electricity black-outs. With limited supply of fuel to run the emergency generators in hospitals the ability of Gaza’s hospitals to provide lifesaving essential health services to 90% of Gaza’s population is seriously jeopardized. Any disruption in the power supply will be immediately life threatening for over 4,800 boys and girls, men and women per month relying on electrical devices, including neonates in incubators. In addition, this could translate into increased risks of waterborne diseases and outbreaks across the Gaza Strip (WHO/Health Cluster Situation Report, 9 October 2018). In 2018, WHO, World Bank, ICRC and other Health/Nutrition Cluster partners have been working on providing the main public hospitals with alternative energy sources, such as through solar panel, hence ensuring access of affected people to essential life-saving services as well as contributing to building resilience of the health system to withstand further shocks. Approximately 8,000 elective surgeries have had to be postponed due to this massive influx of trauma casualties, lack of bed capacity and limitations due to electricity shortages, with patients discharged early every week to make room for the next wave of casualties (HNO, 2019). As a result, a significant proportion of population in Gaza risks having limited or no access to essential life-saving health interventions. The Health and Nutrition Cluster partners estimated that the most vulnerable include those non-trauma patients who would require life-saving interventions in emergency departments of Gaza’s public hospitals, patients with non-communicable diseases, people with disabilities and elderly. Boys and girls under the age of five and pregnant/lactating women are particularly vulnerable.

Activities

The project aims to ensure access to essential life-saving health services of the most vulnerable affected population in Gaza through provision of essential drugs and medical supplies as well as providing priority health facilities with alternative energy sources. The main activities of the project are the following: 1. Procurement and provision of essential medicines and medical supplies/equipment to ensure ability of health care services to diagnose and manage life-threatening non-trauma conditions in the most vulnerable groups, including NCD patients. 2. Promotion and enhance of individual patient strategies for emergencies, including a backup supply of medications and instructions for emergency care, and promotion of community level preparedness among the population group with NCDs through awareness sessions targeting NCD patients and their families to improve their preparedness and resilience building for future shocks. 3. Promote and support alternative energy sources for priority health facilities in Gaza through coordination of partners working on solar electrification and procurement and provision of solar panels, capacity building and support to quality maintenance.
