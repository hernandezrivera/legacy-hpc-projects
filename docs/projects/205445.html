Project Summary

The overall objective of the NRC’s proposed project is to ensure vulnerable crises-affected people have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable, safe, and dignified access to life-saving assistance to meet their needs. The overall objective is to be achieved through three main cluster objectives namely; provision of food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 4 and 5), enhancement and sustainability of emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock and reduction of dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks & contextual changes.
This will ensure improved food availability and access to vulnerable households in crisis and emergency phases through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support, capacity building and reduction of dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks and shift to resiliency. NRC will ensure a timely preposition of assorted crop and vegetable seeds as well as tools, complimented by capacity building in the respective livelihoods areas thus providing beneficiaries with the means to reestablish their own production and help them address the threat of hunger. To support the pastoralist households, refresher trainings of Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) and small ruminants restocking will be supported. In addition, the fisher folk will be supported with fishing gear and skills through training on fish hygiene, processing, and preservation.
NRC will conduct vulnerability mapping during Payam Community Based Participatory Planning (CBPP) consultative meetings at the start of the project, involving key community leaders including women, village headmen, local authorities, key line ministry representatives, religious leaders and sub-chiefs. Households that are extremely food insecure as a result of the severe loss of assets as a result of disasters either natural and man-made including food production inputs and are unable to support themselves will be prioritized for agricultural inputs and livestock support. The projects provided smallholder farmers with both on-farm and off-farm support such as, agricultural value addition, processing and marketing, enterprise development, post-harvest handling, seed multiplication, village saving and loans (VSLAs) as well as cross-cutting issues as gender and GBV, nutrition, HIV/AIDS and group dynamics. In most agro-pastoralists’ communities in South Sudan, women are the main providers of the daily food for their families while men are considered to be providing security and herding livestock. However, women continue to be unequally affected by food insecurity and have less access to land and credit than men, limiting their ability to purchase agricultural tools, seeds, or hire labor that could increase their crop production Reduction in food intake as a coping mechanism has greatly affected the female population. During targeting and implementation, priority will be given to women and people with disabilities and their specific needs will be addressed. The proposed project will be implemented to disaster affected populations in Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Unity, Western Bahr Warrap, Upper Nile, Central Equatoria and Jonglei States of South Sudan where NRC has a long record of implementing food security and livelihoods activities. The project targets a total of 209, 000 through various FSL interventions. The project teams will work closely with local authorities, protection partners, government and key line ministries.

1. Needs

Ten years after gaining independence and three years after signing the most recent peace agreement, people in South Sudan continue to face deteriorating humanitarian conditions. Conflict, sub-national violence, public health challenges and climatic shocks have severely affected people’s livelihoods and hindered access to essential services. According to IPC Report (November 6th, 2023), food insecurity in South Sudan remains worrying with the latest data showing 5.83 M people (46.3%) are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity classified as IPC 3 or above (Crisis or worse). This is in addition to 14K South Sudanese returnees who fled the ongoing conflict in Sudan are also in IPC 5 (Catastrophe). The most food insecure populations are in areas that have been significantly affected by frequent climate-related shocks (flooding/dry spells), the economic crisis (currency depreciation/high food prices), conflict and insecurity – including the spillover effects of the conflict in Sudan – causing forced displacement, low agricultural production and a reduction in humanitarian assistance. The most food insecure states between September and November 2023 with more than 50% of their populations facing IPC 3 or above are Jonglei State (61.3%), Unity State (57.9%), Upper Nile State (56.1%) and Lakes State (52.8 %). During the lean season period of April to June 2024, the severity of acute malnutrition is likely to deteriorate in 66 out of the 80 counties. According to the report, Food consumption gaps are extreme, and people have largely exhausted their emergency coping strategies. Unprecedented multi-year flooding has limited agricultural production and access to markets, while the depreciation of the South Sudanese Pound and the global impacts of the Ukraine, Sudan and currently the Israel-Gaza crisis are contributing to rising prices of fuel and basic goods. This affects humanitarian operational costs as well as the purchasing power of vulnerable households which threatens to further exacerbate acute food insecurity, particularly for market-reliant populations. Meanwhile, flooding, and persistent violent conflict continue to displace households, disrupt livelihoods, and result in the loss of productive assets across the country, while also hindering the delivery of humanitarian assistance. At the same time, there has been a decline in funding for humanitarian programmes despite the steady rise in humanitarian needs. Given the high levels of severe acute food insecurity in South Sudan, there is a need for immediate scale-up of multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance to save lives and protect their livelihoods and reduce household-level food consumption gaps. The conflict in Sudan has already affected South Sudan's economic outlook, particularly for Northern States that rely heavily on imports from Sudan, leading to an increase in food prices and fuel costs. This could potentially exacerbate the protection risks and vulnerabilities of the population of the northern States but also returnees arriving from Sudan and increase food insecurity, negative coping mechanisms and acute humanitarian needs. This is happening amid the protracted conflict and frequent natural disasters including flooding in the country which has resulted into displacement, increased food insecurity and exposure of the population to the risk of asset loss and destitution. Highly vulnerable returnees are settling into areas that are already facing emergency levels of acute food insecurity and a shortage of available livelihoods due to years of compounding shocks and stressors. The majority South Sudanese returnees have endured long, arduous journeys, are traumatized, and many have experienced GBV. People are arriving in South Sudan in extremely bad health, nutrition, and physical conditions, almost all have demonstrated food insecurity and the inability to meet basic needs. Host communities are strained by increasing demand for resources and services by returnees

2. Project activities

HRP Strategic Objective 1: Vulnerable crises-affected people have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable, safe, and dignified access to life-saving assistance to meet their needs. CO 1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 4 and 5)Activities• Cash for Asset (CFA) • Conditional food distribution. • Nutrition education/sensitization • Conditional cash distribution. • Sensitization sessions on proper utilization of cash. CO 2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock.Activities• Distribution of dry season agricultural kits (VK, FK) • Distribution of main season agricultural kits (CK, VK, FK) • Support to fisheries (FK) • Establish/Re-establish kitchen Garden sites at Nutrition Facility • Seed Fairs (Input Trade Fairs) CO 3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks & contextual changes.Activities• Training of Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) and community-based extension service providers (CBESP) • Good Agronomic practices (e.g. farmer field schools, Demo plots, post-harvest management etc) • Integrated Pest Management (e.g. FAW, Desert locusts, Pesticides etc) • Fisheries Training • Provision of water pumps, Irrigation training/support • Ox Ploughs & training • PHL Storage granaries (using locally available resources as much as possible) • Canoe, Fishing, Boating materials/tools• Procurement from local traders (Agro traders, Market groups etc) through seed fairs • Produce/products aggregation • Village Savings/Loans Associations • Processing Equipment for value addition (fish, meat, vegetables, fruit, milk preservation) • Training on value addition (fish, meat, vegetables, fruit, milk preservation, honey) • Vocational & other enterprise/business related support • Provision of hives for bee-keeping livelihood groups• Support downscaling of climate information-flood early warning information through radio programmes (Radio show, solar radios etc.) • Pre-position grain and seed protection kits (hermetic bags, tilted buckets, grain bag etc.) • Provide flood adaptive seeds and approaches (rice, floating gardens), tools and fishing inputs to farmers. • Fuel efficient stoves
