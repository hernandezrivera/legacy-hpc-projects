Project Summary

Save the Children International (SCI) in South Sudan proposes this lifesaving nutrition project in contribution to the reduction of morbidity and overall mortality of children under-five and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) due to acute malnutrition in Eastern Equatoria, Jonglei and Warrap states in South Sudan. SCI will provide timely and quality management of acute malnutrition and Mother, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) services embedded in CMAM program. The program will reach a total of 15,961 U-5 SAM, 37584 U-5 MAM, 31,388 MAM PLW, BSFP 27765, BSFP PLW 13,212 acute malnourished beneﬁciaries through management of acute malnutrition and MIYCN interventions integrated with ongoing health, WASH, FSL and child protection interventions.
The response will be focusing to achieve the result of Children and PLW screened end detected and referred to appropriate nutrition centers. This will be done through active case ﬁnding using CNV/HHP, provision of acute managements of nutrition service through OTP and TSFP static sites supplemented by RRM and mobile team to the area where service is not accessible.
This project will complement the nutrition cluster response objectives through implementation of emergency nutrition program mainly using four main intervention strategies contribute to decreasing suffering, morbidity and mortality related to malnutrition among vulnerable populations in South Sudan to;
1) increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific service delivery for children, adolescents and women in prioritized but more specifically in Eastern Equatoria, Jonglei and Warrap states
11) increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition in prioritized by the Cluster and Save the Children
111) To increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and joint programming targeting vulnerable groups in prioritized locations by the Cluster and Save the Children.
1V) To strengthen nutrition information system for evidence-based nutrition response by the end of 2020.

1 - Needs

Save the Children intends to implement Nutrition programs in 2020 based on the needs identified internally and externally partners through the current programming, The IPC August 2019 report indicates that about 1.2 million children 6-59 months are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition in 2020 based on the results of the Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS), and few SMART surveys conducted during the validity period prior to the analysis. The high rates of malnutrition are attributed to several factors including high levels of food insecurity, poor infant and young child feeding practices as well as high morbidity due to a weak health system in the country.The IPC report August 2019 further indicates that the overall nutrition situation during the lean season of 2019 is Critical and worse compared to same period in 2018. Based on the IPC AMN, all the 79 counties were included in the analysis of which 58 counties are classified as IPC AMN Phase 3 and above (GAM prevalence above 9.9%). Most of Save the children operational areas including Kapoeta North, Kapoeta South, Budi, Lafon, Bor South, Niyrol, Akobo West Counties falls under IPC AMN Phase 4, and Torit and Magwi counties falls on IPC AMN Phase 3. The situation may be worse in the peak hunger seasons of 2020 as most of these counties (Kapoeta North, Kapoeta South, Budi, Niyrol and Akobo west) stays in a state of IPC Food Security Phase 4 (emergency) while Bor South, Torti, Magwi and Lafon counties falls in crisis stages in the period of January to April 2020. Poor quality and diversity of food (Minimum Acceptable Diet: 7%, Minimum dietary diversity: 15%) and high prevalence of diseases (up to 40%). The findings of KAP surveys conducted by Save the children in Abyei (June 2019) and Kapoeta South (May 2019) indicated Low prevalence of exclusive breastfeeding at rate of 71.9% and 44.6% respectively. The same report has indicated low rates of minimum acceptable diet (Abyei – 6.5%, Kapoeta South- 31.7%) and Minimum dietary diversity of (Abyei – 35.5%, Kapoeta South 48.0%). Elevated levels of food insecurity (IPC AFI Phase 3 and above) in counties also contribute to acute malnutrition. Lack of clean safe water was identified as a major issue by community members during the 2018 health review meetings. HNO 2019 reported 60% of the nutrition sites lack WASH services and the same situation applies in the health facilities. While the WASH component is an integral part of malnutrition and infection prevention. The protracted conflict in South Sudan has had a profound impact on the lives of children and their communities. At least one in two children is affected by the humanitarian crisis that is fueled by conflict and economic collapse. Furthermore, poor access to health and nutrition services during the rainy season due to poor road infrastructure constrain service delivery to areas already afflicted by high malnutrition levels. Save the Children intends to focus on treatment of severe acute malnutrition, MIYCN and system strengthening in the selected areas. The treatment of moderate acute malnutrition will be done in nutrition facilities along with SAM management and MIYCN activities. By linking this project to the existing nutrition, health, food security and livelihood, WASH and child protection programs, it will enable a combined outcome that will be brought about through the implementation of related projects. To ensure sustainability and realize functional nutrition services among the communities, continuous monitoring mechanism and capacity mapping will be carried out. Religious and community leaders and other community figures will be considered and consulted efficiently as key players for information sharing and identification of community workers and CNVs.

2 - Activities

Save the Children (SCI) intends to implement lifesaving nutrition interventions in contribution to the reduction of morbidity and overall mortality of children under-five and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) due to acute malnutrition in in Eastern Equatoria, Jonglei and Warrap states in South Sudan. SCI will provide timely and quality management of acute malnutrition and Mother, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) services embedded in CMAM program.This commitment will contribute to the reduction of morbidity and mortality of children under five and PLWs due to acute malnutrition in the stated locations through provision of timely and quality management of acute malnutrition and MIYCN services embedded in CMAM program.Save the Children intends to focus on treatment of severe acute malnutrition, MIYCN and system strengthening in the selected area. The treatment of moderate acute malnutrition will be done in nutrition facilities along with SAM management and MIYCN activities. By linking this project to the existing nutrition, health, FSL and child protection programs, it will enable a combined outcome that will be brought about through the implementation of related projects.To ensure sustainability and realize functional nutrition services among the communities, continuous monitoring mechanism and capacity mapping will be carried out. Religious and community leaders and other community figures will be considered and consulted efficiently as key players for information sharing and identification of community workers and CNVs. In summary activities will include, • Establishment and strengthening of stabilization centers, OTP/TSFP sites in remote areas• Provision of WASH Kits for families of SAM children• Establishment of kitchen gardens at OTP Sites• Conduct cooking demonstrations among MtMSG,• provision of ECD kits to OTP/BSFP sites• Conduct KAP and coverage survey • Provision of refresher training for CNVs and lead mothers of MtMSG on MUAC screening• Establish strong referral system and community mobilization, conduct regular and monthly nutrition screening for early identification of cases of acute malnutrition and provide referral services to the appropriate nutrition program• Support BSFP sites and outreach sites and to MAM beneficiaries• Conduct community sensitization about screening, defaulter tracing and follow up of malnourished children and MIYCN key messages. • Children aged 6-59 months with SAM to receive adequate, quality and timely nutritional treatment and care including MIYCN counseling, • Provide refresher training for CNW and SCI staff on CMAM and MIYCN approaches focusing on SAM and MAM management,• Support Admissions and treatment of MAM children and pregnant and lactating women in accordance the national CMAM guideline• Conduct monthly outreach to ensure service accessibility and nutrition service provision • Deploy mobile/RRM team to inaccessible area and hard to reach people moving from place to place and provide comprehensive acute malnutrition services• Continue supporting existing and additional MtMSGs to promote optimal MIYCN practices among CMAM program beneficiaries Provide MIYCN messages to mothers of children (0-23 months) and pregnant mothers and counseling of mothers with feeding problems.• Conduct Nutrition Surveillance across the nutrition services points (OTP/TSFP) including NIS, FSNMS and KAP surveys • Support rehabilitation/strengthening of OTP/TSFP sites affected by flood or other problems during the rainy season• conduct malaria test of children and treat/refer cases to the appropriate health facilities

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Households and their family members, mainly women and children, who are vulnerable to conflict and gender based violence will benefit from the project, especially from the awareness-raising and capacity-building building activities for the local communities and institutions on community based conflict mitigation and promotion of peace building. In addition to awareness and psycho-social treatments, households and their families who are affected and vulnerable to the ongoing conflict that could aggravate their malnutrition status will be supported. SCI will focus on the below mitigation measures to combat the high prevalence of GBV in South Sudan: I. Advocating for the prevention of different forms of violence based on gender roles (i.e. child, early and forced early marriage);II. Specific consideration to ensure safe spaces for trainings, travel to outreach sites (safety audits);III. Adapt selection criteria to include specific protection related vulnerabilities - identification of at-risk and vulnerable cases, with referrals to protection service provider for support (including SCI’s Child Protection Program in the same area);IV. Using opportunities (review and coordination meetings), enforce Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Child Safeguarding policies by adhering to four commitments (awareness, prevention, reporting and response);V. Linking the proposed SSHF project with other programs under implementation in the area that strengthen community based structures which focus on training of community volunteers in broader protection issues such as GBV prevention and response

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The following measures will be taken in order to ensure the accountability and safety of vulnerable groups, including women, people living with disability, elderly and children: I. Information sharing regarding entitlement of assistance types, quantity, duration – to ensure that whole target communities and vulnerable groups have clear understanding of the targeting and selection criteria, ration entitlements and utilization standards, roles and responsibilities.II. Optimization of inclusive participation: as part of the targeting and beneficiary selection process, communities will be consulted through community forums to ensure that the families most in need are included in the program.III. Gender and protection: ensuring the assistance provided does no harm to the safety, dignity and integrity of women, men, boys and girls (For example: considering the distance to/from the mobile outreach site, scheduling service hours early in the day to avoid traveling before/after dark).IV. Visibility: ensuring timely communication and transparency to communities about the project, its objective, specific activities and beneficiary rights and entitlements.V. Complaint and feedback mechanisms: Save the Children will conduct community sensitization and information sharing sessions on project activities and explore ways of strengthening or setting up joint complaint and feedback mechanisms to enhance accountability and community participation throughout the project period. Feedback from the community and stakeholders would also be collected during needs identifications, KAP surveys, assessments and meetings with beneficiaries. The target groups will be representation from girls, boys, women and men, together with community and government leaders and community structures from onset of implementation, to ensure communities are well informed about the project and getting assistances considering their safety and consultation with identified individual needs and vulnerabilities.
