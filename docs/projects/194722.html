Project Summary

Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA) is one of the most food insecure, conflict and flood-prone areas in South Sudan. An alarming 80% of its population will be in IPC3+ towards the peak of the lean season in 2023, with 45% in IPC4; currently 23,000 people are arleady in IPC5. The resilience and ability of communities to absorb & adapt to shocks is significantly compromised following years of conflict and four consecutive years of atypical flooding. Subnational and localized conflict, coupled with severe flooding, led to loss of harvests, livelihoods and productive assets. A large part of the population resorted to emergency coping mechanisms (ZOA Dorcas, 2022). IDPs, returnees and host communities are all affected, while women, girls, youth, people with disabilities (PWD) and elderly are uniquely impacted by the shocks. Marrying of young girls is a coping mechanism in time of crisis – considering the current food security situation, this is a major protection concern. Women and girls face high levels of (S)GBV when carrying out daily duties; further exacerbated by conflict and flood-related displacement. With few opportunities to engage in productive livelihoods, PWD and elderly face higher levels of food insecurity, while youth have little prospect for alternative livelihood opportunities.
To build resilience and adaptive capacities of communities in GPAA to the adverse effects of conflict and climate and reduce their dependency on food and agricultural inputs, ZOA Dorcas proposes the ‘Sustainable Food and Livelihood Security in GPAA through Conflict and Climate Change Resilience and Adaptation’ Project; an integrated humanitarian-development-peace nexus response. The project targets Pibor, Lekuangole and Gumuruk, and strengthens resilience of 4,597 most affected households (2,303 female, 2,294 male)/27,582 beneficiaries (F:13,818, M:13,764) to absorb and adapt to shocks experienced. Building on a proven and long-standing track record, through the proposed project, ZOA Dorcas will strengthen community capacity to produce sufficient food, mitigate conflicts and build resilience to mitigate and adapt to the effects of climate change.
The overall objective of the proposed project is: To reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs in order to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks and contextual changes (CO3) (while also contributing to CO2). Specific objectives (SO) include: SO1) Women, men, youth and children including those with disabilities have improved food, nutrition and livelihood security; SO2) Communities in Pibor have strengthened local capacity and resilience to mitigate the impacts of climate change and natural disasters; SO3) Communities in Pibor have increased local capacity to address conflicts peacefully and promote social cohesion. The proposed intervention will create a longer-lasting impact along the HDP-nexus and activities are conflict-sensitive. At minimum, 50% of target beneficiaries are women, and age and disability are considered. The proposed project is based on various internal and external needs analyses and input from the community and authorities.
ZOA Dorcas has over 10 years of experience in GPAA in the areas of FSL, WASH and Peacebuilding and is known as a trusted, reliable partner by the community and authorities. Most of ZOA Dorcas staff originates from the area, which significantly enhances contextual understanding and trust. The proposed intervention builds on ZOA Dorcas’ existing track record in FSL and peacebuilding and projects in emergency food assistance and FSL resilience. As for peacebuilding, ZOA Dorcas has worked with youth, peace committees (LPC), promoters (PP) and various other stakeholders, and has in place a contextualized peacebuilding manual developed with the LPCs, PP and youth which will be mainstreamed across the response. Coupled with DRR experience, this makes ZOA Dorcas uniquely positioned to execute the proposed project.

1 - Needs

Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA) with an estimated population of 228,287, is one of the most food insecure, conflict and flood-prone areas in South Sudan. According to the November 2022 IPC analysis, at the moment of writing (Oct-Nov ’22) 23,000 people in GPAA face catastrophic food insecurity (IPC Phase 5); followed by 11,000 people between December 2022 and March 2023 – with Akobo the highest country-wide. An alarming 80% of the population in GPAA will be in IPC3 or above towards the peak of the 2023 lean season, of which 45% in IPC4 (Emergency). Key drivers of food insecurity include 1) (the effects of years of) conflict and insecurity disrupting access to livelihood and markets, 2) climate shocks affecting crop/livestock production/productivity, 3) continued poor economic performance, a depreciating currency, 4) an increase of fuel and food prices in South Sudan due to the Ukraine conflict; coupled with a growing global competition for scarce humanitarian resources. Since late 2019, GPAA has been facing a series of compounding shocks exacerbating existing vulnerabilities. Areas particularly affected include Gumuruk, Lekuangole and Pibor. Since 2019 – when GPAA saw the most severe flooding in a generation – GPAA has seen four consecutive years of severe flooding. An assessment conducted by the RRC in August 2022 cited the counties of Pibor (Kondako, Mator, Langachut and Muduria Payams) and Lekuangole (Lekuangole Centre, Manyahokor and Akozkozea Payams) are among the worst affected, and reported widespread displacement, inaccessibility of roads and river ways, and a destruction of crops and productive assets among others. Further, GPAA continues to experience high levels of insecurity and violence. In 2020, GPAA saw the worst sub-national violence since the civil war (UNHRC), followed by the 2021 conflict leading to displacement, cattle-raiding, a destruction of crops, assets and facilities (burning of Gumuruk market, humanitarian facilities) – events happened at a crucial moment in the agricultural season. In 2022, the Bor-Pibor road closure led to severe market shortages and sharp inflation; the recent market burn-down in Pibor town further compromises market functionality and availability of food and NFIs (see October 2022 FSL cluster market assessment by WFP, ZOA Dorcas and FSL partners). Clearly, insecurity, flooding and ongoing shocks have severely impacted the food security situation in GPAA and resilience and ability of communities to absorb and adapt to shocks. Food consumption aps are extreme and people have largely exhausted their emergency coping strategies. With the displacements in Lekuangole and Gumuruk since 2019, farmer lost not only their means of production but 3 consecutive harvests. The August 2022 ZOA Dorcas needs analysis confirms that 75% of the 230 sampled households in Gumuruk and Lekuangole resorted to emergency Livelihood Coping Strategies (notably selling productive assets, withdrawing children from school, migration); a serious cause for concern.An immediate scale-up of humanitarian assistance is needed to save lives, prevent a collapse of livelihoods and protect and support livelihoods. Therefore, ZOA Dorcas proposes the ‘Sustainable Food and Livelihood Security in GPAA through Conflict and Climate Change Resilience and Adaptation Project’; an integrated HDP response that addresses emergency needs, while working towards resilience building. Informed by an assessment of needs, the project targets Pibor, Lekuangole and Gumuruk, and strengthens resilience of 4,597 most vulnerable households (F: 2,303, M:2,294)/27,582 beneficiaries (F:13,818, M:13,764) – targeting households most affected by flooding, displacement and conflict. At minimum, 50% of target beneficiaries are women, and age and disability are considered. ZOA Dorcas builds on a proven track record in GPAA and in the sectors of FSL and peacebuilding.

2 - Project activities

Refer to the project summary for overall and strategic objectives. Proposed outputs and activities (Att) include: SO1-Output (Ot) 1.1 Households have increased agricultural production and availability to farm inputs. Att 1.1.1 Formation and training of 60 farmer groups targeting 1500 households: ZDSS will form and train 60 farmer groups to restore affected arable land and in farm production through the FFS approach. Trainings follow the agricultural cycle (clearing, planting, weeding, harvesting & post-harvesting) succeeded by ongoing monitoring. Att 1.1.2 Provision of agro-inputs and tools to 1500 farmers: To provide recovery assistance, ZDSS will provide 1,500 farmers with agriculture kits/tools incl. 1 crop kit, 1 vegetable kit and tools (also used for DRR att) per HH. Att 1.1.3 Training on post-harvesting handling (PHH): To increase resilience, the project will train farmers on PHH to reduce losses while ensuring high quality final products, as well as appropriate seed preservation techniques. Att 1.1.4 Support collective marketing committees (CMC): The project will leverage investments of ZDSS smallholders’ resilience project, supporting 3 CMCs to conduct routine agricultural market surveys. CMCs disseminate information to fellow farmers to inform decision making.SO1-Ot 1.2 Women and children have improved access to a diverse diet and knowledge about nutrition. Att 1.2.1 Formation of 3 mother-to-mother support groups (M2MSG): ZDSS will form and train 3 M2MSG with 20 members each (1/Payam, representing farmer groups), to increase awareness on optimal nutrition in farmer groups and beyond. Att 1.2.2 Formation of 3 kitchen/community-demo gardens at nutrition sites (1 per Payam): 3 kitchen gardens at nutrition sites, incl. seeds/tools, supported by M2MSG are established (ZDSS confirmed gaps with nutrition partners). Att 1.2.3 Public awareness raising on nutrition, hygiene and sanitation including cooking demonstration: 2 community sensitizations/Payam through targeted outreaches and cooking demonstrations by M2MSG. Expected reach 2500 people (incl. farmers). ZDSS has an approved curriculum.SO1-Ot 1.3 Women and youth have access to increased income-generating opportunities for sustainable livelihoods. Att 1.3.1 Support 50 youth (30 female) with business skills training: Leveraging ZDSS 2021-2022 Pibor market assessment and skills training conducted, ZDSS will train 50 youths in business skills to set-up IGAs through self-guided analysis and development of business plan. Att 1.3.2 Provision of start-up kit: Youth receive a starter kit – in kind support anticipated based on market functionality. Att 1.3.3 Set-up farmer VSLA groups & provide VSLA kit: ZDSS will form 6 VSLA out of selected farmers groups and provide these with VSLA kits and training.SO2-Ot2.1 Support local capacity in DRR and sustainable management of natural resources.Att 2.1.1-2.1.4 Formation & training of 3 community-managed DRR committees (CMDRRCs) incl. Participatory Disaster Risk Assessments (PDRAs), development and implementation of CAP: ZDSS will train 3 CMDRR and support the implementation of CAPs (e.g. dike, tree-planting, etc.) on cost-sharing basis. There will be an intentional link between CMDRRCs, and farmers supported, to increase resilience against shocks. Att 2.1.5 Pilot use of local biomass briquettes & fuel-efficient stoves: training women in briquettes-making, distribution of fuel saving stoves to vulnerable HH. SO3-Ot3.1 Peacebuilding structures supported and trained. Att 3.1.1 Formation, training and coaching of 3 peace committees (PCs) & 100 Peace promoters (PP): ZDSS will re-engage and train PCs and PP. Att 3.1.2: Facilitation community peace dialogue meetings: together with youth, PC and PP trained will facilitate community dialogues with youth championing peace and social cohesion among fellow youth. Crosscutting: inception/exit meetings, project management committees (PMC). All trainings include peacebuilding mainstreaming.

3 - Project indicators

ZDSS has in place a comprehensive, adaptive MEAL system to track progress towards indicators and promote learning. Indicators for the overall objective include % of the target population with acceptable Food Consumption Score (FCS) (measured via project baseline) and Increased % of targeted household with a high resilience capacity score (measured via FSNMS also providing inputs on Livelihoods Change Index). Where possible, ZDSS will contribute to data collection by the FSL cluster/OCHA in GPAA. ZDSS will specifically measure progress towards the strategic objectives (SO), outputs (Ot) and activities – see section 2 for detailed SO, Outputs and activities descriptions.SO1 indicators: FCS and FSNMS Resilience Score.Ot1.1 HH Indicators: % of targeted HH reporting increased production of (nutritious) crops AND Volume of agricultural land restored to agricultural production (measured via # feddans cleared, planted)Ot1.2 Indicators: # women reached via nutrition campaigns; # kitchen gardens establishedO1.3 Indicators: # of youth and women who have a job started their own business and/or generate income through an IGA. # functional VSLA groups.SO2 Indicator: # targeted HH adopted & applied DRR measures in production/livelihood systems.Ot2.1 Indicator: #CMDRR committees formed and trained. # Functional action plans in place. % women CMDRR in roles mirroring men.SO3 Indicator: # of conflicts in target communities that are both addressed and resolved peacefully by community structures and/or state actors. % women CMDRR in roles mirroring men.Ot3.1 # people attending conflict resolution related meetings.ZDSS will measure accountability to affected populations, gender mainstreaming and functionality of feedback and complaints mechanism through beneficiary satisfaction with information, participation and FCM and % women in committees set up via the project in roles mirroring men. Other monitoring activities include:1) Baseline, Midterm (MTE), End line: ZDSS will conduct a baseline, MTE and end line to measure the outcome and output indicators and progress towards these. Trained enumerators collect data using KOBO with a representative HH sample – the process is coordinated by the MEAL officer. Yield analyses for farmer households are included. Data is quantitative, topped up with qualitative data via FGD with PMCs. 2) PDM: after distribution of farm inputs and starter-kits, ZDSS will conduct PDMs using existing tools, through enumerators. The PDM reviews i.e. beneficiary selection process, quantities received vs. expected, information-sharing, distribution process satisfaction, effectiveness of FCM, gender, protection and PSEAH. 3) Ongoing M&E: Ongoing M&E of activities and progress. Beneficiaries (farmer HH, PC, PP, youth/women, M2MSG, etc.) are registered into (protected) databases. On weekly basis, ZDSS collects data on conflicts resolved/addressed by PCs, and savings and loans taken by VSLA members – entered digital databases. 4) Inception & exit meetings are held at project start and end at County, Payam level. 5) Reporting: ZDSS has a digital internal activity reporting system which is used to ensure timely reporting into 5W. 5) Feedback/ complaints mechanism (FCM): In line with ZDSS standards, ZDSS will roll-out effective FCM based on community preference. FCM are addressed as per ZDSS FCM review procedures. Staff receives training on FCM and FCM-SOPs to ensure effective roll-out. ZDSS ensures pictorials and diverse ways of info-sharing to ensure inclusion. ZDSS operates a FCM telephone line and has various channels for reporting (FCM register, telephone, email). 6) Data disaggregation is done by gender/age/disability (Washington short-set)/Resident status/Location (urban-rural). 7) ZDSS will employ one full time MEAL staff in GPAA reporting to the MEAL coordinator based in Juba and working closely with the project team on adjustments where appropriate - This is key due to the dynamic, fluid project environment.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

ZDSS has in place various mechanisms to ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members in society, notably elderly, representatives of different age sets, girls and women and people with disabilities are included. As stated above, at the start of the project, ZDSS will do safety/disability audits (ZDSS has extensive experience with these working on several disability inclusion projects) to assess accessibility and safety of project activities and locations. Audits are done together with community representatives, including at least women, elderly and people with disability representative on top of community leaders/chiefs. Based on the results, ZDSS together with the representatives may further adapt the design / location of activities and improve accessibility of sites. It is expected the project will make specific adaptations for farmers with a disability/elderly, and youth with a disability. This will not only enhance participation, but also avenues for feedback and input from this specific target groups.ZDSS will furthermore adapt targeting criteria; all activities should include a minimum of 50% women, and leadership positions are to be divided equally over men/women (preferably in mirroring roles). Activities to support youth with business training and IGA will target slightly more girls/women. This will enhance participation and provide avenues for feedback and input through various feedback and complaints.ZDSS ensures communication related to FCM / project entitlements is done via multiple channels including pictorials and promote diverse ways of info-sharing to ensure inclusion for illiterate community-members. ZDSS together with community structures will identify preferred community based FCMs that are inclusive and accessible to illiterate community members. As above, the PMCs will consist of 10 members per committee, and include at least 50% women in roles mirroring that of men; the committee will furthermore represent different age groups including 1 youth and 1 elderly and 1 person with disability representative which promotes feedback from a diverse group of stakeholders.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

At the start of each project, ZDSS organizes project inception meetings where information about the project, selection criteria and entitlements are shared to promote transparency, information-sharing and accountability. Also, during this meeting as well as via public information systems (site banners, radio) and ongoing project activities, information is shared about ZDSS Feedback and Complaints Mechanism (FCMs) and reporting channels (including FCM forms/register, email addresses, reporting phone number). During this meeting, as well as initial meetings with the Project Management Committee (held at minimum on quarterly, where possible on monthly basis) and project participants, ZDSS will engage further inputs from the community on preferred ways of giving feedback (e.g. in previous projects, based on this input, ZDSS employed a feedback desk at the RRC/Payam administrator’s office). Through safety and disability audits, ZDSS will ensure FCMs are inclusive and accessible to illiterate community members. Specific dedicated moments for soliciting feedback include meetings with the Payam-level PMC on (at minimum) quarterly, preferably monthly basis. The PMCs represent a cross-section of society. The meetings provide a forum for sharing of information about the project on the side of ZDSS and the community, discussing the project’s progress, challenges and planning. During these meetings, which are attended by the ZDSS MEAL officer alongside project staff, ZDSS will seek active input on feedback and complaints for adaptation and learning and reflect on the operationality and effectiveness of the FCM. FCM are addressed as per ZDSS FCM review procedures. Staff receives training on FCM and FCM-SOPs to ensure effective roll-out. ZDSS ensures pictorials and diverse ways of info-sharing to ensure inclusion. ZDSS operates a FCM telephone line and has various channels for reporting (FCM register, telephone, email).ZDSS will measure accountability to affected populations, gender mainstreaming and functionality of feedback and complaints mechanism through beneficiary satisfaction with information, participation and FCM and % women in committees set up via the project in roles mirroring men. Other monitoring activities where functionality of the FCM and information-sharing with project participants is reviewed include MTE, end line and PDMs.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

ZDSS has a zero-tolerance policy to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) as outlined in its Code of Conduct embedded in its Integrity Framework. The Code of Conduct describes standards of behavior for ZDSS staff and representatives – these includes general standards for PSEAH. At the start of each project, ZDSS organizes project inception meetings where information about the project, selection criteria and entitlements are shared to promote transparency, information-sharing and accountability. Also, during this meeting as well as via public information systems (site banners, radio) and ongoing project activities, information is shared about ZDSS Feedback and Complaints Mechanism (FCMs) and reporting channels (including FCM forms/register, email addresses, reporting phone number). The operational FCM will allow project and non-project participants to report via safe channels in case of protection concerns/PSEAH. ZDSS together with community structures will identify preferred community based FCMs that are inclusive and accessible to illiterate community members. Safety and disability audits are conducted at the start of the project to mitigate risks and ensure accessibility for elderly and PWD. Further, questions on safeguarding related to the project (e.g. distribution process), as well as ZDSS general operations, are included in PDMs, MTE and end lines. ZDSS have a zero-tolerance policy for SEA, and staff have a duty to report. Staff is furthermore oriented on local referral (GBV) pathways.On quarterly basis, ZDSS organizes focus group discussions/meetings with Payam Project Management Committees to discuss the progress of the project, discuss challenges, solutions and actively seek for feedback/compliant – while SEAH-related matters are reported with utmost discretion and confidentiality, ZDSS believes actively seeking for feedback during such meetings and sharing of reporting lines, will encourage reporting via confidential reporting lines as well.Organization-wise: ZDSS conducts reference checks with all staff, including volunteers. Reference questions refer to PSEAH. Each staff, including volunteers or any other contracted party, signs and is oriented on the policy before employment. ZDSS Integrity Coordinator supported by male and female field focal points will train (new) and existing project staff on PSEAH at the start of the project and provide refresher training throughout the year, to ensure staff understand, appreciate and adhere to policies. As for internal reporting lines: staff can report this via a KOBO tool, or directly to the Integrity Coordinator or HQ or Independent (whistleblowing) lines. ZDSS has in place proper complaints reporting and handling incl. investigation policies and procedures. Staff have a duty to report and are reminded to report feedback and complaints including integrity issues on a monthly basis.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

ZDSS is a learning organization and sees feedback from beneficiaries as an essential and integral part of its work. Therefore, ZDSS ensures beneficiary feedback is included in the design, implementation and evaluation of its projects. Building on over 10 years of experience in GPAA in a range of sectors notably FSL and Peacebuilding, ZDSS has a good understanding of the local context. The proposed project is grounded in various external and internal needs assessments, results from internal evaluations, feedback from communities on ZDSS current projects and requests for further support notably in the area of FSL and youth skilling, as well as Peacebuilding by community leaders, beneficiaries and the wider community. ZDSS will continue learning and receiving feedback from communities throughout the project lifetime, notably through Payam-based project management committees. The PMCs will consist of 10 members per committee and include at least 50% women in roles mirroring that of men; the committee will furthermore represent different age groups including 1 youth and 1 elderly representative and 1 person with disability. ZDSS will meet PMCs quarterly, and document lessons learned, feedback. Besides ZDSS project staff, ZDSS MEAL officer will participate in meetings and feedback is discussed during ZDSS monthly project review meetings. Where needed changes will be made to the design of the project. The MEAL officer will see to it that learnings are captured. Other opportunities for soliciting feedback and adapting accordingly include PDMs, MTEs and day-to-day interactions with project participants. ZDSS discusses results of PDMs and MTEs with staff and captures points for learning and adaptation.

trainers are not alone with trainees 3) Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaints and feedback mechanism - Ensure information on FMC is shared throughout the project lifetime and via different channels including inception meetings, meetings with PMC, and promoted during meetings with/training of project participants- Ensure available FCM are accessible and inclusive to vulnerable and illiterate community members- Ensure FCMs are based on community preference- Training of staff on FCM and FCM-SOP and regularly reminding staff about their reporting duty. 4) Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources. - Where possible develop selection criteria jointly with communities and community leaders through community-based participatory selection approach (while adhering to minimum criteria of 50% women, representation of age and people with disability)- Ensure a cross-section of community is targeted through the response- Ensure validation of pre-selected households by ZOA Dorcas staff- Ensure transparent and clear information-sharing on selection-criteria and process with community leaders, stakeholders and the wider community – among others through project inspection meetings - Have in place operational FCM

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

The project directly contributes to Collective Outcome 1. The project addresses root causes of food insecurity while also addressing direct emerging needs of populations in Pibor. Pibor is classified as IPC 4 and hence targeted project participants are likely in IPC 4 or above. The project is based on the assumption that if people have enough capacity to produce nutritious and sufficient food to cover basic food security needs, if people have enhanced capacity to cope with (climate)-shocks and stressors, and if people have the ability to engage in income-generating activtiies, this will lead to increased stability within communities and greater resilience which will reduce the number of people in IPC 4 and IPC 5. Peace at the grassroots level is considered a prerequisite for access to farming land and sustainable livelihood opportunities; in other words, strengthening local peacebuilding structures to address conflicts peacefully and promote social cohesion underpins the whole intervention. Farmers engaged will benefit from training and agro-inputs; coupled with training in DRR to support climate-change adaptation, and peacebuilding interventions, this will improve resilience against shocks and promote direct food insecurity through land restoration and agricultural production. It is expected project activities will directly enhance household food insecurity and strengthen farmer’s capacity in the long run. This is further supported through direct support in post-harvest handling which will reduce postharvest losses and promote sustainable preservation techniques. Awareness on nutrition, food utilization, hygiene and sanitation through M2MSG, kitchen gardens at nutrition sites and outreaches have the potential to safe lives and reduce the number of people in IPC4/5. DRR interventions (e.g. dikes, tree planting) and training on climate-smart agriculture, protect community and farming fields from the impacts of flooding which will further promote resilience and reduce the likelihood of flood-related crop destruction. Peacebuilding activities are aimed at promoting cohesion and stability and promote people’s ability to go to their farming fields to produce crops and access other livelihood opportunities.Further, VSLAs, agriculture value chains, and youth trainings in business skills, as well as trainings and interventions to promote market access, will enhance HH access to income, and increase HH ability to provide for basic needs including food. It is important to note that the intended project start date is January 2022 meaning investments at the start of the project run concurrently with the intended impact period of CO1 (with harvest of staple crops after the rainy season).
