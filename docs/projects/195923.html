Project Summary

As the crisis continues and schools are destroyed or inaccessible to students, the schools that can be found are not equipped to take in students. The objective of this project is designed to contribute to HRP strategic objective 3 increase resilience and access to services through increased safe and equitable availability and access to quality education reaching total beneficiaries 13,239 (12,920 children and 319 teachers and educational personnel). A focus will be on the IDPs in camps in Idleb and Aleppo governorates( 100 % of targeted children will be in within severity 3, 4 and 5.), through formal and non-formal education programs, and inclusive Support for 8 schools and 5 non-formal education centers. the project will contribute to Help to create a safer and more secure environment for children. The project will focus on children aged 5-7 years in and out of school by providing set of services that targets the gaps and needs of the most vulnerable groups while ensuring the centrality of protection.
MF is keening to achieve the following:
Increase access 12,920 Children (6520 girls & 6400 boys,150 CwD, 5 – 17 years old) affected by conflict in NW Syria to education services, by providing formal and non-formal education programs, divided as following: 10600 children will attend formal education classes in 8 schools and 2320 children will attend non-formal education ( ALp & remedial program) for two cycles in 5 non-formal education centers in line with the distribution of school bags, also operational cost and other needed expenses will be provided, MF will conduct 13 BTL campaigns, focusing on encouraging both students and their parents to get back to schools.
Increase access 12,920 Children (6520 girls & 6400 boys,150 CwD, 5 – 17 years old) to a
clean, sterile and safe environment in 13 schools and education centers in NW Syria, by conducting light-scale rehabilitation 104 classes due to usage during passing last academic year (e.g: one window
in every class, door, desk, etc), ensuring accessibility for CWDs, providing heating materials—Fuel-based heater and fuel— and conducting precautionary measures in schools such as measuring temperature regularly, social distancing and distribution of masks in those targeted schools.
Improve the quality of educational services within a safe and protective learning environment, by conducting a training program for 319 teachers ( 159 female ,160 male). on PSS,EIE,INEE, educational subjects, child-centered and protective pedagogy, and PSEA. Trained teachers will reflect on their in-school attitude and will enhance relations and communications with students. Also, incentives will be provided for teachers and educational personnel.
12,920 of children benefiting from school-based psychosocial support.
The inclusion of CWD will take place throughout most of the project activities, for
example, the rehabilitation will take into consideration CWD's needs, as well as training for staff will
be conducted on how to deal with children with disabilities.
MF will ensure the participation of all relevant stakeholders at all stages of the operations
management cycle. MF will be responsible for the design, implementation, monitoring and
evaluation (M&E), and closure phases. MF will manage the project, provide the contractual
services, ensure the required coordination among clusters, and ensure high-quality results
and efficient progress through the provision of capable programmatic specialized staff.

3 - Needs

In reference to HNO 2022, more than 2M children are out of school. People in need trend (2017-2023) 15.3 M; Women 4.5M, Men 3.8M , Boys 3.8M, Girls 3.2M.WOMEN (29%/ 4.5M), CHILDREN (46%, 7M) ELDERLY (4%, 678K) WITH DISABILITY (17% ,2.6M).The number of children attending school is lowest for the IDP population in sites/camps – particularly for the Aleppo IDP population. In addition, IDP households with a head of households and/or a child living with a disability report slightly lower attendance rates compared to the overall IDP population84 and are also less likely to prioritize education needs.85 Half of children with reported health conditions, injury or disability reported attending school, compared to 84% attendance of children without these barriers. Of all population categories children with reported health conditions, injury or disability were least likely to attend school. For school-aged children not attending school, most had dropped out (51%) or had never enrolled (45%), with families citing no school for their child’s age group at the location, lack of affordabilityand the child needing to work to support the household as primary reasons for not attending. Over 70% of IDPs suggested opening more schools and improving the physical conditions of existing ones (51 per cent) to increase attendance rates 86. Moreover, in light of the Syrian war spanning eleven years and the loss of a whole decade of education, 685 confirmed attacks on schools were recorded.

Monitoring and evaluation will be in line with all project phases. Besides, M&E staff has established many channels for complaints mechanism.MF will directly implement the activities since MF has full access to the targeted location.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Teachers or other education personnel participate in physical punishment, act violently, bully and neglect students.*Mitigation:Learning facilities should have an enforced code of conduct. The code of conduct shouldcover the conduct of teachers and other education personnel that enables learning spacesto being welcoming, inclusive safe, violence-free and promote well-being and a protectiveenvironment.Children are over-exposed to the risk of, or experience, harm by adults suchas teachers, school personnel, organization staff, other adults or older students.*Mitigation: Child Safeguarding polices and related systems should be part of anyeducation programing and should be regularly monitored and corrective action takenwhen needed. Aid workers or associated people use their power or influence to sexuallyexploit or abuse the people they should be serving, particularly children. The risk may beheightened for children with disabilities, those unaccompanied, orphans and youngerchildren.*Mitigation: Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)policiesshould be in place.Awareness and training sessions for staff, diversity, safeguarding, gender and inclusion.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policies took in place, practiced and monitored. Polices cover standards of behavior for the organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and include accountability measures. Polices address risks at the organizational level and the program level.MF will Implement training on PSEA to be mandatory for all personnel. Periodically, with the support of Field Managers, Focal Points organize a training session for recruited staff, ideally within three months of recruitment. MF will incorporate an ‘attitudes and beliefs’ session in the training as many respondents highlighted this as the key obstacle to reporting.MF is a member of PSEA network so when it comes to sensitive complaints they are usually referred to PSEA focal point persons, which are the HR manager and M&E manager, therefore they deal with such cases in high confidentiality.Besides MF conducted trainings on PSEA as for all its personnel.

All these conditions will play an important role to pave the way for children (girls, boys) and youth to have access to education;Risk1:Interference by local authoritiesLikelihood: Possible.
