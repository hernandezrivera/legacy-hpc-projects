Project Summary

The overall humanitarian needs in South Sudan are increasing due to worsening economic conditions, effects of climate change, influx of refugees and returning individuals among other challenges. There are increasing trends of food insecurity and acute malnutrition as revealed in the Acute Food Insecurity and Malnutrition (IPC) Analysis Report (November, 2024). 7.7 million people (57%) of the analyzed population are projected to face high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above between April - July 2025. Further, the IPC Report revealed that 2.1 million children aged 6-59 months are suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition including 650,000 cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM), and 1.4 million children and 1.11 million pregnant or breastfeeding women (PBW) cases of Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) are anticipated.
Jonglei that includes Greater Pibor Administrative Area (Pibor County) and Northern Bahr el Ghazal State (Aweil East, Aweil North and Aweil West Counties) are amongst counties projected to experience Emergency (IPC Phase 4) in the lean season. While the Aweil counties are facing new burden of refugees, returnees and IDP’s, the effect of on-going floods and disease outbreak, Pibor County faces persistent insecurity and cyclic conflict situation. All four counties have high prevalence rate of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) in children under-five based on weight-for-height Z-score (WH-Z score); 25.1% in Pibor County, 19% in Aweil East; Aweil West recorded an increase from 11.3% to 21.3% whilst Aweil North recorded 26% all rates far above the WHO threshold (≥15%). The Pibor SMART Survey Report, conducted by ForAfrika (2024) reveals that 78% of the households relied on negative coping strategies; the under-five mortality rates demonstrated an increase from 0.20 in 2021 to 0.36 deaths per 10,000 persons per day in 2024. 94% of the surveyed households (HH) had no latrines, only 19% and 38.5% of HH’S reported washing their hands at five and three critical times respectively. Severe acute malnutrition Prevalence in Pibor County is 7.5% which is also above WHO emergency threshold (≥2%) signifying high morbidity of SAM.
ForAfrika proposes to integrated Six (6) of the nutrition sites into the government led Health Sector Transformation Project (HSTP) initiative for sustainability and gradual transition as well as contribute to the Sectoral Objective 1: increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services to prevent malnutrition and build resilience among 41,790 children, 19,023 adolescents, and 32,304 women and in line with the following strategic objectives: Strategic Objective (SO) 1: Vulnerable crises affected people have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable and dignified access to life-saving assistance to meet their needs and Strategic ( SO3) - vulnerable people’s capacity to withstand or cope with the impact of new and protracted crises is increased, and durable solutions to root causes of their vulnerability and shocks are enabled by humanitarian action.
With regard to Sectoral Objective 2,36 static nutrition sites (standalone and those embedded within health facilities) and 5 outreaches will be supported through Community-based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) to increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of wasting targeting 9,124 SAM cases in Outpatient therapeutic program(OTP), 23,213 under 5 and 22,613 PBW in Targeted supplementary feeding program (TSFP). Children with SAM and medical complication will be supported in Stabilization Centre (SC). The aim is to integrate response with health, Water Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH), Food Security and Livelihood (FSL), Education and Protection sectors to address key drivers of malnutrition. Stakeholders engagement AAP, protection mainstreaming and environment will be supported.

1. Needs

Humanitarian needs in South Sudan are expected to reach a record high in 2025, driven by multiple shocks, including increasing floods, a worsening macro-economic situation, soaring food prices, insecurity, and the growing burden of returnees and refugees. There is general deterioration of the nutritional status of children and PBW, and the Maternal Mortality Rate (MMR) is high at 1,150 deaths per 100,000 live births. Further, 75% of all child deaths in the country are due to preventable diseases, such as diarrhea, malaria and pneumonia (UNICEF 2024). Only 10% of the population has access to basic sanitation, and 40% has clean drinking water (UNICEF 2021) demonstrating high risks of acute malnutrition. In Pibor County, widespread acute food insecurity is one driver of acute malnutrition. 12,000 people in IPC AFI Phase-5, the most severe phase is projected for 2025. Severe humanitarian needs are attributed to chronic localized insecurity that obstructs access and restricts households to food and livelihood activities, prolonged and repeated disruption to humanitarian services, erratic weather patterns, and spiraling food prices (REACH Report 2024). Violent clashes between competing age-sets are affecting households’ physical access to alternate food sources. From the Pibor SMART survey (2024), complementary feeding is poor as only 19.3% of children aged 6-23 months achieved the minimum dietary diversity (MDD) threshold and among breastfed children aged 9-23 months, only 31.4% met the minimum meal frequency (MMF). Pibor, Aweil East, Aweil North has been classified as inter-sectoral Priority One (OCHA), however, Aweil West is classified Priority Three and the following needs are to be addressed. The prevalence of GAM (WH-Z-Score) across these counties is high; Aweil East is 19%; Aweil West 21.3%; Aweil North- 26% and Pibor 25.1% and this is attributable to morbidity, typically early depletion of food stocks and seasonally low availability of wild foods, fish, livestock products and pressure from refugees and returnees arriving through six entry points in Northern Bahr el Ghazal with limited resources. Unfortunately, this is happening at a time when the state is already grappling with a large number of internally displaced persons (IDP) from other parts of South Sudan, most of whom have fled violence and food insecurity.Poor feeding practices, high morbidity rates, inadequate sanitary conditions, and widespread barriers to accessing health services are key drivers of poor nutrition outcomes. The need to contribute in address the underlying causes or drivers of malnutrition through integrated services with the health sector and nutrition sensitive interventions is paramount for sustainability. There is need to Support diet diversity activities, maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) counseling; breastfeeding promotion, complementary feeding, supplementary feeding, good Caring and feeding practices nutrition education to facilitate self-sustenance.Due to poor hygiene and sanitation, and high morbidity in across the counties, there is need for hygiene promotion, helminthic infection prevention in children which is one of the causes of iron deficiency anemia and vitamin A deficiency and malnutrition overall. Iron and vitamin A supplementation to children 6-59 months and advocacy to enhance access to sanitation facilities will contribute to lowering the risk to acute malnutrition. There is need to provide nutrition specific interventions such as screening, treatment and rehabilitation of children between age 6-59 months with SAM through Out-patient Therapeutic Program (OTP); Moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) through Targeted Supplementary feeding (TSFP) as well as screening and treatment of PBW. Health and nutrition service facilities need to be supported to ensure efficient operation and equip staff with needed capacity to offer treatment of childhood diseases and to refer serious cases when necessary.

2. Project activities

ForAfrika is cognizant of the planned graduation transition of response to government and thus the proposed plan is to scale-up integration of nutrition activities in the Boma Health Initiative (BHI) Structures and primary health care facility in line with HSTP in Pibor County. 210 CNVs/Boma Health Workers (BHWs) and 96 health workers will be trained on MIYCN/Prevention of malnutrition. Furthermore, to increase equitable access and utilization of quality nutrition services to prevent malnutrition and build resilience in Pibor, Aweil East and West Counties, ForAfrika shall facilitate Blanket Supplementary Feeding (BSFP) activities reaching out to 20,900 children between 6-23months, and 4040 PBW. Community engagement will be held at all levels and this will ensure participation of men, women, girls, and Person Leaving with Disability (PWD) to promote responsive nutrition service delivery.In coordination with (CHD) to strengthen care practices and services, 18,120 caregivers of children aged 0-23 months will be provided with MIYCN counselling; improve management of peer groups (MTMSG, FTFSG); scaling-up SBCC on diet diversity, management of Acute Malnutrition in Infants (MAMI) through counselling; implement growth monitoring and promotion; and train 5190 pregnant and breastfeeding women and girls (PBWGs) and caregivers on Family Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) to screen children for acute malnutrition. ForAfrika propose to support Vitamin A supplementation to 40,476 children aged 6-59 and (an age-appropriate dose); deworming with albendazole (deworming) tablets in round 1 &2 and as well as provision of iron and folic acid supplementation to adolescent’s and women to prevent micronutrient deficiencies.In early detection and treatment of girls and boys under 5 years of age and pregnant and breastfeeding women with acute malnutrition, ForAfrika will support 30 standalone nutrition site and integrate 6 within government led HSTP facilities and 5 mobile units. 43,093 children aged 6-59 months and 37,315 (PBWGs) will be screened for acute malnutrition, 9124 children between age 6 -59 months with (SAM) will be enrolled in OTP, it is expected that 23,213 Under 5 years; 22,613 PBW and will be reached through treatment of MAM in TSFP. One In-patient (SC) including reaching out 193 HIV/Care takers in Eastern part of Pibor County (Jebel Boma) shall be supported. Joint supportive supervision visits by the Nutrition Manager/Coordinator shall be facilitated to support equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of wasting in girls and boys under five years of age and PBW in the planned location.ForAfrika will participate in data collection, analysis and reporting in Nutrition Information System (NIS) and DHIS-2, also in FSNMS and any other survey studies to generate evidence for improving service delivery. To address key drivers of malnutrition, over 78% nutrition sites will provide basic ECD and all the 36 facilities will have information on the management of diarrhea. In 2025,84 nutrition staff in standalone facilities and 64 Boma Health Workers will be trained on IMNCI (Integrated Management of Child Illness) and danger signs respectively. 271 health and nutrition workers will also be given in-service training. 12 Sites will be integrated into Health Facilities and more than 90% of children will be tested for malaria and cases treated. Further, ForAfrika will coordinate with WASH partners or provide minimal WASH services in nutrition sites including WASH Kits. Plan is to undertake safety audit for the nutrition sites and ensure GBV activities integrations and appropriate referral. Establishment of kitchen gardens by training of CNV, MTM/FTF-SG to replicate same at the HH level will be considered and It is expected that the integrated response will address the underlying causes/drivers of malnutrition and reduce the level of malnutrition associated morbidity and Mortality.
