Project Summary

This proposed action aims to address humanitarian needs and provide emergency support to conflict-affected communities Central Darfur state: Nertiti, Golo and Rokero; West Darfur state: Kerenik, El Geneina, and Sirba; Localities. Focusing on emergency needs in food security and livelihoods, water, sanitation and hygiene sectors. The target beneficiaries are mainly IDPs, returnees and destitute host communities who are still suffering from the impacts of protracted conflict and displacement, deteriorating macro economy, political instability, and more recently intercommunal tensions (e.g. West Darfur and Central Darfur), in addition to COVID-19 crisis. Due to displacement the affected people in the targeted states have lost their livestock assets, access to their arable lands, and become vulnerable to risks of severe food insecurity and diseases resulting from inadequate nutrition and limited access to safe water and hygiene services. The targeted population is currently facing critical humanitarian needs in food and nutrition security, livelihoods, water supply and hygiene. Hence, this action has been designed to contribute in addressing these urgent needs. This program will be guided by lessons learned by VSF-G and best practices from years of humanitarian work and interaction with conflict-affected communities in Sudan, particularly in the targeted states, through providing life-saving food security and livelihoods support, restoring livelihoods, improving productive capacities and rehabilitating infrastructure.
The proposed project will achieve its objectives through implementation of a package of interrelated activities in two sectors: WASH; and Agriculture and Food security, to timely address urgent needs of the affected population, and at the same time increase their self-reliance and reduce their dependency on humanitarian assistance. Shortages of safe water supply forces affected people to collect water for domestic use from unsafe sources, the communities are at high risk of water borne-diseases which in turn could aggravate the nutritional status of those who are vulnerable (i.e. children under five). Hygiene knowledge and practices are poor among the targeted people which also increases risks of diseases particularly for small children, pregnant and breast-feeding women and elderly. Furthermore, with the recent COVID-19 pandemic crisis, the targeted states have also become affected with confirmed cases. The risks of COVID-19 spread are higher in congested areas (i.e. especially with IDPs) with limited access to water supply and poor hygiene awareness and practices. So, the need to improve access to safe water and hygiene promotion, is very important under the current circumstances. The communities also suffer from limited access to agricultural and livestock inputs and services, which has adversely affected their production capacity, hence their food security. The current economic crises has further worsened their situation in terms of decreased purchasing power for production assets and inputs as well as for food, increasing their risks for food insecurity and malnutrition.
The main activities under WASH include: maintenance/rehabilitation of 10 water pumps to increase the accessibility of vulnerable HHs to safe water for domestic use, the activity involves building the technical and managerial capacities of community-based water use committees to enable them carry out simple maintenance and to contribute positively to sustainable water resource management. Furthermore, under the hygiene promotion subsector, the project will promote good hygiene practices among the communities through training of 100 hygiene promoters from the community, awareness creation events using IEC/BCC materials and distribution of soap and water containers to 2,200 HHs of the most needy and vulnerable. The project integrates WASH interventions with livelihood-based interventions under Agriculture and Food Security sector.

1 - Needs

Clashes between Arab militia and Masalit tribe in Krinding 1, 2 and Al Sultan IDP camps in El Geneina town and neighboring villages have led to large population displacement (approximately 46,000). People from the three IDP camps were displaced inside El Geneina town taking refuge in government institutions, while another 40 villages were displaced either to neighboring safe villages or crossed the border to Chad. These incidents and the gathering of people in government institutions has increased tension and placed further strain on the existing facilities in El Geneina town, creating gaps in basic services such as health and . The Government of Sudan has deployed additional troops in the affected areas, negotiations are ongoing between the government and community leaders to return, and part of the displaced people did return, but not all. The negotiations for return were obstructed by many demands from the affected communities. Besides this, the risk of COVID-19 in the state is very high, as it is already spread in neighboring states and Chad. The precaution measures taken by the state government include applying curfew, which has further impacted the lives and livelihoods of many of vulnerable households in the state. The food security situation in West Darfur continues to be affected by man-made and natural calamities. The 2019/2020 post-harvest assessment carried out by state Ministry of Production and Economic Resources, shows huge gap between production and consumption. The total cereal production in 2019 agriculture season was 149,559 MT while the consumption need is 242,102.2 MT with a deficit of (-41238.3 MT) after adding surplus from last year production. In Central Darfur, there are critical gaps in FSL sector and protection, especially in Rokero and Golo localities. Agriculture is the predominant livelihood activity and the main source of food for over 90% of the residents. The main constraints to agricultural production include access to arable land, availability of improved seeds and access to affordable and sustainable veterinary services, particularly in remote areas. Hence, pastoralists are facing high risk of livestock morbidity and mortality due to vulnerability to disease outbreaks. VSF-G has been implementing OFDA and SHF-funded projects in Central Darfur since 2015, and contributed to address humanitarian needs of the affected population, particularly FSL and protection . Lessons from previous programs to guide future interventions to address current needs and gaps. Furthermore, West and Central Darfur states have been among the states affected by floods in 2020 rainy season. In West Darfur, the number of farming households affected is 213,500, and estimated production losses are 450,173 MT; the number of pastoral households affected is 1,860, and total livestock losses (death) are 8,236. In Central Darfur, the number of farming households affected is 4,974, and estimated production losses are 23,730 MT. More recently, another inter-communal conflict between Masalit and Arab tribes erupted in the Krinding area of El Geneina town, West Darfur in mid-January 2021. The latest update estimates a total number of 108,870 individuals displaced across El Geneina and its surrounding villages. Reports further indicate that 393 individuals have died and 473 sustained injuries, whilst at least 8,382 internally displaced persons (IDPs) have lost personal belongings and livestock. Based on a ranking scale, the three main priority needs in El Geneina town are identified as food, non-food items.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection Mainstreaming: Selection of beneficiaries will be performed with due consideration of “Do No Harm” principles, ensuring fair representation of different vulnerable groups regardless of their ethnicity or gender. Criteria for selection of beneficiaries will be socialized with the communities and ample opportunity will be provided to raise concerns and/or complaints about the selection process. The involvement of the host communities to address their needs and promote peaceful coexistence will be ensured. The target beneficiaries will be empowered through awareness raising with regard to protection principles focusing on prevention and response to exploitation and abuse in project orientation sessions, and by involving them in decision making and participation in the project planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. Complaints and feedback mechanisms will be established and the communities will be encouraged to provide their feedback through multiple channels (.e. VSF-G has a framework for AAP/CFRM that includes a hotline and periodic community outreach). VSF-G will ensure to respond timely and appropriately through its program and project management to the communities’ feedback and/or complaints. This aims to improve programming, understand the targeted communities’ perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in early detection of problems such as targeting (exclusion/inclusion), misconduct including sexual exploitation and abuse, diversion and fraud. As well as VSF-G and partners staff will be oriented on PSEA. The safety and security of both beneficiaries and staff will be duly considered for each location/activity. Selected distribution points, and more importantly water points to be rehabilitated will be in safe and secure areas to promote protection of vulnerable groups such as girls and women who fetch water for household use. The safety and dignity of beneficiaries will be prioritized through assessing and identifying risks associated with assistance provided (e.g. livestock, vouchers, etc.); including analysis of the local environment such as ownership patterns, recent history of looting, husbandry practices and accessibility to livestock services. Ensuring that distribution sites are safe for all people involved, and that beneficiaries will not face problems with theft, intimidation and extortion. If beneficiaries must travel, it must be ensured that the road through which they travel is safe, they might be provided with transportation to ensure safety, otherwise distribution points will be moved closer to beneficiaries’ homes. For elderly people or persons with disabilities, special arrangements will be made through coordination with welfare departments and community-based organizations to identify those with limited mobility to include them in the project activities as appropriate.

Community's engagement in the project activities and decision making will be done at various stages of the program implementation. VSFG and its partner will keep share key information with targeted communities and relevant stakeholders in the targeted localities. VSFG and National partner will do whatever possible to involve the beneficiaries and targeted communities in the activities implementation, for better understanding of the project through meetings, mobilization and awareness campaigns, where VSFG is keen to have all communities groups take part in without any exclusion, to ensure the interest and needs of the different of the communities are assessed and addressed.
