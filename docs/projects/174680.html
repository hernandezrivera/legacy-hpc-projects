Project Summary

NRC in Burkina Fasio, Niger and Mali will target vulnerable IDPs, returnees and refugees as well as host communities with an integrated response, creating a package of activities to a specific need within the targeted regions. NRC's response will target the most vulnerable groups, including women, girls, children, elders and people with disabilities.
NRC will provide Shelter and WASH assistance using community based approach through which 46 000 beneficiaries will be selected in coordination with local leaders and IDP representative in accordance with on harmonised selection criteria. NRC will foster social cohesion and mitigate community tension through a holistic approach to targeting. In the three coutries, vulnerable households among refugees, IDP and host communities will be assisted with temporary and transitional shelter solutions (based on the displacement phase) and essential householld and hygien NFI kits complemented by wash facilities, hygiene promotion with awareneses messages dissimination and Covid-19 kits included.
Through Livelihoods and Food Security (LFS) activities NRC will support the most vulnerable households among the IDPs and host communities. The targeting process will follow the community-based targeting approach combined with the Household Economic Approach, prioritising, those who are the most economically vulnerable. Beneficiaries will be selected according to their level of food insecurity (assessed with the Food Consumption Score(FCS)), their coping strategy (CSI) against the participatory vulnerability analysis, such as pregnant lactating women, children under 5 (< 60 months), elderly, persons with disability, child headed households, widows.
In Education, our primary target group will be children affected by displacement (refugees, IDPs and vulnerable members of the host community. Both school and out of school children from 6 to 18 years old will be targeted (school dropouts or those who never attended school) with particular attention to girls.To this end, AEP program will be developped in close coordination with the Cluster and the MoE. Education activities will reach about 8,000 out of school children in displaced (30%) and host communities (70%), recognising that non-attendance at school is not caused only by displacement. Schools will be provided with material support (school furniture, classroom rehabilitation/construction and additional latrine facilities) and training of teachers and other education stakeholders. Children in the targeted schools benefiting from PSS activities through the Better Learning program.

2 - BESOINS, ACTIVITÉS ET RÉSULTATS ATTENDUS
2.1. Décrivez les besoins, activités et résultats attendus

Since 2012, the humanitarian situation in Mali continues to deteriorate and it is often described as a situation of “no peace no war”. The cumulative effects of climate shocks as drought and floods, armed conflict and widespread insecurity, mainly affecting northern and central regions, exacerbate pre-existing vulnerabilities and have caused the displacement of an additional 287,5001 people as of July 2020. In addition, the health emergency linked to the Covid 19 pandemic exacerbates pre-existing vulnerabilities with significant socioeconomic consequences in the short, medium and long term. Following several weeks of political unrest and demonstrations, in mid-August, elements of the Malian Armed Forces began a mutiny, ultimately leading to the resignation of President Ibrahim Boubacar Keita (August 2020). In response, ECOWAS banned all land and air borders and put in place economic sanctions until mid-October. The military junta initiated a dialogue with political and social forces to establish a roadmap of the transitional period as well as the establishment of a transitional President (September 2020) and a transitional government made up with army officials and civilians (October 2020). Although a political consensus seems probable, the scenario of a political degradation remains also possible. Inter-community conflicts are fuelling displacement and many communities are struggling to access food, water, healthcare, education, shelter and protection services. Human rights violations are common, while women and girls also faced increased risks of gender-based violence (GBV). Failure to meet humanitarian needs will worsen the impact of the crisis in Mali and the wider region and could perpetuate inter-community violence, while exposing youth to a higher risk of recruitment by non-state armed actors and criminal groups. Project outcome: Providing multi-sectorial assistance according to the acute humanitarian needs and vulnerabilities faced by persons affected by displacement, including IDPs, returnees and host communities; Output 1 (approx. % of project budget): Shelter: Activity 1.1: Establish and train construction and monitoring committees Activity 1.1: Awareness campaign and beneficiary selection Activity 1.1: Distribution of emergency temporary shelter and NFI kits for vulnerable IDPs, refugees and host community Activity 1.1: Emergency shelter construction, construction monitoring and completed shelter handover Activity 1.1: UPost distribution and post constructor monitoring Output 2 (approx. % of project budget): WASH: Activity 2.1 Area mapping and need assessment including KAP survey Activity 2.2 Hygiene and public health promotion Activity 2.3: Community engagement and social mobilization around the management of Wash facilities Activity 2.43: Behaviour change communication; Activity 2.5: Restoration of the environment by planting seedlings at water points. Activity 2,6: Sanitation in school through the organization and empowerment of students followed by the provision of maintenance kits. Activity 2.7 Rehabilitation/construction of water points including geophysical diagnostic Activity 2.84: Construction of latrines and distribution of cleaning and hygiene kits Activity 2.95: Construction of institutional latrines (schools) Activity 2.106: Post distribution monitoring Output 3 (approx. % of project budget): LFS Activitiy3.1: cash/vouchers for basic food needs Activity 3.2: food production input kits Activity 3.3: livestock distribution Activity 3.4; vocational training for youth Activity: 3.5: support and mentoring for IGAs Activity 3.6: small business grants
