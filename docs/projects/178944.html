Project Summary

In line with needs and priorities identified in HRP 2021, COOPI aims to give continuity and expand integrated protection services to support the affected populations of Aleppo, Dar'a, Idleb, Hama, Homs and Rural Damascus Governorates, to respond to the substantial gap in the delivery of protection services in the targeted areas. COOPI response will focus on three key results: 1) Improve the protection response through a community-based protection approach 2) Safely identify protection threats and facilitate access to specialized services and 3) Strengthen protection mainstreaming across all its sector of intervention, including food security, livelihood and early recovery. To strengthen communities’ self-protection mechanisms and ensure sustainability, COOPI will support community-led initiatives and the establishment of community-based structures, such as women committees, that proved to be effective in identifying and mitigate CP, PSS and GBV issues in remote areas. Direct engagement of communities will also mitigate the impact of COVID-19 on the restriction of movements and access to remote areas by COOPI staff. Community members will be involved in the dissemination and delivery of tailored risk education messaging to mitigate the risks posed by explosive contamination by increasing the community’s response capacity. Specialized services, such as case management, will be consolidated in the urban areas of Aleppo and in Rural Damascus, where static facilities are operational since 2019, and expanded in Hama, Homs and in cross-line areas (Sheikh Maqsoud - a Kurdish Self-Administration neighborhood of Aleppo city and Abu Duhur - Idleb) mainly through mobile intervention. COOPI intends to enhance the services related to victim assistance, HBR for PwD and home-based care for elderly already implemented in Aleppo. Acknowledging the challenges related to data management and protection and the lack of specialized service providers in the 6 Governorates, COOPI will continue ensuring the direct implementation of case management services, including the management and referral of GBV survivors and the engagement of men and boys in the risk prevention and mitigation activities. As identified in the HRP, COOPI will focus also to respond to the harmful coping mechanism related to child labour that has been detected in all areas of intervention and that necessitate an integrated assistance, including the support of families to meet basic needs with FSL activities. COOPI foreseen to include voucher assistance to prevent protection risks, to respond to ongoing protection concerns and/or to complement protection programs, depending on the needs assessed in each location and the market availability. The activities could be related to provision of vouchers for general or in-kind assistance, vouchers for PwD in order mitigate stigma and contribute to their inclusion in the community or voucher assistance to community-based structures. COOPI will ensure protection mainstreaming in its multi-sectoral response, with a particular focus on safe distribution modalities, AGD approach and focusing on highly vulnerable groups in the definition of eligibility criteria and in targeting. PSEA will constitute a cross-sectoral priority to be addressed and eradicate, while ensuring COOPI staff and third parties to be trained, and to report it and to protect people. MEAL system and the data collection methodology, that already provide disaggregated data for specific services, will be further strengthen and integrated in all activities, to strengthen monitoring, reporting and analysis and to adapt the project implementation. COOPI will put additional efforts to collect data related to disabilities, victims of EO and UASC to inform the humanitarian community. Complaints and feedback mechanisms will be designed, available and adapted to be accessible by the specific community groups, for beneficiaries to express on accessibility, adequacy and equity of services.

4 - Needs

According to the 2021 HNO severity scale, the proposed areas of interventions are ranked 3-4 with a total of 1,452,292PIN. In Nashabiyah, women breadwinner lack access to basic services, with >50% of adolescent girls subjected to early marriage and 20% of women deprived of inheritance. Women in general experiences verbal violence. In all locations, child labour represents the main source of income for at least 25% of assisted families. COOPI often identified UASC with limited options for referrals as services are few and overloaded. Around 35% of the population has no access to quality healthcare, mainly affecting people with chronic diseases, elderly and PWD. In Sheikh Maqsoud, Zerbeh and Abu Duhur, COOPI is the only INGO providing structured CP, PSS and GBV services and home-based rehabilitation for children and PwDs. In Abu Duhur and Zerbeh there is a need to boost community participation and engagement to identify and prevent protection risks. A large proportion of HHs in Abu Duhur and Sheikh Maqsoud is women headed. In Sheikh Maqsoud the social structure encourages women’s participation in decision-making but needs to be supported, while in Abu Duhur, they are often neglected or considered a burden by the community. In all locations, survivors of GBV rarely seek protection for cultural reasons and for the absence of specialized service providers. The provision of GBV assistance encounters many challenges related to direct management and data protection. In Al-Nashabiyah, Abu Duhur, Dar’a, Zerbeh and As San, contamination by explosive hazards threaten lives, hampers safe access to humanitarian assistance, cause long-term physical and psychological trauma and prevent safe returns.

5 - Implementation of activities

Based on participatory assessments and FGDs, in Aleppo and Eastern Ghouta, where COOPI run 3 static facilities linked to mobile units, COOPI will intervene in all protection AoRs, including GBV prevention and mitigation, case management and group counselling, parenting program. UASC will be targeted with individual assistance or inclusion in community-based structures or group activities, considering that the lack of alternative care options limit the implementation of more comprehensive assistance for UASC. In Dar’a, Abu Duhur, Hama and Homs where COOPI recently established a presence, the effort will focus on strengthening children, women and elderly committees. Committees’ members and volunteers identified in the community will support awareness raising, identification of low-risk cases, service mapping and referrals. Community-led initiatives will characterise the intervention of COOPI in all locations. Other targeted services such as home-based rehabilitation for PwDs, speech and hearing therapy, home- based care for elderly and the delivery of in-kind assistance, consolidated in Aleppo city and introduced in Abu Duhur, Sheikh Maqsoud, Zerbeh, Dar’a and Eastern Ghouta, will be further strengthened. COOPI aims to provide the full package of protection services also in Hama, Homs, Dar’a and Rural Damascus. Risk education and victim assistance will be introduced as routine activities in all areas at risk of contamination. Except for Nashabiyeh and Aleppo, protection services will be provided only through mobile units in hard-to-reach areas, while including COVID-19 considerations and using remote and on-line activities, largely implemented in 2020.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1 : Causing harm and/or compromising outcomes of case management services. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigating measures: Direct implementation of the service by internal and trained personnel only. Coordinate with other Partners to ensure referral is done to quality/person centered services.2. Risk of stigmatisation/discrimination of the individual and/or groups receiving the services especially in the case of full mobile implementation. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigating measures: Ensure appropriate communication about objectives and type of services; Identify proper location/space, staffing and gender consideration; Apply existing guidelines and tools, attend and promote discussion in different Task Force and WGs. 3: Lack of appropriate or quality intervention. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigating measures: Include capacity building activities, mentoring and coaching in any project implemented; Link with capacity building initiatives from other partners and coordinate interventions to ensure synergy and harmonise content and methodologies; advocate to ensure continuity in the protection response. 4: Risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to women, girls, and boys (and their caregivers) through the provision of specialised protection services due to lack of services, poor training. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigating measures: Ensure proper training packages in line with minimum standards; Consult communities and other actors to ensure service mappings include services providing safe and quality services. 5: Presence of explosive hazards. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigating measures: provision of ToT to ensure large scale awareness; involvement of community FPs.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Risk: Exploitation or abuse perpetrated by staff members or service providers when delivering assistance. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigating measures: Establish formal and informal complaint and feedback mechanisms in each area of intervention; Provide staff with training on Code of Conduct and PSEA; Ensure the proper communication to the communities about the gratuity of the services; Established mechanisms and procedures for ensuring compliance with the PSEA zero-tolerance policy. Standard operating procedures are in place for submission and receipt of complaints, reporting, investigation and victim assistance. PSEA focal points have been established in COOPI country offices and headquarter and all staff members receive training and information on preventing and responding to acts of sexual exploitation and abuse.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

RISK: Direct access to the target areas and beneficiaries is limited, suspended or becomes unsafe forhumanitarian workers. ASSUMPTION: Escalation of the hostilities in the targeted areas/COVID spread. MITIGATION MEASURES: Maintain constant communication with agencies, GoS and in-country security monitoring networks. All staff receive security and remote management training.RISK: Case referral is not successful. ASSUMPTION: Overloading of humanitarian actors, or the referral pathway is not fully designed/implemented. MITIGATION MEASURES: COOPI will advocate with the sector to fill the gap and finding exit strategies and continuing advocate for the government endorsement of the official referral pathway. RISK: Explosive incidents far reach vulnerabilities of survivors and of their families. ASSUMPTION: Lack of knowledge about risks related to EO and adoption of unsafe behaviour by communities and staff. MITIGATION MEASURE. Contribute to a comprehensive service mapping; Contribute to incident reporting and identification of EO; Utilize sector agreed key messages to ensure consistency and inform sector of discrepancies or if new messaging is required.RISK: Delay in project approvals or in access to locations, challenges in technical capacity, data protection issues and lack of AAP. ASSUMPTION: Extensive involvement of national stakeholder in the project implementation. MITIGATING MEASURES: Advocate to maintain the direct implementation of specific activities as case management to ensure the respect of minimum standards; Advocate with Donors for the inclusion of specific requirements in implementing and monitoring specialized services; Set up formal and informal feedback and complaints mechanisms.
