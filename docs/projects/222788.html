Project Summary

The acute food insecurity and malnutrition situation in South Sudan is deteriorating because of the economic crisis, climatic shocks - primarily widespread flooding - conflict and insecurity. The inflow of returnees and refugees fleeing the conflict in Sudan is exacerbating the situation, putting additional pressure on an already fragile country. Between September and November 2024, an estimated 6.3 million people (47 percent of the population analyzed) are classified in IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). Of this total, 1.71 million people are facing critical levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) – and a further 41,000 people are facing catastrophic levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe).
The population in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) includes 10,000 people in Malakal County (Upper Nile State) and an estimated 31,000 South Sudanese returnees who have fled Sudan because of the ongoing conflict. Compared to the same period last year, this indicates an increase of approximately 500,000 people in Phase 3 or above. In the harvest/post-harvest projection period of December 2024 to March 2025, an estimated 6.1 million people (45 percent of the population analyzed) will likely experience Phase 3 or above. Of this total, 1.71 million people are projected to be in Phase 4, and 31,000 South Sudanese returnees will likely experience IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe).
In the second projection period of April to July 2025, the food security situation is expected to deteriorate with the arrival of the lean season. It is likely that an estimated 7.69 million people (57 percent of the population analyzed) will be in Phase 3 or above. This will include 2.53 million people likely to be in Phase 4, and 63,000 people likely to be in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). The nutrition situation has also deteriorated, with an estimated 2.1 million children aged 6-59 months suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025, out of which 650,000 are cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). About 1.11 million pregnant or breastfeeding women (PBW) are also suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition in the same period. An estimated 67 percent of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap.
In Warrap state (September – November 2024), an estimated 523,000 people (39.1 percent of the state’s population) are facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse, of which 158,000 people (11.8 percent of the state’s population) are in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, and 365,000 people (27.3 percent of the state’s population) are in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. Food insecurity in Warrap State is driven by high food prices, unpredictable weather patterns characterized by irregular rains and prolonged dry spells, flooding that affected farmlands, houses and markets, reduced household income that is occasioned by loss of employment or livelihood opportunities, and illness or death of a working adult household member; incidents of insecurity and violence, including cattle raiding; and crop and livestock pests and diseases. During this period, all counties in the State are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity.
December 2024 – March 2025 (Projection 1): During the harvest/post-harvest projection period, the food security situation in Warrap State will likely deteriorate, with an estimated 656,000 people (49.1 percent of the state’s population) facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 224,000 people (16.8 percent of the state’s population) will be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, and 432,000 people (32.4 percent of the state’s population) will be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. The deterioration is mainly driven by the seasonal depletion of food stocks.

1. Needs

In order to improve the food security and livelihoods and resilience building initiatives in Warrap state, The Organization For Children’s Harmony (TOCH South Sudan) interns to support the affected population in the 2 counties of Warrap state (i.e Twic, Tonj North, ) with integrated response packages of food assistance, agricultural support and resilience-building initiatives. With Twic being the one of the counties ranked as priority number one (1) while and Tonj North, ranked as priority level two (2). TOCH South Sudan will response the needs the populations in the above mention counties through the implementation enhancing food security, livelihoods and resilience project projects with focus provision of farming’s inputs & training farmers on agrology and agronomic practices and well as assets creations, market system development support and capacity building on livelihoods initiatives. The project will be multi- year response ensure that intended beneficiaries will able adapt the support chain and strengthen the sustainability of intervention after the project phase out. TOCH will be in close collaboration with state FSL lead partners i.e. UN-FAO , WFP and state ministry of Agriculture, environment & forestry to ensure an appropriate coordinated response across all levels of intervention and according cluster objectives (CO1, CO2 and CO3) respectively. TOCH will directly response on FSL cluster objective (CO2 &CO 3) through coordination with other partners may be or already providing support under cluster objective1 (CO1) to ensure complementarity activities across the three cluster objectives. TOCH will engage FSL partners providing unconditional or/and conditional food assistance (CO1) in same counties/target location to share the lists of beneficiaries and it use the list for further targeting on other FSL livelihood and resilience support (CO2 and CO3)in order to target the same beneficiaries so that they gradually become more self-sufficient in food security. Targeting IDPs and returnees to reduce poverty among them (integration with local population) - Peaceful coexistence of IDPs and returnees with host communities.Although many humanitarian agencies in Warrap State are trying to fill the gap in access to quality seeds and planting materials, which are essential for increasing food production, this remains a major challenge due to the limited availability of local seeds and tools. Warrap State has available land and huge potential for agriculture, with local traditional farming techniques, using local tools (Malodas) and now started using ox ploughs for farming, with free training in good agronomic practices mostly provided by NGO partners. There is also a need to improve seed quality in terms of proper storage facilities with ventilation and improved seed multiplication technology. Many farmers do not have access to quality seeds and planting materials due to limited storage facilities in the country - an issue that needs to be addressed through private sector partnerships. Water for irrigation during the dry season is one of the problems, but with the construction of wells and multi-purpose pond/road, excavated ponds have provided a huge opportunity for water for livestock, irrigation, and other economic activities such as vegetable growing and brick making for income generation. There is also a need to improve storage and harvesting technology for optimal use during the dry season, which can last about 6 months. Regarding access to the markets, Kuajok is the main market for few counties and the whole of Warrap State, but in recent year’s inflation has made market goods unaffordable for many residents. Proximity to the Sudanese border provides access to supplies from Abyei, but regular insecurity and displacement has often hampered commercial progress. Supplies from Rumbek and Juba are also vulnerable to internal conflict, seasonal flooding.
