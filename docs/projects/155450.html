Project Summary

The project will be implemented through leadership of Ministry of Health and Child Care, and coordinated through the Inter Agency Coordination Committee on Health ( IACCH) with WHO as the secretariat and sector lead within UN.
The health sector will support Ministry of Health and Child Care and other partners to conduct a nationwide risk mapping exercise to identify the hazard profile, vulnerabilities and response capacities. This will ensure that all potential hazards are identified according to their geographical locations for preparedness and response planning. Disease surveillance and early warning systems will be strengthened through training of relevant health staff for early detection, confirmation and response to diseases outbreaks and other public health events through training in IDSR and establishment of Rapid Response teams at all levels.
Laboratory capacity for National Microbiology Reference Laboratory (NMRL), provincial and district level laboratories will be strengthened through training of laboratory staff and procurement of relevant equipment and reagent. The above intervention will ensure that detection, confirmation of priority pathogens is done timely and effectively, and that drug sensitivity is monitored for effective case management.
Management of medical consequences of malnutrition, common communicable diseases including cholera, typhoid fever and measles will be enhanced through building capacity of health workers on case management of priority diseases.
The shortage of medicines affects the poor in the country who constitute 80% of people living below the poverty datum line, cannot afford alternative care from private providers and leads to poor utilisation of services in general and increased preventable deaths, some of which may not be reported to the health service. The cost of health services provided by the private sector is not regulated and could be prohibitive for vulnerable populations. The private not-for-profit is also faced with economic constraints that impacts negatively on their ability to import adequate medicines and supplies. It is against this background the procurement of medicine will serve 80% of the total population in the 80 districts targeted in the prioritization of needs and the total number 9,17 million (out of 11.4 million). Procurement of adequate essential medicines and medical supplies for PHCs will be done through the National Pharmaceutical Company of Zimbabwe ( NatPharm). These medicines and medical supplies will enable vulnerable population to mitigate against preventable illnesses, disabilities and deaths. The stock management system will be strengthened to ensure that the consumption rates are closely monitored to avoid stock out.
These activities will be reinforced by supportive supervision, mentorship of health workers and strengthening monitoring mechanisms and effective coordination of interventions across all the partners

Activities

The Health sector needs to prevent, prepare, respond and control of disease in a drought situation will include the following not in order of priority:1. Undertake a Vulnerability Risk Assessment and Mapping (VRAM) exercise to identify the hazard profile, vulnerabilities and capacities nationwide2. Strengthen disease surveillance/ early warning through training of relevant health staff in all provinces and local authorities in IDSR and providing logistics for prompt outbreak notification and response3. Strengthen laboratory capacity of the National Microbiology Reference Laboratory (NMRL) and all provincial health laboratories, including the Government Analyst Laboratory for early detection, confirmation and monitoring of disease outbreaks and other public health threats.4. Strengthen case management for diarrhoeal diseases including cholera and typhoid fever and measles. 5. Procurement of essential medicines and medical supplies to ensure facilities are adequately stocked to manage disease outbreaks and other priority conditions at PHC level
