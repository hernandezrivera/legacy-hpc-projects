Project Summary

The general context in South Sudan is summarized, based on the Real Time Review (RTR) for the South Sudan Joint Response 4 report 4th October 2018, as that the humanitarian crisis has continued to intensify and expand. As a result of the compounding effects of widespread conflict and violence and a deteriorating economy, 7 million people – more than one in two across the country – need sustained humanitarian assistance. For most of the counties in former Warrap State, according to FewsNet (http://fews.net/east-africa/south-sudan), the IPC level in October-December 2018 and projected January to March 2019 is 3, indicating critical levels of food insecurity. Within Warrap, Gogrial West County is selected and within the County Payam Akon North. The last SMART survey conducted in Gogrial West county in June 2018, reported by ACF, showed GAM and SAM rates were estimated at 26.6.% (21.0 – 33.0 95% C.I. which is above the World Health Organization (WHO) emergency threshold of 15 per cent) and 5.4% (3.3 – 8.7 95% C.I.) respectively. Main morbidity causes among children under 5 in Gogrial West are malaria, diarrhea and respiratory diseases. To address the crisis situation in Akon North Payam, Dorcas proposes a FSL project in addition to its Nutrition project planned with Unicef and WFP. The focus of this project will be on food assistance to strengthen households and community coping capacities by building productive assets to reduce reliance on general food distribution transfers. Dorcas has a similar project with WFP in Jur River County with tangible success. One comment from a new farmer on sustainability was: “we are now farmers and we have learnt how to grow vegetables and agricultural crops”. Missed in Jur River are the agricultural tools and therefor this project will include provision of seeds and tools to complement the food for assets approach.
The objectives for the FSL project are:
Cluster Objective 2: food production for the most vulnerable households: Increase vulnerable household food production to protect the erosion of livelihoods through y agricultural seeds and fishing inputs and
Cluster Objective 3: Transitional emergency food assistance: Provide emergency targeted life sustaining food assistance to strengthen households and community coping capacities by building productive assets and reduce reliance on general food distribution transfers.

Needs

The general context in South Sudan is summarized, based on the Real Time Review (RTR) for the South Sudan Joint Response 4 report 4th October 2018, as that the humanitarian crisis has continued to intensify and expand. As a result of the compounding effects of widespread conflict and violence and a deteriorating economy, 7 million people – more than one in two across the country – need sustained humanitarian assistance. Hunger and malnutrition have reached record levels, and 1.75 million people are now on the brink of a catastrophe. More than 1.1 million children under the age of five were forecast to be malnourished in 2018, including nearly 300,000 severely malnourished and at a heightened risk of death. This makes 2018 the toughest year on record. A crop assessment estimates South Sudan’s cereal production in 2017 at 764.107 tons, which is a decrease of about 8% from 2016 and is 14% below the five-year average – the lowest output since the start of the conflict. Nearly 4.3 million people – one in three South Sudanese – have been displaced, including more than 1.8 million who are internally displaced and about 2.5 million who are in neighboring countries. People who have been displaced multiple times, are more vulnerable to threats to their safety, health and livelihoods. For most of the counties in former Warrap State, according to FewsNet (http://fews.net/east-africa/south-sudan), the IPC level in October-December 2018 and projected January to March 2019 is 3, indicating critical levels of food insecurity. Within Warrap, Gogrial West County is selected and within the County Payam Akon North. The last SMART survey conducted in Gogrial West county in June 2018, reported by ACF, showed GAM and SAM rates were estimated at 26.6.% (21.0 – 33.0 95% C.I. which is above the World Health Organization (WHO) emergency threshold of 15 per cent) and 5.4% (3.3 – 8.7 95% C.I.) respectively. Main morbidity causes among children under 5 in Gogrial West are malaria, diarrhea and respiratory diseases. Project staff monitoring reports on the situation in Ankon North Payam show that many households are coping through reduced number of meals and consumption of less dietary diversity due low incomes. Akon North is among those Payams where harvests are likely to be poor; access to humanitarian assistance may be limited due to poor accessibility and where extreme depletion of livelihoods prevails. As a result, acute food insecurity is expected to persist in Akon North Payam with probably IPC phase 3 if not 4 in 2019. FewsNet commented on the present IPC level in Gogrial West that the level “Would likely be at least one phase worse without current or programmed humanitarian assistance”. The level of acute malnutrition is understood to be attributed to severe food insecurity, poor access to health and nutrition services, high morbidity, extremely poor diets and poor sanitation and hygiene. Levels of acute malnutrition are expected to deteriorate in the first quarter of 2019 with the onset of the lean season as most households are expected to deplete food stocks from own production.

Activities

 Emergency food production: main season agriculture seeds & tool distribution; fish, vegetable & crop kits;  Emergency food & livelihood support: fishing kits/ rapid response kits (fishing kits/ cow pea & three variety of fast maturing vegetable seed) Reducing pest and crop disease e.g. FAW surveillance, detection, prevention & eradication Transitional emergency food assistance Conditional cash transfers (social skills & safety nets)/ Conditional cash transfers (investments in assets, training, or communal work groups/ planning committees)/ Voucher system for seed procurement; Training & capacity building: adaptive and absorptive on-farm production (farmer field schools, demonstration plots), income generation (business skills), vocational training etc.  Diversification & restoration of livelihoods; small grants for petty trade; village saving & loan schemes & micro finance etc. Use of local fuel-efficient stoves

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Activities will be implemented in close coordination with the communities through Community Based Participatory Planning and implementation. This will be done through mobilization of Community leaders, Farmers, both men and women and local Government representatives i.e. RRC, Local Chiefs, County Agricultural Department etc. There will be project Management Committees that will be formed to ensure that the project implementation will be inclusive and representation from beneficiaries will be appropriate through the project management committees but also through community meetings and project structures for accountability and complain feedback mechanisms which allows for the incorporation of beneficiaries concerns about the project.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project will be based and take into account protection risk analysis. Knowledge of the context for Dorcas is an asset as during the implementation of the current Nutrition project alot has been learnt from in regards to protection and risks associated to it for this project. Dorcas will adapt the Do no harm approach to ensure that the project is able to mitigate but also identify any protection risks that will be important to consider before implementation. Main protection risks in the area include early marriages, domestic violence for women and girls and with this knowledge Dorcas intends to work together with the community leaders and also the Project Management Committees whose responsibility is to act as a bridge to resolve and address some of this culturally sensitize rooted risks so that in the implementation stage they are addressed not by external people but together with the beneficiaries, community leaders through the project management committees whose mandate is to ensure that such risks will not affect the project.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Dorcas has field presence in the project location and main barrier normally is access to the area and movement in the rainy season. We have an office in the location and with staff who are locally from there with local means of mobility like boat, motorbikes and bicycles to ensure that our field staff are able to reach to beneficiaries in times when access is challenging. Presence of field office with functional communication gadgets like internet, office space , satellite phones means the project is brought closer to the communities and beneficiaries do not have to walk far distance from where they can be supported and this reduces risks of burden and workload to them.
