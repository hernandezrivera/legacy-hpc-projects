Project Summary

According to the recent Faith Actors case study (2019-20), the main driver of the current crisis has been the armed conflict (between the Government and opposition forces) from 2015 - 2018 which led to multiple displacements of civilian populations resulting in a total loss of livelihoods. Furthermore, the competition for natural resources is the main cause of violent intra/inter communal conflicts in the some states. Pastoralist communities compete for access to grazing land, water, fishing areas and these drivers are exacerbated by civilians having access to guns, erosion of moral authority of local and traditional leaders, weak states institutions to protect the population and a culture of cattle rustling. The majority of the youths that participate in conflicts have less education, are not employed and have disengaged with the traditional authorities. This makes it easy for the youths to be easy targets for recruitment into the army or local militia groups. Local conflicts and militarization are linked to increasing poverty, sexual and gender based violence (SGBV), psychological abuse, denial of education and economic opportunity, have a profound effect on women and girls. It is currently estimated that 50 % of girls in South Sudan are married off before 18 years and 7% marry below ages of 15. Girls are usually considered as a means for transfer of wealth (dowries). Adolescent birth rates in South Sudan are estimated at 300/1000 females aged 15-19. SGBV is promoted by a culture of silence and stigma, linked to masculine identity tied to cattle raiding, bride price, no access to legal recourse, and customary practices that promote crimes like rape. The ineffective justice system limits access to justice for victims and prosecution of perpetrators of violence. The proposed project will help enable durable solutions for IDPs and other population on peace building and peaceful co-existence. World Vision will conduct awareness raising activities and trainings on conflict resolution and peacebuilding with women, men, girls and boys in the former states of Warrap, Upper Nile, Central Equatoria and Western Equatoria. World Vision will work on reducing barriers to engagement in resilience building, food security and income generating activities, especially for women and youth; increasing anticipatory, absorptive, and adaptive capacity with respect to disruptive events including natural disasters and conflict as well as create cohesion among community members in the target counties so that conflicts will be reduced.

1 - Needs

In South Sudan, an estimated 2.7 million people, including 2.3 million children, are at risk of violence, exploitation, abuse, and neglect and in acute need of immediate child protection. Children and youth continue to be disproportionately affected by crisis and exposed to multiple protection risks including recruitment by armed groups, psychosocial distress, family separation, violence and exploitation in 61 most affected counties. High level of stress, loss of friends and family members are also taking a toll on mental health and emotional development of children, further exacerbated by intercommunal violence and cattle raiding in Greater Tonj.World Vision has peace building programs and is implementing projects with funding from RSRTF, SSHF, and Faith and development in Warrap(Tonj East, Tonj South & Tonj North), Western Equatoria (Tambura, Ezo, Nzara and Nagero). Through these intervention, WVISS will continue to use a Peace and Protection of Vulnerable Children and Youths approach which aligns with Minimum Standards for Child Protection in Humanitarian Action. WVSS will work to increase the number of girls and boys protected from violence by a) ending violence against children b) Ending use of children in armed conflict, and c) Ending child marriage. WVSS also focuses its programming on a) Empowering girls and boys with life skills; b) Transforming attitudes, norms and behaviors of children, parents, faith communities and community; c) Strengthening services and support mechanisms; d) Improving laws and accountability through advocacy; and e) Addressing other root causes through pronged multi-sectoral interventions. Through World Vision programming, 24 peace and development committees have been formed, trained and remain function, including 12 youth led peace clubs; and 120 community representatives have participated in peace-building activities. Deliberate targeting of livelihoods assets (cattle raiding, but also destruction of grazing areas and agricultural implements) is common. Hunger itself is one of the major drivers of conflict because people are forced to fight to earn food for their survival. Women’s primary role in food production has been compounded by this situation, as many adult men have been conscripted, killed, or gone elsewhere for work. However, women are still more subject to violence during conflict and insecurity. Traditional gender roles mean they have less access to inputs, training and finance, and little formal support for this role through e.g. land tenure. South Sudan’s youth population is large and underserved – poor availability of education and jobs has resulted in their disengagement with traditional authority figures and increased participation in cattle raiding and intercommunal violence. Traditional governance systems to manage communal use of these natural resources and buffer against climactic shocks and stresses are stretched to their limit as their severity and unpredictability continue to increase. To enhance the localization agenda, WVSS through its partnership department, will enhance capacities of CSOs, CBOs and national organizations through collaborative implementation and knowledge sharing.

2 - Project activities

The proposed project will contribute to the Sub Cluster's objective of enabling vulnerable people to recover from crisis, seek solutions to displacement and build resilience to acute shocks and chronic stresses through targeted programming to support coping capacities and livelihoods in prioritized areas. In particular, World Vision proposes the following: OUTCOME 1: Individuals, particularly children and women are facing less violence at the community and local level. - Coordinate with UNMISS to conduct patrols and peace rallies; - Integrate protection considerations including location selection, beneficiary selection, and training into all activities; OUTCOME 2: Communities have effective mechanisms in place that meaningfully include women and youth to resolve conflicts peacefully. - Conduct awareness raising and sensitization campaigns on the contents of the Tri-State Peace Agreement and the (revised) Marial Bai Agreement, Small arms Act and dissemination of peacebuilding messages - Conduct radio talk shows on issues of conflict mitigation, cattle migration and peacebuilding. - Conduct sports and cultural events to provide opportunities for youth from competing communities to meet in forums that promote social cohesion. - Establish, train and operationalize Peace Committees, including Joint Border Peace Committees (13 members per county) to encourage communities and authorities to mitigate intercommunal conflict, cattle raiding and revenge; - Strengthen Council of Traditional Leaders and Authorities (COTAL) in the states; - Organize and conduct conflict management workshops for county executives, cattle herders, traditional authorities, influential personalities (spiritual leaders), civil society, women and youth;

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

During feedback collection sessions, the team will take deliberate steps to make sure the opinions of the vulnerable groups are recorded and their needs met. Other feedback mechanisms will also be made accessible to people with special needs, girls, and boys.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

All staff are involved in the implementation of this project will involve and inform the beneficiaries and other key stakeholders at all stages of the project management cycle starting from the initial assessment, design, implementation and close out stages. The project participants will also be sensitized on their rights to launch complaints, express grievances, and give other forms of feedback about the implementation process, quantity and quality of services they are getting from the project. Communities are asked which complaints and feedback mechanisms they prefer, with efforts to ensure that unique needs of vulnerable populations are met (for example, considering child- and gender-friendly methods of collecting feedback, accounting for literacy or individuals’ safety, protection, and access to mechanisms, etc.)

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Mass awareness will be done to the beneficiaries on PSEA and other protection concerns; this will include topics such as prevention, reporting of PSEA cases, and services related to psychological first aid. Complaints regarding any protection-related issue will be handled by a Protection Officer to ensure confidentiality and to be able to determine an appropriate response to the feedback received, particularly in the event that such responses require CP or GBV intervention from the case management team. Any complaint involving sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) will be immediately escalated to the human resources team in Juba. All WVI staff are trained on SEA and all have signed WVI ’s code of conduct. Cases of SEA will be investigated by a team led by WVI South Sudan’s human resources staff in collaboration with the Safeguarding Manager in a safe and confidential manner.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Project participant feedback is compiled and shared with the project teams on a weekly basis. The feedback is then verified and responded to by the relevant teams. To accurately respond to feedback and complaints, an investigation is conducted on each feedback item submitted. Thus, after the team verifies the concerns raised, corrective measures are taken and incorporated into project implementation. The feedback gathered from beneficiaries will be included when conducting surveys, monitoring visits and post distribution monitoring to better understand how project can be improved. Feedback will be discussed at the project level as well as with the community and appropriate action agreed upon will be implemented to improve the project’s service delivery. This will entail either feedback good practice to be continued or adopted in other areas or a complaint on something that requires to stop or be improved. The feedback can also be used to improve internal policy of the organization through development and amendment of SOPs and conducts that protect the rights of beneficiaries.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The specific risks identified above will be addressed as follows: 8.2: Men, women, girls and boys will be sensitized on protection concerns, prevention, and where to get psychological first aid in case of violations 8.4: During conferences, trainings and awareness, the participants will be sensitized and informed about the available GBV referral services and where they could get such services when such cases arise 8.5: When doing assessments, information sharing, and awareness sessions, the team will ensure that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and older persons, women, boys and girls, participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project by directly involving them in the discussions. 8.6: The project will ensure that complaint and feedback mechanisms are accessible to all members of the community through increasing access points, diversifying feedback channels, etc. Overall, the project will be based on a protection risk analysis considering the specific needs of different population groups. The project will do protection risk analysis by identifying various possible risks and ascertain their level of severity, likelihood and impact. Barriers to access for different groups will be identified and addressed. The team is mindful the barriers to access could be social, distance, or security concerns. These barriers will be identified and addressed as appropriate.
