Project Summary

Targeting 120,000 people in need living in IDP camps, settlements, collective sites and the increasing numbers living in urban areas, this project will focus on the provision of timely, well-coordinated, and integrated Camp Management services. With the aim of improving the well-being of displaced populations, strengthening their resilience and increasing their participation in decision-making processes, in Jonglei, Unity, Upper Nile, Warrap, Western Bahr el Ghazal, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Western Equatoria and Central Equatoria, all states where UNHCR currently has a static presence through its field office operations,
UNHCR and partners will put in place mechanisms to strengthen camp coordination and monitoring to enable the effective provision of cross-sectoral services. IDPs’ engagement in the delivery of services in an equal and needs-based manner will be possible through regular feedback and dissemination of information for example through community-based networks. In support of humanitarian interventions aimed at improving the well-being of displaced populations whilst providing some level of dignity and equitability by promoting AAP.
A key component of the project will be to support community-based structures to enhance participation in planning and decision-making, in particular seeking empowerment of communities. This will be done with a focus on vulnerable groups, PSNs and PWDs so that their views are received, and acknowledged and service delivery meets the specific needs of all groups. To this end, protection will be mainstreamed into all CCCM activities to respect and promote the rights of the most vulnerable. Site care and maintenance improvement projects will take place to minimize protection risks and determine the level of assistance required by PSNs.
Supporting the ability of vulnerable IDPs to recover from the external stresses they face, be it floods or conflict, this project will seek to promote capacity building and development of community self-management structures and practices. UNHCR and its partners will also ensure that communication channels with the concerned population are strengthened, diversified, and anchored on the views of the community. This will serve to facilitate timely and accurate information to respond to disasters and mitigate identified risks. CCCM will focus on capacity building, multi-sectoral assessments on a number of displaced persons affected by the flood, and conflict resolution services through CCCM monitoring, coordination meetings at the site level, care and maintenance activities conducted by the safety committee through cash for work initiatives and enhancing feedback and complaint mechanisms.
Finally, as CCCM Cluster Co-lead UNHCR will continue to strengthen and support CCCM partners through strategic guidance, advocacy with donors, and vitally step in as a provider of last resort in areas if partners have a low presence and limited capacity to respond.

1 - Needs

Over two-thirds of South Sudan’s population are in need of humanitarian assistance, with a large proportion of those in need being women and children. There are 1.72 million internally displaced people in South Sudan who require assistance in the form of CCCM support. With the precarious implementation of the peace process, South Sudan continues to struggle with the impact of multiple displacement emergencies and protracted IDP situations, as a result of ongoing intercommunal violence. In February 2018 the Transitional Government of National Unity was formed and a ceasefire has been held since 2018 with no major armed conflict between the two main signatories. However, progress on key components of the agreement to ease conflict in the country remains slow. This has resulted in different forms of long-term displacement, requiring strong leadership and coordination for a robust humanitarian response in the form of service provision across a range of sectors. Counties in Upper Nile, Jonglei, and Unity state in particular, recorded an increased number of displaced communities in flood-affected areas facing serious humanitarian service gaps. The response in rapid-onset emergencies is often by nature less planned for and yet creates significant humanitarian needs that must be addressed. CCCM interventions are vital for ensuring that displacement sites are safe, secure and protected from further damage from floodwaters. Flooding limits livelihood opportunities and results in the killing of some livestock and reduction in food production by the affected community and the in-camp setting collection of firewood and charcoal production) affecting the IDP, especially women are feeling unsafe, and children, older persons, and persons with disabilities are particularly vulnerable, exposing them to risks of exploitation.The worsening food insecurity situation has added a burden to the already vulnerable population, particularly the elderly and people with disabilities, and has left young girls and women with the responsibility to feed their families, hence exposing them to protection risks. Access to the protection of the community in those places and the rule of law are limited for many people who experience crimes and violations. Gender-based violence (GBV) remains a threat especially in some camps that with very limied monitoring mechanisms, particularly affecting women and girls who are exposed to risks when carrying out their routine activities. Moreover, sub-national violence continues to be reported in many states across the country, including an eruption of conflict in August 2022 in Tonga in Upper Nile, with spillover in Jonglei State and Unity state. These sub-national conflicts will likely continue to disrupt livelihoods and contribute to an increase in humanitarian needs in 2023. Endemic violence and conflict lead to access constraints and interference in the provision of humanitarian services and partners’ operational activities, in areas facing public health challenges, and there are high gaps inadequate education and water, sanitation, and hygiene services.

2 - Project activities

In 2023 UNHCR intends to implement, static, mobile response, and areas-based CCCM approaches for an estimated population of 252,000 in 48 counties of eight states. UNHCR and partners will focus on project activities that promote the active involvement of beneficiaries, with a particular focus on vulnerable groups, such as women and girls and people with disabilities, through participation mechanisms that will be set in at all stages of the project life cycle. This will strengthen the adequacy of the proposed actions through regular feedback which will be incorporated for the improvement of relevant services. CCCM and other protection projects will conduct joint in-depth multi-sector needs assessments, including safe audits in partnership with protection and WASH partners, in order to identify and address the risks faced by vulnerable people in need of CCCM, and Protection life-saving assistance. Camp Management activities will focus on coordination of services, establishing governance and community mobilization mechanisms, maintenance of camp infrastructure through care and maintenance. Moreover, data collection and sharing, through service mapping, partner service tracking, 5Ws and M&E processes will result in provision of defined services which ensure the suitability of responses. The project will provide early recovery and flood risk mitigation support to flood-affected local communities, building the capacity of communities to construct and strengthen the dykes and protect sites and settlements from flooding. Skills training will bring new solutions for increased resilience and positive coping mechanisms. Mobile CCCM teams will work closely with identified local partners on the ground to build their capacity to ensure the sustainability of responses in hard-to-reach remote locations. In areas affected by conflict, the project will initiate peacebuilding activities and community integration projects, establishing strong community networks, leadership structures, and community watch-groups trained to support each other in a coordinated manner and improve the overall security around the sites. UNHCR will establish a coordination mechanism and reinforce through weekly partners coordination meetings and partners and community coordination where all the coordination of humanitarian actors, local authorities, and community representatives fully participate to discuss life-saving services to displaced populations. Through Cash for work programming and community participation, the project will implement activities in certain geographic locations, utilizing the area-based approach, where IDPs, host communities, and returnees’ populations reside together to realize comprehensive multi-sectoral outcomes.

3 - Project indicators

UNHCR CCCM will measure the success of indicators through regular FGDs and meetings with the affected and targeted communities, inclusive of all groups. Rights-based and Age, Gender, and Diversity Approach principles are applied throughout the program cycle to ensure that the voice, needs, and feedback of persons of concern, particularly those with chronic medical issues, older persons, separated and unaccompanied children, persons with disabilities, women, and children are taken into account, and that gaps, challenges, and solutions are identified. Various community communication and communication modalities will be used to ensure accountability to affected communities in the response. This includes bolstered feedback and complaint desks, hotlines, focus discussion groups, community-based networks, and community coordination meetings. Community based networks and site communities will be composed of members of all groups to ensure representation of women and other vulnerable groups with their specific needs.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

UNHCR holds planned feedback sessions through its age, gender, and diversity (AGD) and Focused Group Discussion (FGD) policies which are at the forefront of every intervention to ensure that every facet of its persons of concern fully participates in decisions that affect them and enjoy their rights on an equal footing with others. At the beginning of each intervention CCCM team will hold consultative meetings with beneficiaries to collect feedback, ensuring representation from vulnerable groups or those marginalised to implement projects which are reflective of everyone's needs.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

UNHCR promotes a community-based approach in all its projects relating to the affected population. Through consultation and participation, communities engage meaningfully and substantively in all programs that affect them and play a leading role in change. UNHCR recognizes that, without the engagement of persons of concern, our intervention cannot be impactful on the lives of persons of concern. UNHCR incorporates the knowledge and resources of communities to achieve a holistic intervention that ultimately strengthens their capacities. If communities affected by crises are empowered, they are in a stronger position to protect and support their families, promote social cohesion and peaceful coexistence with host communities, respond to the aspirations of young people and rebuild their lives. Feedback and complaint mechanisms, community-based networks, coordination meetings, and regular meetings with IDP leadership and communities have been rendered crucial. Complaints desks and feedback mechanisms ensure a two-way channel in which communities (and in some cases other crisis-affected populations) provide information on their experiences of the services being provided by humanitarian service providers.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

UNHCR has a zero-tolerance policy with regards to sexual exploitation and abuse and has clear communication and messaging strategy to inform persons of concern that will be absorbed into the project. UNHCR has systematically integrated the issue of PSEA in its information campaigns, pieces of training, and meetings with PoC’s and applied through the AGD lens. A PSEA focal person is usually appointed and discreet information sharing lines are made available.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

An annual participatory assessments exercise is undertaken using the Age, Gender-Diversity Mainstreaming Methodology in all project locations or implementation areas. The PA outcome sets out the agenda for program planning and design. It is a feedback session engaging the communities which involve representatives from all target populations including youth groups, community leaders, women groups, and persons with disabilities for feedback on implemented projects and provided UNHCR resource for advocacy and review if any of its project implementation based on the needs of the persons of concern. In addition, feedback will be collected at various stages of the implementation of programmes to guide and adapt future activities.
