Project Summary


IMCs provide comprehensive, integrated primary and secondary health care services through static health facilities complemented by regular mobile outreach to increase and expand fair coverage and access, especially for displaced or hard-to-reach populations with intermittent periods of stability and weather-constrained access. Aligned with the 2022 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) for South Sudan, IMC's healthcare interventions are designed to (1) Improve equitable access to lifesaving quality healthcare services that includes maternal, child, and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment of common illnesses, disability, MHPSS, and GBV health services to approximately 550,000 people in crises in Juba, Malakal, and Wau displacement camps, as well as in Canal/Pigi, Akoka, Balliet, and Panyikang Counties (2) Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by improving the capacity of the country's health system for early detection and response to disease outbreaks, (3) Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms.
IMC will continue to provide Primary and Secondary health care services integrated with Nutrition, SGBV and MHPSS across its 26 supported health facilities located in Central Equatoria (Juba IDP Camps, Khor Williang PHCC), Western Bahr el Ghazal (Wau IDP camp), Jonglei (Canal/Pigi County), and Upper Nile (Malakal PoC and County, and Akoka, Balliet and Panyikang Counties). These services will include Integrated Management of Neonatal and Childhood Illness (IMNCI), outpatient consultation for communicable and non-communicable diseases, care for trauma and injuries, community-based health education at the facility and in the community,. Implementation of the Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI) at static health facilities and outreach sites; Provision of Sexual and Reproductive health services across Juba (IDP and Khor Williang health facilities), Malakal (PoC and Malakal Teaching Hospital), Wau IDP camp, and Canal PIGI sites for vulnerable populations targeting women of reproductive age (15-49 years). IMC will continue to provide community health education and social-behavioral change communication to support disease prevention and health promotion activities and facilitate referrals to nearby health facilities. IMC currently provides advanced emergency obstetric and newborn care, trauma care, surgical interventions, and rehabilitation for injuries. IMC will focus on supporting static health facilities, mobile outreach teams, and community-based intervention to ensure access, availability, and utilization of appropriate health services. All of IMC's interventions will have a particular focus on vulnerable persons, such as women of reproductive age, pregnant and lactating women, children under 5, and people with specific needs and vulnerabilities, adolescents, people living with disabilities, survivors of sexual abuse, minorities, and marginalized groups, displaced people, and host communities.

1 - Needs

Access and utilization of essential health services remain limited in South Sudan even as the estimated proportion of the population that can access health facilities within an hour increased from 44% in 2021 to 49% in 2022. The limited access to facilities is further compounded by limited range of services available due to partially functional health facilities (only 11% provide the full package of BPHNS, 46% moderately functional - South Sudan health service functionality report October 2022).Access and utilization barriers were highlighted in the ISNA (2022) report which indicated that access to services declined due to, insecurity, floods, distance to health facilities, and high cost of healthcare. On the supply side, the ISNA reported lack of medicines, inadequate staffing, and logistics as main barriers. Health service delivery was further compromised by long waiting hours, inadequate WASH support, limited range of health services and lack of follow up care. This confirms the findings of a multisectoral needs assessment (December 2021) by IMC, ACF, and CARE which flagged flooding and distance to health facilities as the main barriers in Canal-Pigi County of Jonglei.In terms of disease burden, communicable diseases remain the top causes of morbidity and mortality, especially affecting the IDPs. From analysis of IDSR, and DHIS2 data, it shows that malaria, acute respiratory tract infections, and diarrheal diseases remain the top causes of morbidity and mortality within South Sudan. Malaria remains the leading cause of morbidity and mortality, accounting for 63 percent of all morbidities and 69 percent of all mortalities in 2022. This is confirmed in IMC-supported outpatient clinics where communicable diseases (Malaria, Diarrheal diseases, and Acute respiratory infections) accounted for 66% of all OPD consultations (April to September 2021) and increased to 77% in 2022 (same time period). This indicates the significant disease burden posed by preventable communicable diseases in South Sudan. Prevention of childhood killer diseases through the EPI remains challenging due to extremely low immunization coverage (49% Penta 3 coverage). There are 69% Measles high-risk classified counties and twenty cholera hotspots counties, thus, indicating the level of vulnerability of children to preventable diseases. As of October 2022, twenty counties in eight states have reported Measles outbreaks. Health-related challenges are amplified within the IDP camps, especially water-borne and hygiene-related diseases. This is due to poor WASH situation within the camps, this situation is expected to deteriorate further because of the downsizing of WASH activities driven by budget cuts. It leaves the IDP population in a dire situation with an increased risk of disease outbreaks like Cholera and Hepatitis E which are endemic in South Sudan. MHPSS: Factors including conflict and socio-economic hardship due to the lack of employment opportunities, Covid- 19, and climate-related issues like flooding are stressors that worsen the situation for mental health in South Sudan. According to The WHO the percentage of people experiencing common mental health conditions increase during stressful situations, further increasing vulnerability in the face of limited access to care. South Sudan has limited access to MHPSS services especially due to limited numbers of qualified mental health personnel (3 psychiatrists for a population of 12 million) which make the situation dire (OCHA 27. March 2020 situation report). IMC data showed a significant increase in mental health consultation in 2021 (2,411) compared to 2020 (1,538), with Neurological disorders including epilepsy (71%), psychosis (18%), depressions (7%) accounting for the commonest cases seen. This indicates a worsening mental health situation in South Sudan. MHPSS support will be integrated into all IMCs projects, targeting the most vulnerable populations.

2 - Project activities

Through static and mobile teams, IMC’s trained health workers will provide services that will address core areas of health response: communicable and non-communicable diseases, reproductive health, emergency preparedness and response, MHPSS and community health activities. Management of communicable diseases: IMC will provide outpatient services in all IMC-supported static and mobile health units with a focus on building the capacity of health workers to treat the most common communicable diseases, like Malaria, diarrhea, and acute respiratory infections. Passive surveillance through regular review of the IDSR data will be complemented by active surveillance on-ground by IMC’s mobile teams in the communities to ensure early detection of potential outbreaks. Data collected will be analyzed and shared with the MOH, WHO, and the health cluster on a weekly basis. Non-Communicable Diseases: IMC will carry out consultations for NCDs, prepositioning and distribution of essential medicines for NCDs in accordance with MOH clinical guidelines, training of healthcare providers on the prevention and management of NCDs as per national guidelines. People with mental, neurological and substance use disorders in the static health facilities will be treated and referred when required. Sexual and Reproductive Health: IMC will provide services in line with the MISP in emergencies. Services will include ANC. PNC, ,all the BeMONC and , CEmONC signal functions in appropriate facilities, family planning services, STI prevention and management. Infrastructural upgrade of Khor Williang PHCC; distribution of delivery kits to pregnant women via the mobile outreaches; provision of CMR services; prepositioning of PEP Kits and reproductive health outreach through CHWs. MHPSS: Provision of integrated mental health and psychosocial support services, supply of essential psychotropic drugs, psychiatric emergency services for both in- and outpatients in all static health facilities through skilled MHPSS workers. Close supervision of MHPSS activities through frequent supportive supervisor visits be specialists. Capacity building on mhGAP-HIG and management of mental, neurological, and substance use disorders to ensure the quality of case management. Community Health Services: IMC will provide community health education and social behavioral change activities , including training community health workers to raise awareness on various health topics at the community level. Emergency Preparedness and Response: For early detection of emergencies such as outbreaks, flooding, and violence, IMC will key into- and actively support the existing MoH EWARS platform that monitors morbidities and mortalities reported from health facilities on a weekly basis. IMC will also utilize the MoH COVID-19 vaccination dashboard that provides real-time updates on vaccination in the country. IMC will also strengthen community-based surveillance through trained health workers in IMC mobile teams to monitor health events at the community level and link the community with the health facilities in the event of an emergency. Regarding the response to emergencies, IMC will identify and train an Emergency Rapid Response Team (ERRT) on the common emergencies within South Sudan. This team will liaise with the MoH emergency teams to deploy on short notice to initiate the appropriate response leveraging on pre-positioned supplies and existing supplies in IMC-supported health facilities. Training for the ERRT team will focus on outbreak investigation and response to disease outbreaks like Cholera, AWD, Measles, COVID-19, Ebola, mass casualties, and other man-made disasters as well as natural disasters. Members of the ERRT will be drawn from existing IMC staff deployed in the different supported health facilities and will be linked to the local MoH partners as well as the health clusters.

3 - Project indicators

IMC will adopt the indicators set by the Health Cluster and as appropriate in this proposed project. To measure the achievement of the expected outcomes and ensure high program quality, IMC has developed a robust system for monitoring, including setting up patient tracking systems within health facilities, registering daily community health activities, and conducting routine quality control interviews with targeted beneficiaries. Impact-level indicators will inform more specifically the Change and extent to which high-impact and high-quality essential medical services for vulnerable populations and community engagement services have become available as a result of the project. IMC will disaggregate and centralize monthly activity-level indicators and track output/outcome-level indicators. The project will use performance data gathered through the monitoring of activities implemented. IMC will ensure close monitoring and reporting of project implementation to assess progress and data quality, identify challenges during implementation, and arrange contingency measures, as appropriate. IMC will utilize a range of monitoring tools, including the Project Management Tool (PMT) to ensure that project activities are implemented as per the plan and resources are utilized efficiently. In addition, a MEAL Plan and an Indicator Performance Tracking Table (IPTT) will be developed to assess progress of indicators against targets monthly. The analysis from the IPTT will be shared to the program team and used to inform the extent of the achievement of results and objectives and improve project implementation. With the technical support and guidance from IMC's medical and MEAL team, the field team will be guided by detailed implementation plan to ensure timely execution of program activities and review. Health program gender and disability disaggregated data will be captured routinely using HMIS tools specific for various serviced delivery points, Disease surveillance data will be reported weekly to the health cluster and as defined in health cluster guidelines including the disability monitoring report mechanism. IMC has a well-established monitoring and evaluation system. At the field level, the Program Managers use the developed work plan that will guide the program's implementation. At the Juba level, the Technical Coordinators, working closely with the M&E Coordinator, will provide technical support to the field teams on data collection, reporting, and analysis. Using the existing reporting tools program training reports and supplies consumption reports will be shared internally and externally with the Health Cluster, MoH, donors, and other stakeholders. The MEAL Coordinator will carry out quarterly monitoring visits to support Data Quality Assessments (DQA), to improve the data collected and used to assess program performance. The DQA process will include MEAL officers and program staff in the field. During the project’s quarterly review and planning meetings, findings will be discussed with program teams. During the monitoring visits, Program staff will continue to receive on-the-job training on collecting quality data and analyzing and using the information to produce positive changes during the implementation period. Data collected will be disaggregated with a particular focus on vulnerable persons, such as women of reproductive age, pregnant and lactating women, children under 5, people with special needs and vulnerabilities, adolescents, people living with disabilities, survivors of sexual abuse, minorities, and marginalized groups and Adults, displaced people, and host communities. IMC will ensure accountability to affected populations during the implementation of this project. Mechanisms such as community feedback and complaints handling systems will be in place to ensure accountability to the affected people.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

IMC will further strengthen the existing CBFRM mechanisms to ensure that the needs of the most vulnerable members of communities where IMC programs are located are identified and considered in the project design and implementation. From the program design phase, IMC ensures that mechanisms for accountability to the affected population (AAP) are considered and incorporated. IMC will consult the affected populations through regular community-based discussions to identify their respective needs while ensuring equal representation of the different sub-groups including women, girls, PWDs, elderly, IDPs as well as members of the host communities. All sub-groups will be given equal opportunities to express their concerns, interpreters will be engaged to ensure effective communication in the local languages and non-literate community members will be given equal chances to express their concerns.Feedback mechanisms and channels will be discussed and agreed upon with the community members. Examples of feedback channels to be deployed include AAP monthly meetings, FGDs, KII, suggestion boxes, hotlines, house-to-house visits, and client responsiveness surveys. Feedback and complaints coming from the various channels will be analyzed, and patterns identified to inform program adaptation where necessary Proposed mitigation measures will then be presented to the community forum to ensure the communities understand and accept the measures. Whenever possible, information is shared through trusted pathways, with messages using simple and easily understood terms and languages and other media formats. In addition, regular community consultations will be conducted to ensure the participation of communities in the ongoing implementation and MEAL activities. These will include assessing the level of satisfaction with services provided, unmet needs, identification of lessons learned, and recommendations for project improvement.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

IMC utilizes community-based feedback and response mechanisms (CBFRM) to solicit feedback and receive complaints. IMC’s CBFRM procedures provide guidance on: (1) effective community consultations – building upon community preferences on how to raise concerns and provide feedback; (2)major steps on managing feedback and complaints and how to explain our actions and decisions to the communities (including vulnerable and marginalized people); (3) Clear informed consent - IMC will provide all necessary information on how to manage feedback and complaint process, explain people’s rights and entitlements, as well as key steps that IMC will take to keep confidentiality of sensitive complaints, and (4) Routing and management of sensitive complaints, including SEA and safeguarding, including, protection measures and investigation pathways. CBFRM is aligned closely with the organization’s broader safeguarding and accountability policies; together, these procedures help ensure the systematic use and management of the feedback and complaint-handling process across all field locations and programs. Building on the existing feedback and complaints mechanism, IMC will promote community participation in management and use of the feedback and complaints through community learning meetings, community help desks, post-distribution monitoring, AAP meetings, and field registers as a safe and user-friendly channel to collect, process and respond to the complaints according to IMC and community' preferences, as well as disseminate information about the project. During program implementation, IMC will continue to enhance these already existing community accountability structures in the interventions guided by IMC South Sudan CBFRM country procedures. IMC will share relevant program information including information on organizational procedures, structures, and processes that affect them to ensure meaningful participation. Regular information on the program performance and monitoring results, staff code of conduct, and PSEA reporting lines staff will also be shared. Communities will be sensitized to existing AAP structures as a means of sharing complaints and feedback. During these sensitizations, communities will also be consulted on the most context-appropriate and safe avenue to share complaints and feedback. The information will be used to strengthen the CBFRM in the implementation sites. Further, the existing AAP committees will be trained in receiving, handling, and responding to complaints and feedback on the project interventions.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

IMC in South Sudan has developed country-specific guidance to protect beneficiaries from exploitation and abuse in humanitarian crises. This Guidance complements IMC’s global safeguarding policies. All IMC employees must undergo training on PSEA and sign a pre-employment commitment against sexual exploitation and abuse, as well as report any suspected cases. IMC will conduct regular awareness-raising sessions with the community on PSEA through QAAPCs and CHWs. The project team will incorporate PSEA messages into CBFRM activities, especially during monthly AAP meetings with the community. The project team will also ensure the posting/distribution of IEC materials at strategic locations in the community, including how to report PSEA cases. Regular sessions at the health facilities and other services point will be done on a monthly basis. Community focal points will be empowered through training to be able to pass on key PSEA messages, jointly with IMC project teams. For sensitive cases that arise, communities will be sensitized to report to the field site manager/designated focal point. The field site manager is responsible for logging in the complaints into the IMC reporting system, Ethics point, for further investigation. Communities will be sensitized to report any sensitive cases directly to ethics point reporting systems for investigation. Ethics point contacts will be shared during community sensitizations.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback and Complaints arising from the communities will be processed, analyzed and discussed during program management meetings. The meetings will develop responses and action plans to address the complaints and feedback raised. These responses will be provided through the AAP committees in each project location. In addition, the information will be keyed into a complaints and feedback response database for analysis and learning. During monthly/quarterly program review meetings, the information from the database will form part of the meeting reflections and will inform necessary programmatic corrections

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The current security status of all IDPs and POC camps is calm, however since Juba POC changed to IDPs, the frequency of fighting among youths inside the camp was increased; thus, IMC will work closely with Community Watch Group and SS police jointly with the camp community leaders. IMC will continue alertness on the security situation of health facilities and outreach sites in all project locations, in close coordination with the camp/community leaders, local and national authorities, community watch group, and UN agencies. IMC will also maintain its 24/7 security guards across all its supported health facilities, which also help assess the daily security situation together with IMC Site Managers and Security Director. IMC also has Security Risks Analysis (SRAs), Standard Operating Procedures (SoPs), and Contingency Plans in all its project locations, updated bi-annually or as the need arises. - There are separate waiting areas, wards, restrooms, and WASH facilities in IMC's health facilities for women, men, girls, and boys. The CHWs and community volunteers go around the facility to maintain orderliness in queues and ensure that no untoward incidents of abuse and exploitation may happen to anyone accessing the health facilities. Giant banners about PSEA are also posted in different corners of the health facilities and the community. CHWs also disseminate key messages on the importance of reporting PSEA cases, including where and how to report such issues. - To ensure a continuum of care and provide high-quality health services, IMC ensures that multi-sectoral referral pathways are in place in all its project locations, especially for survivors of protection incidents, cases referred from facility to facility will be recorded and reported. Moreover, monthly safety audits are being conducted, and results are shared with and discussed with relevant national and local authorities and other NGOs to draw timely, actionable solutions and provide targeted support to the survivors. - To ensure readiness to respond to an infectious disease outbreak, including COVID-19, IMC will ensure training of health workers on infection prevention and control, disease monitoring, reporting, and response process. IMC will also implement standard precautions of IPC in supported health facilities including hand washing, waste management system, prevention of needle stick injuries, distribution of PPEs, and regular health facility environmental cleaning. - IMC will continue implementing all planned activities in full equity, without discrimination based on race, color, origin, religion, age, gender, or disability. Particular attention will be given to ensure that children, PLW, vulnerable women and girls, and GBV survivors access quality health and protection services. Full access and preventive activities will also integrate persons with disabilities, the elderly, and the youth. IMC is doing special adjustments to accommodate the special needs of PWDs and the elderly, such as doing house-to-house visits/consultations with them, ensuring that health facilities are accessible to patients with movement constraints and physical disabilities, supporting rehabilitation where necessary such as ramps and grab bars, among others. - IMC will further strengthen the accountability of the affected population (QAAP) committees and CBFRM modalities. All feedback collected from the community through CBFRM will be analyzed, and feedback will be provided timely. For issues raised to the 3rd party, IMC will officially communicate the community concerns that need attention. IMC will also use the monthly coordination meeting forum as an opportunity to share community concerns and address them in a coordinated way.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

Through an integrated and comprehensive health, nutrition, FSL, WASH and protection services delivery, and through working with partners, IMC will ensure to have early warning system in place to reduce the flood related impact. With the support of the stakeholders/partners IMC will carry out a more in-depth needs assessment on flood risks in the selected operational areas, establish and strengthen early warning system and train community members to apply Community Based Disaster Risk Reduction (CBDRR) and Climate Change adaptation (CCA) initiatives in affected communities. Further, through these initiatives in the target communities, each Boma will have an operational Community Disaster Response Team. The trained local facilitators will accompany the communities develop the risk maps, setting up of the early warning system, contingency plan, and awareness campaign. IMC will ensure improved awareness to contribute safe waste disposal and mitigate blockage of drainage system

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

IMC incorporates protection principles in its all its programs and its daily work by prioritizing the safety and dignity of all beneficiaries served. Consultations and other activities will take place in secure areas that are also easily accessible for groups who are extremely vulnerable and/or with limited mobility. IMC healthcare workers will be trained with a focus on gender-based violence prevention and survivor-centered treatment core concepts (clinical management of rape), considering the challenging environment that poses high barriers to vulnerable people, especially women and girls, in terms of reporting GBV cases, as well to counter the general lack of information regarding this topic. IMC’s multi-sectoral health, nutrition and protection project will integrate GBV medical and MHPSS services to survivors, including counselling, management and proper referral for survivors of GBV, including rape survivors. All staff will be trained on protection, GBV Core Concepts and safe referrals. Service mapping and medical referral protocols are in place across IMC’s health facilities and project locations to ensure meaningful and safe referrals, including ensuring patient information shared with third-party service providers is only done so after informed consent of the patient is secured. Furthermore, IMC provides integrated mental health and psychosocial support services, including psychiatric emergency services for both in- and out-patients in all its supported health facilities. Other PSS interventions that IMC provides include psychoeducation, support groups and peer support sessions for the most vulnerable groups, including community leaders, women, children, GBV survivors and PWDs of the displaced and conflict-affected population in the target sites. These are conducted through small group discussions, tea sessions and community outreaches with the aim of bringing vulnerable individuals and group of people together, create social opportunities for their families and care givers of persons with psychosocial disabilities, and the wider community structures, which are tailored to ensure promotion of psychosocial wellbeing is achieved and the human rights of socially disadvantaged vulnerable persons are promoted. In addition, IMC will implement/pilot Self-help -plus (SH+) in all its project locations. SH+ is a stress management intervention based on Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) principles, for use by persons and group of people experiencing psychosocial distress and other stress-related conditions.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

IMC will adopt a multi-sectoral approach in services delivery in all its target locations, integrating health, nutrition, FSL, WASH and protection services. IMC directly implements nutrition-specific and nutrition-sensitive interventions, as well as strengthening the enabling environment for nutrition to address the immediate, underlying and basic causes of undernutrition through provision of both curative and preventive nutrition services. In areas/facilities where IMC does not implement Health programs, IMC will closely work with other partners on the ground to establish a well-coordinated and coherent referral systems. Through this, IMC will contribute to decreasing Morbidity and mortality related to diseases
