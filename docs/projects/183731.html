Project Summary

The West Bank is currently experiencing the fourth wave of the COVID-19 pandemic, with serious socio-economic consequences for Palestinians, including Palestine refugees. The impact of COVID-19 has exacerbated an already complex situation characterised by long-term military occupation, which generated new major humanitarian challenges in 2021, with a significant increase in protection concerns affecting Palestinians, and disproportionally the most vulnerable, including women, children, the elderly, persons with disabilities, Bedouin and herding communities in Area C, and households isolated by the West Bank Barrier.
COVID-19-related restrictive measures, are likely to remain in place in 2022. Likewise, the protection risks and inherent threats identified will continue to negatively impact the lives of Palestinians, including Palestine refugees, and mutually reinforcing with specific groups such as women, children and persons with disabilities characterised by multiple vulnerabilities across these risks.
Through the proposed intervention, UNRWA will complement the case management work of its relief and social services programme by providing immediate response to survivors of gender-based violence, and women and girls at risk within the refugee community in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem. The action will contribute to mitigate the negative impact that COVID-19 and conflict-related violence have had in exacerbating the pre-existing vulnerabilities and gender inequalities affecting women and girls.
In line with Inter-Agency Minimum Standards for GBV in Emergency Programming, UNRWA has a GBV case management framework in place, as well as a solid referral systems, ensuring a multi-sectoral response to GBV survivors. This intervention proposed to complement UNRWA's response by:
(1) Providing unconditional cash assistance and dignity kits, tailored to the needs of women, girls, the elderly and PwD to 150 GBV survivors living in and outside camps, including in remote and isolated communities (Area C, Bedouin and Herders).
(2) Localizing the efforts to respond to the needs of 710 GBV survivors and women and girls at risk living in camps and surrounding areas, and enhance the community-led response by providing them with opportunities for socio-empowerment, skills and capacity building; safe spaces, promoting comprehensive, positive and safe physical and psychosocial inclusion.
(3) Improving the safety and accessibility of complaints, including complaints of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA). The project has multiple channels to receive and respond to complaints which are adapted to the target population (including a free hotline, face-to-face reporting via social workers and protection staff) and UNRWA SOPs abide to Community-Based Complaint Mechanism (CBCM) SOPs from different target populations, including vulnerable groups. Complaints of SEA will be handled by PSEA focal points who will ensure a survivor centered approach in the handling of complaints and the provision of support to survivors. UNRWA West Bank Field Office works closely with the PSEA Network and will undertake compulsory reporting and external referrals.
The intervention is in line with the STRATEGIC OBJECTIVE 2 (The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL) and 3 (Safe, accessible mechanisms are in place for reporting complaints and feedback, including complaints of SEA, particularly in high-risk areas) of the HRP, as well as with the Palestinian national action plan to implement commitments towards enhancing services to women and girls survivor and victims of violence, as a result of Palestine joining the Gender Equality Forum (GEF)’s Action Coalition on Gender-Based violence and its adoption of Essential Services Package.

Implementing Partners

Through one of the activities of the proposed intervention, UNRWA aims at enhancing the community-led support offered to GBV survivors (women, men, boys and girls) and women/girls at risk, by complementing UNRWA case management with socio-economic interventions and referral services led by Community-based Organisations and local service providers in and outside camps. UNRWA establishes general Memorandum of Understanding with these bodies, and signs undertaking detailing the scope of the cooperation. In the framework of this project, CBOs and service providers will be identified as partners to provide GBV prevention and response services to affected populations. UNRWA has already worked towards strengthening the functioning of CBOs in camps and enhancing the capacities of those working within those organisations. GBV and Protection cases identification trainings targeting CBOs staff have already been conducted, and in 2022, UNRWA plans to undertake a wider assessment targeting five CBOs in each of the camps, by assessing their structure and services provided, to ensure that these are in line with UNRWA standards ad to guarantee quality of services and referral path. UNRWA also plans to complement this activity with a SEA-related risk assessment, to check feasibility of CBOs systems and policies with SEA safeguarding mechanism. In partnership with the CBOs, UNRWA will also run consultations women and girls, including women with disabilities, female head of single-headed households and elderly women, on provision of services, feedback mechanisms to ensure provision of appropriate response and on SEA complaint channels.
UNRWA will also advocate to include a PSEA standard clause in the undertaking and will work towards strengthening the skills and capacities of CBO staff, by sponsoring PSEA-focused trainings, following the round of trainings targeting UNRWA staff.

1 - Needs

In 2021, decades of Israeli occupation and conflict-related violence have exacerbated pre-existing risks of discrimination and gender-based violence (GBV), including early/forced marriage, intimate partner/family/domestic violence, sexual harassment and psychological abuse, affecting women and girls in the oPt. Therefore, the disproportionate impact of these contextual factors on women and girls is underpinned by socio-cultural patriarchal norms, which continue to undermine their rights. Preliminary findings from the Multi-Sectorial Needs Assessment (MSNA) launched in 2021 in the oPt to provide an overview of the magnitude and severity of humanitarian needs among crisis-affected populations, have shown that some 10% of households reported that women and girls avoid areas near settlements, checkpoints, community areas and markets, and using public transportation because they feel unsafe. In the West Bank, 31% of households were concerned about the safety and security of girls, (28.5% among refugee HHs), while 24% are concerned about the safety and security of women (18% among refugee HHs). Women and girls with disabilities, Bedouin women and women and girls living in refugee camps are particularly at risk of violence. Being physically cut off from employment prospects leaves them with few options for supporting themselves financially and therefore more likely to marry, and to not report intimate partners’ violence if it means losing their only source of financial support. Women and girls in communities impacted by conflict and displacement, i.e. Area C and East Jerusalem, tend to suffer from higher incidents of GBV, including higher incidence of intimate partner violence, sexual abuse and forced marriage. These intersectional vulnerabilities have resulted in an increase in reported GBV cases in 2021: SAWA, an emergency helpline, reported a 135% increase in caseload in May 2021 compared to May 2020. During May 21 escalations 88% of women surveyed expressed the need for psychological support. The scale of reach out and response provided by the UNRWA Child and Family Protection team, also confirms the growing trends in the number of high-risk cases, with total of 1,624 protection cases, including 1,515 follow up cases and 109 new cases identified, assessed and supported by mid-2021: of those, 1,275 and 49 were identified as cases of gender-based violence respectively. This year, access to GBV services, already compromised throughout the West Bank by the occupation and COVID-19 restrictions, has been further weakened by the most recent escalation of violence and protection threats. At the same time, doing crises, like those recently experienced, GBV-related risks further increase, as social structures break down, norms regulating social behaviour and traditional social system weaken, family members are separated, and there is increased military (ISF) presence (IASC guidelines). In this context, UNRWA continued to provide comprehensive response for protection and high-risk cases that needed urgent and immediate action, building on the lessons and accomplishments achieved during 2020, when field support was reduced, and remote working tools were in place. In 2022, UNRWA aims at further addressing the needs to provide safe and secure mechanisms of assistance to affected person including PSS and GBV case management, by also complementing the core response with community-led activities. A year and half into the COVID-19 pandemic, findings from monitoring activities conducted by UNRWA frontline staff in 2021 and data from the MSNA confirm that the ongoing crisis continues to have a detrimental impact on employment and livelihood opportunities, forcing women and girls, including those with disabilities, to adopt negative coping strategies that expose them to further exploitation and abuse: in the MSNA 2021, at least 43 per cent of female-headed households in the West Bank have indeed reported lack in livelihood and employment opportunities for women. Therefore, women disproportionately experience unemployment in the oPt: 20% of men vs 43% of women are unemployed. Female-headed households (FHH) disproportionately suffer from extreme poverty at a rate of almost 20%. FHH are food insecure at a rate of 31% (5% higher than male-headed households). In Area C in 2019, 36% of FHH were designated as distressed, a rate 14% higher than male-headed households.Thus, UNRWA has identified the need to complement case management work and referral to services, with an immediate response, in the form of cash-based assistance, to cover basic emergency needs, to ensure that vulnerable women and girls, including heads of single-headed households, can address basic needs with readiness and flexibility. Also, the outlined vulnerabilities of targeted population, and the relative power imbalance between humanitarian workers and refugees requires UNRWA to ensure that safe complaints and feedback channels based on consultations with beneficiaries, are accessible and known to the target population, including feedback and complaints on goods and services and more serious complaints related to misconduct and sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). Consultations undertaken by the PSEA Network for the oPt confirm the need for all organisations to strengthen SEA policies and processes, in addition to MSNA 2021 data which found that 23.8% of refugees (vs 11.1% of non-R) knew how to access complaint mechanisms. Information gaps remain on the needs, preferences, safety and confidentiality concerns of refugee women and adolescent girls, especially women with disabilities, single headed households, pregnant/lactating women and new mothers and elderly women. This information will be obtained through surveys, interviews and focus group discussions UNRWA plans to implement across the West Bank in 2022 to inform and strengthen UNRWA’s SEA channels and processes based on the consultation and participation of affected groups.

2 - Activities

Outcome 1.Palestine refugee women, girls, men and boys, including the most vulnerable, are provided with targeted assistance and immediate response in the form of cash-based transfers and dignity kits, complementing the support ensured through the case management work. 1.1-Provision of one-time emergency unconditional cash assistance to 150 GBV survivors (women, girls, men, boys) living in and outside camps, by prioritizing the most vulnerable groups, such as single mothers, people with disabilities , those living in far and marginalized areas. An eligibility tool will be used to assess their vulnerability, determine if they qualify for the assistance, and the amount (US$ 850 average). Social Workers will make sure to clearly outline the tool’s criteria, including that not all identified cases are eligible for assistance. They will also make sure to advertise and raise awareness around available feedback and complaint mechanisms, including on SEA. This assistance will be essential to secure basic human protection and address the most urgent basic needs, and is delivered in the framework of case management action plan. UNRWA includes men and boys in all the services provided within the case management circle, and a family-based approach targets all family members from all ages and genders. In 2020, UNRWA frontline staff were trained on Safe Identification and Referrals of protection cases in general, as well as Caring for Child Survivors of Sexual Abuse, to ensure inclusive identification, referral and provision of services to all. UNRWA will provide such assistance based on the modality already used in similar interventions, with affected persons receiving the amounts through UNRWA Finance staff at the UNRWA area offices. Area Finance Officers will reach the doorstep of those refugees who are not able to access UNRWA Area Offices, and deliver the service directly with maximum confidentiality and do-no-harm standards. To mitigate any risks associated to the relative power imbalance between humanitarian workers and refugees, concerned staff members are duly informed and briefed by RSSP team on the high-sensitivity of the cases receiving the services, in order to guarantee high confidentiality and privacy, ensure protection and do-no-harm. Finance officers will be prioritized as part of 2022 PSEA training plan targeting frontline staff. 1.2-Delivery of dignity kits to 150 GBV survivors (women. girls) living in and outside camps, by prioritizing the most vulnerable groups, such as single mothers, women and girls with disabilities, those living in far and marginalized areas. The kits align with the UNFPA’s minimum standards and will include specific items, like sanitary pads, underwear, hand soap, toothbrushes and toothpaste, alcohol gel, all selected based on the women/girls’ needs, which will holistically contribute to their safety and wellbeing. The kits will also include information materials and booklets mapping all services available to affected persons, including PSS and PFA hotlines, referral pathways, feedback and complaint mechanisms, including SEA-specific. Outcome 2. Localization of GBV response targeting Palestine refugee women and girls living in and outside camps, by strengthening the network of Community-Based Organizations (CBOs) and the referral pathways system.2.1-710 GBV survivors and women and girls at risk, living in and in the surrounding of Palestine refugee camps, will be able to access and participate in socio-economic empowering initiatives, through the established network of CBOs within camps. First, by building on the achievements of activities implemented in 2018, in the framework of a project implemented in partnership with UNFPA that established 7 One-Stop-Shop GBV Centres run by Women Programme Centres in seven locations in the West Bank (Kalandia, Aqbat Jaber, Ein Sultan, Askar, Jenin, Hebron H2, Aida), UNRWA envisages to sustain these seven centres and ensure the delivery of comprehensive self-empowering services. This holistic approach will allow GBV survivors and women and girls at risk to access psychosocial and socio-economic reintegration services at a single place and time, i.e. support groups; social-support activities, stress-management, soft skills trainings, legal services; and fiscal management tools; safe-space. Community engagement and participation will be strengthened, affected women and girls will have the chance to be part and take up roles on the CBOs network in the long run, and further work on their socio-economic empowerment.The existing CBOs network within camps, other than those running established centres, will also be targeted, by sustaining the work in supporting and referring targeted population, particularly for high-risk cases in need for life-saving interventions. Outcome 3 Safe, accessible and child-friendly mechanisms are in place for reporting complaints and feedback, including complaints of SEA, particularly in high-risk areas. 3.1-Consultations are held with target groups to inform the feedback and complaint channels for SEA. Existing complaints channels, SOPs and PSEA strategy will be adapted to reflect feedback. 3.2-This project has built-in clear process for receipt and referral of complaints of PSEA in accordance with CBCM SOPs and UNRWA SOPs on Case Management. Complaints of SEA will be handled by PSEA focal points who will ensure a survivor-centered approach in handling the complaint and provide support. UNRWA works closely with the PSEA Network, including through compulsory reporting and will also strengthen existing processes, raise issues requiring technical support and/or external referral through this forum.3.3-Age and gender-sensitive PSEA awareness raising materials are disseminated to all project sites, and distributed among target population. 3.4-All project staff will complete at least one PSEA training during the project period.3.5-Project staff will indirectly engage in the PSEA network.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

UNRWA has a long experience providing community based services to GBV survivors and women at risk. In the framework of the proposed intervention, an array of services, mainly legal services, mental health services, awareness and economic empowerment training, are provided through CBOs to complement the services it offers to GBV survivors, localize the support provided, and strengthen community participation and social fabric. Since the start of the COVID-19 pandemic, UNRWA has been providing inclusive hygiene kits that take into consideration the needs of vulnerable groups, including women. However, protection principles require a more explicit type of in-kind assistance to GBV survivors. Thus, UNRWA aims to ensure that the productive health needs and protection concerns of women and girls GBV survivors in specific are integrated into emergency and regular responses. In 2020, UNRWA RSSP provided cash-based assistance to protection cases, including GBV survivors, in the framework of an intervention aiming at promoting gender equity. The service proved to be essential for securing basic human protection for the high-risk protection cases and their family members and dependents. The Cash Assistance expenditure was mainly used for essential home repairs, essential livelihood and sanitation repairs, critical home appliances, food and hygiene, health care needs, payments for bills. The vast majority of the feedback from the beneficiaries reflected how the assistance proved to be highly beneficial, as serving the needs and empowering protection cases in a dignifying manner. Based on this, UNRWA wants to ensure that cash assistance is offered to GBV survivors: coupled with other basic services provided by UNRWA, including health, protection and education, cash assistance can be an important tool to help Palestinian women and girls to bolster their social protection and development.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Feedback and complaints relating to specific cases is followed up according to internal SOPs used by project staff to adapt services, interventions and referrals for support services to meet the needs of families and individuals. In addition, project staff collect and analyse feedback and recommendations from the affected populations, through direct feedback, internal hotlines, monitoring tolls, including consultations and surveys. This informs decision making about the scope and modality of service provision and overall design of the project, as evidenced not only by the proposed intervention of continuing providing the emergency cash assistance to GBV survivors, based on the feedback of the target population, but also on the positive accomplishments of the one-stop-centres established in targeted location in 2018, and that this intervention proposes to further sustain. This year, the project also aims to improve the way in which general feedback is recorded and systematically feeds into adaptations to project implementation and design. This will be achieved through measuring the intervention’s impact on the lives of the beneficiaries, as well as their satisfaction, and by building on the one-to-one relation established between the case manager and the GBV survivors to directly feed inputs into the project implementation phase. Beneficiaries will be explained their right to provide feedback and complaints in confidentiality and without interfering with the potential assistance and will provide with information material summarizing the complaint process and the different avenues available for submitting a complaint. The RSS hotline will also continue receiving calls, with staff having been trained in receiving and responding to feedback and complaints (including SEA) and regular monitoring meetings to review data and analyse thematic concerns raised by beneficiaries.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The project adopts a do no harm and conflict sensitivity approach, including specific measures to ensure that identified risks are addressed within the action and unintended negative consequences are minimised. UNRWA has developed an internal guideline for ethical practices in the context of briefings, field visits, communications and others. UNRWA’s Case Management SOPs ensure that protection cases are identified and referred to specialised support services. SOPs cover compulsory reporting on SEA and support to survivors. A dedicated psychological consultation hotline is available to all refugees. In UNRWA's Protection Strategy (2012), the prioritisation of safety and dignity is explicitly address as a key protection standard where UNRWA programmes deliver services in a manner that ensures the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and staff. UNRWA ensures that its actions do not cause unintended harm or result in reduced protection of beneficiaries, communities or staff, including risk mitigation for SEA. On a biannual basis all of UNRWA's activities are subject to a protection mainstreaming audit, which assesses the integration of protection principles in UNRWA's work. This audit included extensive interviews and focus group discussions with beneficiaries, and sought to assess UNRWA's application of the four main protection principles, including the principle of Do No Harm. UNRWA has developed, in coordination with the West Bank Protection Consortium, a dedicated vulnerability assessment tool in order to prioritise assistance to the most vulnerable refugees. This tool was pioneered by UNRWA and has since been adopted by other members of the Consortium and is to be applied throughout the West Bank. Under Outcome 2 of this project, within the Case Management response UNRWA ensures that vulnerable individuals can access assistance by external referrals, UNRWA has developed dedicated case management SOPs and internal/external referral pathways, to ensure that the comprehensive needs are met. In the context of the COVID-19 response, additional steps taken to ensure UNRWA's activities prioritise safety and dignity. UNRWA has adapted its case management in response to difficulty of physical access to concerned individuals/households. UNRWA has established a dedicated psychological consultation hotline available to all refugees. Staff leading on case management as well as those working in camp-based CBOs have been trained to protect the identity and safety of GBV survivors by not disclosing any personal information about them or the incident to anyone without the survivor’s explicit permission and consent. Also, GBV service providers have robust experience in ensuring GBV survivors are protected from harm and in providing psychological and emotional safety to survivors who may be at high risk from the perpetrators or from their family members due to notions of family honour. All UNRWA staff are required to follow the GBV Survivor Centred Approach to provide meaningful access to services, ensuring accountability mechanisms are in place as well as participation and empowerment of GBV survivors. Further, staff directly involved in the provision of cash-based assistance to GBV survivors, i.e., finance officer, will be prioritised in 2022 round of PSEA training targeting UNRWA frontline staff.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNRWA is dedicated to the application of the humanitarian principles in all of its service delivery. Specific measures are taken to ensure that UNRWA's services are neutral and impartial in nature, including through regular monitoring throughout its operations. UNRWA takes measures to ensure that its assistance under this project is available to all persons, and takes dedicated action to ensure that individuals with specific needs are reached. UNRWA takes actions to ensure that persons with disability, with movement and access restrictions, the elderly, women and children are targeted during outreach and communication - and also are key stakeholders in all community interactions and in accountability processes. Services delivered are tailored to the needs of individuals, including for those with specific needs, the case management services delivered are developed in coordination with the affected persons and are guided by their specific needs. Specific needs according to age, gender and disability are identified at the initial assessment stage of case management. The individual case plans are tailored to respond to these needs, including through the provision of additional services, like the emergency unconditional cash-based assistance, the delivery of dignity-kits, the referral to specialized service providers. UNRWA has demonstrated an ability to readjust its operations to effectively face the operational and movement restriction-related challenges arising from the COVID-19 emergency. For example, services were adjusted to be provided remotely, including through hotlines, and other electronic tools and by reaching out directly to beneficiaries’ doorstep. This service delivery modality will also be used in the framework of this intervention to provide unconditional cash-based assistance to women, girls, men and boys who are unable to reach the UNRWA area offices, as they leave in remote and isolated community or have a disability, and the assistance will be provided directly to their doorstep. This is not a designed procedural tool, but rather a measure put in place to respond to case specifications and ensure equal and impartial access to aid. CBOs will also ensure equitable inclusion of PwD in the activities provided through the One-Stop-Shop centres, and will refer PwD to specializes implementing partners, where needed.
