Project Summary

The project will address food security and protection of 9600 IDPs in four camps in and around Burao town through multipurpose cash transfer, GBV awareness, provision of dignity kits and study on viable recovery and livelihood sources for the IDPs.

Needs

The recent Food Security and Nutrition Analysis Unit report (Sept 2, 2018) recognizes that 1.5 million people in Somalia are still in crisis and are in need of sustained humanitarian assistance. The most vulnerable groups are protracted and drought displaced people in and around the urban centers. Burao town hosts significant number of displaced people, both protracted and recent arrivals. According to the FSNAU report, 3000 people in Burao IDP camps are in IPC 5 and would need immediate humanitarian assistance such as food, WASH, protection and shelter. The recent Joint Multi-sector Needs Assessment (JMCNA) by REACH found out that 85% of the population in the IDP camps have lost hope of reviving their traditional livelihood assets and would prefer to remain, for the time being, in the camps. Rapid needs assessment to six IDP camps in Sool, Togdheer and Sahil regions by ActionAid in May 2018 found out that the most pressing need was food and sanitation followed by protection and access to clean water in some places. Burao local council and aid agencies (UNICEF) provide water infrequently whilst WFP is providing cash voucher to 2500 households in these camps until December 2018 whereas the four camps host about 6,000 households. For example, women and girls in Alla-amin camp walk to a private borehole away from the site which exposes them to sexual violence and miscarriages. The only available household water storage facilities are Jeri-cans of 20 liters or less; thus women routinely fetch water to satisfy daily needs of the households.Food consumption was widely uncertain as they eat one or two meals a day. The staple food in the Camps is primarily rice, flour and cooking oil supplemented with pulses. Thus the condition of children and other vulnerable groups remains critical. According to the FGD with women, “there are families in the camp who nowadays do not cook food at all”.

Activities

Providing multi-purpose cash transfers to 1600 unassisted households in four IDP camps (Alla-Amin, Caaqil-yare, Airport and Guryo Samo) in and around Burao town Raising the awareness of IDPs in the camps on Gender Based Violence (GBV)Establishing and Facilitating Women-Led Community Response CommitteesProviding dignity kits to 3200 women and girls in four IDP campsConducting assessment on durable recovery and resilience initiatives for the internally displaced people in Burao camps. Advocating with the Burao local council and Togdheer regional authority to protect IDP population from forced evictions

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The major risks that displaced people can face is forced eviction from the site, gender based violations, poor sanitation and if the humanitarian responses, acute food insecurity. In order to address these issues, ActionAid will raise the awareness of the site population on the GBV, train young women to monitor violence against women and girls, provide dignity kits and establish referral pathways.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

1000 dignity kits and GBV awareness for the community and specifically for women and girls who are prone to sexual harassment and other violations. ActionAid will use UNFPA standard for the dignity kits. The recipients of the dignity kits are among the total project target group. The project will mobilize women into groups to address GBV in their respective camps. The women groups will establish safe spaces for GBV monitoring and will be trained on referral pathways and linked to service providers.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The conflict risk is considered low. However, the population in the IDP camps may conflict over the limited spaces while members from the host community may harass the IDPs on the plots where they have erected the makeshifts. Poorly managed relief distribution may also cause disputes within the community. ActionAid will work closely with the local council, police and regional authority to control forced evictions of the IDPs from the sites. The awareness of the IDPs and host communities will also raised to manage resource-based conflicts.
