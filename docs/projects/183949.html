Project Summary

The goal of the project is to help and support resettlement-affected communities to improve living conditions during displacement, including coordinated assistance and protection, access to information and inclusive community participation. These measures will help facilitate their return and reintegration of IDPs by seeking and promoting durable solutions to stop living in camps for extremely vulnerable groups affected by conflicts and natural disasters.
The project will improve intersectoral coordination to ensure equitable access of vulnerable community members to services through improved redirection mechanisms.
HADC is planning a multisectoral intervention as part of its project activities to have a qualitative impact on resettlement-affected communities in South Darfur. The intervention is planned in the framework of Protection, Shelter, WASH Sectors (establishment of clearing houses, conducting training sessions for opinion leaders, infrastructure projects based on the needs of community members, formation of community groups, provision of access to safe water and protection of water sources, issuance of partial construction kits for home renovation and NFI etc.)
The project ensures that the target communities will have adequate and affordable assistance to meet the most basic human needs related to the support of long-term solutions, autonomy and self-government of the community with the further socialization of IDPs in so-ciety. HADC will conduct periodic collaborative inter-agency assessments to gain insight into new emerging situations in target communities. Post-distribution sur-veys will be an integral part of the project.

1 - Needs

The number of asylum seekers continues to grow, and this trend is expected to continue in 2022 and beyond. The needs estimated for the proposed WASH activities are based on gaps ob-served in 2020, as well as gaps and further instability resulting from renewed conflict and tensions leading to new displacements, unprecedented flooding in the 2020 rainy season. Twenty-seven percent of the Sudan population (around 11 million people) don't have access to basic domestic water. Multi-Sector Need Assess-ment (MSNA) 2020 showed that about 25 percent of the water sources are not functioning. Twenty-five percent of the population reports water quantity is not sufficient to meet their basic needs; 50 percent of the people reported that it takes more than 50 minutes to fetch water which also causes insecurity, especially for the girls and women. In addition, 54 percent of the schools and half of the health facilities do not have basic water services. The population of the communi-ties needs support at the community level for the de-velopment of self-reliance and self-government, social integration, the creation of a community monitoring system and maintenance of community facilities. First of all, it is necessary to mobilize opinion leaders of the community who will participate in raising awareness among the population. Since information is an im-portant form of assistance to displaced people affected by conflict and natural disasters, vital information about services such as protection, water, food, medi-cine or shelter is required. An information manage-ment system needs to be established so that commu-nities can access service and protection information through CICs.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The project involves the implementation of targeted activities with the following results:1. Monitoring and identifying populations most in need (with community participation and coordination with other partners in order to have a deep understanding of the problems and adequately prioritize their needs).2. Establishment of 3 information exchange centers for capacity building and training of affected communities. Conducting training sessions for opinion leaders who will participate in raising awareness among the displaced population. This activity is supposed to cover at least 10 opinion leaders in each camp and at least 50 volunteers.3. Implementation of 5 community support projects based on the needs of community members. For its part, the Foundation will provide in-kind resource assistance in the form of materials, equipment, etc. Community members will be directly involved in the implementation of community projects and will contribute in the form of labor resources. As a result, the population will be able to take an active part in the life of the camp, to set priorities and solve collective problems.4. Formation of 3 community groups of 25 people with 40% women participation to improve project management, intervention and mechanism of filing a complaint.5. Provision of access to safe water and protection of 15 water sources by providing disinfectants, new containers, repair, refurbishment and / or installation of pumps. This activity includes training at least 10,000 women and men in hand pump mechanics in safe chlorination techniques. The priority will be to strive for the sustainability of WASH services in camps through the creation of organizational structures and a gradual transfer of responsibility.6. Train 5,000 community members on shelter construction, risk mitigation and emergency manage-ment, such as proper shelter construction and other selfsustaining activities.7. 1000 households will receive partial construction kits for home renovation.8. 3000 households will be provided with NFI9. In the framework of the project protection monitoring will be conducted, systems of early warning with the community based protection networks will be created. Сommunity consultations will be held at all stages of the project.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The CO “CF “Humanitarian Aid and Development Center” understands that communities living in countries affected by conflict are more vulnerable to the impacts of climate change. Armed conflict destroys infrastructure, weakens institutions, impedes access to basic services and erodes social cohesion - all critical to human resilience and adaptation to climate change. Communities are on a survival rope in the face of these complex crises. Given that the protection of civilians remains the highest priority in the implementation of this project, it also takes into account environmental lenses that will contribute to the effective management of natural resources, which, among other things, contribute to the restoration of basic livelihoods. In the implementation of this project, the Foundation operates with one of the humanitarian principles – "Do no harm", which is part of the concept of reducing the impact on the environment.Thus, the training programs will include mandatory items that train the project beneficiaries in waste management, waste separation and disposal (including construction waste), training in cleaning their local area, careful attitude to natural resources (plants, water, land). When training beneficiaries on water chlorination, the aspect of chlorine handling and disposal will certainly be included, and when training communities to install water pumps, emphasis will be placed on how to install pumps so as not to harm the environment. In the training centers, to train opinion leaders, a separate course on environmental protection will be developed and conducted, which includes hygienic aspects, cleaning and separation of garbage, careful handling of nature, water resources, etc.In the context of climate change, the carbon cost of international deployment will also be taken into account when defining the workforce. The project will employ mainly local staff, with a limited presence of international staff. Local staff will be trained in the principles of environmental protection. Also during the project, the staff of the Foundation will conduct a systematic analysis of the cost-effectiveness of the impact of this humanitarian operation on the environment.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

People affected by conflicts, riots and natural disasters are almost always exposed to high risks of violence (physical, psychological and sexual), rights violations, coercion and exploitation. Individual and community protection capacities, as well as national protection mechanisms, are often compromised or insufficient to cope with the protection risks posed by ever-emerging threats. Thus, the situation in the field of protection will be taken into account separately, including when interacting with stakeholders and government officials. When developing and implementing the project, the location of the facilities where the project is being implemented will be taken into account, the distance of routes from real or potential threats, such as: violence; especially the risk or threat of gender-based violence and attacks by armed groups.Protection risks and violations will be determined by characteristics such as minority status, gender, sexual orientation, age and other diversity factors. Thus, the central role of protection in this project is, together with the communities, to identify groups at high risk in the field of protection, to find out the types and causes of such risk, and also to analyze these features, taking into account external factors that affect the protection mechanism itself. In addition, this approach involves identifying specific weaknesses that give rise to such risks, taking into account the unique experiences of men, women, girls and boys, such groups as the elderly, people with disabilities, LGBT and other groups.A wide range of representatives of the local community will have the opportunity to participate in the decision-making process in an inclusive and non-discriminatory manner that has a real impact on the provision of a humanitarian response. The CO “CF “Humanitarian Aid and Development Center” in this direction will use communicative approaches, assuming the presence of clear and direct feedback between the staff who develop and control the program, and the communities themselves. The project also plans to improve protection by differentiating the risks (needs) of the local community, which will allow for more targeted assistance. This will help, among other things, identify the underlying drivers of conflict and thereby improve the protection of the local community, while avoiding the harm that humanitarian action could otherwise do.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

During the implementation of the project, all activities will be carried out in compliance with the basic humanitarian standards of quality and accountability to the affected population. Actions and approaches will enable local communities to participate in key decision-making at all stages of program implementation: evaluation, analysis, planning, implementation, monitoring and revision. For this, communicative approaches will be used, involving transparent and constant two-way communication, through which information about the risks, needs and opportunities of communities will be collected.To submit complaints, the CO “CF “Humanitarian Aid and Development Center” has developed a mechanism for feedback and complaints. During the project, beneficiaries and stakeholders will have the opportunity to contact the management of the Foundation by telephone, e-mail, or through social networks. Also, at the locations of the project implementation, "feedback" boxes where you can leave requests will be installed. All complaints are brought to the attention of the management of the Foundation, and those who applied are provided with a response (if they have left their contact details). If this is a complaint, then it is communicated what measures were taken. Information about the start, implementation and completion of the project will be published on the organization's website, pages in social networks, and also posted in local media.
