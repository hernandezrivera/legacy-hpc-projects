Project Summary

CARE proposes an integrated approach to gender-based violence and sexual and reproductive health that will reach 52102 women, men, boys and adolescent girls specifically targeting groups with diversity of need including persons with diverse sexual orientation, gender identity, and person with disability living in Camps 11, 12, 14, 15, 16 and Palonkhali and Jaliapalong Host Community in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh through 12 WGSS along with PNGO OPCA. The project will provide sexual and reproductive health services and undertake prevention and response to gender-based violence activities include Case Management, Structured Psychosocial support services (PSS), Skill building of the women and girls, capacity strengthening of the community activists and service providers, local partner NGO, local Government (Chairman, Member). Additionally through community mobilization with proven strategies, to address negative social norms that fuel GBV among the population and awareness on COVID-19, DRR, and monsoon. Beneficiary accountability is embedded in all CARE's emergency response and interventions. CARE’s feedback/ complaint mechanism has been developed in line with CARE's quality and accountability framework and operational context. CARE raises awareness and training on PSEA to staff, volunteers, beneficiaries and community people. Internally CARE has a strong PSEA policy as well as reporting and investigation mechanism. Throughout project implementation, CARE will promote/ensure downwards accountability by consulting with communities and local authorities to ensure community voices are heard and tensions mitigated amongst different groups to ensure Do No Harm and conflict sensitive programming. CARE will seek feedback from affected populations and ensure the local voices feed into annual reviews of the project in order to actively shift our program design on the basis of local feedback from our target groups to maintain the AAP. Groups will be engaged in mixed, and age and sex-disaggregated fora and interviews to ensure that all voices are represented, to ensure the social cohesion.

1 - Identified Needs

As of September, 2020, UNHCR estimated 861545 Rohingya refugee women, men, girls and boys are hosted in Cox’s Bazar District among of them 52% female and 51 children (UNHCR fact sheet, Sep ’20). Many women and girls are restricted to their homes for the majority of the day. This creates additional barriers to accessing services for women, girls and vulnerable groups. Many women have been exposed to severe forms of sexual violence in Myanmar before the crisis and during their movement to Bangladesh (CARE rapid gender analysis, 2017). In the camps, women face high risks of gender-based violence (GBV), domestic and intimate partner violence, trafficking, exploitation and child marriage. Adolescent’s girls have equally been categorized as a vulnerable group in the camp, with lack of education and opportunities for developing their skills, leaving them especially vulnerable to abuse, requiring targeted services to ensure their meaningful participation (ISCG gender profile). There are still critical gaps in essential minimum GBV services including case management, access to PSS, health, clinical management of rape (CMR) and legal counselling, as well as the engagement of men and boys. In addition, there is limited response services for male survivors of GBV. A study by WRC in 2018 found that one third of the interviewed respondents personally knew a Rohingya man or boy who had directly experienced conflict related sexual violence in Myanmar. It is alleged that the Myanmar Army forced Rohingya boys and men to watch sexual violence perpetrated against Rohingya women (Women’s Refugee Commission, 2019).According to the inter-agency Gender Based Violence Information Management System (GBV IMS) quarterly reports, intimate partner violence (IPV) has been identified as the overwhelmingly dominant form of sexual & gender-based violence reported in Cox’s Bazar. Intimate Partner Violence (IPV) remains a large percentage of all reported incidents. The average of reported IPV incidents in Q3 2020 (84%). The increase in percentage of reported incidents in survivors’ residences (89.7%) and public toilets/latrines. In Q3 2020 illustrate the need to strengthen the security and safety of these locations through awareness and prevention mechanisms. (GBVIMS quarterly report, July-Sep’20). 13 % reported security issues concern to them since the COVID-19 outbreak. One respondent reported instances of child marriage, and children going missing during the lockdown. During the quantitative survey, protection-related concerns were raised related to: robbery, kidnapping, child marriage, sexual harassment, rent payments, threats by local people/armed groups, mahjee demanding money/using violence against those reporting problems (MSNA, 2020). GBV has been acerbated with Covid-19, for example Intimate partner violence (IPV) has increased during the lockdown situation. Lockdown, movement restrictions, and increased stress has led to a rise in domestic tensions, which often culminates in physical and emotional violence. Further, men being confined at home is a source of this tension, as well as the additional household work. Child marriage has equally been reported to be on the increase during the COVID 19 period, this is a negative coping mechanism by parents who have lose livelihood sources in the community. In addition, engagement on benefits of safe hygiene practices will be conducted considering gender and age of respective participants to be in line with the religious and cultural norms. CARE collects women voices through the setup MEAL mechanism as well as through our women and girls’ safe spaces. We have regular FGDs with women and girls before we introduce a new product e.g. dignity kit. In addition, for cash for work activities, CARE holds a risk analysis with different groups to find out whether there's any risk associated with the chosen activity, and also to ensure there's gender balance in allocation for work.

2 - Activities or outputs

1. Provide survivor centered GBV response and prevention services with 12 existing WGSSs .These spaces will provide an opportunity for access to information, education, recreational activities and integrated SRH services . In addition, case management, individual or group counseling, psychosocial support, safety planning and risk reduction, skills building, distribution of dignity kits will be provided through the WGSSs in Rohingya and host community.2. CARE with PNGO OPCA will provide quality survivor centered GBV case management and PSS services for survivors including Person with Disabilities.3. Provide PFA,Refferal support as per beneficiaries needs including additional support aimed at addressing survivor needs.4. Provide skill development and empowerment training of women and girls including Person with disabilities on session facilitation, basic counting , communication and Decision making skills, tailoring, problem solving .5. In an integrated approach, conduct meaningful engagement with men and boys includes working with individuals and communities to promote gender equality and equity and to transform gender norms.6. Provide sensitization session with community & religious leaders to change harmful social norms and practices.7. Training and engagemnet of Community Outreach group in GBV prevention strategies using tested socila changes approches in the camps and targeted host community8. Conduct awareness raising sessions among women, girls, boys & men with including PWD by using IEC & Audio-visual materials on GBV, COVID-19, DRR, Monsoon & PSEA9. Pre-position Dignity kits and MHM kits to strengthen the capacity of implementing for emergency GBV responses during natural disasters10. Capacity building to GBV service providers and local partner staff on GBV case management, GBVIMS, CMR PSEA to provide appropriate GBV services in the camps and targeted host community11. Provide life skills training through Girl Shine Program Model to improve their learning and development and adolescent sexual and reproductive health knowledge.12. Capacity building to non GBV service providers & other humanitarian actors (WASH, SRH ,SM) , local govt. (Chairman , Member) on GBV risk mitigation, PSEA and GBV safe and ethical refferal, GBV mainstreaming.13. Promote the inclusion and participation of communities of concern, particularly women, persons with disabilities, sex workers, gender diversity and minorities in all aspects of CARE’s programming.
