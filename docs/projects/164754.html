Project Summary

This project will target the most vulnerable small scale farmers, mainly IDPs, returnees and hosting communities in, Hama, Homs and Rural Damascus governorates which are per the HNO draft for 2020 have expressed most need for income-earning opportunities, fuel, food and health services. Moreover, the thematic geographic focus of the project is based on the vulnerability of population, accessibility to basic services and livelihoods opportunities, the rate of displacement or returns, and the highest severity scale.
Interventions will include:
(1) Provision of training on agricultural practice with the aim of increasing knowledge and skills for better and environmentally, and economically sustainable agricultural production; (2) Provision of agriculture input and farming tools to restore agriculture productive capacity; (3) Support sustainable livestock husbandry practice; (4) Support food processing activities based on local agricultural production, coupled with support for market access (value chain development).
The type of assistance provided will be based on the beneficiaries' identified needs as well as their ability to participate in/benefit from the intervention.
Targeting and Vulnerability criteria: severely food unsecured; IDPs; HHs hosting IDPs; HHs with children under five years old and/or pregnant women and/or lactating women; FHHs; HHs with no productive assets, or functional means of income/ reliable source of income; Elderly headed HHs; HHs headed by chronically ill members or headed by physically challenged heads; 9- HHs not receiving adequate assistance from other sources; HHs meeting other vulnerability criteria as identified by the communities .
Specific criteria for livelihood assistance:
For the support of sustainable livestock husbandry practice proposed activities, UNDP will exclusively target HHs who have livestock and/or experience in livestock management, access to grazing land, availability of labor for livestock activities and willingness to maintain livestock.
For the provision of agriculture input and farming tools, UNDP will target HHs who have agriculture land and sources of water but must be the small holders.
For the support of food processing activities based on local agricultural production, UNDP will target beneficiaries with minimum required Know-How and have access to agriculture input and market. The estimated number of beneficiaries is 700 distributed as follows: 50 persons will benefit from the provision of training on agricultural practice; 300 persons will benefit from the provision of agriculture input and farming tools; 300 persons will benefit from the support of sustainable livestock husbandry practice; 50 persons will benefit from the support of food processing activities.

4 - Needs

The economic crisis in Syria has exacerbated the livelihood situation of vulnerable communities, mainly in rural areas, affecting small scale farmers, IDPs, returnees, and local communities. Currency depreciation and increasing prices undermined food security. As per the HNO 2020, the crisis reduced access to essential services and eroded coping mechanisms. 7.9 million people are food insecure, and 1.9 million people are at risk of food insecurity, four of five live in poverty and 60% of working-age population is unemployed, resulting inability to meet basic needs. Increased Prices of food and basic commodities forced HH to reduce meals. Devaluation of SYP reduced purchasing power and will exacerbate poverty rates in 2020. HH forced to resort to harmful coping strategies including child labor, forced marriage and other exploitative practices. The FAO/WFP crop and food security assessment mission of Sep 2019 shows food prices have increased due to limited access to land and inputs, reduced local production, damage and looting of production assets and processing plants and the devaluation of SYP. In the target localities of Hama, Homs and Rural Damascus governorates, HH suffer from disruption of livelihoods due to loss of assets and rights and access to farmlands. Local communities are vulnerable due to constraints on sustainable income generating activities and returnee and IDP movements, scarcity of resources and difficulty to access markets and financial resources. UNDP started working in some of the areas where communities rely on agriculture and livestock as a source of food and income, identifying agriculture and livestock as live saving assistance and the most sustainable strategy. UNDP will respond to needs by delivering agricultural assistance while ensuring sustainable agricultural practices through community and resilience-based agricultural & livestock interventions. i.e. contribute effectively to increasing the resilience and livelihood opportunities.

5 - Implementation of activities

The action will be implemented in collaboration with local counterparts (NNGOs, INGOs and related government directorates and Ministries). UNDP will provide technical support and oversight on the quality implementation and will lead the overall implementation in close collaboration with different stakeholders. The project team will consist of both programme management and technical teams that will develop a detailed plan based on implementation strategies. Moreover, UNDP field staff will share their expertise in community mobilization, beneficiary selection, data collection and data analysis, awareness raising activities. UNDP will follow its procurement protocols during project implementation. At the start of the project, UNDP will hold a kickoff meeting to discuss the project strategy, logframe analysis, workplan, and to agree on roles and responsibilities concerning project implementation. UNDP will meet with local authorities (Governor Office, MoSAL, MoAAR, Area Managers, and other national authorities) to review the project, secure their ongoing support, approve implementation and invite feedback. UNDP will setup a solid and transparent selection criterion of beneficiaries which will be implemented and monitored by the UNDP team in collaboration with stakeholders. Although there may be variances in the vulnerability criteria in different communities, UNDP will work with local communities to prevent discrimination and ensure inclusion of most vulnerable groups. To ensure the participation of most vulnerable groups, UNDP will follow-up with selected beneficiaries who did not benefit from the project activities in order to ensure that the selection criteria have been implemented correctly. The project team will provide regular, impartial monitoring through direct field monitoring visits, occasional, remote telephone monitoring, and indicator data tracking mechanisms. This is intended to provide a regular assessment for appropriate course corrective actions.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Resources and response capacities being limited and far below know needs, there will always be perception of biased targeting of beneficiaries or of biased selection of response activities, thereby creating or exacerbating tension; Mitigation measures: 1. Perform conflict analysis before planning the response; 2. Ensure that a comprehensive needs analysis informs the response formulation, and that this is openly documented and made transparent; 3. Make sure those in similar situation of need, at least within the geographical areas of response, area reached equally; 4. Obtain validation of the prioritization and of the formulated response from a representative community group, or through well-selected key informants and/or participation mechanisms; 5. Communicate the planned response, and the rationale behind, to the communities concerned; 6. Efficiently deliver on the obligation of ‘Accountability to Affected Populations’, by keeping the communities concerned regularly informed of the project and its implementation; 7. Make sure some process of receiving complaints and of responses to complaints is put in place; 8. Have in reserve a strategy in case the planned response does unexpectedly create tension (e.g. add-on of other activities or change in activities, in consultation with concerned communities. Risk 2: Escalation of the security situation; Mitigation measures: 1. Focus on responses to the benefit of the entire community rather than individual beneficiaries (e.g. rehabilitation of social or community infrastructure, rather than e.g. individual housing support); 2. Ensure there is a process established that allows to consult the communities on how to proceed with the proposed response in a deteriorating security environment.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:1- Levels of support to targeted households are large enough to meet needs on the ground2- Activities supported by the UNDP are coherent and lessons can be consolidated to have an impact on policy and implementation.3- Approvals are granted by local government to start project implementation4- The exchange rate of local currency is stable. Risk: 1- Needs on the ground are higher than available resources.2- Factors of implementation change making a change in the policy or implementation plan a necessity 3- Delay in implementing project activities due to delay in receiving consent from related government bodies.4- Rapid fluctuation of local currency will affect negatively the procurement process where some supplier might withdraw their offers in case of currency depreciation.Mitigation measures:1- UNDP will apply solid selection criteria methods of beneficiaries. 2- UNDP will plan for different implementation methods to be able to adapt with abrupt changes.3- UNDP will advocate through different means to chase up the approval process. 4- UNDP will speed up the bidding process to reduce the risk accompanied to currency fluctuation.
