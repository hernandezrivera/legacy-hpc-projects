Project Summary

Risk Education activities implementation remains a priority concern and the level of need continues to be very severe, as reﬂected within the 2020 Protection Severity Scale. In particular, risk education and safety messaging for at-risk groups remain key priorities in the Idleb and Aleppo Governorates due to the high levels of contamination and the increase in hostilities in the last quarter. Clashes between armed groups and ongoing aerial bombardments in populated urban and rural areas have impacted humanitarian activities and increased the need for further mitigation measures to reduce the impact of explosive hazards and incidents.
NAS has been providing protection services for 4 years in Idleb and Aleppo,NAS organization who are specialized in MA have good access to Azaz and Afrin and have the requested approvals to operate in these areas in which the access will also be more extended.
This project is planning to reduce the impact of explosive hazards, NAS made use of 4 WS info and Forward planning (Mine S/cluster) tool. NAS chose these communities in these areas because they are contaminated with unexploded war remnants and they were frontline areas.
NAS will respond to the issues raised above through the following activities based on the Humanitarian Response Plan 2020
A) Conduct tailored risk education and risk awareness for at-risk groups, including IDPs
Two mobile teams (5 staff each; 1 team leader, 4 male and female educators) will be deployed to reach beneﬁciaries with messages in schools, IDP camps, collective centers as well as IDPs on the move. Each team will be deployed to different places in the targeted locations. Children will be equipped with improved knowledge on the risk presented by unexploded remnants of war to contribute to behavior change that minimizes death and injuries from landmines and remnants of war.
The Mobile Teams will use the UNICEF existing materials endorsed by the Mine Action sub-cluster.
b) Train humanitarian workers, community focal points, and other people of concern to conduct risk education to improve the integration of risk education throughout all other sectors responses.
NAS staff and community members (Teachers, local councillors, nurses and community leaders) will be train on mine risk awareness by hiring specialized trainers in Mine Action inside Syria.
These trainings will be delivery with the support of the Mine Action and sub-cluster (MASC) to enhance the capacity of NAS staff. NAS staff will be equipped with the right skills through ongoing coaching and mentoring. The trained NAS staff will in turn pass on awareness and knowledge to community groups.
• This training will contribute signiﬁcantly to improving the ability of society as a whole to face the risks to children, identify and prevent them.

4 - Needs

Syria remains one of the biggest protection crises globally. People continue to experience a multitude of grave, complex, and often inter-connected protection risks. an escalation in violence in the north-east and north-west of the country, continues to have an immediate and disproportionate impact on civilians, causing death, injury, mass displacement and large-scale destruction of civilian infrastructure. Some 11.5 million people reside in areas contaminated by explosive hazards, with unexploded ordnance continuing to claim civilian lives, causing serious injury and posing barriers to freedom of movement and access to livelihoods across Syria. In 2019, hostilities escalated in the north-west and north-east along the Syrian-Turkish and Syrian-Iraqi borders. Attacks on civilians and civilian infrastructure continue. Hostilities caused civilian casualties, displacement, and freedom of movement restrictions, widespread rights violations, including grave violations against children, damage and destruction to civilian infrastructure, loss of assets and livelihoods, and increased explosive hazard contamination. Mines and other unexploded ordnance continue to claim many civilian lives, as does the use of vehicle- borne improvised explosive devices.It is with this in mind that NAS is planning to implement this 12-month project to reduce as much as possible the dangers and injuries that civilians regularly encounter with land mines and explosives, particularly among children. NAS will respond to the issues raised above through the following activities based on the Humanitarian Response Plan 2020 : A)Conduct tailored risk education and risk awareness for at-risk groups, including IDPs. b) Train humanitarian workers, community focal points, and other people of concern to conduct risk education to improve the integration of risk education throughout all other sectors responses.

5 - Implementation of activities

NAS seeks to Conduct tailored risk education and risk awareness for at-risk groups, including IDPs, women, people who live in remote farms around the communities, children in and out of schools, men in their workplaces .NAS will respond through: A) Conduct risk education for at-risk groups ,Two mobile teams (5 staff each; 1 team leader, 4 male and female educators) will be deployed to reach beneﬁciaries with messages in schools, IDP camps, collective centres as well as IDPs on the move. Each team will be deployed to different places in the targeted locations. Children will be equipped with improved knowledge on the risk presented by unexploded remnants of war to contribute to behavior change that minimizes death and injuries from landmines and remnants of war. The Mobile Teams will use the UNICEF existing materials endorsed by the Mine Action sub-cluster. b) Train humanitarian workers, community focal points, and other people of concern to conduct risk education to improve the integration of risk education throughout all other sectors responses. NAS staff and community members (Teachers, local councillors, nurses and community leaders) will be trained on mine risk awareness by hiring specialized trainers in Mine Action inside Syria. These trainings will be delivered with the support of the Mine Action and sub-cluster (MASC) to enhance the capacity of NAS staff. NAS staff will be equipped with the right skills through ongoing coaching and mentoring. The trained NAS staff will in turn pass on awareness and knowledge to community groups.The project will be monitored on a regular basis in order to ensure satisfactory monthly progress and accountability to the beneficiaries including collection of monthly data from beneficiaries, monthly progress reports, and regular technical supervision missions fielded as required to enhance success.MEAL officers will focus on planning and regular review of activities against indicators.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

PRA.1 Risk:Creation of wrong expectations among beneficiaries, who could wrongly attribute to RE teams the ability of removing explosive ordinance;Familiarization with messages delivered at RE sessions may lead beneficiaries to become overconfident/engage in risky behaviours. Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigation: Delivery of ToT to ensure large scale awareness/sustainability of efforts;Clear explanation of intervention and objectives to beneficiaries; Tailoring of messages/RE materials used in schools according to age/gender;Involvement of survivors of explosive hazards as role model for behaviour change; Tot of local liaison team to ensure self-sustainability of RE messaging within affected communities; linking RE with other interventions.PRA.2 local authorities may dictate mine action priorities not in line with humanitarian objectives. Likelihood 1.1: Medium Impact 1.1: High Mitigation measure 1.1: Promote community participation in priority-setting

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions & Risks: Weakness of interaction of the beneﬁciaries of the activities.Mitigation: Preparing participatory activities.Assumptions & Risks: The targeted beneﬁciaries are not aware of the importance of the activities being provided. Mitigation: We will be announced in cooperation between mobile teams and local councils within the targeted villages. Assumptions & Risks: Airstrikes and bombed cars might happen in Idleb area. Mitigation: 1.security staff will do a daily, weekly and monthly assessment of the security situation to ensure the security of staff and beneﬁciaries. Activities may be delayed when situation is unsafe 2.Choose response centres, which they are far from military locations. 3. Coordination and continuous communication with the S&S assistant and local authorities to ensure the safety of staff and beneﬁciaries4. Suspension of work temporarily in case of intensiﬁed shelling 5. Daily Risk Assessment- movement clearance for staff. Assumptions & Risks: Cultural background and norms could be sometimes as challenges against the achievement of the outputs. Mitigation: NAS team will conduct focus group discussion with all beneﬁciaries to explain that the activities of this project are not against the norms and culture of the target communities.
