Project Summary

To reduce the burden of morbidity and mortality, Somali Aid will provide integrated life-saving primary health care services; improve access to health care systems by providing health interventions to the boys, girls, men and women within the target population to the nearest proximity. Health care services through consultation and management of childhood illnesses with an aim of reducing maternal and neonatal mortality will be given focus. ANC and BEmONC with referrals of the CEmONC cervices will be provided to the pregnant mothers.

Needs

Despite improved rainfall the year 2018, health risks and access to basic health care especially the lower juba and Gedo regions still stands a major concern due to weak functioning health systems and some areas being inaccessible.The population in lower juba placed under emergency IPC4 in FSNAU-FEWS NET Technical Release,Jan 2018 report was 9000 and has increased to 11,000 as per September issue of FSNAU post Gu technical release; a warning more children will suffer from malnutrition and consequent increase in morbidities. North Gedo Riverine, South Gedo Agro-pastoral, South Gedo Riverine, according to FSNAU-FEWS NET 2018 Post Gu Technical Release, 01 Sep 2018, are considered populations hotspots in need of urgent nutrition and health support interventions havjng Critical prevalence of acute malnutrition (≥15% GAM) or10.7% or more of children have Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) below the 125(mm) threshold.Immunization remains very low with measles’ coverage among 1 year olds being only 30% overall and 15% in inaccessible parts of South Central Zone. Only 3% of births are registered and neonatal mortality rate (per 1,000 live births) is 53. Most of the deaths are caused by common preventable diseases, like pneumonia as they cannot access most basic health services in some parts. Women and children in the IDPs are most vulnerable at risk of getting diseases due to environmental and seasonal situationDiminished funding situation in lower Juba &Gedo also led to deteriorating state of health service delivery is of concern with risk of increased morbidity& mortality among most vulnerable girls &boys under fives, pregnant and lactating women; Crude Death Rates (CDR) &U5ve Death Rate (U5DR) surpass emergency thresholds in seven of the population groups surveyed (i.e. CDR > 1/10 000/day and U5DR > 2/10 000/day, respectively). Somali Aid proposes continuity to provision of primary health care and support referral systems in Lower & Gedo Regions of South Central Somali

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The target population is at risk of displacement that may pose human rights violation. The Protection Risk Analysis highlights the extremely difficult situation in IDP settlements and collective centers across the country, in light of growing displacement, due to drought and conflict and continuing protracted and secondary/multiple displacement situations (including the particular challenges facing refugee returnees who get displaced again). The IDPs in these displacement locations frequently live in undignified and hazardous circumstances, where they face multiple protection risks/threats such as, unlawful evictions, overcrowded and unsanitary environments with limited access to basic services, exposure to explosive hazards, increased risk of Gender Based Violence (GBV), negative coping mechanisms such as child marriage and child labor, and tension with the host community. Children and young people (mainly male) in displaced situation, particularly those from Al-Shabaab controlled areas face risks of arbitrary arrest once out of those controlled areas, and therefore live in fear and sometimes isolation. Lastly, IDPs continue to struggle to end their displacement and pursue voluntary, informed, safe, and dignified durable solutions based on their individual and household needs.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Community consultations through focus-group discussions will take place during the project implementation, and stakeholders will be involved in decision-making processes to tailor the project to the needs of different gender and age groups. Somali Aid has gender policy that ensure that when hiring staff that women are given 50% consideration in different positions. In addition, the project target populations are segregated into men, women, boys and girls hence giving emphasis to all gender groups in the community. During the screening and admission of children into nutrition programs, boys and girls will be given equal chances. Furthermore, data will be dis-aggregated by age and gender in all recording and reporting.Gender considerations will be taken into account throughout the assessment, planning and design of the interventions and significant consideration will be given to pregnant women, mothers, and women of child-bearing age. In the provision of health and nutrition promotion campaigns, messaging will be targeted for specific gender and age groups and as appropriate the events will be organized separately. Equitable and gender-sensitive participation and decision-making approaches will be adopted throughout the project, including women’s participation in the community nutrition committees and access to gender-specific health and nutrition information.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Volatile risks in Somalia context with service delivery has been very fragile. Somali Aid will focus of the humanitarian response and provision of protection services has been accessible thus forcing IDPs to move to find assistance or in drought impacted communities in accessible, less conflict prone areas. Somali Aid will undertake necessary assessment of the security situation and work with relevant authorities. By utilizing the reach, capacity, and weight of the whole humanitarian system, the safety, dignity, and well-being of these displacement affected populations (including the heavily burdened host communities) can be strengthened.
