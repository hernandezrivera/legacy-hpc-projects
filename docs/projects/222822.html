Project Summary

Our program objectives address food security sector's objective 2, 3, and 4. The project is to building resilience of the camp and host communities to improve access to diversified nutritious foods and enhance food security at the household level. The project focuses on climate-SMART home gardening activities in the camps and host community to ensure optimum utilization of vegetable from the gardens. Concern also build capacity and awareness for the programme participants through training and other supportive sessions on nutrition. The project will target 19,655 households in the Rohingya camps (Camp 2E, 5, 6, 10, 11, 13, 14, 15, 16, 18, 21, 22, 24, 25, 26, 27, Nayapara-RC) and 2620 household of Ukhiya Upazila (Ratnapalong, Holdiapalong and Jaliapalong) for climate smart home gardening activities. In addition, In the host community of Teknaf (Hnila, Whykong), the project will target 130 household for climate smart agriculture, 10 household for fisheries, 110 household for livestock support, 30 household for food processing and management and 30 household for water management (solar irrigation) activities.
During the implementation phase, programme participants in both camp and host will receive training on vegetable production at the household level through modern, climate resilience techniques and environment-friendly approaches. Programme participants will also receive training on using organic compost, conservation agriculture, using biological pesticides (use of Chilly power, soapy water, neem extract, Pheromone & sticky traps) to avoid chemical pesticides and ensure post-harvest management to reduce food loss and food waste. The project will also develop the capacity of the project participants of host community on preservation and storage of seeds, adaptive and mitigating measures, ensuring agro ecology, adaption of the irrigation system during drought and limited water source, processing and storage of the vegetables by the trainings.
In addition, the project will focus on entrepreneurship led economic development in the area of agriculture, livestock, and fisheries in the host community. Digital marketing will be used for the selling of products in a better price. Programme will also ensure linkage with local and broader market for the sustainability of the activities in the long term.
Climate change and environmental conservation will be key crosscutting area for this project. Altogether, this project will serve positively in the protracted crisis of Cox’s Bazar.
Aligning with nutrition-specific intervention, the project will select programme participants considering the vulnerability of malnutrition of children U5, pregnant and breast feeding women, persons with disability, women-headed family etc.
Major activities includes:
i. Support households to grow seasonal vegetables to improve dietary diversity
ii. Training and awareness raising on climate resilience home garden methods
iii. Livestock production
iv. Agriculture demo plot
v. Regenerative farming
vi. Fisheries
vii. Water Management etc.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

As of December 31, 2023, 971,904 Rohingya refugees reside in thirty-three congested climate-vulnerable camps in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazilas of Cox’s Bazar, impacting a further 538,000 residents in the host communities. The most reported needs by Rohingya refugees include protection, access to skills and capacity-building activities, nutrition services, and accessible quality healthcare (Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment, 2023), and 95% of all Rohingya households are moderately to highly vulnerable and remain dependent on humanitarian assistance, UN Refugee Influx Emergency Vulnerability Assessment. The Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) in Rohingya camps is high at 15.1%, with stunting chronic malnutrition prevalence at 41%. The GAM in the host community is 11.1% (8.3 - 14.7) and chronic malnutrition is 23.3% (19.8 - 27.2) . According to Concern's 2022 Barrier Analysis, infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices continue to be sub-optimal both in camps and host communities, with the exclusive breastfeeding rate at 66.1% and 72% respectively (WHO recommended rate is >75%), and the Minimum Acceptable Diet (MAD) and Minimum Dietary Diversity (MDD) among children 6-23 months equally low (camp 28.2% and 22.7% respectively). At REMA survey 2023, The resilience index of the host community (41) and resilience capacity index for treated Rohingya households is significantly higher (39) than the control group (26). REMA 2023, Food insecurity was more pronounced among the control group, with 67 percent of households categorized as borderline or poor in terms of food consumption, compared to 52 percent for the treatment group However, 33 percent of Bangladeshi households continued to experience food insecurity. Of those, 32 percent faced borderline food insecurity, while 1 percent faced poor food consumption. The control group displayed a higher prevalence of food insecurity compared to the treatment group, with 35 percent classified as borderline or poor consumption, in contrast to 32percent for the treatment group. According to the GBVMIS Q3 report published by the GBV subsector in September 2023, intimate partner violence (IPV) constituted 79% of the reported cases in camps and host communities, with 74% of cases reported by survivors who had experienced GBV incidents before, indicating a recurrence of IPV. 2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Concern ensures accountability to program participants through a transparent Feedback Complaint and Response Mechanism (FCRM) that operates in every camp via AAP/CFM assistants. Concern utilized various channels to reach affected populations as part of its community engagement and accountability efforts. From the end of 2024, Concern will launch its digital FCRM system. The comprehensive tool is designed to capture feedback and complaints from various sources within the organization. By systematically recording essential details such as the date of receipt, source of the complaint, nature of the issue, and location information, the form enables efficient tracking and analysis of complaints. Additionally, features like anonymous reporting and classification of complaint types enhance the form's versatility and effectiveness in addressing diverse issues. Overall, it's an easy-to-use solution that facilitates transparent and systematic complaint management. All data within the FCRM digital system will be encrypted to safeguard against unauthorized access and ensure confidentiality. All personnel with access to sensitive data will receive training on data protection protocols and the importance of maintaining confidentiality. Regular awareness campaigns will also be conducted to reinforce data protection practices.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes. The project's approach to addressing the DRR, and environmental issues through climate adaptation and mitigation through the holistic approach at the strategies and field level to address the immediate and long-term impacts of climate change. Under the project, Concern will ensure dissemination of knowledge and ensure tools to implement climate-resilient agricultural practices to reduce environmental hazard. The programme participants will practice conservation agriculture (zero tillage, mulching), disaster resilient methods (sack gardening, vertical, pit etc.), organic and biological pest control system (pheromone trap, sticky trap, soapy water, ash powder etc.), organic compost, and post-harvest management. Livestock production, fisheries will align with the environmental protection using regenerating farming approach. In addition, project has a strong focus on disaster risk reduction by mainstreaming DRR/DRM messages.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes. The project has a strong contingency plan focusing on the Rohingya camps, host and local community at Cox’s Bazar with a clear guideline on the emergency response standard operation procedures, and allocation of emergency response fund. The programme has also provision to response any EPR related emergencies aligning with the programme intervention locations. Concern is an active member of Ukhiya and Teknaf upazila disaster management committee. In the camps, Concern maintains strong communications and coordination with camp in charges (CICs), other actors / stakeholders for the better planning of disaster risk reduction and response. Concern follow ISCG guided SOP for the disaster risk reduction.
