4 - Needs

The widespread use of various types of weaponry in the ongoing conflict in Syria has left behind a range of explosive hazards including improvised explosive devices (IEDs), landmines, and explosive remnants of war (ERW). Contamination levels are especially high in areas of intense conflict, such as front lines and areas which were under siege. These explosive hazards prevent safe and sustainable returns, cause civilian deaths and injuries, exacerbate vulnerabilities, hinder safe access to humanitarian support, basic services and livelihoods, and ultimately compound the humanitarian crisis. The impact of explosive hazards on communities is immediate and devastating, with the loss of community members, an increased number of people with permanent injuries, and loss of access to properties, basic services and livelihoods. A wide range of infrastructure, such as houses, private property, schools, hospitals, markets, agricultural land, wells and roads are blocked by explosive hazards, impeding access to education, health services, marketplaces and safe livelihood opportunities. Men and boys are more directly at risk from the immediate threat of explosive hazards, while women and girls are more impacted by indirect threat and long-term consequences of contamination, which tend to exacerbate existing vulnerabilities. Provision of risk awareness on these hazards is crucial to providing civilians with basic safety messages to reduce the daily risks posed by contamination. Survey and clearance of located items is the only way to remove perceived and confirmed threats. The need for Risk Education, Survey and Clearance is acute.

5 - Implementation of activities

* Conduct Risk Education for at-risk groups: Train and deploy mixed-gender Risk Education (RE) teams to provide age and gender tailored RE sessions to at risk populations, specifically targeting men and boys, as well as IDPs returning to highly contaminated areas. The RE sessions will provide the targeted population with knowledge and skills to adopt safer behaviours with regards to Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) and Improvised Explosive Devices (IEDs). RE sessions will be tailored to the specific needs of boys, girls, men and women, focusing on the risky behaviour most usually associated with each group. Sessions will be delivered with a specific emphasis on boys and men as they continue to be the highest number of victims, and will encourage safer alternatives to risky behaviours. The RE sessions for younger children will use simplified and repetitive messaging, to assist with memory and understanding, as well as a variety of songs and games. The RE sessions for women will also include advice and suggestions for sharing safety messages in their homes with their children. Community Focal Points will also receive training to ensure the sharing of potentially life-saving safety messages is sustainable. * Conduct Survey, Marking and Clearance of Explosive Hazards: Mixed gender teams will conduct Non-Technical Surveys (NTS) during which they will gather information from boys, girls, men and women about levels and types of contamination in residential areas. These surveys will include information about the impact of the contamination and will help to prioritize the project clearance activities strictly based on humanitarian needs. Clearance teams will focus their activities in residential areas, based on data gathered during the NTS, in order to help save lives, and enable safe and sustainable returns. Should the security/access situation deteriorate, the project will remain flexible and agile with regards to project locations.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members or partner staff members. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: Project staff will communicate regularly and clearly with the community about what services are available and who services are for. Regular training will be provided to project staff on the Code of Conduct, Child Safeguarding and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). A complaints box will allow staff, partners, or community members to raise complaints confidentially. These will be addressed in a timely and appropriate manner by project or management staff depending on the nature of the complaint. DCA also follows Do No Harm principles in all its activities.Protection Risk: Community threshold of acceptable risk increases as a result of living in contaminated areas for a longer period of time, thus leading to more accidents. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: Project staff will ensure that RE sessions include topics on effects and consequences of risky behaviour leading to explosive hazard accidents. Together with community members they will discuss safer alternatives and encourage behaviour change. Community Focal Points will also encourage positive behaviour change. Protection Risk: Once marking of contaminated areas begins, communities mistakenly assume any area not marked must be safe. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High.Mitigation measures: During Community Liaison activities, project staff will inform community representatives and members that areas may also be contaminated with explosive hazards, even if they are not marked. RE sessions will also include the same message.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Safety and security risks in project locations impact project implementation, causing delays and changes in operational areas, and also threaten the safety and security of project staff and beneficiaries. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: The organization has a designated security focal point whoactively monitors the security situation in project locations, sets up security SOPs and procedures, and conducts regular security-related training for staff. Project management remains flexible and changes project locations and modalities as and when required. Hibernation, relocation and evacuation plans are in place.
