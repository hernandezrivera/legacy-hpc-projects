Project Summary

As part of its shelter response, in line with SO1 Provide lifesaving and life sustaining shelter & NFI support & SO2 Reinforce an enabling protection environment and community cohesion by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure, PUI aims through this project to provide vulnerable, conflict-affected populations with access to improved, safe, healthy living accommodations.
Furthermore, PUI’s FOCOS strategy programming continues to be undertaken in a multi-year strategic way, with 2022 representing the eighth year of implementation of PUI’s integrated-area based approach (IABA) in Syria. Through this approach, PUI makes sure to create synergies between the different interventions implemented in areas in need in order to respond to the needs of the Population of Syria as a whole and to help them to go back to their pre-crisis standards of living, to enhance self-sufficiency and resilience in the long run and to promote social cohesion.
Therefore, the plan for 2022 and 2023 includes implementing the following interventions:
- Rehabilitation of 1805 partially damaged apartments (980 in 2022 and 825 in 2023) benefiting a total of 9025 returnees in several high-need areas in 8 governorates: Damascus, Rural Damascus, Dar’a, Aleppo, Deir Ez-Zor, Idleb, Homs and Lattakia.
- As response to any new displacement waves in effects to frontline shifts, PUI will provide rehabilitation of 4 collective shelters based on the identified needs as follows:
- 2 in Hama benefitting approximately 500 IDPs, and 1 in Idleb benefitting around 250 IDPs, in 2022.
- 1 in Idleb benefitting around 150 IDPs, in 2023.

4 - Needs

Syria remains a complex humanitarian emergency characterized by over 10 years of ongoing hostilities and their long-term consequences including widespread destruction and the largest number of internally displaced people in the world. According HNO 2022 draft, It is estimated that 5.92m people in Syria will require shelter support in 2022, where a total of 38% of the overall population lives in substandard, damaged, and/ or inadequate shelter. Furtermore, 54% of IDPs live in substandard, damaged, and/ or inadequate shelter, and 55% of returnees live in substandard, damaged, and/ or inadequate shelter with nearly 80% stating inability to meet their basic needs. With the improved security situation, the severity of need for shelter support is expected to remain in-line through 2022, with IDPs and returnees being the worst affected by poor shelter conditions.Regarding the collective shelters, PUI have not conducted any new assessments recently, but it is expected that more collective shelters will be opened in Hama and Idleb, in case any displacement movements occurred, as a humanitarian corridor might open up in these areas during this year.Regarding the locations in Homs and Aleppo which have a severity score of 2, based on PUI's presence in the areas, the conducted needs assessments, and the implementation of previous projects in these areas under other sectors, the plan to provide shelter interventions was set in order to ensure a comprehensive and thorough intervention

5 - Implementation of activities

In line with the sector’s guidance and with full cooperation with MOLAE and local stakeholders, PUI will rehabilitate collective shelters to provide adequate living accommodations for residing IDPs based on Sphere Minimum Standards & shelter sector guidelines. The scope of work includes Civil works; Sanitary works, Electrical works, sensitization of bathrooms for PwDs or elderly populations; and Safety/protection works for the common spaces in the building. After completion of works, PUI will hand over the shelters to local stakeholdersAlso, aiming to improve the living conditions for returnees, PUI will rehabilitate partially damaged houses with no structural damages and ownership documents are available, where the selection of locations will be in line with the Sector acknowledgment that location is cleared of ERW. Most vulnerable groups will be prioritized (Female-headed households; Families with elderly/chronically ill members; PwDs; Families with children under 2 and under 5; Families hosting IDPs and Large families more than 7). The scope of work will include debris removal; closing holes in roofs; staircases; replacing/installing doors and windows; repairing floors, kitchens and bathrooms; and fixing/installing electric systems. After the completion of works, apartments will be handed over to owners. PUI teams will follow up on the implementation to ensure satisfaction of affected population, and that delivered support is as per agreed standards and guidelines.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Crowded living conditions and lack of lighting and gender segregated sanitation facilities can lead to increase in GBV and sexual exploitation. (Likelihood: High, impact: High).Mitigation measures: 1. advocate for provision of alternative accommodation/expansion of existing sites. 2. provide gender segregated sanitation facilities sufficiently close to dwellings. 3. provide lighting devices around shelter and WASH facilities. 4. work closely with protection and other sectors to understand the issues and take necessary actions together to address them. 5. conduct consultations such as on siting of facilities with all groups of population.Risk: Safety of beneficiaries/communities are at risk due to structural integrity of buildings and/or the close presence of ERWs to potential work sites. (Likelihood: High, impact: High) Mitigation measures: 1. conduct structural assessment of buildings by qualified personnel. 2. Repair/rehabilitate only structurally safe buildings 3. work closely with local stakeholders involved in ERW clearance to ensure that the building/area is free from ERW. 4. orient contractors/workers on risks of ERW and ask them to suspend the works and report immediately if a suspicious object is found. Risk: Lack of documents to prove property/occupancy rights including civil documentation. (Likelihood: High, Impact: Medium) Mitigation measures: 1. coordinate with MoLAE to ensure that alternative documents to ownership are recognized by all governorates.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: beneficiaries exposed to SEA harm (Likelihood: low. Impact: High) Mitigation measures: PUI will raise the awareness on existing service providers where victims can seek help; Enforce policies with/through all partners to prevent beneficiaries’ exposure to harm; Establish a confidential complaint mechanism; Train staff on code of conduct; address any SEA complaints. PUI has a zero-tolerance policy towards sexual exploitation and abuse, and is committed to implementing the six principles adopted in 2012 by the Inter Agency Standing Committee Task Force on preventing and responding to sexual exploitation and abuse. Therefore, PUI has put an alert mechanism in place to allow anyone to come forward to report any incident or abuse. Furthermore, and in line with this policy, a specific complaint reporting mechanism for beneficiaries is put in place by the MEAL team.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions1. No curfew or lockdown due to COVID-19 pandemic2. Stable security situation3. Consistent humanitarian access4. No major fluctuation of Syrian Pound5. No mass movements of populations6. Availability of material in the local market7. Acceptance of community.Risks: (Mitigation measures)1. COVID-19 new waves and lockdown: coordination with the concerned stakeholders to anticipate such measures and full adherence to WHO recommendations2. Local population does not accept PUI´s intervention: ensure full engagement of the community leaders & local stakeholders in project design and well introduction of project3. lack of approvals: continue to work with other humanitarian actors to advocate for access4. Volatile security situation: ensure close follow-up with concerned stakeholders to anticipate any deterioration of the security situation, conduct security training for newly recruited staff6. Fluctuation in the exchange rate: keep the donor updated about changes of the economic situation and report the impact on the project in order to discuss adaptive measures7. Lack of contractor: conduct constant surveys to expand the list of contractors8. Risk of eviction and closure of collective shelters: follow up with local stakeholders to anticipate such measures9. Risk of fire in collective shelters: provide necessary extinguishers
