Project Summary

The project “Improving access to inclusive and quality primary education in protective and inclusive learning environments for children in the Far North region, Cameroon” is proposed to support out-of-school children and those at risk of dropping out of school as a direct result of general insecurity and attacks on education (children, teachers, parents, school infrastructure, etc.)
Interventions will focus on creating and maintaining a protective learning environment for children and teachers, as well as strengthening their knowledge and skills to cope with threats and attacks. Special emphasis will be laid on developing and expanding non-formal and informal education opportunities for adolescents. Cash transfers will also be used to support parents and communities in the enrollment and retention of their children in school and learning centers.
The project will target children, girls and boys (3-18 years) affected by the crisis in the Far-North Region, particularly in Mayo-Tsanaga division. This project aims to carry out activities in collaboration with the Ministry of Basic Education (MINEDUB) MINFOP, MINSANTE, MINIPROFF, MINAS, MINEE, delegations at local, departmental and regional levels to improve the needs of children with a focus on education in emergencies.
Equitable access to safe and protective learning environments is facilitated through the construction of school facilities (classrooms, latrines, and water points) that are usable by all students, girls and boys, including children living with disabilities.
Access to alternatives education opportunities adapted to the needs of out of school girls and boys (3-18 years old) due to the crisis is enhanced through learning or vocational training.
Initiatives will support parents, caregivers, teachers and build alliances at the local and community levels to leverage knowledge, raise awareness and encourage community-based action to support education.
The rights of girls and boys must be protected from both current and future threats through access to gender-and-conflict-sensitive education. Interventions will include disaster and conflict risk reduction and Safe school activities. This project also aims to strengthen the resilience of the adolescents, youth and teachers mainly and education structures affected by the crisis in an effective and sustainable manner, while also considering the particular challenges and interdependencies inherent to the humanitarian-development nexus.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The Far North of Cameroon is currently facing one of the world’s largest humanitarian crisis. Since 2013, the non state armed group (Boko Haram) has been carrying out indiscriminate attacks within the region, civilians have been the main targets of such violence, and as a result large population movement have occurred within the region with an estimated total of 1.2 million people in need of humanitarian assistance in Far North region of Cameroon. Thus, violent extremism and BOKO HARAM activism complicate the situation, particularly by restricting the population's access to basic social services and livelihoods. An increase of over 200,000 people in comparison to the 994,000 people who were considered in need in early 2020 (courtesy of HNO 2021). The loss of livelihood due to the armed conflict, the socio-economic impact of the COVID-19 pandemic, as well as the destruction of houses, goods, and fields have resulted in a drastic increase in food insecurity. 81,900 people are estimated to be food insecure from June to August 2021, 46 to 50 % increase in comparison to the same period last this year. This has led to significant population displacement and widespread school closures. In terms of education, the Far North region is declared as a Priority Education Zone (ZEP) in Cameroon. 29% of young people have no access to education. The primary school completion rate is estimated at 46% compared to approximately 95% in all non-northern regions of the country. In areas impacted by conflict, the education system faces extremely high drop-out rates during the primary cycle, while transition rates to secondary school remain exceptionally low and alternative education is not widely available. The climate emergency serves to reinforce already difficult scenarios including; human mobility; children joining the labour force; difficulty in recruiting teachers for difficult environments; an academic schedule that is not suited to traditional ways of life; weak pedagogical supervision; and a general lack of school materials. The enrolment and retention of girls and adolescent girls continues to be low, a challenge partially attributable to traditional norms. Due to the pressure of these different challenges, there are several forms of violence in the crisis-affected areas. These include psychological violence among children, youth and adolescents, gender-based violence and sexual exploitation with negative impact on teaching and learning who leads to high dropout rates and poor learning outcomes. In such a tense security context, parents may not prioritize education, instead concerning themselves with meeting basic and urgent needs. Children are in distress and teachers lack knowledge and competence to deal with crises affected students. The needs and possible shocks faced by the target groups are based on recent studies carried out in the Lake Chad region such as:” Adolescent Life Skills and Parenting in Crisis Settings; a “Joint need assessment in the Far North Region (Jena) “2020. The most pressing needs for school-age children affected by the crises include: Access to the Protective Learning Environment; Quality and relevant education services provided by teachers and education personnel with the knowledge and skills to deal with large class sizes and children in distress; and Teaching and learning materials. The project will ensure continued access to emergency education for children (IDPs, refugees, and hosts) and increase the safety and security of learning environments (e.g., safer learning environments, implementing barrier measures to COVID-19, focusing on advocacy for the non-user of schools for military purposes and developing innovative approaches including vocational training for out-of-school children), training teachers in psychosocial support and education in emergencies, providing teaching and learning materials and continuous conducting awareness campaigns on the importance of girls' education.
