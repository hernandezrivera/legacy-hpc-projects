Project Summary

The proposed project is in line with the education cluster HNO strategic objectives 1, 2, and 3, and is aligned with the MoE Strategy and Plan in adopting the blended learning modality in education, as response to the COVID- 19 crises. The project aims to ensure that boys and girls have equitable safe access to inclusive and quality educational services in vulnerable MoE schools in the Gaza Strip (East Khanyounis, Rafah, East Gaza, West Gaza and North Gaza). The project fits under a multi-year NRC education intervention applying Psychosocial support in formal education, and co-leading the EiE Strategic Advisory Group in Gaza- is intended to focus on the following four-fold approach: i) Implementation of the Psycho-Educational “Better Learning Program” (BLP), reinforcing the system through training new cohort BLP Master Trainers at General Directors, subject supervisors; Capacity building of teaching and non-teaching staff in MoE schools; provision of emergency kits to the schools; awareness sessions for parents; and provision of a “Supporting the Supporter” package for teaching and Non-teaching staff in need; ii) Strengthen the best practices in education contingency and response plans at school level; establish emergency team in each target school; linkage target schools with emergency actors in Gaza; capacity building of emergency team on best practices during, within and before the emergency; revise and update the emergency plan in target schools in line with the specificity of each target school. iii) Carry out remedial/catch up classes for children not active in virtual classes and who are stuck in disrupted academic functioning; improving access of children to distance learning platforms through providing tablets to specific group of children; printing self- learning materials; rehab. schools’ ICT infrastructure. (iv)Provision of technical support to education sub-cluster in Gaza through the continuation of co-leading the EiE Strategic Advisory Group to steer the EiE priorities.

1 - Needs

Fifty-three years of occupation and thirteen years of siege imposed on Gaza have led to increasingly disintegration of the Palestinian territory and of its people.In a study(Ann Strømmesen,2018)carried out in four most vulnerable schools,it was found that almost half of the students expressed challenges with social relationships and/or communication,and finding difficulties in talking to others.Stress and anger constituted the largest emotional subgroups while another characteristic found was the feeling of lack of knowledge, resulting in students feeling helpless.The protracted conflict has long lasting negative effects on the psychosocial well-being of children and their caregivers.Teachers and counsellors often lack the skills and response methodology to identify these children and respond in an appropriate manner. During 2020, the C-19 outbreak in Palestine, evidenced the weakness of the Education system,and the lack of leadership at school level to ensure the continuation of the education services.In April 2020,NRC conducted a multi-sector rapid assessment through phone survey with 760 individuals split equally across Gaza and the West Bank.The rapid assessment aimed at gaining a better understanding of the context due to the impact of C-19 restrictions on vulnerable households.NRC conducted the rapid assessment with a representative sample of its own beneficiaries across Gaza & WB at household level. The key findings of the rapid assessment show that the negative impact on households is significant and that continuity of learning is severely impacted. The analysis of the data showed that 72% (55%F) of interviewed households have school-aged children at home of which 55% have at least one child not following any kind of education system. There is an expected increase in inequality among children, with the most vulnerable at increased risk of dropout or reduced academic achievement. In September 2020, a rapid assessment through phone survives was conducted across the Gaza Strip on 30 teachers from 30schools out of the 118 targeted in the ongoing BLP program. The findings showed that: 81% (33%F) of teachers are still in need of extra efforts in building their capacities in running the virtual classes and managing the content of lessons. 60% (67%F) of teachers confirmed that not all schoolteachers had the opportunity to get the training in virtual classes led by the MoE. Only 21% (26%F) of students are active in virtual classes; they attributed the inactivity of children in virtual classes to the lacking of ICT equipment. 90% (37%F) of teachers confirmed that, they were not satisfied of the level of students’ participation in virtual classes, and 100%of them confirmed that they don’t have the appropriate skills and techniques for improving and evaluating students’ participation in virtual classes. (NRC, Situational rapid assessment- September, 2020) In addition, NRC conducted a data collection from 15 target schools, revealed that 18% (89%F) of children attended schools’ established virtual classes; all school principals of these schools (100%) (100%F) attributed the inactivity of children to the lacking of ICT equipment at household level, and to the unavailability of internet network at homes. (NRC- 2020). Moreover, due to the last meeting in SAG group in October 2020 with MoE Deputy Mister, 33% of students active in virtual classes, while the rest of overall children enrolled in MoE schools are still stuck in revisable challenges, such as the unaffordability of internet network and ICT devices. In October 2020, a phone survey was conducted across Gaza on parents’ of 804 children enrolled in NRC ‘s ongoing-targeted schools. The findings showed that: 67% (45% F) of parents noticed alarming changes on their children’s behaviour. The changes were ranked according to parents’ answers frequency; as a result, nervous, aggression, stress, and anxiety were on the top of the ranking list. Besides that, in 2020 ,the HNO published by the education cluster which confirmed the need of over 400,000 children for Psychosocial support, due to the accumulative threats on children’s mental health and well-being. Moreover, the NRC ongoing projects’ data collected at the beginning of 2020, reflected the needs of Gaza children for PSS support, in addition to calming and relaxing techniques. NRC data showed that, 83% (40%F) of surveyed children didn’t have the skills to calm themselves when they were scared, and 86% (43%F) unable to calm their selves when they feel angry, and 57% (34%F) were not optimistic of having a better future. As per Cluster information and through NRC’s long-standing presence in the sector and continuous interaction with local educational institutions and authorities; there exists a need for a clearer and improved technical capacity and leadership in designing and rolling-out the distance-learning scheme, including catch-up classes for those at high risk of dropout. Support is particularly required in regards to content, pedagogical approach as well as tools and materials for education professionals and students. At household level, material support (ICT) to families with school-aged children who have limited access to distance learning possibilities is a must. As per the aforementioned NRC rapid assessment, 45% of school-age children have access to distance education, and the remaining 55% are completely excluded from any distance learning programme. NRC's direct work and communications with more than 100 schools all along the Gaza Strip, will guide the implementation of this project. NRC has experience in the most excluded areas of the Gaza Strip has shown the challenges that many families face to follow the education system include lack of ICT equipment. The need for ICT equipment is greater now with the spread of C-19 and closure of schools/depending on distance learning. In addition, the teachers require support to lead the distance education process, including PSS.

2 - Activities

NRC has in place a MoU with the MoEHE for collaboration and engagement across the West Bank and Gaza. A Framework Agreement will be drafted between NRC and MoEHE outlining an overall commitment to ensure the adequate implementation of the project. NRC has its contextualized M&E tool kit developed and regularly updated by the regional office in collaboration with University of Tromso (Norway), and in close dialogue with the field offices. The tool kit has been benchmarked due to the well- known international scales; Strength and Difficulty Questionnaire- SDQ, and Motivated Strategies for Learning Questionnaire- MSLQ. The tools will be administered according to the project needs. There is a M&E work plan in place, which include all project’s milestones, and the tools (surveys, questionnaires, FGD…etc.) that should be used during the course of the project. Furthermore, the NRC Team will conduct field visits to the prioritized schools, in order to have a first-hand understanding of the project implementation at school level. The activities are clustered in four main areas: 1. NRC will provide (remotely and/or in-person) psychosocial support in 113 MoE schools at all levels, by teachers and counsellors at school level. The Psychosocial support of NRC is a multi-level approach BLP intervention that is divided into three components: BLP-1 is on how to manage a classroom with children who have gone through stressful and traumatic events, BLP-2 target underachievers with specific techniques to support improving their resilience and learning functioning; while BLP-3 is a specialised, clinical approach to address nightmares, which many children experience as chronic symptom of traumatic stress. The BLP also connects education personnel with parents, to help them understand their children through a community-based approach - NRC will conduct a vulnerability/need assessment at school level and selection of the most vulnerable target schools - ToT 10 (4F) MoEHE Subject Supervisors and Head of Department to become BLP Master Trainers. 5. Capacity building for 339 (200F) teachers on Increasing Learning Capacity among pupils in Ongoing Crisis' and Group Sessions-Improving Study Skills. - Capacity building for 113 (70F) counsellors on `Fighting Nightmares and sleeping problems'. - Provide technical direct support to selected schools to implement BLP 1 (15,375), BLP 2 (1,130) and BLP 3 (904), activities • Provision of structured PSS intervention for 292 (173F) teaching and non- teaching -staff and awareness sessions for 2,260 (1,506F) parents on child protection and community engagement. • NRC will support the existing “Child Protection – Case Management” mechanism and protocols in place. Even when NRC will not directly do “case management” the education team will be train to use the existing mechanism. - 2. Support the establishment of school level Emergency response committees (3 members: 1 teacher, 1 school mgt., 1 school counsellor).- Building the capacities of school’s emergency teams for possible contingency plan implementation. - Technical support to schools to build the plans in participatory way. - Support schools in carrying out simulation and 1 school-based campaign exercises based on each school needs and updated contingency/emergency plan. 3. Remedial/ catch up classes in 113 MoE schools: - Sign four service contracts with 4 CBOs based on target schools localities (Rafah, East Khanyounis, North Gaza, and East Gaza) for implementing the remedial/classes. - Carry out school-based assessment/screening to identify children from grade 4-8 in need of remedial/catch up classes according to MoE SOPs and protocols. - Carry out community- based (2- 4 classes) remedial classes in both Math & Arabic for children from grade 4-8. Children will receive the remedial classes based on MoE protocols and the methodologies will be discussed with the MoE. - 10 most vulnerable children from remedial/catch up classes in each target school identified and benefited from tablets distribution for improving their access to virtual classes, as per MoE guidances. - Print and distribute MoE self- learning materials to remaining children in virtual classes (who do not get benefit from tablets distribution). - Identify the existing ICT environment in each target school, and providing schools with ICT items and equipment (laptops and computer facilities) based on each school real needs.4. Continue work in co-leading the Strategic Advisory Group in Gaza and support education sub-cluster in Gaza in steering the priorities of Education in Emergency. NRC will support in the coordination and contribute in developing and adjusting the strategic framework, priorities and work plan of the Cluster.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

NRC recognises that safeguarding of children and vulnerable adults engaged in their programmes is a fundamental responsibility. All staff must sign and are trained on NRC’s Code of Conduct, and are now required to sign and be trained on the organisation’s new Child Safeguarding Policy. The training for Education staff and staff with regular contact with children includes content on child rights, understanding child abuse and sexual abuse, reviews behaviour and conduct required from staff and how to receive disclosures and report and refer cases. BLP content is also designed to promote safe and inclusive learning environments for children and should support staff and teachers’ understanding of child protection. NRC Action trusts working on incorporating protection principles and prompting meaningful access, safety, and dignity in the proposed humanitarian aid. At the very beginning of the action in the needs assessment phase, the NRC will identify the people at risk, kind of risks they face and expected to face, and why they face such kind of risks, as well as activating a structured referral system who people who seek more help and support. The NRC action follows the participatory approach, where beneficiaries will be included from the early beginning of the action, participating in the needs assessment, monitoring and evaluation phase, and in identifying the area of improvement. Moreover, all beneficiaries will have the same chance of receiving the service (e.g. school children will receive the service at a voluntary manner and with a respected informed consent from their parents). Both teachers, and counselors as a main beneficiaries in NRC action will participate in a voluntary manner, and due to their interest. A follow up mechanism will be shared with all NRC suggested beneficiaries, where open channels of communication will be offered by NRC (e.g. Generic emails, field visits….etc) with all beneficiaries that guarantee the confidentiality.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The NRC action focuses on three dimensions of intervention: i) a general class room based intervention approach targeting all children in target schools. Its suitable for all children in any context, as it is designed to reach large numbers of children through classroom delivery. This dimension is designed to ensure and guarantee equal chances of access for all children both males and females and their specific needs. ii) The second dimension offers a special kind of support to children who need additional support, to support resilience among them for the early recovery or protracted displacement when conditions for learning have stabilized. iii) The third dimension is a specialised clinical one that is designed to address nightmares, which many children experience as a chronic symptoms of traumatic stress. The equal chances of both males and females, boys and girls, children with or without special needs are affordable based on the real needs, beneficiaries interest and potential. As well as without any kind of discrepancies among the beneficiaries either they are children or adult. The NRC action welcomes all children being part of the service delivered regardless of the differences among them based on inclusion approach. For the inclusive education and contingency plans component andcatch up classes, the project will target the most excluded children (boys and girls) at school level. The project team will work at directorate and school level, to define the criteria for those children involved and benefited by the project.
