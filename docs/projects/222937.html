Project Summary

AMAN's Livelihoods and Skills Development project for the Joint Response Plan 2025 is a comprehensive initiative designed to empower Rohingya refugees, with a particular focus on women, through robust non-formal technical and vocational training. The primary objective is to enhance self-sufficiency by training in diverse vocational fields, such as Tailoring & Dress Making Operation, Industrial Sewing Machine Operation, Tie Dye, Block Print & Handprint Operation, Boutique Management, Jute Ware Operation, and handicraft training. The project aims to mitigate physical and protection-related risks while fostering resilience among refugees for a more self-reliant future.
Project Objectives:
1. Provide Livelihoods and Skills Development training based on recognized certification programs.
2. Empower refugees, particularly women, through training in various vocational fields.
3. Mitigate physical and protection-related risks for vulnerable populations.
4. Promote peaceful coexistence and enhance resilience among refugees.
5. Offer additional capacity-building support in cross-cutting topics.
Project Implementation:
• Project Locations: Concentrated in camps 10, 14, 15, and 16, extending to Extremely Vulnerable Households (EVHHs) of the Rohingya Refugee community, including persons with disabilities.
• Team Composition: A dedicated team, including 4 Community Mobilizers, 08 Trainers, and 2 Market Linkage Officer, ensures effective project execution.
• Concurrent Activities: The project runs concurrently with Health, Shelter/NFI, FSS, GBV, Education, and other activities throughout 2025.
• Alignment: The project aligns with the Livelihood and Skills Development (LSD) Sector policy and guidance, following the Multi-Year Operational Plan through December 31, 2025.
Training Focus:
• Vocational Training Areas: Sewing machines, handicrafts, boutiques, and jute ware, following formally recognized curricula and certification programs.
• Targeted Participants: Refugees, including women, older individuals, people with disabilities, and other gender-diverse populations.
Operational Strategy:
• Training Duration: Non-formal technical training of 120 hours, and vocational skills training for 360 hours.
• Centers and Trainees: 6 centers and 1280 trainees, organized into four batches.
• Class Scheme: Manual Sewing (8 classes), Industrial Sewing (27 classes), Boutique & Handicraft (30 classes), Social Awareness (10 classes).
• Awareness Sessions: Covering GBV, Child Protection, Child Care & Mother Care, Hygiene, Adolescent Hygiene, Wash Management, Disability Inclusion, Nutrition, Health Care & Family Planning, Entrepreneurship, Capacity Building, Mental Health, Gender & Gender Equity.
Pathways for Beneficiaries:
• Skill Utilization: Graduates engage in self-employment and volunteer opportunities within the camps, with in-kind support for formal and informal skill training.
• Entrepreneurship Program: Selected graduates undergo a comprehensive four-month program, receiving daily-based payments from product sales.
• Market Linkage: AMAN facilitates market linkage and job placement for all trainees.
Sustainability Model:
• Initial Years: AMAN reinvests the entire income for the first two years to sustain the business, not profiting from operations.
• Future Operations: AMAN funds and operates the production center after two years, taking 70% of the income and distributing 30% among artisans.
• Social Causes: 35% of AMAN's share is reinvested for sustainability, and the remaining 35% supports various social causes.
Group Formation:
• After 12 months of training, AMAN forms 10 women's groups for employment, providing machinery, equipment, materials, and logistical support.
With a budget of $429,370, this project aligns with the 2025 Joint Response Plan (JRP), addressing shelter and NFI needs while linking vocational training to livelihood opportunities, enhancing refugee well-being.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Evidence underscores the significant livelihood challenges faced by both Rohingya refugees and host communities. Government restrictions prevent refugees from accessing formal employment, leaving 80-90% reliant on humanitarian assistance or low-paid casual labor, while host community households fare slightly better, with at least one member typically engaged in income-generating activities. However, many in both groups remain dependent on precarious work, with youth and women experiencing unemployment rates exceeding 40% (UNHCR, 2023; NGO Livelihood Assessments, 2024). Cultural and systemic barriers further exacerbate gender inequality, with 60% of women in these communities facing mobility restrictions that limit their access to education, vocational training, and economic opportunities. Women are frequently excluded from decision-making processes, heightening their vulnerabilities (IRC, 2024). Additionally, refugees aged 18 and older encounter significant barriers to vocational training due to restrictive policies, language challenges, and low literacy rates, with only 25% of youth participating in skill development programs (ILO, 2023). These challenges contribute to harmful coping mechanisms, such as exploitative labor and child labor, while economic disparities between refugees and host communities strain social cohesion (Save the Children, 2024). Addressing these issues through gender-sensitive livelihood programs, vocational training, and entrepreneurship initiatives is critical to reducing economic disparities, fostering resilience, and promoting self-reliance for both populations. These evidence-based interventions are essential to achieving sustainable development and regional social harmony.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, the AMAN Livelihoods and Skills Development project incorporates climate action through its focus on Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and Disaster Risk Management (DRM). The project takes proactive measures to address environmental issues and build climate resilience by promoting sustainable livelihoods that consider climate adaptation and mitigation. Training in eco-friendly and sustainable vocational fields, such as jute ware production and handicrafts, helps reduce environmental impacts. Additionally, the project integrates climate-resilient practices within its skills training, emphasizing waste management and environmental awareness. This approach fosters long-term resilience for both the refugee and host communities, helping them adapt to climate change while supporting sustainable livelihoods.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the AMAN Livelihoods and Skills Development project has Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) measures embedded. The project includes a comprehensive emergency response strategy with standard operating procedures (SOPs) for crises, ensuring swift and effective interventions during health emergencies or natural disasters. It incorporates a business continuity plan to maintain essential livelihood services during disruptions, with contingency funds set aside to support the immediate needs of affected communities. Additionally, the project ensures staff are trained in emergency response protocols, and the integration of various sectors such as health, shelter, and WASH guarantees a holistic approach to disaster preparedness and response.
