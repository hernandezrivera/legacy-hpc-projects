Project Summary

Proposed (Fund secured)
Since 2018 there have been increasing clashes between unidentified armed groups (UAGs) and Government forces, along with different intercommunal conflicts and natural disasters. These shocks continue to cause major displacements in the Oromia region. According to DTM Ethiopia DTT- 99 there has also been a significant increase in conflict and drought displacement in Guji zones. There has also been new conflict displacement in East Hararge, East Wellega, West Wellega, Horo Gudru Wellega, Kelem Wellega, West Shewa, and North Shewa zones. As a result, the number of Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) in the Oromia region has reached 506,133. CRS conducted a multi-sectoral needs desk review. The assessment highlighted that many IDPs do not have access to shelter and are forced to sleep in overcrowded spaces including emergency shelters or communal sites. These IDPs share household items and sleep communally increasing the risk of COVID-19 transmission and protection issues. The situation of the IDPs in East Wellega is complex, some of these IDPs live with the host communities while others have settled in the rural areas living in substandard living conditions. IDPs are living dispersed throughout the host community in rental houses, and it has been difficult for the IDPs to afford the expensive cost of rental houses due to limited incomes from displacement. Sasiga Woreda in East Wellega Zone and Goro Dola Woreda in Guji Zone are identified as priority woredas for IDP responses by the cluster. In the two woredas, there are a total of 278,682 IDPs: 134,338 in East Wellega and 144,344 IDPs in Guji. The national Shelter and NFI cluster recommended providing six-month rental support for IDPs living in the host communities and Non-Food Items (NFI) V1 for IDPs. Accordingly, CRS with its partner Ethiopian Catholic Church Social and Development Commission Office of Nekemete (ECC-SDCON), also known as NCS and Ethiopian Catholic Church Social and Development Commission Office of Hawassa (HCS) will target a total of 2,780 HHs in East Wellega and Guji zones with the following interventions:
In Sasiga woreda in East Wellega Zone 1,950HHs will be targeted for NFI V1 kits, 1850HH will receive the standard NFI V1 kit (15% directed to host community) and CRS will plan to distribute 100 NFI V1- Disable kits to eligible HHs. The average cost of each NFI V1 kit is 102 USD and the NFI V1-Disable kit is budgeted at 130 USD, excluding overheads.

Of the 1,950HHs targeted in Sasiga, 580 HHs will also be targeted for cash for rent. The cash will be provided for six months with an average cost of cash for rent is 25 USD per month per HH, total $150 per HH. The cash will be provided to targeted households in 2 rounds (distribute 3 months of house rental in one round).

In Goro Dola woreda, Guji zone, 830 HHs targeted for ESNFI V2, 790HH will receive the standard ESNFI V2 kits (15% directed to host community) and 40 ESNFI V2-Disable kits will be distributed to eligible HHs. The average cost of each ESNFI V2 kit is 140 USD and the ESNFI V2-Disable kit is budgeted at 182 USD, excluding overheads. Before conducting cash for rent and NFI distributions, CRS will conduct a rental market assessment in the woreda to confirm the appropriate transfer value. This assessment will involve consultation with affected communities. CRS and partners will provide sensitization to the targeted HHs and the community including families who are renting out their houses. The orientation for the targeted HH will focus on the best use of the NFI and cash for a rental. An orientation on the CRS Code of Conduct will be provided to families who are renting a house

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

CRS and it's implementing partner are both part of the Caritas Internationalis network and share common values and approaches as well as a long history of working together to support local communities in times of crisis across Ethiopia. CRS works with local partners to identify and assess needs and collaboratively work on project design. CRS and its local partners will be responsible for the direct implementation of the intervention. CRS Ethiopia’s Emergency Project Officer and Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) Project Officers will lead the monitoring and evaluation of the project. CRS' robust MEAL Unit will spearhead all monitoring activities using the emergency contextualized Simple Measurement of Indicators for Learning and Evidence-based Reporting (SMILER) M&E system. The project will develop brief tools for data collection, implement participatory data planning, monitoring systems, and organize meetings with community members that involve both women and the youth (male and female), elders, people with disabilities, people with special needs, and other relevant social community groups to solicit issues and comments that will be considered in the ongoing project implementation. CRS accompanies each partner for an agreed number of days per month aligned with critical programming activities to provide technical support and to monitor activities. CRS provides support and oversight during partner-led registration/validation and distribution events. CRS staff lead the following activities in close collaboration with partners: gender and protection assessment, market assessment, capacity building on GBV/PSEA, accountability and staff technical skill, setup of compliant and feedback mechanism, beneficiary targeting, all post distribution monitoring activities, engaging with the national shelter clusters, and working groups as appropriate, reporting financial and narrative report to donors are key activities. The MEAL Project Officers assess the progress and implementation of each activity, in-line to meet set indicators and Sphere standards. CRS has robust financial, management, and logistics processes in place to ensure the highest quality programming from our partners through routine partner assessments

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

CRS and its partner are committed to saving lives, protecting livelihoods, and enhancing resilience to shocks of the most vulnerable households in conflict-affected communities. Target communities are primarily valued stakeholders at every stage of the project cycle. Accountability to project participants and communities is one of the core values in CRS. CRS and its implementing partner will highly involve target project participants in conducting the feasibility study, implementation, and in monitoring and evaluation activities. Regular meetings with community members that involve men, women, boys, girls, and people with disabilities will be organized in coordination local Government at the woreda level. Depending on the preference of project participants, CRS will design and apply Community Feedback and Response system to facilitate two-way communication. Emphasis on Women and Children. Project activity designs and project plans will be discussed in focus group discussions and results will be incorporated into project implementation plans and activity design. FGDs will be conducted with women and men in separate sessions. To further reinforce accountability, promote social cohesion and mitigate conflict sensitivities, CRS and partners will also ensure that targeting of project participants to be in consultation with the local community representatives in which the most vulnerable segments of the society are fairly included in all activities. Project activity/distribution areas will be selected based on the feedback/comments from the local communities to ensure no negative effects to people accessing sites. Verification of beneficiary lists with the local communities will also be done. This will be assessed by conducting post distribution monitoring after the distributions are made. In order to avoid/minimize the possible exploitation (abuse of power) of the affected communities with the power imbalance dynamics there will also be posters developed with respect to individual and/or group entitlements along with expected staff conduct. There will also be community sensitization on staff code of conduct and program participants protection aspects to prevent happening of possible violations and improve community awareness on how to interact with CRS and partners staff during project implementation.
