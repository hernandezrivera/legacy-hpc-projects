Project Summary

Health Action Africa (HAA) has designed this HRP 2024 proposal to complement the continuity of already ongoing Health in emergency projects, supported by RRF/IoM, UNFPA, IMC, GAC, AAHI & AVSI. The Counties are; Canal Pigi and Pochalla in Jonglei State, Fashoda and Nassir in Upper Nile State and Tonj North & Gogrial West Counties in Warrap State, South Sudan.
The project proposed to coordinate and strengthen a network of Health facilities to provide equitable access to Health Care services. These services will be coordinated to other sectors; Nutrition, WASH, SGBV and Food security to give complimentary synergy of Humanitarian assistance. HAA Planned to establish mobile clinics in IDPs settlements, returnees Population or remote hard to reach areas facing Health insecurities. Dysfunctional health facilities in Host communities will be revived to provide optimal health care services.
HAA further proposed to conduct out-reaches for vaccinations against measles and other vaccines preventable diseases. HAA will mobilized all the other antigens (BCG, OPV, and DPT-Hep B-Hib & Vitamin A) from the core pipeline managers to facilitate emergency immunization/vaccination Services in children especially the under 5Years of age. This will directly improve Coverage of PENTA3 in The Project planned to Conduct the Deployment of skilled health workers including mobile teams to priority locations. This wide array of cadres will Conduct out-patient consultation (OPDs) at health facilities and in-patient treatment for common diseases. Coordinate and Support health facilities with essential supplies and drugs from core pipe line managers, Government and other donors.
Under mental health, the project planned to provide prevention services to mitigate trauma induced injuries, Post-traumatic Stress Disorder, Substance abuse such as harmful use of alcohol, tobacco and other drugs, psycho social support services (PSS) and referral for case management of moderate-severe depression disorder(DEP)
HAA will continue to adopt the Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BEMONC) protocol to perform services by providing prenatal and postpartum care.
Surveillance and reporting. The project will coordinate with the respective CHDs and WHO to ensure all sentinel sites are active and timely submits the IDSR/EWARs. Community level surveillance system will also be activated or improved.
The overall project target is 46,523 beneficiaries Men 14,422 Women 15,353 Boys 7909 and Girls 8840
This HRP proposal is aligned to all the three (3) Health cluster objectives: CO1: Improve equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations,
CO 2: Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases.
CO 3: Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms

1. Needs

According to 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) Report, only 149 out of 1869 health facilities in South Sudan are providing the full package of Basic Package for Health and Nutrition Services (BPHNs) and 990 of the facilities are moderately functional, indicating low access and unavailability of some health services to poor, conflict and flood affected communities. The Inter-Agency Needs assessments (IRNA) conducted in Tonj North County at the Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) locations since Aug 2023 described poor sanitation conditions, under-resourced health services, rising disease burden evident by disease outbreaks, including Malaria, Acute watery Diarrhea (AWD), Measles etc. The Tonj North County Surveillance report in Oct 23 decried 95 patience line listed for suspected measles cases. Out of 11 samples collected for testing, 7 confirmed 1 g M Positive indicating and outbreak in the County. The intensified fighting in the Sudan and the suspension of Food in the refugee camps in Ethiopia caused massive influx of returnees Population in Nassir County. IRNA Reports led my IoM on the Month of Aug 2023 indicated Inadequate access to health care facilities. There are no functional health facilities in Jikmir the Payam at Border with Ethiopia. Health Care services are only accessible in Nasir Town. Pochalla County equally have many IDPs settlements that resulted from sub national violence. Key Infrastructures including schools, Health facilities, markets, are dysfunctional since equipment and capital investments in these infrastructures were looted during the sub National violence5. Fashoda experienced cyclonic armed conflict that continue inhibit access to humanitarian assistance. DTM 2023 IoM Reports indicated continues influx of Returnees from the Sudan into Fashoda IDPs settlements on daily bases. RRC estimates indicates, more than 35,000 Vulnerable Population are in dire need of humanitarian assistance including critical lifesaving health care services. Across all these counties, the humanitarian situation remains dire and is still impacted by instability, economic stagnation, and fragility. Poverty is ubiquitous, exacerbated by conflict, displacement and external shocks. The food insecurity levels remain high due to the impacts of the economic crisis, flooding, low agricultural production, conflict, and insecurity in some areasOn access to health services, only 59% of the population can access health facilities while 80% of the population report barriers in accessing lifesaving health care services. Both rural and urban areas have low immunization coverage (49 percent Penta 3 coverage). Estimates from WHO indicate that low birth weight contributes 5% of total deaths in South Sudan, reflecting an age-adjusted death rate of 23 per 100,000 populations and putting the country at number 18 globally. Malaria, diarrhea, and pneumonia are responsible for over 75% of deaths among under-fives, the burden for malaria is over 50% and is a major contributor to the high burden of malnutrition among children in the countryThe corresponding rise in cases of measles, malaria, acute watery diarrhea, and other diseases across the Counties with severity level 4 has overwhelmed existing healthcare capacity. There is low routine EPI coverage for the last couple of years, which can be attributed to the scarcity of skilled human resources at the grass root level for vaccination operations, high attrition rates for the few available skilled human resources, access issues due to insecurity coupled with recurrent flooding for the last three years. The low RI coverage results in building up of the cohorts of "Zero Dose" and under-vaccinated children, especially in the most flooded and security-compromised areas of Jonglei, Greater Pibor Administrative Area, Upper Nile, and Unity states, rendering them susceptible to the outbreaks of vaccine-preventable diseases (VPDs). This HRP proposal is designed to addressed the above needs

2. Project activities

To meet CO1: Improve equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and SGBV health services to crisis affected populations, HAA proposed to Implement the activities below; Conduct Health Promotion activities focusing on environmental hygiene (stagnant water, bush clearance around home states, safe disposal of human excreta to mitigate widespread open defecation, distribution of impregnated bed nets (LLITN) to mitigate WASH induced morbidity, facilitate awareness campaigns on immunization of children and pregnant women against vaccine preventable diseases, advocacy for prevention of Sexual Gender Based violence (SGBV) and awareness to access case management of CMR services.HAA further proposed to conduct out reaches for vaccinations against measles and other vaccines preventable diseases. The immunization guidelines from UNICEF and MoH shall be adopted and followed for compliance.
