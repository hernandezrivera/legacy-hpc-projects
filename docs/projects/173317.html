Project Summary

This project aims to support socio-economic empowerment of Rohingya and Host Community women and adolescent girls through increased livelihood skills, start-up support and establishing market linkages.
UN Women has five Multi-Purpose Women’s Centers (MPWCs) in Camps 3, 4, 4 extension, 5, and 18. In these Centers psycho-social services, 1st aid medical services and counseling and referral services for GBV cases, are provided to Rohingya women, livelihood activities have been carried out. Three new MPWCs will be established in the Host Community. The livelihood trainings with the duration of up to 6 months will be continued in the MPWCs both for Rohingya and Host Community women. The trainings will focus on handicrafts, fish culture, beauty parlor, tailoring, production of masks, and production of re-usable sanitary napkins as part of a joint initiative with IOM and UNFPA. After receiving training beneficiaries will received sewing machine and materials based trade. Besides, there will be trainings on home stead gardening, which will be accompanied with seed distribution. As well, there will be trainings on the skills which are not traditionally associated with women, such as the use of solar energy for producing lights. In addition, the ways to skill women to engage in eco-tourism will be explored. Specific attention will be given to include most marginalized women and girls in the activities, including the female headed households and women with disabilities.
UN Women has established a Women’s Market, which will become operationalized in 2020. Women’s Market will be functional to ensure market linkages for the women producers, not only for the groups from MPWCs; but also for the women producers supported, by other partners such as WFP, FAO, UNDP, AAB, BRAC, HSI, PRANTIC, SNKSS and United Purpose. As the women vendors will be both Roghinya and Host Community, the Market is also expected to contribute social cohesion. Basic entrepreneurship skill development trainings will be delivered for the women who have shops in the Market.

1 - Identified Needs

Even before Covid-19, the Rohingya women and adolescent girls in the have been facing challenges in accessing opportunities and services in the Camps. The Rohingya community is highly conservative and there is deeply rooted gender norms in the Rohingya society, which leads to women and adolescent girls having limited mobility and therefore limited access to information, assistance and services, low level of participation to decision making, low level of participation to livelihood activities; and high level of GBV. In terms of the opportunities of income generation, according to REVA II, 88 per cent of the Rohingya population is highly or completely dependent on life-saving assistance. Some women and men have been able to access income-generating opportunities, such as informal day labour outside the camps, running a small business inside the camp, or working as a volunteer and cash-for-work at NGOs for men and volunteer or cash-for-work for women. However, the numbers remain very small and significantly skewed towards men. By the time of the study, only 2 per cent of women were engaged in an income-generating activity compared to 27 per cent of men. As well, whereas 44 per cent of adolescent boys (aged 15 to 20) reported working outside the home, it was 7 per cent for adolescent girls. However, with Covid-19 the existing inequalities have been exacerbating and the Covid-10 crisis has been disproportionately affecting women and girls, as highlighted in the Rapid Gender Analysis on the Impact of COVID-19 in Cox’s Bazar (conducted by the Gender Hub, UN Women, OXFAM and Care). To start with, women’s and girls’ have been at a higher risk of getting infected due to their primary caregiver roles. However, their access to information and health services was lesser, due to the restrictive gender norms such as purdah. In addition, their home-related responsibilities such as cleaning and water collecting have increased. Meanwhile, the policing of and violence and discrimination against women also increased. Pandemic also created economic consequences. The livelihood and cash-based activities were limited, together with the paid refugee volunteer work opportunities. In this context single women heads of household (approx. 16% of the total Rohingya refugee population), adolescent girls, young women constitute the most vulnerable groups. However, despite the challenges and restrictions, Rohingya women and girls have been playing a key role in increasing the resilience of their families and communities. Rohingya women leaders, including the survivors of conflict-related sexual and gender-based violence, have been self-organizing and forming their own groups in the Camps, to advocate for their rights and call for justice. In addition, Covid-19 also created a new context where there is a need for all household members to engage new activities, which creates an opportunity for greater engagement of women in livelihood activities, which can lead to change in the gender division of responsibilities within households. The situation is also similar in the host communities. According to REVA II, the gender differences in engaging income-generating activities is even more significant, with 47 per cent of men compared to 4 per cent of women. The pandemic has also effected the host community in a similar way Rohingya community, dis-appropriately impacted women negatively.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: Provide self-reliance/livelihood skills training to Rohingya and host community women and adolescent girls. Activity 2: Provide tools and start-up support to Rohingya and host community women and adolescent girls to start-up self-reliance/income-generating activities and Cash for Work options Activity 3: Provide Rohingya and host community women and adolescent girls access to market spaces within camps/villages as well as market linkages Activity 4: Joint activities on socio-economic empowerment between Rohingya and host community women and adolescent girls. Activity 5: Roll-out pilot of new improved Community and Feedback Mechanism for UN Women interventions in camps including use of both complaint boxes, hotline, group and one-to-one consultations including by engaging Rohingya volunteers and CSO leaders in consultations with Rohingya refugees benefitting from UN Women services and activities.
