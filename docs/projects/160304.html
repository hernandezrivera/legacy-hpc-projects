Project Summary

The overall objectives of the proposed project is to contribute towards GBV prevention at community level and provision support services for women affected by violence and conflict to enable them to lead their life with dignity and respect. The project will support the delivery of an integrated package of risk mitigation and prevention of GBV activities, at the community level, and specialized services to survivors in the areas with severe needs. The aim of community-based GBV prevention interventions is to provide immediate life-saving support to women and girls at risk, mitigate risks of GBV, and shift social norms that strengthen GBV by promoting gender equality and behavioral change and working with community-based structures.
To ensure that quality GBV case management services are available to survivors in the two conflict-affected counties of Aweil North and East, Northern Bahr El Ghazal states, the project will strengthen capacity of the existing community frontline workers and Government structures on critical sectors (PSS, health, and security) to provide quality services to survivors of GBV, and will increase access to support services through the establishment of Women and Girls Friendly Space (WGFS) to increase their access to quality case management, psychosocial and medical services and livelihoods opportunities. This project targets women, girls, Men and boys in IDPs settings, returnees and host communities as primary beneficiaries.
This project will also increase access to community based protection services such as WGFS to 8,350 women, adolescent girls, SGBV survivors, and other vulnerable groups of women. The project will provide improved livelihood options for survivors of SGBV to move out of poverty. More importantly, the project will target a total of 8,000 populations; 4800 female (girls-2300, women-2500) 3200 male (boys-2100, men-1100,) with 5% of this population being people with disability. Host communities (60%), 1,200 IDPs (15%) and 2,000 returnees (25%)
SPEDPs comparative advantage is its long presence in Northern Bahr el Ghazal. SPEDP has been implementing various projects in Aweil North, West and Centre since 2013 is has a good working relationship with stakeholders across these former counties. The organisation Piloted a Monitoring and Evaluation Toolkit for GBV Programming supported by War Child Canada and Women’s Refugee Commission’s project “From Input to Impact: Monitoring and Evaluation of GBV Programming in Restrictive Environments” implemented Awiel West and North and therefore this project is going to contribute to the reduction of GBV cases noted during the piloting phase.

1 - Needs

Gender-based violence (GBV) is one of the most critical threats to the protection and wellbeing of women and children in South Sudan. Already rife prior to the conflict, it is now nearing epidemic proportions as threats and risks of GBV against women and girls persist, particularly sexual violence, intimate partner violence, domestic violence, sexual exploitation, sexual harassment, early and forced marriage everywhere in South Sudan. Many women and girls struggle with fears for their personal safety, as possible targets of violence and the full magnitude of the problem is unclear. However, studies indicate that some 65 per cent of women and girls have experienced physical and/or sexual violence in their lifetime, and some 51 per cent have suffered intimate partner violence (IPV). Some 33 per cent of women have experienced sexual violence from a non-partner, primarily during attacks or raids. The majority of girls and women experience sexual violence for the first time under the age of 18(August 2019 - CP GBV Briefing Note – Final). Women and girls have been disproportionately impacted by GBV during the conflict and continue to face risks of multiple and intersecting forms of discrimination and violence in South Sudan. Sexual violence, rape, abductions, sexual slavery, and early and forced marriage were used throughout the conflict. The Piloting of M&E Toolkit for GBV Programming supported by War Child Canada and Women’s Refugee Commission’s project “From Input to Impact: Monitoring and Evaluation of GBV Programming in Restrictive Environments” implemented by SPEDP in Magwi and Awiel West and North 2019 - to date indicates that 90% of the residents reported that there are very few GBV services or programmes available for women and /girls to access and participate in the community. 43% argued that there is a lack of information and awareness on the existing women and girls services. As a result, they were unable to access very few existing support services. As displacement becomes increasingly protracted, the general lack of safety in communities continues to deteriorate, risks of all forms of GBV against women and girls persist, including sexual violence, intimate partner and family violence, sexual exploitation, harassment and early and forced marriages Many women and girls in South Sudan still struggle with fears for their personal safety, as possible targets of violence, and female headed households and adolescent girls are at higher risk of GBV, especially sexual violence. Girls who have been sexually abused are at higher risk of experiencing intimate partner violence and of being involved, or exploited, in sex work later in life. Overall levels of reporting on GBV are low as most women do not trust police to report violence. Fears of harassment and stigmatization are real barriers to accessing services and seeking justice for GBV survivors. Exposure to conflict and violence and chronic stressors may often inflict on their psychosocial well-being and mental health. The limited availability of specialized GBV and PSS services falls short of meeting increasing needs. Case management service needs systematic and institutional support. Significant gaps exist in services for girls, especially age-appropriate PSS, educational and recreational support (UNICEF and Protection Cluster Monthly update of August 2019), GBV service provision is at risk due to the limited funding resources available, resulting in an urgent need to consolidate foundational work to strengthen specialized services and build local capacity to sustainably address this need. There is an urgent need to improve on the resource envelop for GBV to ensure adequate response and prevention mechanism.

2 - Activities

1. Strengthen and establish GBV Referral Pathway in order to enhance access to essential GBV services and protection mechanisms in Northern Bahr El Ghazal State. SPEDP will work with other partners to regularly review and update the GBV referral pathway with a functional contact persons both primary and secondary which will be shared among beneficiaries and partners for incidents reporting, case follow-up and referral. 2. Train SPEDP staff, other GBV partners and community volunteers on GBV case management, psychosocial support (PSS) and psychological first aid (PFA) to provide individualized focused and non-focused psychosocial support for survivors of GBV and other most vulnerable women. 3. Provision of comprehensive case management services for survivors of GBV and other most vulnerable women and girls through trained staff (social workers/case workers) and trained community volunteers who will serve as referral entry points for survivors in their communities with on-going support to community based Protection committees, and local authority focal points to establish referral and protective networks.4. Facilitation of social dialogue and community engagement to promote peace and challenge/address harmful social and cultural norms, practices and Behaviors such as child marriage, dowry systems, cattle raiding, abduction, age-sects, youth defence groups, etc. 5. Training of 60 key community leaders and service providers on GBV, GBV services and referral including protection committee members, model men, police, social workers and religious leaders on basic GBV concepts, GBV consequences, available services and referral options using the IRC/UNICEF/IASC case management/PSS/PFA resource materials. 6. Awareness rising on GBV and available services for survivors and other most vulnerable populations in different communication means including radio talk shows and community dialogues to reach community members with key GBV messages and use different information, education and communication (IEC) materials include posters and t-shirts with different social messages to raise awareness on GBV and services and women’s rights. 7. Conducts public awareness campaigns in schools (targeting youth boys and girls between the ages of 14-25 years) on GBV prevention and response.8. Establish and strengthen Women and Girls Friendly spaces where women can have access to resources and information ensuring confidentiality including materials for focused PSS and a centre for feedback.9. Conduct services mapping and carry out protection assessments on quarterly bases 10. Provision of vocational skills training on bakery, and tailoring for GBV survivors with a provision of seed money/ start-up kit and agronomic training on kitchen gardening including ongoing market assessments and survey

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will establish a range of Beneficiary Communication and Accountability mechanisms to strengthen community engagement and feedback throughout the life of the project. This will be developed during the project’s inception phase, and will be the primary responsibility of Project Management Committee’s to implement, with support from project staff. A core component of this will be the regular dissemination of project-related information and updates to community members (beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries). Communities will be encouraged to identify multiple information dissemination channels that leverage, where relevant and appropriate, multimedia platforms including radio and SMS. Low literacy options will be included, and women specifically consulted to identify gender-responsive content and channels. The CFM will also outline strategies for soliciting regular feedback from communities (beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries). This will be done through monthly community consultation meetings facilitated by PMCs. Information will be fed back to Project Management Team who will be responsible for adapting and iterating implementation strategies and workplans accordingly. Information relating to the project’s response to feedback will be fed back to communities by the PMC via scheduled consultation meetings, ensuring ongoing two-way communication. PMCs will also publicise and manage a complaints and response mechanism which will allow communities to provide feedback between scheduled community consultations, and anonymously report grievances and complaints. PMCs will use project-specific risk-based protocols to respond to complaints.
