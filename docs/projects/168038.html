Project Summary

A large number of Zimbabwe migrants work under informal or casual arrangements in neighboring countries as Botswana, Zambia, South Africa, and Mozambique. The socio-economic impact of COVID-19 imposed restrictions has resulted in loss of jobs and unstable incomes, which leaves them unprotected and vulnerable to exploitation, poverty, and food insecurity, hampering their capacity to access and purchase food and forcing them to return to Zimbabwe as a last coping mechanism. This project will help UNICEF continue to support the Ministry of Public Service, Labour and Social Welfare (MoPSLSW) in provided tracing and reuniﬁcation services to unaccompanied minors and increasing the capacity to deploy additional residential volunteer social workers at quarantine facilities to compliment the ongoing work by the Department of Social Welfare (DSW) social workers through payment of incentives. Provision of Psychosocial Support (PSS) in quarantine centres and promotion of safety, mental and psychosocial well-being of children especially the most vulnerable including children living with disabilities, children living with HIV, children on the move including returnees in quarantine facilities. Capacity building for district response teams working in quarantine centres on PFA, PSEA & PSS in emergencies including online PSS in addition to training of frontline workers including those in quarantine centers on self-care and stress management.
