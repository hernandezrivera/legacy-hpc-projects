Project Summary

The project focuses on food security and livelihoods and aims to satisfy basic food security and needs of Internally Displaced Persons and Host Community because it will foster the economic situation in the market.
In addition to empowering women to generate income and improve livelihoods directly at the HH level. It also aims to provide critically needed livestock inputs, by purchasing and distributing; 4 female calving sheep and 10 laying hens per HH,plus fodder for the mentioned animals for six months.
This assistance will be distributed to an estimated 330 vulnerable HHs in Aleppo Governorate (6 individuals per family) vulnerable HHs (1980 individuals) (224 women, 149 men, 806 girls and 801 boys) in Rural Aleppo.
In addition, the project will:
• Supports the beneficiaries with veterinary care for animals during the project period.
• Provides a skill development training for women on veterinary health care and income generation from animal produce by marketing the production in local markets and micro home business management ,so the project will work on building these women’s capacity.
• Ensuring a supply of two basic essential food needs (eggs and milk) in addition to meat in the long-term.
• For each HH (1 HH consisting 6 members on average), the activities will secure 58% of their protein needs and 26% of the calories.
• Finally, by reducing the rapid decline in sheep numbers, the project contributes in the preservation of the remaining severely drained livestock numbers.

4 - Needs

In order to target the most vulnerable people as closely as possible, MHD designs projects based on: The multi-sectorial needs assessments; Previous experiences of the key stakeholders in the Syria response; The FSL sector updates; And the ongoing communication and work with local committees and beneficiaries. MHD is using data from the HNO, the Whole of Syria Assessment (WoSA), and data collected by MHD and Syrian NGOs. According to the current response plan of the food security and Agriculture sector that is to ensure adequate food consumption for the most affected people, reducing the number of people adopting negative coping mechanisms while supporting early and medium-term recovery of critical agricultural/livelihood assets and essential services related to food security.The assessment which done by MHD shows that the main causes for this situation is the lack of financial recourses and other inputs to breed livestock; thus, farmers were forced to sell cattle, or slaughter them prematurely. The loss of cattle, or ebbs in milk production due to disease, is also raising the risk of malnutrition in areas, particularly for children and pregnant/lactating women.Taking into consideration that a gender analysis is critical to understanding the social and gender dynamics that could help or hinder aid effectiveness. MHD worked on identify gender gaps, such as unequal access to agriculture and livelihoods services for women/girls and men/boys that need to be addressed. During consultations in the targeted locations, MHD found that there is a difference in gender roles in the targeted areas. Men generally focus on work out of the home and women generally work at home and in the home garden. Women work in producing and preserving the dairy products whereas men work in caring and breeding the livestock. This project has a specific focus on women and attempting to enhance their independence and income generating activities through traditional methods.

5 - Implementation of activities

1- Advertisements will be placed in communities, outlining the selection criteria.2- Final beneficiary list will be shared with the local council and relief committee. 3- Baseline Assessment will be conducted to assess food consumption rate of HH and the current situation of farming animals. (As one of the first steps in setting up a performance monitoring process , It will be based on primary data collected directly from the probable beneficiaries). 4- MHD procurement team will go in procurement process. 5- Inputs will be delivered house-to-house (4 female calving sheep and 10 laying hens per HH,plus fodder for the mentioned animals for six months) . 6- The technical team will conduct regular visits to beneficiaries to provide veterinary services for the project animals. 7-Training will be conducted for women of the targeted households. After 2nd round of training targets 90 head of targeted HHs on how to market the production in local market and micro home business management. 8- Ongoing monitoring and documented processes will be implemented to ensure that all beneficiaries received assistance and using the inputs for project purposes. 9- MHD will hire a third party company to monitor and evaluate the activities of the project. 10- The monthly report will be produced on the end of each month, and the final report will be produced on the last month of the project.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

There will be dangerous of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment) of women and girls. It is high Likelihood and high impact in the community. For mitigating measure, we we will establish a confidential complaint mechanism, train staff on code of conduct and add it in all staff contracts and address any PSEA complaints.Our monitoring staff and staff to analyze data will post-monitoring/ client feedback forms are used, include PSEA topic in regular supervision sessions. Also, there are risks of gender-based violence GBV because of women being treated as beneficiaries and they are primary stakeholders. He may be vulnerable to sexual harassment and sexual assault. If situational and risk analyzes are not systematically performed, these risks may not be identified and corrected.Here it is important that we take measures to mitigate these risks: the MHD team will include female staff on the project and will call for adequate assistance. We will include women, girls and other at-risk groups in the steps of project. Also must be consultations with women, girls, boys and men should be organized and community safety audits audited.There are a dangerous of exploitation to child, so must have protected them from not leaving school for working in the grazing or helping to raise animals. For mitigating of risk security and safety on Beneficiaries and MHD staff, we will: Organizing the distribution at the door of home to avoid overcrowding , taking into consideration people with specific needs and women . Pre-consultations with communities and application of checklist at distribution sites. In all the steps of project we will establish a confidential complaint mechanism, train staff on code of conduct.Our monitoring staff and staff to analyze data will post-monitoring/ client feedback forms are used.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

-There will be a risk of problems among the beneficiaries. Whereas the provision of the service is to communities containing displaced persons and a host community, which will expose the project to a problem of discrimination if a service is provided to specific groups or individuals except for others. Therefore, we mitigate the risks by participatory assessments will be employed by MHD to identify, verify and profile targeted beneficiaries. The selections criteria will prioritize vulnerable people, including IDPs, host communities, and female headed HHs MHD anticipates that the prioritization of female headed HHs, families with disabled or elderly family members, families with more than six persons living in one HH will play a key in restoring hope and dignity, which is essential in empowering these vulnerable groups to have an ‘opinion’ and demand for their rights-The project may be exposed to the risk of animal death or disease during the project period. It is a high risk and impact, and to reduce its vulnerability, coordination will be made with organizations working in the animal care field to follow up on vaccines and treatments.-The inability to secure animal feed during the project period is one of the potential dangers that negatively affects milk and egg shortages, which is reflected in the nutrition, especially for children and lactating women. The organization will find alternative markets to buy feed either from Syria or Turkey-It is possible that the times of assistance or training may not match the times of the beneficiaries, so coordination will be done in advance with the beneficiaries in a manner that preserves their security and stabilityWe will involve the beneficiaries and local committees in distribution and post distribution monitoring, and we will also perform periodic feedback sessions with community project beneficiaries to understand their concerns and needs to design tailor made interventions in collaboration with the UN
