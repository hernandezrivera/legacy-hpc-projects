Project Summary

Extensive damage to Syria's water networks and sewerage infrastructure during the conflict has resulted in restricted access to safe water supplies. This has affected domestic and personal hygiene and increased the burden on women (who are usually responsible for the family's household water use and hygiene).
The lack of safe water increases the risk of water-borne diseases and is a potential public health hazard. Providing adequate environmental health services in Palestine refugee camps to mitigate the risks of disease has become even more critical, as evidenced during the recent cholera outbreak, reportedly affecting all 14 governorates in Syria. Between 25 August and 8 October 2022, 15,823 suspected cases of cholera were reported, including amongst Palestine refugees, resulting in 68 deaths.
In 2023, SFO will undertake a self-assessment of all its operations to identify actions that could be made more environmentally sustainable. Recommendations will be used as a blueprint for implementing various actions to improve environmental sustainability, including under the EA. The Agency-wide Environmental and Social Management Framework will also be applied/rolled out at the field level with technical support from UNRWA Headquarters (HQ).
In 2023, UNRWA will continue to provide essential WASH services to the most vulnerable among Palestine refugees including WASH items (Hygiene kits, baby diapers) which will be identified and referred for assistance by the UNRWA Health Programme. Hygiene kits will be distributed to at least 5,190 refugees and will include baby kits/diapers (for 500 infants) and adult incontinence pads (for 3,000 older persons and persons with disabilities).
Solid waste management and sanitation services will continue to be provided in Palestine refugee camps by UNRWA sanitation workers. The Agency will also conduct minor repairs and maintenance of water and sewerage networks and surface drainage systems in accessible refugee camps. Upgraded equipment, such as garbage compactors and water tankers, will be procured to improve the quality and efficiency of service provision in nine refugee camps.
As part of its measures to prevent additional health hazards, UNRWA will recruit 27 additional sanitation workers and ensure that all sanitation workers continue to be equipped with personal protective equipment (PPE). UNRWA will continue disinfection campaigns and the sanitization of garbage collection points to support cleaner environments in the refugee camps, helping to prevent the spread of disease.
UNWRA's Emergency Appeal 2023 estimates to cover a total of 149, 769 vulnerable Palestinian refugees (including 71,889 Male and 77,880 female) with an estimate 2,102,583 USD budget requirement for the planned WASH activities, out of which 100% of the funding needs to be secured.

3 - Needs

The extensive damage to Syria's water networks and sewerage infrastructure during the crisis has resulted in restricted access to safe water supplies. This is affecting domestic and personal hygiene and increases the burden on women (who are usually responsible for the family's household water use and hygiene). Lack of safe water increases the risk of water-borne diseases and is a potential public health hazard. Providing adequate environmental health services in Palestine refugee camps to mitigate the risks of diseases spreading has become even more critical, as evidenced during the recent cholera outbreak in 2022, reportedly affecting all 14 governorates in Syria (Syria Cholera situation report issued on 14 October). It is critical for UNRWA to strengthen its capacity to provide solid waste management and sanitation services in Palestine refugee camps to prevent further escalation of the cholera outbreak. According to UNRWA’s recently completed post distribution monitoring survey conducted in August 2022, 48% of Palestine refugees reported being able to meet at least half of their basic needs, including NFI needs, while about 52% reported being unable to meet basic needs. Providing critical hygiene kits to vulnerable households will remain critical to maintain health and hygiene for targeted groups. UNRWA has not conducted a specific need assessment for WASH, the need and gaps are informed through 2 comprehensive post distribution monitoring surveys conducted in 2022.

4 - Implementation of activities

In 2023, UNRWA will continue providing essential WASH services to the most vulnerable among Palestine refugees including WASH items (Hygiene kits, baby diapers) which will be identified and referred for assistance by the UNRWA Health Programme. These WASH items will be distributed to at least 5,190 Palestine refugees and will include baby kits/diapers (for 500 infants) and adult incontinence pads (for 3,000 older persons and persons with disabilities). Solid waste management and sanitation services will continue to be provided in Palestine refugee camps by UNRWA sanitation workers. The Agency will also conduct minor repairs and maintenance of water and sewerage networks and surface drainage systems in accessible refugee camps. Upgraded equipment, such as garbage compactors and water tankers, will be procured to improve the quality and efficiency of service provision in nine refugee camps. As part of its measures to prevent additional health hazards, UNRWA will recruit 27 additional sanitation workers and ensure that all sanitation workers continue to be equipped with personal protective equipment (PPE). Moreover, UNRWA will continue disinfection campaigns and the sanitization of garbage collection points to support cleaner environments in the refugee camps to help prevent the spread of diseases.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

RISK 1: Inequality in distribution; Physical and psychological harm arising from violence and coercion; Timing and process of distribution put women and girls at higher risk of GBV. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High. Mitigation measures: UNRWA promote Establishment of adequate management plan; Promote involvement /engagement with communities in distribution planning and feedback from different groups on package for improvement; training of staff and enforcement of code of PSEA code of conduct among partner staff, safe and secure distribution points are selected and in appropriate timings.RISK 2: Potential exposure to explosive hazard (IED/UXO/ERW) during assessment/ rehabilitation of wastewater/ water infrastructure, Likelihood: Medium, Risk: High, Mitigation measures: UNRWA works closely with Mine Action actors in the area and conduct assessments/rehabilitation works only in areas declared as cleared; Conduct safety training with stuff and contractors; Add explosive hazard awareness material to UNRWA programming. RISK 3: Health risk associated with garbage removal. Likelihood: high, risk: low, mitigation measures: URWA ensures staff involved in SWM activities wear appropriate PPE (Personal Protective Equipment) and follow established safety regulations; Conduct/repeat safety training for staff


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

To mitigate SEA risk and uphold dignity of beneficiary during all project activities, UNRWA follows the sector guidelines and ensures: 1. UNRWA staff prioritize vulnerable groups such as women, older persons and persons with disabilities in order to reduce their waiting time during distribution of WASH Items. 2. provide training on protection mainstreaming and disability inclusion. 3. protection audits on a biennial basis are conducted to receive feedback from the staff and the community on how to improve its services and how to optimize its compliance to the main protection principles. 4. All UNRWA staff sign the code of conduct on commencement of their contracts. 5. Training to staff on the code of conduct and PSEA and regular reinforcement of the zero-tolerance policy as well as UNRWA staff standards of conduct, reporting obligations and channels for SEA are followed. 6. messaging to community members on the importance of reporting SEA are reinforced and ensure mechanisms are in place to safeguard confidentiality and set up accessible and efficient complaint mechanisms. 7. focus on community engagement and participation, and ensure feedbacks are responded to in a timely manner, 8. Female and Male staff presence in the field while interacting directly with male and female beneficiaries, 9. contractors to follow and sign UNRWA Code of Conduct. 10: close coordination with protection sector during project activity.
