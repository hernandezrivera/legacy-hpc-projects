Project Summary

CARE is proposing a comprehensive food security and livelihoods intervention responding to the immediate and acute food needs and sustaining access through improving livelihoods opportunities, all will be delivered in line with the cluster priority of decreasing morbidity and mortality relegated to food shortage. Main activities include Cash programing for food assistance and livestock’s; Cash vouchers for seeds/agricultural inputs, initial capital transfer in the form of in-kind assistance to selected women and men; rehabilitation of market infrastructures strengthening; promoting community base management of natural resources and public services such as health facilities, education, and community centers; PSEA and GBV awareness sessions and dignity kits distribution. The primary focus will be the affected women, men, girls, and boys, as well as disabled people in South Darfur, East Darfur, South Kordofan, Gedaref and Kassala states. The total target beneficiaries out of this project are 200,000 individuals ( 39199 men, 40800 women, 61201 girls, 58800 boys).

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation related to food security in Sudan kept deterioration in the year 2022 due to various factors including inflation and depreciation of economy, inter-communal clashes, flooding which led to destruction of the agriculture and expected to decrease the level of harvest. Referenced to the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) scale, in 2022, most parts of Sudan will face (IPC Phase 2) acute food insecurity due to low purchasing power. Further, in areas with complex emergencies and having huge number of displaced populations will face (IPC Phase 3) such as Darfur and Kordofan, parts of Jebel Marra, South Kordofan, and areas of marginal agricultural production in the Red Sea, Kassala, North Kordofan, and North Darfur states. Referenced to OCHA update, in 2022, the Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) sector estimated 15.7M in need. The 2022 agricultural season is expected to be below the average production. The USDA Foreign Agricultural Service estimated total cereal production at around 7.23 million metric tons, beside a forecast of 725,000 metric tons of wheat to be harvested in March, this estimated coverage will be approximately 19 percent below last year's harvest and that will widen access gap to food security. The Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022 (HNO) for FSL Sudan estimated that 9.8 million people in need for food and livelihoods assistance throughout the year. Overall, the combined impacts of economic crisis, conflicts, and poor harvests are significantly affecting people’s access to food and will likely double the number of people facing acute hunger in Sudan to more than 18 million people by September 2022 (a joint statement by FAO and WFP, 23 March 2022). Since community financial and technical capacity is a major concern in the operational areas contributing significantly at the current food insecurity situation, CARE designed this project to be delivered in a participatory manner establishing, training and engaging community committees to assist in mapping and delivering relevant livelihoods and economic inclusion activities to increase access to livelihood opportunities and food security, this is beside coordination and collaboration with various humanitarian set-up forums such as FSL cluster in order to engage any new emerging issues such as geographical focus, priority activities and target beneficiaries.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

An environmentally friendly and risk avert mechanism will be adopted across all food security and diversified livelihoods activities to ensure no environmental negative impacts because of this intervention. Project staff will be trained on the importance of collection disposing empty food plastic bags, safe disposal of remanent related to IGAs and market-based activities. Awareness sessions on environmental issues as well will be delivered among communities in the target operational markets.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Gender and protection are main pillars and primary focus of CARE. Community protection and GBV are regularly factored in the center of each project. Needs of women, girls, boys, and men are systematically assessed, concerns, capacity, and opportunities of equal access are usually identified and mitigated in each single project. CARE will take extra caution in analyzing the protection of at risk groups, includingchildren and girls. Efforts will include: no creation of new risks, address discrimination, analysis of the most vulnerable, safety and dignity in service delivery, orientation and familiarization on humanitarian do no harm principles and rights, and promoting empowerment. Furthermore, CARE and partners will exercise principles of protection mainstreaming: meaningful access through consultation and involvement of the communities about the best way to deliver the services and considerations for vulnerable groups, accountability mechanisms and Field visits. CARE and partners will empower communities by training & capacity building & awareness raising about key protection concerns and risks, consulting them in decision making process, identifying volunteers and establishing and training community committees. Strengthening community protection mechanisms and improving access to services through information dissemination on available services, good selection criteria and identification of the most vulnerable and referrals will be prioritized. Do no harm analysis will be carried for each sector and the activities will be screened not to enhance risks for all especially women, girls and children. staff and volunteers will ensure screening of all children boys and girls during the HH visit and nutrition and health facilities; also Facilities such as latrines and water points, income generating activities and trainings will be analyzed and discussed with at risk groups to ensure that the beneficiaries are protected and that the services are easily accessible and safely usable by all. Protection risks faced by women and girls, and in particular S/GBV risks, will be the specific focus of capacity building for staff and partners to ensure that they are able to detect cases of abuse. Staff will also be oriented in order to make sure that programming does no harm and that they are able to respond safely and appropriatelyto protection cases when they are reported, using a confidential and survivor centered approach to disclosures of violence. An internal mechanism for reporting complaints and ensuring that required measure are in place will be promoted throughout the implementation period.

7.1.1 - Please specify other means to collect/share feedback or information if there is any

A comprehensive and multi-entry Feedback and Accountability Mechanism (FAM) portfolio, including phone lines, complaint boxes, and in person reporting mechanisms is in place across all project sites for participants, partners and staff to report any case

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

CARE has an active sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) mechanism and policy in place for prevention, investigating and reporting. The compliance sector in collaboration human resource sector mandated to handle complains, investigation and safely reporting to the Country Director for further actions. Staff member in direct contact with communities in the target nutrition sectors will be trained and mobilized around issues related to sexual exploitation and abuse, messaging through leaflets and banners using local languages will be made available in nutrition centers for ensuring communities are aware of their rights to protection from sexual exploitation and abuse. Complain mechanism boxes will be available and accessible by the target communities in the nutrition facilities. Protection, including protection from sexual harassment, exploitation, and abuse (PSHEA), will be mainstreamed in the response. Key implementing staff from CARE, national NGOs, and seconded government staff will receive protection and PSHEA training and develop action plans to mainstream throughout the response period.
