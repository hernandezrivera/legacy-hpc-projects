Project Summary

The three Counties of Boma, Kapoeta East and Kapoeta South have experienced decades of conflict, both are always involving in the internal conflict between one another and other neighboring communities, this due to inter-communal conflict over cattle rivalry and water points. Protracted conflict, coupled with endemic natural shocks, have triggered a humanitarian crisis that has severely hindered the delivery of WASH, FSL and Nutrition services. Food insecurity, poor water quality, limited diversity and quality of food have led to high levels of acute malnutrition. In Boma County in particular, displacement has driven food insecurity, and due to intercommunal clashes, most people lost livelihoods. Trade and food assistance value chains have been interrupted and completely cut off for greater Pibor Administrative area. All these target counties were heavily impacted negatively by intercommunal conflict and the climate change that resulted to prolonged rain in every year.
Access to humanitarian assistance, exacerbating health and nutrition deficits, high morbidity, extremely poor diets, and poor sanitation and hygiene. The post-harvest acute food insecurity trends place Greater Pibor and Eastern Equatoria at the lowest in comparison to other States which is below 50% gap (priority 1, and 2 which is very acute and severe.
Lack of clean water for drinking and for domestic use greatly affects children under five years who are exposed to water borne diseases and helminthiasis a contributing factor to high levels on malnutrition and compromised immunity. Women have to walk long distances in search of water for drinking and this also exposes them to gender based violence and other protection threats.
Rehabilitation of water points, hygiene promotion, and rehabilitation of sanitation facilities in Nutrition centers will ensure that the vulnerable populations have access to clean safe drinking water and for domestic use, reducing open defecation through hygiene promotion will minimize chances of diarrhea and water sources contamination. Maximizing GBV and protection awareness in the community will as well reduce the cases of gender-based violence among women and girls who are exposed as they seek water in long distances.
Community Emergency Response and Development has physical presence in Great Kapoeta and Boma Counties for the last 2 years before its official registration by RRC. CERD has been implementing community-based WASH voluntarily activities in collaboration with the County Rural water departments and other sectors in the target locations.
CERD has vast experience working with communities and has been supporting hygiene promotion, GBV, Protection and Establishment of farmers groups in push towards eradication of open defecation, mitigation of GBV cases, protection threats against women and increase food production to prevent hunger in the communities.
The project target 11,111 (women 3,700, Men 2,112, Boys 2,000, Girls 2,499 and Elderly age 800) Beneficiaries with water supply, Hygiene promotion, GBV awareness, and Nutrition, food security and livelihood services and Sanitation facilities in Kapoeta East, Boma and Kapoeta South Counties.

1. Needs

The 2023 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for South Sudan sets out the “intersectional severity of needs,” which recognizes the interconnected nature of the impacts of deficits in individual sectors. The report notes a rise in Global Acute malnutrition (GAM) rates attributable to interrelated causes within the Nutrition, WASH and FSL sectors. These counties have weak nutrition facilities, providing substandard services, while frequent stock outs and extensive delays in restocking make treatment difficult. Inadequate access to clean water, improved sanitation and acceptable hygiene practices within the Community and in Nutrition facilities in Boma, Kapoeta East and Kapoeta South Counties contributes greatly to the high levels of malnutrition, disease burdens, food insecurity as well as WASH related protection/GBV issues for the most vulnerable, adolescent girls, people with disabilities and the elderly persons. Open defecation is widely practiced, leading to cycles of water contamination, resulting in acute watery diarrhea, eye infections, and skin diseases. Hand washing is equally low, with socio-cultural practices unfavorable to achieving acceptable WASH standards. The three Counties are hard to reach areas interim of Humanitarian services delivery. This is due to difficult access of some parts in GPAA and Kapoeta East by NGOS based in these two counties in particular. However, due to lack of GBV and Health Education awareness, Girls and women are exposed to risk of rape, force marriages, early marriages and domestic violence. Girls are denied going to schools because they are regarded by their parents as the source of wealth to their families. The culture has impacted girls negatively as most of the harmful practices are attached to culture of which it denies girls and women access to education and raising up of issues that are affecting them to partners implementing GBV and protection programs for the support. The main Community problems that have been assessed needs immediate intervention are - Repair/ rehabilitation of identified broken Hand pumps both at the community and Nutrition facilities _ Hygiene Promotion and GBV awareness recognized a common problem. - Construction of institutional latrines targeting schools and Nutrition Facilities - Training of Hand Pump Mechanics - Forming and Training of Water Management Committee - Training of Hygiene Promoters - Upgrading of 2 existing hand pumps to motorized solar powered system in the concentrated areas. - Distribution of Water treatment products to 1,800 households - Provision of 350 Hygiene kits to caretakers and Mothers of SAM patients at Nutrition Facilities – Distribution of MHM kits to 1000 adolescent girls and women with Productive Age -Training of Vulnerable households on Agronomic farming practices - Distribution of Seeds and farming tools to vulnerable Households - Distribution of Cash to the most vulnerable households.

2. Project activities

The Project plan to address the following areas of needs. - Rehabilitation/repair of 42 broken hand Pumps in the community and at Nutrition facilities. 42 boreholes will be repaired/rehabilitated including changing of fast-moving parts, greasing, and replacement of worn-out parts where necessary, Platform set up, fencing, disinfection and visibility. Spare parts can either be procured or be requested from the core pipeline and delivered to the sites. - Hygiene Promotion: Hygiene promotion will enable people to take action to prevent water, sanitation and hygiene-related diseases by mobilizing and engaging the population, their knowledge, and resources. - Distribution of water treatment products Aqua tabs and Pure to 1,800 Households - Construction of 15 climate resilient institutional Latrines: CERD will construct gender sensitive climate resilience latrines, the latrines will be built on elevated land mounds above flood level height record reduces inundation overflow risks. Each block will have four stances segregated to Males, females and with two stances well design and label for people with disability. - Installation of Handwashing Stations next to the latrine's sites to Nutrition facilities- Training of 21 Hand Pump Mechanics: CERD will train the target Hand pump mechanics mainly to support the community technically by repairing broken hand pumps in their respective boreholes' locations. - Training of 220 water Management Committee: CERD will train water management committee members, selected among the water users with 50% women representation for 4 days. This will empower the WMCs to take charge of their hand pumps by running independently and to as well give awareness to the water users on the proper operation and maintenance of boreholes and water user contribution fee collection. This is a part of the sustainability and ownership of plan, setting up by laws and well define of their roles and responsibilities to them will gradually make them understand and take proper management of their water points at their disposal. - Training of 30 Hygiene promoters: CERD will recruit community Hygiene promoters through the support of local leaders. The selection of these hygiene promoters will be based on literacy and numerical skills for them to gather basic households' data related to the project. CERD WASH technical team will conduct four days training building on the key messages of Hygiene, Sanitation and environmental awareness, Campaigns dissemination of integrated hygiene promotion and GBV/protection messages. - CERD will Train 9 schools Hygiene clubs that will support to deliver hygiene and sanitation messages in each target school. This will improve hygiene practices and proper handwashing and the use of sanitations facilities in schools. - Provision of menstrual Hygiene management kits to 1,000 Adolescent girls, Women with reproductive age and GBV victims. - Upgrading of 2 existing hand pumps to motorize (solar powered water system). 6 pump operators will be engaged to guide and to operate. The planned water yards will increase supply of clean drinking water to the target situation affected population. - Distribution of Water treatment products to 1,800 households - Provision of 350 Hygiene kits to caretakers and Mothers of SAM patients at Nutrition Facilities. - Training of Vulnerable households on Agronomic farming practices - Distribution of Seeds and farming tools to vulnerable Households - Distribution of Cash to the most vulnerable households
