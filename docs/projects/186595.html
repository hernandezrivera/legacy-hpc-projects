Project Summary

The decade-long protracted violence insurgency in the three (3) BAY states has resulted in mass displacement and loss of livelihood and increase in food insecurity, these have equally resulted in increased malnutrition among children of under 5, including nursing and lactating mothers.
The increased malnutrition is very alarming in most parts of the BAY state, it has remained high compared to the 15% World Health Organization thresholds.
The Prevalence of chronic malnutrition and underweight in children 6 to 59 months of age is still indicating high in the Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe states (NFSS IX- Oct 2021).
Also, according to the Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance Round 9 (NFSS IX), conducted in October 2020, the level of acute malnutrition increased in Adamawa and Yobe compared to 2019. The current global acute malnutrition (GAM) rates are 10.7% in Borno (slightly lower than the 2019 mark of 11.3%) and 13.6% in Yobe.
In contributing to the overall objective of the nutrition response in 2021, which is centered around reducing the morbidity and mortality associated with acute malnutrition and prevent the overall acute malnutrition among the most vulnerable members of the targeted community including children under five, vulnerable adolescent girls, pregnant and lactating women. GREENCODE is proposing- Improving Community-based Management of Acute Malnutrition (I-CMAM) project for under 5 Children in Borno state (Kaga, Mafa, Konduga and MMC LGAs)
Activities planned for are;
- Conducting mass and house to house MUAC screening to identify and refer acutely malnourished children, pregnant and lactating women.
- Promotion of “Family/Mother MUAC” approach – Training and supporting mother (and other households/community members) to self-screen their own children and refer appropriately
- Facilitating referral of SAM/MAM cases from very poor households to treatment centres with local transport.
- Conduct screening and referral of infants less 6 months with breastfeeding difficulties and support in identification of foster mother/wet nurses for orphaned children and safe distribution of breastmilk substitutes.
- Provision of prevention and treatment of MAM cases using alternative methods/strategies including use of Tom Brown approach and cooking demonstration by organizing Training of Trainers (TOT) on Tom Brown Approach for other partners in Borno.
- Provision of both Mobile and stationed Outpatient Therapeutic Programme (OTP) services integrated into Health and Educational facilities in MMC and Konduga LGAs Borno state.
- Promotion of appropriate Infant and Young Child feeding and care practices (IYCF) through the formation of Mother-to-Mother Support Groups, Father-to-Father Support Groups; house-to-house follow-up visits, radio talk shows, distribution of IEC materials, local drama groups, and mass awareness campaigns (roadshows, walks, etc.); targeting influencers e.g. teachers, religious leaders.
- Provision of micronutrient supplementation to children and adolescent girls with supplies from UNICEF.
- Conduct growth monitoring promotion (GMP) and screen children under five attending school/ECD centres for acute malnutrition and referral for treatment.
- Promote the use of school-going children as agents of change in the community by training and provision of MUAC tapes for them to screen for acute malnutrition in their households/communities.
- Mainstreaming Protection related issues into the project activities.
- Deploying the Community Management of At-risk Mothers and Infants under six months of age (C-MAMI) in both our TOT and field activities.

1 - Needs

The past and current mass displacements, loss of livelihood, and increase in food insecurity caused by the violent conflict in Borno and Yobe states have pushed a lot of under 5 children, vulnerable adolescent girls, pregnant and lactating mothers into acute malnutrition.This trend is on the high prevalence the last round of Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance Round 9 (NFSS IX), conducted in October 2020 shows that the level of acute malnutrition increased in Adamawa and Yobe compared to 2019. The current global acute malnutrition (GAM) rates are 10.7% in Borno (slightly lower than the 2019 mark of 11.3%), 7.5% in Adamawa, and 13.6% in Yobe. The overall acute malnutrition rate among women/adolescent girls (15-19 years) was 30% and 21.2% suffering acute malnutrition associated with a high risk of low birth weight. The nutrition situation in Borno and Yobe State is classified as Phase 3 (‘Serious’) and Adamawa at Phase 2 (‘Alert’) level, with GAM levels of 13.6% in Yobe, 10.7% in Borno, and 7.5% in Adamawa state. The hostile security situation characterized by indiscriminate attacks on both government forces, civilians including humanitarian actors continues to hamper efforts by humanitarians to improve on nutrition parameters and indicators in the affected populations. The conflict triggered displacements have resulted in disrupted healthcare, social life, livelihoods, and traditional food security safety nets, which together with overcrowding and congestion in the IDP camps exacerbate the risks associated with acute malnutrition, morbidity, and mortalities in the affected populations, more-so among U5 and PLW due to sub-optimal access to health care services as well as compromised child feeding and care environment. Consequently, GAM prevalence rates have persistently remained high, above recommended SPHERE standards.Similarly, the December 2020 UNICEF -The FIT Sentinel Surveillance Alert Report for Nutrition and Health indicates a high rate of GAM in some LGAs of Borno including those proposed for intervention by GREENCODE under this NHF call.In addressing the nutrition challenges in Borno and Yobe state while contributing to reducing the morbidity and mortality associated with acute malnutrition and prevent the overall acute malnutrition among the most vulnerable members of the targeted community including children under five, vulnerable adolescent girls, pregnant and lactating women, which is the Nutrition sector overall objective in the 2021 responses, GREENCODE will undertake series of community-based activities including;Strengthen the screening, identification, and referral of acute malnutrition among children under five, adolescents, pregnant and lactating women, Strengthen the screening, identification, and referral of acute malnutrition of among children under five, adolescents, pregnant and lactating women, Provision of micronutrient supplementation to children and adolescent girls. Promotion of appropriate infant and young children feeding and care practice, Treatment of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) among children less than 5 years, and Treatment and prevention of moderately acute malnutrition (MAM) among children less than 5 years, adolescents, pregnant and lactating women.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

GREENCODE’s mandate of our humanitarian intervention is rooted in the 'Do NO HARM’ principle, also GREENCODE approach to protection is governed by the SPHERE Standards and Protection Principles, the Core Humanitarian Standards.Protection concerns were considered in the planning of all nutrition activities, in order to ensure that any risk of harm, abuse, and exploitation faced by the target population is mitigated.GREENCODE has the following policies in place that guide and protect both staff and beneficiaries (Gender/Inclusion, PSEAH, Child Protection, and Safeguarding).The proposed interventions will be carried out in a non-discriminatory way protecting the safety, dignity, and integrity of the beneficiaries who receive the services and their caregivers. The protection approach to nutrition services is consistent with humanitarian principles and human rights-¬based programming and encompasses a variety of internationally recognized human rights and not just the right to health services. GREENCODE will ensure easy access to the nutrition sites by women e.g mobile to hard-to-reach areas (less than 1hr walking), minimal waiting time; adequate shaded areas will be provided at the waiting areas.In nutrition and health facilities a clear mark will be place at strategic locations or on the facilities to separate toilets for men and women.Similarly, the project will focus on the most vulnerable including orphaned children, women-headed households whose children are more likely to be malnourished. GREENCODE will undertake 2 days GBV mainstreaming and awareness training for both CNMs and project staff. Also, GREENCODE project staff will be conducting GBV awareness by mainstreaming GBV talk and discussion in a support group meetings and cooking demonstrations as well as OTP facilities activities as well as Tom Brown group sessions. GBV referral pathways existing in project communities set up by other GBV actors will be listed and shared among project beneficiaries.GREENCODE with the going support from UN-Women will address some of the reported GBV cases in Konduga LGA where GREENCODE is working on the nutrition project, with the support of UNWomen, GREENCODE Protection Officer will be conducting regular counseling for project beneficiaries that are survivors of GBV.Gender equality will be fully integrated throughout the project, starting from targeted inclusive implementation processes to staff recruitment and the participation of both men and women in project structures and evaluation processes. Women and men will be given equal chances during project staff recruitment, although women will be highly encouraged to apply for positions. In addition, because of their important role in successful community mobilization, the project will deploy as many female field workers (CNMs and supervisors) as possible. While all program staff is accountable for project gender equality and safe programming goals, a Gender and psychosocial support Officer will work closely with staff members to ensure gender and child protection standards integration into interventions. Training/orienting all project staff, partners, and stakeholders on the gender-related barriers, child protection, and building their accountability on safeguarding standards to address issues and concerns on the risk of harm will also take place.The activities proposed are specifically designed to better meet the needs of children of different age and gender groups. Project includes estimated sex and age disaggregated data for the target groups based on the findings of the needs assessment and feedback mechanism.
