Project Summary

South Sudan's years-long conflicts and natural disasters left 8.5 million people in critical need of humanitarian assistance in 2024. Despite the formation of the government of national unity and unstoppable efforts put together by humanitarian agencies, the situation is expected to worsen due to fresh conflict, natural calamity, epidemics, and climate instability. Food insecurity and lack of basic services. ISNA 2023, highlighted that there will be inadequate access to lifesaving shelter materials and basic nonfood items in 2024. More than 2.1 Million people will face a lack of appropriate shelters and nonfood items in the whole country.
Since the year 2019 and beyond, Hope Restoration South Sudan (HRSS) has been launching emergency responses in hard-to-reach areas, through the provision of lifesaving shelter materials and nonfood items to the newly displaced persons, returnees, and residents, in Leer, Mayendit, Mayom, Guit and, Fangak counties. From 2017 to date, Hope Restoration South Sudan (HRSS) has been playing a critical role in carrying out IRNA, household surveys, and multi-sectoral need assessments and KAP in collaboration with static partners and in coordination with OCHA in locations affected by conflict and disasters respectively. The findings arising from these need assessments henceforth informed immediate services deliveries to the most vulnerable households from newly displaced persons, returnees, and residents. In 2024, Hope Restoration South Sudan(HRSS) will aim at providing timely, life-saving shelter materials and nonfood items for returnees, and conflict and disaster-affected persons in Leer, Fangak, Guit, Mayendit, and Mayom counties.
The total beneficiaries in all locations targeted are 12,291 individual and broken down as follows: Leer=3212, Fangak=2281, Mayendit=2260 ,Guit=2278 and Mayom=2260 individuals

1. Needs

Displacement and influx of communities orchestrated by conflicts and disaster have tangled humanitarian situations in unity and Jonglei states. Crises associated with natural disasters or flooding and subnational conflict caused movements coupled with returnees influx out of Sudan creating extreme vulnerability. Heavy rainfalls supported by flooding from the river Nile and its tributaries, in some places, increased water volume, eventually submerged shelters, and the subsequent losses of households’ items, during hard walks to the highest ground and nearest islands. Such displacements have left communities vulnerable, and the coping mechanism remains flagged. IRNA led by SAADO and other humanitarian partners in collaboration with UNOCHA, in Fangak county this year 17 to 19 October 2023, established that almost 75% of the total population was displaced by subnational conflict and disaster and eventually became homeless and had limited access to lifesaving shelters materials and non-food items. Gender-based violence (GBV) and protection concerns were reported to have posed immediate threats to the safety and dignity of women and girls. Returnees coming home from Sudan are facing minor land challenges worsened extremely by the presence of secondary occupants in the lands. The judiciary system to address GBV and land issues, in most counties, has limitations due to the huge presence of small arms in the hands of bandits or unauthorized elements. ISNA 2023, evidenced that 2.1 million people will need lifesaving shelter materials and nonfood items in 2024. IRNA carried out by Hope Restoration South (HRSS) and other partners, in Mayendit, between August and September 2023, established that 7040 individuals had arrived from Sudan 7127 HHS displaced by drought and aftermath flood among others had their shelters collapsed and nonfood items lost, and needed lifesaving shelter materials and nonfood items. Areas affected by disaster and the burden of refugee returnees and disaster included Dablual, Mirnyal, Thaker, and Rubkuac Payams. While in Leer, IRNA led by NRC underscored 7542 newly arrived from Khartoum and reported to have been lacking shelter materials and basic services. Additionally, wars and the influx of returnees intensified already volatile needs extremely compromising the lives of children, the elderly, women, boys girls, and men to higher risks, including some protection concerns. Diseases such as typhoid, malaria, and cholera are conceivable and exceedingly threatening. In this contextual analysis, the humanitarian disaster befalling communities in Jonglei and Unity states is of great humanitarian concern and requires intervention to address the dire needs of conflict and disaster-affected persons through timely provision of emergency shelters materials and non-food items. Items such as; plastic sheets, blankets, mosquito nets, and bundles of rubber ropes are critical in order to appropriately address needs. HRSS will form accountability to the affected population committee (AAP) committee members in an effort to increase community participation during the project implementation. The members will be drawn from population groups to form the AAP committee. HRSS will establish a complaint feedback desk in and around distribution points to track beneficiaries’ feedback about the satisfaction and dissatisfaction felt about modalities employed during the process. The feedback will inform the HRSS team to adjust modalities or adjust the approaches and realign them in favor of the beneficiary’s complaints. Hope Restoration South Sudan (HRSS) will address the Lack of independence instruments to address the stalemates arising illegal occupation of lands and return them to rightful owners. The project will coordinate with relevant authorities and other actors to support the due process of law and ensure that land crises in the project locations are addressed adequately.

2. Project activities

Activity 1.1: Community consultation meetings: (HRSS) will lead community consultations with representatives of population groups in its entirety. These will include representatives of local authorities, affected households, opinion leaders, cultural chiefs, chiefs of religious leaders, representatives of persons living with disabilities, persons living with AIDS, Women leaders, youth leaders, and others in the locations targeted to serve as focal and entry points to the project beneficiaries which later allows effective implementation of the project. Activity 1.2: Establishment and training of the Accountability to the affected population committee members (APP) Committee: HRSS will form 8 AAP committees 10 members in each location and train 60 members of AAP in all project locations. Members of (the AAP) will be drawn from community representatives taking into consideration gender equality in all selection processes. Activity 1.3: Conduct S/NFI Needs Analysis: HRSS will conduct a 7-day S/NFI needs analysis with the full involvement of other static partners in order to establish genuine S/NFI needs and identify project beneficiaries. Shelters and NFI need analysis is a mandatory activity required to justify context complications and which informs the pipeline request. During need analysis, (HRSS) will conduct FGDS and KIIS with stakeholders drawn in all Payams. This exercise will help the project team verify the prevailing needs of the beneficiaries prior to the pipeline request henceforth. Need analysis will also help the project team to under the community dynamics. (HRSS) will recruit 16 enumerators with 2 selected from each location with the assistance of the APP committee and the project team to complete this activity in 7 days Activity 1.4: Establishment or strengthening of existing complaint feedback Desk: The CRM desk will track beneficiaries' feedback about the level of satisfaction served and modality employed. Activity 1.5: Identification, registration, and verification in all locations: Hope Restoration South Sudan (HRSS) in collaboration with the AAP committee, RRC, and local authorities, will conduct identification, registration, and verification of the most vulnerable IDPs, returnees, and residents through effective coordination with community representatives and other stakeholders involved in the process. Activity 1.6: Transportation and storage of the supplies to the project locations: HRSS will hire trucks or consider hiring flights through the logistics cluster to complete the transportation of the approved supplies to the project locations for timely distribution to the persons affected. Activity 1.7: Planning distribution: Hope Restoration South Sudan (HRSS) will develop a distribution plan, which will involve the selection of a distribution site establishing a distribution timeline, and sharing with beneficiaries, SFP, ES/NFI coordination team in Juba, and the pipeline team respectiviely. Activity 1.8: Distribution of emergency shelter materials and nonfood items: Hope Restoration South Sudan (HRSS) will lead the distribution of emergency shelters and nonfood items to the beneficiaries for a period of 7 days with lapses flexibly considered in the project locations. Activity 1.9: Post Distribution Monitoring(PDM): Hope Restoration South Sudan (HRSS) will conduct a matured Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) for 2-3 weeks after distribution and will be consistent with S/NFI tools and methodologies to determine and track beneficiaries feedback on the levels of satisfaction served and modality employed by the team.
