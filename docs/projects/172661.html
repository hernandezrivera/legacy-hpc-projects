Project Summary

As of November 2020, Libya hosts over 45,000 registered refugees and asylum-seekers from Eritrea, Ethiopia, Iraq, Palestine, Somalia, South Sudan, Sudan, Syria and Yemen. Some 2,000 migrants and refugees/asylum-seekers are being held in government-run detention centres in Libya. Of these, 666 are persons of concern to UNHCR. Libya faces multiple challenges, including low cash liquidity and fuel shortages, further compounded by lockdown restrictions due to COVID-19 earlier in 2020. The 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview estimates that 604,307 individuals people 7,213 refugees are in needs for food assistance.
Due to a lack of access, transparency, the impossibility to monitor, and given that, according to international law, individuals under detention in a country must be provided with the means to live with dignity by the government of this country, WFP, along with the partners of the Food Security Sector took the decision not to intervene in detention in order not to legitimize detention and provide assistance blindly where access and monitoring are impossible.
Consequently, alternative forms of assistance to vulnerable refugees have been developed and this project, targeting food insecure migrants outside detention centres, has been designed to support the food security and nutrition status of refugees and asylum-seekers while preserving them from any form of exposure, stigmatization and discriminations, ensuring their protection and promoting their inclusion within the local communities.
WFP and UNHCR have built its functioning partnership since June 2020 to provide food assistance to vulnerable refugees and asylum-seekers in Libya based on analyzed data. As of November 2020, over 17,000 food boxes have distributed since June 2020. The most food-insecure non-detained refugees were identified in close partnership with UNHCR. WFP mobilized its full capacities to provide its ready-to-eat food assistance to some 1,000 urgent cases who were freshly released from detention centres.
Based on the built success, under the 2021 HRP - Food Security Sector, WFP will work with UNHCR and local NGO partners to maximize the impacts, outputs and outcomes for people in need, while targeting the monthly life-saving assistance to a monthly average of 7,213 vulnerable and non-detained refugee/asylum-seekers in urban settings.
Through its distribution cycles in June-November 2020 and under the separate partnership with IOM in April - May 2020, WFP experienced in reaching vulnerable non-Libyan under situations having the COVID-19 restrictions. Adaptation measures such as masks, disinfection, social distancing, temperature checks were systematically applied. These experience will be applied to this proposed project in 2021.
The experience in WFP's food assistance to vulnerable Libyan in the past years indicates that the ratio of beneficiaries is 69% men / 31% women. For comparison, non-Libyan population in Libya ratio is 89% men / 11% women. Women-headed households are proven to be more food insecure. Consequently, the proportion of supported women is higher in this programme.

1 - Needs

COVID-19 related movement restrictions and curfews, as well as the ongoing conflict and economic crisis, have led to sharp increases in food prices while making it hard for most refugees and asylum seekers to find daily work to support themselves. According to the Joint Market Monitoring Initiative, the cost of a minimum expenditure basket that would meet a family’s basic needs, including food items, was 19.2% more expensive in August 2020 than in March 2020, when the first coronavirus cases were reported in Libya. Food needs in the new focus areas for support were verified through WFP rapid needs assessments. They showed that, on average, one out of two respondents had a poor or borderline food consumption score. A majority of the respondents reported a significantly higher frequency of using negative coping strategies, such as skipping meals to adapt to food scarcity. 73 per cent of respondents reported not having any food at home, while 69 per cent had no money to buy food over the last month. WFP-conducted quick needs assessment also revealed that the needs for food assistance among refugees and asylum-seekers have increased. 31% of sampled refugees/asylum-seekers in Tripoli showed their poor food consumption score in September 2020. Over 78% of the randomly-selected respondents among refugees/asylum-seekers answered that they reduced the number of meals per day to copy with food shortage.

2 - Activities

WFP will join its resources and expertise with UNHCR in distributing monthly food rations to vulnerable refugees and asylum-seekers outside of detention centres. The distributions will be conducted through humanitarian protection and conflict sensitivity lens, which means distributing food rations to targeted refugees and asylum-seekers at safe distribution points after having consultations on the distribution points and schedule with the beneficiary groups and surrounding Libyan communities as well as the local authorities. Where the resources are available, As per the priority agreed on within the Food Security Sector, WFP plans to target the monthly ready-to-eat food assistance at 7,213 vulnerable refugees and asylum-seekers outside detention centres, with a priority in Benghazi, Tripoli and Zwara. Apart from the prioritized refugees and asylum-seekers, WFP will keep their agility and response capacities to provide life-saving ready-to-eat food assistance to those who are freshly released from detention centres. People will be receiving locally produced ready-to-eat food which supports the local economy. This food assistance does not require any cooking, which helps people at a time when cooking fuel prices are soaring. While distributing the monthly food assistance, WFP will ensure that the practical programmatic control and quality assurance measures shall be put in place, which includes: i) WFP’s training of its Cooperating Partners and its own staff in cross-cutting issues such as gender, GBV, PSEA, Protection and ConflictSensitivity; ii) WFP’s output monitoring through the verification of distribution reports and spot-checks and WFP’s outcome monitoring through annual post-distribution monitoring; iii) WFP’s distributions of leaflets and visibility materials to raise the awareness of participants and nonparticipants about the CFM.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

4.1) WFP displays posters sharing the CFM number in all food distribution points and also shares this information verbally with all beneficiaries throughout the assistance cycle; 4.2) As part of its conflict-sensitive assistance, WFP conducts analysis and consultations with local authorities and UNHCR before expanding unconditional food assistance to any location. This includes ensuring access to all affected communities and speaking to different groups to enable their participation. WFP also coordinates its food assistance to vulnerable Libyans with food assistance to vulnerable non-Libyans in the samegeographical areas; 4.3) As part of its conflict-sensitive assistance, WFP analyzes before beginning food assistance in any location. This includes ensuring access to all affected communities and speaking to different groups to enable their participation; 4.4) COVID-19 guidelines are shared verbally and through posters throughout training venues. Masks and disinfection gels are provided at food distribution points, and waiting areas and food receipt points are re-arranged to facilitate social distancing;4.6) Sensitive beneficiaries data is only shared between WFP and its partners through secure means and never via email. Access to this data is strictly limited; 4.9) WFP utilizes the UNHCR's established capacities to select the beneficiaries based on the vulnerability. UNHCR-prepared long lists are verified through WFP's quick needs assessments. ; 4.10) WFP consults a range of actors during the quick needs assessments, project implementation and quality control call phases. This includes interviews with locals from a variety of backgrounds; 4.11) WFP facilitates access to food assistance for people with disabilities where possible. This includes having sign language available or other support such as food deliveries byporters as needed.
