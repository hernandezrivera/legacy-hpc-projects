Project Summary

The physical safety of communities in the Libya, especially returnees, their economic livelihoods, and access to basic services are threatened by the presence of mines and explosive ordnance. To address these issues, 3F will conduct EOD and Battle Area Clearance (BAC) to physically remove threats; Non-technical Survey (NTS) to identify threats and prioritize their removal; and Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) to raise awareness about the threat and influence positive behavior change.
Activities will take place Primarily in Tripoli, Benghazi, Tawergha, and Sebha, with the possibility to work in other surrounding areas based on need and priorities of the National Authorities.

1 - Needs

residential, agricultural, and industrial areas in Libya are affected by large amounts of Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) resulting from repeated armed conflicts since 2011. This engenders a direct threat to local communities and migrants, prevents the safe return of Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), and impedes reconstruction efforts and livelihoods. The Triangle between Tripoli-Gharyan-Tarhuna witnessed near constant fighting for more than one year. The Southern Suburbs of Tripoli were most severely impacted. According to UN OCHA, approximately 150,000 people were displaced by the conflict. When LNA forces finally withdrew in May 2020, a vast array of mines, booby-traps, and other explosive ordnance were discovered in the former conflict zone. By the end of October more than 190 persons have been killed or wounded by mines in Tripoli. The majority of these victims have been IDPs seeking to return to their homes, in addition to many Libyan deminers. According to IOM, by end of August some 33,000 IDPs have returned to their homes despite the dangers.Meanwhile in Benghazi, although fighting has ceased since late 2017, large areas of the city remain destroyed and contaminated with mines and explosive ordnance, especially the Downtown and Southern neighborhoods. According to the IOM, 189,000 IDPs have returned to their homes in Benghazi. However, their efforts to rebuild their homes, the resumption of basic services, and livelihood activities are continually obstructed by explosive ordnance. The various conflicts have led to contamination of key infrastructure such as schools, medical facilities, the electricity grid, and water systems in both cities. 3F is already present in Tripoli and Benghazi and is conducting EOD spot tasks, Non-technical Survey (NTS), and Risk Education.Although the largest numbers of people are affected in the Tripoli and Benghazi, according to REACH’s 2020 Multi-Sector Needs Assessment, 17 out of 22 Mantikas in Libya reported the presence of mines and explosive ordnance. Many of these communities have been silently suffering for years while humanitarian actors have focused on the large population centers. Moreover, the work of INGOs has been critically hindered and delayed as a result of security and restrictions on access due to visas and the COVID-19 pandemic. The effects of the pandemic globally, have only served to highlight the importance of localisation and nationalization to ensure that the work of humanitarians is itself resilient.

2 - Activities

3F will deploy three clearance teams. Two teams based in Tripoli and the other based in Benghazi. Additionally, 3F will deploy 1 NTS team in Tripoli to support the survey efforts of the LibMAC3F will deliver EORE to at-risk groups in Tripoli, Benghazi, Tawergha, Gharyan, and Sebha via Community Liaison officers and/or teams of community volunteers.The Tripoli teams under this project will focus on recent conflict zones and returning population centers in Greater Tripoli. In Benghazi, the team will focus on Sabri and Gandfouda areas. Both teams will perform EOD spot tasks based on any emergency reports from the community received in their area of deployment and NTS reports. All clearance teams can also be expanded by recruiting additional two-five searchers to conduct Battle Area Clearance (BAC) or demining based on needs and priorities of the LibMAC. A key 3F strategy is that its EOD and NTS teams are roving. While being based in Tripoli and Benghazi, they will be able to travel to neglected or more unsecure areas for short missions at LibMAc request or approval. Likely locations include Alaziziya, Gharyan, and Tarhuna in the West and Derna, Tobruk, or Ajdabiya in the East. 3F uses a diverse array of tools and methods to conduct EORE. However, 3F maintains that the most effective means is direct face to face interaction with a small group of children or adults. Unfortunately, due to the continuing COVID-19 pandemic, 3F, alongside other actors, have been forced to suspend most face to face activities and instead rely on indirect risk education using Mass Media. As the pandemic persists, 3F will continue to focus on these indirect activities, while conducting direct sessions upon request by communities or other humanitarian actors.3F is also able to train new Risk Education community volunteers quickly and inexpensively outside of Tripoli and Benghazi, anywhere in Libya, should any gaps or needs emerge during the duration of the project.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

4.17- Prior to Intervention, attempts should be made to identify ownership, land use patterns, and history during a desk assessment. During the Task, staff should remain in close contact with Beneficiaries, Local Authorities, and owners. If the owner is absent or not identifiable 3F will not engage. If 3F becomes aware of any potential dispute, LibMAC should be informed and 3F should discuss whether to continue with the task or suspend activity. 4.20- 3F has Standard Operating Procedures for all activities in line with international best practices. All staff under go training on safety protocols and regular refresh trainings on first aid and case evacuation procedures. All staff are insured against injury from explosive ordnance. 3F has also taken action due to the on-going COVID-19 pandemic to ensure the safety of staff and their families while enabling operations to continue. This includes mandatory Mask requirements in Offices, reduced staff levels, and temperature checks. These procedures will continue as long as the pandemic continues or as long as relevant authorities advise.

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

3F strives to maintain the highest standards of integrity, transparency, and accountability in its work. This means that 3F is committed to ensuring that policies are in place to prevent malpractice and abuse taking place within the organization. Any form of malpractice or improper behavior will be taken seriously. All staff members are expected to follow the standards included in the Code of Conduct and are encouraged to raise any concerns that these are not being met within the organization.At the beginning of service all employees, consultants, volunteers, or directors are to receive a briefing about the code of conduct and sign it as part of their induction into the organization. Recently 3F as rolled out a whistle-blowing mechanism as part of a new Safeguarding Policy. This is concerned with all forms of corruption and criminal behavior, but particularly focused on any type of exploitative or abusive behavior, especially against beneficiaries, vulnerable adults and children. Information on contacting whistle-blowing focal points is posted in all offices. 3F also employs community liaison officers in all locations part of whose job is to speak with the community regularly to discuss 3F work and listen to any local concerns.
