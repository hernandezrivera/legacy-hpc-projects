Project Summary

In October 2017, violence erupted in Cabo Delgado, northern Mozambique, when armed men occupied the city of Mocimboa da Praia for two days and attacked three police stations, killing 17 people. Afterwards, armed men from the Islamist armed group Ahlu Sunna Wal Jama (ASWJ) conducted new attacks. The ASWJ is usually known locally as Al-Shabaab or mashababos, not to be confused with the Somali group Al-Shabaab The crisis in northern Mozambique is mostly concentrated in Cabo Delgado province, but it has also spread to neighbouring provinces, such as Nampula and Niassa Protection remains a significant need in northern Mozambique’s conflict-affected areas. The activity of the NSAG and the response of the armed forces have generated multiple protection concerns, including gender-based violence (including sexual violence and sexual exploitation) and child abuse. 1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The ongoing conflict in the Cabo Delgado has led to significant displacement, affecting over [1]61,000 children between December 2023 and March 2024. Many of these children have been separated from their families, exposed to violence, and face the heightened risks of being [2]recruited by NSAGs, abducted, killed, sexually abused and forced into early marriages. The lack of basic services, including education, health care and lack of food, exacerbates their vulnerability. The primary goal of this partnership is to provide immediate and life-saving child protection services to children, adolescents and youth, including their families, affected by the conflict and building their resilience capacity in Cabo Delgado. This partnership aligns with the broader humanitarian response strategy of the Mozambique Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP2024) and contributes to the Sustainable Development Goal 16.2: "End abuse, exploitation, trafficking, and all forms of violence against and torture of children." Target Population: The project will target approximately a total of 36,024 (9006 per district) children and adolescent including unaccompanied and separated minors, children with disabilities, and those living in displacement camps and host communities per district. The project will also reach 4,800 (1200 per district) caregivers and 164 (41 per district) community leaders. Geographic Scope: The project will be implemented in districts of Chiure, Macomia, Mocimboa Da Praia, Mueda, Muidumbe, Nangade, Palma and Quissanga in Cabo Delgado.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Plan International proposes the following methodology, approaches and key initiatives to ensure immediate and life-saving child protection services to children and adolescents, including their families, affected by the conflict and building their resilience capacity in Cabo Delgado province.Comprehensive Child Protection Case Management In coordination with District Social Welfare Services, the project will provide Case Management services to UASCs and children at risk identified through the Community Child Protection Volunteers’ . In case of UASCs foster families will be identified and trained to provide alternative care. Plan International will engage CP AoR, and CAFAAG TWGs to raise awareness among affected communities child protection risks and strengthen referral pathways. Cash Voucher Assistance (CVA) through case management will be provided for most vulnerable households of children affected by abuse and exploitation to address immediate protection needs such as health, food, accommodation, transportation, MHM kits etc. Family Tracing and Reunification and Alternative careSeparated children and unaccompanied children will be identified during the life of the project. They will be documented and receive case management services including referral to the necessary services. Family tracing and reunification support will be provided with the provision of family-based alternative care, including support to informal foster families, follow-up and home visits. Plan will collaborate with the DPGCAS to train foster families in the communities who are willing to take up children in their families. Psychosocial Support and Child Friendly Spaces Plan will establish, reactivate, or adopt Child-Friendly Spaces (CFS) in all targeted districts, using a tailored approach for each district and each location. CFS animators will be hired and provided with extensive training on CFS management, Child Protection, Safeguarding, PSEA, Identification and safe referral of cases, PSS and Psychological First Aid (PFA). The animators will implement recreational activities applying the Plan International CFS Toolkit and Clowns Without Boarders’ Laughter Manual. Mobile CFS will be introduced in hard-to-reach areas and temporary accommodation centers using PLAN Mobile Child Protection Programming Guide. CFS Centre Management Teams will be established to link CFS activities to the community, thereby strengthening community ownership and sustainability.Life and parenting skills Plan International will roll out The Parenting and Adolescent Life Skills (PALS) Programme model (developed by Plan International) for humanitarian team to enable adolescents and their caregivers improve their psychosocial wellbeing and strengthen the family and caregiving environments in emergency and protracted crisis settings. The contextualised programme will equip adolescents aged 10 to 19 years and their parents and caregivers with essential information, skills and resources to support adolescent health, safety and wellbeing in times of crisis.Strengthening of Community Based CP Mechanisms, such as CBCPCs, Community Courts and District Reference GroupsAs a strategy to get buy-in and ensuring sustainability of the intervention, Plan International will build/strengthen the capacity of community-based protection structures in preventing and responding to Child protection with a focus on GBV risks, including PSHEA. Activities to strengthen these mechanisms include : Update or conduct community mapping exercise to establish existing services, actors, risks areas etc; Provide training, equipment, work materials and technical support the development and implementation of community led action plan; and Conduct Awareness raising on CP, GBV and Social Cohesion issues prevailing in the community. 5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The project will identify risks through risk assessment and safety audit and devise mechanisms to mitigate them. Plan International had go risk assessment and safety audit tool which are used to conduct the assessments. Through the rapid needs analysis, the project will also identify potential risks by interviewing different stakeholders and the affected population and community level. The project will regularly conduct risk assessments to update the risk register and make programme adjustments.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The project will identify and address barriers that limit access to assistance, such as cultural, physical, or economic constraints, for example by providing assistance at accessible locations or offer transportation for women, elderly persons and children at risk access protection services in distant place. The design of the project will culturally sensitive outreach to encourage women's participation.The project interventions will be tailored to the needs of women, elderly and children adolescents, with a gender-sensitive approach. It will also ensure physical access to children with disabilities. Safeguards will be established to prevent discrimination, exploitation, or abuse when accessing assistance, for example ensuring private and safe spaces for women and girls/adolescents to discuss sensitive issues. The staff engaged in case management will be trained in age- and gender-responsive approaches. The project will actively involve women, elderly, children, adolescents and their families in the planning, implementation, and monitoring of the project through conducting regular consultations and community feedback sessions. The provide skill-building opportunities to empower HH heads, in particular women will receive training in agricultural practices and VSLA to support their economic independence. The intervention will develop safe, accessible, and confidential mechanisms for collecting feedback, ensuring that the affected population, especially women, elderly, children and adolescents can voice their concerns and contribute to solutions.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

To effectively identify people with disabilities (PWDs) and remove barriers to their access to assistance, the project can incorporate the following measures:Persons With Disabilities will be identified through Community Engagement and Outreach by working closely with community leaders, disability organizations, and local health/social services to identify PWDs. Participatory approaches will be applied, such as focus group discussions and home visits, to locate individuals who may not self-identify due to stigma or isolation.The project will conduct disability-inclusive assessments integrate questions aligned with the Washington Group Short Set of Questions on Disability into needs assessments to systematically identify functional limitations across populations. Disaggregated data by age, gender, and disability will be collected to better understand specific needs. Referral Networks will be established linking mapping partner with healthcare providers, schools, and social services to establish a referral system that flags PWDs requiring assistance. Accessible Communication Channels will be fostered use multiple communication formats (e.g., sign language interpreters, braille, pictorial aids) to ensure people with diverse disabilities can identify themselves and their needs.The project will pro-actively remove barriers to access by retrofitting facilities and distribution points to accommodate PWDs, including ramps, handrails, wide pathways, and accessible transportation. Priority lanes or designated support teams will be ensured for PWDs to minimize waiting times and logistical challenges, apart from providing at-home delivery or mobile services for those unable to travel.The project will also promote awareness and sensitization by training project staff and volunteers on disability inclusion and stigma reduction to ensure respectful and equitable treatment of PWDs. Community awareness campaigns will be conducted to reduce stigma and encourage the participation of PWDs. Child friendly, accessible and inclusive feedback Mechanisms will be establish e.g., anonymous suggestion boxes, toll-free hotlines with voice/visual options) to ensure PWDs can report barriers or suggest improvements. The project will conduct regular monitoring and adaptation, assessing the barriers PWDs face during project implementation and adapt activities based on their feedback and evolving needs.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Child Protection in Emergencies (CPiE) projects, while primarily focused on safeguarding the well-being of children in crisis settings, can have unintended environmental impacts. These may arise from activities such as shelter construction, supply distribution, transportation, or establishing child-friendly spaces (CFS). It is vital to incorporate environmental considerations to mitigate harm and enhance the project's sustainability. The project will conduct preliminary EIAs as part of project planning to identify potential environmental risks. This may ential partnering with environmental experts or agencies to integrate findings into program design. The project will train staff and community members on environmentally sustainable practices and involve children and families in environmental conservation activities to foster awareness and responsibility.
