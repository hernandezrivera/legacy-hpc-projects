Project Summary

The project targets 5,500 HHs with a total of 33,000 of individuals disaggregated roughly as (6,890 men, 8,000 women, 9,574 boys, 8,536 girls) of the most vulnerable conﬂict-affected IDPs, host communities, and returnees in Idleb and Aleppo governorates, prioritizing households headed by female, child, disabled, or elderly, then households with severely ill or disabled members, children below 5, elderly, pregnant or lactating women; with special attention to those recently displaced or living overcrowded.
The project contributes to providing life-saving and life-sustaining shelter & NFI assistance through the following activities:
1. Providing 3,000 HHs with seasonal and supplementary NFIs and assistance including winter clothing, stoves, and fuel in Armanaz, Kafr Takharim, and Afrin community and camps. Each household will receive a voucher of 180$, prioritize its needs of these items, and select the proper shop to get them from.
2. Provision of life-saving and life-sustaining shelter support through installation of 1,000 tents with required associated infrastructure including concrete basement 4*4 m2, and two layers of blocks surround the inside part of the tent to strengthen ﬁxing the metal Skelton properly and to avoid the damage of ﬂoods. These activities will be totally implemented in coordination with CCCM and WASH clusters. SDI will sign the agreements with the local councils and get the HLP documentation of sites to be distributed in Azaz and Qourqania sub-districts,
3. Upgrading of 900 unﬁnished building with an average cost of 850$ per unit in Aleppo (Al-Bab, Suran, Daret Azza), Idleb (Harim, Salqin, Iskat, Kafr Takharim, Dana)
4. The project will also contribute towards the resilience and cohesion of communities and households by improving housing through rehabilitation of 600 of partially damaged houses with an average cost of 750$ per house in Idleb (Harim, Salqin, Iskat, Kafr Takharim, Dana)
Needs assessment was conducted early this year to identify the project locations.
In all activities, SDI considers the protection of the beneﬁciaries, so the distribution centres and the shops provide the assistance will be prepared with functional ﬁreﬁghting equipment, brochures of safety and security practice will be distributed to beneﬁciaries. Moreover, female staff will be recruited and trained to ensure more protection from exploitation and sexual abuse for girls and women and to deal with any GBV cases they may encounter during the project.
Most importantly, SDI pays high attention to people with disabilities, elderlies, and people with special needs and for those assistance will be delivered to home.
Housing, Land and Property (HLP) rights of shelter sites, shelter beneﬁciaries, and house owners will be considered throughout all stages of shelter repair interventions, and agreements will be signed to guarantee the rights of the beneﬁciaries. Coordination with SNFI cluster and other active members will be maintained as an essential tool to ensure geographical separation and avoid overlapping/duplication with other partners and donors.

4 - Needs

According to the SNFI HNO2020 summary report, Shelter needs in Syria are widespread and acute resulting in significant living standard consequences and affecting physical and mental well-being and protection of affected populations. Overall shelter severity has increased across Syria, with a corresponding increase in PiN from 2019 by 20 per cent to 5.65 million people of this, 3.8 million (69 per cent) are in areas of severity or higher. There is still ongoing displacement, with roughly one million people expected to be displaced in 2020. MSND data demonstrate that in the north-west, there are critical levels of overcrowding putting significant pressure on overburdened communities, and ongoing displacement coupled with a lack of safe or adequate shelter, and explosive contamination is a significant obstacle. NFIs continue to be essential and life-saving particularly for those newly displaced of forced to relocate. NFIs become increasingly important during the cold weather months to ensure families can live in safety and dignity. Families report that they are in need of fuel, clothing, and power/heating sources. According to RNA conducted by SDI Between 26 to 31 January 2020. So far, the numbers of IDPs from there are no clear statistics on the displaced people numbers, due to the large numbers of displaced people on a daily basis, and these statistics do not include families who live in the open or are still in their cars because there are no places to live. The estimated number of IDPs in (10) communities of Aleppo (Azaz, Al Bab, Suran, Daret Azza) and Idleb (Harim, Salqin, Iskat, Kafr Takharim, Dana) was 9705 households with 59265 of individuals and the numbers are still significantly increasing. Most the them are living in schools, unfinished buildings, or in very crowded buildings in harsh conditions ,91% of the them reported that they are in urgent need for emergency shelter, Heating fuel, winter clothing, furnishing, Hygiene kits, and kitchen kits.

5 - Implementation of activities

SDI will engage the local communities during the project phases through holding meetings and/or focus groups with prominent ﬁgures, older persons, and women in order to ensure that they are aware of the project goals and activities and to ensure the impartial beneﬁciary selection process to reach the most vulnerable persons. The activities of 2.1.1 Rehabilitation of damaged houses and 1.4.2 upgrading of unﬁnished buildings will be implemented through cash and in-kind modalities. SDI announces and receives applications directly from house owners and beneﬁciaries in the project registration centers. The project staff check the HLP documentations and conduct community veriﬁcation to 100% of houses. The technical staff will conduct the technical inspection, prepare BOQs and scope of works, and closely supervise the progress in each unit to ensure the quality of works delivered, and in parallel, media staff will document each work pre, during, and after the implementation. Beneﬁciaries will be hosted in delivered houses after signing house owner- beneﬁciary free tenancy agreement. In activity 1.2.1 Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs including heating fuel, SDI will receive the beneﬁciary lists and pay visits to a random sample of beneﬁciaries to verify they meet the project vulnerable criteria. The modality of delivering assistance will be voucher and in-kind. For activities 1.3.1 Installation of tents, and 1.3.2 Associated infrastructure, SDI will sign the agreements with local councils, ensure that the shelter interventions incorporate requisite HLP components, and coordinate with CCCM and WASH clusters when distributing the tents. Complaint mechanism is in place to receive feedback, complaint numbers and email will be well-advertised at work places. An M&E plan is in place and according to the activity. All the contractors will be contracted through a transparent and competitive manner according to SDI’s policy and procedures.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk1. Tension between host communities, IDPs and returnees. Likelihood: high Impact: high Mitigation: beneficiary selection be done through a transparent process based on validated needs. Moreover, the project will target about 20-40% of vulnerable host community in each activity. SDI will establish clear well-advertised feedback and complaint mechanism. Local councils and other stakeholders will be involved in beneficiary selection process. Risk2. risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers of women and girls . Likelihood: high Impact: high Mitigation: All SDI staff are required to read and signed the Code of Conduct. A clear well-advertised feedback and complaint mechanism is in place, through WhatsApp and email well displayed on banners in each distribution and work location; through M&E staff who will be present in all distribution centers. Risk3. risk of sexual exploitation or GBV by rehabilitation workshops workers of women, girls, and disabilities. Likelihood: high Impact: high Mitigation: All staff of SDI get trained of PSEA and GBV. Female staff will be hired to monitor and supervise the rehabilitation works in houses headed by women, children or disabilities, staff through M&E staff who will be present and explained the mechanism of complaints. Risk5. Risk of physical harm at distribution points (security, safety). Likelihood: Medium Impact: high Mitigation: Beneficiaries will be divided into small groups, distribution date and time will be coordinated with local authorities and informed to each beneficiary group before distribution in order to avoid crowdedness, make separate women and men rows, long waiting times or disorder in distribution places. SDI will coordinate with SCD to help in case of any unexpected emergency. Distribution process will complete in time for beneficiaries to reach their homes during daylight. Items will be brought to the doorsteps of the very vulnerable HHs who cannot come to the distribution centers.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption1 targeted location is accessible and secure Risk: Armed conﬂicts and clashes affecting access to the project location, Likelihood: medium. Effect: medium. Mitigation: Monitor the security instances in the targeted location and the routes leading to the area to ensure staff access. SDI will advise on alternative routes to mitigate the risk. Reporting and coordinating with the SNFI cluster and OCHA to change location or suspend activity. Assumption2: HLP documents are available and veriﬁed before implementing shelter rehabilitation. Risk: Fake documentation and lack of formal property documentation. Likelihood: medium. Effect: medium. Mitigation: Provide the ﬁeld staff with the required awareness about inter-linkages between HLP rights and shelter, as well as engage the local authorities in shelter activities. HLP ownership community veriﬁcation. Assumption3: The IDP beneﬁciaries are accommodated in the targeted units for free. Risk3: IDP Eviction from shelter or they are asked to pay rent. Likelihood: medium. Effect: high. Mitigation3: SDI will prepare and sign rent- free tenancy agreement between landlords and the beneﬁciaries, and two of community representatives. Assumption5: SDI project centers and warehouses are safe and secure. Risk5: Centers and warehouses might be exposed to dangers such as robbery and ﬁre. Likelihood: low. Effect: high mitigation5: SDI warehouses are chosen in secure locations, regular checks on ﬁreﬁghting equipment and 24/7 active guards. Risk6. risk of sexual exploitation or GBV. Likelihood: high Impact: high Mitigation: All staff of SDI get trained of PSEA and GBV. Female staff will be hired. M&E staff who will be present and explained the mechanism of complaints. Risk7. Fire hazard during or after distribution of fuel. Risk mitigation: Ensure diesel is of good quality; not too inﬂammable according to profession standards. Brochures about usage of stoves safety instruction and ﬁre hazard will be distributed to all beneﬁciaries.
