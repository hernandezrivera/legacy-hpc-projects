Project Summary

This CSP aims to contribute all year-round to: Crisis-affected populations to meet their basic food and nutrition needs during and after the crises; Food insecure school-age children, to access adequate and nutritious food; Vulnerable groups (children, pregnant women and adolescent girls) to have enhanced nutritional status; Food insecure populations and communities to have more resilient livelihoods and sustainable food systems; Capacity strengthening of National institutions; Humanitarian and development partners to access common services unti appropriate an sustainable alternative are available.

1 - Besoins

The 2020 national nutrition survey based on SMART methodology indicates that the prevalence of global acute malnutrition (GAM) in Niger was 12.7%, which is slightly above the 10% serious threshold. GAM rates among children aged 6 to 23 months stood at 18.9%, above the emergency threshold (15%) compared to children aged 24-59 months (9.5%). Analysis show disparities between regions with five regions Diffa (19.3%), Zinder (14.9%), Agadez (14.8%), Maradi (13.9%) and Tahoua (13.7%) having rates above the national rate (12.7%). In Zinder region, the ten administrative divisions have GAM rates above the 10% and five of the ten have GAM rates above the 15% : Dungass (17.3%), Kantché (15.4%), Takeita (17.8%), Tanout (17.2%) and Tesker (17.4%). The rate of severe acute malnutrition (SAM), was estimated at 2.6% above the WHO critical threshold of 2% with four regions (Diffa: 5.3%, Agadez: 3.3%, Zinder: 3.3% and Tahoua: 2.9%) having rates above this threshold. Chronic malnutrition remains above the 30 percent "critical" threshold with 45.1% at national level with all the regions except Niamey (19.7%) having rates far above the critical threshold. With the deteriorating securtiy situation in Niger's neighbouring countryies (Nigeria, Burkina Faso and Mali), there is a great possibility of split over of these crises in to Niger thus affecting and worsen the actual poor food security and nutrition situations in Diffa, Maradi, Tahoua and Tillabéry regions due to poor access to these areas. The Nutrition Cluster estimated that some 1.25 million people require nutritional assistance, including 457,200 children suffering from severe acute malnutrition (SAM), with 114,300 cases with medical complications. Some 396,467 children aged 6 to 23 months (boys and girls) suffering from moderate acute malnutrition, 202,222 pregnant and lactating women with an upper arm circumference of less than 210 mm, and 65,468 caregivers (accompanying persons) in the CRENIs and will need appropriate nutritional supplementation. A total of 162,212 children 6 to 23 months (boys and girls) and 11,500 pregnant and lactating women (not malnourished) will need blanket supplementation to stabilise their nutrition status.
