Needs

Haiti ranks as one of the countries with the highest exposure and vulnerability to multiple hazards, including hurricanes, floods, earthquakes, landslides and droughts. After three years (2014 2016) of consecutive drought, Haiti was further devastated by Hurricane Matthew in October 2016. Many Haitians have seen their livelihoods disappear.Since 2014, Haiti has been regularly hit by the "El Niño" phenomenon characterized by repeated episodes of drought accompanied by a poor distribution of rain. Currently, the situation does not seem to be improving because according to the latest IPC analysis (CNSA, October 2018), a prolonged drought hit again several communes of Haiti and strongly affected crops and pastures of the spring 2018 agricultural season. Therefore, 986,251 people in the departments of Grande Anse (203,451 people), South (145,590), West (149,389), North (329,112) and Northeast (155,058) are in acute food insecurity. The weather outlook indicates a strong likelihood of strengthening this "El Niño" phenomenon for the next year.The effects of Hurricanes Irma and Maria that hit the northern part of the country and the effects of drought of the 3 last years could complicate recovery and/or drag additional people to require humanitarian assistance. Humanitarian actions in response to hurricane Matthew has contributed to improve food security situation in the areas of Sud, Nippes, Nord Ouest, Sud Est and Artibonite. Since June 2017, food security response is scaling down even if some humanitarian organizations continue to assist vulnerable groups in some areas affected by Matthew and flood that mostly affected the departments of Grand Anse and Sud in April and May 2017. Domestic food availability in 2017 is anticipated to increase moderately as production shortfalls during the spring 2017 season of maize and beans are anticipated to be compensated by an increase in starchy root output. However, agricultural production would still remain well below its levels from the 5 year period before the El Niño phenomena. The effectiveness of actions in the area of food and nutritional security necessarily requires a thoughtful coordination of the interventions of national and international actors, a clear political arbitration to bring together all the actors and partners involved in this thematic for an effective governance of the food security and nutrition.The absence of a functional and responsive information system capable to anticipate crises (drought, price increase, etc.) through early warning must be resolved. It is in this context that the CNSA plans to revitalize its information system by setting up an alert system. This project proposal aims to support this approach.

Activities or outputs

- Ensure a harmonized approach is used for all sector’s partners by providing appropriate tools and capacity building for assessment, analysis of data and monitoring.- Maintain a mechanism for sector partners to share the tools and findings and update assessment registry.- Develop sector contingency plans and report their emergency response.- Promote alignment and harmonization of partners’ geographical targeting and selection criteria with sector guidelines.- Promote harmonization of partner’s assistance/response package as per sector technical guidelines and ensure that early recovery approach is embedded in the response from the earliest phases as a guiding principle of the humanitarian action. - Provide trainings to partners in sector’s related topics, including food security concepts, analysis and programming.- Update WOS 4W regularly by partners for response and gaps analysis.- Provide an inclusive platform for all cross learning initiatives and advocacy (policies and strategies assistance/livelihoods linkages, monitoring, transfer modalities, gender, protection mainstreaming GBV, accountability and other people centric issues).- Ensure the coordination and collaboration with other sectors (e.g. nutrition, early recovery, protection, S/NFI, Wash, CCCM) through inter sectorial working meetings.- Support CNSA to develop and implement an effective food security early warning and monitoring system to guide the sector's interventions- Support the IPC process. At least 2 IPC analyzes are planned per year to update the national food insecurity classification and to enable emergency FS response planning or simulation exercises. A CNSA staff will be trained by FAO to continue activities at the end of the project.
