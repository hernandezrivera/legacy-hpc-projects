Project Summary

This project aims to save lives by addressing the acute needs of 21,000 conflict- and flood-affected individuals, including returnees, IDPs, and host communities in Twic East, Pibor, and Uror counties (Jonglei State) and Terekeka County (Central Equatoria State). It focuses on enhancing agricultural production through the distribution of crop and vegetable seeds, farming tools, and fishing gear, complemented by training in good agricultural practices to boost productivity.
Key activities aim to prevent famine, improve food consumption, dietary diversity, and strengthen the coping strategies of beneficiaries in IPC Phases 4 and 5. By supporting vulnerable households with crop and vegetable inputs, fishing, and livestock assistance, the project seeks to enhance resilience, enabling households to meet immediate food needs and rebuild assets. These efforts also aim to reduce acute malnutrition rates among the affected population.
To ensure a safe and inclusive approach, mitigation measures against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) will be integrated into all beneficiary interactions. The project will promote accountability to affected populations (AAP) by addressing age, disability, gender, and protection needs holistically. Protection will be mainstreamed throughout the project cycle to ensure beneficiaries’ safety and dignity, equitable access, and active participation from all groups.

1. Needs

The humanitarian crisis in Twic East, Uror, Pibor, and Terekeka counties remains severe, driven by acute food insecurity, malnutrition, displacement, and repeated climatic shocks. According to the IPC analysis projections for April to July 2025, an estimated 7.7 million people (57% of South Sudan’s population) will face IPC Phase 3 (Crisis) or worse. This includes 2.53 million in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) and 63,000 in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe), with Uror and Pibor among the counties projected to have populations in Catastrophe. Terekeka and Twic East also face significant portions of their populations in Crisis or Emergency phases. These conditions are exacerbated by conflict, displacement, and barriers to accessing livelihoods (IPC September 2024-July 2025, South Sudan FSNMS 2024). The impact of the 2024 flooding on agricultural production has been particularly devastating, leaving communities in Twic East, Uror, and Pibor with depleted food stocks and damaged farmland. Similarly, in Terekeka, the flooding destroyed crops and grazing lands, further destabilizing food sources. These shocks have disrupted the livelihoods of thousands of households, leaving many unable to recover independently. Repeated flooding has eroded community resilience, forcing populations to rely on external food assistance to meet their basic needs (IPC September 2024-July 2025). Economic shocks and high food prices have compounded the crisis, making food inaccessible for many vulnerable households. In Pibor, the price of white sorghum in September 2024 was 190% higher than the five-year average, severely limiting purchasing power. Similar price increases in Terekeka and Twic East have reduced household access to staple foods, leaving families unable to meet their nutritional needs. These economic pressures, combined with the destruction of productive assets like livestock and agricultural tools, have significantly weakened the coping capacity of affected populations (FEWS NET October 2024 - May 2025). Conflict and displacement remain critical challenges, particularly in Uror, Pibor, and parts of Terekeka. Intercommunal violence and cattle raiding have forced thousands to flee their homes, disrupting agricultural activities and markets. Displaced populations face limited access to land for farming and grazing, further hindering livelihood recovery. These challenges have left conflict-affected households reliant on humanitarian assistance for survival (Uror IRNA Report August 2024, Twic East IRNA Report September 2024). Malnutrition is an urgent concern across the targeted counties, with high rates of acute malnutrition driven by insufficient food consumption and poor dietary diversity. Nationally, 2.1 million children under five and 1.1 million pregnant and lactating women are projected to suffer from acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025. Jonglei and Central Equatoria States contribute significantly to this burden, with inadequate access to livelihoods further compounding the crisis (IPC September 2024-July 2025). To address these challenges, immediate and targeted interventions in food security and livelihood support are required. Priority actions include distributing crop and vegetable seeds, farming tools, and fishing equipment to restore agricultural production and support dietary diversity. Training farmers in good agricultural practices is essential to increase productivity and build resilience to climatic shocks. Livestock interventions, such as veterinary support, can stabilize livelihoods and improve food security for pastoralist communities. By addressing acute food insecurity, restoring productive capacities, and strengthening community resilience, SLDA can help alleviate suffering and enable recovery for vulnerable populations in these counties in 2025.

2. Project activities

The following Activities will be implemented by SLDA in these 4 counties:• Distribution of emergency livelihood inputs (vegetable, crop seeds and fishing kits)• Livestock treatment and vaccination and disease surveillance: The project will facilitate Community Animal Health Workers Training and inputs support, cold chain establishment and maintenance, prevention & treatment especially zoonoses e.g. RVF. • Training and capacity building: Beneficiaries will receive training and extension services on a range of topics including but not limited to: training on fishing best practices; food utilization and appropriate feeding practices; crop and vegetable production; Climate Smart Agriculture techniques and Practices, adaptive and absorptive on-farm production (farmer/agro-pastoral field schools, demonstration plots); business skills development, entrepreneurship and financial literacy.• Sensitization on basic nutrition IYCF: This will focus on topics such as food hygiene, nutritional value of different foods, cooking classes and nutrition requirements of children and pregnant and lactating women (PLW).
