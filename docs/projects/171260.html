Project Summary

This project will support the delivery of quality, integrated child protection and psychosocial support services to the most vulnerable boys and girls aged 4-18 in Gaza, especially those affected by COVID-19. The pandemic has affected all aspects of life in Gaza, including child protection needs and services. The project will ensure that 12 family centres and 30 outreach sites in Gaza are fully operational, responding to the pressing mental health and psychosocial as well as child protection needs of affected children. Service delivery will be targeted to the most affected locations and the most vulnerable children through a coordinated continuum of services in Gaza. Each family centre will be connected to a community-based child protection committee that informs the family centre of community needs and acts as a focal point for identifying vulnerable children for referral. Family centres will operate through established Community Based Organizations (CBOs) and outreach sites, and will assess/screen and refer children for further services through:
1) a case management approach for the worst affected;
2) referral of complex child protection cases to child protection services (relevant stakeholders and Civil Society Organizations (CSOs); and
3) referral of children with further psychosocial support needs to more specialized psychological support services. Services will also strengthen outreach capacities to detect and link up children in need with service providers, whilst providing mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) support through the family centres.
This project is in line with and contributes to the Protection Cluster Objective Two, which calls for putting in place protection response mechanisms to prevent and mitigate the of the occupation and conflict related violence.

1 - Needs

It is estimated that 197,903 children in Gaza need structured psychosocial support (PSS) and child protection (CP) services (2021 HNO). In addition, a total of 35,815 children affected by severe and moderate mental health distress are estimated to require individual case management. In Gaza, case management of CP cases are undertaken by trained case managers who are responsible to assess the needs and address child protection risks. Recommended response varies depending on the risk level with cases referred to the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) or family centres as per Gaza referral pathways. Between Jan-June 2020, MoSD was only able to reach 30 children with case management out of 1,114 reached by protection partners. Thus, fully functional family centres providing case management services are crucial. In Gaza, the COVID-19 crisis has further undermined the humanitarian situation already affected by a longstanding closure with intensified access and movement restrictions, the recurrent violence including the aftermath of the Great March of Return (GMR) demonstrations in 2018/2019, the ongoing political tensions and fuel, water and electricity crises. According to the preliminary findings of a Child Protection Needs Identification and Analysis Framework (NIAF) conducted by UNICEF between July and October 2020, confinement and intensified economic pressures compounded by the loss of income have heightened tension and stress in the household, in turn increasing violence against children including physical, emotional violence, abuse and neglect. Whereas crucial violence prevention and response services are delayed, suspended or interrupted due to containment measures, in Gaza children’s exposure to violence remains widespread with some 33.4 per cent of boys and nearly 25 per cent of girls under the age of 11 experiencing severe physical violence including regular severe beatings by their caregivers (PCBS 2019). In addition, social isolation, limited contact with informal support networks outside the home, and limited access to technology have left children more vulnerable. With some 53 per cent of the population in Gaza living under the poverty line (PCBS, 2017), the unemployment rate among the highest in the world at about 45 per cent (PCBS March 2020) and children below the age of 17 representing 50 per cent of Gaza total population (PCBS, 2019), children living in extremely poor households and other marginalized children are particularly at risk of harmful practices with the result of increasing demands for PSS and CP support on the already overwhelmed service providers. Data from a remote protection monitoring exercise with key informants launched by the protection cluster and its sub groups showed violence, harassment or abuse (46 out of 50), psychological distress (39 out of 50) and lack of access to education (15 out of 50) as the top protection issues most impacting children. The pandemic has contributed to higher expectation for older boys to engage in work including exploitative forms of labour and also amplified gender imbalances rooted in social norms with girls expected to perform household chores. According to the NIAF study, the percentage of child labourers accessing CP services in Gaza increased by 52 per cent between January-July 2020 in comparison to the same period in 2019. The respondents also identified negative changes in caregivers’ attitude/behavior towards their children because of COVID-19. These include verbal and physical abuse, all forms of neglect including emotional or medical neglect, and bullying. Despite the movement restrictions and the recent enforcement of lockdown in Gaza, family centre case managers provided case management and psychosocial support to 1,472 and 7,637 vulnerable children between Jan-Sep 2020. This included referrals made to medical, PSS, rehabilitation and educational support services, in response to psychosocial distress violence, and child labour cases in line with the Gaza referral pathways. Family centres achievements represent 61% and 87% of Protection Cluster reached beneficiaries for psychosocial support and case management respectively, reflecting the vital role of the centers. Intermittent funding to the family centres limits their capacity to meet the spikes in needs. Through case management, this project will respond to the protection needs of boys and girls, in particular older boys affected by child labour, and younger age boys and girls subject to PSS distress, neglect and violence. Unless critically under-resourced CP services are funded, children will remain at significant risk. Training of field staff on MHPSS minimum standards, in line with IASC Guidelines, including remote counseling is needed to ensure no harm to benefiting children.

2 - Activities

With the support of UNICEF implementing partners, individual protection needs of both girls and boys (4-18), especially adolescents aged 14-17, will be addressed. This will be done through the project, including, but not limited to, case management of children affected by PSS distress, domestic violence and child labour. In addition to case management, psychosocial support, life skills education, expressive arts, child parent interaction will be provided to children, while awareness raising on positive parenting and COVID-19 will also be provided to caregivers. Support to children injured during the GMR will also continue. In-house counseling manuals developed by the partners and reviewed by UNICEF will be used with the children. The I-deal manual will be used for life skills. Each manual contains at least 12 session to be implemented with children. Both case management and individual counseling support can go beyond this number of sessions depending on the severity of the case at hand. To support children with Disabilities (CWD), implementing partners will target them in coordination with, and/or through disability organizations they partner with. Currently, three out of the twelve family centres are disability organizations. In 2020, UNICEF supported more than 781 children with disabilities through these twelve centres. Considering the COVID-19 lockdown, activities will be conducted remotely, unless the situation allows for face to face interaction, with safety procedures related to COVID-19 protection taken into consideration. In remote counseling/support, children are connected under their parental oversight and guidance to social media platforms, where they can access the support sessions. This modality has been used in the family centres between April-Sep 2020 and has showed success in attracting children to the centres’ activities. Special attention will be paid towards CWD to ensure their smooth access to the centers, taking into consideration physical distancing to minimize close interaction with others and allow access to hygiene materials including hand sanitizers to minimize their need to go to the washrooms. Children coming from vulnerable families with no access to the internet can be reached though phone and distribution of recreational and awareness kits, Project outputs include: Output 1: Holistic child protection services equally provided to boys and girls aged 4-18 through 12 family centres and 5 Child Protection Networks. Activity 1.1: Provide structured child protection and psychosocial programs – multi-week life skills, including sessions on Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) awareness, individual and group counselling using gender and age sensitive resilience building approaches, parent/child interaction programs and expressive arts programs, especially children with disabilities. Those activities will be implemented face to face and/or virtually in line with COVID-19 governmental guidance. Activity 1.2: Provide case management services for children with protection needs accessing the family centres and identified through screening. Priority will be given to children who have been directly affected by COVID-19, and its repercussions on families in Gaza, children who lost caregivers, those subject to abuse, neglect, and exploitation and children with disabilities. Output 2: Integrated child protection services supported to strengthen parental capacity to respond to children's needs, including those with disabilities. Activity 2.1: Organize awareness raising sessions for parents on gender specific child protection issues and positive parenting (including a session on ERW awareness). Output 3: Capacity for professionals working on mental health and psychosocial support strengthened. Activity 3.1: Conduct trainings on mental health and psychosocial support guidelines for field and emergency workers.Activity 3.2: Conduct trainings on child protection detection and referral to non-CP professionals.

Monitoring the adherence of service providers to the case management standard operating procedures will also be included, as well as use of resilience building approaches. The project will be monitored using the UNICEF regular monitoring framework. Project implementation will be reviewed as part of the Annual Programme Review jointly conducted by UNICEF and project implementing partners. Regular field visits to project sites will be conducted, where feasible considering COVID-19 restrictions. Partners will submit quarterly progress reports as a component of the project monitoring mechanism. In addition, UNICEF will provide technical support to partners through regular coordination. Partners will assess benefit to children, caregivers and professionals through pre- and post-questionnaires, as well as focus group discussions. UNICEF is committed to reporting towards the Humanitarian Response Plan periodic reporting and the updating of the OCHA Financial Tracking System.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The safety of children, caregivers and family centres’ staff will be ensured through the selection of safe and child friendly spaces. In the context of COVID-19, hygiene materials related to disinfection of the workplace and personal hygiene in line with WHO guidelines will be allowed in the budget. To ensure the dignity of beneficiaries, staff will be observing the way they approach beneficiaries, which will respect their privacy and ensure confidential handling of their information. Additionally, the family centres will use context appropriate manuals for life skills education and group counseling. The child protection case management Standard Operating Procedures will govern the work of the case managers in the family centres, which prioritize the needs and the best interest of the child and takes into consideration their holistic welfare and protection without compromising their safety. The principles of inclusion of children with disabilities will also be taken into consideration when designing the various family centres' activities.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Individual protection needs of both girls and boys (4-18), especially those directly affected by the COVID-19 crisis and its repercussions on families’ protection and wellbeing will be addressed through the project. This will be done through the provision of case management of female children who might be affected by violence and early marriage and boys affected violence, child labour and injuries by Explosive Remnants of War (ERW). Both men and women will be targeted through the project, making sure that they understand their contribution to their children’s well-being and ensuring their safety and protection, especially during lockdown. Adolescent children injured at the borders will continue to access necessary support, critical for their physical and mental wellbeing. Both male and female case managers will be available to provide support either face to face or remotely over the phone or through social media platforms.
