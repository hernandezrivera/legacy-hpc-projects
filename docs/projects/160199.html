Project Summary

The project goal is to save lives by providing timely and integrated multi-sectoral assistance aimed at reducing acute humanitarian needs and building resilience for 5500 households (33,000 individuals) in Kapoeta East and North, Kassingor payam in Pibor County (bordering Kapoeta North), and for 1500 HH in Lainya and Morobo counties. The project is informed by the highest humanitarian needs and priorities in the locations, as identified by affected populations, needs assessments conducted by the various Clusters and knowledge and observations from ACROSS field staff on the ground. We have combined assistance in Food Security and Livelihoods with WASH and Protection, for integrated outcomes for the target populations.
ACROSS is a South Sudanese organisation with regional reach (a "Southern International" organisation) and has been supporting the positive and holistic transformation of communities in South Sudan since 1972. ACROSS is currently operating in some of the most isolated, hardest to reach areas in South Sudan and enjoys strong community relationships at multiple levels from the grassroots to State and National levels. Our mandate is holistic transformation, and we specialize in delivering multi-sectoral interventions with proven strong outcomes in these hardest to reach places. This holistic approach is beneficial not only to the affected populations, but is also appreciated by authorities (at all levels) and assists the broader humanitarian coordination system and community of actors and donors.
This project builds off current multi-sector humanitarian interventions delivered in 2019 by ACROSS in the five target locations, and incorporates successful and innovative strategies from ACROSS projects in other locations. A large portion of the project is likely to be funded by Sida via PMU in continuation of the support they provided in 2017, 2018 and 2019. The bulk of the project focuses on FSL and WASH activities. We will deliver timely lifesaving food assistance and livelihoods support in the form of seeds and tool distributions, training in farming practices and agribusiness, and livestock care. In terms of WASH, we will increase access to safe water, distribute hygiene kits and deliver hygiene promotion activities and trainings in latrine construction, borehole maintenance and water management. Furthermore, women and girls in all five target locations will be supported with dignity kits and menstrual hygiene education to be empowered to manage their periods safely, confidently and with dignity. The project promotes peaceful coexistence and peace-building as an important facilitator to the humanitarian-development nexus to move beyond crisis to resilience. An innovative aspect of the project is the use of solar powered Digital Audio Players uploaded with prerecorded messages in the relevant languages of the affected populations. ACROSS has been using DAPs to support learning, awareness raising and community dialogues in hard to reach communities for over 10 years. These devices do not depend on electricity, batteries or radio signal and can be listened to over and over again by 20+ listeners at a time, for deeper and wide-reaching learning and awareness raising, since the DAPs can be shared with neighbouring communities.
ACROSS recently used DAPs with high success to promote Ebola prevention (and other cross cutting messages) in inaccessible communities at risk in Morobo County. In this project, the DAPs will be uploaded with relevant training content from the FSL, WASH and Peacebuilding trainings, and will also include messages on conflict resolution, peaceful coexistence, nutrition, human rights, gender equality, relevant government policies, and any other messages tailored to each community. The ACROSS Media Department will prepare and translate scripts and record them in local languages in a story-telling approach most appropriate for the target communities (including for child audiences).

1 - Needs

The cumulative effects of South Sudan’s decades of civil wars, intercommunal conflicts, coupled with natural/climatic shocks and economic collapse has left many communities in South Sudan in dire need of humanitarian assistance in all sectors. This project targets 5 areas: Kassingor, Kapoeta North and East, Lainya and Morobo. The Jie community were displaced from Boma to Kassingor in 2018 due to violent conflict with Murle tribe. Subsequent shocks including 2 large-scale fires have undermined resilience. Kassingor is very remote, only accessible from Boma or Kapoeta in the dry season. Only 3 agencies support: RMF (nutrition), the Carter Center (guineaworm eradication) and ACROSS in FSL & WASH. In 2019, ACROSS responded to the crisis with FSL and WASH assistance. In September 2019 our project staff was advised the current needs and priorities as identified by the community are: (1) health - no health facilities, skilled personnel or access to medications; (2) access to water as well as hygiene and dignity kits remains an urgent concern, especially for women (3) access to food & FSL. Our staff also observed protection concerns, particularly around water collection and lack of sanitation facilities and menstrual hygiene kits. These needs are reflected in the needs assessments and mapping done by the clusters and agencies such as IPC projections, REACH assessments and HRP mapping. Pibor county is anticipated to be Emergency (IPC4) for food insecurity as household food stocks start to deplete and livestock products and wild foods reduce seasonally during the lean season Jan-April 2020. Kapoeta East: ACROSS is currently delivering Sida-funded integrated FSL, WASH and Nutrition activities in remote communities in far east Kapoeta East, and will continue in 2020. In Kapoeta East, cattle raiding and disease outbreaks, as well as climate shocks have increased community vulnerability to food insecurity. Severe lack of access to water results in women walking overnight in search of water. Nanyangacor, Kauto and Lotimor payams are remote and difficult to access and lack infrastructure and humanitarian support. IPC levels are high in food insecurity and malnutrition; water and hygiene remains an urgent concern as well as protection, as reflected in various needs assessments. Kapoeta North: is classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) due to the recent floods in July as well as frequent cattle raiding. The situation is further worsened by reduced physical access to markets and poor purchasing power, with a projection of remaining in Emergency Phase to April 2020. The current and projected malnutrition rates in Kapoeta North are alarming. The fact that the major driver of high malnutrition in Kapoeta North is poor dietary intake, there is absolute need to integrate food security and nutrition activities. There is a high need for diversifying livelihoods, so that cattle keeping can be complemented with farming, and providing training on agribusiness to boost agricultural livelihoods. Although several humanitarian agencies operate in Kapoeta North in health, nutrition and protection and FSL, needs remain extremely high and are projected to worsen. Furthermore, ACROSS is aware that there are large sections of the population not currently receiving humanitarian assistance and importantly, there is a high need for safe access to drinking water (integrating WASH with Protection concerns). These needs and concerns have been highlighted in REACH WASH assessments from Aug 2019 and the WASH and Protection Clusters. Finally, access to safe water is urgently needed in Morobo, whereby conflict has resulted in destroyed/looted water points. In Lainya, ACROSS has been delivering WASH interventions in three of the six payams, but need to extend the assistance to other locations if security situation allows access. Women and girls lack menstrual hygiene kits, and widespread open defecation is a cause of disease spread.

2 - Activities

This project consists of a multi-sector approach in FSL, WASH, Nutrition and Protection to deliver integrated outcomes. ACROSS through this humanitarian intervention will: > Distribute food items 33,000 people or 5,500 households (1500HH in Pibor, 2000HH in KN, 2000HH in KE) during the lean season to the most vulnerable affected households. This will improve the physical and mental well-being and reduce morbidity, mortality & suffering (HRP SO1 and FSL CO1). > Provide complementary livelihood support by distributing seeds and tools to 2750 households (16,500 people) (750HH in Kassengor, 1000HH in KN & 1000HH in KE) in order to boost crop production (2020 HRP FSL CO2) and address dietary diversity - the major underlying cause of malnutrition - with vegetable production. ACROSS will deliver practical training on basic agronomic practices, agribusiness and post-harvest handling to build capacity and resilience that will in turn enable communities recover and absorb shocks (2020 FSL CO3 and HRP SO3) and establish kitchen gardens at sites near boreholes to encourage vegetable production during the dry season. > Given the cattle keeping communities in target locations, ACROSS will deliver animal health trainings;> To complement the FSL trainings, ACROSS will provide communities with solar powered digital audio players (DAPs) loaded with prerecorded messages (in the relevant language/s) of training content. The messages on the DAPs can be listened to repeatedly in groups (20+ listeners) to consolidate learnings, and furthermore can be shared between households and communities, to enable widespread and consistent knowledge sharing and awareness raising. The DAPs will be loaded with cross-cutting messages on other themes such as peaceful coexistence and peace-building, protection (preventing GBV, gender equality, child protection etc), hygiene and health, nutrition and others. Furthermore, DAPS can be uploaded with more messages throughout the project cycle if other learning needs or topics of interest are identified. This will create a lasting impact and contribute to the integration and sustainability of the project outcomes. > ACROSS will offer nutrition services of screening and referral of malnourished children and lactating and pregnant mothers to the nutrition lead or focal agent in the locations of Kapoeta East and Kassengor, knowing one of the major causes of continuously high malnutrition rates is the difficulty accessing the target locations. Since ACROSS has staff already on the ground in these locations, we will can complement the nutrition program with screenings and referrals, and in some instances support partners with access to these areas to distribute supplements and transport urgent cases to the nearest treatment centre.> The project will drill 10 new boreholes in various locations in consultation with communities to reduce protection risks and rehabilitate 12 existing boreholes. ACROSS will conduct hygiene promotion activities and provide communal hand washing units and hygiene kits (bucket, soap, water storage container etc) to vulnerable households. > Provide tools and deliver training in latrine construction> Provide reusable dignity kits to women and girls for sustainable and dignified menstrual hygiene management, and facilitate interactive menstrual hygiene education with skilled, trained female facilitators to women and girls in the target communities. >Facilitate workshops with community elders, men, women and other stakeholders to raise awareness of key protection issues, promote peaceful coexistence, conflict resolution, gender equality and prevention of gender based violence. > Form and train water users committees and train local pump mechanics in all the project locations. Borehole spare parts and maintenance tool boxes will be provided to communities in Kapoeta North, Kassengor, Morobo (already provided in 2019 to Kapoeta East and parts of Lainya).

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

ACROSS operates according to the SPHERE / Core Humanitarian Standard, which addresses the inherent human rights of the beneficiaries, and ensures and encourages active involvement of the affected population in the different phases of the project cycle. Being a faith based organisation, ACROSS project staff also have a strong moral imperative and motivation to deliver activities from a rights-based approach, and our positive relationships with local communities are a testament to this.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes - one of the three target locations, Kassengor, is specifically chosen to meet the needs of displaced populations (the Jie), which arose from the needs identified by the community in a inter-agency assessment from last year. More up to date needs and priorities were communicated to our project staff during a stakeholders' consultative meeting with the affected community in Kassengor last month. In the other four locations, ACROSS is currently engaged with target communities and their needs, ideas and perspectives have all informed this proposal. Women in all locations have requested support to manage their menstrual hygiene. In Kapoeta East, ACROSS constructed two boreholes which brought joy to the population in Kauto Plateau, however the demand is too high, causing the boreholes to break down regularly. The community have requested another two to be drilled in the Plateau region. In Kapoeta North, there are many boreholes that need rehabilitation, this was also requested by the community. During the Ebola Prevention activities conducted throughout September and October in Morobo, communities pleaded with our staff to help improve access to safe water. In Lainya, ACROSS is delivering WASH interventions in three target payams, the other locations were not able to be supported throughout 2019 due to inaccessibility caused by insecurity, however during periods of stability the communities have requested hygiene promotion, increased access to water and other WASH support.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes. The project is linked with the government's policy of working towards resilience in all humanitarian programming. We will incorporate messages on govt policies across a variety of ministries/departments on our DAPs and trainings, to raise awareness at community grassroots levels about government policies. Our projects are also always coordinated with and involve participation of local authorities and community governance systems and stakeholders.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, further to the above comment, this project is aligned with the Comprehensive Agriculture Master Plan (CAMP) - developed by Ministry of Agriculture and Food Security, Ministry of Livestock and Fisheries, and Ministry of Environment and Forestry.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes, for target locations in Eastern Equatoria and Kassingor, the communities are sufficiently safe/stable, although internal protection concerns, especially GBV, continue mainly related to lack of access to safe water and sanitation facilities. There are also ongoing cattle raiding occurring in Eastern Equatoria, but this isn't too disruptive to the overall safety/stability of the populations.In Lainya and Morobo, insecurity spikes at different times, at at different times certain areas become inaccessible due to occupation by armed forces and violent conflict. During periods of stability, these areas open up to humanitarian assistance. There has been recent insecurity throughout Morobo and Lainya during September and October 2019, and it is difficult to predict what the security situation will be like in 2020. Surges in insecurity lead to massive but temporary displacements of population, and when the stability returns, displaced populations return to their homes. Therefore, there is a lot of movement, which results in important needs in WASH and NFI support.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes; as mentioned above in the project activities section, this project will include trainings and distribution of solar powered Digital Audio Players with prerecorded messages from different sectors and cross cutting issues. These will enable repeated listening to training material and cross cutting messages to consolidate learning, and also enable knowledge sharing of consistent messages with other communities for long term sustained outcomes. Furthermore, this project will be complemented by a three year development FSL project (in Kapoeta East) to build longer term community resilience and reduce dependency on humanitarian support. Menstrual hygiene kits will contain menstrual cups, which can be used for 10+ years without any waste or other inputs, which will significantly empower the women and girl beneficiaries in the long term and contribute to environmental sustainability.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

ACROSS uses informal and formal beneficiary Feedback and Complaints mechanisms so that beneficiaries can choose the channel most appropriate for them. The project team provide regular opportunities to receive feedback and complaints through any possible channel, including through Church Committees, Local Community leaders and complaints desks at each project site. Feedback can be both oral and written, so that groups that are illiterate will not be excluded (the majority of our feedback and complaints are received verbally). Gender and protection issues (including not limited to cultural appropriateness of face-to-face communications between people of different gender and age groups), and what risks beneficiaries may face if it is found that they provided feedback or made a complaint will be taken into account. The following methods will be used: consultative meetings throughout the entire project cycle, which gives any community member the opportunity to raise concerns; beneficiary help desk during registrations and distributions; focus group discussions (during trainings/workshops), individual interviews during the field visits; regular open meetings with communities; and individual home visits, especially to the most vulnerable/marginalised community members such as women, girls and people living with disabilities, in order to ensure that their voices are being heard. ACROSS has female staff on field teams, to be sensitive to the needs of female beneficiaries. ACROSS also has an AAP officer dedicated to ensure quality AAP mechanisms are put in place, and that the community are made aware of their rights, appropriate treatment they can expect from ACROSS/humanitarian staff, and how to report any inappropriate behaviour and breaches of code of conduct.
