Project Summary

"IOM’s Protection project for the 2023 JRP connects two strategic objectives with activities that aim to benefit the most vulnerable Rohingya and host community persons, specifically to reach older persons, widows, single-headed households, persons with disabilities and persons with diverse sexual orientation, gender identity and expression, and to target authorities and partners in a way that does no harm, assists based on needs, and prioritizes respect for human rights, safety and dignity.
IOM will continue serving as the Protection focal point lead in seven camps, directly implementing most activities. IOM’s protection monitoring activities will facilitate beneficiaries’ meaningful access to all humanitarian assistance, dignity, and human rights. IOM will strengthen community-based protection through community engagement, awareness raising, sensitization, case management and specialized Protection services for individuals in need. IOM’s core counter-trafficking activities will be prevention, protection, and capacity building, which will be implemented in 17 camps and 10 Union of host communities in Cox’s Bazar district. These activities aim to reduce trafficking incidents and risks in the camps and host communities through the comprehensive activities that follow the governmental National Plan of Action. The Counter Trafficking activities will foster collaboration among stakeholders who respond to human trafficking cases. The aforementioned integrated approach to Protection strives to have sustainable impact and improve peaceful coexistence between various communities.
The research component that is now embedded in the IOM protection team will help tailor activities, institutionalize, and analyze the impact of IOM Protection and IOM’s general programming. The information that is gathered through the research team serves as a basis for monitoring and evaluation to ensure that IOM does no harm, and activities remain relevant and culturally appropriate. As well as being aware of the constantly changing dynamics that need to be considered.
For disaster risk reduction, IOM will work with a range of partners, including other Sectors, community-based protection networks in the camps and relevant authorities to identify extremely vulnerable individuals or households needing specialized assistance and preparation before a disaster. IOM will verify the needs of EVIs, coordinate services, and support other partners to mitigate harm during disaster response. IOM will continue leading the Protection Emergency Response Units (PERU).
IOM’s protection mainstreaming activities will promote a holistic lens, incorporating the age, gender, and diversity needs of the affected population in Cox’s Bazar into programming through capacity building, training, as well as activity monitoring and modification. Finally, IOM will continue to co-chair the Sector’s Anti-Trafficking Working Group (ATWG) and be a proactive member of the Protection Working Group (PWG), the Protection Task Team and other related working groups."

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"The Rohingya refugees in Cox’s Bazar continue to face formidable challenges, needing Protection and other life-saving humanitarian assistance. Among the 902,798 Rohingya population, 80% consists of girls, boys, women, and elderly men and women; 3.85% of individuals were identified with at least one specific need (UNHCR Population Datasheet) and 12% are persons with disabilities according to REACH Factsheet-2021. Frequently, refugees approach protection actors to have their disability status updated in databases and to access specific support. Limited self-reliance activities render the refugees highly dependent on humanitarian assistance and with unmet needs. Violent incidents and conflict have occurred between and within Rohingya and host communities. Influential community leaders, who extort, physically punish, and psychologically coerce the refugees, grew more controlling of humanitarian services. Recent trends indicate that gang culture, petty theft, abduction, and similar other crimes have risen. Gangs seemingly carried on without impunity, gaining influence and illicit profits and instilling wide-spread fear among the community. The unsafe camp conditions frustrate many refugees who told IOM that they want a community-based approach to help them combat such threats. Noted by IOM protection monitoring, refugees who rented land from host community families struggled to pay increased rental fees. HLP issues were particularly vexing in Camps 19, 24 and 25, where IOM observed that families, who struggled to pay higher rents, started to send their children to work in houses, grocery shops and restaurants. Barbed wire fencing that encircles all camps restricted the unauthorized movement of refugees outside the camp, limiting the scope of work available to them. The fencing also impacted several host community families, who lived inside the camps, restricting movement and fomenting dissatisfaction and evictions of refugees. IOM’s protection monitoring highlighted new needs caused by the government’s relocations of the refugees to Bhasan Char and the monsoon flooding across many camps. Affected refugees experienced family separation, disrupted humanitarian services, secondary displacement and upsetting moments. The research team builds on different research initiatives and works closely with other areas of programming to ensure that protection is mainstreamed, and proper considerations are being made. The need is constantly evolving and is mainly dictated by the community. Through continuous interaction with the community and the Rohingya researchers, IOM is able to identify priority areas of intervention. There is a need to continue counter-trafficking efforts through awareness raising and capacity building of government authorities and humanitarian actors, and victim identification and assistance. Some trafficking victims identified by IOM said they left the camps because of limited livelihood opportunities. According to IOM’s case data, 44% of the caseload included Rohingya women, girls, and boys and 56% of the cases were Bangladeshi men, women, and children trafficked for mainly forced labor. Self-reliance through skills development and income generation for Rohingya and Bangladeshi trafficked victims along with community awareness sessions and capacity-building training for stakeholders are needed to address and prevent human trafficking. Community-based protection activities that promote inclusion, participation, and meaningful access, and measures to mitigate harm are necessary for Rohingya and host communities. The vulnerabilities and thus the various needs of Bangladeshi and Rohingya men, women, boys and girls require a comprehensive and fortified Protection approach while enhancing dignity and fulfillment of human rights and capacities at the individual, household and community level."

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

"IOM uses an integrated Protection approach to respond to the needs of vulnerable children and their families, GBV survivors, victims of trafficking, and other persons who experience harm or are at high-risk. Specifically, to strengthen community-based approaches and increased meaningful access to information, protection services, and other humanitarian assistance for men, women, boys, and girls. IOM will continue to serve as the Protection focal point in Camps 9, 18, 19, 20, 20x, 24, and 25, coordinating protection activities to improve Rohingya refugees’ access to protection services and other humanitarian assistance, strengthening community-based protection mechanisms through a network of 121 Rohingya volunteers and dedicated Rohingya-led protection committees and by conducting risk mapping exercises with the community; identifying and assisting Extremely Vulnerable Individuals (EVIs) and engaging with the community to raise awareness on protection risks and threats; and to enhance knowledge and skills to mitigate the risks and strengthen self-capacities and reliance. Conduct protection monitoring activities (Direct observations, key informant interviews, focus group discussions) and analysis of the ever-changing situation in the camps and select host communities to mitigate risk and threats, while informing humanitarian operations. Direct observation, key-informant interviews and focus-group discussions with beneficiaries are the standard methods that IOM will use to collect data for protection monitoring analysis. The protection monitoring findings will be coordinated with the Protection Sector to compile an analysis for all camps. "

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

"IOM Protection continues to incorporate protection mainstreaming as a cross-cutting issue in coordination with other sector units at IOM such as Health, WASH, Shelter/NFI, and Site Management and through its specialized sub-units (Protection, Child Protection, GBV, and Counter-Trafficking) providing orientation, capacity building and training to community-based networks. IOM contributed input into the protection mainstreaming tools developed by the PWG and IOM protection staff were available for protection mainstreaming capacity initiatives that the PWG has established with other sectors. The protection mainstreaming sessions focused on the protection mainstreaming principles which included meaningful access, safety, and dignity of beneficiaries, participation and empowerment, and accountability. The sessions with the non-protection partners allowed IOM Protection to draw protection mainstreaming work plans with key service providers from WASH, Health, food assistance, and shelter."

3.2 - Gender

Gender mainstreaming is identified as a key strategy to achieve gender equality across programmes and policies. Each sector strives for gender balance of IOM staff, both office, and field-based teams. IOM is an active member of the Gender in Humanitarian Action (GiHA) Working Group to promote good practices and learn from each other on emerging concerns and key challenges in gender mainstreaming and promoting women´s participation in humanitarian programming and camp management in line with a do no harm approach.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

With Age and Diversity considerations IOM is aware of the various needs of different age groups and, thus as part of its protection monitoring activities, it seeks to discuss with elderly persons and persons with disabilities the unique challenges that they face, whether it is mobility issues, access to services, porter support, adequate specialized humanitarian agencies active in the area, involvement in cash for work/livelihood activities or being involved in the collective community decision making. To ensure child safeguarding, the IOM General Protection team works collaboratively with Child Protection Focal Points in the camp to promote a child-friendly and accessible environment for all. IOM actively participates in Age and Disability Working Group (ADWG) providing practical inputs and briefings on IOM’s engagement in projects tailored to better promote inclusion and tailor assistance and support to persons with disabilities.

3.4 - Diversity

IOM seeks to hear a diverse range of voices from the community and is responsive to its needs. The interventions planned under this project are identified through a consultative process where different needs, capacities, and priorities of diverse people like persons with disabilities, the LGBTIQ community and others were considered, and the assistance is targeted to the persons and groups most in need. IOM actively promotes Age, Gender, and Diversity (AGD) in all kinds of project engagement and work to ensure inclusion of person with disabilities.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Protection activities Accountability to Affected Populations initiatives will continue with specific Rohingya groups and surveys with individuals who will receive Protection services to assess satisfaction level and receive feedback. The Community Feedback Mechanism, which IOM Site Management launched is promoted by IOM Protection during FGDS, KIIs and outreach activities. IOM Protection checks in daily with the CFM desk about complaints requiring action along with beneficiary satisfaction feedback (BSF) to ensure quality service to the affected people. IOM Protection Monitoring activities are another way that IOM is accountable to affected populations because IOM uses the findings to improve programming. IOM is the co-chair of the PSEA Network and continues to rely on the IOM PSEA Champions in the camps and conduct training about sexual exploitation and abuse and the obligatory SEA reporting system for staff and partners in the field.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

IOM will contribute protection technical expertise to the disaster preparedness and emergency response by collaborating with camp-based sectors and agencies, focusing on unique needs of EVIs. IOM will identify EVIs in need of additional support or relocation because of disaster-related risks and conduct outreach to develop and reaffirm individual and household preparedness and response plans, prior to disasters. IOM will serve as a focal point in the Protection Emergency Response Units (PERUs). After a disaster, IOM will assist beneficiaries with re-establishing meaningful access to humanitarian services, monitor protection trends, including any changes to the human trafficking context, for new or emerging threats and risks, and apply mitigation measures.
