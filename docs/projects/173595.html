Project Summary

Newly displaced, existing IDPs and returnee households continue to be reliant on humanitarian support to enable them access to timely and suitable shelter solutions. Mercy Corps propose a tri-folded shelter intervention to provide a most appropriate shelter solution to meet the various needs of newly displaced at reception centres and/or at transition sites, existing IDPs in over-congested camps and the IDPs living with cost communities, returnees and the host families themselves. Mercy Corps focus will remain to continue providing customized shelter and S&S NFIs assistance in Ngala, Dikwa, Gwoza, Bama, Monguno and Damboa LGAs. Damasak town (Mobbar LGA) and Konduga LGAs will be added as new areas considering the gaps and needs found in rapid need assessments carried out recently. Mercy Corps will ensure to harmonize the assistance packages aligned to shelter CCCM sector guidelines in these locations. Assistance packages will include; a) A customized new arrival kit comprising basic emergency shelter materials and S&S NFIs for 1000HHs. Materials will include, tarpaulin, timber poles, rope, nails and fixtures) paired with household utensils such as bedding, blankets, floor mat, mosquito net, kitchen set, jerry cans, buckets, basin, solar torch). A communal tool kit – shared by up to five households – will also be provided, including necessary items like shovels, claw hammers, and saws. b) A tailor-made NFIs replenishment assistance for 3000 HHs c) Mercy Corps will also provide tailor-made assistance for vulnerable households in camp settings to access the needed shelter materials such as timber poles, tarpaulin sheets, rope, and nails to maintain their deteriorated shelters. d) To assist existing IDPs and returnees living in host communities, a shelter repair package will be provided to 2,000 HHs (IDPs, returnees, and host community) living in poor sheltering conditions (i.e. unfinished, abandoned, or damaged buildings). Repair package will include access to needed materials (cement, sand, mud blocks, cement blocks, timber, doors and windows with fixtures), technical assistance (advice training, supervision), skilled labourers and HLP support. e) 1,500 overcrowded HHs will be assisted with partitions or privacy screens to increase privacy and reduce protection risks for women and girls. These partitions will ensure social distancing in COVID-19 pandemic context for overcrowded families. f) Distribution of 1000 energy efficient fuel stoves to female-headed households (IDPs, returnees and host) on account of massive resource degradation and skipping meals due to insufficient access to fuel sources. Extensive technical assistance will be an integral component to all the aforementioned assistance packages that will ensure appropriate utilization of the assistance provided.

1 - Needs

The Shelter CCCM sector for HRP 2021 aimed to target 1,587,664 people in need, among them 66% are IDP, 1% are returnees and 33% are host families. For the HRP2020 funding target, the Financial Tracking System (FTS) shows that the emergency shelter & NFIs sector is significantly underfunded that will lead to subsequent deterioration in the shelter situation of the people already living in poor conditions. As of November, 2020, only 4% funding has been received against 72M USD the total funding target for the emergency shelter & NFIs sector. Humanitarian needs in Borno state remain high and concerning as 19,780 households lack shelter, while another 78,562 households are in dire need of NFI assistance. Further scale up urgently needed given the increasing multiple displacements prompted by the deteriorating security environment and other socioeconomic factors for new and existing displaced people. Most in need among them are those who are sleeping in the open, without shelter, living in extremely damaged shelters or sharing shelters with other households resulting in over-congestion that leads to potential SGBV and protection risks and also expose to COVID-19 outbreak. There is an urgent need to construct more shelters to accommodate 24,596 IDPs who are sleeping outside or sharing spaces and 6,653HHs who are living in reception centres in Dikwa, Bama, Konduga, Pulka and Banki LGAs. Shelter/NFIs needs are continuously generated by forced displacement due to continuously deteriorating security situation, heavy rains and poor living conditions in the North-East Nigeria. Shelter CCCM sector reports that the majority of the affected populations in need are Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) in camps, IDPs in host community and returnees who are living in makeshift shelters or shelters that are either partially or completely damaged and affected by heavy rains and waterlogging in the garrison towns. Most of the IDPs with no access to shelters has left no option other than sleeping in open and constrained to use inadequate materials to construct provisional shelters. These HHs also have limited access to NFIs and fully rely on humanitarian assistance to survive (UNOCHA humanitarian situation - updated on 26th November report). LGA level damage assessment has been carried out in the camp settings across Brono state by the CCCM partners during October 2020 to assess shelters repairs needs, results show that Dikwa LGA remains among the most in need of decongestion and shelter maintenence/up-gradation assistance, with 1614 damaged shelters, while 5,879 HHs are living in makeshift shelters. In Monguno LGA alone there are 5158 HHs in need of urgent assistance living in damaged shelters with another influx of 18,283 HHs living in improvised shelters within the camps – and in Ngala, 5,572 HHs live in improvised shelters inside the camps and 3884 households in need of urgent shelter upgrades. In Konduga LGA, 3,439 HHs live in makeshifts while 899 in damaged shelters. 873 HHs live in damaged shelters in Damboa, another 1,713 HHs in Gwoza and 319 HHs in Bama. Damasak witnessed an increase in the influx of returnees resulting in the congestion of the temporary camps where over 400HH reside in makeshift shelters while over 855 HH remain without shelter, hence sleeping in the open. In the camps, sporadic fire incidents since January 2020 to date has destroyed some 39,106 shelters across camps in Muna Garage, Ngala, Mafa and Monguno.

2 - Activities

1. To meet critical life-saving shelter needs of new arrival IDPs with prepositioned resources and specialized mobile distribution team. Mercy Corps aims to provide assistance to 1000HHs for basic emergency shelter materials and NFIs i.e. timber poles, rope, nails, shovels, claw hammers and household utensils such as bedding, blankets, floor mats, kitchen sets, mosquito net, solar torch, jerry cans, buckets, basin, and a fuel-efficient cooking stove. 2. Mercy Corps will provide tailor-made shelter repair assistance to 1500 households in camps and/or informal settlements who urgently require repairs to existing shelters in poor conditions. Mercy Corps will be working closely with the Shelter CCCM sector at the state level and the concerned camp management actors in Gwoza, Dikwa, Ngala, Bama, Damboa, Konduga and Monguno LGAs to identify the most in-need families. In each of the target camps, respective camp maintenance committees will be mobilized to support Mercy Corps technical team to identify the needs, prepare tailor-made BOQs and supervise the repair works. Mercy Corps will work with the CCCM sector to validate the referrals for this assistance. 3. MSNA 2020 showed that 59% of HHs are living in partially damaged shelters and overall 80% of households across Borno state are in need of roof repairs [1]. In Bama 79%, Gwoza 85%, Dikwa 74%, Damboa 43%, Damasak 53%, Konduga 46%, Monguno 39% and Ngala 30% of the HHs are in need of repairs to their partially damaged shelters. 6% in Gwoza are living in completely damaged shelters. In these locations, with the help of Shelter CCCM sector, Mercy Corps will provide customized shelter repair assistance to 2000 vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host families living in inadequate shelters (i.e. make-shift or damaged) with the pre-identified critical materials needed to create a dignified shelter solution in accordance with their needs. MSNA 2020 shows 12% are living in emergency shelters, 7% in makeshifts, 28% in masonry houses, 1% under the open and 37% in traditional mud houses and another 13% in transitional-mud block mixed shelters. 4. Mercy Corps shelter interventions also focus on ensuring the privacy, security, and dignity of all household members. However, an additional problem concerns lack of privacy for women and girls within shelters. The provision of partitions or privacy screens will help overcome this. An overall 4% shelters are shared by at least two families with 7-12 individuals and 22% of the HHs share the shelter with the members from different HHs, Damboa 30% and Konduga 27% remain in high need of privacy for women and girls. In Bama 30%, in Gwoza 11%, and in Damboa 7% of the shelters are shared by 2 families (7-12 individuals). Mercy Corps will target single women, people with disabilities, and unaccompanied children, to maintain their privacy whilst being protected from stigmatization or targeting. A restricted e-voucher worth 5USD will be provided to access the materials such as rope and locally made thatch or the plastic sheeting.5. Blankets, sleeping mats, mosquito nets, solar torch and jerry cans are the major NFI needs as shown in the MSNA2020. 83% of the HHs are in need of blankets while demand for mosquito nets and the sleeping mates remain as high as 76% across Borno state. Dikwa, Ngala, Bama, Gwoza and Damboa are among the most in need in terms of NFIs. In Bama only, 89% lacks blankets, 94% in need of sleeping mats and 91% demand mosquito nets. In Gwoza, 94% show who lacks blankets. Looking at the MSNA results, it shows customized replenishment assistance will help to alleviate the situation. 3000 HHs (IDPs, returnees and host) in Gwoza, Bama, Damboa, Dikwa and Ngala LGAs will receive assistance to replenish the NFIs needs from a list of pre-identified items. 6. To overcome this cross-cutting protection, food security and nutrition problem, Mercy Corps will distribute 1000 locally made energy-efficient fuel stoves to female-headed households.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Gender and protection mainstreaming will remain at the heart of the shelter & settlement assistance. During the targeting, priority will be given to families sheltering separated/unaccompanied children (below 18 years old), household headed by a woman - priority to be given to widows who lost their husband due to recent conflict, households headed by children (below 18 years old) . To help improve the privacy and safety of the women and girls, lockable doors and windows will be incorporated in the design of the shelters. The scope of the repairs will consider the safety of the children, accessibility needs of the people with special needs and privacy and safety of the women and girls. Female headed HHs will be provided with the fuel-efficient stoves that will minimize the SGBV risks associated with fetching firewood from the bush. Privacy screens will be provided to overcrowded families that will also minimize the risk of SGBV. Elderly, women and children headed HHs will be prioritized throughout and receive additional unskilled labor assistance when others are supposed to contribute. Furthermore, field supervisors, daily hire workers and the field staff will be trained on DNH principles. Mercy Corps shelter interventions indeed focus on ensuring the privacy, security, and dignity of all household members, however, an additional caseload for the families has access to a liveable shelter yet lacks privacy for women and girls. These partitions will also help in COVID-19 pandemic context for overcrowded families.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

As described above, Mercy Corps has developed and maintains Community Relief Committees (CRC) as its primary community mobilization mechanism, with representatives of diverse age, gender, ethnic, religious, and socio-economic groups, including displaced and host communities, so that their concerns are heard and addressed and to avoid community tensions. CRCs have at least two female members, helping to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse of people seeking shelter and services. Mercy Corps’ Community Accountability Reporting Mechanism (CARM) will be continued in the RAI Borno 2 project to enable a safe and effective feedback/complaint system for bene\0ciaries and non-bene\0ciaries. The CARM includes a community-based feedback mechanism developed by Mercy Corps in Gombe in 2014 that allows people (in particular non-literate bene\0ciaries) to give feedback verbally and with discretion. Complaints will also be gathered through designated community focal points and through the use of a logbook updated by the focal point within each CRC, with electronic recording, analysis and follow up of all complaints within a speci\0ed timeline by Mercy Corps’ M&E staff. CARM will also be used to monitor protection issues, and issues will also be monitored under the Protection-sector speci\0c programming described below; that information will be used to reduce existing and newly identi\0ed risks. The program will monitor the security situation to adjust its implementation approach if the security situation deteriorates.
