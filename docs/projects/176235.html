Project Summary

The South Sudan NGO Forum aims to support International and National NGOs in their ability to provide a principled, coordination and effective response across the country. The NGO Forum is established as a project through Concern Worldwide as the Host Agency, and overseen by a Joint Steering Committee of 10 INGOs and 10 NNGOs. Through this project, the NGO Forum will work to enhance the capacity of the humanitarian system through improved information management and analysis, and representation and coordination of NGOs with government, donors, UN agencies and the IASC cluster system.
The project will focus on five specific areas in line with the NGO Forum’s 2021-2023 strategy: 1. Coordination, information sharing and representation of NGOs providing humanitarian assistance, 2. Policy engagement and external representation relevant for humanitarian assistance, 3. safety, security & access support, 4. capacity development for NGOs with a focus on localization, and 5. Enhanced accountability and participation for affected populations. These four areas will support the CCS sectors objectives 1, 2 and 4
The Forum is a voluntary, independent networking body of around 400 national and 120 international NGOs that support its members to effectively respond to the humanitarian and development needs and demands in South Sudan across the four project areas outlined above. The project is intended to closely align with existing coordination efforts by OCHA, clusters, assessment agencies and other NGO Safety Actors and the NGO Forum will work closely in 2021 to ensure that there is effective coordination across the entire response without duplication of coordination efforts or services.

1 - Needs

The formation of transitional government of national unity in February 2020 has provided some hope in term of political stability and reduced conflict related incidents however, the overall progress on implementation of peace agreement has remained slow, and localised violence has worsened, creating significant challenges in accessing populations in need of assistance. According to the Humanitarian Need Overview, 8.3 million people in South Sudan estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2021. South Sudan continue to be one of difficult humanitarian context for humanitarian organisation in term of limited infrastructure, access constraints due to insecurity and political and bureaucratic impediments. With the scale and the complexity of the crisis, there are a significant number of NGOs working across the country delivering lifesaving humanitarian assistance. Experience in the past years has highlighted several critical challenges that these organisations face in trying to deliver effective and principle humanitarian assistance. 1. Information access and coordination: Analysis of the coordination architecture by ACAPs and operational NGOs has highlighted that there is a significant amount of information and work ongoing in South Sudan, but coordination remains a challenge and information sharing is not strong enough for the response. NGOs also face difficulties in accessing up to date information from government and other stakeholders with paper circulars and letters far more common than digital communications. To ensure that the response remains effective, there is a need for a high level of coordination and further capacity to assist in information sharing and coordination amongst agencies. 2. Safety, security & access constraints: 2020 saw an increase in the numbers of direct and indirect attacks on humanitarians in South Sudan, and safety issues have continued to hamper access across the country. The 2019 Mid Term Review of the NGO Forum highlighted a need to continually advocate for the protection of humanitarian space and operations to be respected for the interest of the populations of South Sudan. OCHA and the NGO Forum access reports highlight an increasing threat towards humanitarian space, both in terms of the number of access impediments and the severity experienced. In 2020, several major response locations had to suspend delivery of regular assistance due to threats from local power brokers, and locations in or at risk of IPC Phase 5 populations were prevented from receiving lifesaving assistance due to security or political access restrictions. In addition, requests for formal or informal taxation, diversion of aid. Increasing attempts by state, non-state actors to instrumentalise aid for political purposes continue impacting the ability of actors to deliver lifesaving assistance. 3. Policy engagement & representation: with an increasingly complex crisis, humanitarian principles under threat and a number of national and global policies and interventions affecting the response, there is a need for coordinated consideration of key issues and ability for NGOs to present key view-points to the Government of South Sudan, UN system and global stakeholders. This is particularly important for national NGOs who often lack the networks to engage with relevant coordination mechanisms, obtain information and advocate for their beneficiaries. 4. NGO capacity: National NGOs play a critical role in being the first responders to provide humanitarian assistance due to local staff and presence. However, many lack access to the systems, training or networks of humanitarian organisations that international counterparts do and they risk lacking capacity to expand and improve their programmes. Resources for NNGOs to invest in training and organisational development remains limited perpetuating slow progress on localisation.

2 - Project activities

Specific Objective 1: Strengthen coordination and contextual analysis of needs conducted at national and sub-national levels and Specific 2: Enhance strategic response planning to facilitate a principled and well-coordinated humanitarian actionThe NGO Forum will deliver following activities to contributes toward above two sector objectives:1. Strengthened information sharing and coordination: The NGO Forum will continue to participate in all regular coordination meetings to represent members in strategic response planning. The NGO Forum will also work closely with other CCS actors and OCHA to jointly enhance the coordination system itself and minimise duplication. To support members, the NGO Forum will ensure that detailed information necessary for the humanitarian response is freely available to humanitarian agencies, including regulatory measures and information released by the Government of South Sudan, information pertaining to humanitarian operations, contextual information and other relevant information from coordination mechanisms, as well as providing information from agencies to these platforms. The activity will be carried out through daily, weekly and ad-hoc information updates, regular virtual and in-person meetings and briefing sessions and management of live communication platforms. The NGO Forum will work with other stakeholders to improve the archiving and digital access to all forms of humanitarian information to minimise duplication of assessments and studies, and to increase the impact of research and guidance documents on the humanitarian response, working closely with OCHA and other actors.2. Safety, Security & Access: The NGO Forum will provide safety and security services to members. This will include collating incident reporting on a daily, weekly and monthly basis, live updates and communications platforms, advice to members on safety, analysis of key trends, and reactive relocation or evacuation coordination. In addition, the NGO Forum will provide access support through a dedicated advisor, working closely with the Access Working Group and OCHA to both proactively and reactively minimise and address access challenges affecting operations. 3. Policy engagement & external representation: The NGO Forum will act as a coordination mechanism to represent NGO view to the Government of South Sudan, UN system, and other stakeholder on key issues affecting humanitarian response. Through specific briefings, ad-hoc meetings and policy work, this may include engaging with government on policies affecting humanitarian agencies, representing NGO view on issues such as durable solutions or returns, engaging with the UN Mission in South Sudan and advocating for principled funding of humanitarian assistance.CO-4 Enhance programme quality through strengthened Accountability to Affected Populations 4. Enhanced accountability & participation of affected populations: The NGO Forum will provide dedicated PSEA capacity who will support the development of a strategic plan and mechanisms for supporting INGOs and NNGOs in preventing and responding to SEA risks and incidents, helping to ensure that there is accountability of humanitarians to those they serve. This work will be coordinated through the PSEA Task Force to ensure a system wide approach to addressing the issue of SEA.5. Capacity Development: The NGO Forum will work to enhance the capacity of member NGOs to deliver high quality, effective, principled and accountable aid delivery to vulnerable populations in South Sudan. The capacity building for national NGO include but not limited to monitoring and evaluation, Accountability to Affected Population (AAP), conflict sensitivity, gender integration, protection integration, specialized training for senior management staff on communication, leadership and management, IT literacy with the purpose of enhance program quality through strengthened accountability to affected population.
