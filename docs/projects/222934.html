Project Summary

BRAC will deliver gender-responsive & comprehensive healthcare services in 13 camps of Ukhiya and Teknaf, Cox’s Bazar to ensure that Rohingya refugees have equitable access to essential primary healthcare services through 5 health posts (HPs) and 5 primary healthcare centers (PHCs), including SRHR, nutrition, MHPSS in line with the MoH and Health Sector recommended minimum package of essential health services. Health facilities HPs are located in camps 2E, 7, 9, 14, 22 and PHCs are located in camps 1E (2 PHCs), 1W, 8E, 13. Our CHWs will provide community health services and community messaging at the HH level, which also includes awareness sessions, case identification and referral to health facilities in camps 2E, 7, 8E, 8W, 9, 11, 13, 14, 15, 16, and 22.
PHCs will offer a BEmONC service within maternity service as NVD with 24/7 comprehensive services with ambulance, emergency service; OPD for consultation of communicable diseases and NCD; IPD service with 12 beds (8 female, 4 male); laboratory for variety of tests; and PAC service, ANC, PNC and Family planning including LARC, as well as Prevention of Mother to Child Transmission of HIV service & SGBV services (CMR and IPV). These PHCs will offer integrated services with Nutrition providing screening and IYCF-E counselling, MHPSS services, Adolescent Sexual and Reproductive Health (ASRH) services and physiotherapy services. PHC at Camp 13 will provide treatment services for Hepatitis C and be a referral point for Hepatitis cases. Moreover, PHCs in camps 1E, 8E, 13 provide cholera sentinel testing services and offer services as referral points for adjacent health facilities. HPs will provide services according to the MEHSP. Health facilities are staffed by Doctors, Midwives, Medical assistants, Nurses, dispensers, Pharmacists, psychologists, Lab technicians, Lab assistants, HIS officers, Physiotherapists, IYCF counsellors and Adolescent counsellors. Special focus is given to providing services and making health facilities inclusive for persons with disabilities considering all genders and providing assistive devices as per their needs.
The health programme also focuses on improving the health-seeking behaviour and health practices of the Rohingya refugees. BRAC has engaged community health workers to conduct regular household visits and refer eligible children and women to health facilities for immunization, ANC and family planning services. They also refer pregnant women to avail facility-based delivery by reducing widely practised home delivery.
Psychologists under MHPSS provide individual-level focused counselling services and community para-counselors provide individual-level basic psychosocial support to support the recovery of Rohingya people with mental health needs. Besides, community psychosocial volunteers (CPV) help raise awareness, prevent mental health difficulties, identify vulnerable cases, and make referrals.
BRAC have a robust MEAL system and according to the Program’s Logical Framework, develops MEAL plan including a monitoring Plan that specifies indicators, data disaggregated by sex, age and disability, methods of data collection and responsibilities for data collection and analysis. The performance monitoring tracking table has been developed based on Performance Indicator Reference Sheet to track the progress every single month and will augment the indicators proposed in the Monitoring Table.
BRAC has an updated and simulated contingency plan to respond to any health-related emergencies to ensure immediate response with uninterrupted services and to increase further capacity during disasters and any disease outbreaks. Additionally, mobile medical teams (MMTs) are prepared to respond during cyclones, floods, fire incidents and other emergency situations. BRAC will ensure infection prevention, medical waste management and control, embedded in the programme. Moreover, IPC committee focuses on the monitoring of IPC measures in each health facility.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

According to the Public Health Needs Assessment (PHNA) in the Rohingya camps 2024-25, about 64% of Rohingya people do not have knowledge to prevent common health problems in households. This clearly indicates the need for community awareness by CHWs. This is also visible from the survey that the major causes of medicine needs are NCD and pediatric diseases. The survey also indicates the top health conditions that have a high impact on public health status of Rohingya population are acute respiratory infection, skin disease, and diarrhoea. About 32% people or anyone in the family experienced emotional distress or trauma in last six months according to PHNA survey in 2024. However, mental health needs are clearly mentioned here to address the gap.In the Rohingya refugee camps, women and girls represent a significant proportion 51% of the population, which heightens their vulnerability due to cultural and gender norms. This has resulted in a significant rise in incidents of sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV), including intimate partner violence, rape, and child abuse. Additionally, they also faced serious challenges in accessing healthcare services. According to the Public Health Needs Assessment (PHNA) in the Rohingya camps 2024-25, around 45% of medical workers reported gender-related barriers in providing healthcare services in Rohingya community. Furthermore, 66% of healthcare workers did not receive specific training to address gender-related health issues.The Sexual and Reproductive Health Survey among Rohingya refugees in Cox’s Bazar 2024 by UNFPA and ICDDRB indicated that the average age of marriage is 17.5 years, with 19.5% of married women expressing a desire to marry later. Moreover, 55% of married women reported that they make joint decisions with their husbands regarding family planning and the contraceptive prevalence rate is 55%. Only 0.5% of male respondents use contraceptives themselves. Around 7% experienced teen motherhood. Only 10% of ever-married women were aware of all five danger signs in pregnancy. 49% of EMWRAs (Ever Married Women at Reproductive Age) had delivered their most recent birth at healthcare facilities. The ratio of women who had facility birth who were three times more likely to have PNC within two days of delivery than those giving birth at home is 69%: 21%. Social exclusion further complicates matters for marginalized groups, including the elderly, person with disabilities, and individuals of diverse gender identities, restricting their access to essential healthcare services.BRAC places significant emphasis on family planning services at all their health facilities, administered by trained midwives who provide short-acting and long-acting reversible methods. All BRAC staff undergoes compulsory annual gender orientation, while community health workers (CHWs) share awareness about SRHR and available GBV services. Male CHWs facilitate sessions for men in the community to sensitize them regarding SGBV issues and family planning methods. Psychologists, community para-counselors, and psychosocial volunteers offer mental health support and referrals for GBV survivors.CHWs gather feedback and suggestions from community members including men, women, and gender-diverse individuals during awareness sessions to enhance health service delivery and SGBV support.BRAC’s health facilities are committed to delivering high-quality services to individuals of all ages, genders, and diverse populations, in line with sector goals. The project aims to transform gender norms and relationships by engaging men, women, community leaders, and adolescents, fostering inclusive decision-making in health matters like childbirth and family planning. CHWs play a vital role in promoting health-seeking behaviour and equitable participation across gender lines.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

BRAC Health ensures services to all irrespective of age, gender and diversified community. In health facilities, BRAC considers protection issues to ensure safety and dignity of patients. Health facilities ensure separate waiting areas for males and females, maintain privacy in consultation rooms, separate toilets for males and females and separate wards for male and female patients in IPD of PHCs. BRAC facilitates SGBV referral pathway orientation to health staff for urgent referrals. BRAC community health team is also sensitized to identify cases of SGBV and refer them to health facilities, psychologists and case management focal of protection sector. Community para counselor (CPC) and community psychosocial volunteer (CPV) ensure mental health support to SGBV cases at HH level in camps.BRAC has been working closely with the protection sector where the health sector is providing survivor-centred services such as clinical management of rape (CMR) to survivors of rape, physical examination and treatment of cases of grievous violence. BRAC ensures all the health facilities have necessary alignments with the protection sector services according to the protection guidelines. BRAC has MHPSS team in PHCs where psychologists counsel the SGBV survivors for management of mental trauma. BRAC health team refers cases of SGBV to higher health facilities on a need basis. BRAC health facilities make referrals to legal actors while prioritizing the safety and confidentiality of the survivors throughout the process.

2.3 - Age & Disability

BRAC health facilities provide service and ensure accessibility for all irrespective of age sex gender, persons with disabilities. BRAC’s healthcare data management includes segregation of age, sex, gender and person with disabilities. According to public health need assessment 2024 report, 48% of PWD or anyone in the family experienced emotional distress or trauma in last 6 months. BRAC psychologists along with community para-counselors and community psychosocial volunteers provide counselling to persons with disabilities. The physiotherapists assess the types of disability in camp areas using the Washington Group Questionnaire on Disability and special focus is given to persons with disabilities by providing assistive devices as needed. Besides, wheelchairs are available at our health facilities for persons with disability to ensure persons with disabilities-friendly service. Physiotherapy service is offered to persons with disabilities by dedicated physiotherapists in PHCs. In collaboration with the health sector, health staff are participating in capacity-building initiatives to develop their knowledge and skills on disability inclusion.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to the Affected Population (AAP) is an integral component of a rights-based approach to programming-related response. It supports multiple rights, including the right to dignity, the right to protection, and the right to be heard and participate in decision-making. BRAC has introduced Exit Interviews with patients to determine their satisfaction level and seek their suggestions to improve the quality of health services. Every month, certain Exit Interviews are conducted among randomly selected patients to understand their satisfaction and dissatisfaction levels and suggestions for improvement. Their suggestions are considered to modify the health services as necessary.BRAC follows a few methods to receive beneficiary feedback on the services offered. Using CRFM (Complaint Response Feedback Mechanism), BRAC will deal with recommendations, suggestions, complaints and feedback. BRAC has installed a feedback box in each health facility to get service seekers’ complaints. Subsequently, it has taken initiatives to link them with the relevant sectors so that they can access necessary support when needed. A 24-hour dedicated health facility number is avaialble in health facilities to ensure access of the participants to any emergency healthcare services. Also meetings with religious leaders and community members on a quarter basis both for CHW & MHPSS components to address the emerging needs and feedback of the community.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

BRAC maintains a safeguarding umbrella policy and 5 subset policies including PSHEA policy, Child and adolescent protection policy, Whistle blowing policy, Prevention of Workplace Bullying and Violence Policy and Adult with special needs policy aimed at preventing PSEA and child abuse across all stakeholder interactions. BRAC has dedicated team of safeguarding champions (focal persons) who are continuously providing training to staff, programme participants and volunteers. It includes community health workers, community psychosocial Volunteer, Community Para Counselor, and facility volunteers (guards, cleaners) from the Rohingya and host community to prevent SHEA. Safeguarding is strengthened through diverse preventing measures, reporting and feedback mechanisms, such as confidential complaint boxes, 24-hour Helpline numbers and PSEA focal persons contact, and availability of survivor centric assistance to all community members. If any case is reported the PSEA focal is informed where the matter is dealt with centrally for investigation. BRAC shows zero tolerance against sexual exploitation and abuse. BRAC, within its policy emphasized on PSEA being a mainstreaming agenda. As a result, inside and outside of the health facilities in the Rohingya camps, the health programme is committed to ensure PSEA is mainstreamed. Building on its 2024 successes, BRAC will continue to prioritize safeguarding and PSEA in 2025. This includes training sessions for all staff, volunteers, vendors, suppliers, and service providers (including drivers and guards), awareness sessions for programme participants, and standing agenda of PSEA and Safeguarding in staff meetings. A robust response mechanism will remain in place to support survivors maintaining survivor centered approach, and CFRM will be maintained. Additionally, SEA risk assessments and dissemination of awareness materials will be ongoing.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

BRAC regularly budgets for the repair and maintenance of health facilities, considering risks of natural disasters or severe storms. This helps BRAC to be prepared for immediate response and restoration of healthcare services if such events occur. In terms of WASH facilities, these are connected to properly managed fecal sludge management (FSM) systems to prevent environmental contamination. Surrounding areas of health facilities are maintained with vegetation and gardens wherever feasible, supporting the ecosystem. BRAC also ensures that medical waste is effectively segregated and disposed of in an environmentally safe manner, following the best practices for waste management and disposal. Through these initiatives, BRAC’s health program contributes meaningfully to environmental sustainability and climate resilience.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

BRAC has an updated and simulated contingency plan to respond to any health-related emergencies to ensure immediate response with uninterrupted services and to increase further capacity during disaster and any disease outbreaks. BRAC has DRR subsector embedded within all the prorgramme to plan, design, and execute Emergency Preparedness and Response services. Additionally, BRAC is working with sectoral EPR and MMT WG to support during emergencies with the existing resources. Additionally, three mobile medical teams (MMTs) are prepared to respond during cyclones, floods, fire incidents, and other emergency situations. In case of national conflicts, turmoil, and disasters, BRAC has a well-documented SOP and contingency plan to continue the services on an emergency basis. BRAC has reserved and secured funds for the EPR and MMT for any emergency and urgent management.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

BRAC PHCs has dedicated adolescent counsellors who provide counselling to 15 to 19 years age group of the Rohingya community. CHW supervisors also conduct session with youth of 15 to 19 years old to discuss lifestyle issues, disadvantages of early marriage, menstrual hygiene, and contraception use. Additionally, BRAC also recruited volunteers of youth groups of the community as CHWs and other volunteers to engage them in community awareness and health promotion issues.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

BRAC has developed a sustainable strategy focused on building local capacities by ensuring community engagement and maintaining health facilities. Considering the repatriation of the Rohingya is unlikely in the near future, the BRAC approach is to ensure the long-term functionality of the facilities.To achieve this, BRAC will concentrate on capacity building for CHWs and volunteers. The training programme will cover essential healthcare services, including NCDs, maternal, and child health and psychosocial support and this effort will be complemented by the health awareness activities to increase community engagement and utilisation of the services delivered. To further sustain the intervention's, BRAC will actively involve the community local leaders and religious figures on educating the community about the importance of healthcare utilization, particularly for maternal and child health and NCDs, ensuring that health-seeking behaviours are sustained beyond the project's duration.
