Project Summary

UNRWA Technical and Vocational Education and Training Programme (TVET) will continue to provide short-term courses in Damascus, Dera’a, Homs, Aleppo and Latakia. As demand for TVET services grows, the Agency will continue its efforts to better link students with the job market in an effort to increase employability rates of graduates. Reconstruction/rehabilitation-focused courses, such as electricity, plumbing or masonry, will remain an area of focus. Despite significant challenges, the microfinance programme continues to provide a growing number of loans to Syrians and Palestine refugees in Syria. In 2020, the microfinance team will continue to provide inclusive financial services to Palestine refugees and Syrians requiring additional capital through the direct disbursement of loans to meet rising demand. The majority of the loans requested are consumer loans, which enable a household to cover unforeseen expenditures, such as the purchase of assets. Entrepreneur loans continue to be less in demand, reflecting the ongoing constraints of the Syrian economy. In 2020, UNRWA will continue to work to resume microfinance activities in areas of spontaneous return such as Sbeineh and Qabr Essit camps through the provision of consumer loans, business loans or youth and women focused loans. Special microfinance products focusing on women and youth will continue to be developed by UNRWA and will be further promoted in order to stimulate livelihood activities in recovering areas. Finally, and in response to the evolving needs of the Palestine refugees in Syria, UNRWA will explore the feasibility of conducting small-scale cash-for-work initiatives in 2020. Work opportunities will be provided to support the provision of current UNRWA services (health, and education), or to support the restoration of Agency services in newly accessible areas.

4 - Needs

Out of the 438,000 Palestine refugees currently estimated to be inside Syria, two-thirds have been displaced at least once since the beginning of the conflict. As of the end of 2019, an estimated 40 per cent of Palestine refugees continue to be displaced within Syria, with Yarmouk, Dera’a and Ein El Tal camps, previously home to more than 30 per cent of Palestine refugees in Syria, largely destroyed by the hostilities. Despite the challenging humanitarian situation in Syria it is expected returns in 2020 will increase. A socio-economic assessment of living conditions conducted by UNRWA in December 2017 estimated that 91 per cent of Palestine refugee households in Syria live in absolute poverty (less than US$ 2 person/day) prior to receiving UNRWA cash assistance. It also found that old age households, female-headed households, and households headed by persons with disabilities are three of the most vulnerable Palestine refugee groups and suffer from high unemployment rates and poverty. In 2020, lives of the Syrians and Palestine refugees from Syria continue to be marked by the loss of opportunities to pursue further education and employment, and hindered ability to establish their own businesses. Prolonged displacement further compounds existing instabilities, and entrenches unemployment, and loss of livelihoods opportunities within established Palestine refugee communities and camps. Livelihood interventions are critical to increase the resilience of Palestine refugees in Syria, who continue to endure the damaging impact of the conﬂict.

5 - Implementation of activities

The Agency will undertake the following activities in 2019: • Delivery of vocational training and career guidance services, which will provide Palestine refugee trainees with marketable vocational and technical skills, employability skills and computers and language abilities; • Provision by the Microﬁnance Department of a range of ﬁnancial products to Palestine refugees and Syrians. These include consumer loans, small business loans, as well as loans targeting women and youth; • When feasible, Cash for Work programme (CfW), will be designed to enable Palestine refugees who are most in need, to receive ﬁnancial support against work carried out under the supervision ofUNRWA programmes, such work will cover the gaps in labor force in UNRWA regular services (health and education) as well as the additional work needed to restore newly accessed areas (rubble removal, rehabilitation of facilities, WASH services…etc.)

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: Exposure to Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) for Palestine refugees participating in cash for work programmes especially those focusing on reconstruction and rehabilitation of UNRWA installations in newly accessible areas. Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigating measures: 1. Training to staff, contractors and community members including vocational education students and microﬁnance beneﬁciaries on reducing exposure to these risks will be undertaken. 2. Thorough assessments are undertaken prior to site selection. Protection risk: Risk of exploitation and abuse by staff members or service providers providing cash for work, microﬁnance, and vocational training opportunities. Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigating measures: 1. All UNRWA staff sign the code of conduct on commencement of their contracts. 2. Training to staff on the code of conduct and PSEA and regular reinforcement of the zero-tolerance policy as well as UNRWA staff standards of conduct, reporting obligations and channels for SEA. 3. Reinforced messaging to community members on the importance of reporting SEA and ensure mechanisms are in place to ensure conﬁdentiality and set up accessible and eﬃcient complaint mechanisms for beneﬁciaries to report concerns about sexual violence, exploitation, corruption or other abuses. 4. Frequent communication to recipient communities about the available services and clear criteria for accessing the same. 5. UNRWA will adapt work hours and locations, as appropriate, to reduce exposure to sexual violence. This may require shortening workdays to only daylight hours, including times for travel to and from work sites. 6. UNRWA has a mechanism to address SEA complaints including investigations and sanctions.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

A mix of humanitarian and post-conflict interventions: Whilst Damascus, Rural Damascus and most of the major urban concentrations in Homs, Hama, Latakia, and Dera’a experienced relative calm throughout 2019, hostilities continued in other areas of the country and the overall situation remained tense and unstable. In 2020, the Agency anticipates that a mix of humanitarian and development approaches that are supportive of post-conflict rehabilitation will be needed to respond to the different needs of Palestine refugees across Syria. High levels of humanitarian needs especially amongst the most vulnerable Palestine refugees: A socio-economic assessment conducted by UNRWA in December 2017 estimated that 91 per cent of Palestine refugee households in Syria live in poverty. Depreciation of the Syrian pound, rising inflation, increasing prices of basic commodities, destruction and damage of houses, and limited livelihood opportunities continue to undermine resilience and coping capacities, particularly for those most vulnerable.Increase in returns; a large proportion of Palestine refugees in Syria will remain displaced: the levels of displacement are foreseen to remain high among Palestine refugees, as access to Yarmouk is still restricted and the level of destruction there is significant. Returns to Dera’a and Ein El Tal camps, which have sustained major destruction, remain slow, due to the level of damage to basic infrastructure and the lack of public services. Despite the challenging humanitarian situation in Syria it is expected returns from out of Syria in 2020 will increase, as will HLP and CD needs. Secondary displacements is also anticipated among Palestine refugees due to their legal status. Palestine refugees in Syria will continue to face serious protection challenges: It is anticipated that protection risks will persist and may intensify in newly accessible areas where spontaneous returns, both from within and outside of Syria, are expected to increase.
