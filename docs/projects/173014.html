Project Summary

Libya continues to face an ongoing crisis since 2011, which has exerted a great burden on the country’s economy and services and put it in a state of volatile security and political instability. This has led to the displacement of hundreds of thousands of people to safer areas within Libya with the need for health and social services. Furthermore, the increase in COVID-19 cases added an extra burden on the fragile health system, already functioning with weakened disease surveillance, overwhelmed response capacities and a suboptimal level of public health preparedness.
Due to the challenges listed above, the International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies (IFRC) aims to support the Libyan Red Crescent Society (LRCS) through its Community-Based Health programme, and that is in seven areas, namely: AlKufra in the East, Azzawya, Sirt in the West and AlJufra, Ghat, Sebha and Ubari in the South.
The project described herewith aims to:
- Continue to support the health promotion efforts of the authorities in the prevention of communicable and Non-Communicable Diseases (NCDs) and mother and child health. Teams of Community Health Volunteers (CHVs) will provide health and hygiene promotion information and messages to returnees, Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), migrants and host communities through household visits, seminars and through social media and other media channels.
- Continue to support the LRCS in the deployment of medical convoys to identified areas where access and availability of basic health services are lacking.
- Provide a link to the health care system through the referral of patients to a network of Primary Health Care Centers (PHCCs) affiliated with the health authorities, international organizations and Red Cross Red Crescent (RCRC) Movement partners.
The International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies (IFRC) Libya Country Office (CO) located in Tunis has been supporting the LRCS in the planning, monitoring and implementation of its Community-Based Health programme since December 2018, and will continue to do so in the scope of this project.

1 - Needs

According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2021, 18% of people are in need of health care services for 2021 in Libya. The conflict has led to the displacement of hundreds of thousands of people since 2011. Most recently, the escalation of fighting in May of 2020 has led to new waves of displacement of thousands of people to the East. Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), refugees and migrants are the most vulnerable populations in Libya. As for returnees and host communities, they also suffer the economic, social and political repercussions of the conflict. Within the mentioned populations, the needs are greater for children, adolescents, the elderly, and for individuals suffering from chronic illnesses that make the disruption of healthcare services especially difficult for them. In terms of the healthcare system in Libya, the availability of functioning healthcare facilities is dwindling year after year, where it was reported that more than half of the health care facilities that were functioning in 2019 have closed and those functioning suffering from lack of electricity and fuel shortages. This is especially reflected in rural areas, where security threats are prevalent and national and health sector funding lacking. As for the highest burden of disease, it is carried by NCDs, which is reported to be 72% of the overall disease burden. Outbreaks of diseases such as diarrhoea, lice and scabies and influenza-like illnesses were reported in 77% of assessed communities. The same report indicates that child and emergency services are available for 40% of communities and general services are available for only 35% of communities. As for reproductive health services, only 15-20% of communities benefit from such services and the same is reported for NCDs and communicable diseases. This requires strengthening the referral system to meet the healthcare needs of people of concern. Moreover, the mobility restrictions imposed in light of the COVID-19 pandemic further reduced access to PHC services and has disrupted routine immunization services, which has led to the sounding of grave concerns that children are at high risk of contracting preventable, debilitating and life-threatening diseases such as polio and measles. As for mental health services, they are widely unavailable in Libya with stigma surrounding access to it. The pandemic has further stressed the need for more rapid response teams, PCR testing kits, Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) for healthcare personnel and responders, improved isolation and treatment facilities for COVID-19 patients, improved Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE), and the continuity of essential health services. For instance, Libya is seeing a rise in the number of COVID-19 cases, but the numbers, although high, still do not reflect the actual severity of the situation due to the weak testing and tracing capacities. The response to the pandemic lacked a unified and well-coordinated response plan across the country due to the dismantled governance structure. Improper management of logistics and supply chains caused shipments of essential medical supplies, equipment and life-saving children vaccines to be stuck at customs for weeks. Furthermore, the lack of clear communication around COVID-19 has led to individuals relying on social media platforms for information and to inter- and intra-communal tensions and a reported rise in xenophobia. Looking at healthcare needs in terms of geographical areas, the HNO reported that for the year 2021, 72% of municipalities are in areas ranked ‘3’ and above on the severity scale, distributed between the West, East and South of the country and specifically in Derna, Benghazi, Tobruk, Ejdabia, Jabal Al Akhdar, Nalut, Almargeb, Tripoli, Aljfara, Azzawya, Misrata, Zwara, Al Jabal Al Gharbi, Wadi Ashshati, Sebha and Ubari.

2 - Activities

This community-based health project will target the following geographical areas: East: Al KufraWest: Azzawya, Sirt South: AlJufra, Ghat, Sebha and Ubari The activities implemented through LRCS will be as follows: - Conducting Training of Trainers (ToT)/team leader trainings in six of the selected areas: AlJufra, Azzawya, Sebha, Ubari, Ghat and Sirt, using the trainers already available and trained by IFRC in Benghazi, Derna, Ejdabia and Al Kufra. - Conducting roll-out trainings by the ToTs in the six areas to Community Health Volunteers (CHVs).- CHVs from all ten branches of LRCS conducting health promotion activities focusing on communicable and non-communicable diseases and mother and child health, including immunization, through seminars, household visits, social media and other media channels.- Mobilizing communities, where team leaders and CHVs trained on delivering health promotion messages within their communities are responsible for engaging with stakeholders and mobilizing and forming community committees that will continuously engage with the target population and work on creating environments conducive to a healthy lifestyle. The committees will consist of the relevant stakeholders and volunteer representatives from the branch.- Referring cases identified in the community to PHCCs (LRCS-affiliated and others supported by Movement partners and others) capable of receiving referred patients to link the community with the healthcare system (service provided and financially covered by partners).- Deploying medical convoys in four municipalities lacking access to health care services (Umm Arrazam, Tazirbu, Gharb Azzawya and AlJufra) with basic services including paediatrics, internal medicine, dermatology, dentistry, ophthalmology, urology, and gynaecology.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

4.4 - Project results in Increased exposure to COVID-19 transmission. The project includes meetings with community members and stakeholders, household visits, health promotion seminars and the deployment of medical convoys. Each of these activities includes the risk of exposure to COVID-19 transmission. The LRCS, as per the SOPs of community interventions adapted based on the COVID-19 prevention measures, will be equipped with the appropriate PPEs and disinfection material that would be used during the physical encounters with people of concern. Activities will include a clear plan on how to ensure physical distancing between participants in health promotion activities. Where and if it is not possible to conduct an activity without minimising the risk involved in transmitting COVID-19, the activity will be substituted with the online modality or will utilise different media channels. 4.6 - Disclosure of sensitive personal data of affected persons, including safety and confidentiality of case management conducted in detention centres, leading to increased protection risks and further harm. Community Health teams and medical convoy teams will undergo a protection mainstreaming training focusing on confidentiality of patient data and referral mechanisms to specialised services should be coordinated and agreed on ahead of time with the relevant organization. 4.22 - Lack of access to/awareness of referral services for survivors of rights violations and other protection incidents (GBV, child protection, Explosive Hazards, eviction for example). The Community Health team and the medical convoy teams will be briefed on referral mechanisms employed by the LRCS in coordination with relevant partners. This mechanism is still unclear at this stage of developing the project document. However, the IFRC and the LRCS will be ready to play its role in the coordination channel between the Health and Protection sectors in Libya in order to address protection incidents.
