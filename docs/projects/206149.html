Project Summary

The proposed NRC’s Shelter interventions for 2024 is continued to support the people affected by conflict, natural disaster and displacement in emergency context and static as long-term support for the IDPs, returnees and host community to return, resettle and reintegrate with host community. The interventions are designed to address the emergency shelter / NFIs needs through rapid response mechanism (RRT) and emergency response and contribute to improve people’s protection and durable solutions through NRC static shelter response.
The proposed shelter interventions are to provide access to basic shelter services in underserved and hard to reach locations affected by disaster, disability inclusion and mitigating GVB and durable access to shelter among 125,000 IDPs, Returnees and host communities in South Sudan. NRC’s Shelter interventions will be integrated and implemented in close coordination with NRC’s other core competencies of WASH, Education, Livelihood and Food security, ICLA and protection programming and teams, applying an integrated approach to uplift people’s lives and contributing to South Sudan 2024 HRP and Shelter Cluster objectives.
The project aims to provide Survival Kits /Emergency shelter/Non-Food items both in-kind and in cash modalities to the most vulnerable persons, newly displaced/host communities, and returnees. The project will focus on the lifesaving shelter/NFIs needs, as identified through assessment to protect the affected displaced people from harsh weather conditions and protection risks targeting all states of South Sudan. Moreover, the project will provide static as long-term and durable shelter solutions to the IDPs, returnees and host communities who wanted to return, resettle, or reintegrate with communities. The first line intervention will be implemented through mobile response as well as using its static teams based in Kajo Keji, Juba, Koch, Mayom, Leer, Bor, Duk, Twic East, Akobo, Aweil, Wau and Alek. However, the major focus areas will be in Jonglei, Unity, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Central, Western and Eastern Equatoria states.
Cash programming will also be considered in areas where markets are fully or semi-functional and shelter/NFI items are available and accessible by the community. When blanket targeting is not applicable, the intervention will target mainly lactating and pregnant women, households with malnourished children, and households with members who have certain health complications. Besides unaccompanied children, unaccompanied elderly and persons with no community linkages will also be targeted.
The project will promote timely delivery of assistance through deployment of rapid response teams in line with humanitarian priorities for 2024 HRP's. NRC’s mobile response teams will be ready for deployment within 8 days to conduct assessments and within 22 days for full-fledged delivery of NFIs and emergency shelters and other activities per location.

1. Needs

The South Sudan Humanitarian Need Overview 2023 sectoral analysis revealed that; total of 3.0 million people will struggle to meet their basic shelter and NFIs needs due to continues destruction of houses and loss of essential goods from ongoing flood and conflict. The conditions are worsened by ongoing displacement in Tonga and ongoing flood and conflict mainly in Jonglie, Lakes, Upper Nile, Unity , Part of Western Bahr el Ghazel and Western Equatoria .Out of the total population in need of S/NFIs ; 58 percent IDPs, 33 percent returnees and 12 percent vulnerable host communities, (HNO,2023).The cluster differentiates the context into different contexts; including collective sites and other spontaneous sites; and relatively stable locations where people are returning to their former habitual residence. Out of the shelter cluster target; NRC will be supporting 150,000 individuals across the country including NRC’s first line emergency response through Emergency Rapid Response Mechanism and static as long-term shelter solutions. The cluster assumes 70 per cent of the displaced population in the country are in need of S/NFIs assistance. The targeted caseloads lack access to essential lifesaving NFI items and are often unable to support themselves through communal connections or coping mechanisms through other locally available resources. The limited and depleting natural resources such as timber and grass for shelter construction result competition and may generate conflict and localized violence and lead to multiple displacement. The markets are also lack essential items or have become non-functional, making it difficult for the community to access the required items for a safe and dignified life, (HNO,2023).Lack and Limited access to shelter may cause health and protection risks and poses a negative impact on the economic situation. The lack of privacy and protection risks, particularly for women and girls who usually go to the bushes to collect the increasingly scarce construction materials, exposes them to GBV. As a result of these compounding factors, the affected people have limited coping strategies. Some adopt harmful coping mechanisms, such as engaging in exploitative work, commercial sex, stealing and living under trees or in open shelters, exposing them to health and protection risks, (HNO,2023).In the recent assessment done in Renk in October 2023, there is new transit site identified by the local authorities for semi-permanent shelters to accommodate returnees from Sudan. In the new site there is a gape about 100 communal shelters to be constricted. While UNHCR, NRC and Plan International are constructing around 50 communal shelters but still there is a huge gap while returnees are still coming from Suda. Till now the total number of Returnees entered through Renk border is about 300,000 individuals and on daily bases about 1000 individuals are entering through Renk. In cases of returnees, where the areas of returns are stable, the returnees assumed to stay, rebuild their lives and able to cope with the existing/available resources. With support of shelter cluster, NRC will look into alternative support modalities, such as cash vouchers, training, and other engagement with communities. People’s S/NFIs needs are primarily linked to conflict, and natural disasters induced displacement, but at times also to food insecurity, economic deterioration and disease outbreaks. Thus, timely provision of shelter and NFIs support to the crisis affected population will be able to live a dignified life.

2. Project activities

The intervention will provide access to emergency shelter (in-kind or cash modality) to newly displaced people in a secondary displacement. The project will target 150,000 individuals for emergency shelters including those who will be supported by cash depending on the market functionality.• The project provides access to lifesaving NFIs (in-kind or cash) for newly displaced people in a secondary displacement. It will target 80,000 individuals for provision of NFIs including those who will be supported through cash.• The project provides cash / voucher support to rental solutions as first line shelter response. • Shelter upgrades (In-kind or cash) for returnees /protracted IDPs/Host Community and people in secondary displacement. • Construction of communal shelters for longer term transitional centers and settlement sites specifically in Renk. NRC will construct 80 Communal shelters with approximately @$25000 per shelter. • Construction / rehabilitation of shelter through owner driven approach. • Mainstream Housing, Land and Properties (HLP) and Collaborative Disputes Resolution (CDR) approaches throughout S/NFI response.• Mainstream GBV throughout all stages of S/NFI response.• Training communities/stakeholders on Accountability to affected population (AAP).• Establishment of Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFM).• Carry out Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) in two phases; Rapid Monitoring while distribution is carried out to receive immediate feedback from beneficiaries while the second one will take place two to four weeks after the distribution.
