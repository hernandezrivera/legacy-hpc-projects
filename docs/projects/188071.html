Project Summary

This project aims to improve the NES EiE response capacity to offer safe and accessible learning environments to conflict-affected school communities both in non-formal and formal education settings. The project is multi-faceted and targets challenges in educational provision, access and quality combined with integrated psychosocial support for students, caregivers and teachers, and referral to other projects under the same organisational response for vulnerable students and families.
The sub-districts targeted have a severity of 4, with the exception of Manbij which has a severity of 3 and Ar-Raqqa which has a severity of 5. Locations are chosen based on the number of OOSC determined in collaboration with the local Education Committees, information from the Education Working Group on the severity of need and the NGO's own door-to-door assessments. It is estimated that 15% of people targeted in this project are persons with disabilities.
This Education in Emergencies Humanitarian response in NES will create opportunities to ensure the reintegration of Out-Of-School-Children (OOSC) in education settings by providing non-formal education to build the confidence and skills (literacy, numeracy and social and emotional learning) required to integrate back into and remain in formal education. The response will support OOSC’s transition to the formal education system (primary or secondary, or to vocational training for older adolescents in the organisation's Livelihoods project or other locally available vocational opportunities where applicable) and promote the retention of students attending formal education but at risk of dropping out due to learning difficulties, attending schools in a lower grade for their age etc. The NFE class-based activities will be monitored through structured formative and summative assessments including baseline and endline assessments per cycle using EGRA/EGMA and SERAIS tools to review impact and identify areas for improvement in activities and instruction.
Additionally, due to gaps in school infrastructure and the safety risks posed by damaged, non-climate-proofed buildings, limited access to sanitation facilities and clean water the project will provide public school rehabilitation (windows, doors, holes, fences). Existing WASH facilities will also be fixed and additional facilities will be added adhering to SPHERE standards regarding the number of latrines as well as a gender separation of latrines, latrines for teachers and at least one accessible latrine per school.
In coordination with the Education Committees, teachers and students, the project will also promote quality and education by providing basic material for teachers to support differentiated teaching and learning including basic School Furniture, Hygiene Supplies and a limited amount of Recreational and Learning Material. Complementary, EiE in NES will contribute to protective educational services by providing Teacher in Crisis Context training to teachers and other education personnel to foster a safe and positive learning environment and offering caregivers the opportunity to increase engagement in children’s' academic and socio-emotional learning by attending positive parenting sessions.
In Tabqa, Ar-Raqqa, this project also supports a local partner to deliver NFE activities to children with disabilities focusing on children with hearing, speaking and visual impairments and delivered by specialised staff. We provide funding, teaching and learning material (including PSS manuals) and capacity building.
These activities are designed to be flexible, allowing adaptation to the rapidly evolving needs in NES and ability to respond to a wide range of scenarios. This will also allow the project to provide a more tailored response in each of the districts in accordance with their different needs and authorities’ approvals and priorities.

4 - Needs

The cumulative effect of conflict, displacement, COVID-19-related closures of learning facilities, the worsening economic situation, and other factors continue to negatively impact the overstretched education system. The right to education continues to be severely infringed across NES, where the proportion of children out of school remains high, particularly in Ar-Raqqa (35%), Aleppo (26%) and Deir-ez-Zor (25%) and half of children not in school have never enrolled (MSNA, 2021). During 2021, the NGO assessed over 2,700 students in Deir ez-Zor and Raqqa, which found that less than 10% of students attended formal education with many students out of school for over three years. Nearly 60% were not attending due to lack of available schools, and 20% due to school quality and safety. Nationally almost half of students complained about the lack of WASH facilities whereas nearly all students complained about facilities’ cleanliness and lack of soap (MSNA, 2021). Prepondering poor teaching practices and corporal punishment negatively impact children’s wellbeing and learning, and contribute to low enrolment and high drop out; 60% of households perceive the need for enhancing teacher capacity as key to improving education services (HNO, 2021). The Self Administration lacks the resources and capacity to provide support to OOSC or for school retention (less than 2% of children have access to NFE services, MSNA, 2021) while education remains one of the most underfunded sectors in NES.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Many students continue to learn in unconducive and unsafe learning environments and many schools in NES are in dire need of intervention, since they were not rehabilitated after the conflict and remain a tremendous risk to childrens safety and health. Unsafe learning facilities – which are structurally unsafe, lacking protection from elements, with no or insufficient WASH facilities (and heightened COVID risk), contribute to low enrolment and high drop out e.g the lack of windows and heating in classrooms exposes children to below 0oC temperatures in winter. These risks will be mitigated through rehabilitation and improvement of learning facilities so they are safe and conducive for learning. Child safeguarding measures are to be put in place in all activities.Prepondering poor teaching practices and corporal punishment negatively impact childrens wellbeing and learning. To counter this, TiCC training will be provided to teachers on positive discipline methods; prevention and reporting of violence; sensitisation to code of conduct; basic self-care and PSS.The project will ensure that NFE staff, facilitators and contractors sign and abide by the child safeguarding policy. The NGO will ensure all classes are free from any kind of risk including physical, sexual and psychosocial risks.The project has a Community Response Mechanism; participants can directly provide feedback or complaints about programmes, staff and systems via WhatsApp, email, telephone and complaints boxes


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The NGO's Code of Conduct and Associated policies are consistent with the six core principles of IASC Task Force on PSEA in Humanitarian Crises. Risks related to this project have been identified in a separate multi-sectoral strategy with appropriate mitigation measures identified. The latter includes, but is not limited to,the appointment and training of PSEA focal points in all project sites and offices and trained with supported from the Syria PSEA network. It also includes the use of the PSEA networks developed checklists for the observation of operations to identify, prevent and mitigate potential SEA and SEA risk factors in ongoing field activities.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks-Further escalation of conflict, or deterioration in the security situation, as a result of any of: - Escalation in the conflict between the SDF and Government of Turkey, extending beyond the current areas of operation.-IS resurgence in Deir ez Zor with extension of attacks to surrounding areas-Tension between authorities and communities or inter-community conflict, jeopardising access to affected project areas. - Markets affected by continued currency devaluation, border closures or escalation of conflict, leading to shortage of supplies and market price fluctuations - COVID-19 pandemic escalates, new lockdowns restrict access Assumptions-The project will be able to access beneficiaries, assess their specific needs and implement the programme accordingly. There will be no significant shifts in control over key locations. Markets continue to function, internal and regional borders remain open and goods are available locally.Mitigating Measures- Strengthen operational resilience. In the event of increased insecurity, shift operations to nearest unaffected base of operations. Flexibly shift modalities between in-kind, voucher and cash depending on cross-border access, the state of markets and availability of key goods. Full engagement in, and the development of robust, continuity of services plans to minimise the potential humanitarian consequences of any disruption in services to target area COVID-19 SOPs for general IPC in place as well as activity level SOPs.
