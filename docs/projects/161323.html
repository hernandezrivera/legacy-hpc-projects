Project Summary

Please provide a short summary of the intervention, describing its purpose and target. – Limit 4000 characters
According to the South Sudan seasonal flooding snapshot 21 Oct 2019, unusually heavy seasonal flooding is affecting large areas of South Sudan, with estimates that between 600,000 and 800,000 people have been affected. SAADO’s proposed intervention is intended to focus primarily on life saving WASH activities that will include rehabilitation of emergency existing water facilities (boreholes, motorized boreholes, and emergency water treatment units), distribution of hygiene/sanitary kits and construction of emergency sanitation facilities and reinforce with emergency targeted hygiene promotion to reduce the risks of increased WASH related morbidity and mortality.
This project is going be implemented in selected counties of Terekeka and Talli where SAADO is currently implementing a school feeding programme targeting 19 schools in the targeted location as well as implementing Food for Assets(FFA)and a livestock vaccination and treatment projects and already has an established presence since 2014 in the targeted location with well qualified local staff to ensure timely delivery of services with good standards.
The proposed project will target women, children, boys, girls and men including IDPs, returnees and most vulnerable host populations. Inadequate WASH services in these two counties contribute not only to disease outbreak, but also to increased malnutrition. There is lack of improved sanitation and limited knowledge of hygiene practices specifically for those communities displaced into Gemeiza highlands and the host communities. This proposed intervention will reduce the vulnerability of those communities who do not have access to safe water supplies and proper sanitation and hygiene in Terekeka and Talli counties through provision of improved water, sanitation and hygiene services and increased knowledge and coping capacity of affected communities to prevent outbreak of WASH related diseases such as cholera and diarrhea.
In addition, this proposed emergency intervention is designed to improve the quality and access to services especially through rehabilitation of existing water points and household emergency water treatment, construction of sanitation facilities, promote good hygiene practices through training and capacity building community based hygiene promoters. SAADO’s proposed project will integrate with the nutrition and food security partners through referrals to specialized partners with OTP centres since it is widely noted that nutritional status is greatly impacted by diarrheal and other WASH related diseases/ illnesses which largely affect children under 5 years of age.
SAADO will also encourage active participation of the local community including women and children in the implementation of the proposed project through consultation of local authorities at all levels, women, men, boys and girls in the selection of the project payams within the county based on the identified needs. The local authorities, host community and IDPs men, women, girls and boys will also be involved in the design of the community and institutional sanitation facilities putting into consideration the need for privacy for females for the case of latrines and bath room’s construction.

1 - Needs

According to the South Sudan seasonal flooding snapshot 21 Oct 2019, unusually heavy seasonal flooding is affecting large areas of South Sudan, with estimates that between 600,000 and 800,000 people have been affected.This situation further complicates an already dire overall situation of water supply and sanitation in Terekeka County which is one of the poorest in the Greater Equatoria Region with 68% not having access to improved sanitation and nearly 45% still using unimproved water sources as a result of localized insecurity and intensifying conflicts along the Terekeka to Tindilo corridor, repeated natural disasters and poor operation and maintenance which is a huge burden on the WASH infrastructure. Moreover,unplanned and continued arrival of migrating cattle keepers from the neighbouring counties of Talli,Tindilo and bor is putting additional pressure on already limited and dilapidated infrastructure. Majority of the cattle keeping population mostly rely on public hand-pumps (49%) with female headed households having far less access to the hand-pumps (23% by male and 13% by female). According to a UNICEF situation report 2019, much lower percentage of returnees have access to water within the compound (4%) compared to conflict displaced people which leaves both the host cattle keepers and migrating populations facing the burden of traveling long distances to fetch clean water with only 29 per cent of the population having access to a borehole, tap stand or water yard within a maximum 30-minute distance without facing protection concerns. Such conditions are pushing affected population (including host communities) to share their limited water sources and often seek refuge to unimproved water sources and practice risky behaviours like open defecation. Anecdotal reports estimate that female headed households are more likely to opt for open defecation compared to male headed households, presumably because of their inability to construct pit latrines. Such conditions are conducive for manifestation of diarrhoea. Attention needs to be paid to female headed families, as these families are less likely to have access to the means of hygiene (access to soap by 43% male headed families compared to 35% by female headed) and sanitation. The condition of poor WASH compromises the dignity of women and girls, put them at risk of harassment, and expose people to life-threatening diseases including outbreaks of acute diarrhoea, cholera, ARI and measles, especially to young children and sick and elderly people. SAADO’s proposed response intervention will be targeting host communities,school communities,cattle keepers, both male and female headed households without pit latrines, communities without protected bore holes that use unprotected water sources and communities displaced by ongoing conflict.

2 - Activities

1.Rehabilitation of existing 15 hand pumps (10 in Terekeka and 5 in Talli). Establish sustainable water management committees responsible for the operation and maintenance of water facilities with 40% comprising of women. 2.Mobilize women and men to participate in the location, design, and maintenance of water points. 3.Organize distribution of WASH/NFI to 1000 households in Terekeka county. SAADO through core pipeline manager UNICEF will access water treatment products and water containers. The beneficiaries will be provided with education on how to use these WASH emergency supplies specially the proper storage of the water containers and application of water purifiers 4.Construction of gender segregated communal latrines with consultation of all groups. Carry out concerted hygiene promotion campaigns on safe excreta disposal and hygiene practices. Design communal latrines in consultation with women and girls to ensure that users have privacy. The selection process of communal latrines sites will be based on the needs of the community e.g IDP's locations will be prioritized in this selection process. 5. Mobilization and training of 100 hygiene promoters. Hygiene promoters will sensitize the IDP's about the use of latrines and host communities, water sources, importance of cleaning water containers, solid waste management, the importance of hand washing at critical times and the importance of personal hygiene among the affected community. 6. Conduct daily hygiene campaign/awareness among the internally displaced persons in the islands of Gemeiza payam. Ensure all users in particular women and girls participate in identification of risks hygiene practices and coordination.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

SAADO 's proposed emergency intervention will ensure that beneficiaries rights are legally recognized in all field sites.This will be done through having a robust Beneficiary Communication and Accountability Plan.This will include delivering initial orientation and ongoing information to communities on project activities,rationale behind beneficiary selection, and supporting the management and resolution of complaints throughout project planning and implementation.Established Project Management Committees will also be involved in supporting the implementation and management of project activities to ensure that these resources do not create a source of competition and dispute, the project will strengthen the capacity of community structures to develop and enforce context appropriatesystems for ensuring the equitable access and utilization of communal assets and resources. The project will also ensure communities are actively involved in beneficiary selection, will conduct face-to-face information,question and answer sessions, and establish a complaints system to receive and pre-emptively respond to issues with the potential to negatively impact community relationships.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

SAADO's proposed intervention will include perspectives of displacement affected people to ensure that they also benefit from the project.This will be done by ensuring that the 30% quota in selecting beneficiaries is adhered to and implemented in all field sites of Terekeka County.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

To ensure the sustainability of the project SAADO will work with Water Management Committees (WMC’s) members and other key stakeholders and the host communities and will develop detailed plans relating to key tasks and persons responsible. The WMCs will own these plans, and be responsible for theirimplementation and adherence following project closure. Technical and soft skills training will be delivered through the project to ensure WMCs have the capacity. The training approaches employed by the project will also encourage sustainability by training community members as trainers. This will ensure that technical knowledge and skills remain in communities following project closure.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

SAADO's proposed project is aligned with the national objectives as enshrined in the revitalized agreement on the resolution of conflict in South Sudan (R-ARCISS)chapter 4 for reconstruction and recovery of communities as well as the partnership for recovery and resilience(PFRR)in South Sudan which places special emphasis on the delivery of WASH services to under served communities.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

SAADO’s proposed emergency intervention will ensure that the protection environment is safe and stable by mainstreaming protection concerns into consideration and by further ensuring that females have equal and equitable access to services and that targeted action to advance protection is based on a gender and age analysis. All activities and results (output and outcomes) will enhance protection considerations.The project embraces ADAPT & ACTC framework for protection and gender equality programming. For example our project (1) analyses the impact of the crises (violent conflict, shocks and economic) on girls, boys,women and men and what this means in terms of divisionof labour/tasks,workload and access to humanitarian assistance and protection (2) ensures that girls and boys,women and men participate equally and equitably in the design,implementation, monitoring and evaluation; and that girls, boys, women and men are involved in decision-making positions.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

SAADO's proposed project will ensure that feedback is provided as part of a wider AAP which is central to SAADO’s project intervention and community engagement approach.The proposed project will achieve this through establishment of Water Management Committees (WMCs)composed of 40% women and 60% men which will enhance community participation, ownership,and satisfaction in SAADO’s proposed intervention. Furthermore,the WMCs will include a membership that represents all subsets of society including marginalized groups and will be responsible for implementing a two-way BCA strategies identified in the project’s Beneficiary Communication and Accountability Plan. The project will also ensure communities are actively involved in conducting face-to-face information, question and answer sessions, and establish a complaints system to receive and pre-emptively respond to issues with the potential to negatively impact community relationships. This will be based upon the five pillars of AAP(Leadership, Transparency, Consultation, Partnership and Feedback)as informed by the recommendations and guidance of the IASC on AAP and contextualized for application in South Sudan (considering issues such as traditional decision making structures, access to communications, local culture and literacy).SAADO’s commitment to accountability will also be clearly demonstrated in ensuring that the affected population in the target areas has a genuine voice in the assessments, design and decision-making process that inform the priorities, modalities and trajectories of this project. In addition,as part of their orientation training and continual development, project personnel will receive instruction on why AAP is prioritized, how it is operationalized, and what their roles and responsibilities are in making it work.
