Project Summary

Over the past three years, intercommunal conflict and extreme climate variability and shocks have displaced tens of millions of people in Ethiopia. In the Oromia, Somali and Southern Nations, Nationalities and People’s (SNNP) Regions there are over 1.1 million people in need . As the conflict subsides, the government continues to force IDP’s to return to their former homes, but in the haste of this operation, most return to their communities with few belongings and no productive assets. The provision of seed vouchers alongside basic agricultural advice, for major staple crops (e.g., wheat, tef, OPV maize, sorghum), pulses (e.g., haricot beans) and cash for nutritious vegetable seeds and/or other needed agricultural inputs are essential for the IDP returnees to provide a rapid restart to food production, diversify production for improved consumption and strengthen livelihoods for income generation.
To support both vulnerable farmers and IDP returnees Catholic Relief Services (CRS) and our local partners built on the effective response of the first phase of this project funded by USAID/BHA, the Agricultural Seed Activity Project (ASAP) in Oromia Somali, and SNNP by scaling up the cash and voucher-based seed response to benefit farming households who are vulnerable to acute seed insecurity caused by conflict and acute environmental shocks. The purpose of the project is to improve agricultural production through the provision of agricultural inputs (seeds), training in good agricultural practices and links to input suppliers by reaching out to 97,392 beneficiary households.

1. - Projects activities

The project implements activities like distribution of major cereal and pulse crop seeds through commodity voucher, distribute cash for vulnerable households to purchase nutrient-dense vegetable crop seeds, provide ToT training on IPM (including topics on FAW and DL) to development agents, woreda experts, project staff to strengthen their capacity to enhance their crop protection knowledge and skills. In addition, the project prepares and provides development agents and woreda experts with FAW manuals, leaf lets and posters to cascade the training to project beneficiaries and to the wider farming community.
