Project Summary

The project aims to reach the most vulnerable group of people including Pregnant and lactating Women and Children Under the age of Five with rapid nutrition and nutrition-sensitive lifesaving interventions of early detection, referral, and treatment of malnourished through preventative and curative activities of Community Management of acute malnutrition.
Infant and Young Children Feeding in Emergencies, Integrated Management of Childhood illnesses, in a close linkage to the nearby Health and Nutrition centers providing Targeted Supplementary Food Program, Outpatient Therapeutic Program, and Inpatient Care services to the Acutely Malnourished cases. Moreover, a tightened involvement of awareness raising of maternal and children nutrition and health will be scaled up by the dissemination of integrated key messages of WaSH, Health education, COVID-19, and optimal feeding during the first 1000 days of the child's life.
BINAA Organization for Development will provide nutrition services to GU5 and PLWs through 4 mobile teams and 10 fixed teams In PHCs in 5 Districts, 10 Sub-Districts, and 71 Communities from the high-level severity areas.
The nutrition services will include screening CU5 and PLWs to identify the malnutrition cases, treat and follow up the malnutrition cases till their recovery, prevent malnutrition through the distribution of nutrition supplies including BSFP, and raise awareness activities.
Direct beneficiaries: 170,000 individuals (PLWs and CU5)
Children under five: 110,000 (50,000 boys, and 60,000 girls)
Pregnant and lactating women: 60,000.
Disabilities: 8500 individuals
The project will also include the distribution of CVA assistance to 813 PLWs and 1353 children (6-23 months) while each child will receive 15 USD for 8 months and each PLW will receive 35 USD for 8 months.
-The beneficiaries will be children and pregnant and lactating women within a household receiving GFA based on the following criteria:
- Pregnant women in the first trimester
- Lactating women with children under two
- Children 6-23 months

0 - Project Summary (extended)

According to HNO 2024,16.7 million people are in need, including 5.5 million displaced individuals and 5.7 million requiring urgent nutrition assistance. Syria faces multiple layers of malnutrition issues, including rising levels of chronic malnutrition.data, there are 1.46 million PLWs, adolescent girls, and children under 5 years who are considered people in need (PIN) for nutrition services in NW Syria. The project activities will include:1-Conducting MUAC screening for CU5 and PLWs, along with skilled IYCF counseling for primary caretakers of CU2 years.2-Screening and referral of CU5 and PLWs showing signs of abuse or violence to specialized centers.3- Distributing supplies including BSFP to prevent micronuterients.4- Provide treatment to SAM & MAM cases.5-Training women in the Family MUAC Approach (FMA).5-Distributing cash and voucher assistance to 813 PLWs and 1353 children (6-23 months) based on nutrition guidelines.7-Monitoring, Evaluation, and Accountability through BINAA MEAL team, including baseline, end line, and PDM. Protection Risk Analysis:1-Risk of GBV on CHWs during home visits:Likelihood: MImpact: HMitigation Measures: CHWs selected from targeted communities, staff trained in first-line support and GBV identification.2-Risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers:Likelihood: LImpact: HMitigation Measures: Establish a confidential complaint mechanism, train staff on the code of conduct, and address PSEA complaints promptly.3-Risk of discrimination in service provision:Likelihood: LImpact: HMitigation Measures: Ensure project design includes accessibility for vulnerable groups, implement effective community-based complaints mechanisms, and adhere to humanitarian principles and a "Do No Harm" strategy.4-Risk of exploitation of beneficiaries by workers:Likelihood: LImpact: HMitigation Measures: Carefully select staff with high behavioral qualities.5-Risk of traditional habits hindering women from seeking services:Likelihood: LImpact: HMitigation Measures: Conduct house-to-house screening and treatment visits.PSEA Strategy:During project implementation, BINAA staff will adhere to five key messages on AAP & PSEA. Feedback mechanisms will be established for targeted populations. The targeted population will be involved throughout the project cycle via ME questionnaires and focused meetings.Risks:1-Security changes in project areas:Monitor INSO reports.Assess access security records.Ensure regular access to locations.Explore flexible delivery plans.Adopt a proactive security risk management approach.2-Vendor shop attacks:Report daily works.Temporarily suspend operations if needed.Coordinate with clusters for location changes.Fraud in voucher redemption:3-Use E-voucher system.Monitor daily through dashboard.Field team monitors markets.Publish complaint posters.4-COVID-19 and Cholera risk:Organize distribution to avoid gatherings.Display awareness posters and conduct sessions.
