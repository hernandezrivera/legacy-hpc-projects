Project Summary

Syria is one of the largest protection crises of our times. Hundreds of thousands of civilians have been killed, and armed battles continue to carry out violations of human rights, with women and children particularly vulnerable to exploitation - including breaches of international humanitarian law.
After more than eight years of conflict, the situation in the Syrian Arab Republic remains one of the most significant humanitarian crises of our time. The United Nations estimates that 11.7 million people were in need of various forms of humanitarian assistance, including an estimated 6.2 million people remained internally displaced, close to 1.4 million displaced persons reportedly returned home spontaneously during the same period. 'Syrian Arab Republic: 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview', OCHA, 2019
The crisis in Syria continues to have a profound impact on people across the country. Countless civilians have been injured as a direct result of hostilities. In areas where hostilities have subsided, enabling some stability and security to take hold, the impact of prolonged crisis remains together with large-scale vulnerabilities, limited services, and damaged infrastructures and productive assets.
Since the capacities of livelihood activities remain insufficient in certain domains and locations. GOPA is seeking to provide the needed support in new high severity scale areas, as this project aims to support returnees in their own towns and IDPs in the hosting communities. Assisting beneficiaries through this project would help them either starting with self-employment or finding job to alleviate the financial burdens on the hosting communities or giving them the opportunity to return with some life skills to even start a new life in their own locations. Accordingly, the project for IDPS and returnees would have a positive impact on individuals and societies in many aspects
Rural Damascus governorate:
Rural Damascus has been severely affected by the war for several years, resulting in losing all goods and chattels. A very important update took place in 2018, the siege of Eastern Ghouta ended by late March, which allowed the families to return to their villages; the IDP Spontaneous Returns OCHA December 2018 report indicated that 26,000 IDPs returned to their residential places in Rural Damascus. The control over that areas has also shifted marking the entire governorate safe and accessible. Accordingly, hundreds of families decided to return to their own towns to restart a new life with their relatives and friends. The returnees could find housing possibilities, but they are in lack of any income resources.
Based on HNO 2019, 9.9 M people asking for livelihood assistances, where there is (1,601,209) people in need to ERL services in Rural Damascus with severity rate of 3.17, as the following sub-district ( Jaramana 328,583.- Babella 196,472 – Kisweh 80,159 – Kafr Batna 24,996 – Arbin 13,391- Maliha 4,963- Qudsiya 181,278).
And to go for more details through Kafr Batna communities (Beit Sawa 789 - Eftreis 147- Ein Terma 8,603 - Hammura 1,598 - Hezzeh 806 - Hosh Al-Ashary 104 - Jisrein 1,419- Kafr Batna 4,336 - Mahmadiyeh 65 – Saqba 7,129)
The objective of this project is to enable 950 returnees, IDPs, and host communities’ members (male and female), to have an honorable source of income to help their families and other society war affected members.
The program will strengthen access to livelihoods by creating income-generating opportunities and by improving access to production and market infrastructure to restore local economy recover through Vocational and skills training provided and Entrepreneurship supported
Beneficiaries will receive life skills and management experience during the program so that they will be able to start their own small business by the end of the program.
Tool kits are to be distributed for all of beneficiaries by the end of the program.

5 - Implementation of activities

GOPA will start implementing this project by conducting social evaluation visits by ORVs in Rural Damascus to help selecting process, taking into consideration the available data from GOPA DERD registration offices, moreover, we can announce the activities through GOPA DERD Facebook page or distributing brochures to meet more people in need to this kind of support, the following selection criteria to be adopted -Families/individuals that have been unemployed up to 12 months.-Youth (17-24 years old).-Families/Individuals having PwD member(s) or injured member(s).-Female-headed families.-Families/Individuals who did not receive any kind of assistance-Female who experienced sexual abuse or violenceEntrepreneurship courses for youth selection criteria-Age ranges between 18-30 years; secondary school certificate is preferable-60% of the selected youth should be among returnee, while the remaining 40% will be from the affected host communities or IDP-60% of the youth should be female while the remaining 40% will be male. The project will take into consideration the involvement of young people with disabilities among the targeted beneficiaries. The selection should ensure equal composition of young people representing the different communities in the area. Based on the beneficiaries’ needs assessment, we will specify the vocational courses such as: Sewing, Hair dressing, Mobile maintenance, Cooking. A public invitation to tender is to be announced in order to choose qualified training centres/ trainers and to find the best offers to provide the needed tool-kits.The average time frame for each Vocational training course is about 140 hours. Whereas the Entrepreneurship training is about 60 hours. By the end of the training courses, the staff will be in charge of tool kits distribution process.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

 Escalation of the security situation. Possible sexual abuse during the courses. Staff and volunteers will conduct awareness sessions with the beneficiaries, besides applying daily supervision to prevent this kind of abuse. Slight possibility of gender-based violence, as the program considers the diversity of gender and age.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

 Drop-outs of some of the registered beneficiaries during the training period  Unavailability of qualified vocational training centers in the targeted areas Unstable security in the country Employment insecurity Delay caused by authorizations Delay caused by financial transfers Mitigating measures: Perform conflict analysis before planning the response. Asking for evaluation session (seminar) before the training, in order to evaluate the trainer performance  Provide transportations or transport allowance for the beneficiaries Provide a day care service for the children so that the mothers can attend the courses with them. implement monitor visit on by GOPA DERD team on weekly Basis  Efficiently deliver on the obligation of ‘Accountability to (Affected) Populations’ by keeping the local authorities and communities concerned regularly informed of the project progress.
