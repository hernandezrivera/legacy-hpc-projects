Project Summary

Through this project, Muslim Aid aims to support most vulnerable communities in North Waziristan and Orakzai tribal districts of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. These communities are in a transition phase where they have been affected by crises when government of Pakistan carried an operation against terrorists.
Through this project Muslim Aid intends to contribute in the FATA Transition plan through outcome # 1 Livelihood and Economic growth and outcome. Strengthening the humanitarian-development nexus in service of economic growth and livelihoods in the Tribal districts will not only improve the lives of the most vulnerable, but the reductions in risk and vulnerability will be essential to ensuring that development progress is accessible to all, including those affected by crises. Putting the new way of working into practice will enable the immediate rehabilitation and restoration of community livelihoods, coupled with long-term initiatives that make these livelihoods sustainable over time, endow Tribal Districts’ populace with the skills needed to thrive in the labour market, and spur business development across the region. These aims are vital for ensuring that FATA/Tribal districts have a skilled, secure and healthy workforce capable of achieving economic growth targets and coping with potential future crises.
Given the fact there has been temporary displacement took place over the past years, Muslim Aid has incorporated the length of displacement in its program design. This program will priorities female beneficiaries of the current returnees, some only 45% registered returnees are women, which reflect the cultural and administrative hurdles that limit their ability to get registered and access assistance. Given the fact that returnees have different skills set, this program will customize services provided by Muslim Aid to the individual needs of returnees, skills they possess and the suitability of livelihoods opportunities in each area. Through local CBO's and Community Committees, MA will identify on-farm and off farm rehabilitation projects; environmental improvement projects; initiatives to restore abandoned agricultural land, irrigation systems and new command areas; flood protection and soil erosion control measures, and new infrastructure requirements, including improved market linkages. Implement these through cash for work / In-kinds modalities, ensuring screening for natural resource impacts. Provide agricultural and livestock inputs to restore livelihood capacity at the household and community levels.

Needs

Agriculture is the mainstay of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa’s economy. According to the FATA Vulnerability Assessment data, 71% of population reside in households that rely on income from their own farm . Farming property land is the main source of income for households in all agencies, although the level of off-farm diversification varies. Agriculture has the lowest contribution in NWA and Khyber, where unskilled labour is the second most common source of livelihood, and Mohmand, where trade/business comes second. Reliance on agriculture is not significantly affected by displacement status as most displaced households also list farming as their main source of income. However, displaced households are on average less likely to rely on salaried jobs (private or public) and more likely to rely on (non-agriculture) unskilled day labour.Based on findings from the joint rapid needs assessment ninety per cent of the households reported that their livelihood has been badly affected due to the displacement. Before the displacement, almost two third (63.8%) of the households had farming (own farm) as the main source of livelihood, followed by wage labor (unskilled labor: 12.5%, skilled labor 6.7%), shopkeeper/trader (6.8%). Other sources of livelihood included income support, trade, services, share cropping and remittances. However, there has been a marked change in the source of livelihood as a result of displacement, particularly from farming to unskilled wage labour. Similarly there was more reliance on income support and humanitarian assistance. Almost two-third of the households are now relying on wage labor (unskilled wage labor: 58.4%, skilled: 3.7%), as the main livelihood, followed by income support (17.4%), humanitarian assistance (3.4%) and services (1.5%). ery few of the households were able to bring their livestock with them during the displacement. While 86% of the households reported owning large ruminants before the crises, this ratio decreased to 21% after the crises. Likewise, ownership of small ruminants also decreased from 74% to 12%, and that of poultry from 87% to 7% .
