Project Summary

In NWS, children are experiencing child labor, early marriage, recruitment into armed groups, and separation from caregivers, according to 69% of surveyed KI through the CP situation monitoring dashboard. Girls and boys are adopting adult roles and responsibilities and often experience elevated levels of psychological strain. The Covid-19 pandemic has compounded the existing protection concerns. Women and girls have experienced increased violence in their homes and increased intimate partner violence during the lockdowns which trapped them at home with their abusers, and 58% of KI in Voices from Syria report, reported restriction on movement of women and girls. The major concern affecting children is their access to education, which has been severely affected by lockdowns. Adolescent girls’ risk being deprioritized for education when they are at home, and have reported increased isolation, as 55% of them experience early marriage. Adolescent boys are also experienced particular protection concerns of their own, especially that they are sometimes viewed as adults which normalizes violence against them, recruitment into armed groups and child labor.
The goal of this project is to reduce protection risks and vulnerability for children and families in Northwest Syria through the provision of holistic child protection services for children, caregivers, and community members. Our interventions include:
1) Comprehensive child protection case management to identify the most vulnerable children in NWS and supports them to full recovery from violence, abuse, neglect, and coercion.
2) Parenting skills education for caregivers (Male and female) to equip them in dealing with the stress and implementing positive discipline techniques that ensures safe and healthy child development.
3) Structured and non-structured psychosocial support to children and adolescents aimed at helping them overcome trauma and distress as well as learning how to healthily express themselves and their emotions.
4) Awareness raising for community members on important topics such as early marriage, child labor, child development and psychological needs and COVID-19 precautions.
5) Training and establishment of child protection committees from community leaders and influential individuals who support the child protection team in raising awareness, supporting existing positive community structures, and channeling assistance to the most vulnerable community members.

4 - Needs

Referencing Voices from Syria 2021 Report, by January 2020, displaced persons in northwest Syria reached 390,0003, then skyrocketed to 960,000 in early March. Over 80% of those displaced are women and children. Coupled with the depreciation in economy causing prices to rise unprecedentedly, instability of the security situation, and COVID-19 Pandemic repercussions we anticipate increased needs and adverse effects.The HNAP Socioeconomic Overview Report showcases the vulnerability of the population in Syria, where 98% live in extreme poverty and in NWS 80% reported their monthly income is insufficient to meet their needs listing the barriers to employment as lack of opportunities, skills, and financing in addition to COVID-19 effects and the security situation leading to negative coping mechanisms including 57% reporting borrowing or buying on credit and 2% reporting resulting to child labor to overcome their economic challenge.Children, due to existing power dynamics and social norms, are overburdened with the aftermath of the Syrian crisis where their rights are undermined on daily basis. According to the latest child protection situation monitoring , over 82% of KII’s have reported children in NWS need psychosocial support and are engaged in some type of child labor preventing them from accessing education, while around 43% reported children in NWS are separated from original caregivers.Finally, as adolescent boys and girls face particular protection risks such as isolation of adolescent girls and early marriage and child labor and violence for adolescent boys, there is an increasing need for activities tailored for adolescents and youth and their caregivers, such as PSS, case management and youth empowerment.

5 - Implementation of activities

CP team established 4 mobile centers in: Armanaz, Kafar Takharim, Salqin and Darkoush working with WPE and PRoL in Maaretmisreen and Dana areas through outreach.Through protection monitoring, community outreach and coordination with local councils and service providers, IRC identifies the most vulnerable children and families. IRC provides regular information and introduction sessions to community members to make them aware of available services and complaint and feedback mechanism. Our interventions include raising awareness in the community on CP concerns, forming and training of child protection committees, providing structured and non-structured PSS and case management for children and adolescents, providing parenting skills training to caregivers and conducting assessments to identify persons in urgent need of support who will be linked to services, including cash assistance which increases their coping capacity facing unexpected and significant shocks which may be mitigated through a one-time injection of cash - this intervention is usually integrated in the case management or targets households with pressing protection needs, such as recent eviction; and support for persons with disabilities.IRC provides monthly and on-needs basis reporting of activities to maintain oversight and mitigate risk in Remote Management environments and collects regular data on service satisfaction and client responsiveness and uses Child Protection Committees (CPC’s) and discussions with caregivers to assess implementation and promote child participation. IRC promotes Client Responsiveness and Feedback handling Mechanisms (CRFM) and child friendly mechanisms to ensure children’s opinions inform the design and delivery of services.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks:*Community tension due to service delivery locations or types of services offered.*Discrimination/Perceived discrimination due to service provision to specific groups or individuals.*Existing traditional and religious practices linked to normalization of violence against children and women and girls, and other practices that are protection concerns.*Direct attacks at the CP service provision facilities and seizing beneficiaries’ related data and violating the confidentiality of children and families.*Exposing children to further harm at home or in the community due to participation in IRC CP services.*Wellbeing of child protection service providers due to exposure to violence or witnessing extreme violence incidents.*Exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information is limited for specific persons/groups.Mitigation:Ensure a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken and proper conflict sensitivity programming coaching takes place for frontline staff.Consultation with local communities with regards to services, barriers of access to services and information for PWDs included.Ensure locations of services are screened for safe access.Linking child protection interventions with existing services that respond to urgent needs.Activate CPC's to reinforce community-based protection and engagement of youth and PWD.Conduct awareness raising and introduction sessions on service provision and complaint mechanism.Train staff on data protection protocols and activate data destruction protocols.Engagement in positive parenting practices’ sessions/Activation of case management to respond to domestic violence.Self-care strategies for staff and PSEA policy activation.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information is limited for specific persons/groups.Risk of lack of safe, accessible and inclusive feedback and complaint mechanism.IRC recognizes the unequal power dynamic and the resulting potential for exploitation inherent in our work with local beneficiaries and that exploitation undermines the credibility of our work and severely damages victims of these exploitive acts and their families and communities.Accordingly, IRC maintains and upholds PSEA policy which explicitly prohibits all forms of beneficiary exploitation and abuse by IRC staff.IRC expects staff to uphold the highest ethical standard of integrity, accountability and transparency in the delivery of goods and services while executing the responsibilities of their position.This includes prohibiting sexual favors or relations, exploitation, discrimination, engaging in sexual misconduct with children, and abuse of power.IRC considers: Exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries, whether of a sexual nature or not, by staff constitutes an act of gross misconduct grounds for immediate termination of employment.IRC staff are obliged to create and maintain an environment that is free of sexual exploitation and abuse, if developing concerns or suspicions regarding abuse or exploitation he or she must report.Reporting mechanisms are made clear and introduced to the community via email, WhatsApp, hotlines and MEAL staff.
