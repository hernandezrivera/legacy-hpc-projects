Project Summary

"The project will implement life-saving activities to improve access to water and sanitation in the Rohingya Refugee Camp.. The project targets 12000, direct beneficiaries (11000 refugees and 1000 members of the host communities). The project will be implemented in the Refugee Camps in Ukhiya (7/8e/9/10/HC - Jalia palong union) & in the host communities of Cox’s Bazar District.
To facilitate our capacity strength and existing water trucking resource mobilization & active Health facility for the COVID-19 response, Maintenance & Construction/equipment of deep tube wells (DTWs) with lift pump like deep TARA or IMK2/3s with appropriate drainage - Operation/maintenance of hand pumps and tube wells/water point attendant trainings/equipment - Construction of boreholes w/ solar powered submersible pumps and piped distribution systems/ provision for chlorination & appropriate drainage - Operation and maintenance of piped water supply network - Emergency phase bulk water treatment: batch treatment w/ coagulation/flocculation & chlorination for surface water - Distribution of Aqua tabs at HH level.
Safety, privacy and dignity in accessing and using of WASH facilities and services will be at the heart of WASH strategy and programming. Protection strategies in WASH will ensure that all groups of people facing additional vulnerabilities have adequate and safe access to water collection and use; functional latrines and bathing units with safe and accessible pathways, solid doors, locks and safe spaces for practicing MHM. including increasing women’s participation in decision making at camp level as well as the strengthening of gender and age responsive feedback and complaints mechanisms
As hand washing with soap and water and social distancing are the key targeted and recommended preventive measures by WHO to fight against the corona virus . Maintenance & repair Hand washing points .Training of communities to monitor, report and provide feedback on WASH disease incidence and risk behaviors - Training of male and female outreach workers to encourage health seeking behaviors. Hygiene promotion and communication engagement strategies, approaches and methodologies.

1 - Identified Needs

"The Rohingya Refugee population is entirely reliant on Humanitarian assistance.. Essential to sustain the response for preexisting priority humanitarian needs, while strengthening and complementing it to address. The additional response requirements from the COVID-19 pandemic and its impact on the overall protection environment. Currently 54%(percent) Rohingya Refugee have access to chlorinated water supply through small borehole –fed water networks ,although water quality varies. Complementary water comes from shallow boreholes with hand pumps, again with varying water quality. Water consumption overall is estimated at 29 liters per person and per day. Safe water availability is essential to support hand washing and Hygiene for COVID-19 and other infectious diseases, such as Watery Diarrhea (AWD). The extension of chlorinated water networks or an increase in daily operation of existing networks is essential to increase safe water coverage and Availability. Access to adequate sanitation remains a challenge in the camps, mainly due to the lack of space and need for constant maintained and improvements to Sanitation facilities .While coverage average 20 person per latrine. Despite 99% of house hold reporting using pit latrines in the camp. only 78% percent are functioning, 32% of the refugee house hold are facing challenges to access latrines due to low coverage as too many people are using one latrine ,while 14% face a problem of distance between the latrines and their shelters. Meaningful gender segregation of latrines (only 13% of latrines are currently reported as female only) redesign of facilities and lighting to help facilitate a safe environment for women and girls to use latrine and accessible latrines for people with mobility challenges are needed. Hygiene promotion being strengthened to increase awareness of the routes of contamination and disease prevention measures ,Including addressing misconceptions and adapting social norms. For women and girls of reproductive age, lack of privacy and safety within camp means that their menstrual hygiene needs are left unmet. Inadequate sanitation facilities to ensure protection and privacy-especially critical during the night – as well as lack of practical information on care and disposal of menstrual hygiene materials continues to post hygiene related and reproductive health challenges. Regarding bathing or latrines facilities women and girls struggles to get a safe and hygienic place to dispose or wash menstrual items. Solid waste management remains a challenge in the camps, with only 33% of the refugee household reporting use household bins or shared garbage bins, 39% reporting the presence of garbage near their house. To mitigate virus transmission, Hand washing points have been installed in strategic places and these will be increased as well disinfection through spraying of chlorine solution on WASH infrastructure and communal spaces. Maintaining physical distancing at water points is a major concern. The new SARI ITCs and other health facilities require water, sanitation & medical waste management services and hygiene facilities ,maintenance and supplies.
