Project Summary

Malaria consortium has been in Northern Bar El Ghazal (NBeGs) for over 14 years implementing health projects in various counties in collaboration with the SMoH and county health department (CHD). For the planned intervention, MC will improve surveillance and response towards epidemic prone diseases and provide accessible health care services to communities at crisis that are not covered by the HPF grant, and other donor responses through other partners thus leading to tangible reduction in morbidity and mortality. Services will be extended to 186,895 beneficiaries [direct & indirect] within a radius of 10km from the nearest health facility and hard to reach affected population in Aweil South (47,242), center (56,839), and west (82,814) [HPF3 Qtr2 report].
The project interventions will focus on delivery of life saving essential health care services guided by the South Sudan basic essential health care package. The key notable interventions will include immunization of children under the age of five against vaccine preventable diseases, treatment of common communicable diseases, Provision of mental health services through diagnosis and referral, improving community health behavior through spreading of behavior change messages at both the static and mobile clinic days. Provision of maternal and adolescent sexual and reproductive health services; provision of BEmONC services by skilled birth attendants. Improving on the nutritional status of the children, pregnant and lactating mothers through screening, referral, and coordination; improving the WASH structures in the non-supported health facilities through renovation and provision of wash supplies; procure, pre-position, and provide essential medicines and supplies to non- supported health facilities. The project will also seek to improve the screening and referral of people with disabilities using the Washington group of questions at every point of contact with health service providers.
For quality assurance of service delivery, malaria consortium will conduct trainings for all the engaged project staff, including trainings on packages such as clinical management of rape, sexual and reproductive health, and safeguarding. Emphasis will be placed on capacity improvement for the county health department to empower them lead effective supportive supervision sessions and mentorship to health care workers to develop skills and knowledge.

1. Needs

In South Sudan, a critical shortage of healthcare workers, inadequate infrastructure, and low service utilization contribute to one of the world's highest rates of maternal and child mortality. About 75% of child deaths are preventable, stemming from diseases like diarrhea, malaria, and pneumonia. A mere 25% of women access healthcare facilities or receive care from skilled birth attendants during childbirth, elevating the risk of complications. Political, economic, and geographical barriers, compounded by ongoing conflicts, further impede access to essential health services. With 90% of the population residing in rural areas and over half living more than 5km from a health facility, securing healthcare proves to be a significant challenge. Government allocation to health accounts for less than 2% of the national budget, resulting in substantial out-of-pocket expenses [Crown Agents 2022]. The shortage of skilled healthcare providers is severe, with only 3.5 per 10,000 population, a figure significantly below the WHO's recommended minimum. In Northern Bahr el Ghazal (NBeG), a staggering 62% of the population face severe food insecurity, with 66% experiencing extreme levels. In January 2023, NBeG reported the highest cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) in the country, with 17,668 children admitted for treatment. Hepatitis E outbreaks are widespread due to a lack of essential WASH services. According to the NBeG 2021 annual EPI performance bulletin, NBeG state annual Penta 1 coverage was 82%, however the Penta 3 coverage was 68% and the Drop Out Rate (DOR) was 18%, an indication of low use of EPI services. The Aweil region, particularly in Aweil Centre, has been severely affected by devastating floods, resulting in the displacement of over 128,000 individuals and restricting access to essential health facilities. We identified 4 hard to reach Bomas prone to floods in Aweil Centre, 15 hard to reach Bomas in Aweil South and 9 hard to reach Bomas prone to floods in Aweil West. The area also grapples with a scarcity of skilled healthcare workers, high attrition rates, and damage to health infrastructure due to a combination of conflict and floods. The existing health facilities are primarily housed in traditional tukul structures and face a shortage of continuous supplies, including medications, which has been further exacerbated by the increased caseload [MC assessment].Despite some health facilities being within approximately 30 minutes' reach for refugees and returnees in Wedweil West, the population often struggles to cover the distance due to food and water scarcity. Additionally, the delivery of babies for refugees and returnees predominantly occurs within the communities or camps with the assistance of traditional birth attendants. Each health facility has only one midwife.Aweil South County has 18 health facilities [20 PHCUs & 2 PHCCs]. Five out of 20 HFs, not functional and therefore populations around these facilities are largely reached through Mobile outreach clinics supported South Sudan Humanitarian Fund. Outpatient consultations for children and adults in the last 2 years have increased by 10.6% from 117,260 in the calendar year 2020 to 129,680 in 2021 (HMIS Data) and with trends of children below 5 years sharply influencing these increased morbidity trends with a rapid rise by 28.5% from 39,865 in 2020 to 51,238 in 2021. In the month of January 2022 alone among the 9 HPF supported facilities, up to 5,188 treatments were provided for Diarrhoea, Malaria and Pneumonia out of which 79% (4,081) were for Malaria, 12% (606) were RTI/Pneumonia cases while 10% (501) represented Acute Diarrhoea cases (Facility registers/records). Other problems include poor structures, Nyieth and Ayai PHCUs are grass thatched; storm damaged structures at Tiaraliet, Panthou posing a risk to female patients and pregnant mothers; reduced HPF funding for the county creating a big gap in terms of supply of essential drugs.

2. Project activities

1. Delivering basic lifesaving bio-medical services through static and mobile clinics to reach 186,895 beneficiaries. Nine (09) static health facilities will be supported to offer services to affected communities of Aweil center (Longwurkech, Umora, and Baau PHCUs. Aweil south: Angol Rual, Aladich, Manyiel PHCUs) and Aweil West (Pantit, Achana and Maduany PHCUs), Services will include routine immunization services, treatment of preventable communicable and non-communicable diseases, screening diagnosis and referral of malnutrition, ANC, skill birth attendance, post maternal and child health services, etc. The same HFs will coordinate with BHWs to hold outreaches or Mobile Clinics targeting affected communities in hard to reach and stay locations such as farmlands with the mentioned services above. Notably, procurement of essential medicines and supplies for the treatment of common preventable illnesses will be done to facilitate service provision at static and mobile clinics/outreaches. 2. Improvement of Health Facility structures and general environment. Basic renovations will be done to especially improve access to health facilities – This will include supporting facilities with ramps so that persons with special needs can access them; WASH services such as water tanks; demarcating consultation rooms to improve privacy especially during antenatal days. Health facilities with leaking roofs will also be supported. 3. Strengthening community health department driven surveillance. Capacity of the BHI infrastructure will be improved through routine mentorship and coaching. In turn, these community health workers will apply the knowledge for effective client follow-up. Also, the CHD surveillance offices will be supported for monitoring and reporting of notifiable diseases through EWARS and IDSR for real time reporting and response to any suspected and confirmed outbreak. Data quality sessions will also be held at the health facilities – reviewing performance of health facilities and generating community response to the gaps. 4. Scorecard for community engagement and sensitization for accountability. Malaria consortium will adopt various strategies including community scorecard meetings and dialogues facilitated by the Health Facility Management Committees to lead awareness drives and information sharing sessions as well as co-creation sensemaking sessions. These will help improve awareness and foster community driven accountability. 5. Improving partnership and collaboration with the state ministry of health (SMoH) and county health department (CHD) authorities. SMoH will be supported to convene review and learning events/meetings including quarterly state oversight committee meetings and monthly health cluster meetings. Similar coordination meetings will be cascading at county level – led by the CHD. MC will hold advocacy meetings to solicit for their support in all the activities during this project. They will be supported to carry out supportive supervision to strengthen the skills and knowledge of the health workers, participate in community meeting with stake holders, recruitment of health personnel, etc. the CHD team will be supported to lead most of the activities in their counties for possible sustainability.
