The project aims at developing a comprehensive Protection program in Aleppo, Deir-Ez- Zor, and Raqqah governorates, focusing on Risk Education (RE) and Child Protection (CP) as key pillars to carry out tailored community-based activities. Regarding RE, the extensive use of explosive weapons since the beginning of the crisis generated explosive hazards, posing a threat to kill or injure civilians, humanitarian response operators, and disrupting livelihood. As school is the most suitable environment to deliver RE to children, teachers need to be properly trained (Training delivered in schools, including both teachers working in those schools and other teachers working in other facilities in the same/nearby areas). For this, Action includes:
- Training of 500 teachers in protection-related topics of RE, allowing themselves to raise children’s ability to deal with threats posed by IEDs, landmines, and ERWs;
- School-based RE awareness campaigns: each trained teacher will transfer the acquired knowledge to an estimated number of 50 children, reaching 25,000 students.
WWGVC will constantly monitor the training and implement FGDs with children to evaluate the impact of the information transferred by educators.
Standardized training materials/kits to deliver awareness sessions to children will be provided by UNICEF and approved by Ministry of Education (MoE).
A second component of the Protection program foresees community-based CP activities focusing on a diversified range of protection needs and tailored to different groups based on age, gender, social role, responsibilities. WWGVC will carry out events and awareness initiatives (in schools and public spaces) as follows:
- Training of 350 teachers on CP contents, mainly focused on protection needs of children with disabilities (CWDs);
- Training of 350 teachers in PSS contents;
- School awareness campaigns on CP and social inclusion of CWDs: teachers trained in CP will sensitize 17,500 students;
- Community-based PSS activities: 6,000 out-of-school children and children at risk of drop out will be reached with tailored events implemented with trained SARC volunteers’

support (volunteers selected among SARC PSS teams) ;
-Community-based awareness sessions on CP and social inclusion of CWDs: 400 community members (mainly educators) will be reached by 40 FGDs facilitated byWW GVC
WWGVC considers that capacity building to teachers/SARC volunteers contributes to the delivery of quality assistance, allowing educators to acquire correct information and methodologies to transfer quality knowledge and skills to targeted groups, finally enhancing their protection and their capacity to address protection needs.
All the activities will be structured in a way to adapt to different target groups of beneficiaries and to the specific context (e.g. rural/urban) and social dynamics of the community where they take place. The Action targets a total of 26,100 individuals, with an estimated cost/beneficiary of around 5.5 USD.

4 - Needs

Based on data from the HNO Protection section and need assessment conducted by WWGVC in Dec. 2020 in the targeted areas, the occurrence of protection issues in communities is a fact: civilians continue to be directly exposed to effects of hostilities resulting in forced displacement, trauma, distress, violence, and discrimination. Based on current population estimates, 1 out of 2 individuals in Syria lives in areas reporting explosive hazard contamination in the last two years. Such hazards have devastating consequences on the lives of children and their families. The crisis is threatening the mental and physical health and overall development of children and youth: teachers and caregivers are reporting violence and bullying among children as worrying behavioral patterns. Disruption of traditional safety nets during the crises aggravates protection concerns such as psychosocial stress especially for vulnerable groups such as children and children living with special needs. The latter, as evidenced by FGDs conducted by WWGVC in the selected areas, are victims of exclusion and social discrimination to a greater extent than it was in times of stability. This also occurs in the Education system, where teachers are not well prepared to deal with the needs of vulnerable children affected by the crises, nor with the specific pedagogical and psychosocial requirements of CWDs. A school-based and community-based approach is therefore necessary, maximizing outreach capacities and resilience. Even though in targeted sub-districts Protection severity scale ranges from 2 to 5, they have been considered due to the integrated nature of the Action with Education (related severity scale from 4 to 6).

5 - Implementation of activities

In order to address protection risks related to explosive devices, the Action includes: Activity 4.1 (MA) Conduct risk education for at-risk groups and Activity 5.1 (CP) Community-based child protection for girls and boys is available. Quality programming, sound management, monitoring, and evaluation mechanism are essential throughout the program cycle. WWGVC’s MEAL Department is structured to allow continuous and regular control on the implementation of monitoring activities at the field level as well as a quality check on the monitoring of indicators. In particular, the MEAL team is composed of a MEAL Manager and a MEAL Officer based in Damascus, and two MEAL Officers based in each targeted governorate. Provided that the required authorizations are granted, the MEAL Manager will perform regular field visits to ensure the correct implementation of monitoring activities. The MEAL Manager will also be in charge of conducting initial training to field officers on data collection and data entry.The MEAL system will ensure:- Routine M&E activities based on a systematic reporting activity and implementation of the M&E plan.- Development of data collection tools, establishing information management mechanisms leading to efficient use of data and informative reports aiming at strengthening internal decision-making process, external and internal accountability.- Inclusion of qualitative information and data triangulation to better monitor indicators, and the achievement of outcome and results.- Monitoring and coordination with the DoE’s and/or SARC’s volunteers for activities directly implemented by them.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

A.4.1 Risk: Creation of wrong expectations among beneficiaries, who could wrongly attribute to teachers delivering RE sessions the ability to remove explosive ordinance; Familiarization with messages delivered at RE sessions may lead beneficiaries to become overconfident/engage in risky behaviors. Likelihood: High Impact:High Mitigation: Clear explanation of intervention and objectives to beneficiaries; Tailoring of messages/RE materials used in schools according to age/gender; Involvement of survivors of explosive hazards as a role model for behavior change.A.5.1-Risk 1.Protection risk faced by girls/boys may persist if community members do not play an active role in the delivery of protection services. Existing traditional/religious practices increasing due to limited community engagement/acknowledgment of certain practices as protection concerns. Girls'/boys' exposure to protection risk due to limited initiatives to enhance children's participation in their own protection. Lack of sustainable protection intervention due to limited involvement of national stakeholders/community members in CP service delivery. Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation: Ensure service mapping is undertaken so that risk mitigation/communication with communities can be proactive/informed; Use sector agreed on key messages to ensure consistency/inform sector of discrepancies if new messaging is required.Risk 2.Existing traditional practices/community structures conflict with CP principles. Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigation: Training of staff as key resource persons to act as trainers/facilitators; Regular engagement/communication with communities; WWGVC adopts complaint mechanism ensuring data protection/confidentiality.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

WWGVC has a child safeguarding policy aimed at ensuring that children’s rights are recognized and respected and that children are protected from any form of violence. The policy contains a set of standards that all WWGVC staff must take into consideration, including:• Placing children’s safety well-being above any other considerations• Reporting any concerns cases of suspected child abuse or concerns about the welfare of a minor• Planning and organizing project activities whilst minimizing risks for children• Avoiding culturally inappropriate physical contacts with children WWGVC is committed to create and maintain an environment where children’s well-being is promoted in every aspect of its operations. Preventions measures include:• AWARENESS RAISING: All new staff are introduced to the Child Safeguarding Policy.• TRAINING: WW-GVC commits itself to conduct periodical training in relation to this Policy to adequately inform the staff, raise their awareness and enhance their capacity in preventing, and dealing with possible. • RISK ASSESSMENT: WWGVC endeavors to reduce these risks to a bare minimum. Regular risk assessments are carried out in order to identify, evaluate and mitigate risks throughout the organization’s operations. • SAFE PROGRAMMING: WWGVC takes proactive measures at every stage of project implementation to avoid causing harm inadvertently whilst mainstreaming best practices.
