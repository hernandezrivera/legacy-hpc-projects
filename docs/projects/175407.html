Project Summary

South Sudan protracted crises have left 7.5 million people in dire need of humanitarian assistance in 2020 despite the signature of the Revitalized peace agreement, and continues to face conflict, natural disasters and climate instability, food insecurity, and lack of basic services. According to the latest IPC analysis for South Sudan published in December 2020, an estimated 7.7 million people are expected to experience acute food insecurity and worse (IPC 3, 4 and 5) in 2021.
SI has started to implement its humanitarian response in South Sudan in 2006. Its long lasting presence has contributed to building a wealth of experience in Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) and Food, Security and Livelihoods (FSL). This allowed SI to pilot large-scale unconditional cash transfers, seeds distribution amongst IDPs and host communities in Western Bahr El Ghazal, Upper Nile, Jonglei and Central Equatoria states. SI aims at supporting the most vulnerable households, who are struggling to restore their livelihoods, in order to help them to improve their food security situation. Currently, SI is running five operational bases: Raja town and Wau town (Western Bahr el-Ghazal), Aburoc/Malakal (Upper Nile), Lankien (Jonglei) and Juba (Central Equatoria). For 2021, SI will focus on linking its first line response, through its EPR and static teams, with activities having longer-term impact in WASH and FSL sectors. In FSL specifically, this will mean emergency livelihood response to quickly replenish the destroyed assets. In second step SI will complement the response, wherever possible, with capacity building to allow for income generation and longer-term food preservation. SI will therefore aim at providing timely, equitable and integrated food security and livelihoods services to the conflict, epidemic and natural disaster affected populations of South Sudan.

1 - Needs

Extreme flooding across the country in 2019-2020, combined with intercommunal violence and ongoing armed clashes have led to displacement, extreme lack of basic needs, an increase in food insecurity, loss of livelihood means in the displacement and further pressure on land and natural resources. South Sudan is projected to reach 7.2 million in IPC Levels 3, 4 and 5 in the lean season of April-July 2021, an increase of 0.7 million from the same period in 2020 and 1.1 million since 2017. COVID-19 had also a severe impact on the economy, limiting movements of goods and persons, impacting directly the most vulnerable that depend on daily goods selling and buying. All SI’s areas of intervention are in IPC Level 3, 4 and Akobo county is at risk of passing to a Level 5 (famine) during the 2021 lean season. Insecurity related to ICV in the wider Jonglei state as well as localised clan conflicts limit the ability to conduct livelihoods as people are unwilling to move to farming lands further from their villages. Exceptional floods have also destroyed livelihoods in many areas in 2020, notably in Khorfulus and Canal where more than 50% of the livestock died as well as 32% for Nyirol. The crops have been removed by floods for 61% of households in Jonglei, and the harvest is not expected sufficient in 2021, leading to a longer lean season. The levels of negative coping strategies such as reducing number of meals or borrowing food/money are high for the vast majority. As mentioned during the FGDs organized by SI in Fashoda, access to fishing grounds is an issue for most fishermen. The lack of canoes also makes transportation and access to markets almost impossible during the rainy season when the roads are blocked by streams and swamps. It is therefore of extreme importance that the population in productive farming areas is able to resume their agricultural activities and thus support their own food security as well as boost the supply of crops and vegetables to the local markets, thus making the food more accessible. This point is especially important given the rising prices in 2020. The average cost of Multi-Sector Minimum Expenditure Basket rose across the country by 46% between November 2019 and November 2020. In combination with the depreciation of the South Sudanese pound the population’s purchasing power and their ability to access sufficient food items in the local markets is drastically decreased. Increased agricultural productivity would therefore allow people to produce their own food as well as providing a supplementary source of income. This analysis is supported by the discussions within the National FSL cluster which came to the conclusion that there is urgent need to “support local production in order to improve livelihoods as well as create labour and income opportunities.”Since 2016 more than 1.5 million South Sudanese have returned to their areas of origin from abroad (33%) or from areas of displacement within the country (67%). Although many of the returns face insecurity and natural disasters, it is important to address the lack of basic needs of the returnees to allow them to settle down permanently and sustain themselves. Especially in the areas where the security situation is relatively safe, such a response would contribute to long term stability of the area. Furthermore, it would allow the utilization of natural resources for the production of food for the country.Recent data from IOM show that with 204,312 returnees Western Bahr el Ghazal is the state with the second highest number of returns since 2016. These return movements should increase throughout 2021, linked with the improvement of the perceived security situation in Raja County. While participating to the Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI) on a bimonthly basis in Raja market, SI FSL team monitored the market trends. It remains limited in supplies and number of traders.

2 - Project activities

SI 2021 strategy in terms of FSL will focus on two axes, following the HRP strategic and FSL sectoral objectives and in a way that prevents the spread and transmission of COVID-19:1) On a short term basis, to reduce the mortality and morbidity for the most vulnerable population in IPC 3 and 4 areas, the first axis of SI’s response will follow the FSL cluster CO2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support;2) On a longer-term basis, to enable vulnerable people to recover from crisis, seek solutions to displacement and rebuild their resilience to acute shocks and chronic stresses in IPC 3 and 4 areas, the second axis will contribute to the FSL cluster CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks. In the first axis, SI will implement the following activities:- Emergency livelihoods recovery: Distribution of basic livelihood assets for farming and fishing – fishing, crop and vegetable seeds and tools; - Trainings on farming and fishing best practices, on crop storage, on cooking practices and basic crop preservation;- Cash distributions to complement and revitalize the markets;- Seed fairs in the areas where traders are available and access feasible.In the second axis, SI will implement the following activities:- Agricultural training, capacity-building: Farmers and fishermen trainings, distribution of booklets, extentionists services, income generation, business skills, pest control, crop preservation, food hygiene.- Creation of farming groups and associations - Creation of farmers’ schools, demonstration plots, kitchen gardening- Rehabilitation of public infrastructure supporting economic activities (road, bridge, market) through cash for work- Distribution of tools and seeds: hand-tools, improved vegetable and crop seeds, ox plows- Seed fairs, using locally sourced seeds- Value additions activities introduction: Solar drying and smoking, Safe crop storage activities, Cooking hygiene training- Pilot on a collaborative garden: through Cash for Work, farm a land and inject the production into the local market with affordable prices (agri-business) for women or youth groups- Strengthening local market linkages creation: Training and support for IGAs and basic marketing skills, Distribution of basic means of transportation (bicycles, carts)- Linking new FSL activities with awareness on avoiding overexploitation of natural resources through new livelihood alternatives- DRR trainings and natural resources management- Mainstreaming protection in FSL activities: Identification of vulnerable HHs for trainings and distribution, Increase woman and youth participation and empowerment, selection in livelihood groups, pay attention to protection risks in the cash distributions: distribute in the health facilities (if SAM patients) if possible, less information sharing on the date of distribution, use of vouchers if possible- Fencing of the farms and training to avoid intercommunal conflict

3 - Project indicators

SI implements an extensive framework of tools for the monitoring and evaluation of the activities conducted. All the indicators are designed in order to closely monitor the meeting of the project objectives. KII and FGD will be conducted in the targeted areas at different stages of the project in order to assess the distribution modalities, the type of livelihood practiced and the type of crops cultivated in the area and adapt them to the targeted population needs. SI will carry out post distribution monitoring surveys covering all the distributions during the project, to gather information on satisfaction of beneficiaries in terms of timeliness and relevance of input distribution, quality and quantity distributed, overall organization and modalities of distribution including security and protection issues. A baseline Knowledge, Attitudes and Practices (KAP) is done at the beginning of all the projects, to assess the KAP of the communities in each area and adapt the capacity-building activities to the needs and what the communities would be more sensitive to. SI will also carry out post-harvest monitoring surveys to evaluate the impact of crops given to the farmers, their level of production, their appliance of the trainings and the challenges they encountered to lower their level of production. An endline survey will also be conducted at the end of the projects to support the final reporting and assess level of indicators achievement, satisfaction of beneficiaries and lessons learnt of the project to be shared with the donor for further projects. FGD on AAP will be implemented at the beginning of each static or EPR intervention and at the end, to adapt activities in between and assess the level of AAP as per the beneficiaries’ perception following AAP framework policies. SI will apply the following actions and tools to ensure that the objectives are reached and needs covered: Activity Progress Update (APU) tools and MEAL plan; Follow-up of the different assessments and interventions implemented by the project teams; Weekly update to state level Clusters on achievements and challenges; Monthly reporting to the Clusters through 5Ws and GBVIMS reports; Routine field visits by the Coordination teams. At SI coordination level, a program coordinator and a MEAL assistant will monitor the implementation, achievement of indicators, and quality of the program using the APU, and with support from SI HQ.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

SI will do a market assessment before any project, tailored to the context and the area. SI can carry out rapid market assessments to complement longer term market assessments to determine if the market is functional enough to absorb cash assistance to vouchers and avoid create shortages in the market or inflation in the areas. This assessment will also try to identify vendors willing to accept the vouchers and the type and quality of items that can be found on each market. SI is taking part of the Joint Market Monitoring Initiative led by REACH every month in several areas of the country, to contribute to the determining of Multi-Sector Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (MSSMEB).SI will also link with market information management systems to monitor the price of items and early warning systems in case of a big inflation in the price of basic food items for instance, or pest infest in an area.While implementing a project, SI will promote linkages between buyers and sellers, as well as the whole value chain, boosting local economy and business development.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash and Voucher assistance in the FSL sector can be very useful and relevant for the following reasons:- Distributing cash or vouchers instead of in-kind - which is food most of the time - will help develop and reinforce markets: indeed, injecting cash and having people buying FSL items on the markets will reinforce traders’ capacities and income generation at the same time. It will have a benefit at different levels of the supply chain. - It will increase populations’ autonomy and independence from humanitarian support, as well as increasing their dignity. Indeed, receiving cash or voucher, especially if they are not too restrictive, will allow people to choose what they will purchase, according to their preferences and needs. If they can choose the FSL support with voucher or cash, they can also adapt their livelihoods to the seasonal calendar, depending on when they receive the support, they will choose farming or fishing kit, if it is coming at the right season for instance.- It can provide useful information to the humanitarian actors to determine if the real needs of the population have been targeted. Indeed, if communities receive cash, and purchase X or Y, whereas humanitarian thought the priority needs were Z, it will lead them changing the angle and assessment of priorities. Sometimes in the surveys, people are giving answer on their needs to ‘satisfy’ the humanitarian actor and receive support but it’s not their ‘real’ or priority need.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

During the FGD about AAP with the population of the affected area, SI will start by presenting the principles and mechanisms for which SI are accountable. For the complaints made in terms of PSEA and CP policy, when CRM committees can be created, one man and one woman will be selected by the community as PSEA focal points. They will be considered focal points for the community. At every activity, awareness will be raised with regards to their availability to receive particularly sensitive feedback, especially regarding the behavior of the project staff. They will receive specific sensitivity training and direct communication line with the consortia manager and the PSEA focal point of each partner, will be provided. This will ensure that all PSEA and CP policy complaints reach the coordination offices without the program staff on the ground being aware. These complaints will be stored in a separate database compiled at the coordination level of each partner and following specific confidentiality SOPs. Complaints or issues not under the framework of this project will be referred to the other actors or clusters.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Ensuring all different groups of beneficiaries and communities have access to safe complaint and feedback mechanisms is one of the first steps in mainstreaming protection across the activities of the project. Each CRM committee is selected with the communities directly and ensuring all groups of the society and particularly women with specific needs, children, People living with Disabilities and individuals who are not literate have a representative. Each CRM committee will be trained in the standard procedure for taking complaints and feedbacks. If the beneficiaries cannot write their feedback, they will be able to reach program staff and particularly community volunteers who will also receive CRM training and will be available to receive complaints and feedback during collective activities and household visits. Whenever possible, distribution sites complaint boxes and desks will be set up, as well as a phone line in the areas where network is available. On a regular basis, the CRM committee will gather the complaints received by this mechanism.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

To gather complaints and feedbacks on the implementation of activities (including for example delays of implementation, complaints about selection of beneficiaries, etc.), CRM committees will be selected with the communities directly and ensuring all groups of the society and particularly women, children and PWD have a representative. Each CRM committee will be trained in the standard procedure for taking complaints and feedbacks. Program staff and particularly community volunteers will also receive CRM training and will be available to receive complaints and feedback during collective activities and household visits. Additionally, offices and distribution sites complaint boxes and desks will be set up. A hotline is not relevant in the areas where phone network is absent. On a regular basis, the CRM committee will gather the complaints received by this mechanism and by the community volunteers, and forward to the coordination at field and Juba levels. Each CRM committee will share on a regular basis directly with the Field Coordinator and SI coordination office the database that compiles all the complaints and feedback received as well as the measures taken at field level to be remediated. The community leaders and RRC will also be involved and sensitized to this topic, especially for very sensitive complaints that could concern staff members and sharing information to be handled with respect to confidentiality. SI provides accessible and timely information to affected populations on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices, and facilitate a dialogue between the organization and affected populations over information provision. SI will always include the most vulnerable in its messages (i.e. women, children, elderlies and disabled) and make sure the printed and Information Education and Communication materials are properly transmitted in the local dialect. The same materials will also include information on the Complaints and Response Mechanism so that beneficiaries can access to the mechanism in a safe and confidential way. FGD on Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) will be implemented at the beginning and at the end of each intervention and to adapt activities in between and assess the level of AAP as per the beneficiaries’ perception following SI AAP framework policies.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Before the implementation of activities, SI will organize FGDs focused on the identification of beneficiaries’ perceived levels of vulnerability to define the selection criteria. The beneficiaries’ opinions will be crosschecked with findings of the assessments conducted at the beginning of the project. Moreover, SI Program Managers report quality checks based on beneficiaries’ feedback to the Coordination level on a monthly basis. Each project is analyzed through this quality check and followed up in the internal monitoring tool (Activity Progress Update). Based on the findings, Coordination and field bases determine to what extent it is necessary to adapt the project and messages to the needs captured through feedback and complaints. Should any issue raised exceed the project’s scope, SI will advocate this situation to the relevant Clusters (at National, State and County level), local authorities and other humanitarian partners to tackle the issue and ensure onset needs can be covered.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

IPC 4 and 5 indicators are a trigger for EPR response in SI. All the activities aim at giving access to basic services to vulnerable populations in these areas. In static interventions, SI aims at boosting local food and agricultural production through provision of seeds, tools, inputs, assets, trainings, market linkages, safe food storage, preservation, post-harvest processing. SI will also support people on movement, contribute to bringing access to services to displaced populations and host communities, as well as in areas of return – adapting the livelihood support to these different targets.Linking FSL activities with nutrition, with WASH through irrigation, or with Health will aim at reducing the food insecurity targeting its multiple root causes at the same time.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

SI will prioritize vulnerable households with protection risks, and provide them with integrated, multi-sectoral projects, between WASH and FSL, but also with Protection, Health and nutrition. The selection assessment database will comprise a scoring taking into account the levels of vulnerability, types of vulnerabilities, number of people in the households to select the targeted beneficiaries.For instance, the distribution of kits will depend on vulnerability criteria during selection, answering specific needs of women, children, elderly, persons with disability. Indeed, if erderly cannot carry out livelihoods, SI will provide them with cash. The distributions and trainings will be done as close as possible to the places where communities live to reduce distance to access humanitarian aid. SI will take into account protection issues for its selection of locations, such as demonstrations plots for instance.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The MAM children under 5 are one of the main vulnerability criteria for distribution: indeed, SI is prioritizing families with malnourished children under 5 for distributions of livelihood kits, along with a training on how to use them and basic business skills if relevant.The main areas where SI is targeting GAM cases are the ones in IPC levels 3, 4, and 5 meaning Juba, Wau, Raja, Fashoda, Malakal, Nyirol, Akobo, Pigi/Canal, Maiwut, Melut counties. SI is also intervening in close partnership with Nutrition and Health actors, especially in Western Bahr el Ghazal, to complement their support to malnourished children and individuals with a livelihood support.
