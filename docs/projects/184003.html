Project Summary

Medair will expand in 2022 to include sites in Blue Nile (host communities) with the main objective to contribute to the reduction of preventable excess mortality and morbidity in the selected host communities who are identified as highly vulnerable by providing integrated primary health care and nutrition services. Medair’s plan is to exit when all key targets are achieved inclusive of a well refurbished and rehabilitated (nutrition and EPI integrated) health facility with a clear sustainable medical commodities supply pipeline and a strengthened and reliable competent health staff capacity or an available competent local health partner to continue the project service delivery. On the other hand, an unplanned exit may occur as a result of lack of funding, no (continued) permission to operate in these areas, or high-level insecurity especially in high-risk conflict areas, etc.). No doubt, in any of the stated circumstances, we will properly communicate this to the appropriate authorities.

1 - Needs

Blue Nile state has been in a protracted crisis since the 2011 conflict between Khartoum and the SPLM-North. The conflict has resulted in an outflow of refugees into South Sudan and Ethiopia as well as movement of IDPs between its localities. The recent signing of the peace agreement between both parties has brought relative stability and security as well as anticipation of a high flow of returnees from South Sudan numbering approximately 200,000 back into Blue Nile. In fact, the locality of Bau has already seen more than 528 returned families with more expected in the coming months. The UNHCR BaNVA 2021 report revealed that across both the refugee population and the host communities, host communities residents in Blue Nile were found to experience the greatest health vulnerability. In November 2020, Medair conducted health assessments in Damazine (population: 298,377), Al Roseires (population: 248,903) , Bau (population: 167,251), Geisan (population: 113,879) and El Kurmuk (population: 132,435) and Wadi El Mahi (population: 101,200) localities. The assessment results are consistent with the UNHCR BaNVA 2021 report that host communities reported to experience a great health vulnerability, it is believed that this is now compounded with the impact of the heavy rain flooding in these areas. Major findings are: Urgent need for nutrition services as these services were absent in many facilities, high unmet need for malnourished children due to long-term out-of-stock of therapeutic foods, lack of proper waste management, a significant number of non-functioning latrines were seen during visit to the health facilities, very poor quality of care as renovations are urgently needed in almost all facilities, poor data recording, and insufficient and poor essential healthcare services.The current state health system which serves a population of 1.27 million people possess significant gaps in health service provision. In regard to food security that affects overall health and nutrition, Blue Nile is classified at level 3 (crisis)1. The state is vulnerable to natural disasters and has annual rainy seasons that leave most of the state entirely inaccessible for six months.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The project aims to reach a minimum of 12,000 beneficiaries, Medair will rehabilitate and support two existing MoH health facilities each in Geisan and Baw localities of Blue Nile state to reach both host communities and returnees in those localities. Each health facility will provide antenatal care sessions to provide essential primary and secondary health care services with a 200 targeted number of antenatal care visits to be conducted. In the course to increase access to health services for the vulnerable host communities, Medair will rehabilitate and/or refurbish these health facilities with the provision of medical equipment, and recruit and strengthen the capacity of health care providers and community health care workers to provide essential health services. This is achieved by providing formal and on-job training to 42 health staff on variety of topics, however, this will strongly focus on COVID-19 secure delivery of integrated packages of health interventions including curatives medical consultations (3000), routine EPI (500), IMNCI consultations (800), child growth monitoring (500), clinical management of acute malnutrition, ANC/PNC follow-up (2500), family planning (100) and provision of care to survivors of SGBV (100) as well as referrals to other specialized service centre (150) as appropriate through health partners with proper outcome reporting procedures. Medair Skilled birth attendants will conduct 250 vaginal deliveries and conduct postnatal sessions to these new mothers. The project activities also include referrals to other secondary and specialized health care services (200) as appropriate through health partners with proper outcome reporting procedures to provide essential primary, secondary and specialised health care services to the targeted population. In addition, Medair will build MHPSS capacity of health staff and volunteers (42) by providing them tools to support people with psychosocial needs and refer those who need more care to the clinic for further mental health assessment. Medair will set up an integrated OTP and TSFP unit within the health facility which will provide screenings, treatment, referrals, and follow-up of acute malnutrition cases to ensure timely recovery to the targeted beneficiaries; this service delivery will strictly adhere to the Sudan COVID-19 guidelines. Medair will cover 8 OTP/TSFP sites. Where safely possible, (500) MUAC screenings will take place in the health facility, however, alternate methods will be explored, where mothers screen for MAM or SAM criteria and refer their children to facilities for therapies such as RUTF, RUSF and CSB. This project activity is planned to achieve 62 children (aged 6-59 months) that received outpatient SAM treatment and 250 children (aged 6-59 months) that received MAM treatment. Additionally, micronutrients, deworming, and antibiotics will be provided as per OTP and TSFP guidelines to 510 children, with (10) referrals to the nearest stabilization center for severe SAM cases. These services will primarily target PLWs, mothers and children aged 6-59 months who are particularly vulnerable to the health and nutrition impacts of displacement and disease. Medair will not, at this stage, be implementing CMAM-I/MAMI. Rather, a targeted 50 at-risk children aged less than 6 months will be referred to the stabilization center through the nutrition partners. Dissemination of messaging on optimal infant feeding and other critical health information will be conducted to 1838 targeted people (1,370w, 468g). In this regard, Medair’s CHVs will be trained to spread facility- and community-based messaging to the targeted groups, and inclusively through Mother Support Groups. Messaging delivery will follow strict national COVID-19 guidelines by limiting the group sizes, meeting locations, social distancing, and PPEs will be used in messaging models. Medair will integrate WASH by providing WASH at HFs and explore working with FSL partners.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Medair acknowledges and supports that there is a need to create and maintain a healthy environment to live in, otherwise, the targeted beneficiaries and their neighbouring communities are put at higher-risk especially in humanitarian crises. In this view, to avoid humanitarian response's negative impact on the environment, this project has the bare minimal environmental impact. There are no activities of deforestation in the course of the project implementation, and the procurement, transport and choice of materials, or land and natural resource use will not degrade, but instead will protect the environment further. The waste management and safe water supply are planned to create an environment free of dangerous and toxic waste inclusive of human excreta which is essential for beneficiaries' dignity, safety, health and well-being.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Specific considerations will be made at all stages of the project (assessment, planning, registration, verification, distribution and post distribution monitoring) to ensure fair,safe and equal access for all. This will ensure inclusion of men, women, child headed households, PLWs, PLWDs and the elderly within the consultation of needs, planning and follow up. Considerations will be made, in coordination with community members and relevant organizations to ensure fair, safe and equal access to registration and distribution activities. This will include risk assessments to establish mitigating measures such as timing, location, layout, function and support given at the distribution and or service locations. Medair will ensure that protection mainstreaming is included throughout all phases of the project. Medair will engage and coordinate with the Camp Management partners and Protection partners in Health, Nutrition, WASH, Shelter and NFI components. Medair will ensure that the needs of vulnerable groups are prioritized (including people with disabilities) and addressed during the interventions where key beneficiaries will be consulted and encouraged to actively participate.
