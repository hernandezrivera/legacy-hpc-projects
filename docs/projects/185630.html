Project Summary

"Through the proposed project, gaps in availability of tailored adolescent and youth programming will be addressed through the provision of life skills and life enhancement education. This component aligns with – and furthers the achievement of – objective 4 of the Protection Sector Strategy. These activities will encourage youth and adolescent girls to realize their full potential in a space where they can feel comfortable and able to express themselves. These interventions will moreover empower adolescent and youth to have agency in their own lives and communities and thus improved opportunities to realize their full potential. In addition, the programme will reduce the vulnerability of adolescent girls by supporting caregivers to understand the needs of their adolescent children and fostering the development of adolescents under their care via the implementation of caregiver curriculum. The construction/upgrading of facilities will provide the physical spaces for girls and their female caregivers to safely participate in the structured sessions and to gather informally as well, which is an important indirect element of women and girls safe space programming.
This project will train girls and their female caregivers on producing reusable sanitary pads. This component directly addresses menstruation-related barriers to reliable access to menstrual hygiene products in relatively environmentally sustainable manner.
This project will enhance capacity of Girl Shine participants on youth-led initiatives to cascade their knowledge through art, photojournalism, talk show and drama. This directly links to policy and strategy objectives to build skills and create space to encourage girls’ leadership and to consider mental health and psychosocial aspects as important as physical health for overall wellbeing and providing spaces for creative expression.
This project will also support to ensure effective GBV prevention and response training, gender-responsive humanitarian action and effective localization for crisis-affected women, women’s groups and organizations.
Additionally, key community members, including men and boys, religious leaders, community leaders will be engaged to mobilize and sensitize other community member on gender equality and menstrual health management. These trained community members will lead community initiatives for creating awareness on GBV, Gender equality, SRH and menstrual health management. The program will promote change agents among the adolescent boys and youth by implementing Girl Shine and Caregiver Curriculum, Champions of Change curriculum, Early Marriage Prevention Curriculum, with improve knowledge and understanding on gender equality and menstrual health educations. This component clearly links to the conviction visible throughout sector best practice that engaging whole communities – and men and boys in particular – is essential to reducing the threat of violence and bolstering the overall supportive environment around women and girls.
We are planning to implement this project in the camp 1W, 8W, 11, 12, 13, 15, 20Ext, 27 and our existing facilities is only at camp 15 (supported by UNICEF) among the targeted locations. If we fails to establish new center, we are also palnning to use other organizations facility on the targeted locations with signing a formal MoU.
The project is planned for providing a range of accessible and rapid channels of communication with affected populations, and use them to inform affected populations about procedures, structures and processes that affect them, so that they are able to make informed decisions and choices followed by GBV guiding principles.
In addition some training and awareness session, how to provide instant response or to create a response team among the community. Some SOP will be developed for the DRR aspects like; cyclone, fire and flood issues. Also need contingency fund to be continued supportto the vulnerable populations during covid 19.

1 - Identified Needs

Protection risks remain of grave concern more than four years into the Rohingya refugee crisis. The congested nature of the camps poses serious safety, security, and wellbeing risks, particularly for women and girls, who continue to be at disproportionate risk of GBV, including domestic violence, forced/child marriage, and exploitation and trafficking. The current COVID-19 crisis increases the risks for women and girls as forced and voluntary isolation in many contexts is proven to lead to an increase in intimate partner violence, women and girls engaging in risky coping strategies, lack of access to SRH, and increased stress for women and girls. These risks are heavily compounded by the massive reduction in the availability of protection and GBV services. Further, men are the household level decision makers, reducing the economic power of women and girls. This combined with conservative religious beliefs reduces women’s mobility in the community and even engagement in leadership. In addition, low awareness of available services and deep-rooted socio-cultural barriers limit the ability of survivors to access services. Reduced access/availability of basic goods and services for vulnerable populaions. Risk significant harm to physical or mental well being also increased among the vulnerale groups.
