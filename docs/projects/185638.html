Project Summary

In 2022, Community Based Protection will reach host and camp community people with specific needs like the elderly, disabled, single women in the target areas as well as inform communities about what, where and how access the available protection and other services. BRAC will address protection concerns in Rohingya and host communities based on protection monitoring information and enable the communities to lead in the provision of protection services via Information Service Centres and community groups and legal services.
BRAC will monitor protection environment in the Rohingya camp through mixed monitoring methodologies and tools and by engaging relevant stakeholders. BRAC will identify Person with Specific Needs and People at Heightened Risk, provide referral and case management support as required. In the camp and host community, BRAC will ensure project beneficiaries are capacitated to have better self-management and empowerment within their communities. The community outreach members in the camp will respond to the protection and assistance needs of member of their communities, arrange awareness sessions and social dialogue on various protection issues to promote CBP and focus on broader challenges undermining social cohesion. BRAC will continue to support discussion on environmental issues primarily on slope degradation, landslides, deforestation, water logging, water scarcity and contamination, solid waste management and ways to place measures that will reduce negative impact during natural disasters and accidents. BRAC will also build capacity of the Rohingya volunteers to strengthen emergency preparedness , response to natural disasters, incuding Covid-19 in conjunction with site management and other relavent sectors. 3% of the total project amount is geared towards volunteer remuneration who play a key role in message disseminations, volunteer lead project initiatives and outreach activities. Community center and information centres will facilitate a variety of activities to bring people together for creative and constructive individual expression and social dialogue to enable safety and dignity and meaningful access, participation and empowerment of affected people. Feedback mechanisms such as complaint boxes in service points, hotlines numbers for PSEA focal, sensitization on right-based assistance in all existing information points will be implemented. BRAC’s strict safeguarding policy, PSEA, community resilience, AGD, Disability Inclusion will be applied across all its projects and tap into BRAC's existing PSEA TOT training pool to engage with relevant stakeholders, and organize evidence-based advocacy and capacity building sessions in PSEA and AGD for service providers in other sectors and relevant local government on protection mainstreaming. BRAC currently has an assessment system in place to evaluate risk of sexual exploitation and abuse to capture incidents and protect. BRAC has in place a strict safeguarding policy and code of conduct outlining such risks, the impact and penalties. In host community, BRAC will provide legal aid to resolve intercommunity disputes, provide assisstance on cases related to gender-based violence and mediation and counciling on gender based protection risks. BRAC will regularly consult, provide information and implementation updates to the different ISCG sector coordinators, government authorities and relevant Sector and Sub-Sector Working Groups including representation in Protection Working Group, Male Engagement Task Team, Case Management Task Team., train community volunteers on PSEA matters to ensure BRAC’s commitment to AAP and GBV principles and do no harm.

1 - Identified Needs

The Joint Multi Sector Need Assessment (October 2021) reports concerns on intimate partner violence, child marriage, sexual harassment, threats by local people/armed groups, informal mediation to resolve gender based violence. These are not only in the camps but also prevalent in the surrounding host communities. Lack of youth engagement in social cohesion and resilience building by participating in cultural activities contributes to increasing girl protection risks in the camps. JMSNA (October 2021) also reports child marriage takes place in Rohingya households as a negative coping strategy either for the perceived need to protect a girl through marriage or reduce the economic burden on households. The necessity of regular monitoring of the protection environment, community based protection and legal protection evolves from the extent of protection risks and concerns resulting in violation of refugees' rights and obstructing meaningful access to their rights and the available services. Safety and security risks have increased during the pandemic for all, but pre-existing gendered risks are exacerbated. BRAC carried out an internal gender analysis and needs assessment in September 2021 which prioritizes increasing need for community-based. In the JMSNA (October 2021), community engagement was highlighted as a gap with efforts needing to be scaled up, including through the rapid establishment of community centers, recreational and protection spaces as critical infrastructures for the community. Protection Monitoring is essential to identify the persons at heightened protection risik and including effective protection assessments of the situation of populations of concern. This will lead to informing and designing effective responses by efficient utilization of humanitarian actors, and also help establish a common inter-agency understanding. Noting the importance of maintaining the link between monitoring and devising strategies, regular situation analysis of protection environment will provide an evidence-based knowledge for medium and long terms planning. Situation Analysis of Protection Risks at Host Community is Cox’s Bazar, a study conducted by BRAC HCMP in 2021 reported at of 47% of household engaged in child marriage and 42% of household with girls face risks of sexual and abuse and violence. There is limited access to formal justice in the host community. The current formal justice system is plagued by structural issues, lengthy wait times, prohibitive expense, and corruption. There is an overall high level of reliance on informal resolutions for inter-community disputes. Additionally, low percentage of survivors (20%) reports of gender based violence cases to local authorities due to fear of retaliation. Host community union is surrounded by camps and continue to contend with inter-communal disputes with Rohingya refugees. 63% of the population have direct interaction with the Rohingya community whilst 58% of households has reported being unhappy or very unhappy with the presence of Rohingya refugees in their community. 68% of the participants of the community level assessment of the protection sector reported that frequency of drug abuse is high. Day laborers are losing their job opportunity due to the availability of Rohingya workers at a very cheap remuneration. Due to unemployment, people are engaging in drug abuse which are leading to financial crisis and highly contributing in increasing domestic violence on women and children including physical torture, mental abuse, humiliation, economic disadvantages, deprivation from resources and opportunities. 64% participants reported that the general crime have happening frequently. In situations of displacement where the willingness of community members to cooperate with each other to better cope with threats and improve resilience is weakened, tensions become protracted. There is a need to mitigate protection risks by developing a community self-protection mechanism
