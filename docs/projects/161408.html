Project Summary

This project proposal will facilitate construction of five (5) sustainable latrines, rehabilitation of one hundred (100) wells and hygiene education to more than 1,600 through WFSS training sessions in the villages. In these training sessions, villagers will be educated on hand washing, water safety and safe disposal of feces.
This proposal falls under WASH Cluster (CO3): Provide timely/sustainable, equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs, host communities and returnees.
WASH temporary and sustainable services will address the vital needs, public health and protection issues through an AAP community approach to target the acute vulnerabilities based on inter/cross-sectorial evaluations and analyses.
Project Activities:
• Construction of sustainable latrines
• Rehabilitation of one hundred (100) wells
• Hygiene education to 1,600 villagers

1 - Needs

Water for South Sudan (WFSS), founded in Rochester, New York, USA in 2003, delivers direct, trans-formative and sustainable quality-of-life service to the people of South Sudan by efficiently providing access to clean, safe water and improving hygiene and sanitation practices in areas of need. WFSS has consistently been delivering WASH programming since 2005 when the first well was drilled. The local leadership has navigated country's challenges without interruption to the work. WFSS is based in Wau and has worked in the Upper Nile, Bahr El Ghazal, and Equatoria regions. To date, the organization has drilled 400 wells and aims to drill 40 new wells each drilling season. The organization currently has one drilling team comprised of ten local South Sudanese employees, and it has a plan to add one more drilling team so that many areas in the region are reached and provided with safe drinking water. WFSS has also rehabilitated 72 wells and plans to rehabilitate 100 wells in the next drilling seasons. The purpose of this program is to return to WFSS's older wells to repair broken parts, upgrade the cement platforms around the well, replace the animal feeding trough, and improve the well design to current standards. The rehabilitation team also responds to requests near the main Operation Center in Wau to rehabilitate wells originally drilled by other organizations. In this scenario, the team visits the broken well and employs necessary measures to make the well functional. This project proposal will facilitate construction of five (5) sustainable latrines in selected primary schools, health centers, rehabilitation of one hundred (100) wells, and hygiene education to the villagers in Bahr El Ghazal region. Our main aim for this project is to reach more 50,000 people during the life of the project and continue to have similar projects in other regions in South Sudan.

2 - Activities

1. Construction of five (5) sustainable latrines in selected primary schools and health centers and help more 4,000 people with sanitation. 2. Rehabilitation of one hundred (100) wells by providing broken parts, upgrading the cement platforms around the wells, replacing the animals troughs, improving the well design to current standards. 3. Hygiene education to more than 50,000 villagers in Bahr El Ghazal region in areas such as hand washing, water safety, safe disposal of feces/stools, and prevention of diseases.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes, beneficiaries' right will be legally recognized. For example, sustainable latrines are designed to contain male and female facilities and with capacity to serve more 800 students and teachers. They are also designed to accommodate disable people so that they have access to these facilities. We do all these so that everyone is accommodated and have access to these facilities. On the other hand, each time the drilling and rehabilitation teams visit a village, we make sure that they are accompanied by a trained team of hygiene education. Each team comprises of one male and female trainer on it. WFSS uses the participatory hygiene and sanitation transformation (PHAST) program to engage local individuals and encourage the participation of women. The hygiene education curriculum is two days long. Four main areas of hygiene training include hand washing, water safety, disposal of feces/stools, and prevention of diseases. Our hygiene trainers work with four men and four women from each village who can then share information with others in the village. We also provided translation service during M&E that views from illiterates are also listened to and the data collected is used to improve how we implement our projects. We do all this to protect the right of beneficiaries and individuals participating in our projects.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes, the design of this project includes everyone. The IDPs, returnees, refugees and their hosts are the beneficiaries of this project and must have equal and unimpeded access to the benefits provided by this project. Through this project, everyone will be reached and have a chance to benefit from the services provided by this project. WFSS is committed to delivering on their mission. The organization strives to improve efficiencies through regular and deliberate evaluative activities to examine progress towards the goals and mission. The Water for South Sudan M&E Team regularly visits previously drilled and rehabilitated wells to monitor their status. Monitoring and evaluation data are then used to improve processes and procedures. For the above reason, WFSS will ensure that everyone benefits from this project by periodically sending in its M&E team to collect data on the beneficiaries and ask people how they are accessing services rendered to the communities. The collected data will then be used to improve access to services by all.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, the project is linked to government and community systems because the National Ministry of Water and Irrigation and state Ministries for Physical Infrastructure provide water, hygiene and sensation services to the people in urban and rural areas. The community systems also support initiative for the provision of water, hygiene and sanitation services to the people in urban as well as in rural areas. So, by providing these services to the people in both urban and rural areas, the project also supports government and community systems in their attempts to provide these services to people.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, it's aligned with national objectives because project will include everyone in its services such as the IDPs, returnees, refugees and host communities.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes, the protection environment is sufficiently safe/stable because the locations where these services are provided are protected by the community policing and those who try to cause problem are arrested and punished for their deeds. On the other hand, conflict that arise over competition over scared resources is reduced in these communities because provision of water, hygiene and sanitation services to communities brings people to live together and share resources among themselves without conflict. People like to settle where there is enough water for people and their livestock. Therefore, having enough water for everyone in all communities means peace among IDPs, refugees, returnees and their hosts. Because, lack of water in a particular community causes serious conflict when people compete over scare resources. It also makes it difficult for the host communities to welcome refugees, returnees or IDPs to their community if there is no enough enough Water resource for everyone. So, this project will lead to peaceful coexistence among among refugees, returnees, IDPs and hosts because there will be enough water for everyone in the community.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, the project includes sustainability plans after handover. the community members will be trained on hygiene and sanitation and will continue to train others, while schools and health centers will be trained on how to raise money to empty the latrines when they are full. For example, when WFSS completed its pilot project at Zogolona Primary School in Wau by constructing a sustainable latrine in 2018, one immediate area of concern was how the facility could be maintained after the project implementation by paying for waste removable? After this question, WFSS employees worked with leaders at Zogolona Primary School and designed a system to raise enough money for this service and it was a success. Now, the leaders at Zolgolona Primary School raise money to empty their latrine whenever it's full and they are being successful in maintenance of their latrine. As a result of this success at Zolgolona Primary School, WFSS plans to expand sustainable sanitation programming at additional schools and health centers by following the same model. On other the hand, the community will be trained be trained on how to raise money for the repair of their wells when they break down. So, with these plans in place, the sustainability of these services will continue beyond the period of the project. These plans will also teach the communities how to take ownership of services rendered to them through project implemented in their communities.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members by making sure that individuals with disabilities, those who are not literate, women and girls, are provided with tools that allow them to share their opinions about the project. For example, those who speak Dinka can provided with translators who speak Dinka Language while questions translated to them and answers are translated and recorded in English.
