Project Summary

based on comprehensive needs assessment carried out during 2019 for beekeepers in North West Syria (NWS), Seeds of Hope (SOH) aims to protect and develop the livelihood of 100 Households working in beekeeping (in Idlib/Harim and Aleppo/Al Bab & Azaz) by providing them with financial, technical, and entrepreneurial support that enhance productivity and marketability of their honey, in order to create feasible income that saves their dignities and sustains their businesses and protect them from negative coping strategies under the stress of conflict.
The project include the study of Honey value chain in the local context and finding solutions for main barriers hindering this social/economic activity from reaching its maximum limits.
The project will provide the following services for the beekeepers targeted:
1- Vocational/Professional Training
2- Productive Assets
3- Operational Support
4- Tools and Equipment
5- Agricultural Inputs
On the other hand, the Project will support beekeepers by buying their production of honey under specified criteria (and distribution to people with special needs for honey) and helping them to reach new markets for selling their production and promote their businesses.

4 - Needs

The project will address the following assessed needs:1- Productive Assets owned by beekeeper (quantity, quality, and sufficiency for supporting livelihood of HH)2- Professional Practices followed by beekeepers and business experience (maturity level)3- General constrains aborting beekeeping activity (Security, Checkpoints, Agricultural practices ...etc.)4- Availability of inputs necessary for production and affordability to beekeepers.Seeds of Hope carried out primary needs assessment for beekeepers starting 8/4/2019 and the MEAL activities are continuous and ongoing. Needs Assessment Clarification (by the date of this revision) The NA answer the following questions:At Individual Level:1- Is beekeeping generating feasible income for the beekeeper in question?2- If Not! what are the limitations and gaps in his practices and assets?3- What are the possible interventions to close the gaps and enhance the beekeeper livelihood?At Global Level:1- What are the major issues hindering/threatening the profession in the local context2- What are the bottlenecks in the value chain of honey,3- What Can be done at social/political level to ease the threats and open new possibilities for beekeepers in the region?The NA is under progress (37 Beekeeper covered so far at the date of this revision) Beekeepers Selection Criteria "in brief":1- beekeeping as the main source of income for HH,2- the beekeeper is in business,3- beekeeper having the minimum assets necessary for income generation, 4- beekeeper needs assistance, having a gap or limitation that makes him weak/threatened compared to other standing beekeepers.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project activities will be implemented by professional agricultural engineers specialized in beekeeping, and utilizing reliable connection and relationship in the local communities targeted by the project, and according to FSL Cluster framework to standardize the intervention.The project Intervention (activities) will focus on the following: Expert beekeeper (Consultant) will evaluate the beekeepers businesses/livelihoods from the following aspects:1- Professional practices of beekeepers in utilizing existing beekeeping assets in production (to be followed by Vocational Training whenever necessary). 2- Supporting small beekeepers to increase their production and generate suitable income, (to be achieved by distribution of production assets such as "beehives", "queens"...etc.)3- Explore Global solutions for the profession at the value chain level, to open new markets and possibilities for beekeepers and related businesses. This project is integrated with ERL Cluster

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk/Threat 1: Sale or exchange of livelihood kits at lesser value,Likelihood: medium, Impact: medium,Mitigating Measure: Consult community throughout program cycle to determine the reasoning behind the sale/exchange of assistance and solicit their feedback with regards to solutions,Monitoring: Enhance data collection: (i) align PDM tool with global standards; where possible, (ii) collect qualitative data through FGDs with different segments of the community; and (iii) consistently review and address contents of feedback mechanisms.Resources: Staff time to: (i) enhance tools; (ii) conduct FGDs; and (iii) regularly monitor feedback mechanism. Protection Risk/Threat 2: Risk of theft of Livelihood kits or inputs given,Likelihood: medium, Impact: high,Mitigating Measure: Agencies provide feedback and accountability mechanisms to different groups of users; work closely with all partners on the ground,Monitoring: Agency field monitoring and consultation with communities; Proper stock management system in place.Resources: Dedicated staff member and good storage management. Protection Risk/Threat 3: Cash exacerbates existing tension in HHs and leads to domestic violence,Likelihood: high, Impact: high,Mitigating Measure: ensure coordination among cash implementing organisations, such that they apply best practice and share knowledge and experience,Monitoring: collect qualitative data, e.g. through FGDs with different segments of the community,Resources: monitoring staff and time for training on cash and voucher programming.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The project took in consideration the critical situation in Idlib based on the escalation reported in January 2020, therefore, provisional measures were taken to control all activities from redundant office in Al Bab city in Northern Aleppo in case the situation worsen.
