Project Summary

Save the Children (SC) will work closely to engage 1,400 Rohingya refugees and 2,092 host communities in small-scale agriculture, cash support, and plantation activities that will foster self-reliance, food security, dietary diversity, access to fresh foods and environmental sustainability. Agriculture activities focus mainly on homestead vegetable gardening and vertical gardening. The project will have a training component for the beneficiaries on improved agricultural production in small gardens. The technical training will be followed with distribution of seeds, fertilizers, fingerlings, and livestock components for the beneficiary households. The monitoring for the output will include key food security indicators on the supported households, as well as income generating indicators. Additionally, behaviour change communication (BCC) and awareness activities on climate change, gender and DRR will be incorporated with social (social relationship, connectedness, orientation towards the common good, and equality) and health messages.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

While socio-economic impacts on Rohingya refugees are multi-dimensional, the secondary impact of COVID-19 also threatens to exacerbate the already dire conditions for the communities. Due to lack of money 70% household adopted livelihood based coping mechanisms (J-MSNA 2021). Limited livelihood options as well as a reduced ability for vulnerable households to meet basic needs, including a reduced ability to borrow money, compounded by limited access to food, lead to an increase in the use of negative coping mechanisms. Unemployment remains rife throughout the camps, with most of the employment being either “casual labour” or “unskilled labour”. 60% of surveyed refugee households “considered income generating activities (IGAs) as a priority need”, compared to 42% in 2019. Absence of resilience mechanisms, inability to produce food and inability to work to cover for the needs in host communities have driven many households to the severe poverty levels that induce many of the negative coping mechanisms, some of which contribute to further deterioration of overall safety of the people.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

SC will ensure that staff and volunteers are appropriately trained to provide equitable and appropriate services to the women, girls, children and persons with disabilities. SC emphasizes the importance of ensuring that the ‘Do No Harm’ principle and conflict sensitivity approach is implemented throughout the project. SC will also enable better integration between sectors through mainstreaming protection in the training for food security/livelihoods staff and/or volunteers. This will include specific actions to support programme integration, for example, referring children, girls or women who may be facing protection risks, including those who may be showing signs of protection risks, to other Save the Children’s programmes and/or to other relevant actors. This training will include understanding the meaning and importance of confidentiality where referrals are required.

3.2 - Gender

Save the Children’s programmes are rooted in developing safer and gender-sensitive interventions. In acknowledging socio-cultural norms, the project will ensure gender segregated servicesto enable effective participation in the planned programming of boys, girls, women and men. It also entails, where relevant, keeping in mind the gender of the staff and volunteer leading selected activities. With gendered sociocultural barriers, information on and access to available services can be limited. The specific needs of women and girls will be considered in the distribution of agri-materials. Attention will also be given to gender in MEAL activities, for example, having female facilitators and/or gender segregated focus group discussions.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

Save the Children (SC) provides opportunities to children, adolescents, adults and vulnerable groups to express their views and influence decision-making across the programme cycle (discussing, deciding, working together through group discussions). SC listens to the voices and opinions of the children, adolescents and adults to identify the components of livelihoods and understand the outcomes of its actions. Particular attention is given to enable the participation of groups or individuals (girls, women and persons with disabilities) traditionally excluded from power and decision-making processes. SC is committed to ensuring that the rights and entitlements of the communities that it serves are protected and met. SC holds a Zero Tolerance Policy towards child safeguarding which applies to all SC employees (regardless of contract type) and all personnel associated with the work of SC that includes partners, contractors and vendors. All SC partners must adhere to all SC Policies.

3.4 - Diversity

SC undertakes to recognize, understand, respect and value the differences in each specific context and operation, to ensure that all persons of concern are protected appropriately. SC specifically designs activities to support the inclusion of people with disabilities. Home and community-based activities will increase the likelihood of reaching this vulnerable group and ensuring their participation in the food production activities.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

For this project, multiple accountability mechanisms will be applied to ensure that feedback is actively collected, whilst ensuring the safety of beneficiaries, service providers, stakeholders, partners and staff. SC has in place a combination of reactive channels (such as hotlines) and proactive channels (such as, community groups feedback, community consultations, adopting questions in exit interviews etc.). Generally, Feedback and Complaints (F&Cs) received by SC are processed, analysed and referred to relevant sectors, external agencies via site management on a weekly basis (within 24-hour for Child Safeguarding-related and other complaints that require an urgent response). SC reports on F&Cs through a monthly Accountability Snapshot and work with sector-level field staff to close the feedback loop on an individual/community level, as feasible.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

SC has a comprehensive resource on Disaster Risk Management (DRM) to ensure children, communities are prepared, and through community groups, DRR messaging will be continually disseminated during monsoon and cyclone seasons. Mainstreaming environmental considerations is one of SC’s core principles to ensuring high quality programming. The project includes activities that may involve the transportation, storage materials, which have the potential to affect the quality of soil due to possible creation of waste/pollution. SC is committed to limiting negative environmental impacts through robust planning, risk mitigation and monitoring. With the implementation of prevention and mitigation measures, as well as the promotion of good practices, SC expects to have little impact on the environment.
