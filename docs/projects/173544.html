Project Summary

This proposed project, intends to scale-up Sexual Reproductive Health and menstrual hygiene Management support and care services for vulnerable displaced girls, young women and women living with disabilities in targeted host communities and selected schools Bui and Donga Mantung in the North West and Meme and Fako in the South West. This will be achieved through strengthening capacity of psychosocial experts and GBV case managers who will response via mobile clinics to survivors of GBV in hard to reach targeted host communities.
The project aimed at building capacity of young girls and women (mostly survivors of GBV) with the confidence, knowledge and skills to manage their reproductive and menstrual health challenges safely, with dignity by breaking down stifling barriers to information, supplies and access to services, and thus reducing the dangerous risk that exposes this population.
The project is founded on the H4BF captured in the core -package of intervention, and it proposed to reach 6 LGAs, 20 host communities with a total of 9,000 girls/young women in 6 LGAs within its project life span.
Beneficiaries targeted include, displaced adolescent girls between the ages of 9 to 20 years, (Most are survivors of GBV), and women of reproductive age between the ages of 22 – 36 living with disabilities, totaling about 9000 minimum living in displaced and host communities of Bui and Donga Mantung in the North West and Meme and Fako in the South West.
These beneficiaries comprises women and girls who are currently affected or are at high risk of been affected by complications resulting from reproductive health issues as well as poor menstrual Management practice. With non-existent livelihood source, and coupled with the harsh consequences of the COVID 19 global pandemic that further plunged them into more deplorable conditions.
The project will evoke a participatory approach by involving not only the beneficiaries, but Community Based Organizations, health personnel, community leaders /members, educational authorities, corporate bodies and business as well as hundreds of volunteers and women youth leaders. The program will strengthen linkages to other related services particularly those of reproductive health nature such as HIV/AIDs, STDs, etc. It will comprise of community awareness and outreaches to target beneficiaries by mostly health workers and other volunteers. The fundamentals of sexual reproductive health (SRH) management by women and girls and that of menstrual hygiene will be co-mainstreamed, and taught in a club format (30 persons per club) for women and girls ages to run for a period of 3 months per each cohort group/persons using certified modules. Women and girls with disabilities will be reached via a one-on- one and door-to-door outreach (mobile clinic) by volunteer coaches with abilities to engage with them. Within this period, beneficiaries will be supported with free menstrual reusable pads that will last for a minimum of 3 years. Free SRH services comprising of medical tests, counselling support, and treatment will be made available, and a community improvement team comprising of multi-stakeholders forum of the target community will be set up, trained and supported to engage in sustained advocacy to related authorities (governmental and non-governmental) on access and availability of medicals and SRH services in their local communities. H4BF have set up a reusable pad production centre, which is almost at 95% production ready. There will be capacity building on women on production of reusable pads, for easy access of pads by displaced women and girls hence support epidemics preparation and response. The project will seek to expand this centre and make it responsible for the provision these of pads to the target beneficiaries,
An emergency livelihood component will also be mainstreamed to support and assist most vulnerable and at risk women and girls.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The escalating conflict in NWSW has since 2017 created a vast, protracted crisis in which millions face risks to their safety and basic rights, and are struggling to survive. Within this context of conflict and fragility, millions of people in the crisis –affected North West and Southwest regions are further affected by the high risk to famine and recurrent epidemics of a large magnitude such as cholera outbreaks. The 2020 Cameroon Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) shows a bleak picture for populations in the North East Cameroon following ten years of escalated conflict. It is estimated that across the two affected regions, North West and South West, 4 million people are greatly affected, 1.3M people are in need, 530,000 are internally displaced, 376,000 People in need in host communities and 330K Other people in need. The displacements and COVID 19 have equally led to many people losing their means of livelihood, reduced farming which constituted the main means of survival for most people; explaining the observed increase of GBV especially towards women and children. Recent findings from assessment with a focus on Humanitarian Needs and Displacement Dynamics in Hard-to-Reach Areas revealed that 82% of assessed settlements reported that some population remains in the hard to reach areas. This consists largely of captive populations and vulnerable sub-groups unable to journey to safer host communities; and mostly have limited to no information on the availability of humanitarian aid services. Although access to health services has been identified as a priority by the health sector, as identified through rapid assessments and systematic community engagements. The most pressing gaps include access and availability of quality SRH services, limited safety space for GBV survivors, and continuation of public health programs, scale up of health service capacity, access to medicines and supplies, and covering operational costs. Health partners support the majority of functioning healthcare facilities across the targeted health districts. • Strengthen epidemic preparedness and response capacity of government and partners. • Non functionality of health facilities due to attacks, damages, lack of staff and resources. • Mental health continue to be a constraint due to inadequate manpower. • Secondary health cares and referral services in hard to reach areas; lack of ambulance services and specialized health care providers. • Insufficient number of skilled and appropriately trained health care staff. • Malaria and cholera endemic, in the South West region increasing risk for child mortality in malnourished children. • Clinical Management of Rape for GBV services remains a huge gap. urvivors of GBV incidents are mostly women (85 percent), 11 percent of which are living with disabilities, and 49 percent are children. There is a critical need to scale up lifesaving GBV services including prevention activities, treatment and social support. In such situations, Persons with disabilities especially women and children face a triple jeopardy resulting from their gender, economic situation and disability. UNOCHA had postulated in 2019 that if the funding situation does not improve 64 % of the 1.1 million people in need of lifesaving GBV services will receive no assistance in 2020. Many of these victims end of getting pregnant if medical care is not sought early. Many of such victims report the lack of financial resources to seek care and lack of information about where to seek care and the unavailability of the needed services in most communities. The health system in the Northwest and South West regions have been hard hit by the crisis with many health facilities in remote hard-to-reach areas destroyed and most healthcare personnel have vacated their areas of work due to insecurity.
