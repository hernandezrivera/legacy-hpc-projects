Project Summary

Following armed confrontations in the Tigray region of Ethiopia between federal and regional forces in November 2020 high numbers of refugees crossed into Kassala, Gedaref and Blue Nile States in Eastern
Sudan. Ethiopian refugee children staying in camps are in high need of education services provided in safe spaces, to support the resolution of trauma, and facilitate learning which upholds children's rights and equips children with skills for life, whilst mitigating protection risks. Across Sudan, there are high unmet needs for refugee children's education and about 67% of primary-school aged refugee children remain out-of-school.
Save the Children will provide quality primary education service to all school-age refugee children in Camp 6, Blue Nile, and will ensure the provision of equivalent services to children from the Sudanese host population (Village 6) in order to also address their needs and mitigate tensions. Save the Children will construct classrooms, furnished and equipped with appropriate learning and recreational materials and establish gender-sensitive latrines and WASH facilities to support children's learning and protection, and adhere to COVID-19 protocols. Moreover teachers will receive training on teaching in crisis contexts, COVID-19 and hygiene, and protection topics including psychosocial support and GBV, and will further be supported through incentive, and teacher kits to facilitate effective teaching. In order to address key needs of refugee children, and facilitate effective learning, school uniforms will be provided to students and school feeding will be undertaken.
Furthermore, Save the Children will continue to support Early Childhood Care & Development (ECCD) centres to prepare young children for formal learning in Tunaydbah and Um Rakuba camps in Gedaref up to February 2022, when the ongoing service delivery will be responsibly transitioned to another actor.
Child protection activities will be mainstreamed throughout, and the project will seek as far as possible to address the challenges faced by children in schools and at in partnership with child protection actors, and that the most vulnerable children are supported to enrol, attend and achieve in school. A conflict sensitive approach will also be applied, with consideration of ethnic and political affiliation of teachers and volunteers.

1 - Needs

There are over 1.1 million refugees in Sudan, of which 43% (478,000) are children. Education plays a critical role in ensuring the developmental rights of children, and are a critical means to achieve other rights, and break from a cycle of poverty. Schools can provide a safe space for children affected by conflict and forced displacement, and mitigate against key protection risks, including risk of exploitation and abuse, as well as GBV and early marriage for young girls. Education needs remain high for refugee children in Sudan, with about 67% of primary-school aged refugee children remaining out-of-school, and higher rates in remote out-of-camp contexts; and over 90% of eligible children for secondary education are also out of school. Challenges to education provision include limited availability of schools in host communities; lack of basic school resources and facilities; and lack of availability of teachers. Given the current COVID-19 context, the provision of adequate WASH facilities is especially important to meet COVID-19 protocols set out by the Federal Ministry of Education (FMoE). User fees and costs, limited access and under-resourced schools, lack of school feeding, and delivery of curriculum in Arabic are amongst the challenges limiting refugee children’s education, especially in out-of-camp settings. Furthermore, as pre-primary education is a prerequisite for admission to basic education under the Ministry of Education’s policy, refugees face difficulties in the current circumstances because of shortage of pre-schools.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Save the Children will provide quality primary education service to refugee children in Camp 6, Blue Nile, and will ensure the provision of equivalent services to children from the Sudanese host population in order to also address their needs and mitigate tensions. Furthermore, Save the Children will support Early Childhood Care & Development (ECCD) centres to prepare young children for formal learning in Tunaydbah and Um Rakuba camps in Gedaref up to February 2022, when the ongoing service delivery will be responsibly transitioned to another actor. Save the Children will work to ensure that the school environment is adequate with enough learning spaces, gender-sensitive latrines and all WASH facilities to support children protection and learning. Also, Save the Children will provide of all learning and teaching materials that will reduces the burden on families encouraging school attendance and retention, particularly amongst girls. To this end, Save the Children will construct 8 classrooms to extend primary education provision for all eligible refugee children, and will ensure they are furnished and have appropriate learning and recreational materials through supply of classroom kits. WASH in schools will be strengthened following the COVID-19 protocols developed by FMoE, and construction of handwashing stations, and gender-segregated latrines. Furthermore, hygiene awareness activities will be integrated into learning Save the Children will equip the teachers with the necessary knowledge and skills to improve the quality of teaching. Teachers will receive comprehensive training including on “Teachers in Crisis Contexts” (TiCC), as well as on topics including COVID-19 school protocols and preparedness, Psychosocial Support and GBV, and positive discipline (avoiding physical and humiliating punishment [PHP]). They will further be supported through incentive, and teacher kits to facilitate effective teaching. In order to address key needs of refugee children, and facilitate effective learning, school uniforms will be provided to students and school feeding will be undertaken. School feeding supports improved nutrition and hygiene status of school-aged children, increased access to learning opportunities for the most vulnerable children. Provision of uniforms not only supports morale, but can be particularly important for vulnerable refugee children who were displaced without their belongings and whose families lack the financial resources to buy clothes. Awareness campaigns in the schools and communities will be undertaken to encourage enrolment and ongoing attendance, and to emphasise the importance of education especially for girls and prevention of early marriages. The project will be implemented in close cooperation with the Ministry of Education both at the Federal and State levels to ensure quality education for all refugees and appropriate integration into public systems.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The climate crisis is an intergenerational on child rights crisis, and the greatest threat to children's survival, learning and protection. Save the Children has an Environmental Sustainability and Climate Change (ESSCC) Policy, and is committed to minimizing the negative environmental impact of our programmes and operations, contributing positively to environmental sustainability and climate change action, and implementing interventions which reduce vulnerability to climatic shocks. Save the Children will include environmental considerations into all interventions. In particular, the sourcing (local, national, international) of all supplies and materials, especially those used for construction and rehabilitation of infrastructure, will be carefully considered to select the option with the least negative environmental impact, considering both local dynamics (e.g. deforestation, brick manufacturing processes) and contributions to global carbon emissions caused by freight and production processes. All construction of schools and associated WASH infrastructure will be conducted in a way to limit negative environmental impact on the soil and its surroundings. Furthermore, the project will contribute to resilience and a sustainable use of natural resources through involving and encouraging children and their communities to implement better practices which support and better environmental protection and a safe local environment. Recognising children's roles as agents of change, school clubs will be established in all schools where children will be trained on how to keep their school and surrounding areas clean and green, including through hygiene and sanitation, appropriate management of resources (like water), and including activities such as tree planting within their schools as appropriate. Save the Children in Sudan will also communicate publicly about the impacts of climate change and environmental degradation.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Through all programming, Save the Children aims to increase safety, uphold dignity, and ensure equitable access to services, including through addressing the concerns of individuals and groups with specific needs. Programme design and implementation is informed by context analysis including local power and conflict dynamics (including between different ethnic groups and political affiliations amongst the refugee population, and between refugee and host communities). Save the Children will work closely with multiple stakeholders (community members, government partners, local NGOs) to avoid unintended negative effects on individuals or exacerbation of community tensions, and will seek programming which safeguards, protects, and capacitates vulnerable and marginalized individuals, and reduces tensions. Save the Children in Sudan Protection staff support the effective mainstreaming of protection across all programming including though training on referral pathways, conducting protection audits, and providing technical advice. Furthermore, a Gender Equality Specialist supports with improving the gender sensitivity of programmes, and all projects are assessed through gender equality marker. Teachers, members of Parent Teachers Associations, and other stakeholders will be trained on child protection risks, how to identify vulnerable children, and appropriate prevention and reporting actions. Child protection activities will be mainstreamed throughout, and the project will seek as far as possible to address the challenges faced by children in schools and at in partnership with child protection actors, and that the most vulnerable children are supported to enrol, attend and achieve in school.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Save the Children will ensure that girls and boys, women and men (of all ages, abilities and backgrounds) are consulted in a meaningful way in programming at all stages of the project lifecycle, and have regular opportunities to provide feedback and concerns. Gender, age and disability sensitive measures will be adopted, including through adapting meeting/service schedules and meeting venues to ensure equitable participation, using single sex discussion groups, considering ways to support the dignity and privacy of beneficiaries, and ensuring facilities are physically accessible to all, and adopting child-friendly methodologies. Save the Children will provide timely and accessible information (in relevant languages, culturally-appropriate and age-appropriate mediums) to affected communities on proposed interventions and deliverables, expectations of staff behavior especially with respect to our Child Safeguarding policy and Code of Conduct, and how to raise concerns or provide feedback. Save the Children in Sudan has a well-established Complaints & Feedback Response Mechanism, including a telephone hotline, and multiple further modalities which provide affected populations opportunities to give feedback anonymously and/or privately, and options for those literate and illiterate. In particular, special efforts are made to ensure that information is available in multiple languages used by refugees, and that all accountability modalities are available to them. To further support accountability, community accountability volunteers are recruited to support the effective exchange of information. Field level MEAL staff are responsible for receiving complaints, managing responses, and reporting them to Country Office MEAL staff who report to Senior Management. Data is anonymised, analyzed and utilized for project management and planning. There are clear processes for responding to PSEA and other safeguarding concerns raised through any feedback channel. Save the Children has safeguarding specialist staff at Country Office level, who will provide additional support and conduct monitoring visits.
