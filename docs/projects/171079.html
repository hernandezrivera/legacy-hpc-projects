Project Summary

Lack of protection from violence, abuse and neglect remains a key consequence of the current humanitarian crisis in Somalia for up to 3.4 million people. Out of those, 2.1 million are living in displacement and 1.3 million people belong to host communities. Whether directly exposed to armed conflicts or violence, or the destitution resulting from climatic shocks, affected communities face serious risks to their physical safety, integrity and access to their rights. The number of people in need of protection has increased since 2019. This can be partly attributed to a new methodology encompassing more accurately people in host and displaced communities affected by the lack of a protective environment. However, the increase also echoes an environment of growing protection risks aggravated by continued armed operations, communal violence, and severe drought conditions. Despite many efforts, durable solutions still remain a remote prospect for many displaced. Civilians continue to be victims of armed operations between government forces, their allies and non-State Armed Groups, as well as inter-communal violence, with increased number of people killed or wounded, as well as cases of recruitment or use of children by parties in conflict. Sexual violence against women and girls is also reported, while men and boys are subject to arbitrary arrest and detention or harassment by armed actors. In areas of displacement, socio economic destitution exposes women and children to domestic violence and exploitation, while girls are forced into early marriage. Contamination of land by landmines or explosive remnants of war remain predominant in districts bordering with Ethiopia. In areas exposed to armed operation or violence, explosive remnants or war and the use of IED puts the physical safety of civilians at considerable risk. IDPs sites mostly do not offer sufficient protection against women and girls. Substandard services and living conditions conducive to insecurity and violence, including GBV prevail in majority of settlements, where most IDPs lack also security of tenure. Lack of access justice, including justice and compensation for GBV survivors, and exclusion of marginalized groups, women and youth impede meaningful participation and access to rights. Other risks of conflict related violence include sexual slavery, forced marriages and forced pregnancies by parties in conflict. A total of 192 cases of conflict-related sexual violence have been documented in 2020.This project seeks to sustain and scale up lifesaving protection services in the IDPs along Dhusamareeb/Guriel in Galgadud region as well as Hobyo and Xaradheere district to prevent and respond to protection risks/concerns experienced by women, boys, girls and PLWD affected by droughts especially in Internally Displaced Peoples camps and host populations.
The key focus will be sustaining and scaling up prevention and response through access to mental health and psychosocial (MHPSS) support services, safe space for women and girls, provision of dignity kits to women and girls as well as individual case management of boys, girls, women and people with disability while ensuring separated children from their families and gender based violence survivors including these with disability are integrated into community based child protection support sites. DEH will ensure to strengthen the referral pathways by enhancing coordination and referral service to the vulnerable population including these with disability, through this project DEH will provide improve clinical management of rape and psychosocial support services to the vulnerable sexual Gender based survivors (SGBV) enhance capacity. This project also strengthens service mapping to facilitate appropriate referrals, coordination and linkage in service delivery to ensure quality comprehensive services to unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) children at risk of being recruited by armed forces or armed groups (CAAFAG).

Mitigation Measures

DEH adheres to monitoring and evaluation standards and believes in the full participation of beneficiaries throughout the implementation of the project. DEH will promote the poor as informed participants in development since the organization considers top-down development approaches as dis empowering and ineffective. The beneficiaries will be involved in targeting and DEH will use local knowledge to identify needy households and/or review selection, beneficiary liaison of both selected and un selected members of the community, beneficiary representative, community communication forums, monitoring of program implementation to ensure transparency, and management and/or monitoring of supply and quality of services while it will ensure inclusion of people with disability. DEH staff are trained to ensure that collective agency plays an important role in improving well-being and reduces information problems in determining priorities & targeting appropriate beneficiaries. The participation of beneficiaries also allows the project interventions to strengthen community based institutions that organize the poor & build their capabilities to act collectively in their own interest which in turn increases the sustainability of intervention through fostering ownership of program objectives and processes. In the Somali context the beneficiaries including people with disability will be consulted at local level, through community, clan and religious leaders regarding project location sites and the quality of services to ensure that all the vulnerable people can actively participate in the project. Within the targeted IDP camps, IDPs will be engaged in the project activities through the IDP Management Camp Committee which comprise IDP members and some representatives of the local authority as well as people with disability. Due to the complexity of Somalia, SPHERE standards may not be fully met, but DEH will make a concerted effort to increase beneficiaries’ access to life-saving activities by engaging the target community in both project design and implementation. Traditionally in Dhusamareeb, Xaradheere, Hoyboy district and many areas in Somalia, where women have not been allowed by religious and cultural norms to take a lead in community development activities; until recently when the local leadership structures began to promote women’s participation in community based activities. Women do participate actively but are frequently side-lined when it comes to decision making processes despite playing a vital role in household and community dynamics the same applies people with disability. The project will seek to encourage the increased participation of women and people with disability in project meetings so that they can provide input and feedback regarding the quality of services they will be receiving from the interventions. Encouraging increased participation by women, boys and girls in the project structures does not in any way diminish the role to be played by their male counterparts.DEH will ensure to strengthen its internal monitoring and reporting of the project activities while it dedicate accountability officer who will oversee the project milestone status while also ensuring prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) related risk is dealt with while also capacity building the project staff on sexual exploitation and abuse hence enhancing project accountability framework
