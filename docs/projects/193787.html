Project Summary

The proposed project is designed in-line with the education cluster framework to respond to the specific needs of girls and boys with disabilities who are dropout from schools (those children with disabilities who never attended education or dropped from schools) or at risk of dropout. Under the cluster objective one “Strengthen advocacy and reporting on education-related violations across oPt and ensure that school staff and students affected by the education-related violations and pandemic benefit from protective environment” the project plans to develop a mechanism to connect the school with its children to map and identify the hotspots to allow them to take preventive measure to avoid threats at these areas. In line with the IASC guideline on MHPSS in emergency, in particular under the third layer of the MHPSS pyramid of interventions, the trained and supervised school counsellors and PSS volunteers will conduct school based inclusive psychosocial support, collectively and individually targeting children with and without disabilities. In addition, the PSS volunteers will contribute implementing outreach support visits to parents of children to provide psychoeducation sessions enhancing knowledge of parents on how to respond to the needs of their children when they are in a psychosocial distress. Emotional support, structure collective support sessions, psychoeducation, counseling, and PFA will be provided. Moreover, the project will provide school-based inclusive PSS services for those girls and boys who are affected by the education-related violations, while under the education cluster objective two “Increase equitable safe access to inclusive, quality educational services for vulnerable children”, the project will provide its support at two levels, for affected children and affected schools, where after the identification of girls and boys who are out of school or at risk of dropout, children will be supported by personalized support based on the results of social assessment and plan; support will include, provision of assistive devices, educational kits, provision of safe and accessible transportation, referral to other basic services; at the school level, the project will be supporting the development of safe and accessible school environment to welcome all children, capacity-building of teachers to respond to the individual educational needs of learners including those with disabilities, support to parents clubs to develop and lead initiative on the right to education, provision of furniture and equipment to resource rooms to support the learning of students, organization of remedial and catch-up classes and implementation of inclusive recreational activities to support inclusion and diversity and reduce bullying. Under cluster objective three “Children and school staff in vulnerable schools are supported in preparing and responding to emergencies”, the project will support the provision of prepositioned educational materials and stationaries to be used by the school and children in case of emergency.
The project aims to target 675 girls and 825 boys with disabilities, and will be implemented through a partnership with local organization with substantial experience in the education sector “Teacher Creativity Center”. The project will target 20 schools, 10 in Gaza (across the five governorate) and 10 in West Bank (South Tubas, South Nablus)). The project will also collaborate with technical partners as the Ministry of Education and Organizations of Persons with Disabilities.

1 - Needs

Occupation, blockade, political-division, armed-conflict, restricted movements, limited access to basic services, protection and education-related violations and re-occurring attacks are marking the protracted crisis in the Occupied Palestinian Territory (oPt), leading to humanitarian consequences. In 2021, UNICEF reported that 12.3% of children aged 2-17 have at least one functional challenge; the Multi Sectorial Needs Assessment 2022, reported that the disability at the household level reached 21% (in Gaza) and 9.7% (in West Bank), of which 2.4% girls and 4.9% boys; the same assessment also indicated that children with disabilities face higher out-of-school and dropout risks due to bullying and social stigma linked to their gender and location (55% in Gaza, 27.7% in West Bank). The MSNA emphasizes that, in Gaza, 90% of households who reported receiving assistance in the 6 months prior to data collection showed difficulties to meeting basic needs compared to 56% of households who had not received assistance; for those households with a member with disability more difficulties are reported, Gaza (88% compared to 79%) and the West Bank (43% compared to 29%). Around 4% - equals to 25,385 out of 608,556 children - are children with disabilities at school-age, 58% of their households reporting challenges to accessing education services due to; inability to afford school-related costs (37.3% Gaza and 9.1% West Bank), illness (24.6% Gaza), lack of schools’ physical accessibility for children with disabilities (20% Gaza, 34% West Bank) and limited capacities of schools to accommodate children (20% Gaza and 38% West Bank). HI experience confirms that children with disabilities lack assistive technologies at schools, inadequate transportation means. Indeed, experiences from HI’s in oPt stress that mainstream teachers often have minimal competencies to teach and support children with disabilities in their classrooms, specifically in providing accessible remote and blended learning modalities. Notably, according to MSNA (2022), 81% and 78.7% of households in Gaza and West Bank respectively reported the need of their children with disabilities for catch-up learning due to school closures due to COVID-19 and other reasons.The analysis of the MSNA 2022 data for HHs with disability indicates concerns related to their children safety during the travel to schools; traffic and crossing roads (88.2% Gaza, 61% West Bank), Violence or harassment travelling to school (18.4% Gaza, 26.1% West Bank), violence or harassment by settlers on the way to/from school and Military presence around schools in particular in Area C (11% West Bank). The gender equality study, HI, 2022 confirms that, 70% of parents are concerned that girls and boys with disabilities are more exposed to sexual abuse when away from home. As a result, parents decide not to send their children with disabilities to school. Conclusions of HI and MoE in Gaza and WB indicate that children at 4th grade are the most affected children in terms of learning loss due to the experience of COVID-19, furthermore 4th grade seen as a transformative grade for those children as the stopped to receive remedial education through resource rooms leading to increased risks of dropout and low academic performance.Through its monitoring, the education cluster observes an increase of education-related incidents at WB, in the 1st half of 2022, which recorded 115 incidents affecting 46 schools, 7906 students (2767 of them female), 418 teachers and school staff (101 of them female), and 3,996 Class-minutes were lost which represents a significant increase in the incidents targeting schools and students compared to the first half of 2021 and 2020, furthermore, HNO 2023 confirms that around 84,000 HHs with school-aged children in Gaza, Area C, Hebron H2 and East Jerusalem reported that psychosocial support is not available at the school for children, and children lacks opportunities to participate in extracurricular activities. On the other hand, as of September 2022, 8,779 structures (which include schools) have been demolished; HI stresses that most efforts on renovation/rehabilitation of the damaged schools tend to disregard disability accessibility standards or even basic gender-sensitive accommodations.

2 - Activities

In response to the identified needs of out-of-school and those girls and boys with disabilities at risk of dropout, the project is designed to address the needs at two levels, 1) the school and 2) child and family both in the West Bank and Gaza. These two levels correspond to two main components. The first component looks at strengthening the coordination between the community actors to identify and provide support services to dropped-out school girls and boys with disabilities to increase their access to education. This will be achieved through a sequence of activities: 1.1 - Adapt and equip targeted schools to be gender and disability responsive through development of child-friendly, safe and accessible school environment. Since the project targets Area C, reasonable accommodation and emergency rehabilitation will be implemented due to construction permissions. 1.2 - Provide school-based disability inclusive psycho-social and outreach psychoeducation for boys and girls with disabilities and their parents, in addition to hiring assistant counsellors to provide additional assistance to school counsellors; this will include the provision of PSS kits for targeted counsellors. 1.3 - Train and coach teachers and school staff on disability-inclusive education to improve the teaching strategies at school to respond to the different needs of learners; directed towards the specific educational needs of the children; which includes didactics for teachers on how to ensure inclusion within classroom, inclusive teaching methodologies, assessment of educational needs, follow up on children’s individual educational plans, child-protection related signs and links with referral committees at school level; 1.4 - Organize recreational remedial/catch up classes for identified children with and without disabilities in need through activities such as education. 1.5 - Develop PSEA' feedback and complaint mechanism among mainstream schools and enhance awareness of among children with and without disabilities and their teachers and parents in mainstream school for existing mechanisms.The second component of the project focuses on improving safe and protective learning environment for the targeted mainstream schools under emergency situations. The package of measures is as follows: 2.1 - Identify and assess girls and boys with disabilities out-of-school or those at risk of dropout in the targeted communities, this includes conducting awareness raising on community level in relation to HI safeguarding policies. 2.2 - Refer identified girls and boys to support services providers of health, protection, shelter, livelihood, GBV, PSEA services based on their needs to support their inclusion. 2.3 - Provide mobility, hearing, visual and educational assistive devices and educational technologies for boys and girls with disabilities. 2.4 - Provide safe accessible transportation for vulnerable boys and girls with disabilities to enable their access to schools and reduce their risk to dropout from schools, the provision of accessible transportation will reduce the concerns of parents of girls with disabilities related to touch and harassment during the transfer from wheelchair to bus. 2.5 - Donate educational materials and stationery, emergency kits, hygiene kits to girls and boys of poor and vulnerable households. All project interventions will be implemented respecting a Do No Harm approach. The approach is being ensured on many levels for HI interventions; on the institutional level HI has its developed policies related to safeguarding including child protection policy, PSEA policy, gender policy, and policy of anti-fraud bribery and corruption. On the operational level, HI focuses on protection against abuses, violations and sever neglect of people with disabilities. As a reflection on project interventions, HI ensures capacity building of its staff, partners and all stakeholders in relation to awareness of safeguarding policies. HI ensures that targeted communities including children with disabilities are aware of its feedback and complaint mechanism in addition to enhancing local partners actions towards setting their own safe accessible complaints mechanisms. The project will be implemented together with Teacher Creativity Center (TCC); TCC will be leading on activities directly implemented with targeted beneficiaries, while HI will be working on promoting inclusion into project activities and the overall coordination.

3 - Indicators

HI adopts a monitoring system that will ensure proper tracking of the progress and captured any challenges in the implementation in order to deliver the requested support on time. The assigned Project Manager will be in charge of the M&E system with close support from the Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) unit. This system is composed of: I) a monitoring tool (PMBox) developed at the project’s inception phase and regularly monitored during the project’s implementation period. This tool tracks the progress of different activities, achievements and indicators; it also allows for identifying the challenges and delays within the project activities and identifying potential solutions. II) A project review meeting is scheduled and conducted every four months according to HI standards. During this meeting, all the departments and the project team discuss the progress of the project’s activities and the achievement of results. Additionally, recommendations for improvements are identified and followed to ensure the quality and timely implementation of activities. III) Technical monitoring can be provided by the Regional MEAL and Technical Units based in Jordan, as required. IV) Field support visits conducted by HI project team to partners and beneficiaries, on a regular basis, to monitor and support the activities’ implementation and outcome achievement. The project monitoring will use and apply the following tools: pre & post tests, Post Intervention Monitoring (PIM), and satisfaction surveys. The M&E of the project is designed in accordance with HI Planning, Monitoring and Evaluation Policy, which is derived from HI institutional policies on Child Protection (CP), Protection Against Sexual Exploitation Abuse and Harassment (PSEAH), Prevention of and Fight Against Bribery (PFAB), Code of Conduct (CoC), and Safety and Security (SaS). The M&E system complies with IASC guidelines on the inclusion of Persons with Disabilities in Humanitarian Action through the application of Common Humanitarian Standards (CHS) Commitments 4 and 5, SPHERE Standards, and Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP). Concerning indicators, the project will be monitored against the following set: Output 1: # of female and male students and school staff have benefited from structured school-based psychosocial support services (341 boys and 279 girls) : # of teachers/other educational personnel trained on psycho-social support (201 female and 142 male); teachers will be trained on detection of child protection signs and will support the referral of identified children. While school counsellors will be trained on the provision of inclusive PSS for children with and without disabilities; # of schools’ aged-children benefited from recreational activities (250 boys and 250 girls); # of male and female students and schools’ staff benefiting from the provision of educational supplies and hygiene kits. (448 boys and 672girls); # of female and male students have benefited from learning support services (825boys and 675 girls). Output 2: # of female and male children with disabilities have benefited from the adapted education services and assistive devices. (110 boys and 90 girls); 70% of parents with school-aged participated in school activities; # of out of school children with disabilities supported by education services aiming at reintegrating them in the education system (165 boys and 135 girls); # of feedback received (including complaints) which have been acted upon.Community engagement and participation is a core component of HI approaches; it falls under the user-centered approach that HI is promoting along its interventions and projects. Based on the use of user-centered approach, female, male, boys and girls are engaged at the level of needs assessment through surveys implemented in 2022, and based on HI’s experience, male and men are included in the design and implementation of activities such as peer-to-peer support and awareness raising actions; boys and girls are also involved in the implementation of children-lead initiatives at school level. At the monitoring and evaluation, gender and age are mainstreamed.A Feedback and Complaint Mechanism (FCM) exists and will allow different stakeholders to raise their concerns through different available modes of reception such as complaining via: a phone line, email, boxes, HI staff, and data collection exercises. Data confidentiality protocols are in place. All HI Palestine staff have received training on the HI safeguarding approach by the Safeguarding focal point; refresher trainings are implemented on a systematic manner. Project partner will be trained and will be introduced to the already existing complaint mechanisms. HI also has internal (via intranet, email) and external (website, email, post) complaints and whistle-blowing mechanisms with the clear investigation and disciplinary procedures. A PSEAH toolbox is available on HI’s intranet to implement the existing policy. Beneficiaries with and without disabilities are able to fill complaints if needed. The project will ensure the implementation of awareness raising sessions for project beneficiaries and targeted schools on PSEA. The project will support targeted schools to develop an adapted feedback and complaint mechanism to be accessible for all. The PSEA committee will be responsible on monitoring the system and reporting about its achievements.
