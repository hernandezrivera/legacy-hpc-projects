Project Summary

The December 2020 IPC analysis indicates about 7.24 million people likely to face severe acute food insecurity (Crisis – IPC Phase 3) between April and July 2021 and will be in need of urgent assistance. While, around 2.5 million will be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) with very high acute malnutrition and excess mortality. Around 1.4 million children aged between 6 months and 5 years are expected to be acutely malnourished in 2021 and will need life-saving treatment. This includes 313,000 children suffering from severe acute malnutrition and more than a million children suffering from moderate acute malnutrition. At the same time, 483,000 pregnant and breastfeeding women are expected to be acutely malnourished and in need of treatment.
Climatic extremes eg; floods and drought have led to displacement, loss of crops and livestock, and increased incidences of human and livestock diseases. There has been large decrease in livestock ownership because of cattle raiding incidents and incidents of pests and diseases. High food prices, with staples at 200-400% higher than last year, and 400-600% higher than 5 year average, as well as, inter-communal conflict has further resulted in destruction of crops, displaced farming households, led to loss of assets and limited humanitarian access. Action Against Hunger’s food security and livelihoods strategy is centered on long-term reduction in the burden of undernutrition. Therefore, in direct response to the crisis in the project areas, the organization will increase access to food and dietary diversity levels by providing opportunities for beneficiaries to improve their agricultural knowledge/practices and food preparation practices. Additionally, access to sources of food will be improved through the improvement of community infrastructure, diversification of livelihood sources including access to credit/loan for increased supply of nutritious foods. To improve the efficacy of nutrition and health programs, our FSL intervention will be fully integrated with local nutrition and health programs, targeting households with children and pregnant and lactating women facing acute malnutrition. Moreover, the program will be linked with local WaSH interventions (e.g. hygiene promotion) to promote development outcomes through prevention of disease, as well as opportunities for improved water resource management (e.g. climate-sensitive irrigation schemes).

1 - Needs

The cumulative effects of years of prolonged conflict, the COVID-19 Pandemic, chronic vulnerabilities and weak essential services have left over 6 million people in need of humanitarian assistance in South Sudan. The country remains in a critical period of severe food insecurity with 7.24 million people projected by the IPC to be facing severe acute food insecurity in the first half of 2021. Despite the signing of the 2018 peace agreement, and the establishment of the Transitional Government in February 2020, severe humanitarian needs persist. This is in a context where the level of development remains compromised and fragile: South Sudan ranked fourth last out of 189 countries in the 2020 Human Development Index. It is a setting where, two years into the country’s independence, basic services were limited or absent in many areas, even before the 2013 round of conflict.South Sudan has experienced a protracted humanitarian crisis since the escalation of the conflict in December 2013. The Government’s ability to provide basic services to its people is low and inconsistent across the country. Food insecurity is one of the drivers behind the relatively high number of people in need in South Sudan—despite a period of relative stability over the past year, the malnutrition and food insecurity situation across the country remains critical. Levels of acute malnutrition and food insecurity are expected to rise in 2021 as post-harvest stocks become depleted and the situation worsened by the internal displacements due to floods and inter communal conflicts in parts of the country. A large proportion of the land is arable/fertile providing grazing land for cattle rearing and opportunity for cultivation. However, due to protracted conflict, the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic and insecurity, utilization of this endowed resource has been very limited. The situation is compounded by the lack of appropriate farming technology, lack of extension services and lack of access to affordable farming tools and quality seeds. A combination of these factors exacerbates needs and complicate the humanitarian response. These include a lack of access to a steady and diverse food supply as a result of disrupted markets, low health services coverage, disruptions of livelihoods resulting from the COVID-19 pandemic and its resultant effects and limited knowledge and practice of healthy nutrition. Heavy flooding at the end of 2020 disrupted agricultural activities including damage to agricultural field, and death of livestock. This shock contributed to the eroding of livelihoods and access to essential services and markets. Nutrition needs remain high throughout the country despite partners’ efforts geared at reducing the humanitarian needs. The 2020 IPC reflects a grim food security situation in many parts of the country including parts of Jonglei, Aweil and Warrap States; three of the states where Action Against Hunger has presence. In view of the above, Action Against Hunger intends to support populations in need in Jonglei, Warrap and Northern Bahr El Ghazal to enhance their resilience in dealing with the recurring shocks that are an inherent part of their community reality.

2 - Project activities

Provision of weather information to inform decision-makingAction Against Hunger will collect weather information to support the forecasting of the weather in our project locations. Nutrition-sensitive cultivation of arable land using cash The project will support the cultivation of arable land by the beneficiaries to enhance steady supply of both staple food and vegetables in project locations. The cultivation of arable land will last for 3 months during which participating households are expected to work for 15 days in each month. In return, participants will be paid cash through a third-party financial service provider via a voucher/electronic card. Construction of dykes and access roads through cashThe project will promote the protection of farmers’ field from recurrent flooding through the construction of flood control Dykes. Participants will also work on multipurpose-pond and/or an access road identified by the community through a participatory process. Participants will be paid for this, to support households’ dietary needs for the period of the lean season. Household DialogueThe project will organize Household Dialogue sessions to perception/stereotypes and practices that hinder rural women from successfully engaging in alternative livelihood activities. Community facilitators will be trained to facilitate the sessions at the organization’s project catchment areas. Social MarketingThe project will implement Social marketing approach aimed at changing people’s behaviour for the benefit of individuals and their communities. This approach is currently being implemented by the Action Against Hunger network and will be introduced in the South Sudan Mission programs. Construction of demonstration plots around nutrition centers Demonstration plots for vegetables will be established around nutrition sites to teach farmers and mothers vegetable cultivation methods. The staff at the nutrition sites will conduct cooking demonstrations using vegetables produced from the demo plots. Demo plots for crops including sorghum and groundnuts will be established at lead farmer’s sites. Support to people with special needs to participate in alternative and appropriate livelihood activitiesThe project will identify people with special needs including those who are physically challenged and willing to cultivate backyard gardens and support them with funds to hire additional hands to support with activities that cannot be performed by the beneficiary. Formation of Village Saving and Loan AssociationsMembers of the cultivation groups (farmers) will be organized into Village Saving and Loan Associations (VSLAs). Farmers who are already involved in farming and earning some income from the cash for work will be encouraged to join the associations. Capacity building for nutrition sensitivity in agriculture Demonstration plots established around the nutrition centers will serve as learning centers for both farmers and mothers enrolled at the center. Mother/farmers will be trained and supported to grow nutritious vegetables/crops and taught preparation skills. Support for value chain additionThe project will support groundnut farmers’ groups with appropriate groundnut oil extraction machines and marketing skills. Seed fairs (seeds and tools)/cash for seedsVulnerable households that qualify for cultivation support, will be given seeds and tools through input trade fair or direct distribution. In instances where the protocols of the COVID-19 does not allow for ITF, the organization will distribute cash to beneficiaries to procure their own seeds. Livestock health support to agro-pastoralist The project will work with veterinary service providers to provide support to project beneficiaries who have livestock, to promote the health of livestock own by our beneficiaries.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

The project has been designed to involve all market actors including farmers, traders and other associated entrepreneurs by using integrated approaches that would benefit direct and indirect project participants. These are inclusive and broad based approaches that would outline key steps, including networking, consultations and collaboration at all levels. The Project will also focus on the following components to ensure market linkages at all levels:• Weekly analysis, market assessment and production of food and non-food commodities prices to update and keep farmers informed of existing market conditions, which would help them in making decision on when to bring or withhold their produce for trading • The project will also link farmers and traders through the execution of input trade fair where the different actors (farmers and local seeds producers) can negotiate and determine best price and quality of seeds that satisfy their individual needs. • Value addition through processing facilities and training of targeted farmers/groups to make their processed food more marketable and attractive to consumers and will be linked to other traders and shop owners for more profitability and sustainability • The VSLA groups will be supported to save and utilize groups’ saving wisely and trained on basic business management techniques and organizational development to further enhance their knowledge and skill to establishing meaningful businesses for income generation; this would eventually contribute to the viability of the local market • Targeted women groups will be engaged and supported through Kitchen gardening all year long for the production of vegetables to increase household dietary diversification and surplus sold on the local market, where the local population will have access to the availability of nutritious food.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Usually during the year, there are specific periods where farmers experience severe hunger gap especially between planting and harvest time; therefore cash voucher/assistance helps the families to ensure the continuous food purchase at the household level. Furthermore, cash provides several opportunities and options for family to consider priorities; address the most urgent needs and decide food preferences and other varieties. Additionally, during harvest period, when food commodities prices are lower; the families have more opportunity to restock food for the next lean season. Through our Beneficiary Feedback Mechanism System at the community level, it has revealed that the in-kind food handling process from prepositioning up to distribution may result to food spoilage or contamination compared to cash, where families could decide to buy fresh food and consider dietary diversification.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Action Against Hunger trains all of its staff and volunteers on the organization’s PSEA Policy. During our mobilization meetings Action Against Hunger shares information about PSEA from the organization’s PSEA Policy. During project implementation, the organization regularly shares PSEA information with beneficiaries as reminder/refresher to emphasize the premium the organizations places Zero Tolerance for PSEA.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

A diverse set of feedback mechanism modalities promotes the access of those with varying genders, ages, and abilities. Community Volunteers and extension workers are also able to collect feedback at the community level when project beneficiaries are unable to access facilities. Feedback and Complaints Mechanism IEC materials include images and text in both English and the local dialect to ensure comprehension by a maximum number of individuals in target communities. These visuals are displayed on project facilities, structures, and equipment used by beneficiaries. Confidentiality and single-sex/age group Focus Group Discussions (including facilitators of the same sex/age group) promote a more comfortable environment for beneficiaries, especially women and girls, to share their concerns. The organization will solicit feedback from women, people with special needs and all project beneficiaries through different media including 1-on-1 sessions, focus group discussions, complaint box, and hotlines where appropriate to ensure that anyone regardless of disability, age or academic background has the access to provide feedback to the organization.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project provides information to beneficiaries through a number of channels. Payam administrators are informed about ACF’s interventions and entitlements of beneficiaries. This information is also shared with the Boma Administrators and the commissioners. At the level of the villages, the information is shared with the village leaders. The information is also shared with the beneficiaries of the project through a mass meeting. During input distribution, mobilization for the distribution is done by the project staff working with the community volunteers who are assigned in the communities and work directly with the beneficiaries.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Action Against Hunger considers the feedback from beneficiaries as an instrument to improve the organization’s acceptance, and improvement of our interventions in the community. Hence, all feedback from beneficiaries are analyzed and used to improve the relevant intervention/project as we place emphasis on the organization being a learning entity.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

To prevent instigation of SEA and other forms of GBV by leading gender-targeted interventions, Action Against Hunger will aim to sensitize all gender and age groups on project activities before their implementation. All our project activities are designed to safely challenge harmful gender dynamics in a context-appropriate manner. Results of gender analyses will directly inform project design and implementation strategy. All staff implementing GBV referral pathway awareness, Men Group Discussion facilitation, Psychological First Aid, and any other sensitive discussion with target community members will be instructed on our mandatory policy for maintaining the identity of project participants strictly confidential. Data related to GBV and other sensitive topics will be anonymized in organizational databases to preserve the privacy of survivors. Moreover, survivors or reporters will not be solicited for further participation/discussion. Any GBV case concerning staff or beneficiaries which proceed to investigation will only involve the Country Program Ethics Committee, consisting of the Country Director, Head of Logistics and Head of Operations. All information will be confidential with access restricted to those personnel. We will train key stakeholders on the use of the feedback mechanism at each project location. Additionally, the organization will print complaint procedures in the local language and in simple English. The project team will regularly review the use of the feedback mechanism and information gathered from the review process will be used to make improvements. A diverse set of FCM modalities ensures that differing gender, age and ability groups have access.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

Action Against Hunger provides integrated services to beneficiaries at community level. Key protection services are incorporated within our programs which include GBV focal persons identified at all facilities; training of staffs on GBV risk mitigation and available referral pathways and services; provision of Psychological First Aid (PFA) to GBV survivors approaching the project teams; hiring more female staffs in the project team and committees formed at community/village level; conducting safety audits at our project sites etc.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

The production of more food, provision of weather information to support farming decision, activities aimed at improving relationship at the household level and microfinance (VSLA) activities will go a long way in supporting populations in IPC 3, 4 and 5. Enhancing the productivity of people in need of humanitarian aid will reduce the number of people in IPC 3, 4 and 5 as they become self-reliant and continue to fend for themselves.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

Action Against Hunger has a policy on fair provision of cross-sectoral services to all sectors of the affected population including women, children, the elderly and persons with special needs. The program will ensure that this policy is followed and all sectors of the affected population are served according to their needs. The provision of services will be based on the organization’s needs/vulnerability analysis.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The overall food security program is aimed at providing sustainable sources of food which will positively impact a reduction of the level of malnutrition in our intervention areas. Our nutrition sensitive agriculture activities are specifically intended to reduce the GAM rate among the U5 children. This is complemented by our intervention in the nutrition programs being implemented by Action Against Hunger at static nutrition and outreach sites.
