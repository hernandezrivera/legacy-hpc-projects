Project Summary

The project aims to provide WASH services to 11,000 HHs of approximately 56,000 individuals (12,230 men, 13,440 women, 15,120 boys, 15,120 girls) the most vulnerable IDPs, and host communities in Idleb and Aleppo governorates in communities and IDP informal camps
The main intervention would be concentrating on the following:
1- 15000 individuals will have access to safe drinking water through the operation and maintenance support to the malfunction water systems in the communities/camps including water pumping stations and water networks, valves, reservoirs, and water infrastructures in addition to developing the cost recovery system and forming the relative water committee in the targeted areas
2- 20,000 individuals will access safe drinking water in camps by Providing 35/capita/day chlorinated drinking via water trucking to IDPs in addition to providing the needed galvanized water tank
3- Water sterilizing through providing liquid chlorine to the water in addition to regularly conducting the proper physical, chemical, and biological water tests, especially FRC,/ At the water network source and water tank level the FRC value should be 0.5 mg/l, At household and consumption points it should be >=0.2 mg/l / PH, conductivity, turbidity, Nitrite, Nitrate E coly tests.
4- 20,000 individuals will have safe access to a clean environment and vector control through Solid waste management including the rehabilitation of the communal landfills and providing/maintaining the garbage containers / 100 L size garbage container for every 50 individuals / in communities in addition to solid waste removal in camps
5- 20,000 individuals will have safe access to proper safe sanitation through Rehabilitation of the damaged sewage networks including damaged pipe replacement or manhole cleaning/covers provisions in communities and construction of the required connections/ extensions to the outlets where needed in addition to desludging the septic tanks/ sanitation pits in the IDP camps
6- 10,000 individuals will access to safe sanitation through the construction of latrines / one latrine for every 20 to 50 individuals according to the area availability/ segregated for men and for women in addition to latrines for people in special needs PWDs in the informal settlement camps equipped with the proper doors with locks and solar lighting system in order to maintain the safe approach and protection for women and children.
7- 35000 individuals will have Raising awareness about water-borne diseases and good hygiene practices through conducting hygiene sessions segregated between women, men, and children into the distribution of motivating material within the sessions especially for children.
8- 15000 individuals will have good hygiene practices through receiving hygiene kit includes the most needed cleaning materials in line with WASH cluster hygienist standard content

4 - Needs

According to WASH HNO 2022, IDPs living in displacement sites are often fully dependent on humanitarian assistance, and those in informal sites often experience worse WASH conditions making them particularly vulnerable. Also newly displaced populations face specific WASH needs and require emergency WASHAll population groups in the community could be affected by limitations in functionality and efficiency of WASH infrastructure and services, this is true both in urban and rural communities. Particularly impacted are the overburdened communities hosting a high ratio of IDPs and areas of high returnees, where water and sanitation infrastructure was not designed for the increased demand, was damaged or undermaintained over years. Similarly, the population underserved by public services are collectively in a worse situation, and so highly dependent on informal water sources, facing water supply shortages and insufficiency, poor water quality, severe issues with sewage disposal or lack of garbage removal services. Communities in areas with access constraints (parts of Aleppo, Deir-ez-Zor and Idleb governorates) can face similar issues with infrastructure functionality, and it is unlikely they could be reached with humanitarian assistance. Even in areas with secure access, there are high severity sub-districts, with people experiencing WASH needs and with very limited humanitarian assistance provided

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will begin by ensuring coordination with all stakeholders inside Syria to ensure a coordinated and complementary approach for comprehensive services, avoid duplication and ensure accountability to affected populations. Staff of SDI and contractors will receive induction about PSEA, GBV. SDI will create IDP representative committees, if not existing, and ensure they have female and PWD members, and coordinate project activities with them to ensure participation of Men, women, boys, girls, adolescents and elderly in the designing, implementation and monitoring of activities through focus groups, surveys and complaint mechanism. Technical study will be conducted by a team of engineers. Materials and services will be procured based on SDI's policies and procedures in a competitive and transparent manner to rule out fraud and ensure cost-effectiveness. The project will be handed over to committees/ camp management in camps upon project closure in addition to prior coordination with WASH cluster to ensure follow-up and enhance the sustainability of the project. Project activities will take into consideration the age and gender differences and the needs of disabled, elderly and PWDs in addition to protection concerns, accessibility of PWDs to WASH facilities and providing lighting, locks for privacy, and ensuring sphere standards and WASH cluster's standards in term of sufficient water and soup, especially under covid19.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: violation of property rights, hindrance to activities or beneficiary eviction. Likelihood: moderate; Impact: high; Mitigation: Conduct HLP due diligence according to clusters guidelines. Risk: Child labor, risks on workers' safety and health. Likelihood: high; Impact: high. Mitigation: zero tolerance to child labor and requirements to provide safety equipment to laborers will be included in the code of conduct and contracts signed by contractors and followed up. Risk: Explosive remnants of war and mines. Likelihood: moderate; Impact: high; Mitigation: selected sites are declared as clear. Awareness banners and awareness-raising sessions will be included. Risk: The spread of Covid19 across NW Syria. Likelihood: high, Effect: high. Risk mitigation3: SDI will take Covid19 prevention measures according to Clusters and SCHF guidelines such as social distancing, using PPE and proper hygiene for all staff and workers...etc in addition to awareness-raising of IDPs in sites. Risk: Lack of participation and accountability to beneficiaries; Likelihood: moderate, Effect: high. Involving the local authorities, camp management and IDPs representative committees including women and children (and forming committee if they are absent) in all stages of the projects, including needs assessment, designing the project activities, and HLP verification in addition to active information sharing through meetings and focus group discussions.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Beneficiaries are at risk on being exposed to any sexual misconduct by the humanitarian workers and they might not report the misconduct due to the following reason: 1. lack of knowledge of reporting mechanisms, especially for people with disabilities and women living in far away areas. 2. feeling afraid of retaliation. 3. fear of stigma. 4. fear of deprivation of assistance. 5. being unable to understand written banners for being illiterate and/or for having a visual disability. 6. for children, language might be complicated To mitigate these risks, SDI will : 1. aim at clearly announcing the hotline number and email specialized for PSEA of SDI and UN numbers. through many means, like, banners, social media, and messages to beneficiaries phones through telegram channel to ensure reaching to the biggest number of beneficiaries including women living in far away areas and people with disabilities. 2. Conduct PSEA training for all staff members and contractors/suppliers dealing with beneficiaries and make them read and sign CoC that state Zero Tolerance towards SEA violations. 3. Conduct awareness raising sessions on PSEA for beneficiaries to encourage them to report any violations and stress on the confidentiality and on eliminating any other fears that they might have. 4. Conduct awareness raising sessions for children in an appropriate language. 5. complaint boxes 6. Using PSEA field and organizational checklists tools.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption1: Access to all project locations is secured; Risk1: Access issues due to security concerns or local actors. Likelihood: low; Impact: high. Mitigation: Security assessment to targeted sites, coordination with local authorities, staff training, provision of safety tools and equipment, identification of appropriate services and medical facilities, evacuation plan, relocation (plan B). Assumption2: the borders will keep open for humanitarian response. Risk2: Closure of Border Crossing Point, Likelihood:High; Impact: Low. Mitigation: using locally available materials while advocating through advocacy channels. Assumption3: Needed material, workshops, and skilled and non-skilled workers are available in the project location. Risk3: Some material, workshop, or skilled and non-skilled workers become unavailable. Likelihood: very low; Impact: high. Mitigation: RNA and market assessment. SDI already has experienced staff and engineers in the area, and has the capacity to procure items from a wider geographic range. Risk4: Danger on children or residents due to construction or digging works; Mitigation: Awareness raising, clear warning signs, safety tapes..etc. to keep civilians out of danger zones. Risk5: Fire risk in camp; Likelihood: moderate; Impact: high. Mitigation: Fire mitigation, awareness, training and equipment is one of the main components of this project.
