Project Summary

CARE/PMRS will respond to this situation with a comprehensive intervention that combines elements of direct service delivery to the identified vulnerable communities/groups in Gaza strip in response to the continuous crisis and the need for urgent emergency response as highlighted by the HNC , with special focus on women and children, elderly, youth and people with disabilities who are more vulnerable than others.
The project will enhance the emergency preparedness and response of 4 PMRS centers in Gaza that provide PHC services to vulnerable groups and provide necessary treatment and emergency response during the crisis and receive emergency cases at any of emergencies. The project will also support PMRS venter in North Gaza that provide CBR services by well-trained physiotherapists and provide the assistive devices and required rehabilitation programs. additionally, the project will build the preparedness of PMRS centers and the 12 communities by providing Emergency 1st aid training, distribution of first aid kits to the trainees (communities focal points).
the targeted communities in addition to the main are: Al Boura, Alsaifa, Al Muntar, Magboulah, Abu Hamam, Abu AlAjeen, Alfaraheen, Um Almahd, Satar Algharrbi, Al shoukah, Al Salam and Sofa

Needs

An estimated 1.2 million people are in need of humanitarian health and nutrition interventions across the oPt, of which, almost 900,000 are in Gaza. 54% of the caseload are female, and children under the age of 18 constitute over one third of the total. Some of the acutely vulnerable groups include; conflict related trauma casualties, pregnant and lactating women, children under the age of 5, people with disability and elderly, and mental health patients. In 2018, the Gaza Strip witnessed a significant increase in Palestinian casualties in the context of mass demonstrations taking place along Israel’s perimeter fence with Gaza as a result of the Great March of Return and, to a lesser extent, in the context of hostilities. This led to a wave of trauma casualties and a disruption of the health system that is already overburdened by the protracted emergency situation. In 2019, Health and Nutrition Cluster estimates that 40,000 people will be in need of trauma care as a direct result of the conflict, including the existing cohort of patients that will need extensive surgery, management of post-trauma complications and rehabilitation. Meanwhile, the attacks against healthcare in Gaza, have raised a serious concern over the protection of health workers and patients. Since the mass demonstrations began until the 9 September, 180 people have been killed.1 165 people were killed by Israeli forces during the demonstrations and 15 people killed by Israeli attacks. The total figure of people injured amidst the conflict since the 30th March stands at 19,640. From the total number of injured, a total of 10,072 people required hospitalization, including at MoH hospitals and NGO-run hospitals in Gaza. Since 30 March, according to WHO reports three health workers have been killed and 417 injured in 236 recorded incidents against health staff and facilities. 61 ambulances and 5 others health vehicles have been damaged, as well as 2 health facilities (a specialized health centre for people with disability and the MoH central ambulance station were damaged as the result of an Israeli air strike on 14 July). The latest figures provided by the Ministry of Health (MoH) indicate that during the demonstrations between 23rd September and 6th October, 14 Palestinians including four children were killed and 1,434 were injured by the Israeli forces, out of the total 1,434 injured, 737 patients required transfer to the MoH hospitals or NGO clinics, including 150 children and 32 females. Of the hospitalized injured, 12 cases were critically life threatening, 245 moderate, 453 mild, and the remaining 27 were unspecified cases (WHO sit rep 6 October 2019). The preparedness for such big number of casualties per week, resulted in weak preparedness to respond to the caseload, as well as for the follow up and rehabilitation of people. As a result of that, vulnerable groups like children, women, elderly and people with disabilities are suffering more due to weak of resources for the health providers, urgent priorities in addition to the social and cultural barriers that will leave those groups with no or weak access to quality services, this includes the mobility as well as the economic situation that prevent the targeted people especially in the identified areas to seek services, based on the field experience in working with some of these areas revealed that some people can’t afford the transportation cost to seek the services. The identified 12 communities suffer from access to health services. The burden is doubled when it comes to disabled people especially those who were injured recently, since the load is too high and few organizations offer the CBR services. Elderly, the forgotten group at time of emergencies, especially those with NCDs are in need and due to the shortages of medications, they are exposed to complications that would jeopardize their lives. CARE/PMRS have been working for years and build on the long history of work

Activities

The project was designed in coordination with other actors in order to avoid duplication, maximize the number of beneficiaries those in need in the most effective and equitable manner. CARE has fully participated in the HRP process and aware of needs and key actor’s responses. Accordingly, we will be working to achieve the following resultsResult 1: PMRS increased their capacity to better respond to health emergency and health related challenges in their communities.1.1 Training of 20 medical team on advanced lifesaving (10 males and 10 females)1.2 Assessment, procuring and provision of medications and supplies and preposition of emergency supplies in the 4 PMRS PHC centers in Gaza to build their capacity to better respond to emergencies, and support vulnerable groups and hardship cases with proper service and medication.1.3 Assess the needs of the PMRS rehabilitation center in north Gaza and procuring of medical supplies and assistive devices and trained staff to be deal with the referred cases to the center for rehabilitation at short and long term.1.4 Monitor the use of medical supplies 1.5 Identification of health awareness materials and topic in coordination with the communities1.6 conducting health awareness sessions1.7 Printing and distributing the health awareness materials.Result 2: 12 targeted communities are better prepared to respond to emergenciesActivities under Result 22.1 Conduct a 24 hours (3 days), first aid training for 180 community members (volunteers, 100 males and 80 females) to be better prepared for responding to medical and other health related emergencies. First aid training will include (principles of first aid, the human body and vital signs, unconsciousness, CPR, wounds and bleeding, shock, fractures, transport of injured and sick, burns, tear gas inhalation, poisoning, completion test). 2.2 Distribution of 1st Aid kits in the different communities (to be with volunteers) for use at time of emergency.
