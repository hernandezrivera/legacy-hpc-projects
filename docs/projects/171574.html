Project Summary

The Food Security Sector (FSS) as FSS the primary source of information and analysis on the food security situation in Palestine. aims to: 1.reinforce the information management capacity to inform joint analysis of underlying causes of food insecurity; 2.optimize interactions between information/analysis and planning/implementation; 3.maximize complementarities among agencies and interventions; 4.coordinate responses, within a structured vision to enhance their efficiency.
The proposed project intends to:
-Strengthen coordination and prioritization of humanitarian interventions and facilitating linkages with developmental programmes.
-Support the gathering, production and sharing of information required for effective planning and coordination for food security.
-Improve cooperation with line ministries and institutions in charge of coordination at the central and decentralised levels in order to progressively absorb FSS coordination functions.
The activities under the Food Security Sector (FSS) address key protection needs of vulnerable people in the country by enabling coordinated efforts to ensure direct access to food and critical livelihood needs, and by promoting protection mainstreaming in programming.
The budget presented in this project sheet represents the calendar year requirements for Jan – Dec 2021, covering the specific activities planned during this period only. For the following periods of the multi-year planning, specific yearly budgets will cover each year of operations. The requirements and contributions will be reported in FTS accordingly.

1 - Needs

The Palestinian Food Security Sector (FSS) was established based on the need for a single coordination structure tailored to the specific protracted crisis in the West Bank and Gaza, while inter-linking humanitarian, recovery, and development interventions. The sector synergizes the efforts of active partners across three main pillars – food assistance, agriculture/livelihood and cash-based programming. The FSS works toward building stronger partnerships to support sustainable, comprehensive, and locally owned food security interventions in Palestine.The sector addresses the multi-faceted food security challenges facing the Palestinian population, and assimilates production, access and utilization for an integrated comprehensive approach.The sector’s approach and strategy is to work closely Ministry of Agriculture (MoA), the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) and other dedicated governmental bodies like the Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistic (PCBS) and UN priorities of linking relief and development. The purpose of this coordination body is to provide accountability in leadership, inclusive and varied technical working groups, and ensures a partnership-driven approach to support the Palestinian government priorities in regard to food security in which the measure to contain the COVID-19 impact over the agor-food sector is one of the main priorities.Currently, it is estimated that 1.9 million Palestinians are food insecure. This estimate is based on the results of SEFSec 2018, PCBS population growth rate, and inferences to quantify the effect of COVID-19 pandemic impact over the food security status of the Palestinian population. In Gaza ,72% of the population are food insecure due to the longstanding Israeli blockade and related limitations in economic opportunities, recurring escalations. The already limited freedom of movement afforded by Palestinians in Gaza has been further reduced by the pandemic. While in WB 28% are food insecure. Where the livelihoods of households and entire communities located in, or relying on access to, Area C and East Jerusalem continue to be undermined by the occupation regime such as destruction/confiscation of structures, lacking building permits, restrictions on access to farming and grazing land, and lack of adequate law enforcement on violent settlers. In Gaza, the ongoing Israeli blockade. This has also decimated the local economy, eroded self-reliance and households’ living standards. The unemployment rate in the Gaza during the 2nd quarter of 2020 reached 49.1%, an historical high, up from 45.5% in the previous quarter. The pandemic is placing significant stress on all components of food systems (production, trade and distribution, and consumption), threatening the food security and livelihoods of thousands of vulnerable people in both WB & Gaza. Sustaining, protecting and improving food systems during/after the crisis is vital to reduce economic and food insecurity risks to communities, especially the most vulnerable (such as poor and unemployed people, small scale farmers, herders, Bedouins, single-woman headed households, etc.) and enable a quicker and more inclusive recovery. These circumstances inevitably hinder economic, social and urban development. These constraints affect aid actors as well, preventing them from delivering effective and long-term assistance. Specific humanitarian and development needs coexist in this context; therefore, addressing the shortcomings of purely humanitarian assistance is of utter importance. Moreover, focusing only on the delivery of material subsistence and on interventions substituting local capacities is demonstrated to contribute to exacerbate the chronic vulnerabilities of Palestinian communities. More efforts are needed to provide strategic coherence between humanitarian and developmental schemes. A Nexus approach could bring a desirable shift towards more comprehensive and long-term responses. The nexus has the potential to make aid more effective and efficient. It also provides a good opportunity to work with all stakeholders towards a common goal. Coordination efforts is a point of entry to achieve this synergy and alignment, where the FSS with other actors will support the complementarity and coherence between humanitarian actions and development activities. In light of the current circumstances, there is a need to inform joint analysis of underlying causes of food insecurity, in order to maximize efficiency of responses carried out by different actors and improving the coordination among FSS partners and enhancing information gathering and sharing. The collection of data and related analysis needs comprehensive and coordinated approach to humanitarian & developmental domains, in order to create synergies among all actors. The aim of the FSS is to improve the food security of needy and vulnerable Palestinians, including non-refugees, refugees, Bedouins and herding communities in Area C, and IDPs, by ensuring immediate direct access to food and protecting/restoring their livelihoods, while enhancing resilience. Dedicated resources are required to maintain/reinforce food security analysis and response. The analysis needs to mainstream cross cutting issues, highlighting the different needs of women/men/boys/girls and elderly. Disruption of food systems by COVID-19 are likely to reinforce women’s greater vulnerability to hunger and malnutrition, than that of men. Where possible, these data will be used to provide information about to guide interventions. Strengthening the food security coordination system in the oPt is therefore key. Currently more than ever due to the impact of the COVID-19 outbreak, it is crucial to: a) Strengthen coordination and prioritization of humanitarian interventions, while facilitating linkages with developmental programmes. b) Support the gathering, production and sharing of information required for effective planning and coordination for food security

2 - Activities

1) Support strategic planning of FSS by coordinating a participatory and consultative platform to increase efficiency/effectiveness of FSS partners assistance:• Humanitarian Programme Cycle: collect, analyze and plan for FSS component needs, gaps and responses with involvement of all FSS stakeholders;• Inform the Humanitarian Pool Funds (HPF) and other ad-hoc funding opportunities;• Facilitate roundtable discussion among all partners for better understanding of nexus approach, as well as better engagement in related policy discussions at the national and global level.• Identify priorities, possible gaps or duplications for improved information and data sharing to inter-agency and government plans on food security analysis issues and operations, considering the dynamic scenario and impacts of COVID-19 and any other shocks.• Ensure coherence of FSS partners planning and strategy proposal with the HDP nexus approach.• Coordinate response to demolitions of agricultural assets and settlers’ violence towards farmers in the WB and other ad-hoc responses.• Contribute to inter-sectorial coordination at the central and governorate levels: Identification of needs and response modalities that may require inter-cluster ad-hoc harmonised response. Coordination with other international agencies & NGOs: additional collaboration will be elaborated with Health, protection, WASH, Cash Working Group (CWG), Nutrition on analyses and joint projects.• Set minimum acceptable standards of deliverables, streamline interventions, facilitate operational communication and coordination of inter-sector and intra-sector operations, and review technical specifications. The FSS will continue supporting thematic/ operational/ situational working groups were established according to needs. 2) Support and provide generation of data, analysis and information for planning and coordination of FSS interventions and informing policy adaptation:• Engage FSS actors in sharing information on planning, priorities and findings, and assessing gaps and duplications, to promote complementarity and joint programming;• Monitor and provide technical support to assess possible impact of COVID-19 and other shocks on Palestinian agro-food sector to inform planning and operational responses.• Support the implementation of the Socio-economic Food Security (SEFSec) survey(s) and PCBS data collection capacity• Design and establish a methodology to monitor and inform on the impact of shocks on food security • Analyze needs related to increasing gender mainstreaming in food security programming. FSS gender and protection focal points will continue to work in coordination with UN focal points feeding FSS partners with inputs/analysis on gender-related issues and supporting FSS and partners in promoting gender equality ad mainstreaming protection in all activities.• Incorporate the commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) into all relevant policies ,operational guidelines and to promote them among FSS partners• On quarterly basis, maintain an updated inventory of who-is-doing-what-where-when of FSS partners and maximize the use of the FSS projects module within existing MoA information systems, and 4Ws-style information regular updates to OCHA.• Maintain/improve FSS components of the inter-cluster contingency plan in coordination with OCHA and line ministries. 3) Support strengthening national capacities to contribute to effective coordination of and advocacy for humanitarian interventions within a HDP nexus approach:• Strengthen coordination between humanitarian and developmental interventions for enhanced protection of livelihood and socio-economic inclusion of vulnerable groups • Strengthen capacity of NGOs, line ministries (central/decentralized) in relation to practicing and leading coordination functions. • Promote collaboration among FSS partners for increased awareness of root causes of food insecurity and support campaigns against violation of IHL affecting vulnerable groups • Ensuring greater dialogue on food security emergencies such as COVID-19, especially in the most vulnerable areas, among food, nutrition and health officials, civil society, donors, partners and other key stakeholders.• Support participation of the FSS partners in national efforts towards the achievement and monitoring of SDG2 (like in the activities of the SDG2 Working Group), also contributing to the process of developing FNS Governance institutional capacities, as needed.• Develop and/or support resource mobilization and partnership strategies to strengthen relations with other clusters, UN agencies, governments, donors, I/NNGOs and other key partners.• Foster qualitative programming components like Protection and People Centric issues also by delivering trainings and supporting learning opportunities for FSS partners. • Supporting government-led coordination structures, LACS, and AIDA, PNGO, NGOs consortia, to facilitate the humanitarian-development nexus approach.

3 - Indicators

This project aims to strengthen all aspects of the FSS Response Plan. A sector wide evidence-based needs identification and harmonized response among active sector members should lead to more effective interventions including food and cash assistance as well as agriculture. The staff hired under this project will provide support to the various technical working groups and fora operating under the FSS. Overall outputs-A sector-wide, evidence-based and inclusive response framework linking food, cash and agricultural interventions is improved;- FSS participation in the HPC process for the years 2021-2022 is efficiently facilitated;-The response framework activities links emergency response to development interventions;-FSS partners are facilitated to share best practices and improve their technical capacities in line with recommendations from line ministries;-Food Security related indicators and data published when available;-FSS advocacy priorities are embedded into national plans as well as reflected within FSS partner programmes.-Food Security analysis includes gender gaps in food security, resilience, livelihoods and access to productive assets for females in agriculture as key drivers of resilience for households dependent on agricultural livelihood.
