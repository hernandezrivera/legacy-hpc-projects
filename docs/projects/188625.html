Project Summary

PUI’s FOCOS strategy programming continues to be undertaken in a multi-year strategic way, with 2022 representing the eighth year of implementation of PUI’s integrated-area based approach (IABA) in Syria. Through this approach, PUI make sures to create synergies between the different interventions implemented in areas in need in order to respond to the needs of the Population of Syria as a whole and to help them to go back to their pre-crisis standards of living, to enhance self-sufficiency and resilience in the long run and to promote social cohesion.
In line with SO1 and SO2, this project aims at meeting the basic WASH needs of the conflict affected population in targeted locations by enhancing their access to safe and adequate water supply and to adequate sanitation services, while fostering sustainability through the rehabilitation of community infrastructures.
Therefore, the plan for 2022 and 2023 includes implementing the following interventions:
- 25 Water network and borehole rehabilitation interventions, 21 in 2022 benefiting 225,975 persons (113959 females and 112016 males, 96582 children and 129393 adults) of different community categories, and 4 in 2023 targeting 25,000 PoCs (12607 females and 12393 males, 10685 children and 14315 adults).
- 26 Sewage network rehabilitation interventions, 21 in 2022 benefitting 342,875 persons of different community categories (172912 females and 12393 males, 146545 children and 196330 adults), and 5 in 2023 targeting 14,000 PoCs (7060 females and 6940 males, 5984 children and 8016 adults).
- The removal of 150,000 cubic meters of debris which would benefit approximately 81,000 beneficiaries (40848 females and 40152 males, 34619 children and 46381 adults).
In addition to the rehabilitation of 2 waste treatment plants in Hama, the distribution of water tanks in Aleppo, Hama and Idleb, and water trucking in Idleb to ensure safe supply of clean water to relieve the suffering of beneficiaries and improve health and hygiene conditions for individuals living in the area.
It is also worth mentioning that PUI has already secured around 30% of the needed budget.
Lastly, regarding the locations in Homs and Hama which have a severity score of 2, and based on PUI's presence in the areas, the conducted needs assessments, and the implementation of previous projects in these areas under other sectors, the plan to provide WASH interventions was set in order to ensure a comprehensive and thorough intervention responding and addressing all the needs of the most vulnerable categories of these communities.

4 - Needs

Syria remains a complex humanitarian emergency characterized by over 10 years of ongoing hostilities and their long-term consequences. According HNO 2022 draft, It is estimated that 13.2 million people in Syria will require WASH support in 2022, which represents a rise from 12.2 million in 2021, despite all the extensive efforts and interventions.To better understand the needs on the ground, PUI obtained information from primary and secondary resources in targeted locations, and identified an acute need for WASH support; in many locations, debris is blocking the smooth movement of population, basic WASH infrastructures are out of services due to destruction and lack of local municipalities support to secure spare parts, people are relying on expensive water trucking or unsafe sources for water consumption to meet their needs. Sewage networks also are partially damaged. People are using septic tanks to dispose waste water, and are collectively renting tanks that draw the water from those septic tanks to a proper disposal location. This is not only very costly for vulnerable people but those tanks are also flooding in the streets, exposing the population to health hazards and the spread of insects and rodents, which is negatively impacting the community public health, in addition to the health risk of mix between leakage sewage water and ground potable water in points of damages.

5 - Implementation of activities

Rehabilitation of water supply systems: to ensure that all individuals targeted under this activity have safe access to clean, sufficient and adequate water supply, 21 interventions in 8 governorates are planned to be carried out during 2022, and 4 water networks are planned to be rehabilitated in 2023.Rehabilitation of sewage systems to improve the sanitation conditions of the populations living in the targeted area; where 21 interventions in 8 governorates are planned to be carried out during 2022, in addition to 5 additional interventions in 2023. Also, 2 waste treatment plants will be rehabilitated during 2022.Debris removal: to ensure that populations in targeted areas are living in safe and healthy environments, thus reducing public health risks. This activity is planned to take place during 2022 in Damascus, Dar'a and Homs.Water tank distribution: 1,200 water tanks will be distributed in 2022, and 200 additional tanks will be distributed in 2023. It is worth mentioning that these interventions will be in line with PUI's FOCO and IABA strategies ensuring the complementarity of the implemented activities.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Unequal distribution of services (Likelihood: medium, impact: high). Mitigation measures: Establish strong links with authorities and operators to promote equity and “do no harm principle” all the time; Organize joint planning culture and agree on minimum standards of vulnerability criteria; Include community and authorities in the design and implementation of projects.Risk: Health risk associated with construction works and exposure to chemicals. Mitigation measures: Ensure staff/partners/contractors involved in construction works or working with raw sewage wear appropriate PPE (Personal Protective Equipment) and follow established safety regulations; Conduct/repeat safety training for staff/partners/contractors.Risk: child labour among contractors, (Likelihood: High, impact: High) Mitigation measures: to ensure proper registration system for staff/partners/contractors’ labourers; add zero tolerance disclosure on child labour in the contracts; check and register civil documentation.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: beneficiaries exposed to SEA harm (Likelihood: low. Impact: High) Mitigation measures: PUI will raise the awareness on existing service providers where victims can seek help; Enforce policies with/through all partners to prevent beneficiaries’ exposure to harm; Establish a confidential complaint mechanism; Train staff on code of conduct; address any SEA complaints. PUI has a zero-tolerance policy towards sexual exploitation and abuse, and is committed to implementing the six principles adopted in 2012 by the Inter Agency Standing Committee Task Force on preventing and responding to sexual exploitation and abuse.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions; No curfew or lockdown due to COVID-19 pandemic, Stable security situation, Consistent humanitarian access , No major fluctuation of Syrian Pound, No mass movements of populations , Availability of material in the local market and finally acceptance of community. Risk: COVID-19 new waves and lockdown (Likelihood: medium, impact: high) Mitigation measures; 1) Good coordination with the concerned stakeholders to anticipate such measures. 2) full adherence to WHO recommendations. Risk: shortage of Fuel (Likelihood: medium, impact: high) Mitigation measures: rely on private transportation means, more activate remote management and decrease the site visits, Risk: Quality and/or quantity of water not adequate (Likelihood: High, Impact: High) Mitigation measures: to ensure provision of safe water and monitor water quality including water source, water trucks Risk: Potential exposure to explosive hazard (IED/UXO/ERW) (Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High) Mitigation measures: to work closely with Mine actors and conduct works in cleared areas only; to conduct safety training with staff and contractors - Risk: Theft of WASH rehabilitation/construction materials by armed groups and used in conflict activities ( likelihood: low, Impact High ) Mitigation measures to sign MoUs with local ministries to ensure local ownership pf martials & facilities to promote cost recovery systems for longer terms maintenance and to promote community ownership .
