Project Summary

Protection without Borders seeks to implement the following interventions to address some of the child protection deprivations, which are in line with the overall northeast Child Protection Area of Responsibility strategy, centrality of protection strategy and the HRP 2023. The inventions are summarized below:
A. Create awareness and extensively disseminate the CRA (Child Protection Law)
• The BAY States have domesticated the CRA, this is a great move in ensuring that the rights of children are respected and provides an enabling environment for programs around justice for children, for this to kick off smoothly there is need to create awareness of the law to all stake holders from the line ministries of women Affairs and social development, social welfare workforce at LGA levels, child protection partners, community based child protection committees, cultural leaders, political leaders, the communities and the children themselves, this will take a phased approached that will lay a solid understanding of the law and hence ensuring its smooth implementation. Protection without Borders was key during the contextualization process while the law being drafted and actively participated in the process, given the legal and social welfare background of protection without borders with two skill sets this area suits our mission and objectives.
• Case management support including legal assistance and representation will be supported in this project to ensure that all children who are victims of Violence, abuse and neglect do receive appropriate case management support and referrals as appropriate, Through this support the project seeks to bring in innovation to real time case management systems by setting up a child helpline in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states. POWIB shall work with law enforcement agencies like the police, civil defense and courts to create a rapid response approach to children as well as advocate to create a department with focal points that receive child rights violation within the police.
• Birth Registration, this project shall be an entry point for providing legal documentation for the protection of children and family, especially boys and girls 0-6 years who are mostly without birth certificates. POWIB shall also raise awareness on the rights of children & importance of birth registration working closely with the mandated ministries, health centers and hospitals to ensure all children are registered at birth.
• The project will focus on supporting the training of justice actors on the diversion guidelines for petty offenses and this will reduced the number of children in contact with the law in detention centers, this will also be linked to advocacy for speedy trails and representation for child offenders, the project will also aim to support justice actors on child friendly investigation procedures, the project will initiate at state level audio video links to ensure that children can testify in private if need be to avoid further traumatizing the children to come face to face with the offenders and also ensure they are not further subjected to harm and physiological torture.
• Localization through this project, efforts will focused on strengthening the justice systems and actors on child protection, efforts will be undertaken to provide relevant trainings and institutional support to local and international organizations with justice for children programs, the project will merit on the internal capacities of its team lawyers and where necessary local and international consultancies will be sought to bring in broader global and best practices as applicable to strengthen the justice for children programs in the Northeast Nigeria.
• POWIB shall monitor and evaluate the project progress and deliverables through conducting project baseline and end line after the close of the project to measure impact and progress, as well as challenges lesson and projection for opportunity

1 - Needs

13 years of war in the Northeast Nigeria has deprived children of a normal childhood, exposing them to violence, abuse, neglect, exploitation, and conscription into non stated armed groups. The most vulnerable include children in IDPs camps, secondary displaced children from closed camps, returnee communities, minorities, special interest groups, CAAG, unaccompanied/separated children, and children with disabilities. Girls constitute 53% of the children in need and are exposed to complex /grave violations of children’s rights such as denial to the basic right of education, forced child marriage, sexual abuse, emotional/ physical violence, and trafficking. Boys constitute 47% of the children in need and face with a number of risks, including forceful recruitment/abduction/re-recruitment by non-state actors. Approximately 2.1 million people are in need of child protection services due to the breakdown/absence of child protection systems and community-based safety networks. Symptoms of child emotional distress were reported by 6% respondents in the MSNA, which was most prevalent in IDPs, secondary displaced people from close camps and returnee households. The DTM Site and Location Assessments estimate that 442,600 children have been displaced due to conflict/insurgence, which has a significant impact on a child’s rights. Further compounding the issue, is that 10% of the sites/locations are occupied by IDPs who have been displaced more than once, contribute to even higher levels of stress among children highlighting the urgent need for mental health and psychosocial support services (MHPSS). An estimated 7,700 children have been separated from their families due to the conflict, and urgently require family tracing, case management and alternative community-based care. Case management is urgently required to link children who have survived a form of violence with response services. UNICEF estimates that 60% of the children have experienced some form of violence in Nigeria. Violence against children increases in emergency settings due to the erosion of social protection mechanisms, insecurity, unmet basic needs, limited legal support and food insecurity leads to school drop-out and child labour (1,590 households in the MSNA raised this and child marriage stands at 44% for girls UNICEF), Children in contact with the law -Children in Nigeria come in contact with the justice system as survivors or victims, witnesses to a crime, and could be alleged, accused, or recognized as having broken the law, especially the case for vulnerable children exiting armed groups, including those from minority groups; children with disabilities; migrant children, trafficked children and children in jails, detention centers and other places that deprive them of liberty. Currently, the justice systems at federal and State levels are not equipped to fulfil children’s rights and needs especially in the Bay states, for instance, justice professionals - police, prosecutors, lawyers and judges often lack the specialized training to support child survivors, victims, witnesses or alleged offenders, and many of these actors may not understand the gender-specific vulnerabilities children face when coming into contact with justice systems. In the northeast data of children in detention centers is difficult to get due to the sensitive nature of the issue, detention conditions for these child specific facilities can be severe, and correctional facilities, including Borstal homes, are generally overcrowded and substandard, depriving children of their rights in many ways - including by limiting their access to health care, nutrition, psychosocial support and education. Protection without borders aims to work with government entities at state levels to roll out guidelines on diversion and alternatives to detention, including reintegration for children exiting detention facilities and Borstal homes.
