Project Summary

Project Objective: The resilience and the productive capacity of vulnerable households are restored/enhanced and livelihoods protected in Gaza, drawing from the following Country Strategic Objectives and FSS activity:
• Country Strategic Objective #3: The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued.
• FSS Objective #1: Livelihoods of vulnerable households are protected, and their resilience, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced and Objective #2: Households suffering from limited of access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs.

Needs

In the oPt food security continues to be affected by the protracted nature of the crisis, repeated shocks, continued restrictions on freedom of movement, constrained productive capacities and a lack of economic opportunities. The nature of food insecurity in the oPt is primarily caused by limited economic access to food. Farmer communities in the Gaza face challenges in their protection, livelihoods and resilience due to ongoing crisis, blockades and climate change shocks.In addition to what is already reported in the FSS HNO narrative, October 2017 and the Strategic Objectives (CSOs 2018) Humanitarian Response Plan and in reference to the HPC 2019 FSS Needs/Response Framework, and in Line with the Gender Guidance for the Humanitarian Program Cycle 2018 2020. For the past decade, the Gaza has suffered from a chronic electricity deficit that has undermined its already fragile living conditions. As of May 2017, the electricity deficit has become the worse rolling blackouts of 16 20 hours per day. The significant shortages have left 2 million residents of Gaza with just a few hours of power a day, turning many aspects of ever women, men, boys’ and girls’ life miserable and raising additional deterioration to the already impoverished Gazan. This has had a direct impact on the delivery of services, including water supply, sanitation, health, education and agriculture. According to the UNCTAD, if the economic trend in Gaza continues at the current pace, Gaza will be uninhabitable by the year 2020.The overall guiding principle for CARE WBG’s theory of change is adopting an impact driven approach, acknowledging that our impact groups (women, girls, boys and youth) require emergency and early recovery supports, however CARE will utilize interventions that address both immediate needs, as well as the underlying drivers of those needs. CARE focuses on restoring the capacity of vulnerable farming communities and livestock holders, and enhancing the ability of women and men farmers to recover from the protracted crises and cope with the environmental shocks.Based on CARE various reflections and learning form the gender transformative programming, female Gazans negative experiences of the crisis are compounded by social norms which inhibit their access to food, inputs, finance and livelihoods’ services, as well as lower control over income and household decision making (CARE, 2014, validated 2018) where the economic space for women is normally confined by what is culturally acceptable for women to engage in. Women own or co own only 7.2% of agricultural holdings, but they contribute about 60% to labour requirements in agriculture. In Gaza Strip, 46% of female headed households are severely and moderately food insecure, while for male headed households is 39%. Female unemployment reaches as high as 71.5%, (compared to 36.2% for males), and is higher amongst younger age groups (PCBS 2017)The intervention builds on CARE and its partners (MAAN & PARC) various and comprehensive needs assessment, baseline/endline surveys, (50% females and 50% males) constraints and opportunities analysis, gender in agriculture research (CARE 2017 targeted 100% women) and context analysis, that completed throughout 2016/2017. Findings indicate that, the destruction due the conflict heavily impacted the agricultural sector; about 17,331 dunums of vegetables plots were destroyed. As of 2016, only 10% of damaged infrastructure is rehabilitated while 90% remains not rehabilitated so far, and there are still significant needs in the animal and poultry farms which recently remain unmet (MoA 2017). Most of the interviewed framers indicated constrains accessing to irrigation water due to damaged wells and carriers as well high needs to rehabilitate the damaged open lands. The lack of possibilities to rebuild these agricultural infrastructures leaves households unable to provide neither for their own consumption, nor for income generation.

Activities

This intervention will aim to restore and enhance the resilience, food security, productive capacity of vulnerable households in Gaza to protract their livelihoods taking into account the local impact of climate change. As such, the project aims to support affected communities in Gaza by rehabilitation and provision of productive assets and inputs, for small scale women and men farmers, breeders, water wells and carrier lines including promotion of technical capacities and improving their production and using the BBB (Build Back Better) approach for increased resilience. The activities are designed based on standards adopted by technical working groups of the FSS, and in line with the accountability to affected populations (AAP) with respecting the rights, dignity and protection of all segments of affected communities. The activities are closely coordinated with FSS, stakeholders, local CBOs, while being aware of what is offered in the targeted locations, as per the FSS coordination mechanisms 2018. The activities will be implemented in partnership with MAAN who will be responsible of the technical implementation at the field level. CARE will be responsible of the overall quality management and will act as the main contact for donors and stakeholders. The project activities will be as follows: Activities for O #1: Lands Rehabilitation 1: Conduct a baseline assessment and selection of end bene ciaries. 2: Conduct needs assessment to identifying their needs by gender, age, and vulnerabilities. 3: Cleaning & ploughing, and installing protective fence and the local irrigation system. 4: Distributing farming manual tools, accessories, local fertilizers, seeds and seedling. Activities for O #2: Poultry Farms Rehabilitation including provision of renewable power supply 1: Conduct a baseline assessment and selection of end bene ciaries. 2: Conduct needs assessment to identifying their needs by gender, age, and vulnerabilities. 3: Rehabilitating the poultry farm at targeted HHs. 4: Installing solar system for the targeted poultry farms (1Kw) as per their outlet capacity needs. 4: Provide extension services for the targeted poultry farms in close coordination with MoA. Activities for O #3: Maintenance/Rehabilitations of Water Wells and Carrier Lines as a complementary to water wells and the land rehabilitation activities 1: Conduct a baseline assessment and selection of end localities and bene ciaries. 2: Conduct needs assessment to identifying the localities and damages. 3: Rehabilitating the wells and maintaining/installing the carrier lines as complementary to water wells and land rehabilitation as this will improve access to water for many farmers who don’t have access to water and will be able to get the water after the wells’ rehabilitation, land rehabilitation and installing/rehabilitation of the water carriers . Activities for O #4: Installation of renewable power supply for Irrigation water wells and collective ponds. 1: Conduct a baseline assessment and selection of end localities and beneficiaries. 2: Conduct need assessments to identifying the areas of damages. 3: Installing the solar system for the water wells (25Kw) and ponds (6kw) as per their outlet capacity needs. We would like to confirm that all extension services to farmers in all activities will be conducted in strict and close coordination with MoA.

Indicators

O 1: 300 affected vulnerable small scale farmers, at least 15% are female of open fields supported with inputs and services to improve productivity. Ind. 1.1: 600 affected dunms of small scale female and male farmers rehabilitated. Ind. 1.2: 300 small scale HHs farmers at least 15% female farmers improved production. Ind. 1.3: 300 small scale HHs farmers at least 15% female HHs increased access to agricultural services and resources. Ind. 1.4: Temporary employment created for 70 unemployed skilled and unskilled workers to generate income. O 2: 120 women headed HHs supported with rehabilitation (not establishment) of their poultry production units including provision of renewable power supply. Ind. 2.1: 120 existing damaged poultry production units rehabilitated and provided with solar systems with outlet of 1kw each. Ind. 2.2: 720 individual women, men, girls and boys have increased resilience. O 3: 330 small scale (women and men) farmers supported to access to water for irrigation purposes. Ind. 3.1: 12 water wells damaged and or weekly functioning rehabilitated. Ind. 3.2: 10,000 meter of carrier lines maintained/installed as a complementary to water wells rehabilitation. Ind. 3.3: 330 small scale farmers improved access to irrigation water. O 4: 470 small scale (women and men) farmers benefited from provision of renewable power supply to improve productivity. Ind. 4.1: 10 solar systems for 10 irrigation water well installed with outlet of 25kw each. Ind. 4.2: 8 solar systems for 8 water collection ponds installed with outlet of 6kw each. M&E: CARE/MAAN will conduct a baseline survey using participatory approaches engaging CBOs, local committees, community’s representatives through focused group discussions and re ection workshops for women, men, girls and boys to ensure effective implementation, gauge interest, ownership, vulnerability etc. Two beneficiaries’ selection forms/questionnaires will be led out by social workers to measure the socioeconomic vulnerabilities including damages from war/con ict/shocks. The second form will address the livelihood conditions and will be led by the agricultural engineers. Coordination with FSS and other stakeholders will be conducted to avoid selection duplication/overlapping. CARE/MAAN will implement a complaint handling mechanism, building on CARE/MAAN’s existing mechanism for documenting, processing and responding to complaints tailored for this project to ensure transparency and accountability to affected population (AAP). Information gathered will be used in the project cycle review to identify/make recommendations for improvement. Protection will be mainstreamed in the project delivery (including respect for principles of non-discrimination, do no harm, etc.); and cross cutting issues, including gender, age, environment and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR). In addition, the project will be incorporated into CARE’s broader Impact, Learning and Accountability System (ILAS), which has overarching quality control standards and process for regular monitoring, promoting and capturing learning, evidence, outcomes and impact, as well as value for money.
