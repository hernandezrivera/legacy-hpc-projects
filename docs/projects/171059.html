Project Summary

Somalia has one of the world’s lowest school enrolment rates. Conflict and natural disasters. An estimated 893,000 people have been displaced in Somalia between January and August 2020, 633,000 food insecurity, 177,000 conflicts,s and 71,000 by drought. An estimated 357,200 (40%) are school-aged children of the displaced population in 2020. Children with disabilities face additional challenges related to cultural stigmas, limited capacity (teachers and schools) to respond to their needs, and lacking targeted/specialized support. This results in 1.37 million crisis-affected children (691,295 girls) being in need of education assistance during 2020.
Banadir is one of the locations host most IDPs in Somalia that have recorded 129, 116 people in need distributed 27114 non displaced, 18076 displaced, Boys 20336 and girls 24855. On 25 September 2020, a recent assessment conducted by Banadir Education Cluster reported a total of 34,642 ( 15,589 girls, 19053 boys) out of school-age children are out of learning who are mainly displaced from Bay and Lower Shabelle regions of Southwest state. In the last Banadir education cluster discussed the challenges faced by out of school-age children as Somalia confirmed (COVID-19) pandemic and Minister of Education announced to close all educational institutions including nursery, primary and secondary schools, and universities, to restrict the spread of the virus. This closure has affected 147 IDP schools in Banadir region where 304944 internally displaced children (14,755 boys and 13,189 girls) were learning. The lockdown of schools disrupted school feeding, distribution of WASH and hygiene promotion materials, and other resources for those children. Some partners reported that the school’s closure has not only affected IDP schools but also will have a bad impact on the livelihoods of many Somali teachers who mainly work in private schools, while the government and NGOs who support education have a budget to support the teacher incentive but Private school cannot pay teachers’ salaries if the lockdown continues, arguing that they depend on the fees paid by the parents and present of children at school.
This project will address to construct new 12 schools with 24 classrooms and inclusive children with disabilities and available to emergency-affected school children and youth in kaxda and Daynile of Banadir region. Each school will be constructed with 4 gender-sensitive latrines (2 for girls and 2 for boys) with handwashing facilities. each classroom will be made of Corrugated Galvanized Iron (CGI) sheets measuring 6x8 meters with 4 windows each and equipped with school furniture of 14 benches and 14 desks reached with protection activities as per the Integrated Education-Child Protection Response Framework. 2400 (1320 Boys and 1080 Girls) of school-age children with disability inclusion will access to emergency school feeding provide with one-time meal food for five days a week for three months benefiting cash in education in emergencies assistance, a total of 2400 (1320 Boys and 1080 Girls) will be access to safe drinking water and hygiene awareness sessions. The project will recruit and train 48 emergency teachers (30 males and 18 female) and a total of 60 CEC members (35 males and 25 females) will be engaged and train in school management, child protection, and school safety for school improvement and retention. Community mobilization will be made since COVID 19 pandemic has been affected by IDPs and community enlarge. A total of 2400 (1320 boys and 1080 girls) benefitting from emergency teaching and learning materials, a total of 48 teachers will be supported with emergency incentives for one year. A total of 8 (2 from MoE, 6 from Cluster) members will be trained in emergency preparedness and response planning and coordination

Mitigation Measures

All SCC staff, newly recruited, field officers, and all other parts that are part of the project like CEC members, training facilitators, teachers, and contractors will sign the SCC SEA Code of Conduct (CoC) to mitigate the risk of SEA cases which may occur in any place any time. Any infringement of the code will result in the termination of the contract and other relevant charges. SCC has implemented a similar project in the target location before and used stakeholders' participatory approach to establish possible risks and discuss mitigation approaches where necessary to reduce risks. SCC shall apply the same approach in this project where applicable using the already established stakeholder networks to assist in establishing other networks in other target locations. Through these networks, the risks to the project implementation will be identified through involvement by the project team, appropriate stakeholders that will include an evaluation of environmental and security factors, organizational culture, and the project management plan including the project scope. Careful attention will be given to the project deliverables, assumptions, constraints, cost/effort estimates, resource plan, and other key project documents. A risk Management Log will be generated and updated as needed and will be stored electronically and as a hard copy printed on monthly basis in the project file. After identifying the risks SCC will assess the range of possible project outcomes and a matrix will be used to determine which risks are the top risks to pursue and respond to and which risks can be ignored as there is no big implication to the project outcomes. The probability and impact of occurrence for each identified risk will be assessed by the project manager, with input from the project team and the SCC education team. in relation to building effective partnerships for children, particularly with teachers/headteachers, CECs and community leaders have to ensure safety strategies with children to improve school enrolment and a better quality of education through improved school management. These groups will develop a coordination team to avoid risks exposed to violence during enrollment and retention as a result of the safety of the school environment.
