Project Summary

INTERSOS proposes a tailored and gender-sensitive winterization response in Hama, Southern Idleb and Rural Damascus, where its ongoing humanitarian programme will ensure a comprehensive and holistic approach to the needs of the most vulnerable. The proposed action is targeting vulnerable HHs living in dire conditions in Hama, Idleb and Rural Damascus Governorates. This intervention has been designed based on outcomes of needs assessments conducted by SARC Hama Branch in prioritized areas, ad-hoc consultations with SARC Livelihood Department; Rapid Need Assessment (RNA) conducted in January 2020 with a follow-up in July and September 2020 in the Hama Governorate as well as 2021 a Labor Market assessment (LMA) in Suran and Muardeh sub-districts. Findings shed the light on soaring poverty, food insecurity and lack of income-generation opportunities. The NFI emergency assistance continues to be essential, where the sector reports 4.7M PiN for 2021, 0.3 more than 2020. This increase is due to the unprecedented economic downturn, rising food insecurity and increasing cost of basic items. Of this, 2.70 million people (57%) are residing in areas of severity 3 or higher with a concentration in 73 out of 270 sub-districts. According to the MSNA, 90% of families at the end of 2020 reported diminished access to NFI’s with unaffordability being the main driver". (2021 NFI sector strategy). Following internal regulations and in compliance with minimum standards in the country for the sector of interventions (NFI), beneficiaries will be selected if multilevel of vulnerabilities are assessed and found. Consequently, over the beneficiaries’ selection process, INTERSOS and SARC will apply a multi-sector vulnerability criteria, including clusters' criteria for NFI in the country and guaranteeing protection mainstreaming (ensuring specific attention to the specific needs of vulnerable groups such as female head of HHs, pregnant/lactating women, elderly, persons with disabilities). Winter-relief items will be distributed to the targeted HHs to reduce the impact of exposure to extreme weather. The kit composition will be tailored according to the specific needs of beneficiaries, each geographical area, availability of items and materials. The NFI kits are set as per NFI sector recommendations: winterization kit specifically costs between $150 and $170/HHs. Each basic kit is composed of winter clothing (children and adults), winter jackets (for men and women), thermal blankets and extra plastic sheets. Additional items such as items for babies, boots, tarpaulins, flashlights and buckets may be added according to need, availability and budget. The composition of the kit has been done using the NFI Sector standard composition. A total of 1650 NFI winterization kits are distributed combined with awareness sessions on COVID 19 prevention. In September 2020, surveyed communities reported different NFI needs among different groups, based on gender and age. For instance, boys and girls are in greater need of clothing and shoes. Men are in greater need of heating fuel, light sources, and water containers. Women and girls need sanitary pads and kitchen-related items, whereas the elderly reported requiring blankets, energy, and light sources. These findings demonstrate the wide range of items that HHs continue to struggle to access and which become particularly urgent in winter season. A PDM exercise is carried out after the distribution, usually about 2 months after in order to survey beneficiaries on their satisfaction and usefulness of the distributed items feeding sector's info sharing and continuous understanding of the needs. As part of the action, INTERSOS staff and SARC volunteers are provided with briefing sessions on core concepts and policies related to humanitarian principles, Child Protection and PSEA. Moreover, awareness-raising sessions are delivered on hazards, risks, preventive and mitigation measures related to the COVID-19 pandemic.

4 - Needs

Ten years of crisis in Syria have eroded the socio-economic foundations of the country, the rapid depreciation of the Syrian currency, soaring inflation, and increased enforcement of international sanctions. Over 90% of the population are living under the poverty line and COVID-19 pandemic has exacerbated an already dire situation. INTERSOS relies on a set of different analyses: findings from needs assessments conducted by SARC Hama Branch in prioritized areas; ad-hoc consultations with SARC Livelihood Department; Rapid Need Assessment (RNA) conducted in January 2020 with a follow-up in early July 2020 in the Hama Governorate; 2021 LMA in Suran and Muardeh sub-districts, whose findings shed the light on soaring poverty, food insecurity and lack of income-generation opportunities. Higher protection risks have been detected (child labor, school drop-outs) and negative copying mechanisms increasingly adopted by the local population. Lack of income generating activities to provide the necessary financial means continues to hamper access to basic HH items among the most vulnerable population in need. In fact, NFI emergency assistance continues to be essential, where the sector reports 4.7M PiN for 2021, 0.3 more than 2020. "This increase is due to the unprecedented economic downturn, rising food insecurity and increasing cost of basic items. Of this, 2.70 million people (57 per cent) are residing in areas of severity 3 or higher with a concentration in 73 out of 270 sub-districts. According to the MSNA, 90 per cent of families at the end of 2020 reported diminished access to NFI’s with unaffordability being the main driver". (2021 NFI sector strategy).

5 - Implementation of activities

i. Selection of targeted BNF (individuals) according to vulnerability criteria. INTERSOS coordinates with the SARC Operation and Distribution Dep to identify the areas that have not benefited from such interventions previously triangulating it with the 2021 NFI severity location matrix. Following internal regulations and in compliance with minimum standards in the country for the sector of interventions (NFI), BNF are selected if multilevel of vulnerabilities are assessed and found. Consequently, over the BNF’ selection process, INTERSOS and SARC apply a multi-sector vulnerability criteria,(Head of HH, Displacement Status, marital status,PwD, family size, Income, pregnant/lactating, ) including clusters' criteria for NFI in the country and guaranteeing protection mainstreaming. ii. Distribution of 1650 NFI winterization kits for 1650 HHs in Hama, Idleb and RD Governorates, combined with awareness sessions on COVID 19 prevention. In coordination with SARCwinter-relief items will be distributed to the targeted HHs in designated distribution points, and when possible, within SARC-managed community centers to reduce the impact of exposure to extreme weather. The kit composition will be tailored according to the specific needs of beneficiaries, each geographical area, availability of items and materials. Each basic kit is composed of winter clothing (for children and adults), winter jackets (for men and women), thermal blankets, matrasses and extra plastic sheets. Additional items such as items for babies, boots, tarpaulins, flashlights and buckets may be added according to need, availability and budget. The composition of the kit is designed for an average family size of six people. INTERSOS will carry out a PDM exercise.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk of discrimination, perceptions of discrimination/exclusion of vulnerable groups, lack of communication and engagement of some affected groups. Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High. Mitigation: Ensure that consultations are undertaken with communities, in a way to tailor and adapt the intervention according to needs; Continuous involvement of community throughout the project cycle, in line with the principles of accountability to affected populations (AAP); Adopt and enforce an effective Complaint Response and Feedback Mechanisms, Risk: vulnerable girls and women are exposed to GBV. Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High. Mitigation: Ensure SARC volunteers are trained on GBV principles and response strategies and are able to detect GBV risks; integrate distributions with PSS sessions and/or awareness raising; secure protection mainstreaming across the all intervention. Risk: PSEA. Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High. Mitigation: strict PSEA policy and SOPs are in place; Provide inception and refresher training to INTERSOS and SARC staff; Ensure a proper reporting system, data protection and referral pathways are in place. Risk: Increase of tensions between IDPs, Returnees and Host Communities due to unequal provision of assistance by the project. Likelihood: Low-medium; Impact: High. Mitigation: Secure that a thorough mapping of affected communities and conflict-sensitivity analysis are performed at the onset of the project; consultations and transparent communications with all groups to foster awareness, cooperation and social cohesion.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Security and Access: Local authorities suspend/do not concede authorizations to operate; access to project area is suspended due to military operations, increased attacks/other threats to safety and security; security incidents targeting INTERSOS/SARC occur; Comprehensive Security Analysis and Contingency Plan in place; Continued discussions with local stakeholders/authorities to ensure access to project areas; Up-to-date security analysis; SOPs enforced by staff members, constant coordination and monitoring with security agencies; coordination with SARC Branches and other local authorities prior to deployment in the field. Procurement and Finance: shortage of liquidity, diversion of funds, devaluation of the currency, increase in prices and inflation; breach of sanctions, blacklisted suppliers attempt to bid, poor quality of items and services; solid Financial SOPs are kept up-to-date and contingency measures related to fund transfers are adopted; regular internal audit are carried out; due diligence exercises on suppliers and services providers are conducted with support of Legal Advisor, lists of pre-qualified suppliers are prepared and checked; Health Hazards: covid-19 spreading in target communities; exposure of staff and beneficiaries; strict SOP in place, precautions enforced, distribution of PPEs and awareness-raising mainstreaming on covid-19.
