Project Summary

World Vision Syria Response through this project aims to provide child protection services, mine action and GBV prevention and response services in northwest Aleppo, north-west Syria, Tal Abyad and Ras Alin to ensure improving the quality of community- based services through a holistic approach and expand the reach and facilitate the access of widows and their children to CP and GBV services, in addition of strengthening the capacities of local actors in the child protection and gender domains. WV is currently not operating in NES but with the intention to expand operations in possible locations and initiate close coordination with the cluster/AoR in the northeast region. The proposed project will include community centers, CFSs and WGSSs and will cover urban areas and camps specially widow camps. To ensure the access for the hard-to-reach areas the project also will include mobile teams. The main objective of the project is to enhance the prevention and response of child protection and GBV concerns and ensure the mitigation of related risks with adopting a holistic approach: through the provision of quality child protection services which will include general and specialized CP services (case management) including children with disabilities, the provision of GBV prevention and response services which includes (case management). The project will include establishing effective and community-based service mapping, PFA, PSS for children and adults and PM+ for adults, in addition of supporting the most vulnerable children and women with CVAs and Cash for protection. Risk education and awareness will be provided in supporting the implementing partners to provide quality, gender and age-appropriate messages including child friendly material. When relevant, the project will integrate mine risk education into CP and GBV awareness and advocacy activities. Actions will implement strategies that empower women and girls and prevent GBV, with a particular focus on adolescent girls and to reduce the impact of violence and physical harm. The project aims to strengthen the capacity of humanitarian actors and stakeholders at the national and community levels to assess, analyze and address protection risks and needs and to respond to child protection concerns in Syria and build the capacity of GBV specialist and non-specialist to respond, prevent and mitigate GBV.
When appropriate and needed, World Vision and partners will offer legal counselling and assistance, referrals and information sharing where a legal advisor will be presented to discuss with individuals who may have challenges in documentation, marriage and/or custody disputes. This activity will be part of the services provided under individual case management.
As part of accountability to affected populations and taking into consideration that WMGB are the primary stakeholders WV will ensure the needs of different age and gender groups are addressed and considered throughout the project through regular MSNAs and other needs assessment processes at the local and national levels.
All above activities will be taken into consideration the Safeguarding and PSEA minimum standards and best practices specially after the COVID pandemic and the increased risks after the online modalities and the stressors that raised due to the socio-economic pressure and shortage of services.
Monitoring and evaluation activities will be carried out for all the above-mentioned activities taking into account the sensitivity of some activities (such as case management), All monitoring and evaluation activities will be in accordance with the guidelines shared by the coordination teams (Protection cluster; CP, GBV and MA AoRs), in addition to the periodic reporting mechanisms (4Ws, CM reporting tool), and other reporting mechanisms.

3 - Needs

According to the HNO 2023 (draft), it is estimated that more 6 million children are in need of critical child protection services while approximately 7.8 million people require GBV assistance, out of which over 51% are women and nearly 38% are girls. Women and girls are subjected to increased denial of economic resources, movement restrictions, exploitation, forced & child marriage. Indicated by 84% of children, child marriage is an issue for girls aged 15 to 17 in the community where they live. The HNO 2023 also highlights the concerns of households related to the presence of landmine. Increased number of children and adults have reported signs of psychological distress, and this is highest among female-headed household. According to a WV's study on issues of suicide in NWS, 85% of the respondents agreed that suicide attempts have been on the rise in their community due to economic pressure, violence, and loss of hope. Children continue to be exposed to grave violations including risk of being killed, injured, recruited, used in hostilities, tortured, detained, abducted and sexually abused. A total of 1,895 grave violations were verified by the MRM in Syria as of September 2022. WV conducted a MSNA between August and September 2022, the findings show that over 50% of the respondents indicated children in their communities are affected by child labor. Among those who are in labor work, around 48% of them work in danger/heavy physical jobs, while 13% are involved in begging.

4 - Implementation of activities

WV will ensure enhancing equitable access to quality child protection, mine risk education and GBV services, including children with disabilities and hard to access areas as widow camps. The project will implement safe spaces. structured awareness raising of the community using the CPMS and GBV toolkit that endorsed by the CP and GBV AoR, strengthen safe identification and referrals of children and women who receive PM+, GBV and CP case management. CVA and Cash support will be used to address immediate needs and root causes of protection concerns. Other services such as PFA, PSS for children/adults, life skills for children/adults using the interagency standards and WV models, in addition of MRE will also be core components of the programme. All activities will include capacity building of staff about CPMS, Safeguarding, PSEA, CP CM and GBV SOPs and when relevant MRE. WV will ensure the implementation of all activities in the centers and outreach beyond the centers for reaching the most vulnerable. 4Ws will be ensured to provide coordinated response, avoiding overlapping. WV has its field staff who will be responsible on monitoring, field visits and support for the partners’ staff, M&E activities will be carried out for all the above-mentioned activities taking into account the sensitivity of some activities (ex:CM) using standard and adapted tools in line with the guidelines shared by the coordination teams and AoRs.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Women & girls have restricted movements outside of home which further prevent them from accessing services & support. The deepening economic crisis has led to an increased child Labor particularly among adolescent boys & girls. The pressure to meet basic needs have put children & their caregivers under high level of stress, with cases of suicide & attempted suicide being frequently reported. The reduced funding for basic services has led to increased risks of exploitation & violence against women & children. WV will consider gender & contextual analysis throughout the implementation including to conduct safety audit to ensure safe access, engage with local authorities on safety in the community. Outreach efforts will be ensured to reach to women, girls & other marginalized groups who have limited mobility. Considering the physical risks due to the conflicts, protection risks related to cash distribution, WV will develop clear communication to manage community expectations, training of related staff on risk mitigation and organizing community consultations. To address few disclosures and lack of trust in services, WV will involve community stakeholders to ensure buy-in of the interventions & continue to build relationships with them. This is particularly important as communities may perceive the provision of protection services as interference to the family’s affairs. Training of staff & actors on safe identification and CM SoPs will reduce the risks of creating stigma.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

WVSR adopts a zero-tolerance approach towards SEA and it is committed responsibility at all times to ensure that all people – women, girls, boys and men – are treated with dignity and respect, furthermore, uphold humanitarian principles and ensure the integrity of the humanitarian operation. Support will be provided for the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation & Abuse mechanisms to protect beneficiaries and their caregivers which include the technical support related to the safeguarding and PSEA available policy review and development with partners, ToT and training to be provided for first line workers, assigning field focal points with partners in addition WVSR field and national focal points, raising the awareness of the community in proper way including child friendly material, regular reporting on all related activities and incidents in coordination with other clusters in addition of PSEA network which is led by IOM. Child safety concerns are identified and monitored through regular child/beneficiary checklists which are used by WVSR field staff which all is constantly monitored through the project lifecycleFurthermore, WVSR has its own incident preparedness plan which is understood by all staff and partners and is summarized by the procedures and actions related to proper responding to safeguarding and PSEA incidents in multiple levels and categories, in addition WVSR uses a standard online platform which is called Ethics Point that ensures the proper and timely response


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

WVSR developed an extensive knowledge of security and access issues related to NWS and is able to manage them and design programming accordingly in close coordination with Partner LNGOs along with WVSR being actively engaged with OCHA Access Working Group through Humanitarian Access Briefing (HAB) platform and bilateral engagement and other multilateral arrangements. Existing and new agreed on arrangements allow WVSR and Partners to avoid and/or address security and access-related challenges taking into consideration the areas severity ranks, while maintaining principled and safe access across operational areas. The risk of not preserving impartiality of intervention is present in NWS due to interference by stakeholders (Local Councils, military groups, etc.) in the beneficiary selection process, in activities in the “Widow Camps” and GBV services as well as recruitment, procurement, etc. WVSR will adopt clear beneficiary selection criteria and will be communicated with members of the communities targeted and authorities to increase the transparency and limit possible interference by third parties. In order to limit possible interference in activities, MoUs with Camp management and other relevant entities will be signed along with a coordination at cluster levels with CP and GBV working groups. Finally, WVSR comply with the minimum procurement standards while being aware of context-sensitivity, and staff recruitment must adopt impartial and transparent procedures.
