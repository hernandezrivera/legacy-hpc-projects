Project Summary

In South Sudan, acute food insecurity is induced by a mixture of many, frequently overlapping shocks, including climate-related shocks, insecurity, large-scale population displacement, and macroeconomic shocks. The cumulative effects of years of wealth depletion, as well as limited access to essential services, contribute to the continued erosion of many households' coping capacity.
As a result of Sudan's start of the war, already overburdened villages along the South Sudan-Sudan border must divide scarce supplies among an ever-growing population.
In Unity State, findings of October and November 2022, show an estimated 751,000 people (66.7% of the population) face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 480,000 people are in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity and 271,000 people are in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. From a climatic perspective, the State experienced drier-than-average conditions until mid-July 2023 along its western borders, leading to significant delays (up to six weeks) in the onset of favourable conditions for planting and early crop development. In Central Unity, flooding has disrupted household planting and crop production.
The situation in Central Unity has been further exacerbated by sub-national conflict, which has impeded most households from accessing fields. Access to inputs has also been disrupted, impacting negatively on agricultural production.
Overall, 6 out of the 9 counties in the State are classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, while the remaining 3 counties are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. With Rubkona, Leer and Mayendit being the greatest concern as over 75% of their total population is facing Crisis (IPC phase 3
The majority are subsistence producers due to a lack of quality inputs such as seeds, and farm tools coupled with storage facilities and capital access. As a whole, there is limited knowledge of seed production and improved farming techniques and methods for reducing post-harvest losses.
This project aims to build the capacity of 400 households (farmers) in Rubkona county, to produce seeds locally by supporting essential crops and vegetable production and productivity through the provision of vegetable seeds, RDAA shall purchase field crops seeds locally (Maize, Groundnuts, cassava and Sorghum) and give them for the FFS establishment and multiplication, field crops seed production techniques and multiplication and other agriculture inputs shall be addressed in this intervention.
RDAA wants to promote vegetables and fruit production as a means of diversifying household diet to address malnutrition which remains a major concern among children, and pregnant and lactating women as further detailed in the humanitarian context analysis, this will be realized through the empowerment of households and as well the IDPs. The provision of improved inputs like vegetable seeds and other tools will significantly contribute to the availability and accessibility of food from farmer’s production.
RDAA will fully engage the state ministry of agriculture and County Agriculture Department of the three counties and other relevant offices, community leaders, women’s groups, youth groups and other social segments throughout the life of the project.
The project will be integrated and enhance responses to the immediate needs of IDPs and the host community in Rubkona County and aid restoration of livelihoods and capacity building to pave the pathway for long-term recovery to strengthen communities’ ability to cope with and recover from ongoing lack of good seeds, malnutrition and poor diet

1. Needs

RDAA has completed an analysis to estimate the impact of floods based on information from multi-sector fast assessments conducted by clusters and other humanitarian groups, as well as information from the RRC. According to IPC ACUTE FOOD INSECURITY AND MALNUTRITION ANALYSIS SEPTEMBER 2023 - JULY 2024, 5.83 million people (46 percent of the population) are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity classified as IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse), with 1.64 million people in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency). An estimated 35,000 people are classified in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) in the Duk (3,000) and Nyirol (3,000) counties of Jonglei State; and the Rubkona County (15,000) of Unity State. An estimated 14,000 South Sudanese returnees who fled the ongoing conflict in Sudan are also classified in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). The most food insecure states between September and November 2023 with more than 50 percent of their populations facing IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse) are Jonglei State (61 percent), Unity State (58 percent), Upper Nile State (56 percent) and Lakes State (53 percent).The number of severely food-insecure people during harvest periods has grown at an accelerating rate in Rubkona County due to poor seed accessibility Seasonal fluctuations in food security are becoming less stack and the overall food-insecure population has grown considerablyNevertheless, Rubkona County has the potential to progress in agricultural practices. Rubkona residents practice agro-pastoralism, augmenting their diets with fishing and foraging. This mix of livelihoods, however, differs depending on the payam. In 2021, an anticipated 20% of Rubkona County households will be farming, with a gross grain output of 0.5 tonnes per hectare (FAO/WFP 2022), rising to 0.7 tonnes per hectare in 2022 (FAO/WFP 2023). The main crop grown is maize, with a smaller amount of groundnut sown as well. Sorghum, sesame, and vegetable farming are also practised, but on a small scale.This only needs some capacity building and supply of some basic agriculture inputs then they can produce for the entire state and reduce the dependence syndrome on foreign importation of seeds. This project stands as a chance for them to get support so that, their one-time production capacity and interest may be enhanced through this intervention. Since the outbreak of conflict in South South Sudan, RDAA has played a critical role in alleviating hunger most in the Counties in Western Equatoria. An effort that will extend RDAA’s service to Unity State and specifically to the people of Rubkona County where there is a limit to the quantity of food available for consumption will help improve local seed supply and sustainable food production.
