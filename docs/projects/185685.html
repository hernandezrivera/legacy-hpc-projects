Project Summary

UNICEF will reach 331,137 beneficiaries (2% PWDs) with child protection services, among which are 224,209 children and adolescents, and 96,130 adults. Women and girls will constitute 52% of beneficiaries. 75% of the project target will be in Rohingya refugee camps and 25% in host communities.
UNICEF will adopt a public health approach to preventing abuse, exploitation, violence and neglect of children, within a social-ecological framework and according to fundamental Protection principles including 'Do No Harm' and the 'Best Interests of the Child'.
UNICEF will work with government institutions to strengthen child protection systems. We will maintain partnership with the Department of Social Services (DSS) to build capacity of the social service workforce, and provide technical support to justice sector stakeholders including judges, police, probation officers and social workers to improve children's access to child-friendly justice, while promoting juvenile justice principles such as diversion and alternative measures to detention. UNICEF will provide human resources and technical input to the Office of Deputy Commissioner to accelerate Birth Registration through upgraded systems and financing.
UNICEF will ensure that survivors of abuse, exploitation, violence and neglect - including Unaccompanied and Separated Children and other vulnerable children - have access to case management, care plans and referrals. Family-based alternative care will be promoted. UNICEF will ensure that adolescent girls and boys have access to Life Skills and are engaged as agents of positive change in their communities through access to information, social mobilization and participation in activities centered around Social Hubs, Multi-Purpose Centers and Adolescent Clubs. We will also provide structured and unstructured Mental Health and Psychosocial Support for children and adolescents to build resilience and ability to cope, and Positive Parenting guidance to caregivers. Covid19 mitigation measures embedded within regular programming.
UNICEF will establish and make functional Community-Based Child Protection Committees through trainings, technical guidance and logistical support.
UNICEF will embrace its role as a lead agency in the Child Protection Sub-Sector Strategic Advisory Group, Protection Working Group, Case Management Technical Working Group, Anti-Trafficking Task Force and other coordination forums, ensuring close collaboration with Government, NGOs, Civil Society and the private sector.
Child Safeguarding and Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) will be mainstreamed. UNICEF will lead the training of implementing partners and ensure mechanisms are in place in line with the humanitarian code of conduct.
UNICEF will mainstream Emergency Preparedness and Response through DRR capacity building for service providers and communities, and through pre-positioning supplies.
This project will give priority to the inclusion of Persons with Disabilities (PWD) ensuring equal access to services in dignity.
UNICEF will promote social cohesion and peaceful existence within and between communities through promoting free flow of accurate information, dialogue, coordination, joint activities, sport and recreation.
UNICEF will strengthen Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) by facilitating complaint and feedback mechanisms, and monitoring to ensure that feedback improves programming. Children, adolescents and caregivers - including those with disabilities - will be consulted.

1 - Identified Needs

According to the Protection Working Group, 896,879 Rohingya refugees currently living in camps in Cox's Bazar require humanitarian assistance in the year 2022. An additional 541,021 vulnerable girls, boys, women and men require similar assistance in the Bangladeshi host communities of Teknaf and Ukhiya upazilla. There are many child protection concerns in the refugee camps and host communities. The Child Protection Sub-Sector Assessment in Rohingya Camps in September 2021 established that the most prevalent child protection concerns perceived by respondents are Child Labour (64%), Neglect (59%), Child Marriage (57%), Risk of trafficking (32%), Separation of children (25%), Physical abuse (23%) and Sexual and gender-based violence (12%). An iMMAP COVID-19 Situation Analysis May 2021 further reported Child Labour as having increased by 16% and the marriage of girls under 18 by 9%. In the host community almost half of all households surveyed (49%) reported that child labour had increased in their communities in the six months prior to data collection; while the increase in child marriage is attributed to increased financial pressure faced by families as well as the desire to keep girls safe. 5% of households in the refugee community reported an increase in violence against children; and Mental Health and Psychosocial distress needs has increased, affecting both children and their caregivers. There is a need to increase community-based, unstructured and structured Psychosocial support, as well as specialized Mental Health services. Parents and caregivers need guidance on positive parenting and coping skills both at the household level and through community-based mechanisms. Adolescent boys and girls face unique challenges that call for tailored measures to mitigate the impact of limited access to education and livelihood opportunities exacerbated by Covid-19. Adolescents are not able routinely to engage and participate in decision-making, and live under the spectre of child marriage, further refugee movements, and exposure to criminal activity such as drug dealing. Child protection systems in Cox's Bazar are under strain. The justice system is not child-friendly and requires investment to bring it in line with the Children’s Act 2013 (amended 2018). Capacity building is needed to operationalize diversion and strengthen the monitoring of children as they move through the justice system. Children’s Courts, Child Welfare Boards (CWBs), Child Affairs Desks at police stations requires further strengthening and coordination. Technical support to the Department of Social Services (DSS) to strengthen the social service workforce is ongoing but requires further support to raise quality standards especially around case management. Birth Registration calls for technical assistance to, and advocacy with, the Office of the Registrar General at national and sub-national levels, and the District Administration. Additional human resources and capacity building of Local Government Units is required on using the upgraded BDRIS and accelerating Birth Registration in the district. Cox’s Bazar District is vulnerable to natural hazards (monsoon, cyclones and landslides) which regularly impact safety, livelihoods and infrastructure.
