Project Summary

According to the results of 35 separate nutrition surveys conducted by FSNAU and
partners in June and July 2021, the overall median Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) in
Somalia remained Serious (11.1% weight-for-height z-score). The drivers of acute
malnutrition include high morbidity, low immunization, low vitamin-A supplementation,
reduced access to milk, and food insecurity. Urgent treatment and nutrition support are
required for approximately 1.2 million children under the age of five years (total acute
malnutrition burden), who will likely face acute malnutrition between August 2021 and
July 2022, including 213 400 who are likely to be severely malnourished. Integrated
interventions should be provided to support recovery and prevent deterioration in the
health situation.
The proposed project will support two mobile and 2 static sites in Jowhar and Waajid district. ARD plans to deliver an integrated package of promotive, preventive and curative maternal, newborn & child health services and scale-up outbreak early warning and response (EWAR) and case management of epidemic prone diseases through integrated response team as part of the efforts to support the flood/drought affected communities in Jowhar and Waajid districts. The project aims to establish mobile clinics in Jowhar and Wajid IDP and host community’s sites and scale up the health care through skilled health professionals. ARD through this project will provide regular medical supplies and life-saving drugs to established health facilities, establish referral mechanisms and improve the coverage of measles vaccinations, support safe motherhood and reproductive health while also ensuring readiness to prevent and respond to outbreaks such as malaria, measles or AWD/Cholera and promote health update through health education and beneficiary sensitization

1. - Projects activities

Provide free outpatient consultations to 9250 drought affected people both IDPs and host communities (including 8550 women, 49000 girls, 4400 boys and 1150 men) through support of two mobile and 2 static clinics in Jowhar and Waajid district. The services to be offered include to treatment of pneumonia, malaria, diarrhea, measles, skin conditions, injury care in children and urinary tract infections, sexually transmitted infections.Conduct routine and supplementary immunization to children under the age of five years against measles, pneumonia and other communicable disease in the target IDPs and Host communities in Jowhar and Waajid district. This is approximately 9300(4,400 boys and 4,900 girls) and estimated 8550 women of child bearing age with protective dose of tetanus (TT) in IDPs and host community. Support four (4) integrated emergency response team (2 static and 2 mobile sites) with medical supplies and equipment to provide medications to affected communities and hard to-reach host communities during emergencies and epidemics with specific focusing on hard-to-reach rural areas, IDP settlements and nomadic populations in Jowhar and Waajid district.Provide emergency obstetric care services including skilled delivery, early initiation of breast feeding within the first hour after delivery to 1150 women. Provide health education messages related risk factors during pregnancy and child birthThrough the integrated emergency team ARD will ensure to provide mental and psycho-social support including clinical management of rape and other form gender-based violence as well as provide referral mechanism to ensure the GBV cases are handle in confidential and care requirement will liase both partners working on GBV and child protection for any further related assistance as well as the local authority and community leaders in the target location.ARD will conduct five days capacity building of 30 (15 male and 15 female) community leaders, religious leaders, women and youth leaders in the target sites with basic mental health and psychosocial services to enable encourage the community leaders role as a sources of community-based social support by working with them through the integrated response team, this will ensure coordinated and comprehensive health care services delivery through the active community participation and involvement of the community earlier health seeking behaviour.ARD will conduct five days training of 30 (15 male and 15 female) health workers (although some will receive more than one training) in total. Trainings will focus on building the capacity of health workers in the mobile team to be able to treat common childhood illnesses using the standard IMCI guidelines. In addition, health workers will be trained on effective disease surveillance and reporting on EWARN as well as preparedness for Communicable disease outbreaks.
