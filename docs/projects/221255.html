Project Summary

Based on RAGL’s Legal Aid Clinic, which was established by RAGJ's Justice and Rule of Law (JRoL) policy and procedures, this project shall provide survivor-centred pro bono (free) mobile, on-site and online legal and non-legal services (including Shelter/NFI – essential household items) for victims and persons at risk of human rights violations in 30+ sub-divisions in Northwest and Southwest regions, of Cameroon. Hence, it provides varied pathways to achieving through various activities involving legal education and research, legal advisory, legal servicing, non-legal servicing, litigation and policy advocacy. A comprehensive legal rights sensitisation package addressing legal issues related to displaced persons' rights, child rights, disability rights, women’s rights and land/property rights will constitute the basis for collective and individual counselling and consultation sessions in communities with over 16,000 persons. Based on agreed strategies and following tailored messages, legal clinical sessions will be held in churches, schools, meeting groups, detention centres, etc.
To facilitate tracking and reporting of human rights violations and cases needing protection assistance, the project will utilise established/created community-based protection committees (called legal support committees as established by UNHCR-RAGJ) comprising various community leaders and protection actors to set up reporting mechanisms in each community. A toll-free line will be made available to community members and coordinated to ensure case-by-case allocation of specialised legal and non-legal servicing to conflict-affected persons. The project referral system shall primarily be based on existing legal referral paths in each community and complemented with non-legal services such as specialised socio-economic, MHPSS, health, medical, and other services. Dignity kits, post-rape kits, and cash to mitigate protection risk (multipurpose, shelter/NFIs, etc.) shall be provided to survivors.
Shelter/NFI are critical to ensuring the security of persons facing multiple protection risks and vulnerabilities. Shelter/NFI will be implemented through cash transfer using MTN Mobile Money. Specifically, data collection and screening on shelter/NFI will be done by locally hired and trained enumerators and database managers with support from local authorities, community leaders and members. RAGJ, YOP and SS4CD will work in partnership with other Shelter Cluster partners and relevant actors to build a synergy of actions, minimise duplicity, and reinforce the impact of this intervention, including risk mitigation towards environmental and man-made shelter needs.
Over 16,000 persons will be reached; 10,000 will receive legal counselling through sensitisation and consultation with over 100 litigation and advocacy cases (out-of-court negotiation). 2,400 persons will be supported in obtaining civil documentation, and 2,500 persons will be reached through HLP awareness raising and specialised HLP servicing. GBV will be mainstreamed throughout the project, with approximately 10,000 persons sensitised on GBV prevention and mitigation. About 10,000 persons will be reached to strengthen child-protection services in communities, including protection from sexual exploitation and abuse. Approximately 500 people with disabilities will be supported socio-economically (CBI) to improve their livelihoods.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Vulnerable, marginalised and displaced persons often lack the knowledge and the means to access and use their legal rights to protect their interests. Having endured political unrest, human rights violations and widespread violence, particularly evident in the Anglophone crisis and its effects on the Northwest and Southwest regions since 2016, the protection environment has worsened, making it difficult for conflict-affected persons to access legal services, which are expensive and absent where most people live. Protection Cluster’s protection monitoring updates for July-September 2024 indicate 1,929 victims of human rights violations, with 614 victims of arbitrary arrest and detentions, 352 victims of physical assault/abuse (not related to GBV) and 231 victims of killing (including extra-judicial, arbitrary or summary execution) amongst other rights violations. Amidst such insecurities, the changing dynamics of the family setting further destabilise protective measures for victims of GBV as some families have been dispersed across communities and regions in search of shelter and security. Positive parenting and family cohesion are weakened, and many are unaware of the legal implications of their actions and the risk of prosecution in case of child abandonment, irresponsible parenthood, deprivation of education, early/forced marriage or spouse dissertation, which often affects children negatively. School-related SGBV is pertinent, with sexual assault and rape incidences on the rise. Besides ignorance of the legal implications of GBV, most people in communities are not acquainted with the necessary support required to deal with the practicality of legality vis-a-vis case-by-case scenarios. Also, the absence of adequate protection mechanisms for survivors makes denunciation of GBV perpetrators a critical challenge, and families/communities need to explore alternative ways of getting justice and security, which, of course, includes food security, shelter/FNI and other socio-economic needs. Besides, the widespread destruction of property, displacement of populations, and insecurity regarding land ownership make access to secure housing and land a crucial factor for recovery and stability, whereas most families have limited livelihoods. According to the analysis of data collected for the MSNA in 2025, about 1,387,862 Cameroonians will need adequate shelter and household items. The project communities of Mezam, Kupe, Meme, and Fako divisions have the highest concentration of people needing shelter/FNI. However, across remote communities in the Northwest and Southwest regions, elderly people without family support, children, women-headed households, people living with disabilities, and the chronically ill have serious shelter/NFI needs. Lessons learnt from RAGJ's 2021-24 experience providing legal and socio-legal assistance to GBV survivors in the Northwest and Southwest regions reinforce the need to strengthen the referral system and coordinate with other GBV actors to ensure adequate servicing to GBV survivors, especially on MHPSS, health, Shelter, NFIs, and cash to mitigate protection risks. It is in this regard that the Legal Aid Clinic is expanding its survivor-centred pro bono (free) mobile, on-site and online legal and non-legal services (including Shelter/NFI) to an increasing number of victims and persons at risk of human rights violations in 30+ sub-divisions in Northwest and Southwest regions, of Cameroon. However, following RAGJ’s experience in facilitating data collection on CBI, including on Shelter/NFI in 2023 and particularly in 2024, with strengthened data collection capacity by UNHCR, the Legal Aid Clinic is extending its work to implement shelter/FNI and not just only submit referrals to UNHCR. The Shelter/NFI needs encountered in communities are alarming, whereas protection, especially legal protection, requires attaining security or at least a degree of safety.
