Project Summary

The beginning of 2023, people in South Sudan continued to face multiple challenges – conflict, endemic violence, climatic shocks such as flooding and localized drought, food insecurity, and disease outbreaks. A staggering 76 % of South Sudan's population needed some form of humanitarian and protection assistance in 2023, up by 4 per cent since last year.
The April 2022 agreement between SPLA-IG and SPLA-IO leadership to unify army command structures and forces has continued to reduce political tension. However, the slow implementation of the remaining elements of the 2018 peace deal and the decentralized command structure of the armed forces continue to limit the Transitional Government of National Unity’s ability to enforce orders nationwide.
South Sudan’s vulnerability to climate change and natural disasters compounds the country’s humanitarian crisis, jeopardizing recovery and undermining development efforts. The country ranks among the five countries in the world that is most vulnerable to the effects of climate change, as evidenced by communities devastated, destroyed and displaced by large-scale flooding across the country. Since its independence in 2011, the country has suffered from severe droughts (2014, 2017, 2019, 2020, 2021, and 2022), resulting in high numbers of fatalities, displacements and loss of livestock which have severely impacted people’s livelihoods.
South Sudan remains in a serious humanitarian crisis. Some 9.4 million people, 76% of the population, are estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2023, an increase by half a million people compared to 2022 ( Sept 2023, World Bank). Women and children continue to be the most affected. The humanitarian crisis has been further exacerbated by the recent conflict in neighboring Sudan, which has led to an inflow of refugees and returnees into South Sudan. As of September 2023, 262,298 individuals have arrived in South Sudan, 91% of whom are returnees.
Aweil South and Aweil Centre Counties have poor food production capacities and limited sources of income generation, making them extremely vulnerable. Poor utilization of land and lack of agricultural inputs, the inadequate extension services and government support programs, and poor farmers’ crop production knowledge and skills are among the major limitations in the targeted project areas. Most are primarily subsistence farmers relying on rain-fed to meet their food and cash needs. They have an average farm size of 1.8 ha. Cultivation is often carried out by women which limits the area households can cultivate.
FLDA’s proposed interventions aims to deliver lifesaving humanitarian interventions that meet both short and medium term hunger gaps while helping households build longer term food security and resilience against future shocks and stresses. FLDA’s proposed interventions contributes to Food Security and Livelihoods cluster objectives - CO1, CO2, and CO3. This directly contributes to achievement of SDG Goal 2: End Hunger, achieve food security and improved nutrition, and promote sustainable agriculture, whilst simultaneously contributing to several other SDGs.
The program will target vulnerable populations, especially women, girls, elderly, disabled, PLW, children U5, HIV & the chronically sick amongst IDPs, refugee, returnees or host communities. FLDA is well placed to implement the above program as it has been operating in the location for the last 9 years, having established a strong working relationship with local authorities, built trust, delivered inclusive and impactful programs that mainstream PSEA. FLDA is an active member of the coordination mechanisms and ensures community participation in project management cycle. WFP’s ACL (3, 853 HH) and FAO’s Resilience for Agricultural Livelihoods Project (4,956 HH) and Emergency and Resilience Livelihoods Project (4,000 HH) in Aweil South will further help FLDA to address food and nutrition security and strengthen synergies and the impact.

1. Needs

In Northern Bahr El Ghazal, during the period of October to November 2022, an estimated 580,000 people (56.9% of the population) faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 337,000 people were in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, and 173,000 people were in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. During this period, all counties were classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. During the post-harvest period of Dec 2022 to March 2023, the food security situation was expected to improve with an estimated 533,000 people (56.9% of the population) likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. Of these, 349,000 people were expected to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, whereas 184,000 people will be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Food insecurity is mainly driven by the depletion of food stocks for households whose agricultural activities were affected by climatic shocks such as flooding and dry spells; a reduction in access to livestock products as animals started to migrate with the onset of the dry season; the hoarding of food stocks by traders as they wait for the lean season that is characterized by high food prices; and closure of the trade routes between South Sudan and Sudan which is affecting market functionality. To mitigate these effects, some of the households will engage in collection and sale of natural resources, with fishing also supplementing household food sources. During the lean season period of April to July 2023, the situation somewhat deteriorated with an estimated 656,000 people (66.3% of the population) anticipated to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. Of these, 379,000 people are anticipated to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, and 277,000 people are likely to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. During the lean season, all counties will be classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Specifically for the targeted county of Aweil Centre with the population of 76,117 persons during the period of Oct-Nov 2022 (15,000 persons); Dec 2022-March 2023 (11,000 persons) and April- July 2023 (19,000 persons) respectively will and/or be classified under this IPC phase 4. Whereas for Aweil South County with the population of 141,217 persons during the period of Oct-Nov 2022 (28, 000 persons); Dec 2022-March 2023 (21,000 persons) and April- July 2023 (36,000 persons) respectively will be classified under this IPC phase 4. Food insecurity is mainly driven by the depletion of food stocks for households whose agricultural activities were affected by climatic shocks such as flooding and dry spells; a reduction in access to livestock products as animals start to migrate with the onset of the dry season; the hoarding of food stocks by traders waiting for the lean season that is characterized by high food prices; and closure of the trade routes between South Sudan and Sudan which is affecting market functionality.

2. Project activities

1. Provision of emergency food production for main season agriculture seeds & tool distribution; fishing, vegetable & crop kits. Though voucher approach, 4,756 households will receive seeds and tool in Aweil South and Aweil Centre; to be implemented through seed fairs organized to bring traders and farmers together to transact agricultural inputs.2. Conditional cash transfer (investments in assets, training, or communal work groups/ planning committees etc.) distribution to food insecure households in Aweil South benefiting 9,569 HH. FLDA has already running LoA with FAO. 3. Provision of nutrition vouchers for poultry & goat distribution to 900 households; targeted counties of Aweil South County. 4. Provide main season agriculture seeds to 18,756 vulnerable HH in Aweil South and Aweil Centre counties.5. Support 5 community based ago-nutrition extension networks; targeted counties of Aweil South and Aweil Centre.6. Nutrition Trainings for Households with Children under 5: Working closely with the nutrition partners, FLDA will also target 25% of the beneficiaries benefiting from CFA/conditional food assistance to participate in nutrition trainings. Trainings will be targeted towards ensuring that food preparation and consumption practices promote dietary quality for children under 5 as well as pregnant and lactating women. 7. Provide training to 1,800 farmers/agro-pastoralist on good agronomic practices, and pest and disease through farmer/agro-pastoral field schools, demonstration plots. Farmers will be organized into 60 groups to learn through demonstration gardens established in strategic locations identified by targeted communities. Acquired skills are expected to help them improve agricultural knowledge and skills to produce better yields. 8. Training in Disaster Risk Reduction/Management which will focus on natural resource management, early warning systems, peace building and conflict resolution. And strengthen local peace structures.9. Strengthen access to sustainable agriculture extension through establishment of community-based agriculture extension network that seeks to strengthen peer to peer learning networks at household level. 60 HH will be trained as Master Trainers to build skills and enhance knowledge among participating farmers in communities.10. Training and capacity building for entrepreneurship and business development that is expected to support the establishment and/or strengthening of 15 Village Savings and Loans groups (VSLAs).11. Training farmers’ group/community leaders on complaint feedback mechanism, gender, leadership, group dynamics, hygiene and sanitation, and primary health care trainings. 12. Support value chain addition/ Post-harvest handling activities: Skills training on agribusiness, post-harvest handling, preservation and processing will also be offered to these organized groups. As a post-harvest- value addition intervention, the organized groups will be supported with tarpaulins to dry their crops. The selected aspects of value chain development will be supported with greater linkages to the market. Through specific activity, value chain support at the group level will be geared towards creating a supply of the demand driven commodities on the market.
