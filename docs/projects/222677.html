Project Summary

This intervention seeks to strengthen protection, resilience, and psychosocial well-being for 8,437 vulnerable individuals across the districts of Palma, Mueda, and Nangade in Cabo Delgado. The primary targets include internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and host communities, with a strong emphasis on addressing the needs of women (53%), children under 15 (28%), persons with disabilities, and older persons (5%). These groups face heightened risks of exploitation, trauma, and exclusion due to the ongoing conflict and displacement in the region. The project’s primary goal is to mitigate protection risks, enhance psychosocial recovery, and build community resilience through an integrated approach that includes protection monitoring, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), referrals to essential protection services, and community-based protection. Each activity is tailored to the unique needs of the targeted districts and designed to address both immediate vulnerabilities and long-term challenges.
Protection Monitoring will be conducted in Palma, focusing on identifying and documenting protection risks faced by 75 returnees. The findings will inform evidence-based advocacy and guide responsive programming by humanitarian actors.
Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) will benefit 699 individuals in Mueda and Nangade, including 405 IDPs, 162 returnees, and 132 non-displaced persons. Services such as psychological first aid, individual counseling, and group therapy will address trauma, anxiety, and other psychosocial challenges, promoting recovery and resilience. Community-based counselors will be trained to ensure culturally appropriate and sustainable support.
Referrals to Protection Services will facilitate access to critical services such as legal aid, healthcare, and social assistance for 2,015 individuals, including 1,275 IDPs and 740 returnees, in Mueda and Nangade. A robust referral mechanism will ensure timely and effective responses to beneficiaries’ needs, with regular follow-ups to enhance service delivery.
Community-Based Protection will engage 5,648 individuals across Palma, Mueda, and Nangade, focusing on strengthening local mechanisms and fostering social cohesion. Activities will include capacity-building workshops, awareness campaigns, and explosive ordnance risk education (OERE), empowering communities to take active roles in identifying and mitigating protection risks.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

This project aims to enhance protection, resilience, and psychosocial well-being for 8,437 individuals in the conflict-affected districts of Palma, Mueda, and Nangade in Cabo Delgado, Mozambique. The primary target groups include internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and host communities, with a focus on vulnerable populations such as women (53%), children under 15 (28%), persons with disabilities, and older persons (5%). These groups face heightened risks of exploitation, trauma, and social exclusion due to prolonged displacement and limited access to essential services. The project adopts a multi-faceted approach to address immediate protection needs while fostering long-term resilience and community empowerment. It includes four core activities: protection monitoring, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), referrals to essential protection services, and community-based protection. These activities align with the Protection Cluster’s strategic goals and focus on inclusive, rights-based interventions tailored to the specific needs of each district. Protection Monitoring will target 75 returnees in Palma to identify and document protection risks such as gender-based violence, exploitation, and displacement-related vulnerabilities. Findings from protection monitoring will inform evidence-based advocacy and guide interventions by humanitarian actors, ensuring that responses are timely and effective. Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) will be delivered to 699 individuals in Mueda and Nangade, including 405 IDPs, 162 returnees, and 132 non-displaced persons. Services such as psychological first aid, individual counseling, and group therapy will address trauma and psychosocial distress, promoting emotional recovery and resilience. Trained community-based counselors will ensure that support is culturally appropriate and sustainable, creating lasting impact. Referrals to Protection Services will facilitate access to critical services for 2,015 individuals, including 1,275 IDPs and 740 returnees, in Mueda and Nangade. Beneficiaries will be linked to essential services such as legal aid, healthcare, and social assistance through a structured referral mechanism. Follow-ups will ensure that services are delivered effectively and that gaps are addressed promptly. Community-Based Protection will engage 5,648 individuals across Palma, Mueda, and Nangade, focusing on strengthening community mechanisms and fostering social cohesion. Key activities will include capacity-building workshops, awareness campaigns on protection risks, and explosive ordnance risk education (OERE). These initiatives will empower communities to take active roles in identifying and mitigating risks, ensuring sustainable local solutions. By addressing protection gaps and strengthening community resilience, this project will create safer, more cohesive communities in Cabo Delgado. Its inclusive approach ensures that vulnerable groups are prioritized, while fostering local ownership and capacity to sustain protection efforts beyond the project’s lifecycle. This intervention contributes to the long-term recovery and social stability of affected populations, aligning with the humanitarian priorities in Mozambique.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The proposed project incorporates a proactive approach to identifying potential risks and mitigating harm to ensure that all interventions are safe, inclusive, and do no harm to affected populations. Comprehensive risk assessments will be conducted at the project’s inception and throughout implementation to identify vulnerabilities, protection gaps, and unintended consequences of project activities. These assessments will include consultations with beneficiaries, community leaders, and local stakeholders to ensure diverse perspectives are considered and risks are contextually understood. One primary risk is the potential for exclusion of vulnerable groups, such as women, children, persons with disabilities, and older persons. To address this, the project will ensure that all activities are designed with inclusivity at their core, incorporating measures to remove barriers to participation and access. For instance, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services will be culturally adapted and physically accessible, and community-based protection activities will involve representatives from all demographic groups to ensure equitable participation. The risk of retraumatization or stigmatization, particularly in MHPSS and gender-based violence-related interventions, is another key concern. To avoid this, the project will adopt survivor-centered approaches, prioritizing confidentiality, informed consent, and culturally sensitive engagement. Trained counselors and service providers will be equipped to manage sensitive cases with professionalism and care, ensuring that beneficiaries feel safe and supported throughout the process. Coordination gaps among service providers present a risk of fragmented support, which could leave beneficiaries without critical services. To mitigate this, the project will strengthen referral pathways and establish clear protocols for coordination with local authorities, humanitarian actors, and community-based organizations. Regular follow-ups will ensure that referred individuals receive the services they need and that any service delivery challenges are addressed promptly. Another risk is the potential for tension between IDPs, returnees, and host communities, particularly in resource-constrained environments. To minimize this, the project’s community-based protection activities will include social cohesion initiatives, such as dialogue sessions and collaborative capacity-building efforts, to foster trust and reduce conflict. Awareness campaigns will emphasize shared goals and the importance of collective action, reinforcing community solidarity. Monitoring mechanisms will play a critical role in identifying emerging risks during implementation. The project will employ a robust monitoring and evaluation (M&E) framework, including real-time feedback mechanisms and periodic reviews, to track progress and detect unintended consequences. Beneficiary feedback will be actively sought to understand their experiences and address any concerns. By integrating risk identification and mitigation strategies into all phases of the project, GCR aims to deliver interventions that are not only effective but also safe, respectful, and responsive to the needs of all affected populations. This approach ensures that the project upholds the principle of “do no harm” while contributing to the long-term resilience and recovery of communities in Palma, Mueda, and Nangade.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The proposed project is designed to ensure meaningful access to assistance, with a particular focus on the participation and empowerment of women and elderly persons. By addressing systemic barriers and promoting inclusive practices, the project aims to create an enabling environment where all groups, especially those who are often marginalized, can benefit equitably from interventions. Ensuring Access to AssistanceThe project prioritizes removing physical, cultural, and social barriers that may limit access to assistance for women and elderly persons. Activities are structured to address specific needs, such as providing gender-segregated spaces for services like mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) and creating accessible venues for elderly persons and those with mobility challenges. Trained counselors and field officers will engage in culturally sensitive outreach to ensure that assistance is not only available but also relevant to the diverse needs of beneficiaries. Additionally, awareness campaigns and referral pathways will target underserved populations, ensuring they are informed about available services and how to access them. Promoting Women’s Participation and EmpowermentWomen’s participation is central to the project’s community-based protection activities, which emphasize the importance of their leadership and decision-making roles. The project will conduct capacity-building workshops for women, equipping them with skills to lead protection initiatives, advocate for their rights, and address issues such as gender-based violence. In MHPSS interventions, women will have opportunities to engage in peer support groups, fostering collective healing and confidence-building. The inclusion of women in planning and implementation ensures that their voices are heard and their unique challenges are addressed. For example, during protection monitoring, women will be consulted separately to ensure they can speak freely about their concerns. This approach not only empowers women but also enhances the overall effectiveness of the project by integrating diverse perspectives. Engaging Elderly PersonsElderly persons, often overlooked in humanitarian responses, will play a critical role in this project. Their participation will be encouraged through accessible platforms such as community meetings, where their insights and experiences can inform project activities. Elderly persons will also benefit from tailored assistance, such as inclusion in MHPSS services adapted to address the mental health challenges they face due to displacement and isolation. Awareness campaigns and social cohesion activities will specifically target older persons to ensure they are not excluded from protection mechanisms or community-based support systems. Fostering Long-Term EmpowermentThe project is committed to fostering long-term empowerment for women and elderly persons. By strengthening local protection mechanisms and building community capacity, it ensures that these groups are not only beneficiaries but active contributors to their communities. Women’s empowerment initiatives, such as leadership training and participation in social cohesion activities, will create opportunities for them to influence decision-making and advocate for lasting change. For elderly persons, their involvement in intergenerational dialogues and community-building efforts will reinforce their value within the community and address issues of neglect and marginalization. Feedback and ParticipationTo further promote meaningful participation, the project will integrate feedback mechanisms that allow women and elderly persons to provide input on project activities and outcomes. Regular consultations and focus group discussions will create spaces for them to express their needs, evaluate interventions, and suggest improvements. This ensures that their perspectives are continuously incorporated, making the project responsive and inclusive.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project places significant emphasis on identifying people with disabilities and ensuring their equitable access to assistance. Recognizing the heightened vulnerabilities and systemic barriers faced by this group, comprehensive measures will be implemented to identify individuals with disabilities and remove obstacles that hinder their participation in project activities and services. To identify people with disabilities, the project will integrate disability-specific questions into community-based surveys and protection monitoring activities. Tools such as the Washington Group Short Set of Questions will be used to capture information about functional limitations, enabling the identification of individuals with visible and non-visible disabilities, including mobility, sensory, cognitive, and psychosocial impairments. Additionally, community consultations will involve local leaders, family members, and representatives from disability-focused organizations to ensure a thorough understanding of the presence and needs of people with disabilities in the target communities. This approach will also identify individuals experiencing compounded vulnerabilities, such as women, children, and older persons with disabilities, who are often excluded from traditional support mechanisms. To remove barriers, the project will address challenges related to physical and communication accessibility. Service delivery locations, including those offering mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) and community-based protection activities, will be evaluated for physical accessibility. Where necessary, adjustments such as ramps, handrails, and appropriately designed seating will be installed to ensure that individuals with mobility challenges can access these facilities. Communication barriers will be addressed by providing materials in accessible formats, such as braille, large print, and audio recordings, and offering sign language interpretation for individuals with hearing impairments. These measures aim to ensure that people with disabilities can fully engage with the services provided and feel included in the project. The design and delivery of all project activities will be inclusive and responsive to the needs of people with disabilities. For instance, MHPSS services will be tailored to address the specific needs of individuals with cognitive or psychosocial disabilities, while referrals to protection services will include support for transportation and personalized follow-up to ensure individuals can access legal, health, and social services. All project staff, including field officers and counselors, will receive disability inclusion training, equipping them with the knowledge and skills to identify and address the unique needs of people with disabilities respectfully and effectively. The project also promotes active participation and representation of people with disabilities in the planning and implementation of activities. This will involve close collaboration with disability-focused organizations and the inclusion of people with disabilities in community dialogues and decision-making processes. Their involvement will ensure that interventions are informed by their lived experiences and that solutions are both practical and impactful. Moreover, their participation will help raise awareness within the broader community about the rights and contributions of people with disabilities, fostering a more inclusive and equitable environment. Monitoring mechanisms will ensure that people with disabilities are accessing services effectively. Regular data reviews will track their participation in project activities, while feedback mechanisms such as surveys and consultations will provide opportunities for individuals to express their concerns and suggest improvements. 7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The proposed project has a minimal direct environmental impact as its primary focus is on protection, mental health support, and community-based interventions. However, it acknowledges that even small-scale activities can have unintended consequences on the environment, particularly in resource-constrained settings like Cabo Delgado, where natural resources are already under significant strain due to displacement and conflict. The project incorporates measures to mitigate these risks and ensure environmental sustainability is integrated into its design and implementation. One potential impact is the increased demand for resources in host communities due to the influx of internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees. This can exacerbate pressure on water, land, and forest resources, potentially leading to overuse or degradation. While the project itself does not directly involve activities that contribute to resource depletion, its interventions, such as community-based protection initiatives, include sensitization on sustainable resource management. These efforts will promote awareness about the importance of conserving local ecosystems while addressing social cohesion and protection risks. The project’s operational activities, such as transportation for field staff and logistics for delivering services, may contribute to a minor increase in carbon emissions. To mitigate this, efficient transportation planning will be employed, prioritizing shared vehicles and coordinated travel routes to reduce the carbon footprint. Additionally, where possible, virtual meetings and training sessions will be conducted to minimize the need for travel, reducing environmental impact without compromising the quality of service delivery. In interventions involving community engagement, such as awareness campaigns or workshops, the use of printed materials could generate waste. The project will mitigate this by limiting the use of paper and opting for environmentally friendly materials, such as digital content, where feasible. For materials that must be printed, the project will ensure they are produced sustainably using recycled paper and environmentally friendly inks. Proper disposal and recycling protocols will also be integrated into activity planning to minimize waste generation. The project recognizes the importance of preserving the natural environment as part of broader community resilience. Activities that involve social cohesion and capacity-building will incorporate discussions on environmental stewardship. These discussions will emphasize the interconnectedness of environmental health and human security, fostering a holistic approach to resilience-building that encourages communities to adopt sustainable practices. Environmental assessments are not specifically planned for this project as its activities are unlikely to have significant environmental impacts. However, the project remains flexible and responsive to any identified risks during implementation. If any activities are found to have potential environmental consequences, appropriate mitigation measures will be developed and implemented promptly, in line with national regulations and international best practices.
