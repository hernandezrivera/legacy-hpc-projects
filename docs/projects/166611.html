Project Summary

The majority of the school population in Zimbabwe have no reliable connection to the internet. To reach these learners during the period of the COVID-19 emergency, we propose to identify locally adapted, technology based, solutions for alternative and home based education that do not require a high performing and permanent internet connection.
Community radio can be highly effective in reaching the most at risk and addressing people about their local concerns in their local languages. They can support distance learning and the continuation of education in hard to reach and rural communities through life tutoring.
Advantages of life- radio lessons via community radios are that they can be designed in accordance with the specific national curriculum and to the needs of the targeted community and reach remote populations with no connection to the internet. Such lessons can be designed in an interactive manner whereby students pose questions via phone text messages to be answered by a moderating teacher.
Well-designed video lessons, developed with teachers and universities can be broadcasted via national sub-national TV stations.
TV and radio lessons are critical in ensuring the continuation of education during a period of crises such as COVID-19 and similar crisis’s which lead to massive school closure and education interruption . In the period following the crises, these the radio programmers can be adjusted to serve as a complement, rather than an alternative, to classroom teaching.
UNESCO will support the development and broadcasting of lessons Interactive radio instruction (IRI); mobilize the network of select community radios and teachers and support MoPSE to facilitate the collaboration between radio broadcast stations and schools/ local teachers. Partners will include MoPSE, CDTS, teachers, national and community radio and TV stations
