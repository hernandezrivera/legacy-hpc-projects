Needs

During the critical post-conflict transition phase, IOM will continue to target its assistancein a responsive and timely manner to meet the most urgent humanitarian needs ofdisplaced and returnee households while simultaneously contributing to increasing theresilience of households and working for durable solutions in conflict-affectedcommunities. As of 11 November 2018, IOM's Displacement Tracking Matrix hasidentified 1.8 million IDPs across all 18 governorates of Iraq, with a large concentration inthe northern and central parts of the country; in the governorates of Ninewa, Salah al Din,Baghdad and the three Governorates of the Kurdistan Region of Iraq. In addition, morethan four million people have returned particularly to disputed territories in thegovernorates of Anbar, Ninewa, Salah al Din and Diyala. It is estimated that 51% of thesepopulations are women and overall, 51% are children. The large-scale return of over800,000 displaced persons in 2018 revealed the overstretched capacity of communitiesand the Government to meet basic needs and provide essential services. The scale,severity and complexity of needs for shelter and basic household items remainsignificant. Over 1.8 million individuals remain displaced of which 580,000 in camps andthe rest in informal settlements in urban and rural areas. According to IOM’s IntegratedLocation Assessment (ILA) Round 3, 37% of returnees and 66% of IDPs are in need ofbasic household items and NFIs. Moreover, as displacement continues, displacedhouseholds increasingly require replacement of their previously provided NFIs which havebeen damaged or stolen. Of the IDPs who do not intend to return to areas of origin withinthe next year, 47 per cent cite damage to property as the primary reason to remain indisplacement highlighting the urgent needs to support sustainable durable solutions fordisplaced households. Some 32 per cent of returnees have returned to their areas of originbut not to their homes, for reasons including damaged or destroyed housing and propertydisputes. Current assessments confirm widespread damage to shelter - some 138,000houses were damaged, half of which beyond repair, across 63 urban centres and 1,746villages in the seven most conflict-affected governorates. IDPs and returnees are in criticalneed of essential services such as shelter and NFIs. As of 1 October 2018, 12 per cent ofIDPs live in critical shelters (school buildings, religious buildings, unfinished or abandonedbuildings, and other informal settlements). Living in critical shelter especially impactswomen and girls that might be more exposed to GBV, as well as children and adolescentsthat are affected by lack of access to primary services especially education and health.Lack of adequate shelter might also affect the ability of young adults and adults toparticipate in social life and secure livelihood opportunities. Another 24 per cent of IDPsreside in formal camps or Emergency Sites. IOM’s ongoing assessments revealed thatmany IDPs have limited access to basic household items and shelter necessities due tolack of markets, cash and capacity. IDPs in critical shelter arrangements are extremelyvulnerable, living in inadequate shelter that does not sufficiently protect them from theharsh winter weather conditions in Iraq, which reach below 0°C during the coldest monthsand above 50°C during the height of summer. The Shelter Cluster has identified thefollowing priority areas of need: • IDPs: whole camp population (0.48 M people) • IDP Out of Camp: people who reported at least one shelter issue in the Multi-Cluster Needs Assessment (MCNA) (0.23 M people) • Returnees: people who reported at least one shelter issue in MCNA (1M people) • Host Community: World Bank poverty rate in interest areas (0.39 M people). The total need being 2.1M people. In order to ensure the most vulnerable populations are targeted and prioritized with the intervention, IOM will use data or conduct assessments utilizing the Cash Working Group socio-economic vulnerability tool, while retaining a focus on IDPs and returnees as per the HRP priorities.

Activities

In line with Strategic Objective 2: Increase Access to Basic Services, IOM intends to provide both shelter and NFI support for highly vulnerable IDPs returnees. Specifically, IOM will contribute to all three SNFI cluster objectives:1. Maintain adequate living conditions of vulnerable displaced people in camps through adequate shelter and non-food items support:To cover basic NFI needs of new arrivals and highly vulnerable IDPs, IOM will distribute basic NFI kits in targeted camps in Ninewa, Diyala, Kirkuk, Baghdad, Anbar, Salah-al Din, and possibly others depending on needs and coordination with the SNFI cluster, to an estimated 90,000 individuals. The estimated beneficiary number reflects number of individual kits, and some households may receive both a basic and a seasonal NFI kit.2. Assist highly vulnerable people out of camp transitioning to durable solutions through the provision of adequate shelter and non-food items in priority areas of displacement and return. For IDPs residing in critical shelters outside of camps IOM will provide emergency upgrade or repairs, focusing on safety and protection of IDPs, distribution of Sealing-Off Kits to allow those in critical shelters to withstand harsh seasonal conditions, and rental subsidies to enable IDPs in critical shelters to improve their living situations, for a total of 24,000 individuals. IOM will aim to refer those targeted for rental subsidies internally and externally to partners to ensure sustainability of the intervention. In addition, IOM will target vulnerable IDPs out of camp with basic and seasonal NFI assistance to cover their most immediate needs in terms of household items, targeting 121,500 individuals. For highly vulnerable returnees, IOM will provide both shelter and NFI support to ensure their basic needs are covered and to improve their protection. 3,000 of the most vulnerable returnee individuals will benefit from the distribution of SOKs, to protect them from the harsh climate in Iraq. For war damaged shelters IOM will differentiate its assistance depending on the category of war damage. For shelters for which the damage corresponds to a category two (CAT 2) IOM will undertake emergency repairs utilizing two separate methodologies: 4,200 individuals will be supported through direct shelter rehabilitation work provided by a contractor; in addition, 1,200 individuals will be supported through owner driven rehabilitation: beneﬁciaries will receive specialized training, construction support and materials to undertake rehabilitations on their own. Through a cash for work component, this will also benefit the wider community. For 1,200 of the most vulnerable IDP households in critical shelters or deteriorating shelter in camps, who cannot return due to the severe damage or destruction of their homes (CAT 3 and CAT 4), IOM will provide low cost interim solutions to enable them to leave their critical shelter situation for a more dignified solution until they are able to repair/reconstruct their houses. The most vulnerable returnees will also be targeted with basic NFI support to cover their most immediate household needs and improve their protection against the elements. A total of 87,300 highly vulnerable returnees will be targeted with NFI assistance. 3. Contribute to preparedness levels, contingency plans and capacities for large-scale emergencies through shelter interventions and NFI provisions. Pockets of new displacement continue to exist throughout Iraq due to either localized conﬂict, natural disaster, or increasingly, evictions from camps. In response, IOM will establish a Rapid Response Emergency Cash (RREC) tool to assist vulnerable households in situations of new displacement where they are unable to meet their basic needs as well as to support vulnerable households evicted from IDP camps. The RREC tool will support households to meet their critical and urgent basic needs, including shelter and NFI and contribute towards preparedness, ensuring rapid assistance to improve response timeliness and prevent further degradation of humanitarian needs. A total of 250 households will be targeted with this assistance. IOM will deliver a part of the Shelter/NFI assistance through cash-based programming, wherever appropriate and depending on the market assessment, to provide households with the ﬂexibility to prioritize their most urgent needs as well as to support the local economy. As the response has moved from an emergency to a post-emergency phase, a more targeted approach will continue to be applied for SNFI interventions and people will be served based on their identiﬁed critical need rather than on their status, by using the Cash Working Group socio-economic vulnerability tool and also considering that many families may only need replacement of certain items and not full kits.

Protection Risk Analysis

1. Extremes of weather during the summer and winter seasons, including health risks for children and elderly; 2. Selection of preferential families by the mukhtars; 3. Delays in Procurement to implementation; 4. Security during distributions and provision of assistance; 5. Difficulties to access affected communities;6. Continued GBV risk, especially for vulnerable individuals in camp or critical out-of-camp shelter arrangements;7. Housing, Land and Property issues preventing the sustainable return of IDPs, access to adequate shelter and causing conflict in the communities of return;

Mitigation Measures

1. Summer and Winter preparation, including awareness of health risks for children and elderly; 2. Establish repertoire with the members of neighborhood to verify mukhtars information and ensure Cluster standards for vulnerability assessment tool are carried out; 3. Plan early to include lengthy procurement periods and secure effective supply chain; 4. Compliance and close coordination with UNDSS; 5. Collaborations with the Joint Coordination and Monitoring Center (JCMC), National Operations Center (NOC);6. Incorporate strong protection mainstreaming in the response with a focus on GBV, strengthen the capacity to implement preventive approaches, carry out advocacy and sensitization in and out of camps, cooperate closely with protection partners to identify synergies between the S-NFI and protection response;7. Support the capacity of local actors to provide HLP services, mediate HLP issues to prevent conflict; refer HLP cases to partners for support and legal advice.

How is your project accountable to affected population

IOM carries out assessments involving community engagement, community-basedfeedback, Post distribution monitoring. NFIs procured and distributed will be inaccordance to the Cluster guidelines and tailored based on beneficiary needsassessments and post distribution monitoring. Wherever possible, IOM will align it's out of camp response with other humanitarian and recovery interventions to ensure anintegrated response. Within the NFI kits, IOM provides the toll free number to the IDPhotline for individuals to call with concerns, queries or complaints. Moreover, duringdistributions, complaints desks are erected for the target population to directly share their concerns. As an additional accountability measure, IOM carries out Post Distribution Monitoring to assess the relevance, effectiveness, efficiency and satisfaction with the assistance provided, which is then tailored based on the feedback received. Shelter interventions involve consultations with the beneficiaries and the owner of the property when applicable. Beneficiaries are informed about the procedure and the support that can be provided, while they are invited to share their ideas and take the lead on the repair of their shelter whenever possible. When contractors are employed, IOM supports the interaction between the beneficiary and the contractor to ensure that the specific needs of the household are met within the limits of the intervention. IOM negotiates agreements between the tenants and the owners to ensure that HLP rights are respected and that the support provided contributes to sustainable solutions. For all of IOM’s shelter interventions, HLP issues, in particular security of tenure, will be taken into account, such as ensuring title deed are in place for housing rehabilitation, rental agreements for those in rental arrangements, owner agreements for those in critical shelters to avoid evictions, and/or referring those to partners that need legal advice regarding their housing, land and property issues.For NFI distributions, IOM provides information for safe use of items and works with the CwC team to ensure information is shared in appropriate ways. IOM promotes the use of the IDP hotline and sets up accountability and feedback desks during distribution. All of IOM’s response will be conducted in close coordination with partners on the ground and with the SNFI cluster. In addition, IOM will involve beneficiaries wherever possible in the design of activities. IOM’s communication with communities team will serve as a conduit for two-way communication with beneficiaries, in addition to feedback and accountability desks, and the IDP hotline, which will be promoted by IOM. IOM accompanies NFI distributions with clear guidance to ensure the safe and proper usage of items and ensure no harm is done.
