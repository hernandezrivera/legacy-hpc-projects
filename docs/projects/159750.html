Project Summary

The proposed action will respond to the needs of IDPs who are drought affected; at risk of being evicted and in need of humanitarian assistance and protection; and have huge gaps in terms of access to food, water and sanitation, life-saving basic services such as health and nutrition, shelter and education. The targeted districts include Kismayo in Lower Juba, Bardera and Garhabarey in Gedo, Caadado and Dhuusamarreeb in Galgaduud region. The 60 IDP sites in these districts will be selected based on the needs observed at field level and in coordination with the CCCM and protection clusters to avoid any double targeting. SADO will prioritize sites whose IDPs have not received any or very little humanitarian support in addition to sites that have underdeveloped community structures, high incidents of GBV cases, poorly performing infrastructure and sites at risk of eviction. Currently most IDPs in the targeted districts are currently characterized by very high levels of vulnerabilities i.e. they are experiencing poor food consumption where levels of acute malnutrition have been reported in the past few months. The IDPs also lack basic access to essential WASH infrastructures such as access to clean water, latrines, and hygiene items resulting in poor hygiene habits and recurrence of water borne diseases. In addition, the IDPs sites have improper site planning and poor coordination thus creating environments more prone to SGBV and protection concerns.
In the targetd towns of Bardera, Garbaharey, Caadado and Dhuusamarreeb IDPs, the intervention is vital due to the absence of CCCM and lack of site-level coordination and management. There is also the likelihood of further displacement into these towns in the near future due to the recurring drought and eviction with a dire need for CCCM activities to further support such vulnerable individuals. Therefore, SADO will introduce CCCM interventions in these IDP affected towns by prioritizing sites using site priority assessment tool which measures the need for CCCM response based on community governance structures, site conditions, and active service providers. Then in all the districts including Kismayo where SADO has been implementing CCCM activities in partnership with ACTED, focus will be on supporting the strengthening of IDPs site coordination and monitoring, support the improvement of specific site infrastructures, improve accountability to affected populations by managing information and ensure proper collection and dissemination of information at site level and through communicating with communities (CwC) i.e. passing information and also receiving information in form of feedback and complaints.
In summary, the action aims to contribute to CCCM’s 1, 2 and 3 objectives by improving the living conditions of IDPs in the camps of Gedo, Lower Juba and Galgaduud by increasing access to services and infrastructures; capacity building the camp management committees and site management committees in order to improve the delivery of services and, information transmission and sharing with communities at the sites; enhancing the coordination by monitoring service provision and standards within sites using the safety audit tools developed by ACTED and recently used in Kismayo camps. To ensure acceptance, SADO will engage with the government and other stakeholders including the CCCM cluster to ensure no duplication and overlapping of interventions. SADO will utilize key principles of CCCM interventions by mainstreaming protection and gender and by ensuring proper Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) through support to Complain and Feedback Mechanisms (CFM) in sites.

Project Activities

Needs and Activities Urban towns in Gedo region have been receiving a large influx of IDPs who have flocked the towns with the hope of receiving humanitarian assistance. An estimated 3,740 IDPs have been reported to arrive in the IDPs camps in the districts who are mostly pastoral drop-outs. Over 5 new IDP camps have been established in Baardheere. These IDP camps in Gedo are underserved and the people have minimal assistance. In Galmuduug, IDPs face huge food insecurity, health concerns and protection related crisis particularly those in Dhusamareeb and Caadado as they are expecting possible eviction after the elections. Eviction problem is common throughout Galmudug because majority of IDPs inhabit temporary locations. It is also expected that the government will launch new offensives resulting in further displacement. The IDPs in the above districts live in poorly constructed shelters, exposing the women and children to diseases and protection issues including GBV. Kismayo town has four IDP sections with a total of 133 IDP sites and a population of 70,000 individuals. In June 2019, SADO/ACTED in coordination with JRIA, conducted a Safety Audit Assessment in 40 IDPs site in Galbedka, Kismayo town. The survey indicate that there are still gaps of service provision in all sectors including education, shelter, WASH, livelihood, protection and health. Activities: Activity 1: Site infrastructure improvements: SADO will facilitate rehabilitation and upgrading of priority basic infrastructures including roads, shelters and latrines among others to improve settlements at target camps. Site Management Committees(SMCs) will be revived /established as a device for residents to identify site maintenance needs and receive technical and financial support in carrying out upgrading work. This will be done by involving community members in small tasks through a CFW. The number of CFW labourers will be determined by SMC and they will have paid $ 7-10 based on MEB for the district. The target is 60 sites (40 in Kismayo, 5 each in the four new districts)Activity 2: Coordination and information management – through communicating with communities (CwC) i.e. passing information and also receiving information in form of complaint and mechanism, SADO will be able to map existing community infrastructures sites and use the data for advocacy. SADO will install information boards to 20 sites (5 each in 4 districts and if necessary, additional ones in Kismayo) to provide relevant updates from all service providers and this will improve protection and participation and information sharing.Activity 3: Capacity Building and Advocacy - Training on leadership and governance conflict resolution, GBV, CCCM will be provided to CMCs who will also be trained on the importance of inclusivity. The target is to have one CMC per site in the four districts totalling 20 CMCsActivity 4: Advocacy - In addition to the information dissemination, SADO will advocate for IDPs to all service providers and government authority when there is emergency, evictions and incidents that need high attentions and identify durable solutions for the displaced families and affected communities in coordination with CCCM cluster lead. Activity 5: Sustainability - SMCs will train individuals at identifying site risks and training members to conduct maintenance with tool kits that will be will procured and handed over tool kits to site maintenance committees in order to support proper maintenance of site infrastructures after the end of the project. It is envisaged that 20 tool kits will be procured and handed to 20 sites. SADO will also provide training to CMCs based on gaps and needs and the trainings will be tailored to the specific context potentially focusing on community participation and coordination and awareness on GBV risks and the services available to report and address such issue. Two trainings will be conducted in each region.

Project Indicators

Indicators Indicator 1a: Number of site maintenance committees elected based on vulnerability criteria- The indicator will measure viability of the selection criteria to ensure inclusiveness. In Kismayo the committees will be revived but in but in new areas like Bardera, Garbaharey, Caadado, Dhusammarreeb, they will be established one per site for the targeted 20 sites. The maximum number will be 20 committee comprising of 10 members each. Indicate 1 b: The percentage of women/girls involved in site management committee will also be measuredIndicator 2: Number of sites with operational camp management committees offering inclusive community governance. The information will be collected through TORs for each site, key informant interviews with committee members and residents about CMCs use and if the CMC fully represents the communityIndicator 3: # of sites infrastructures improved and number of people in the IDP settlements receiving support from settlement activities. The project targets at least 5 sites per district in Gedo, and Galgaduud and 40 sites in Kismayo. Indicator 4: % of residents aware of how to obtain protection related support from GBV partners - Data will be collected from key informant interview with residents about their knowledge of GBV and how who to contact in case of a GBV case. The intention is to ensure that in all site sites, the GBV referral pathway is being advocated for via awareness campaigns and CwC (Communicating with communities) work with close partnership with the GBV sub cluster as well as the information boards that will be installed in all the targeted 60 sites.

Mitigation Measures

Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement -To prevent risks of exclusion, protection principles are mainstreamed into all programs by incorporating protection concerns from assessment to implementation of the interventions. The Do No Harm approach creates harmony between beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries. This is achieved through awareness raising and joint targeting and selection process that targets the most vulnerable HHs to participate in site infrastructure. SADO maintains consistent and accurate messaging about selection criteria, resource availability, prioritization of assistance, and recognition of need at the community level to reduce misunderstandings and to maintain stability in the project implementation area. Protection measures will be implemented throughout the project ensuring the needs of men and women, the elderly, children, and the disabled are taken into account. These measures include, among others: • Sex and age disaggregated data as well as conditions of vulnerability; • Sensitizing the Code of Conduct (CoC) regarding Sexual Abuse and Exploitation (SEA) to project staff and RCs. • Beneficiaries will be sensitized to the nature of the assistance and an accountability system will be established for feedback and reporting; • The timing and location of beneficiary registration will be organized to accommodate women’s roles as caregivers and the needs of vulnerable groups. Furthermore, SADO has adopted Code of Conduct and therefore will ensure that all its staff have been sensitized on and have signed Code of Conduct, which is consistent with the six core principles of the IASC Task Force on PSEA including implications for violations. All new staff hired during the course of this project will be required to sign and undergo this training and will be required to sign and understand the Code of Conduct. Beneficiaries will be made aware of the CoC and will benefit from sexual exploitation and abuse prevention messaging, and will receive information on how to report violation. SADO will apply all key principles of CCCM interventions by mainstreaming protection and gender and by ensuring proper Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) through support to CFM in sites.A Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) will be set up during project initiation stage with beneficiaries being sensitized on beneficiary accountability standards and procedures. They will be provided with brochures, banners and pamphlets containing these standards and for those unable to read or in the case of children will receive visual illustrations from SADO field based staff on the accountability standards. A toll free telephone contacts will be provided through which they can call and register their complaints. A database will be developed by our IT staff in which all the complaints will be recorded and appropriate redress taken within the shortest time possible and recorded as well. All complaints will be responded to within a maximum period of 72 hours and the responses will take into consideration the specific needs of women, men, girls and boys. This field based CRM database will be analyzed on a monthly basis. During meetings with project beneficiaries, 2 desks will be set up, one manned by a male staff and another by a female staff to receive feedback from beneficiaries. Child friendly accountability standards will also be displayed in these meetings.

Please provide further explanation

SADO will only implement the projects in accessible areas in Urban / Peri urban areas, where IDPs have settled after being displaced. SADO is accepted in the project areas through on-going and recently completed projects in Gedo, Lower Juba and Galgaduud regions. SADO will conduct strong community mobilization at the outset of the project and will identify the camp site infrastructures for improvement through a consultative process with the target communities. By working closely with the targeted SMCs and CMCs, engaging local leadership and prioritizing the sensitization of communities to support project activities and participating in project design and implementation, this ensure sustainability of the project. Prior to the start of any project, SADO engages with the government and other stakeholders including the clusters to avoid duplication of efforts. Before improving site infrastructures, MOU is signed with CMCs to show that the community is willing to participate in the rehabilitation of the priority community infrastructure. This way the beneficiaries are aware of their entitlement prior to the commencement of the project. Furthermore, the established site maintenance committees act as a device for residents to identify site maintenance needs and receive technical and financial support in carrying out upgrading work. The goal of site maintenance committees will be to train individuals at identifying site risks and training members to conduct maintenance with tool kits that SADO will hand over. The SMCs will be revived and supported by SADO. SADO will also procure and hand over tool kits to site maintenance committees in order to support proper maintenance of site infrastructures after the end of the project. SADO will also provide training to CMCs based on gaps and needs and the training will be tailored to the specific context potentially focusing on community participation, durable solution and site closure or coordination. In addition to the information dissemination, SADO will advocate for IDPs to all service providers and government and identify durable solutions for the displaced families and affected communities in coordination with CCCM cluster lead.
