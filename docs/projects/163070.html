Project Summary

The project seeks to contribute towards diversifying livelihood opportunities and improving the food and nutrition security situation of at least 42,500 people (22,100F; 20,400M) vulnerable urban and rural households disaggregated as follows Buhera rural 15,000 people, Chipinge urban 12,500 people and Chipinge rural 17,500 people . Chipinge and Buhera districts have low food consumption scores of 24.6% and 31.9%, respectively. Buhera is ranked amongst the most food insecure districts, with an average of 55% of HHs food insecure, premised on access to and availability of cereals for consumption (ZimVac, 2018).
Mercy Corps Zimbabwe’s approach to food security is focused on addressing critical barriers facing male and female smallholder farmers, while building more inclusive market systems. In urban areas, Mercy Corps has worked to provide households with assistance to meet their basic needs while providing livelihood opportunities. Complementing our strong experience in addressing food security, Mercy Corps Zimbabwe is a leader in resilience research and thinking. We also bring strong technical experience in water, sanitation, and hygiene and the ability to quickly pivot and respond during emergencies. The combination of a market system approach to addressing food security, underpinned by a deep knowledge of the context and informed by resilience thinking is unique and allows Mercy Corps to ensure programming is meeting the needs of households today while building their ability to survive future shocks and stresses. Mercy Corps proposes to implement in the selected districts in order to leverage on the current work being implemented in Chipinge and Buhera districts funded by the EC. The EC Value project is working with smallholder goat producers promoting productivity and enhancing market access opportunities. This project will leverage on the current work by Mercy Corps on WASH and the EC Value which will phase out in 2023.
The project proposes to reduce food and nutrition insecurity through rehabilitation of productive assets, capacity building of extension staff and farmers, distribution of crop and livestock input packages through , distribution of small livestock to vulnerable households and increasing access to affordable feed for livestock through an electronic voucher system. The project proposes to use the electronic voucher system instead of cash transfers to support the distribution of crop and livestock feed inputs, medicines to ensure that farmers access the service in a more efficient and cost effective manner that support growth of the market system of the target areas. Mobile money transactions have been observed to disadvantage the beneficiaries through extra charges levied by service providers for this type of transaction.
Mercy Corps recognizes that women and girls face challenges in the society given their limited capacity to contribute better to household food security and income. The proposed project recognizes the roles that women, men, boys and girls play in families and society and activities have been tailored to accommodate these needs. Mercy Corps will build capacity of program staff as well as government and private sector partners in gender analysis tools, gender perception, and their influence on food security. Gender balance will be integrated in the following ways:
 A gender analysis to understand gender roles, power relations, decision-making patterns, and the division of and access to resources, and how all these factors impact food security;
 A responsive M&E system that tracks success, while identifying challenges and impediments to participation and progress;
 Promotion of “whole household” decision-making to empower women and increase investment in child health and nutrition; and
 Selection of commodities that strike a balance between crops that are produced and sold by men and those produced and sold by women, purposely targeting commodities that women are already producing.

1 - Activities

The project activities will include identification and registration of beneficiary farmers targeted to receive crop inputs, livestock as well as baseline, mid-term and end-line survey. Crop inputs, livestock feed, small livestock, drugs and vaccines for livestock will be accessed the electronic voucher system. The project will engage agro-dealers and private sector players in the target areas to supply the critical inputs for the farmer producer groups. The livestock kit for farmers that will benefit from the project will include vaccines for goats/sheep and cattle; dosing drugs, syringes and needles for vaccination programmes. The project seeks to implement a cash transfer component targeting 12,500 Chipinge Urban vulnerable population , through conducting needs, identification and registration of beneficiaries, regular market monitoring surveys for basic commodities. This activity will target to reach out to 2,500 households with interventions aimed at increasing their buying power. Key activities are outlined as follows: a) 79,306 Farmers receive crop inputs;1.1 Hold inception workshops at district level to sensitize beneficiaries and stakeholders on project objectives.1.2 Identify and select wards for project interventions according to criteria developed by stakeholders.1.3 Register beneficiaries to receive crop inputs and maintain a comprehensive beneficiary database. 1.4 Set up and manage an electronic voucher distribution system. 1.5 Train farmers on optimum utilization of crop inputs and on appropriate agronomic practices. The project will support communal farmers with establishment of nutrition gardens at high yielding water points. These will be supported with fencing materials, start-up seeds and farmers will also receive training on the growing of the selected vegetables from government extension workers. b) 30 Community gardens established2.1 Identify and select wards for the community gardens establishment 2.2 Identify and select sites for the community gardens2.3 Set up and manage grievance redress mechanism2.4 Select and register beneficiaries of the community gardens and maintain a comprehensive beneficiary database. The farmers that will be targeted to receive small livestock will also be targeted to receive feed for the received animals to supplement them. c) 6,860 Farmers (3,430 farmers per district) receive small livestock3.1 Mobilize and select beneficiaries in line with vulnerability criteria as developed by stakeholders3.2 Identify, select and register beneficiaries to receive livestock.3.3 Maintain a comprehensive beneficiary database3.4 Train farmers on good animal husbandry practices focusing on poultry and small ruminant production practices d) 6,860 Farmers receive supplementary stock feed4.2 Register beneficiaries to receive stock feed4.3 Maintain a comprehensive beneficiary database4.4 Distribute stock feed vouchers to selected farmers for the project

2 - Indicators

• Number of households benefiting from Cash Based Transfers in urban and peri-urban areas.• Number of households receiving season-sensitive emergency crop and livestock input assistance.• Number of critical community assets for crop and livestock production rehabilitated and built.• Number of vulnerable smallholder farmers receiving extension and advisory services to improve yields, manage crop pests and livestock diseases.Building on experience from previous projects, Mercy Corps will employ a robust monitoring and reporting approach for the project. At the onset of the project, a standardized and rigorous data collection and analysis procedure will be developed to ensure credible results and minimize any potential for bias. The success of the project will be measured through the baseline and endline project surveys, and post distribution and monitoring surveys. Additionally, the team will conduct knowledge, attitude, behaviours and perceptions (KABP) studies to identify the barriers, opportunities and monitor intended attitudinal and behavioural changes to understand if the logic for intervention results chains is accurate or needs to be adapted. The team will also encourage a culture of learning throughout the team by conducting regular data and process reviews to identify opportunities for increased impact and efficiency.
