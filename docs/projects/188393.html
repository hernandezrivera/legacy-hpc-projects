Project Summary

The project aims to assist /8000/ of the affected people including (1970: men, 2210: women, 1845: boys, and 1975: girls) in NW Syria as follows:
1. Provision /396000/ liters of heating fuel (diesel) for /1100/ households (5500) individuals including 1320men, 1485 women, 1320 boys, and 1375 girls), where each household will get 360 liters of diesel during the winter season.
2. Rehabilitation /500/ shelters for /500/ households (2500 individuals include 650 men, 725 women, 525 boys, and 600 girls) depending on (HLP) and Shelter guidelines.
This project contributes to life-sustaining assistance to the most vulnerable groups in Northwest Syria; Aleppo governorate; sub-districts of Afrin, Sharan, and Mabatli, through the following two interventions that designed based on the attached Need Assessment (Attachment.1):
Firstly, provision of house repairing for /500/ of unfinished buildings for the IDPs communities. HLP due diligence will be included in implementation in order to achieve as much legal certainty about tenure as possible to ensure the security of tenure by verifying the ownership of the land or shelter and to prevent the exploitation and eviction of the IDPs. Contractor- modality will be adopted for preparing houses, this modality supports an accountable, timely, and efficient use of resources while protecting the rights of the beneficiaries.
HLP due diligence will be included in implementation in order to achieve as much legal certainty about tenure as possible to ensure the security of tenure by verifying the ownership of the land or shelter and to prevent the exploitation and eviction of the IDPs.
Secondly, provision winterization assistance (diesel stoves with accessories, diesel fuel) for the most vulnerable people(5500) for the IDPs who live in the camps, or (outside camps) in the makeshift shelter or in damaged/unfinished buildings, and they do not receive winter assistance in the past. All the beneficiaries will be selected based on the vulnerability criteria, so the blanket distributions will be avoided. The in-kind- modality will be adopted for distributions of the fuel and stoves. Diesel fuel was chosen for heating because it is the most common and available in the targeted areas, it is most efficient and relatively safe, so vulnerable HHs can install the stoves and use them easily, also it will be useful in cooking food in addition to heating. SRD will purchase them from local markets for revitalization, and will be responsible for quality control.
SRD will utilize a detailed Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability & Learning (MEAL) framework to carry out this project, MEAL Processes will include a combination of quantitative and qualitative data analysis. MEAL activities will involve interviews with beneficiaries to understand their views on SNFI's services quality. In addition, the M&E plan would develop outlined data collection methods (interviews, Focus Groups Discussion, observation, data sources, timeframe, responsibilities, and dissemination procedure).
During the life of the project, SRD outreach workers will ensure that all vulnerable groups are involved in project activities, and will accompany the shelter teams during the door to door verification to detect vulnerability cases in the beneficiaries' houses and refer them to nearby SRD community centers to respond (Attachment.2). In addition, they will ensure that technical assessments conducted by shelter teams meet the demand of persons with disabilities and the elderly. Also, they will deliver awareness messages for beneficiaries who receive the winterization assistance to protect from COVID-19 and ensure that the distribution process is in line with protection principles.

4 - Needs

According to the (HNAP, IDP_shelter_situation report, September 2021): in NWS, approximately 1 in 10 IDP households shares their shelter with at least one other household.79% of IDP HHs report having faced issues in their current shelter. Based on HNO 2022, the Shelter needs in Syria are widespread and acute. A significant portion of the population continues to experience protracted displacement. in North-west Syria, resorting to living in unsustainable settlements with poor access to basic services. in the Afrin sub-district, 12186 reported no shelter, and the shelter severity is 4 (Extreme), 45% of the IDPs are living in inadequate shelters, and 15% are living together with other IDPs. the occupancy arrangements of the shelter are that more than 50% of the IDPs are hosted in an un-own shelter. the top adequacy issues are lack of electricity (100%), lack of insulation from cold/heat100%), lack of water (100%), and the lack of privacy (67%).Based on HNO 2022, Improvements in NFI access were short-lived as nearly one million people were displaced in North-west Syria, accompanied by the impact from COVID-19 and the deteriorating economic conditions. Access to NFIs is seen as lifesaving, especially during winter when vulnerable families are unable to access basic items to keep them warm, such as heating fuel, blankets, and winter clothes. in Mabatli, and Sharan sub-districts, the NFI severity is 4 (Extreme) and the main NFI needs are the winter heaters and fuel.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

The tension between host communities, and IDPs. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigating Measures:Ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs and not based on status.Selection criteria are transparent, and groups are fully informed.Distribution partners engage with the communities to sensitize the population.Make an effort to target a % of the vulnerable host communities in every intervention that aims to support the IDPs.Establish feedback mechanism and conduct follow-up visits if needs be.Involve the local stakeholders throughout the process 2. Abuse of Authority by distribution actors (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion) Likelihood: High. Impact: High Mitigating Measure: SRD ensure that their staff has read and signed their Code of Conduct. SRD developed a range of measures and internal procedures to identify staff who are perpetrators of abuse and exploitation and act accordingly. SRD has a complaint mechanism in place. Lack of HLP documentation, Likelihood: Medium. Impact: HighMitigation:Work together with the local figures and communities to establish a mechanism to deal with such issues at the local level.Work to disseminate the content and objective of HLP information among local council, etc., and the linking between HLP rights and shelter assistance to save the rights of owners and the residents.Community verification is necessary even if the beneficiary and/or respective land or property owner is in possession of HLP documentation.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

All contractors and SRD staff will sign on the PSEA and Safe Guarding Code of Conduct with their contracts. The awareness session will be conducted by the outreach team to understand all issues related to protection from sexual exploitation and sexual abuse, GBV prevention, and mitigation guidelines, humanitarian principles, and they do not harm principle. All proposed activities will be delivered in adherence to the principles of humanitarian neutrality and ensure equal access to all beneficiaries in need without discrimination. Practical measures have been implemented, such as training staff on protection core principles and appointing focal points for at-risk women and children in addition to signing a code of conduct (CoC) and training on PSEA. SRD enhanced these procedures and created awareness training for all staff regarding CoCs, PSEA, and Child Protection. SRD improves overall PSEA & CS, ensures that all of HQs and field staff have fully understood PSEA and child guarding policy and signed Code of Conduct. SRD will continue to enhance PSEA reporting channels and respond to the complaints related to the SEA in a structured manner


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Provision heating fuel-diesel: Risks and Assumptions: 1.1 Diesel fuel available in local markets is of poor quality, and its combustion, when used in heating, causes the emission of toxic and harmful gases. Mitigating Measures: • Contracting with reputable, well-known suppliers in local markets. • Our field team will conduct periodic and surprise tests prior to purchase. 1.2 The indirect purchase of conflict-associated resources such as diesel fuel might contribute to conflict. Mitigating Measures: • Conducting (Anti-Terrorism) check for suppliers before contracting them. • Monitor the lines of supply of the fuel track the source down and ensure fuel is not procured from parties involved in the conflict SRD staff should understand the risks of COVID-19 introduction and propagation in the site, be trained and monitored on self-protection measures. Avoid direct physical contact and shaking hands with others. • SRD outreach team will deliver awareness messages about protection from COVID-19.
