Project Summary

"Caritas Bangladesh (CB) shelter strategy for 2020 will focus more on improving the current living conditions within the camps and enhancing social cohesion. Our proposed project for 2020 will target Camps 4, 4 Extension, 5, 19, 20 and 20 Extension. We aim to pilot the Catchment Focal Point responsible for Camps 19, 20 and 20 Extension, with an aim to improve field-level coordination with other agencies, sectors and the authorities.
We will improve current living conditions by constructing MTS for 600 households, providing TSA I for 500 households, TSA II for 3,000 households and repairing existing MTS shelters for 1,497 HHs and providing 2,500 households other shelter support. Within the host community, we plan to re-construct or repair shelters for 200 households. The purpose of providing different shelter solutions is to tackle the varied needs arising from material deterioration, topography, settlement density and individual household needs in the existing self-settled areas, the re-developed areas and the newly developed areas. We will also provide some NFIs to 4,216 households using a voucher system.
We believe that 2020 interventions should also contribute towards improving social cohesion and enhancing household and community resilience. We aim to achieve this by implementing our shelter activities with a community-led approach, targeting 3,000 households, whilst providing training to 10,256 individuals on safer cooking practices, shelter upgrading and maintenance, basic building techniques for strengthening their shelter structure, etc. As part of our plan for enhancing social cohesion, we aim to set up community committees in the locations where we will be working and connecting these trained communities together, so that their capacity as a wider community to prepare for future disasters and their ability to support one another can be strengthened over time. We will also explore alternative building materials and identify other appropriate construction techniques which can be considered for future shelter interventions. "

Needs

"The majority of the Rohingya refugees still live in inadequate bamboo shelters and makeshift settlements that are below humanitarian standards, overcrowded, and will not withstand adverse weather conditions. As the response enters its third year, the need to introduce more durable materials should be a priority. The previously constructed shelters require regular maintenance and replacement of materials, and only a small percentage of materials can be partially reused or repurposed. Direct and regular exposure to weather and beetle infestation are two of the common causes of bamboo deterioration. Landslides caused by the 2019 monsoons also highlighted a continuous need for household-level site improvement work not currently covered by the SMSD Sector. Most settlements are located in hilly terrain with exposure to risks such as landslide, flooding, soil erosion and high winds. Constructing shelters with more durable materials would reduce some of these environmental risks. In addition to structural integrity and material quality, the JRP 2020 Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) for refugees also identified the lack of privacy and ventilation, and the need for additional NFIs as other key concerns for households. Most of the shelters have no internal partitions and given the common practice is to cook inside using firewood cooking stoves, this leads to severe health-related issues caused by daily exposure to smoke. According to the MSNA findings, these issues affect women, adolescent girls and other vulnerable groups disproportionately as they tend to spend more time inside their shelters. The close proximity between shelters also poses fire risk and living in such congested conditions has also contributed to increasing tension amongst the Rohingya refugees. Households typically possess a number of household items from previous rounds of assistance. Therefore, the current need is more for additional items to complement or replenish what beneficiaries have, rather than to provide everything. "

Activities or outputs

"All data collected in 2020 by CB will be Sex and Age Disaggregated. 1. Catchment Focal Points - CB proposes to be the Catchment Focal Point for Camp 19, Camp 20 and Camp 20 Extension since we are already the Shelter Focal Point in Camp 19 and have been active in TSA and MTS shelter construction, maintenance support, site improvement activities and other protection projects in the aforementioned camps. 2. Provision, prepositioning and pipelines for emergency shelter support for weather related incidents and fire (not cyclone) – CB proposes to target this intervention at Camp 19 which will cover an estimated total 1,000HHs. 3. Mid-term shelter - CB proposes to construct mid-term shelters for 600 households across Camps 4, 4 ext., Camp 19, Camp 20 or Camp 20 ext., where land is available and meets the necessary spatial requirements. 4. Maintenance and repairs for existing MTS – CB plans to replace any damaged or deteriorated materials in the MTS constructed in 2018 and 2019 in Camps 4, 4 ext., 19, 20 and 20 ext. which will cover an estimated total of 1,497 HHs.5. TSA I (Transitional Shelter Assistance 1st phase): CB is targeting TSA I in Camp 19 which will cover total 500 HHs. 6. TSA II (Transitional Shelter Assistance 2nd phase) – CB aims to reach a total of 3,000 HHs in Camps 19 and 20 who had received TSA I in 2019. 7. Other Shelter Support – CB proposes to provide shelter support to 2,500 HHs in Camps 4 and 5 to ensure their shelters are upgraded to meet all Minimum Performance Standards.8. Provision of NFIs through comprehensive voucher system - CB will provide partial NFIs in Camps 19, 20 and 20 ext. to 4,216 HHs. 9. Shelter re-construction/repair for the Host Community – CB aims to repair or reconstruct shelters for a total of 200 HHs. 10. Trainings – CB will provide training and orientation in Camps 4, 4 ext., 5, 19, 20 and 20 ext. on safe cooking practices, shelter upgrade and maintenance, small scale site improvements, basic building techniques, etc. to community leaders, members, workers and volunteers (including women and youth). This will include training on how to provide technical support to People with Special Needs. Our proposed target is a total of 10,256 individuals. 11. Community-led approaches: CB is proposing to implement shelter activities using a community-led approach in Camps 4, 4 ext., 5, 19, 20, 20ext., targeting a total of 1,800 HHs (in approx. 6 Blocks). 12. Alternative materials and techniques – CB will look to piloting and researching other viable alternative materials and techniques for the main structure, superstructure and roofing. Total target 80 No. shelters."
