Project Summary

Help a Child proposes an intensive child protection project whose main purpose is prevent, mitigate and respond to the protection risks among the 7,000 vulnerable households in Aweil north through enhanced preparedness and resilience. Help a Child has been implementing a comprehensive child protection program in different locations in Aweil north under the South Sudan Joint Response (SSJR4) program since January 2018 and this proposed project is aimed at reaching out to other locations not currently targeted with child protection interventions from Help a Child and other partners on the ground. Help a Child will make use of its context-based experience, connections and structures in Aweil north to effectively start up the proposed interventions.
Help a Child and Dorcas Aid International conducted a joint rapid needs assessment in the selected locations in Aweil North to understand the current priority needs which have informed the development of this project. Nevertheless, Help a Child understands the dynamic nature of humanitarian programming and periodic needs assessments will be conducted to ensure that the project is well adapted to the emerging needs of the program locations. Among the key activities planned for implementation under the project include; child protection awareness through campaigns, banners, radios, conducting support sessions and the provision of case management and referral to services for the victims of child abuse and gender based violence in Aweil North. Help a Child will also facilitate the formation and training of the community based child protection networks, (CBPN) who will be able to monitor and make appropriate follow-ups on all the child protection issues in the community. The trainings on child protection will also be extended to the relevant local government officers to ensure their appropriate support to the referral pathway and to enable them respond to child protection issues in the different communities that they are working.
Household heads under the project will be targeted with appropriate parenting skills so that they are able to take their parental roles in ensuring the safety and welfare of their children. Girls and boys will be imparted with life skills aimed at building their self-esteem and confidence to become productive citizens. The project will also set up school based child protection help desks, which will be able to receive reports of any child abuse cases in schools and make appropriate follow up with the school administration and the parents teachers association. A complaints handling and community feedback mechanism will be developed in order to get community feedback on the community concerns coming from the program implementation. The project will work hand in hand with other cluster partners including the relevant local authorities in the commemoration and celebration of key child protection and gender days like the day of an African child, the 16 days of activism campaign against gender based violence and the campaign against child marriage. Participation in these campaigns will help in raising awareness, strengthening collaboration and consolidate the child protection messages to the targeted beneficiaries. The project will actively participate in the child protection sub cluster meetings, forums and all other relevant cluster technical working groups including psy-chosocial support and gender based violence technical working group.
Help a Child’s comprehensive monitoring and evaluation system will be used to track the performance of the project and to ensure effective reporting of the program outputs to different stakeholders. Data collection tools, which are well aligned to the child protection sub-cluster objectives and indicators, are already developed and are being used in the current project. These tools will be adapted to the specific needs of the proposed project.

Needs

Many children in South Sudan continue to be at risk of many violations, stemming from the impact of the armed conflict that ravaged the country over the years. The civil war has destroyed the social fabric of communities and many children have been left without the protection of caring adults. This is exposing them to high levels of violence, including gender based violence, exploitation, abuse and deprivation. Thousands of children in South Sudan do not have parents, and some of them have been targeted for recruitment by armed forces and groups. There are also increasing reports of children without appropriate family care working and living on the streets in major cities and towns; many of these children are at risk of getting into contact with the law.Help a Child conducted a needs assessment in Aweil north locations of Mangok, Jorbioc and Gualang where the interviewed community members indicated that they are usually affected by inter-communal caused by boarder lines, revenge killings, cattle raiding which affected about fifteen (15) villages/ settlements and an estimated 5850 people were displaced. However, these villages are accessible now and communities can move freely. The community also sited poverty, poor attitude to work, cattle raiding as factors influencing the conflict. Aweil north is probably one of the most isolated parts of South Sudan's Bahr el Ghazal region with many communities being unable to access basic social services due to limitation of government and stakeholders to provide the same. The challenging geographical profile with a lot of perennial rivers and drifts makes movement very challenging. No wonder there are very few organizations and government actors working there. Major child protection needs identified in Aweil north include; 1. Family separation and unaccompanied children:• Many children have been separated from parents and guardians due to frequent inter-communal conflicts and violence. The initial assessment by Help a Child established that more than 200 children in Aweil have been separated from their families.2. Lack of proper spaces and expertise for supporting child abuse victims.• There are no places in Aweil North for children to get the psychosocial support to recover from their traumatic experiences as well as interact with each other.3. Child Labor/ Exploitation• Children affected by the conflict are at a greater risk of being exploited and involved in child labour. Help a Child previous assessments revealed that exploitation is rampant in Aweil north with many un-accompanied children being exposed to child labour especially in market areas. 4. Psychosocial distress for young boys and girls who have been exposed to horrible scenes of violence.• Many children have been psychologically affected by the traumatic experience they have witnessed through the conflict and there is an urgent need to provide psychosocial support to such children so that they can be able to recover from those experiences. 5. Sexual Abuse and early marriage• Many girls are at a greater risk of sexual abuse and early marriage due to separation with parents and abductions that is caused by the inter- communal conflict. In addition to this, there is a local cultural practice in Aweil north that consider girls as source of wealth and as such they are forced in to marriage, practices of arranged marriages where the girls are given to wealthier families and hence both putting their lives at risk and violation of rights.

Activities

S.O.1: Provide lifesaving assistance to address priority protection needs for the most vulnerable populations in hard-to-reach, and priority areas.• Conduct a comprehensive needs assessment in selected locations in Gualang, Mangok and Jorbioc to prioritize needs.• Training of staff and community volunteers in child protection referral system and case management.• Set up and roll out a referral system for supporting child abuse and gender based violence victims.• Conduct psychosocial support activities to the vulnerable groups in order to help victims recover from their past bad experiences.• Setting of five school based child protection desks in ten schools in Aweil.• Conduct Identification and re-unification of UASC with parents and relatives.• Establish and train adolescent groups/clubs in social and life skills.S.O.2: Prevent, mitigate and respond to protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience• Conduct community awareness on child protection through community campaigns, radio campaigns.• Identify and train community based child protection facilitators.• Establishment of three child friendly spaces at Mangok, Jorbioc and Gualang locations in Aweil. • Conduct life skills and resilience training for the youths in Aweil.• Provide parenting skills session to caregivers and community members based on existing tools.• Training of staff and volunteers on child protection in prevention and response and on code of conduct in handling cases of children, particularly girls who may be at risk of exploitation and abuse.• Setting up of a community based complaints handling and feedback mechanism.• Training of community child protection committees, teachers and local leaders in child protection.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

The project will provide the beneficiaries with information through the project management committees which will be composed of trusted community members in all the project locations who will be involved at all stages of the project. The project management committees will be oriented on all the project activities and the targeted beneficiaries. The project management shall then orient the community members on the same. The program will also have opportunities for direct community engagement and dialogue between the project field staff and the community members in order to ensure frequent and consistent flow of the project information which is very vital in building building trust among the project stakeholders.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The messages are context-specific and tailored to different vulnerable groups. There are different messages for children, adolescent girls, adolescent boys, women and men. Our psycho-social support sessions are provided to specific age groups and the contents are tailored to the specific ages starting from children who are taken through the what's up child, ladies who are taken through the what's up ladies, local leaders are taken through the what's up leaders sessions and the community members at large are taken through the what's up people sessions. All these sessions are tailored to the specific audiences and delivered according to the gender and age of the beneficiary targeted groups.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project will take into consideration issues of protection from sexual exploitation and abuse seriously. The project will facilitate a training of all staff and the community volunteers on PSEA. All the project staff will sign a code of conduct where any sexual relations with the beneficiaries is strictly prohibited. In addition this, the community feedback mechanism will also provide a platform where community members will be able to report on the potential sexual exploitation cases to the project management team for immediate action. The project general protection awareness sessions will also include all the critical PSEA information which will be shared to the beneficiary communities in order for them to be aware of their rights as beneficiaries under the project.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

The consultations were very broad and comprehensive enough. It involved local leaders, government workers, other NGO staff working in the area and community members including the most vulnerable groups like the disabled and the aged. Field officers conducted key informant interviews to the local leaders and other community members who were selected on random while focus group discussions and group interviews were conducted among the beneficiary community members to get an in-depth view of the status of child protection in the locations.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

The project has a feedback and complaints handling mechanism which will be introduced to help communities share their feedback and complaints to the top management on how the program is being implemented. The project will engage communities in specific locations their preferred feedback method like the suggestion boxes and or toll free/ paying phone calls depending on the local context. The chosen feedback mechanism will be professionally managed in order not to compromise on the confidentiality, identity and privacy of the complainants while at the same time time protecting staff and community volunteers of malicious allegations from other disgruntled community members who may wish to settle their private issues through the system.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The program include community engagement in its monitoring and evaluation through the project management committees who will be involved in collecting data and assessing the performance of the program. The project management committees will also be involved in the planning of all the program activities in line with the approved activities. In addition to the project management team members, the project child protection facilitators will also be involved in data collection and consolidation.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project is based on a needs assessment that was conducted in October, 2018 where the specific needs of the different vulnerable groups were identified . The what’s up approach brings together the different peer groups to discuss some protection cross cutting issues and together find a solution, come up with action plans. This approach is more safe given, interventions are built on the existing community protection mechanisms which is much safer and enhancing community resiliency. The project shall ensure that the community facilitators trained to support the protection program comes from the respective communities or have a better understanding of the specific communities and protection issues at hand as well as the culture dynamics. In order to avoid causing more harm than good, community members are involved during the program design and implementation processes to ensure the traditional community protection mechanisms in place are in cooperated as well as a continuous assessments to capture the changing trends for possible change of strategies. Safety audits shall be ensured throughout the implementation processes to mitigate the occurrence of harm.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project identifies and addresses barriers that various groups particularly children, women and girls could face. The formation of women's and youth groups was meant to specifically address the needs of women and youth separately. An activity planning session will be conducted by the project staff and the field facilities where specific barrier to access for different groups shall be identified and some mitigation measures would be proposed in order to address those barriers. Both males and females including people with disabilities will equally participate in all trainings and group activities to ensure access to information and equal opportunities to all. Appropriate measures will be taken in to consideration to enhance the participation of children with special needs.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

In addition to this, the general awareness sessions will help reduce the inequalities that exist in relation to protection. The project will undertake deliberate efforts to include the marginalized groups in the community like the disabled, women and children in all the project activities.
