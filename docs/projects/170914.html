Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims to contribute to reduction of WASH vulnerability in marginalized communities while simultaneously responding to COVID 19 financial impact and PAs reduced capacity to provide sustainable and scalable efficient solutions to prolonged challenges in the chain of water provision disproportionately impacting vulnerable HHs in the West Bank.
The intervention components respond to the 2021 HRP SO2 and SO3, in addition to the WASH cluster O2, and O3 through its integrated approach to strengthen WASH preparedness and response capacities designed to address root causes contributing to HH WASH accessibility vulnerability. The intervention will further contribute to enhance target communities capacities to deal with WASH related shocks during emergencies and beyond.
The intervention will achieve the aforementioned through two main components that respond directly to the identified 2021 WASH priorities: i) Improving Access to water ii) contributing to enhance governance of WASH sector. Through:
i.a) Support the operations and maintenance material and tools for 14 water service providers servicing the entire populations of 14 of the most WASH vulnerable communities in the Governorates of Bethlehem and Hebron ( inclusive of Areas C, A&B) for efficient water provision. A total of 310391 persons, of which 218183 are WASH vulnerable, will directly benefit from this component across age groups and gender including PWDs.
ii.a) Piloting new techniques to improve access to WASH Services: ii.a.1) assistance in developing zoning systems for detection of losses and efficient network management in the Yatta and Tuqu. ii.a.2) provide the targeted 14 water service providers with tech and software solutions for improvement of distribution, management, billing and customer service based on individual needs assessment of capacity.
ii.b) Conduct Technical capacity building on provided assets, and systems through 6 centralized topic oriented trainings on ( Network manager, customer service, cost recovery, inspection, tariff calculation, and billing and collection systems) for 94 staff of water service providers and regulator(WSRC) of which at least 5% are PWDs.
ii.c) Conduct technical WASH assessment to analyze Supply-Demand Hydraulic on water sources (Fresh and Treated) vs consumption needs at a national level thus informing design of a national plan for carrier pipes network that would improve water distribution and address the imbalances between water supply and demand.
MA’AN has designed and proposed the above intervention to serve an aid gap in WASH sector at the community level and further to complement the current and foreseen work of partners and other WASH aid actors in their interventions at the HH level. The intervention was designed based on consultations with relevant WASH Sector stakeholders, decision makers and Water service providers at the community level. Due to the nature of proposed program, an equal impact/ benefit on beneficiaries ( including those of marginalized groups) is foreseen. MA’AN will ensure, where relevant, to consult with relevant demographics in the communities the proposed response targets.

1 - Needs

The fragmented Palestinian territorial Sovereignty has many ramifications including inability to enforce the rule of law, undertake physical and socio-economic development planning, construct residential and public service facilities, or provide adequate level of public services and infrastructure including WASH. The pervasive poverty and unemployment is compounded and caused by occupation regimes restrictions further hinders the PAs ability to invest in WASH infrastructures. One of the main factors contributing WASH accessibility vulnerability at the national level ( Areas A,B &C) is the existence of inadequate and deteriorated water infrastructures. This is coupled by the lack of resources and capacities at the national and water service providers level to provide efficient solutions to management of water distribution across communities. PWA estimated that water losses in the West Bank due to substandard infrastructure reaches more than 40%. Further, the lack of updated technologies and software's reduce the capacity of the national water service providers to manage water provision and collections that would contribute to better financial capacity to address infrastructural gaps. The 2021 WASH HNO stated that the growing PA budget deficit and COVID-19 impact on the deterioration of the Palestinian economy further exacerbated the undermining ability of services providers to collect fees from consumers and, as a result, their capacity to maintain the level of services. According to the Palestinian Water Authority (PWA), more than 60 per cent of WASH providers in the oPt could not collect their services fees, thus losing the main source of their revenues. The aforementioned thus places communities in a non ending cycle of vulnerability that has been reliant its coping on external non sustainable assistance. In addition to the joint assessments with WASH partner for other HH and institutions level WAHS responses, and the WASH clusters elaborated 2020 WASH Vulnerability index, MA’AN has conducted a rapid assessment during the months of Sep and Aug an assessment with local water service providers, Governorates offices, local councils, municipalities and the PWA. The assessment revealed that southern governorates, Particularly Bethlehem and Hebron, are greatly impacted by water loss rate due to physical (Deterioration of infrastructure causing water leakages, water theft..etc) and apparent losses (inefficiency of meters, network management systems by service providers, leak detection, reduced capacity for collection of tariffs and billing processes). Such conditions are also prevalent across the entirety of WASH vulnerable communities in the West bank. With a majority of water in the WB being purchased from Israel, the aforementioned has lead to an increase in the standard cubic meter water tariff, as service providers struggle to balance between paying for purchased water supply from limited collections below the supplied average of 60% reaching households. Further, water gauging meters and lack of pressurized networks have also been found to add from (6-11%) increased readings at the household levels. Coupled with the water tariff by the service provider, this translated into HHs paying a noticeably higher price per cubic meter than that charged to the provider, while also not being supplied with the planned water supply allocation per service providers distribution plans. As a result, some network connected communities still suffer from lack of accessibility to water especially during summer where water consumptions increases. The reduced access to water at the HHs level as revealed by the several assessments and the WASH HNO lead to negative coping mechanisms affecting sanitation and hygiene capacities and reduced water consumption than the needed threshold as recommended by WHO, and especially for groups that are disproportionately impacted by access impurities such as women, children and PWDs. Further MA’AN and partners including (GVC, UNICEF, AAH) have conducted assessments in recent months resulting in joint responses to address critical needed responses to address the current gap in assistance to WASH vulnerable communities and HHs. The proposed response here, aims to build on the collective effort and fill gaps in anticipated assistance for the sector through addressing vulnerabilities related to access of water sources and governance of the sector at the national level, and contributing to shifting a recovery responses to sustainability. Thus outputs of the proposed response will further contribute to increasing the efficiency and sustainability of partner proposed interventions at the communities and HH levels. Due to the level of engagement on the national and community for the proposed response, direct interactions with women, children, PWDs for response implementation and feedback is limited. However, the response to maximize impact of other MA’AN and partners responses at the HH level. Community members feedback will be collected and utilized during implementation when tailoring service providers systems and mechanisms to ensure suitability for end beneficiary of water supply chain ( HHs and Community members).

2 - Activities

MA’AN will lead coordination meetings w/ WASH stakeholders ( decision makers) including the PWA, Water Sector Regulatory Council (WSRC), WBWD and MoLG to map out implementation plan in target locations and explore special regulatory considerations for program implementation in the 14 target localities in Bethlehem and Hebron. Further, Community Based Project Support Committees will be formed in in each location , a known MA’AN approach when intervening in communities for increased community ownership and accountability, CBPSC’s will inform the needs of the communities and the different marginalized groups. The CBPSCs will serve an integral role in increase accountability, information dissemination and transparency and report protection concerns throughout the response and will be comprised of representatives from ( local council/municipality, schools, clinics, and community members including Women and PWDs)1. Improving access to safely managed water ( Water Source)1.1 The provision of water facilities critical operation and maintenance materials and Tools:1.1.1 14 Individual technical assessment of water provision services in each community: The assessment criteria will be placed in coordination with consulted stakeholders at the central level. Each assessment will be comprised of two components: the first component will assess the needs of the water service providers in each community (technical capacities of staff, efficiency of existing tools and systems, detected infrastructure malfunctions and current water losses across the network, network management software(billing, collections, customer service,GIS)) The second component will be conducted primarily with the CBPSC and other community members to verify community members perception on the service providers performance in terms of ( water availability, maintenance response time, billing accuracy, collection and debt handling..etc). The assessment will be conducted by a formed committee lead by MA’AN WASH engineer and technical teams from PWA, WSRC, and MoLG. The dual component assessment per target locality will serve the creation of comprehensive tailored intervention plan per water service provider assistance. 1.1.2 Provision of equipment and tools for 14 water service providers and WBWD needed to improve services offered, reduce physical losses, and increase efficiency. This could include but is not limited to (Automatic/motorized valves, leak detection systems and tools, pressure release valves, pressure transducers), The particular inputs of this component will be informed by the conducted technical assessment. MA’AN in partnership with the WSP will create the specific BoQs for equipment and materials to be provided. The installation of inputs will be conducted by contracted suppliers teams in partnership with the WSP technical staff where relevant. For WBWD and other WSPs, will provide access to these equipment and tools package on a loan basis.2. Enhance the governance of the WASH sector 2.1 Piloting new techniques to improve access to WASH services2.1.1 Provide software solutions such as (GIS, Billing, Mobile Solutions, Network Management, Customer Management, Complaint Management) to the 14 targeted water service providers to improve distribution, management, losses detection, billing, and customer service. The tailored assistance will rely on the findings of the individual technical assessment and its resulting plan in activity 1.1.1. 2.1.2 Development of zoning systems for detection of physical losses in two of the 14 localities (Yatta,Tuqu): Through this activity, zoning systems will be piloted in the two localities to detect water losses. The new technology was discussed with WSRC during the needs assessment stage of the program and will contributed to will allow for accurate detection of source of physical losses while increasing accuracy of billing. It is expected that such new technology will contribute when upscaled on reduced water tariff at the HH level and increased supply and satisfaction. The two localities were recommended by WSRC due to the existing capacity and responsiveness of their water service providers which would allow for piloting of the model. 2.2 Conducting technical capacity building training2.2.1 Conduct 4 centralized training and provide of technical assistance to staff of WSPs, WSRC, &WBWD on Network management, customer service, cost recovery and inspection in relation to provided inputs and software to each service provider from previous activities. The trainings will be conducted through contracted Experts by MA’AN and technical assistance on provisions will be conducted by specialized teams from the contracted suppliers in previous activities. The training participants will be 70 persons ( 50M.20F with at least 5% of PWDs staff) from relevant departments of the service providers. 2.2.2 Conduct 2 centralized training on tariff calculation, billing and collection systems for WSPs and WBWD (water & waste). The training participants will be 28 persons ( 20M.8F with at least 5% of PWDs staff) from the relevant departments of service providers.2.3 Conducting technical assessments about specific WASH cases and concerns:2.3.1 Conduct a Supply-Demand Hydraulic Analysis on water sources (Fresh and Treated) vs consumption needs at a national level to design a national plan for carrier pipes network that would improve water distribution and address the imbalances between water supply and demand. This plan will allow for efficient distribution of water, and directs current and future expansions and investments in water infrastructure towards the appropriate locations and communities.The proposed activities will collectively contribute to ensuring equitable, affordable, and sustainable safely-managed access to sufficient WASH services and Strengthening WASH preparedness and response capacity in the West Bank. 218183 WASH vulnerable persons (4930PWDs) will directly benefit .

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Due to the nature of the proposed response, most of the intervention will not be directly implemented in Area C ( despite benefiting area C WASH vulnerable communities) and further, the response is addressing community and national WASH priorities in comparison to other responses interacting directly with HH members. However where works might prove risky to the community MA’AN , in alignment with the “Do No Harm” Principles for humanitarian assistance, depends approaches to reduce risks to beneficiaries as a result of intervention. MA’AN adopts key principles that serve to reduce unintended negative impact of the project; these principles include: Effective feedback mechanism:, strong linkages with beneficiary communities, with a feedback mechanism that has allowed targeted communities to participate actively in the design of the project, and will further allow them to participate in the beneficiary selection process, and determining needs per each beneficiary. Feedback/complaints, and suggestions of targeted communities will be documented and taken into consideration. The project staff will further hold regular meetings with villages’ representatives and will update them about the progress and workplan of the project. Further, MA’AN will implement the project in a transparent manner where beneficiary list will be made public and reason for any person not being selected will be stated. The community will have a period to object to the list. MA’AN will ensure that the assistance is provided in times where control is limited (at night, during week-ends) in coordinated times with the community committee and beneficiary to assess the security situation of the community. MA’AN mobilizes/employs local coordinators for its projects in Area C to mitigate access risk in locations where security incidents are likely to occur. And when possible in alignment with its procurement policy and guidelines procures from local sources. MA’AN has been working for many years in the target , including for support to livelihoods, and have a good relationship with the communities, so they will be made aware of any problem with ICA or IDF in the communities in real time. Aligning with the “Do No Harm” Principle, the implementation team will ensure all planned rehabilitation work by the beneficiaries through and project is completed with project closeout).
