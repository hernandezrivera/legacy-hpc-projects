Project Summary

The collapse of Venezuela's healthcare system and the shortage of medical personnel, medicines, medical supplies, and overall national insecurity significantly challenge its ability to monitor or address outbreaks of infectious disease such as dengue. As of May 2023, 4,809 cases had been recorded in Venezuela, and the country has the second-highest death rate from dengue in the region; it harbors all four dengue serotypes, which increases the chance of severe morbidity. In Bolívar state, International Medical Corps-Venezuela is providing primary healthcare in hard-to-reach communities, including the indigenous populations, in tropical areas near the Caura River in the Cedeño and Sucre Municipalities. The Pan American Health Organization (PAHO) has reported 396 cases in Bolívar state between January and May 2023–the fourth highest number of cases in Venezuela—however, these numbers have likely been undercounted and underreported: more than 50% of International Medical Corps staff working in these areas have been infected since the beginning of the year.
International Medical Corps aims to reduce the morbidity caused by dengue in the Sucre and Cedeño municipalities of Bolívar state through a six-month, cross-sectoral program of health, environmental management, education, and communication on the prevention, control, and management of dengue.
In terms of Clinical Management of Dengue, International Medical Corps Venezuela will improve access to quality clinical care by enhancing the dengue-related operational capacities of two primary outpatient healthcare centers: CPTIII Maripa in Sucre municipality and CPTIII Chaguaramal in Cedeño municipality. We will also add dengue-specific capacity to the clinical services provided by the existing mobile medical unit (MMU). By equipping the health staff of the centers and MMUs with the knowledge, skills, and resources they need, the teams will be able to reach patients in both urban and hard-to-reach, rural areas.
To reach possible dengue patients in remote communities, International Medical Corps Venezuela will add dengue-specific capacity to three deployments of an existing MMU to San Antonio, Camurica, Las Bombitas, and Canaguapana in Sucre municipality; and to Santa Inés de la Arenosa, Romulo Gallegos, Los Caballos, and La Cervela, in Cedeño municipality. In addition to primary and reproductive healthcare, the MMU will therefore be equipped to provide and monitor treatment for non-severe cases of dengue. This MMU, which consists of one medical doctor, one nurse, one pharmacist, one logistics assistant, and one security officer, deploys monthly at the rate of one community per day. We will provide the local trained community health worker in each area with the diagnostic tests, supplies, and medicines necessary to continue care in-between MMU deployments.
Regarding Environmental Management of Dengue, International Medical Corps Venezuela will work proactively with health authorities to identify community members for committees to be established and trained in or near dengue hotspots in the catchment areas of the supported health facilities and in the eight remote communities served by the MMU. Moreover, prevention kits will be handed to minimize exposure and human contact with mosquitoes. Finally, International Medical Corps Venezuela will support the local public health authorities to ensure the chemical control of larval habitats in natural sites, as well as adult mosquitos, through outdoor spraying in compliance with the national standards for dengue control and WHO guidelines. Donated equipment will include cold-fog generators, compression sprayers, insecticides, and personal protection equipment. These supplies will be sufficient to carry out two-to-three application during the course of one year.
