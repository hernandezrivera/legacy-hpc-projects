Project Summary

With UNICEF support OPHID has been implementing the project in the seven designated districts since June 2019 in response to cyclone Idai and the current drought, which is now exacerbated by Zimbabwe’s economic decline and health sector incapacitation. Under the current situation there has been an increase in GAM prevalence in Zimbabwe from 2.5% reported in 2018 to 3.6% in 2019. The emergency is ongoing and requires perpetual vigilance and support for the most vulnerable members of the population. Under a previous UNICEF supported OPHID project, OPHID has ensured that all health facilities in the 7 designated districts have at least one health worker mentored in Nutrition, HIV/SRH/GBV prevention, care, treatment and support in emergency and that all children under 5 referred to health facilities with acute malnutrition are screened, tested and treated for HIV and TB. However with the current incapacitation of staff in the health sector, more capacity building and support is required.
The project aim is to ensure that in 7 of the most vulnerable drought affected and food insecure districts pregnant and breastfeeding women are tracked to receive the appropriate PMTCT and HIV services and are mentored in IYCF. Also that those children and adolescents and young people living with HIV are supported to remain in HIV care and treatment; and are given GBV prevention and care support. Additionally continuing the work from the previous UNICEF/OPHID project, this project tackles the vulnerabilities of the children under five who are at the risk of dying due to acute malnutrition because of the drought, and ongoing economic crisis, ensuring that they are not only treated for acute malnutrition but also screened, tested and treated for HIV and TB.

1 - Activities

The main outputs of the project will be:•Pregnant and breastfeeding women, and children and adolescents and YP living with HIV are tracked and continue to receive PMTCT and HIV treatment services through emergency related capacity building and mentoring of HCWs in affected health facilities on Nutrition, HIV/SRH/GBV prevention, care, treatment and support IYCF in emergency.•Malnourished children under 5 are provided with life-saving and protective nutrition treatment and are tested for HIV, screened for TB and referred to treatment and care through emergency related capacity building/mentoring/monitoring activities for facility based and community based HCWs and VHWs for active screening and treatment of acutely malnourished children through both in-patient and outpatient services.•Strengthened reporting, information management and utilisation of nutrition data at all levels. Through data triangulation, mentoring and monitoring HCWs and VHWs in accurate and complete case documentation of children affected by acute malnutrition. The project will achieve this through district wide coverage of 259 health facilities in 7 districts to ensure that 2000 HCWs are capacitated to provide PMTCT and HIV services, life-saving treatment to 18298 children under 5 years affected by acute malnutrition; are screened, tested and appropriately treated for HIV and TB; and cases are accurately reported on. All 3885 VHWs, HCC members, EHTs and WNCs are capacitated in active screening and case finding of children under 5 affected by acute malnutrition to ensure that they are appropriately referred for treatment.Also one SMART Survey per district will be conducted during the course of project implementation

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

Project implementation will involve participation of cluster planning meetings at national, provincial and district level. Also Health Centre committees are part of the target group together with VHWs hence affording the community's voice to be heard. SMART Survey will be conducted in the areas of implementation hence informed decision will be taken to address the needs of affected people Each health facility will be provided with a suggestion box to be place preferably in the family health area/department where under5 children are seen.The complains will be actioned periodically by the facility staff together with the partner to respond to any issues raised by the beneficiaries through the suggestion box.
