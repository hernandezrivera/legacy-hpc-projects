Project Summary

Building on experience of project interventions in Syria since 2013,Oxfam continues to work with local authorities in responding to the vulnerable populations’ WASH needs in districts/subdistricts of Aleppo, Hama, Idlib,Rural Damascus,Dar’a &Deir Ezzor, based on assessments, severity of needs and in response to further emerging needs in NES and NWS.The subdistricts that will be targeted by this project have all been heavily affected by conflict and require WASH support.OXFAM conducted integrated WASH&EFSVL assessments from January 2022 to date covering up to 50 communities through group discussions and key informant in three governorates.This includes consultations with LNGOs, INGOs, secondary data and the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022.The project will focus during the years 2022&2023 on life-saving WASH interventions and support the resilience of existing WASH systems (water supply &sewer networks, wastewater treatment, solid waste management)&WASH in institutions, such as schools and healthcare facilities, ensuring coordinated and cohesive response strongly aligned to the WASH Sector. It is gender mainstreamed, with a response strategy overarched by a community engagement approach suitable to access and security challenges; implementation engages trusted community structures carefully screened private contractors, community-based volunteers and identified local partners, and strong collaboration with other actors.
The total estimated population, as per UN-OCHA datasets in these sub-districts, is over 3M people.OXFAM targets the timely response to emerging rapid onset emergency needs in hot spots& potential flashpoints, new returns,new displacements,affected by C-19, economic issues, and the current returned areas with persisting WASH needs. The target locations proposed are according to severity of humanitarian needs and number of populations in need which will be adapted to the evolving ground context, access and people's movement. Although Hama considered under severity scale 1, majority of AlKousor neighborhood suffers from water shortage& still rely on water trucking.Accordingly,Oxfam considered WASH needs there. The project aims to support lifesaving WASH interventions that are resilience-focused, sustainable, durable &cost-effective WASH systems using community engagement approach and mainstreaming C-19 mitigation measures and response throughout all regular WASH activities: The objectives of the project are;1):support to water,sanitation/sewage&solid waste management systems to ensure regular services for affected people in Syria; 2):deliver humanitarian WASH supplies and services,&improve hygienic behavior and practices of most vulnerable people; 3) WASH coordination structures facilitated and enhanced;4) improve WASH facilities and services in institutions to minimize sub-standard WASH conditions of the most vulnerable people in Syria.The type of interventions strongly aligns with: SO1,SO2,SO3&SO4 which captures the sector efforts to respond and assist local water establishments in restoring WASH services to basic functionality for communities, schools&community health facilities.Oxfam will target all age groups for water and sanitation interventions. Hygiene interventions will target careers women,adolescent girls&school children. Accurate disaggregation of population data will be collected from relevant ministries and actors to ensure coordinated and relevant data and appropriate interventions during WASH assessments and interventions. Oxfam has unique MOUs with several ministries gaining trust,strong relations,&approval of interventions. Bi-monthly meetings at the MoWR allow insight and agreement for selection of prioritized possible activities and discussion of operational challenges.This project’s close alignment with the HRP in terms of outcomes, activities&methodology reflects Oxfam’s close engagement with the Sector,particularly through strategic discussions&information sharing throughout 2019&2022.

5 - Implementation of activities

OXFAM’s emergency response integrated with resilience activities using community engagement approach are:1)water trucking,2)repair/rehabilitation of water systems to functioning level,3) rehabilitate existing electricity&diesel-powered water pumping systems with solar system as alternative to increasing high maintenance cost and lack of fuel supply;provision of communal and HH water tanks;4)support to water quality assurance, operation and maintenance of water supply systems;5)repair,rehabilitation and operational support to sanitation/sewage which includes sewage pipe repair&unblocking, septage tank desludging and solid waste management systems;6) Distribution of essential emergency age & gender-sensitive hygiene materials and WASH non-food items,hygiene promotion on WASH related diseases&C-19 through behaviours change campaigns&perception tracking;7)support humanitarian life-saving/emergency WASH facilities and services,including strengthening Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) measures;8)market-based assistance for WASH services to improve resilience and dignity;9)rehabilitation or construction of separate toilets for male and female,&develop community level communal water delivery plan for equitable share;10)support to WASH in institutions&child friendly spaces in close coordination with Education and Protection sectors,support,11) capacity building of LWE WASH technical staff.The aforementioned activities will be implemented throughout the years 2022 & 2023

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Incidents during the rehabilitation works that might cause injuries to community people and disabled people/ children can’t use the facility• Rehabilitated facilities are not maintained after completion of the project.Risk mitigation: (1-Oxfam will place proper exit plan along with handover to local authority. 2-Ensure agreed context/SPHERE standards and closely monitor these provisions/aspects during implementation. 3- Ensure communities are consulted during design, implementation, and monitoring phase of projects. 4-Accountability mechanisms are put in place and received feedback from users is followed up. 5- Consider specific support to people with disabilities to ensure they access sufficient access to structures. 6-Ensure that the working location is secured with fences).


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Exposure to safeguarding risks by women and girls’ especially the vulnerable and marginalized groupsSexual harassment, exploitation and abuse perpetrated by OXFAM staff, partners, and representativesViolence against women (VAW) and GBV: Changes in usual home dynamics and those brought by challenging gender norms and redistribution of care work, may lead to tension Risk mitigation: (1-Work with existing referral pathways through provision of technical assistance on the prevention of and management/handling of GBV & SEA. 2- Continuously review referral pathways especially during COVID - in case a service is closed or operating differently.3- Link with other INGO’s for community awareness raising on SEA, & disseminate messages 4- Establish feedback/reporting mechanisms and safeguarding focal points whose members should come from Oxfam, project partners and community representatives on safeguarding issues, and disseminate information on the existence of such to project participants. 5-Ensure there are community-based complaints and feedback mechanisms that are safe, accessible and functional. 6-Disseminating the information about safeguarding to the project participants. 7-Conduct or include in inception meetings/project activities on gender sensitivity or awareness raising and sensitization workshops/ learning sessions with project participants, local leaders especially on prevention of and protection of women from GBV, VAWC, SEA, shared household management, childcare&UCDW


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Risk of tension develop between communities who will benefit from services, and communities who will not be reached by these interventions. Risk probability: High. Mitigation: 1) selection of communities with least or compromised access to WASH infrastructures and NFIs and who are using the resources of the neighboring communities 2) consultations with representatives of neighboring communities explaining the selection criteria and highlighting how the intervention will ease extraction from their water resources and improve sewage functions, 3) coordination with WASH actors, local authorities, and community leadersRisk: Quality, durability and sustainability of interventions Probability: High Mitigation: 1) Community engagement not only in implementation but in complete project cycle 2) capacity development of local community and service providers, 3) Ensuring context/SPHERE standards and regular monitoring, 4)establishing a robust feedback mechanism, 5) coordination and collaboration with all stakeholders, 6) local, indigenous technology use for being cost efficient as well as O&M, 7) Ensure proper registration system for staff/partners/contractors; add zero tolerance disclosure on child labour in the contracts; check and register civil documentation, 8) Financial controls and continuous monitoring of exchange rates with regular updates to donor
