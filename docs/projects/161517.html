Project Summary

This project will undertake food distribution and livelihood resilience projects for affected persons in Tonj North, Twic and Gogrial East. According GOAL’s 2015 SQUEAC assessment, Greater Bharelghazal region is largely affected by extensive flooding during the rainy season leading to poor harvest as crops are destroyed in the process and rendering many areas inaccessible. Access to humanitarian assistance has improved throughout the region, though periodic access constraints have persisted notably in many parts of Greater Bharelghazal region. Despite this improvement, Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) remain widespread during the peak of the 2019 lean season, and an estimated 2.75 million The food security situation is expected to deteriorate in all the areas around South Sudan during the January to April 2020 projection period as household food stocks start to deplete and access to livestock products and wild foods reduce seasonally during this period. An estimated 2.63 million, representing 60.2% of the regional population will be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase).
For lives saving activities such as food distribution and livelihood program, these are important to reduce burden of hunger, malnutrition, encourage school children to go to school, prevention of malnutrition related conditions (diarrhea, Kala-Azar, TB, HIV), low birth weight result to stunted growth leads to brain complications that result to learning impairment due to lack of food intake from the mother during pregnancy. Food distribution and livelihood projects will contribute significantly to prevention of common diseases; having health community that will have strong immune system to fight infections, children are learning properly when they have food available either at home or in schools, once there is food in households girls and boys are allowed to attend classes than when there is no food in the house. It has been seen in many places across the country, children are the most likely to search for food in assisting their parents and this makes it difficult for them to attend classes, to avoid sending children to look for food in bushes (wild Food) this is needed to assist vulnerable community across these four counties. Food assistance preserves assets for household that can be kept for resilience recovery use. However, the community activities such as normal cultivation has to be encouraged that will lead to the recovery and resilience life in post conflict respond strategies should South Sudan continue to experience lasting peace. Good awareness on agriculture and food production can be target that community has to adapt in their thinking.

1 - Needs

After decades of war, South Sudan’s time for peace and prosperity had finally surfaced in 2011, however, post referendum conflicts exposed most South Sudanese to living below the poverty line with the highest female illiteracy rate of approximately 88% and poor access to the most basic services such as healthcare, food items and proper shelter. DTM team have been extensively conducting mobile tracking for returns from abroad and also within the country and the data highlighted the continued influx of returnees to their homes with hope and anticipation of the revitalized peace agreement (R-ARCSS).On the other hand, although most displacements have been due to the conflicts, about 13% are attributed to communal clashes (encompassing cattle raids and other forms of violence) that have forced relocations within the country. With the continuous gross number of displacements, hunger remains at critical to catastrophic levels. Approximately 76% of the population is said to be food insecure with an alarming 6.35 million people (54%) facing crisis of IPC (phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, an estimated 1.7 million people are facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and 10,000 people are in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5) compared to the same period in 2018, according to IPC report September 2019 (attached). The numbers are skyrocketing despite continued humanitarian efforts (food insecurity has doubled compared to yearly trends). Although food security situation in Northern Bhar el ghazal has improved by 15%, the situation is said to still be precarious. Overall, these issues around food and livelihoods can be consequently attached to deteriorating or even worse persisting economic crisis that has affected both markets and households even pre 2013, 2016 crisis. It’s reported that 42% of households have indicated decreased household income within 2019 with 63% of household income spent on food and half of that was used to purchase cereal alone. Cereal production is affected by lack of land for cultivation and additional poor productivity where an average household produces enough for only 3 months per year. This has made crop production (cereal) insufficient to meet the national cereal need by harvests. Although there is critical need for food supply, it’s important to consider the proper dietary requirements as per household surveys, only about 7% met the minimum dietary needs raising the concerns around malnutrition especially in 6-59 months old and Pregnant and lactating women (PLW). The lack of dietary diversity has shown the continued prevalence of GAM at about 23% which is considered serious as per WHO standards with much critical levels seen in Warrap state. Stunting continues to be a challenge within this age group and is a cause for public health concern. Displacements, heavy rainfalls and recurrent malnutrition caseloads in Warrap particularly Twic threaten to increase morbidity and mortality in these areas. According to IRNA assessment, sharing of food with IDPs has been critical in sustaining them, however, food stocks are expected to get depleted amongst host community households as the lean season sets and may lead to large consumption gaps.There is a complex relationship between gender roles, social norms, food insecurity and GBV and a persisting phenomenon of ‘hyper-masculinity’ amongst men often exacerbated by the conflict of risk and insecurity. This reinforces harmful power differentials between men and women (and children), often leading to GBV and other forms of violence. This project will aim to address these difference while considerate of the existing gaps in planning for emergency food distribution and designing resilience livelihood projects in Twic, Gogrial East and Tonj North counties. Target population across these counties is 1,000 beneficiaries, 56% adults and 44% children. FOWSSI will integrate the GBV component by targeting 392 women and 264 girls.

2 - Activities

The estimated hunger gap is April-July; however, nutrition partners report that the peak for acute malnutrition occurs earlier than this. This reliance on food assistance presents a concern as the rainy season commences, with many remote locations becoming more difficult to access for humanitarians. Areas of flood and difficult roads found that populations face difficulty in accessing humanitarian assistance due to flooding that sometimes renders them inaccessible for months at a time. The project in collaboration with partners on ground, this project will provide a platform for livelihood support and resilience programs through food distribution for 1,000 men and women Boys and girls with in both IDPs and host communities in across four counties catchment areas based these activities; • Undertaking registration of all beneficiaries (IDPs, returnees, and host community)• Food distribution to the right beneficiaries at the time with calculated food ratios• FOWSSI will complement this project by continued consultations and screening of malnutrition status for children under 5 nearby health facilities and mobile outreaches undertaken partners on ground for South Sudan Humanitarian Fund under UN-OCHA/UNDP for 27,494 beneficiaries of men, women, boys and girls and refer the them to nearby treatment centers. Our projects integrate Nutrition, health, FSL, NFI, protection and WASH services in Health facilities and community levels.• Health education and health promotion will be targeting the same population We anticipate;• Risk of return to violence can be anticipated with back and forth rhetoric’s around formation of new government between the SPLM and opposition groups. • Fear of community violence between villages such as cattle raiding, or community revenge can hinder population movement to the distribution sites and food distribution in each county. • However, with current overview of the area and stationed staff, we can plan and predisposition food items at these critical times to avoid some of the risks or impediments that may occur as it the same mitigation we use to health care services in our locations. • Discussing these issues with local authorities is very vital to tackle challenges that can face project and will always keep WFP team, World Vision in Kuajok Office and Juba informed on any impediment that arise in the field. We have field coordinators that coordinate our projects with local authorities (ROSS, counties administrations).
