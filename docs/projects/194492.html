Project Summary

Concern will provide life-saving curative and preventative nutrition services in Aweil North County in Northern Bahr el Ghazal (NBeG); Guit County in Unity, and Juba IDP camp and Kajo-Keji County in Central Equatoria State. In NBeG, Concern will provide nutrition services across 28 sites in Aweil North, 15 in Guit and 2 in Juba IDP camp, while the OTP services are integrated in primary health care services provided by Concern in Kajo-Keji County. Concern will use the Community-Based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) approach and the guidelines approved by the national Nutrition Cluster and State Ministry of Health (SMoH) for treatment of malnutrition in Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP), Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP) and Stabilization Center (SC). Concern will continue to prevent malnutrition through a combination of interventions including Social Behavior Change (SBC) through group sessions and individual counselling, promotion of home gardening, cooking demonstrations and promotion of good hygiene practices. To ensure community participation and improve access and coverage, Concern will involve community volunteers for community mobilization, screening, follow up cases and dissemination of recommended nutrition behaviors. Concern will continue its success with pre-emptively reducing risks of malnutrition through supporting mother and father groups in improving Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN). The SBC curriculum mother/fathers groups promotes gender equality and male involvement in child caring and feeding practices. The nutrition service provision will be integrated with WASH, health and FSL interventions to realize broader outcomes including improved access to safe water, sanitation, adequate diet and health care services.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan has continued to deteriorate with the number of people in need is growing due to multitude of crises, including the socio-economic impact of the COVID-19 pandemic, continual climate shocks and conflict induced displacement, dwindling economic conditions, and most recently the rise in food prices due to Ukraine crisis and fourth years of consecutive flooding is likely to further compound the food and nutrition insecurity. The latest IPC analysis indicated that 7.8 million people (about 65% of the population) are projected to face acute food insecurity during the lean season (April-July 2023). Similarly, the latest IPC Acute Malnutrition (AMN) analysis indicated that, between July 2022 and June 2023 an estimated 1.4 million children under five are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition including approximately 345,893 will need SAM treatment, while another 1,057,703 will need MAM treatment. The admission level at nutrition centres across all locations indicate an increasing trends, potentially due to impact of flooding, food insecurity, hike in food prices and disease prevalence. In the proposed locations, the latest IPC analysis projection (Apr-July 23) indicated an emergency level (IPC 4) of food insecurity in Aweil North and Guit counties while Juba IDP sites and Kajo Keji Counties are projected in crisis level (IPC 3). The latest SMART survey conducted by Concern in Juba IDP camp, indicated GAM of 19% and SAM was 4.9%. While no latest SMART survey conducted in Guit and Aweil North, however current GAM prevalence as per latest Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS) is estimated at 14.3% and 9% respectively. The situation is likely to further deteriorate due to the disruptions in imports, food production and the related surge in food prices induced by the current conflict in Ukraine. The recent IPC AMN analysis indicated a Critical AMN situation in Aweil North and Guit Counties and Serious AMN in Juba IDP sites, while Kajo Keji is indicated in IPC AMN Phase 2 (Alert) however with the increased number of returnees predicted in the area which may further increase SAM burden in Kajo Keji. The key drivers of high level of malnutrition include, inappropriate feeding practices; poor health seeking behavior; inadequate health services; women’s heavy workload; and lack of availability of food or unequal food distribution skewed towards male family members. Given the depleted local coping capacities, the need for the continued nutrition services has increased. Higher levels of malnutrition are often found among women and girls due to gender-inequitable access to nutritious foods, healthcare and WASH services. It is therefore imperative that Concern continues its life-saving work providing gender-responsive nutrition services, integrated with Concern other donor funded multi-sector interventions for wider outcomes. The programme will also contribute to Concern Country Strategic Plan 2022-2024 with a specific focus on quality nutrition services and addressing the root causes of malnutrition.

2 - Project activities

CO1: equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition: In order to improve the knowledge and practice of MIYCN, Concern will strengthen prevention of acute malnutrition by focusing on behaviour change communication. This will be done through individual counselling and group sessions at nutrition sites and community level. Beneficiaries will receive soap to promote good hygiene practices and mosquito nets to prevent children from malaria. Moreover, mothers and fathers groups will be established for repeated sessions. Gender transformative topics are included in the curriculum for both mothers and fathers groups to improve the knowledge and commitment of men in maternal infant and young child nutrition. Demonstration gardens will be established in nutrition sites for mother to learn on how to produce vegetables. In Aweil North, WFP support Concern on BSFP to prevent acute malnutrition among children under 2 years. Vulnerable HH will be targeted for BSFP and GFD CO2: equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition: Concern will continue to respond to the management of acute malnutrition through the CMAM approach for the treatment of both severe and moderate acute malnutrition, following the National CMAM guidelines. In Unity, Concern will provide nutrition services in 15 sites. In Juba IDP camp, Concern will provide nutrition services in two sites and in Kajo-Keji Concern will provide nutrition services in 5 health facilities. Similarly, in NBeG Concern will provide nutrition services in 22 sites in Aweil North. Severely malnourished children without medical complications will be managed through the weekly OTP while those with medical complications will be referred to the inpatient care until the medical complication is resolved and return to OTP to complete treatment. Children discharged from OTP will be referred to TSFP to prevent relapse. Children and PLW with moderate acute malnutrition will be treated in TSFP. Only in Juba IDP camp elders will be targeted for TSFP based on the WFP plan. To maximize the coverage, CNVs will conduct screening, follow-up of absentees and defaulters. Concern will continue to scale up family MUAC approach. CO3: To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information: Concern will continue to contribute to the national database by providing monthly NIS data to the nutrition cluster: The nutrition cluster analyses the admission trend and exit performance indicators and shares with partners during the bi-weekly coordination meetings. Concern also has a CMAM database to monitor the admission trend and exit performance indicators on a monthly basis. In order to estimate the prevalence of acute malnutrition, to evaluate the impact of nutrition intervention and to contribute to IPC analysis, Concern will conduct SMART surveys in some of the priority project locations. CO4: To increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors: Concern will use all the possible ways to integrate the nutrition with other sectors. The nutrition sites are integrated with health facilities, all SAM cases at admission and SAM and MAM cases with malaria symptoms will be tested and treated for malaria and those who are not vaccinated will be referred to the health facility. To prevent malaria, beneficiaries will receive mosquito nets. Concern provides soap for OTP and TSFP beneficiaries as part of good hygiene practices. Concern promotes home gardening for OTP, TSFP beneficiaries and mother groups. Moreover, cases with protection issues will be referred to the protection partners working in the area.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Concern understand different vulnerabilities within target communities that may exclude target beneficiaries in their access to feedback and complaint mechanism. Therefore, Concern will consult with the target community, the local chief and state authorities to understand the overall target population profile, access to communication channel and nature of vulnerabilities. While establishing the Feedback and Complaint mechanism, Concern will consider such vulnerabilities and access constrained therefore the target population will be consulted on their preferred mode of complaint/feedback channel. Different mode of feedback such as complaint boxes, phone numbers, email, verbal feedback/complaint during Concern monitoring visits, in-person to Concern office and Help desk at distribution points/beneficiaries registration points will be used to avoid exclusion of access as for as possible considering the operational challenges in target locations. Concern will use both pictorial, written and verbal channels in local languages to communicate the feedback mechanism to target beneficiaries. The individuals living with disabilities, illiterate, women and girls can also share their complaint/feedback through lead mothers and CNVs hired from local community.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Concern’s policies and operational guidelines are in line with IASC’s five Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations. At the start of project implementation, Concern usually introduces the project to community leaders such as block and sector leaders; the Camp High Committee (CHC); boma and village chiefs and payam administrators, County Commissioner and the RRC; the SMoH; and the CHD. CNVs also conduct community mobilization and sensitization to ensure community is aware about the program, target groups, targeting criteria and entitlement. Caregivers will be informed of admission criteria and beneficiary entitlements (size of the ration) during TSFP/OTP days, which will also be displayed at nutrition sites. The banners and IEC materials displayed at nutrition sites will have information about beneficiary entitlements and feedback mechanism. In IDP sites, the Camp Management Committee (CMC) and block/zone leaders will be informed about the feedback mechanism. The staff will regularly orient the caregivers and mothers about the admission criteria, entitlements, feedback mechanism, their right to assistance and complaints during the OTP/TSFP days and in counselling sessions. The mother and father groups will be sensitized about feedback mechanism. In IDP camp, the camp manager implements the CRM but Concern will have a Community Feedback Mechanism (CFM) in all sites where beneficiaries can register suggestions, feedback or complaints. The monitoring and CRM (Dedicate staff) will conduct regular visit to refresh beneficiaries about the right to complaint, suggestion and how to make complaint.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Concern has a Code of Conduct and its associated policies including Program Participant Protection Policy (P4), Child Safeguarding, and Anti-Trafficking in-person Policy. Concern staff and any one engaged by Concern including consultants, contractors, partners and visitors are required to understand and comply with Concern safeguarding policies. Concern’s Code of Conduct and Associated Policy is consistent with the six core principles of IASC Task Force on PSEA in Humanitarian Crises. The safeguarding focal points each office ensure implementation and compliance with Concern safeguarding policies. During project inception and regular consultations with the beneficiaries and relevant stake holders, Concern will inform them about right to assistance, entitlements, right to appropriate behavior including Concern’s commitment to zero tolerance on Sexual exploitation and abuse by humanitarian workers. The beneficiaries will be informed about the Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) to raise their voice in case of any issue pertaining to PSEA. The monitoring mechanism will ensure that the beneficiaries are aware about PSEA and how to complaint in case of any exploitation and abuse. In all locations, Concern has a Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) where beneficiaries can register complaints about gender related or protection concerns which are referred to Protection partners. All recruitments of staff, volunteers and trainings will ensure that there is equal chance to women and men to ensure balanced representation. Information will be collected on safety and protection concerns and to improve future programme designs.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

During project implementation Concern will closely collaborate with government health department, community leaders and communities. The Camp Management Committee (CMC), sector and block leaders in IDP camps and boma and village chiefs in other locations will support supervising and nominating Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNV) lead mothers and lead fathers based on agreed selection criteria. CNVs and lead mothers will continue to screen for malnutrition, defaulter tracing, early case detection and referral of malnourished children 6-59 months. The regularly monitoring and quarterly reviews will collate the feedback, lessons and will inform about necessary adaptation to project assistance. Similarly, the Complaint and Feedback Mechanism and regular consultation with beneficiaries during monitoring will provide opportunity to beneficiaries to share their feedback which will be considered in adapting the program accordingly. Concern will consult with the beneficiaries about the site selection and distributions location/timing and plan according to the feedback from the community.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

During assessments, Concern will coordinate with CCCM and Protection partners and identify safety, security and protection risks and findings will be used to adapt the programme. Distribution sites will be located in consultation with communities to ensure they are in safe locations. Prior to distributions, Concern will conduct sensitisations on how beneficiaries can reduce protection risks such as travelling in mixed groups and during daylight hours. Concern ensures gender balance staffing and ensures that all staff aware about protection and gender related risk. The CRM will be used to identify protection cases and make subsequent referrals to Protection partners. The elderly enrolled in nutrition program will be assisted in their access to nutrition program/messaging. Concern safeguarding policies require all staff and any one engaged in its programs to comply with code of conduct and reporting of breaches. The beneficiaries will be sensitised on CRM and their right to share feedback and complaint. Concern will ensure that the staff were trained on GBV, aware about referral pathways and sensitise the beneficiaries on reporting and use of GBV pathways.
