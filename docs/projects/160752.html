1 - Needs

The need is to address the long standing challenge of food and nutrition insecurity in the areas of concern, the intervention shall be through training the communities on: (i) Sustainable production and productivity of fish and fish products; (ii) Healthy post-harvest fish handling procedures, contemporary preservation and storage methods; (iii) Novel cooking methods to enhance fish protein nutritional value for reduction of communities’ malnutrition levels. The strategy is to train fisher folk in Ganyliel in Payinjiar county and Jaah payam in Koch County (Rier/Tharjiath location) in order to reduce chronic food insecurity, improve nutrition and increasing household incomes. There are already demonstration sites where the population will be trained on construction of fishponds, fingerling production, water quality management, fish feed formulation, fish diseases management and post-harvest handling.

2 - Activities

Our project activities are as follow: - Setting-up demonstration farm for vegetables, for trees seedlings and construction of fishponds Training small scale farmers targeting youth and women Supporting institutions to Plant fruit and ornamental trees, efforts to increase food production and preserve the environment Mobilizing the youth and other community members into farming activities Capacity building for the youth and women on issues of NRM, agriculture and emerging technologies in aquaculture-Fish farming

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes; with the records of RRC and UN agencies in Bentiu office, the displaced communities in the area are currently in POC and some are already returning to their places of origin in all the counties of (Unity state), through this project, this communities will have chances to participate and produce food for themselves. CLIP Poverty will come up with cluster grouping mechanisms which will see to it that every beneficiary benefits

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

- Yes, indirectly the project will provide food security and environment resiliency which are national concerns, through this, returnees will have skills to produce their own food for basic consumption and increase production to market demand , this will also make the IDPs from the POCs and refugees return to their homes to start vegetables farm, planting fruit and ornamental trees and fishing farming and preservation.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes; according to the survey done by CLIP Poverty staffs and considering the factors involved in environment management through Environment impact assessment (EIA), this is taken care of through planting and distribution of trees seedlings and training of farmers on environment preservation.

Yes; by setting up the demonstration farms, and training the beneficiaries this ensures that beneficiaries have the knowledge to carry on with the activities even when funding end, fishponds built for beneficiaries shall be handed over to them for future sustainable use. CLIP Poverty staff are always in the ground to help in sustainable mechanisms upon project time-out from FAO.
