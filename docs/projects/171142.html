Project Summary

The project targets 12 primary schools namely Ayuub, Ali Banaadir, Hodan/Alaqsaa, Buulo Mareer, Bakaal/Sahal, Eel Munye, Shalambood, Tima ‛Adde, Nuurta Taliyow, 21 Oktobar, IDP One and IDPTwo in Lower Shabelle region affected by conflict, floods and ongoing droughts in South West State. This project aims to retain learners in existing schools and enroll new IDPs/returnees, refugees and host communities targeting 4612 learners (1772 girls) 460 of whom are disabled whose needs include transportation to school, brails and ramps along entrances to classrooms and toilets among others, and 115 teachers (female 37 female) and 24 CECs (12 female) at school in mitigating the case of high school dropout rate after the Covid-19 six months break. All these has been proposed in realization that only 30% of children aged 5-16 years are enrolled in primary education in Somalia. An estimated 3 million school-aged children 6-18 years are out‐of‐school and that 1.4 million crisis affected children (45% girls) will need education assistance in 2021 18% of whom come from Lower Shabelle. The SG Report on Children and Armed Conflict 2020 further states that in 2019, 1,442 children (53 girls) were recruited and/or used mainly by non-state armed groups and that 227 girls reported sexual violence. The Country Task Force on Monitoring and Reporting verified 1,184 grave violations, registering the recruitment of 546 children (30 girls), the killing and maiming of 469 children (107 girls) and 29 attacks on schools during the reporting period of January and June 2020. COVID-19 school closure had also affected 1.1M8 children previously enrolled in schools from March to August 2020 leaving many children to engage in child labor, early marriages and increased child trafficking. GBV AoR rapid assessment on effects of COVID-19 reported a 34% physical violence, 20% sexual abuse and harassment and intimate partner violence. 81% of the respondents stated that they know ten or more girls cut or undergone FGM. Also, girls were not able to access sanitary kids as parents could not afford.
The project will enhance quality learning opportunities in safe and protective learning environments by ensuring emergency affected children have equal access, engaged in live-saving activities and promotes personal well-being and social cohesion through; provision of quality and disability friendly Teaching and Learning Materials in 12 primary schools aimed at improving the physical and mental growth of learners. Teacher incentives to 117 teachers and head teachers spread over the 12 targeted schools, school feeding, rehabilitation of classrooms with ramps for physically disabled in locations previously destroyed during civil unrest and floods to attract the many IDPs children to enroll, construction of separate gender appropriate VIP latrines in conformity to WASH and SPHERE standards, Install water tanks and build the capacities of CECs to better manage school resources with a focus on finding sustainable solutions.
The project will provide protective learning environments for most vulnerable learners through; Training of 24 CECs on child protection issues, PSS for learners and care givers, awareness creation on child safeguarding including Hygiene and messaging on COVID-19 prevention and response, DRR, referral of GBV cases to GBV AoR and provision of recreational materials to CtC clubs. All aimed at increasing resilience through the provision of basic protection services and support to school-based resilience mechanisms that will contribute to the sustainability of children’s well-being and build the capacities of teachers and community to better respond to child protection issues. AYUUB NGO strives to build resilience, capacity and education opportunities available to vulnerable groups in Lower Shabelle including child protection response capacity, coordination between the different groups and administrations within the region to promote child protection and Education rights

Project Activities

Overall Objective: To ensure retention of learners where they live through protective and lifesaving education activities that integrate fully protection to achieve quality education for most vulnerable learners affected the ongoing droughts in Lower Shabelle region. Outcome 1: 4612 learners (2840 boys 1772 girls) most vulnerable, crisis affected children of school going ages have been supported with quality and protective learning environments in Lower Shabelle region. Output 1.1: Improved access to quality education through provision of supplementary food, safe drinking water, TLS, TLM, WASH and incentives to help retain 4612 learners (2840 boys 1772 girls) from vulnerable communities in school. Activity 1.1.1: Support 4612 learners (2840 boys 1772 girls) learners through school feeding to drought affected school going children in 12 primary schools. The CEC will supervise the school feeding. Activity 1.1.2: Distribute quality and disability sensitive teaching and learning materials for 4612 learners (2840 boys 1772 girls) in Lower Shabelle. Activity 1.1.3: Provide EiE teachers’ incentives to 115 teachers (female 37 female, 78 male) in 12 schools for 9 school months to help retain some 4612 learners (2840 boys 1772 girls) at school in Lower Shabelle.Activity 1.1.4: Rehabilitate 30 classrooms in 12 public schools to serve 4612 learners (2840 boys 1772 girls) in Lower Shabelle. Activity 1.1.5: Construct 20 separate gender appropriate and disability friendly VIP latrines with hand washing facilities to support the 30 newly rehabilitated classrooms in Lower Shabelle. The latrines shall comply with WASH and SPHERE standards to ensure sustainability and durability within local context. Activity 1.1.6: Provide water to 4612 learners (2840 boys 1772 girls) through 7 constructed water tanks in IDP schools most affected by drought. The tanks shall be connected to the already existing water pipes distribution system and a monthly consumption paid in accordance with UNICEF’s acceptable standards. Activity 1.1.7: Train 115 teachers (female 37 female) and 24 CECs (12 female) on school management of resources and sustainability of education programs including disaster risk reduction including Hygiene and messaging on COVID-19 prevention and response in Lower Shabelle. Outcome 2: Ensure that 4612 learners (2840 boys 1772 girls) learners in emergencies are provided with safe, protective and conducive learning environments that promotes personal wellbeing and psychosocial support. Output 2.1: 4612 learners (2840 boys 1772 girls) in emergencies are supported through PSS, protected from GBV and are made aware of their rights to education. Activity 2.1.1: Provide Structured and sustained psychosocial support (PSS) for learners and their caregivers and referral mechanisms with provision of recreational activities for 4612 learners (2840 boys 1772 girls) through support to club to club activities and out of schools through CFSs /Community structures. Activity 2.1.2: Train 24 teachers (2 from every school) and 12 CECs (1 from each school) as child protection focal points in 12 schools to provide the necessary child safeguarding programs including community outreach and awareness raising for out of school children and their caregivers on child protection and the importance of education. Activity 2.1.3: Provide structured and disability friendly recreational materials including sanitary pads for adolescent girls in 12 primary schools to help in the child wellbeing and reduction of absenteeism due to menstrual cycles among girls.

Mitigation Measures

For the risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance, AYUUB will mandatorily ask all staff and contractors engaged in this project to sign against AYUUB code of conduct and AYUUB PSEA policy before they sign the contracts. For risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms; AYUUB will provide a complaint toll free number and create awareness on the use and management of data reported including sensitization on the confidentiality of the information received.on the risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity in targeted project area, AYUUB will coordinate with local civil society organizations, local authorities and community leaders to ensure there is a common agreement on land usage and lease terms.
