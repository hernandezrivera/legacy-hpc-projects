Project Summary

The proposed project aims to contribute to the HRP's Strategic Objective 3: The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued. Specifically, it is aligned with the Food Security Sector's (FSS) Strategic Objective 1: The resilience of vulnerable households is protected, and their livelihoods, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced.
The project aims to protect and to enhance the resilience and livelihoods of farmers, herders and poultry farmers’ communities in Rafah and Khan Younis Governorates. Specifically, the project seeks to strengthen the productive capacities of farmers, herders, poultry farmers and fisherfolks - and their HHs- to sustain their food security and to increase their livelihood resilience . Oxfam will implement the project with its local partner, the Economic and Social Development Centre (ESDC). Oxfam and ESDC have a long-standing record and solid experience implementing agricultural activities and providing humanitarian assistance and livelihood support interventions in the Gaza Strip.
Activities of the project will include supporting the livelihoods of (60) farmers and their families through the rehabilitation of greenhouses, the rehabilitation work will be through direct implementation by. Oxfam and ESDC, where an additional (60) farmers will benefit from the establishment and rehabilitation of rainwater harvesting ponds (linked to the greenhouses)the (with an average value of 2500 USD per HH), , and (130) farmers/herders will be supported in the rehabilitation of 20 poultry farms(with average value of 2500 USD) and 110 small ruminant sheds(with average value of 1500 USD). . The project will also support small-scale small ruminate herders with essential veterinarian services that will cover around (280 HHs) to overcome the high rate of lamb mortality and to promote herd management best practices. The project will also support the resilience of (200) fisherfolk will benefit from the provision of essential fishing tools and equipment with an average value of 250 USD ,Moreover, the project will also support the rehabilitation of (30) fisherfolk's boat bodies, which have been damaged, either as a result of daily confrontation with Israeli forces or by natural hazards.The rehabilitation will be implemented directly by Oxfam and ESDC .The project's activities have been designed in consultation with the target groups, through focus group discussions (FGD), and with the FSS, the Ministry of Agriculture's (MoA) fishery departments and fishery syndicate. The proposed interventions were selected based on the HRP 2023 priority matrix.
This project is based on Oxfam’s resilience framework; ultimately aiming to increase beneficiaries’ absorptive and adaptive capacities. Oxfam and ESDC have extensive experience working with targeted livelihood groups. In cooperation with ESDC, Oxfam has implemented several projects with fisherfolk, in coordination with the MoA and fishery syndicate in the Gaza strip, including one regional project Oxfam aligns its programs with safe-programming strategies and guidance that continue to steer its project implementation, across all activities in emergency and non-emergency situations, to ensure communities and beneficiaries are safeguarded against sexual exploitation and abuse. Oxfam will ensure mainstreaming protection throughout the project cycle, starting from assessment through the implementation and monitoring. This will be represented through beneficiaries’ selection through tackling protection-based criteria in addition to the livelihood vulnerability. It will also address protection risks in a referral mechanism in liaison with protection cluster members.

1 - Needs

For the last 16 years, Gaza has suffered, and is continuing to suffer, from the social and economic impacts of a land and naval blockade, where the movement of people and goods in/out of Gaza has been under restrictions imposed by the Government of Israel. The recurring hostilities and shocks in Gaza continue to hit already vulnerable communities chronically deprived of full recovery, worsening the socio-economic condition on the ground. Livelihood groups in Gaza are impacted by the difficulties faced in the agriculture sector, such as restricted entry of materials needed to restore damaged livelihood assets, inability to access 22% of agricultural lands (due to Israeli restrictions), restrictions on trade and access to resources, and high unemployment and poverty rates. In the last two years alone, Gaza witnessed several escalations with the most devastating ones occurring in May 2021 and August 2022. According to the MoA in the Gaza Strip, the last hostilities in August 2022 severely damaged agricultural lands, greenhouses, livestock, and poultry farms, causing direct and indirect (inability to access lands) losses estimated at $1 million. In addition, fisherfolk in the Gaza Strip, according to Al Mezan Center, have faced more frequent confrontation with Israeli forces in the first half of 2022, where 225 boats were damaged with gunfire and 12 boats were confiscated. The ongoing instability and recurrent crises in the Gaza Strip go far beyond direct losses of assets, as the inability of farmers and fisherfolk to reach their facilities impacts the profitability and sustainability of their agricultural activities, driving them to continuous losses and risking their farming practices.Specifically, this project aims to address the following immediate needs of the target groups:Increased prices of essential livelihood commodities: Decades of movement and access restrictions, including the past 16-year blockade, have left Gaza residents particularly exposed to a global spike in the price of necessities. The recent Ukraine-Russia conflict has added numerous challenges to the already vulnerable food system, particularly the price volatility and uncertainty it has contributed to. The disruption of several supply chains worldwide will severely affect Palestine's agricultural production, leading to dramatic price increases and shortages of many inputs, like fodder and fuel. Poor poultry farming environments and limited access to services have negatively affected small-scale poultry farmers production capacities. In addition, small-scale producers have very limited chances to export agricultural products due to the blockade. Electricity cuts and water scarcity: Gaza’s water crisis has been steadily worsening over recent decades. The area’s only source of fresh water is the Coastal Aquifer, a groundwater basin that runs the length of the Gaza Strip and along part of the Israeli coast. The portion of the aquifer that lies below the Gaza Strip is severely degraded because of continuous over-pumping of groundwater. Every year, more water is extracted from the aquifer than is naturally replenished. The daily lives and livelihoods of Palestinians living in Gaza are severely impacted by these cuts. In addition, climate change exacerbates this crisis, with rising temperatures and sea levels that cause drought, alongside the lack of irrigation water. Smallholder agriculture, on which many people depend, is the sector that is most vulnerable to the effects of climate change. Farmers claim significant decreases in their already fragile yields due to weak extension services and traditional agriculture practices.Poor veterinarian services and animal shelters: Herders in Gaza also continue to struggle with limited grazing areas, poor or non-existent animal shelters, and a lack of veterinarian services, in addition to climate change which has led to intense heatwaves and cold depressions. All these factors have caused re-occurring deaths and illnesses to small ruminants and in poultry.Damaged equipment: The fishing sector has been significantly damaged by the sea blockade (with human rights violations (arrests, confiscation of fishing assets and low access to the fishing zone), pollution and poor resources (limited fishing equipment). Ageing boats, nets, engines, and other equipment are in dire need of rehabilitation or replacement to reactivate their productive capacities and livelihoods, but the high cost of fuel and restrictions on imports have made this very difficult.The above-mentioned needs were identified through 17 focus group discussions (FGDs), conducted by Oxfam and its partner in July 2022, covering all areas of the Gaza Strip with participants from both genders: 7 in the southern governorate farmers, 5 with herders on all Gaza governorates, and 5 with fisherfolk in all Gaza governorates .Although the gender roles in the targeted communities may indicate the near absence of women in these livelihood activities, women's contribution in agricultural work is significant; however, it is often invisible and rarely recognized. Women in these target groups largely perform extensive house tasks, in addition to the livelihood activities, which include helping in farm work such as harvesting. In the herders’ community, women hold the responsibilities of taking care of the livestock, herding the sheep, feeding them, fetching them water, maintaining a clean shelter, milking them, processing the dairy products ,and marketing the products. The fisher community does not have working women; still, women in this community are particularly affected since they are the main caretakers of the house during their spouse’s absence during fishing. Women are also affected by the financial hardships their HHs face and the lack of income, The FGDs contributed define the priorities on this project.

2 - Activities

In order to contribute to protecting and enhancing the resilience and livelihoods of farmers, herders and poultry farmers’ communities in the target communities, a variety of activities will be implemented to strengthen the productive capacities of farmers, herders, poultry farmers and fisherfolks. The proposed project will address the limited access of farmers to agricultural assets and services and enhance their livelihoods through the rehabilitation of productive assets and the needed inputs to support agricultural activities, such as the rehabilitation of greenhouses and the access to water resources (Output 1). The project's activities will also address the needs of herders and poultry farmers through the restoration of their productive capacities, specifically through the adoption to renewable energy in poultry farms, the rehabilitation of animal shelters, and the provision of veterinarian services (Output 2). Moreover, the project will provide support for the rehabilitation of fishing boats and to replace lost fishing equipment. . Moreover, improving the perception of female workers in the agriculture sector will be key in this project in order to raise the awareness of men and women (especially youth) working within the sector on the importance of the division of labor, women’s participation, and agricultural rights. Output 1: Livelihoods of farmers and their families are protected through access to agricultural assets Activity 1.1 Rehabilitation of 60 greenhouses (30 in Khan Younis and 30 in Rafah) through direct implementation by Oxfam and ESDC, considering BBB approach on the rehabilitation work.Activity 1.2 Rehabilitation and reconstruction of 60 rainwater harvesting ponds (40 in Rafah and 20 in Khan Younis). The rehabilitation work will include any needed excavation work and installation of geomembrane layer and fencing of the ponds, in addition to all connections needed for water harvesting from the roof of the greenhouse. Output 2: Restoration of the productive capacity and stability of income for vulnerable herders and poultry farmers Activity 2.1 Rehabilitation of 20 poultry farms in Khan Younis governorate. This activity will entail rehabilitating the structure and installing solar systems aligned with FAO guidelines (the average capacity per farm is 1 kW which can provide the necessary power for light and ventilation). This will support small-scale poultry farmers to cope with climate change impacts, including extreme weather conditions.Activity 2.2 Rehabilitation of 110 small ruminant animal sheds (80 in Rafah and 30 in Khan Younis). This activity will support targeted herders to deal with heatwaves and cold depressions, which are occurring more frequently and more intensely in the last few years due to climate change. The activity will include the rehabilitation of the entire structure of barns with a feeder, water tank and water drinkers.Activity 2.3 Provision of essential veterinarian services to small-scale herders in Khan Younis and Rafah (around 280 HHs), which will include supplying the needed veterinarian medicine and treatments, under the supervision of veterinarian. The veterinarian will also provide the herders with advice on best practices regarding feeding and herd management. This activity will be implemented in close coordination with the MoAOutput 3: Improved livelihoods and food security of vulnerable fisherfolk households through the rehabilitation of productive assets and access to essential equipment and inputs Activity 3.1 Rehabilitation of 30 boat bodies in the Gaza Governorate. The rehabilitation will be supported through direct implementation by Oxfam and ESDC considering BBB approach on the rehabilitation work The majority of needed materials for boat rehabilitation is labeled as “dual use” by the Israeli Authorities making it overpriced on the local market, but the quantity needed are available on the local market for rehabilitation of the proposed number of boats. Activity 3.2 Provision of the most needed equipment to 200 vulnerable fisherfolk. This in-kind distribution will include drift nets, long fishnets, lighting systems, cork, strings, ropes, hooks, and plastic boxes, which will allow fishermen to better manage their fishing practices and replace their lost equipment. The selection of beneficiaries will prioritize those fishers who have faced direct confrontations, injuries or confiscations while engaging in their livelihood activities – specifically those cases which have caused the beneficiaries to stay without work for a long period of time.Oxfam will collect disaggregated data by sex, age and disability for all project beneficiaries. Oxfam has provided training on safe programming to its partner staff working in its humanitarian programme, particularly in the field and those responsible for cash and voucher assistance. It will ensure that risks related to access of cash are continuously analyzed and mitigated.

3 - Indicators

Based on Oxfam’s Common Global Approach to MEAL and Social Accountability (CAMSA), the project will integrate a comprehensive Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) framework. CAMSA is an approach based on minimum requirements for MEL and Social Accountability and is aligned to Oxfam’s program quality efforts, providing guidance, tools and methodologies for various topics such as gender analysis and assessment of gender outcomes. Oxfam and its partners are committed to the EU Data Protection rules represented by the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and will enforce Oxfam and partners’ staff members to strictly consider its rules in all data processing and beneficiaries' database, including accountability mechanisms. Oxfam is also committed to participatory MEAL in which partners, service providers, project stakeholders and beneficiaries have active and significant involvement in monitoring and evaluation actions through the continuous and regular consultation in the entire project cycle. All targeted groups, project beneficiaries and stakeholders are considered as influencers to the design, implementation and results of all monitoring actions, including needs assessments FGDs, baseline/end line surveys, project reviews, and evaluations. Monitoring of project actions will be conducted at two levels: Quality Monitoring: Oxfam’s MEAL staff will lead the design of the project's logical framework, theory of change, MEAL plan and monitoring, project internal and external evaluations, and learning. The MEAL framework will promote gender sensitive and results-oriented MEAL practices, whilst ensuring accountability towards the affected populations, throughout project implementation.Technical Monitoring: Oxfam FSVL technical team and partners will ensure that the quality of implementation is in line with technical standards, action plan and budget, as well as baseline and end-line data collection. Oxfam and partners will conduct direct implementation of the project's actions with day-to-day follow up by their field teams and experts. Oxfam has designed specific monitoring mechanisms for food security programs which consist of the following monitoring actions:1. Targeting HHs verification: It aims to ensure that all selected households fit with the selection criteria, updating their contact information through which they will receive the payment related information.2. Baseline Assessment: To identify the pre-intervention status of the project's outputs and indicators. In regards to the baseline methodology, the main tool for data collection is the Household Questionnaire, which is a structured questionnaire that seeks for quantitative and qualitative data to measure the following indicators: - The average livelihood Coping Strategy Index (LCSI) based on the cash working group (CWG) agreed criteria (None, Stress, Crises, emergency). Baseline: 80% of HHs, either crises or emergency; Target: 40% of the HHs crises or emergency - The Food Consumption Score (FCS) (Poor, Borderline, Acceptable); Baseline: 80% poor and borderline scores, Target: less than 10% poor, and more than 50% Acceptable- % of households to have recovered access to income and effective livelihoods; Baseline: 0, Target: 60%- # of HHs and individuals who received inputs and services for their livelihood projects, including greenhouse farmers, poultry farmers, herders, fisherfolks; Target of 420 HHs (2352 individuals, 1192 male, 1160 female) - # of greenhouses rehabilitated; target of 60 greenhouses - # of m3 of water storage rehabilitated or provided; target of 6000 m3- # kW of renewable power supply systems; target of 20 kW - # of boats rehabilitated or equipped; target of 30 boats - # of livestock and poultry farms/sheds rehabilitated; target of 130 (110 small ruminant and 20 poultry)- # of poultry farms to be rehabilitated by the project end; target of 20 farms- # of small ruminate herders reported reduced lambing mortality; target of 280 HHs (1568 individual 795M and 773F)- # of fisherfolk who report improved income; target of 430 HHs 3. End-line Assessment: the endline assessment aims to identify the post-intervention status of the project's outputs and indicators. The end-line methodology will use the same tools of the baseline, a Household Questionnaire, to collect post-intervention data. 4. Learning Event: The final learning event workshop will be conducted to present and disseminate the key findings of the assessment actions, including the baseline, PDM, HH verification, payment verification, and the end-line. The presentations will be “recommendation focused” on both policies and procedures of the social transfer programs and modalities in Gaza. Feedback Mechanism: Oxfam’s Accountability, Feedback, and Response Mechanism (Your Word Counts) provides wider access to diverse vulnerable groups to raise their thoughts, positive or negative feedback, and minor or major complaints at project implementation level. This system uses Information Communication Technology (ICT) for receiving feedback, either through an online link, verbal feedback or recording complaints received through other channels; such as the free helpline, text messages, Missed Calls, and feedback boxes. The system automatically refers feedback to relevant program staff and is monitored by Oxfam’s MEAL Officer. However, complaints related to safeguarding, GBV, misconduct, or fraud, will be referred to Oxfam’s Country Director and Oxfam’s Global Safeguarding Team, and will be treated with confidentiality to ensure privacy. Moreover, media and communication tools will be utilized during public awareness sessions to raise the targeted population’s awareness on safe and responsive feedback tools. However, all received feedback and complaints will be reported and managed by utilizing the ICT tool. Furthermore, minor or major issues will be followed by programmatic action and response to complaints providers.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Oxfam's Accountability, Feedback, and Response Mechanism (Your Word Counts) is an accountability mechanism that provides broader access for diverse vulnerable groups to raise their thoughts, positive or negative feedback and minor or major complaints during project implementation. This system uses information communication technology (ICT) to receive online feedback through verbal feedback or recording complaints received through other channels, such as helplines and feedback boxes. The system automatically refers feedback received to relevant program staff and is monitored by Oxfam's MEAL Officer. However, complaints related to safeguarding, misconduct, or fraud will be referred to Oxfam's Country Director and safeguarding team in line with Oxfam's confidentiality policies and reporting, thus will not be recorded by the system. Field supervisors will be handling inquiries and operational complaints from beneficiaries by making their numbers available to all beneficiaries. This mechanism will help resolve issues in the field. All feedback and complaints will be handled responsibly following Oxfam Responsible Data Policy which ensures its confidentiality to protect the identity of those providing data, unless otherwise outlined and agreed to in the informed consent. The result of the feedback collected during the project will be used to address any potential concerns of beneficiaries/stakeholders, to inform decision-making and improve response, and to increase accountability to affected populations. Additionally, relevant lessons learned will be taken into consideration in the planning and designing of future projects. Affected populations’ feedback will be documented and analyzed, which may strengthen strategic choices in programmatic work; for example, change the type of assistance provided or advocate for a change in donors’ funding allocation. These lessons learned will be shared with all Food Security Sector stakeholders

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The action, in alignment with Oxfam programming parameters, will be premised on the grounds that the lives and dignity of all women and men beneficiaries, both direct and indirect, must be protected. The main humanitarian standards and protection principles will be given careful consideration, especially the do-no-harm principle, to ensure that project activities do not present any harm to the targeted population by facilitating access to essential protection needs. This is particularly important in the Gaza context, where social and political instability and conflict are evident in many areas with conservative protection norms. Explicit plans to reduce risks and to competently mitigate and respond to any incidents, including safeguarding training (i.e., on sexual harassment, discrimination and exploitation, and child protection) will be elaborated by Oxfam, monitored and adjusted if the context changes. These will address and take into account risks like COVID-19, risks related to safe programming, safeguarding, and potential gender specific risks. The Safeguarding focal point will carry out a refresher session for the project team on the overall protection principles, equipping them with the knowledge and tools to conduct a risk analysis and mitigation measures.As Oxfam is committed to following a participatory and inclusive approach, all activities have been designed in close coordination and full engagement with the target communities. The targeted communities will also be involved in the implementation and monitoring of all activities that aim to respond to vulnerability in all its forms, ensuring its reach to all marginalized people (including children, youth, older people, people with disabilities, ethnic groups and others who are marginalized due to their social status).Specifically, this project will work towards:Participation: We will systematically engage with all affected people and encourage active and meaningful participation of the most marginalized, including people with disabilities, to ensure that their views and capacities are reflected and built into all aspects of the response including assessment, design, delivery and monitoring and evaluation.Data: We will collect, fully disaggregate, and utilize data for different population groups in order to provide robust evidence to design, revise and learn from programming that is reflective of and appropriate for the people and needs identified.Oxfam has already carried out assessments through previous projects, resulting in learning and applying corrective measures during the implementation of the project. Regarding the protection of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), Oxfam has a designated PSEA team, including integrity and safeguarding focal points, as well as confidential counsellors trained on PSEA, that is responsible for ensuring that all field staff comply with Oxfam’s SEA policy. Oxfam’s PSEA focal point is trained as a trainer of trainers by the UN PSEA Network, which is expected to have a greater role in further enhancing awareness on the PSEA and improving current mechanisms and systems in partner organizations.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Project community committees (with equitable gender and age representation) will be established at the community level to ensure their direct engagement with targeted communities and beneficiaries throughout the intervention. This will enhance community engagement and reduce any bias in selecting, targeting beneficiaries and service distribution. Moreover, the project design has considered gender and protection principles by emphasizing the participation of women in the project’s committees. The project will promote community engagement through:- Orientation sessions to share the project’s objective and activities with community committees. - Dissemination of designated feedback mechanisms designed under this project, under both Oxfam and ESDC’s responsibilities.- The use of accountability community engagement mechanisms: dissemination of the beneficiaries’ selection criteria, which will focus on scoring the most vulnerable and affected groups (small-scale farmers, having livelihood activities as the sole source of income, HH protection concerns, etc.). The community engagement mechanisms will also provide clarity, orientation of the selection process, and the announcement to communities about planned activities and procedures to receive benefits.- Further development of Oxfam's existing feedback mechanisms to improve accessibility and appropriateness of these mechanisms to the specific target groups. - Safe programming training for partner field and project staff to ensure the continuous identification of risks and response plans throughout the project cycle.
