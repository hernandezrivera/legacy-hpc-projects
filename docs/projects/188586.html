Project Summary

This project aims to enhance the accessibility of conflict-affected children in NES in Kisreh sub-district, and in NWS in Afrin sub-district to quality, safe, and equitable non-formal education. The project will target children (7-17 years) who have dropped out or at risk of dropping out of schools with NFE programs and children (3-7 years) with Early childhood Education (ECE).
The planned approach of implementation is face-to-face, and in case of imposing curfew by the authorities in the area/s of implementation due to covid-19 outbreak, the modality will be shifted to remote learning.
The project has 3 objectives, that are aligned with the HRP Education sector objectives, which are:
1- Increase safe and equitable availability and access to in-person non-formal education for crisis-affected children and youth.
2- Enhanced quality of non-formal education for children and youth.
3- Education system is better able to support the education response.
The activities that will be implemented in both NES/ Kisreh and NWS/Afrin are similar including the following:
1- Establishing and running 2 TLSs, 1 in each of Afrin and Kisreh communities, where the NFE, ECE, and parenting programs/PTAs meeting will be implemented.
2- Undertaking light rehabilitation to 10 educational facilities where NFE and PTAs will be implemented. These 10 facilities include 2 TLSs and 8 schools. 7 of these educational facilities out of 10 will be in Kisreh sub-district, and 3 will be in Afrin sub-district, i.e.:
- In Kisreh: there will be 1 TLS and 6 schools
- In Afrin: there will be 1 TLS and 2 schools
3- Support the 10 educational facilities with operational cost, IT equipment, solar system for lighting, sterilizers, and PPE for educational staff and beneficiaries.
4- Conducting 10-days Back to Learning (BTL) Campaigns in each of Afrin and Kisreh in order to introduce the project activities and invite the children’s caregivers to register their children to the NFE and ECE programs, note that the BTL campaign will include conducting diagnostic tests for children registered in order to be able to select the most in need children for NFE programs.
5- Implementing ALP and remedial NFE programs for children dropped out of schools and these children will undertake pre and post-tests to measure the impact of the ALP and remedial program implemented and assess their educational improvement.
In average, 11 classrooms will be utilized in each of the 8 schools (6 in Kisreh and 2 in Afrin), 10 for the implementation of NFE programs, and 1 classroom for admin; therefore, 10 teachers (1 teacher per classroom) will be providing NFE in each of the 8 schools during the afternoon shift.
While in the 2 TLSs, the space is smaller, so 4 rooms in each TLS will be used to implement the NFE programs but during the morning and afternoon shift, so 8 teachers will be recruited for each of the TLSs, 4 for the morning shift and 4 for the afternoon shift.
6- Implementing ECE for children (3-7 years) using 2 approaches: static-based in the 2, and through community-based outreach approach in order to reach a wider scope of beneficiaries geographically (those living in distant locations from the TLSs) and physically (EX: people with disabilities facing barriers to come to the TLSs).
7- Providing transportation for children with disabilities (CwD) from and to the educational facilities in order to attend the NFE (ALP & remedial) and other activities in the schools and TLSs.
8- Establishing 2 PTAs, 1 in each of Afrin and Kisreh communities, and conducting regular meetings with these PTAs in order to enhance communication between educational personnel and caregivers and get their feedback to improve the services provided.
9- Distributing monthly incentives and building the capacity of 104 Educational personnel on EIE and INEE, school and classroom management, SEL, PSS, and PFA.
10- Providing the children attending the NFE programs with school bags, stationery, and curriculums.

4 - Needs

NWS/Afrin:Afrin sub-district is at 4th education severity scale according to the 2022 HNO, with a total of 75,223 PIN including 72,796 children (3-17) and 2,427 educational personnel.Bahar conducted assessment recently in Afrin, and it showed that only in Afrin camps, there are no non-formal education services provided in the assessed locations, even though there are high numbers of children that are out of school (the percentage is as high as 95% in some sites as suggested by KIs) or at risk of dropping out of formal education. This is linked to the fact that where education is available, some caregivers are unable to afford education materials (or other expenses) or/and there is lack of awareness on the importance of education, while where Education is not available, the issue is related to access. In addition, PSS services are severely lacking in Afrin.NES/Kisreh:Kisreh is at 4th severity scale (SS) according to the Education sector HNO 2022, with a total PIN of 46,249 including 44,757 children (3-17) and 1,492 educational personnel.Bahar has ongoing Education projects in Kisreh SD and has conducted recent assessment to 38 schools in 6 communities in Kisreh and it showed that none of these schools are supported by neither formal nor non-formal education, as well as, all of them need heavy or light rehab, note that according to the schools' records and data there are more than 4500 students who dropped out during the past 2 years.100% of the BNFs are in 4th SS areas.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Bahar has a Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA policy that usually is attached to each employee’s job contract clarifying all the related procedures. Bahar will ensure the field teams attend PSEA\safeguarding training and refreshing sessions provided by Bahar internally or externally. Bahar’s PSEA focal points responsibility is to ensure training is delivered to the project staff, informative materials (either soft copies in case of online or in-place approach of implementation) are distributed to both staff and beneficiaries, and updating and develop the PSEA policies including the complaints pathway/reporting mechanism regularly. The MEAL team will monitor if any danger is threatening the safety of the beneficiaries and will report findings in order to take the appropriate measures. Bahar team will also inform the community, particularly men and youth, about the objective of the project and its aim in order to mitigate exposing women and girls to any potential harm or violence.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: COVID-19 pandemic imposes lack of students’ access to schools due to curfew restrictions, home lockdown etc... Mitigation: Baharadopt other implementation modalities, including distant learning or SLP programs, which require less physical contact between students and children.Risk 2: Bab Alhawa border is closed due to security reasons which cause lack of materials in the market. Mitigation: Sending the needed resources when the border is functioning., Coordinating with authorities to be informed before the closing of the border., Checking for alternatives inside Syria, Using another border crossing.Risk 3: Host community rejects Bahar’s presence in schools where NFE programs are conducted (due to a lack of understanding of selection criteria, or other factors). Mitigation: regular discussion with parents, teachers, social counsellors; clearly communicated and transparent selection criteria for NFE education, as well as the objectives of the NFE programs; close coordination and engagement with local educational authorities.Risk 4: students might not go to school due to long distances. Mitigation: ensure BTLs are implemented in communities’ levels and they include good information sharing, ensure that residency information is collected in the registration process and distance is one of the selection criteria, consider having transportations service as a project activity.
