Project Summary

In 2023, with strong expertise and lesson learned developed throughout the years, COOPI aims to continue to respond to the main protection concerns identified throughout protection program implementation and need assessment with local communities, IDPs and returnees. The program is a continuity of the project approved in 2022 (HSYR22-PRO-188024-1) and aims to respond to the strategic Objectives 1/3/4/5 established by the protection sector for the 2022/2023 strategy. COOPI response is based on a community-based and participatory approach involving the affected population in actively participating to the decision-making process, focusing on the establishment and/or support of existing community committees, that, with the guidance of COOPI staff, is working on the identification of protection risks within their communities to address them appropriately. To reach an equal access for boys and girls (0-17) to quality child protection interventions, COOPI is supporting community-based child protection responses for girls and boys in targeted locations, including provision of MHPSS services for girls, boys and caregivers, parenting programs, awareness-raising and establishment and/or support of existing committees to ensure the community’s active participation to prevent and respond to child protection issues. The combination of these activities aims to develop a long-term positive practice through the targeted populations with effects on the whole community. Psychosocial assistance will also continue to be provided in order to cope with stress relief/PTSD. For women and children quality and life-saving response services, including case management, will continue to be a priority in the response, as the needs are assessed raising. CLIs in each locality targeted by the project will be also promoted and implemented as more efficient tool for protection risk’s prevention and response. In addition, COOPI intends to integrate innovative protection-related activities, such as Sports for Development, which promotes sport as a
change children's lives by helping break down barriers, promote participation and inclusion and alter attitudes. Cash for Protection, which will both act as a responsive and remedial action, aimed at preventing, reducing or mitigating exposure to protection risks, or limiting the effects of violations on victims. This will be considered as a welcoming initiative especially to the areas where COOPI is entering for the first time and assessed as severe, where the lack of services and negligence of the PiN contributes to critical protection risks. Finally, one of the main elements that COOPI will dedicated a great importance to is the operating of Community Centers, this will further centralize the services in the hard-to-reach-areas and will be a valuable asset to initiate protection activities from. Static centers will be accompanied by mobile outreach, based on COOPI strong experience in providing protection services through mobile team interventions in order to guarantee assistance also to the most vulnerable populations in rural, underserved areas. By providing a hybrid outreach approach, together with the extensive panel of protection services available, COOPI aim to strengthen its comprehensive area-based approach by reinforcing internal and external referral mechanisms to other sectors' (FSA,ERL, WASH, Shelter etc) services available in the same locations
Intervening in multi- sectoral response, COOPI will ensure protection mainstreaming across all sectors, with particular attention to safe distribution modalities, gender, age and diversity approach and focusing on highly vulnerable groups in the definition of eligibility criteria and in targeting the assistance. Moreover, the SEA will constitute a cross-sectoral priority to be addressed and eradicate, while ensuring COOPI staff and third parties to be trained, to recognize and to report such violations, to do no harm and to protect people affected by the crisis.

3 - Needs

In terms of severity of needs, out of the 17 sub-districts targeted, 2 are ranked 5, 11 are ranked 4, 3 are ranked 3 and 1 was downgraded to 2.Through previous field assessments in the targeted locations and ongoing/past protection programs implemented, specific needs have been detected in the areas targeted, reflecting also the findings of newly updated HNO 2023.Particular protection risks such as child marriage, child labor, exposure to explosive ordnance and movement restriction imposed by spousal, family, community or due to security threat and lack/loss of civil documentation have been identified as increased in the last months, linked to the deterioration of the economic situation in Syria. In addition, it was observed an increased number of vulnerable women-headed HHs, whose capacity to meet needs and enjoy basic rights is hampered by socio/cultural norms, exposing them to further GBV risks or heavily relying on humanitarian assistance. Moreover, survivors of GBV encounter numerous barriers to seek protection, from lack of services available such as safe houses, to cultural reasons, and lack of information about their rights. Presence of EO and movement restrictions further limit the chances of community recovery. Moreover, it was identified that the population struggle to access quality care, especially for PWDs and/or chronic diseases in rural areas and protection services responding to widespread neglection of elderly and PwDs, discrimination and social stigma.

4 - Implementation of activities

To address the needs identified above, the most vulnerable are identified according to internally established SoPs on beneficiaries targeting and selection criteria, that has been adapted to the constraints experienced in the Syrian context. Activities are carried through mobile units to provide protection services in hard-to- reach areas and/or through static facilities and include all CP, PSS, GBV programming package. Protection support is provided firstly through individual assistance (CP case management, PSS individual and group counseling) while COOPI will continue advocating for the implementation of GBV case management, currently extremely challenging in Syria. Community mobilization and sensitization to protection risks prevention and response is ensured by CP, GBV and PSS awareness sessions, EORE, as well as by the establishment of community-based protection structures (committees) and the implementation of community-led initiatives. These activities are in place to meet urgent needs and to increase capacity building (through the communities for the communities) adopting a culturally and socially appropriate approach to generate ownership, participation, sustainability, using local capacities.Structured assistance is also delivered through parenting and life-skills programs, and COOPI-specific physicals, speech and hearing therapy services targeting PwDs, and Victim Assistance, complemented by material assistance in-kind (i.e., MIKA, dignity kits) and cash tranfers.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Exploitation or abuse perpetrated by staff members or service providers when delivering assistance. LL: Medium. Impact: High. MM: Establish formal and informal complaint and feedback mechanisms in each area of intervention; Provide staff with training on Code of Conduct and PSEA, according to COOPI Global PSEA policy in place; Ensure the proper communication to the communities about the gratuity of the services; SoPs are in place for submission and receipt of complaints, reporting, investigation and victim assistance. PSEA Focal Points have been nominated and trained in COOPI sub-offices, Syria CO and HQ and all staff members receive training and information on preventing and responding to acts of SEA. When intervening in new areas, COOPI nominates and trains a dedicated PSEA FPs in charge of supervising the use/dissemination of PSEA reporting tools among communities, staff and third parties. COOPI sensitizes the beneficiaries, requiring them to report any SEA cases in written (COOPI reporting tool) or at presence of the PSEA FP, that guarantees anonymity and confidentiality. At the HQ level, it is available a dedicated e-mail address: help@coopi.org. Monitoring actions are envisaged to check the process of reporting misconduct through a register. To maximizing risk reduction, all agreements with partners, suppliers and staff contracts make specific reference to COOPI Code of Ethics, which violation constitutes reason for terminating the partnership/collaboration.
