Project Summary

Following the devastating Beirut blast on Tuesday August 4th and its consequences on hospitals and healthcare in Lebanon, there are high risks preventing childhood cancer patients from accessing adequate treatment due to the following factors:
• Destruction of hospitals and facilities that accommodate patients
• Destruction of medications’ stock, medical supplies & equipment, in addition to the potential shortage in the coming months.
• Ongoing security concerns in the country
• Shortage of funds to cover treatment costs (already intensified due to the prevailing Lebanese economic & financial crisis)
To mitigate these risks, and despite all the difficulties and hardships surrounding us, the Children's Cancer Center of Lebanon (CCCL) Chair and Board of Trustees have taken the lead and pledged to support all kids with cancer in Lebanon.
The CCCL will play the facilitator role coordinating & providing solutions for the doctors and patients who can’t get treatment at the ruined hospitals; and expands support to medications & medical supplies.
This CCCL Relief Response will ensure pediatric cancer patients in Lebanon have access to adequate treatment and care until end 2020.
A budget of 200,000 USD is needed to cover the treatment of 10 kids with cancer in Lebanon until end 2020.

1 - Needs

The project will address the funding gap which will ensure and cover adequate cancer treatment to 10 kids in Lebanon for the coming 6 months. Treatment can include chemotherapy, radiotherapy, surgeries, medications, psycho-social support, and palliative care. Following the devastating Beirut blast, a number of major hospitals are left damaged risking access to treatment for children with cancer, as well as medications & medical supplies. In addition to the deteriorating economic and financial situation, which leaves a majority of citizens unable to cover treatment charges. The kids supported through this project will be newly diagnosed with cancer or continuing treatment where their parents don't have the resources to cover costs. Given the nature of cancer where immediate care and treatment is needed to enhance recovery & survival chances, prompt care to patients is needed. Based on recent statistics by the Ministry of Public Health, is it estimated that there are around 450 new diagnoses of childhood cancer in Lebanon. Prior to the Beirut blast, the CCCL covered around 40% of kids with cancer. To mitigate the high risks on childhood cancer patients following the blast, the CCCL Chair & Board of Directors have pledged to support all kids with cancer in Lebanon for the coming 6 months, in cooperation with all hospitals & pediatric oncologists in Lebanon. Having a non-discriminatory enrollment policy, the CCCL supports patients on a first-come first -serve basis- budget permitting- and relies on the clinical assessment of pediatric oncologists and the medical & clinical review and accurate diagnostic tests done at the AUBMC.The ensure support to all kids with cancer in Lebanon for the coming 6 months, the CCCL is also working with local and international partners and NGOs to secure the needed budgets.

2 - Activities

The CCCL will cover needy patients in all hospitals across Lebanon where a referral hotline (76-371717) has been communicated & publicized for patients' and doctors' use. The CCCL will facilitate and cover treatment costs for kids with cancer in all hospitals across Lebanon, reference medical invoices which are also monitored & audited by the CCCL Patients’ Relations (PRD) Team which includes an experienced registered nurse & budget analyst. Kids are referred to the CCCL for approval of coverage by their treating pediatric oncologist who has already done the clinical assessment and shares with the CCCL: the patient's profile (name, age, address, nationality, etc), diagnosis, treatment plan, and prognosis. Upon that, the partner hospital sends CCCL the estimated cost of treatment and CCCL PRD team assesses and compares it to the available budget, and accordingly approves coverage. Coverage of patients is communicated through an electronic coverage letter issued by the CCCL to hospitals detailing the treatment, procedures, admissions, medications, etc... covered. Each hospital admission is reassessed by the CCCL and coverage is issued accordingly. The CCCL disburses payments to the hospitals based on invoices from the hospitals which are reconciled with issued coverage letters by the CCCL team and then audited with the support of a Third Party Administrator when needed. In the case of over-charging, the CCCL sends back the invoices to the hospitals with the notes and awaits a new correct invoice.
