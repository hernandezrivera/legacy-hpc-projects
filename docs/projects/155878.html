Project Summary

Coinciding with HF’s vision of food security and livelihoods support for crisis affected area. This project aims to increase the resilience and improve the livelihoods of the most vulnerable populations and crises-affected in seventeen communities in Afrin and Ariha districts, where IHR currently have access to.
This project aims to protect the livelihoods’ assets of the livestock section and improve drought resistance by rehabilitating 30 km of the irrigation channels in the region, thereby improving the access of irrigation water by farmers.
• Establish two mobile veterinary clinics; one to give veterinary service for 500 animals breeders in Afrin, Marata(joaaik), Shawarghet Eljoz, Bablit, Qarzihel communities of Afrin district, and the other to serve 1000 animals breeders in Balyun, Kansafra, Mhambal, Farkya, Northern Laj, Ora Qabli – Edwan, Sararif, Banin, Moataf, Shinan, Thaheriya, Saraj communities of Ariha district. Totally, 1500 beneficiaries of small animals’ breeders for maximum number of owned animals (25 sheep & goat, two cow and 50 poultry). Each beneficiary will be visited monthly (for ten months) by technical team to provide the required treatment, if any, explain the best practices about animals’ care.
• Conduct vaccination campaign for 50,000 sheep against Entrotoxemia, Smallpox, Aphthous fever and Pasteurellosis diseases in same intervention locations.
• Rehabilitate 30 Km of irrigation canals in Afrin community of Afrin sub-district. Using 100 worker (cash for work activity) and some equipment and tools, the soil, dust, waste and grass will be removed from canals within 30 calendar days (26 actual days) and approximately 52 pipes will be installed on those canals to facilitate movement. Each beneficiary will receive 5 $ per work day.
Moreover, empowering small breeders by providing the essential veterinary services can ensure keeping their livestock are alive and in good health situation. That can be reflected in the household’s income by improving the productivity level of milk and meat. Therefore, the availability of those basic materials in the market will facilitate access to it, by most people in the same community. As the awareness sessions aim to build livestock breeders with strong livestock knowledge background. To ensure sustainability of project, the IHR will handover the mobile clinic assets for the local councils of Ariha and Afrin and build their capacity to be able of running that veterinary services, with continue supervising for five months after finishing the project period.
The medicines is selected according global standards and according to livestock working groups’ recommendations to reach for the best quality, and full adherence to the specified amounts, which reduces the negative impact that may result from overuse of medicines. moreover, the vaccines schedule will agree with livestock working group’s recommendation.
Meanwhile, rehabilitation of 30 km of irrigation canals improve the access to irrigation water for more than 5000 hectares, which owned by no less of 1500 farmers. therefore, improve the productivity due to diversity in planted crops. furthermore, to ensure the sustainability of work those canals, IHR will create irrigation committee from the community members and build their capacity about canals management, cost recovery and the best methods of keeping it active. As the used tools in cleaning works will be handed them to enable service continues. In general, the number of farmers who have abandoned their land or depended on rainfed agriculture for limited crops will decrees, which improve the food security for more than 1500 households.

Needs

In April 2019, IHR team need assessment which included 20 communities in Afrin(5 communities) and Ariha(15 communities),The data collection process lasted from 4/5/7 Apr 2019.6 IHR field team (male and female) collected data through (KIIs) with members of the local council agriculture committees, FGDs with farmers, filed Observations of the field team. The sample was 85% male and 15 % female.the population of the five communities in Afrin was 51,511 people (8,585 families) 51% of population are males, 49 % females, children represent 41% of the communities' population.53% (27300 individuals) of the communities' population are productive. Elderlies are about 4 % (1,859 individuals) of population. IDPs represent were 27% about 14,052 individuals. Finally, about 6% of communities' population was people with disabilities. While the population of the fifteen targeted communities in Ariha was 36,135 individuals (6,023 families) including 45 % IDPs (4,786 individuals), 45% of the population are males, 51% females, children represent about 40%. The productive category is about 55% (19874 individuals) of the communities' population, Elderly represent 7% (2476 individuals) of these communities. 39% of interviewees' responses mentioned that the most vulnerable families were families headed by women, 35% families with disabled members, 13% of them were families with infants, young children or children in school, 7% were families have one breadwinner.The main sources of income for people in the assessed communities were 24 % production and sale of agricultural crops, 24% breeding and sale of livestock products and about 23% unskilled workers. While for women the main income-generating activities were 41% Breeding and sale of livestock products, 33% were food industry, 13% were household occupations while 13% of women did not work. 88% of HHs had limited access to appropriate income. During the last 3 months, the percent of HHs who did not have fixed income were 53%(29% of them were HHs who headed by women), 35% of HHs' monthly incomes were about 50 $ (19% of them were HHs who headed by women)The livestock number in Afrin were 11800 sheep, 3200 goats, 120 cows and 8,500 poultry. In Ariha, they were 42,190 sheep, 7,389 goats, 2,042 cows, 51,700 poultry. The average number of livestock wondered per breeder were about 14 sheep on Ariha and12 in Afrin.37% of breeder were women, as for families that headed by women, 50% of these families depended on livestock care. Women expressed losing in the livestock due to high cost against economic return, less access to veterinary services besides the absence of veterinary extension unit made the animals in case of continuous illness. As a result, many of families were forced to sell their livestock. The major constraints that affected on livestock production were the insufficient coverage of veterinary services and high cost of fodder. Respondents referred to the absence of the veterinary extension units which provide them with veterinary outreach, since 78% of respondents preferred veterinary outreach sessions to be practical through field visits, and 22% preferred it as theoretical topics. while for women the recommendations include helping them get to the outreach sessions by providing childcare, transport and female instructors,Families relied on agriculture as the main resource of their income, which is considered the most important economic activity in the whole areas in Ariha and Afrin. The cultivated land areas in Afrin were about 263,000 dunom, and 42,340 dunom in Ariha. Most women were engaged in agricultural and food industry activities such as hand harvesting, seeding/sowing and weeding. The main irrigation sources in Afrin were Irrigation channels, while in Ariha wells and rain. Those canals in Afrin were key channel with of 6 to10 Km length and sub-channels. All of them were not clean, which led to decrees the irrigated area if compared with the previous years.

Protection Risk Analysis

The planned locations to target are under two different regions, politically and militarily. Which impose different transactions suit with each one. Whereas, Afrin district is under Turkish control, while Ariha district is under HTS. The security situation in Afrin is safe and stable with full flexibility of movement by IHR team due to formal registration from Turkish government. Furthermore, the access in Idleb governorate is available for IHR team, since the IHR has office and registered by local authorities, as well their humanitarian intervention exceeds the five years in the area. But from time to time the area are facing airstrikes, which lead to suspend the activities for several days to keep the beneficiaries and staff safe. the road between those area is open and under control from both sides, but sometimes a tension happened among them which lead to close the roads for several days. For that, the IHR will hire the project staff from same targeted area to avoid any risk may happened on team movements during implementation. Same scenario will be implemented for procurement process, the IHR will advertise on tenders and contract with vendors of same intervention area, except some items that will be procured from Turkey like vaccines. This aims to ensure the access of all items to the targeted location easily with minimal risk. For items purchased from Turkey, the contract terms will ensure reach for IHR office, while the Syrian items, the contract terms will confirm supplier responsibility to drop the items down in the distribution or redemption locations. For beneficiaries’ selection, the process will extend for several days which is clear in the advertisement, that aims to avoid the crowding and reduce the risk resulted of gathering people for the minimum level. The security instructions will be clear for all field staff to implement in case of an emergency change in the situation. As the security standards will be implemented in the field to ensure the safety for staff and beneficiaries, and evacuation if necessary. the distribution process will be safer since the mobile clinic and vaccination campaigns will be by field visits to beneficiaries’ houses, meanwhile, the workers under cash for work activity will be divided to small groups to facilitate the following the activities’ progressing and avoid keeping in one point. During community engagement, the IHR will try to create a community representative board and build his capacity to be active and took his responsibilities towards community population. This process aim to prevent any potential interfering from armed groups or local authorities in the registration or distribution process, since the CRB,s members will include on representatives of most categories of society, therefore they will have capability of facing those kind of challenges. As well they can be link between the organization and beneficiaries to transfer opinions and recommendations on implementation stages.

We assume that no less of 30 % of selected breeders will be female. We assume that none of the selected beneficiaries will leave their place of residence to received the planned treatment.
