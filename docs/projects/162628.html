Project Summary

This project is designed to contribute to Child protection sub-sector strategic objectives SO1, SO2 and SO3 proposed for Abujebuha, Um-Durein, Toladi and Abukersola localities in South Kordofan State. it is expected to improve child protection services in a coordinated structure to prevent and response child protection cases;
Unaccompanied and separated children, at other at risk children identified, documented, traced and reunified, or put in Interim or longer-term alternative care placement with regular care-givers, Stakeholder men and women capacity and resilience built in child protection to prevent and response through community based mechanisms, Coordination strengthened and demobilized children, survivors of S/GBV, exploitation, abuse and neglect supported.
ASSIST will sign memorandum of understanding (MOU) and sign agreement with technical departments at the Ministry of Social Welfare women and child affairs, as well as coordination with cluster coordinators, community based organizations (CBOs), and other relevant partners to provide UASC and other children at risk with needed child protection services.
ASSIST will set child protection benchmarks to collect, analyze and use sex-disaggregated data, 53% of staff will be women, 60% of staff will complete basic gender training and learning sessions, and 100% of the project reports will have specific gender results and lessons learned.
Secondly, we will ensure that 100% of the project activities have resource allocation to achieve IASC Gender with Age marker code 4(M) by the end of 2020. ASSIST will also have financial tracking mechanism to quantify funds disbursement for promoting gender especial girls, and women empowerment.
3% of the project grant will be cash based disbursement for foster families and UASC reunified with their parents as Unconditional and unrestricted transfers as it gives the foster families, children and parents chance to chose and prioritize their needs. In addition, UASC and other children at risk that will be referred to child protection especially FTR, Psychosocial support, returned to school etc make decision and prioritize their daily needs that meet their protection concerns.
ASSIST will ensure child protection functioning complaints and feedback mechanisms as an accountability to affected beneficiaries who receive assistance through their participation in the project implementation, Monitoring, evaluation accountability and learning (MEAL), and ensure gender and age dimensions are integrated project Plan, and guidance manuals.

1 - Needs

According to Inter-agency assessment conduct in Abujebaha,, Toladi, Umdurein and Abukersola locality in South Kordofan State in Oct-2018 and June-2019 shows that there are cases unaccompanied and separated children, case of domestic violence, child marriage & labor, cases of SGBV and children face additional threats posed by UXOs and ERWs mainly among IDPs, returnees and host communities. Community structures, that include parenthoods, relatives and neighborhood care, formal and informal schools, Religious institutions which use to contribute to child protection broadly affected by violence, conflict and displacement in recent years, high rate of poverty among IDPs, returnees and host communities badly affected protective environment for children. All 4 localities have child protection issues that include children safety and security, family separation, women issues, limited social services welfare for UASC, and poorly functional community based child protection Network and Family tracing and reunification (FTR) services. In addition, most IDPs have not properly documented figures with gender and age disagregrated data for child recruits, undocumented/registered separated and unaccompanied minors and child laborers among others have increased risks for the most vulnerable minors The difficult economic situation resulted to limited livelihoods source coupled with extremely high inflation, lack of cash liquidity, high prices and scarcity of bread and fuel led to dramatic increase in prices to above 370% which have affected children in alternative care/fosters families, reunification and support vulnerable children among with disabilities. The 2 assessments findings indicated that; 67% of child laborers in Toladi, Abujebaha, Umdurein and Abukersola are UASC who are not registered or verified, high number of cases involving children who are conflict with the law and have limited access to Juvenile justice, 3 incident of mines/UXOs reported involving children between 2016-2018, in one village have over 80 UASC are identified but none of them received support and Toladi locality have more than 1000 cases of UASC who are in need of best interest assessment and determination.The need to support children access to National number registration, which is the main reason that prevented from sitting for primary exams and access services, over 1000 UASC need best interest assessment and determination. The communities reflected on the need to address increasing cases of domestic violence, child marriage and child labor as reported in all the assessed locations. As indicated in the assessment, during May and June 2019, twelve cases of SGBV were reported in 3 villages, only 1.3% that is 13 cases of separated and unaccompanied children were placed in alternative families.

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

During design and implementation we will ensure consideration for protective spaces for women and children to provide a safe environment where recreational activities for children, and all basic services eg. WASH. Psychosocial support can take place with confidential, complaints and protection reporting mechanism system has been designed and beneficiaries know where to refer or report incidents of rights violations The day, time, and location of the child protection has been discussed with different community members (age, gender, disability) to determine when/where would be the safest, Working hours during which services are provided are appropriate for all persons, including women, children, and the elderly, the day, time, and location of the Child protection services has been clearly communicated using a variety of approaches to all, including those who may have difficulty accessing information points.Adequate levels of female staff with skills and experience working with women and with children exist, vulnerable individuals who may need assistance eg. pregnant women, chronically ill, disabled) have been identified and prioritized by placing them first lists or placed in separate lines,Measures are in place to distribute supplies to vulnerable individuals and those who are unable to travel, Access roads between where beneficiaries live and centers/referral points or where services are provided are safe, If the services point is far away from the shelters, arrangements have been made to assist people to transport their goods so they are not forced to pay for transport themselves, All recipients know how to use and are able to use the materials they have been provided. Confidentiality and privacy are respected in consultation, counseling or personal information sharing with beneficiaries, Outreach services are available for areas that are far due to geographic difficulties or in insecure areas for beneficiaries to access. A system is in place for regularly finding out about new needs, Monitoring teams are in place to assess, for example, whether service delivery locations are adequate and can be used in a dignified way. All child protection Staff have signed a code of conduct agreement or zero tolerance policy for sexual exploitation and abuse, a confidential complaints and protection reporting mechanism system is in place and beneficiaries know where to refer or report incidents of rights violations. Feedback on the complaints should be regularly be provided to the beneficiaries, beneficiaries have been clearly informed of the cost, if any, of the services or that the services are free
