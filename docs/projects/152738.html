Project Summary

This project aims at building on and deepening ARC's on-going GBV response and prevention interventions in Ulang, Kajo Keji and Kapoeta East Counties. The activities are centered on Women and Girl's Friendly Space (WGFS) programming through complementary funding from other ARC funding sources including South Sudan Humanitarian fund (SSHF) in Kapoeta East County and OFDA in Ulang and Kajo Keji Counties respectively. ARC is running six (6) WGFSs-four in Ulang and one in each County in Kapoeta East and Kajo Keji Counties and plans to use HRP funding mechanism to add one more WGFS in each locations of Kapoeta East and Kajo Keji.

Needs

Five years on from the outbreak of conflict in December 2013, nearly 7.5 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance and protection across the country as a result of armed conflict, intercommunal violence, economic crisis, disease outbreaks and climatic shocks. Following independence in 2011, there were efforts to develop and endorse gender equality laws and policy, most prominently through the Transitional Constitution and Bill of Rights (2011) which guarantees for equality of men and women. However, the escalations of conflict in 2013 and 2016 have significantly undermined the capacity of the nation to progress sustainably in gender equality. The SSHRP (2018) suggests that more than 6.1 million South Sudanese and 304,560 refugees will require some form of protection from violence, abuse, exploitation and violations of fundamental human rights in South Sudan in 2018. Violence and rights violations continue unchecked and have become a persistent reality for civilians. GBV – including rape, sexual assault, domestic violence, forced and early marriage, sexual exploitation and abuse, abduction and other harmful practices – is a widespread, chronic and persistent violation of the basic human rights of girls and women. Particularly vulnerable groups such as children, people with disabilities and older people, suffer the most intense consequences of sustained displacement, violence and lack of access to services. 90 percent of the population does not have access to improved sanitation, half of the population does not have access to improved water sources, open defecation is persistent in 61 percent of settlements in the country, and only 28 percent of the population have access to a borehole within a 30 minutes’ walk which presents GBV risks to women and girls. Strict gender roles and identities, patriarchal authority, women’s low social status, power imbalance, limited access to decision-making and reproductive rights, poverty, inequality in the areas of employment or education, and discriminatory practices are deeply rooted in the fibre of the society and perpetuate gendered power inequalities between men and women (the root cause of GBV), and are exacerbated by the risks and socio-economic changes brought by the conflict. The on-going crisis in South Sudan also has a negative impact on the most vulnerable population’s ability to access sexual and reproductive health services, and also leaves them particularly vulnerable to sexual exploitation and abuse, including rape, and limited freedom of movement among women and girls, distance to health services and cultural impediments including shame and stigma remain barriers to accessing life-saving health care by survivors of sexual violence. Proposed interventions in Ulang, Kapoeta East and Kajo Kejo Counites will seek to provide case management and PSS services to provide support and recovery services to GBV survivors and women and girls at risk. ARC will strengthen WGFS programming by increasing the number of WGFS with one more space in Kapoeta East and Kajo Keji to improve beneficiaries’ access to services.The WGFS will serve as entry points for other services and information including SRHR, nutrition, WASH and so on. Inter-agency referrals for services will be coordinated accordingly while strengthening coordination mechanisms and linkages among partners/clusters to improve the quality of services delivered and ensure the dignity of survivors is protected during the process. Outreach team will organize day to day awareness raising activities to educate the communities on GBV through community approach where local leaders and gatekeepers will be supported to take lead in educating their communities on GBV and other issues affecting them. ARC will recruit 2 Caseworkers and four community mobilizers per location in Kajo Keji and Kapoeta East to support the new activities.

Activities

ARC proposes the following GBV activities in Ulang, Kajo Keji, and Kapoeta East Counties. 1. Comprehensive case management ARC will continue providing case management services in the existing WGFSs. However, in Kajo Keji and Kapoeta East where new WGFS will be established, case management activities will involve setting up systems and procedures by recruiting and training Caseworkers on survivor centered case management approach. ARC will also print case management and GBVIMS tools and orient the staff accordingly. Lockable cabinets will be required for confidential storage of survivors’ information and files. Case management services will be through individual and group counselling sessions. 2. Psychosocial support (PSS)ARC will continue operating Women and Girls Friendly spaces (WGFS) to ensure that females have spaces in which they can feel safe and comfortable. The PSS activities at the WGFS will be guided by UNICEF’s guidelines for WGFS in South Sudan and will include emotional therapy sessions, PSS techniques, and ice-breakers to support bonding and building relationships, self-esteem, self-confidence and sense of self-worth among the participants. Women and girls will have a safe place where they feel physically and emotionally free to express themselves, connect and support each other. 3. Safe referrals, including information dissemination on available servicesARC will deliberately organize weekly information sessions on sexual reproductive health rights, GBV, and nutrition for women and girls. ARC will collaborate with other sectors in identifying specialized personnel to facilitate SRHR sessions at the WGFS to educate women on sexual health, sexual rights, reproductive health and reproductive right and empower them to use these rights. The response team will organize GBV sessions with sex-separate groups of women and girls to discuss various GBV issues. Rape and sexual violence in general will be given special attention due to the life threatening consequences it presents to survivors as well as the importance of accessing Clinical Management of Rape services within 72 hours after the incidence. Referrals will be based on consent and choice of services the survivor wants to access. 4. Women & girls friendly spaces programming, including strengthened integration with child protection to support child survivorsARC plans to establish, equip and operationalize one WGFS per County in Kapoeta East and Kajo Keji Counties while strengthening the one four WGFS in Ulang County. Some of the proposed activities will include but will not be limited to (1) Case management services and referrals, (2) Psychosocial support (PSS) services, (3) Awareness raising on services available, (4) Information dissemination sessions on GBV, WASH, nutrition and reproductive health (5) Open days/unstructured sessions, (6) Skill building activities, and (7) Integration activities with other clusters such as nutrition, NFIS, food, livelihoods and child protection. To integrate CP services within the WGFS, ARC will introduce “children’s corner’ within the WGFS and will equip them with toys and context friendly plays items for young children.WGFS will serve as entry points for women and girls to access other services through coordinated referrals.5. Community awareness raising and sensitization on GBV and women’s rightsProposed GBV response and prevention activities will adhere to global standards and the national priorities as outlined in the GBV Sub-Cluster strategy and validated tools for South Sudan. To prevent and mitigate GBV, ARC promotes community led activism to enable and encourage communities to participate in all activities aimed at preventing violence. ARC will do so by providing guidance and support to women’s associations, community activists, and community-based protection network members in their transition from GBV awareness and learning to proactively designing their own community-based interventions.

Strategic Objective 1

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Strong community engagement and communication is a central output in ensuring accountability toward the affected population. ARC will give attention to all groups in society, with particular consideration to gender, age and those with disabilities through meaningful, informed and effective participation. Emphasis will be put on including feedback mechanism to ensure two-way feedback between humanitarians and the affected populations and better understanding of how ARC will be engaging communities through this project and who they are engaging. ARC will make efforts to provide accessible and timely information on organizational procedures, structures, service available and processes that may impact communities in order to support informed decisions, and engage communities in a dialogue as part of information provision. The target population will be informed and involved in the process of selection of beneficiaries and in defining the operations and WGFSs for cultural acceptability and ownership. In addition, ARC will give feedback to allow communities to contribute in designing, implementation, monitoring and evaluating proposed interventions to achieve intended impact.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

To ensure effective communication and engagement, ARC recognizes the importance of ensuring that messages are context specific and are tailored for different audiences. ARC will contextualize all information about the project and the services it’s providing and will organize sex and age appropriate sessions to disseminate information at the field level. When designing Information, education and communication materials, ARC will ensure they are translated to the local language and pre-tested to ensure they meet the intended purpose. Also, WGFS activities will be organized in such a way that women and girls have separate days to access the safe spaces to avoid barriers related to the generational gap which deter full participation and communication among women and girls.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

ARC is committed to the protection of vulnerable women and girls from sexual exploitation and abuse. To achieve this, ARC enforces a zero tolerance policy on PSEA through a code of conduct for its staff and will make efforts to ensure that its personnel conforms to the highest standards of moral and ethical conduct when interacting with the beneficiaries. ARC will continuously sensitize the staff and beneficiaries on safe, accessible and confidential reporting mechanisms available for PSEA cases at all levels to encourage reporting. ARC will take preventive measures against sexual exploitation or abuse to investigate allegations or take corrective measures if such issues are reported.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

ARC has on-going activities in the proposed locations of Ulang, Kajo Keji and Kapoeta East since May/July 2017. During this period, ARC has interacted broadly with community members and leadership structures both at community and County levels. This has given ARC a good understanding of the needs and priorities for each category of people and the existing gaps where support is needed. ARC will use this information to design this project and to align it to the realities on the ground.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

ARC places a strong emphasis on receiving feedback from the individuals and communities that we seek to serve in order to improve ownership and the quality of our programs. A feedback/complaints response mechanism that both receives inputs from beneficiaries and regularly provides feedback will be utilized during this project. This will include a suitable data management system to ensure timeliness and quality of GBV assistance as well as to quantify beneficiary satisfaction of assistance received. Outreach efforts will ensure beneficiaries are aware of this feedback/complaint response mechanism and ARC’s accountability commitment, to which our beneficiaries should hold us accountable. As per ARC procedures, the analysis of feedback information from communities is fed directly to senior management for action, ensuring confidentiality and appropriate response to sensitive protection issues. These mechanisms have proven to be an effective tool in providing beneficiaries and communities with a voice and ultimately improving ARC operations and identifying misconduct within an organization. Analysis of beneficiary feedback will be regularly fed directly to management for action, enhancing lessons learned and good practices across the program.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

ARC will maintain a strong community engagement and communication is a central output in ensuring accountability toward the affected population and the response will continue to build on efforts from previous years to strengthen community engagement and participation of people receiving aid in making the decisions which affect their lives. Attention will be given to all groups in society, with particular consideration to gender, age and those with disabilities through meaningful, informed and effective participation. ARC will deliberately involve the beneficiaries in all phases of its project and will conduct focus group discussions with beneficiaries at the end of the project to document best practices and lessons learnt for future programming. Involving all ARC staff in system processes will ensure the beneficiaries’ experience we create is consistent and that we are continually learning from one another. Organizational knowledge and lessons gathered from engagements with the community will fed back into the system to be made available to staff at all levels. This will ultimately support need driven programming along each stage of development aligned to the priorities of the target communities which will foster ownership and sustainability.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

ARC recognizes the need for protection risk analysis that considers the specific needs of different population so as to develop mitigation measures to either eliminate the risks altogether or to reduce the intensity of it. The protection of all persons affected and at risk must inform humanitarian decision-making and response, including engagement with States and non-State parties to conflict. ARC will contribute to an improved protection environment by aiming to achieve collective outcomes on priority issues. Through collective protection and gender risk analysis, ARC will ensure that humanitarian responses do not exacerbate risks and reinforce patterns of violations. However, this will require specialized and integrated programmes, protection mainstreaming across sectors, and promoting an age-gender-diversity needs-based and conflict-sensitive response.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Access constraints in South Sudan encompasses a wide range of dimensions (from bureaucratic to physical) and each of them must be taken into consideration to address the broader challenge of access in the country. ARC will focus on building a general holistic access advocacy approach to mitigate a silos approach that is still too prevalent and creates competition and narrows the focus for a problem that must be addressed by engaging all actors. Key to this will be adopting an inclusive approach with all type of actors including engaging decentralized actors. ARC and partners will be vigilant in identifying and where possible negotiating with the leadership of the day as pertains the bureaucratic impediments and will share such information for advocacy purposes. ARC will use available channels of communication to escalate to Juba level such issues for advocacy. Access barriers related to limited services will require mobilization of resources to expand the services to areas not reached with services. ARC will share such information accordingly through the cluster for advocacy and prioritization during funds allocation.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes, the project activities seek to address inequalities or protection threats. Protection threats continue to intensify in scale and scope, and the population is exposed to repeated deliberate attacks on civilians and other human rights violations. Through the various activities under this project, ARC will be addressing issues of violence, coercion, exploitation, deprivation or neglect which is what GBV programming is all about-in the various forms it is perpetrated.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes the project activities seek to address inequalities or protection threats. Women and children face specific protection risks, including gender-based violence, forced recruitment, family separation, forced and early marriage. Through this project, ARC will advance gender equality between men and women by challenging socio-cultural norms, practices and beliefs that promote GBV through community sensitization efforts. The response will also focus on static life-saving services in case management for GBV survivors to provide support and build their resilience. Psychosocial support services will be strengthened to build people’s capacity to cope with the threats. ARC is well positioned to provide multi-sectoral response in health, nutrition and WASH services to address the multiple needs for the vulnerable populations. Integration will other sectors will be a key consideration to maximize the impact, proper utilization of limited resources and avoid duplication of efforts.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The target beneficiaries will be informed and involved in the process of project monitoring and evaluation. Also, beneficiaries will be made aware about the Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) system which ARC will analyse from time to time to measure beneficiaries’ access barriers to services. Through period program activity reports, ARC will documents the bureaucratic and physical access impediments, its magnitude, scope and impact on programming. All the information will be documented in the Complaints and Response Mechanism database held by the MEAL Coordinator at a central location in Juba. Appropriate action will be taken accordingly. However, sensitive issues will be channeled through the channels provided by ARC.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

All the information collected that contains details of beneficiaries will be kept confidential to the organization and utilized for project purposes only. This information will be kept within ARC office in lockable cupboards under the custody of the MEAL department. The MEAL Coordinator will develop tools for MEAL staff to periodically conduct Post Distribution Monitoring (PMD)/Post Service Surveys with beneficiaries to collect and analyze information on various issues including beneficiary-safety. This information will be analyzed and reported accordingly to the project management team.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

ARC will measure the dignity of beneficiaries through organized focused group discussions and interviews with beneficiaries to evaluate the nature and quality of services provided including if the services are provided in a dignified manner and identify areas for improvement. The M&E team will assist to collect and analyze information shared through the feedback mechanisms and Post Service Surveys and to develop a report which will be shared with the program teams for action. Issues that will require the attention of the senior management will be escalated accordingly.
