Project Summary

PARC has extensive experience in shelter rehabilitation in the West Bank to improve the living conditions and increase protection of highly vulnerable Palestinian families who suffer from settler violence, demolitions and /or military operations and inability to rehabilitate their shelters. The project builds on such experience and extensively extends PARC’s role as an active emergency responder within the West Bank addressing IHRL/IHL violations practiced by the Israeli army and settlers across the West Bank. PARC will coordinate its intervention with key actors of the Shelter Cluster. PARC will also liaise with the local protection committees as well as governor offices where the activities will be implemented. PARC has previous experience in similar actions including Improving, rehabilitating, and winterizing of vulnerable and inadequate shelters in the Jordan Valley and Tubas for vulnerable women and men in herding and farming communities in order to increase their resilience. Protection threats and HHs specific structural as well as occupation related vulnerabilities affect groups differently. The communities identified are mainly herding and Bedouin and/or semi Bedouin communities mostly living in shacks and tents. The types of shelter and their specific setting increase household’s exposure to potential consequences of adverse weather conditions such as: flooding, winter storms, extreme heat in the summer as well as access to basic services and geographical deprivation. When it comes to settler related incidents; isolated vulnerable households are more likely to be attacked by settlers and most often in times where only a few people are around, in many cases only lonely women, elderly people and children. Furthermore, the project will also tackle demolition related incidents where identified as gaps. The unlawful demolition practices are regularly imposed by Israeli army for herding and Bedouin communities adding another layer of an ongoing vulnerability and suffering all around the year.
The project includes the rehabilitation and/or upgrading of shelters, which aims to reduce the exposure of targeted beneficiaries to both man-made and natural disasters and weather conditions, by enabling them to live with adequate shelter standards supporting dignity and wellbeing. In addition, the action includes the distribution of shelter protective materials to households exposed to demolition practices. Indeed, the project intends to rehabilitate/upgrade 80 shelters during the project cycle and provide protective materials to another 80 households where the substandard shelters cannot be upgraded due to security risks or technical difficulties. Both activities will be implemented in the Jordan Valley communities.

1 - Needs

In 2018, PARC participated in a Substandard Shelter Assessment led by the Shelter Cluster in area C of the West Bank. The methodology included a unified assessment form for all partners (PUI, ACF, GVC, PHC, MAAN, PARC, UAWC and ACTED). 10 indicators, based on technical criteria (no socio-economic data was collected) assessed the vulnerability extracted from the minimum standards. In total, 264 communities were included in the assessment, out of an initial list of 305 (41 communities with no significant shelter vulnerabilities were excluded). Out of the 20,846 residential shelters surveyed, 16% (3,425) of them were considered substandard and in need of rehabilitation. Particular vulnerable group were herding and Bedouin communities (representing 60% of substandard shelters). During September 2019, PARC had conducted a rapid needs assessment with Jordan valley communities to update their profiles and identify unmet shelter needs. Based on this assessment, the list of communities has been produced and reported with the below main needs: The type of shelters in need of rehabilitation were as follow: 1. 100% have roof which is not suitable to control heat (thermal insulation). 2. 80% have roof and external walls which are not sealed and there are water leaks. 3. 90% have external walls which are not protected for thermal comfort. 4. 91% don’t have a minimum space per person (including kitchen, corridors, bathroom, or toilet facilities). 5. 60% don’t have smooth floors suitable for children and not supporting hygiene and cleaning works. 7. 75% have window units which are not sealed (from rain water and wind). 8. 75% don’t have functioning latrine per shelter. 9. 12 shelters are built from clay, is an appropriate and needs rehabilitation. Moreover, the local committees, representing the diversity of the communities and established at community level will be consulted to identify common needs regarding the implementation. In addition, PARC will include households to identify the appropriate responses / needs/demands for people with disabilities (PwDs), women, girls. PARC will scrutinize every part of the process to ensure that interventions will not negatively affect the communities. Furthermore, whenever designing an intervention, a focus will be given to the legal context as well as risk analysis and reporting as the whole project is targeting Area C.PARC’s Coordinator in Jericho and Jordan Valley visited the possible targeted communities and held meetings with the village councils there. A discussion between PARC’s staff and the VC’s members took place in order to determine the most vulnerable areas in the local communities, site visits were conducted by the organization’s experts to ensure the need and the vulnerability in the targeted communities. Moreover, village councils’ members were interviewed, in addition to interviews with potential beneficiaries to have a better idea about the targeted communities. OCHA’s data and maps were used as a secondary data to double check the selected communities regarding the shelter vulnerability. Meetings with Jericho’s and Tubas’ governorates were held to take a better idea about the communities’ burdens and needs and to take into consideration the governorate’s data about risks, settler attacks, geographical distribution of communities in the governorate and many other notes, concerns and suggestions from them.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Community outreach, beneficiary selection and technical assessment. PARC will reach out to the targeted communities through different channels such as posters in public infrastructure, mosques and village councils as well as through CBOs with information regarding project’s objectives and criteria for eligibility. In addition, local committees will be established at community level to secure active community participation in all phases of the action. The local committees will respect the diversity of the community including representation of different groups based on gender, age, and disability. Technical assessments including socio-economic criteria will be conducted at household level to assess the needs and identify potential beneficiaries. Activity 2: Shelter rehabilitation at household level.PARC will upgrade 80 substandard housing units located in the targeted communities of Tubas and the Jordan Valley. Women, men, girls and boys will be consulted during all the project phases (targeting, design and implementation). Special care will be granted to specific needs in relation to the different groups and in their use of the shelters both for privacy, socializing and work. This project will place particular attention on families with people with disabilities and female-headed households as these are the beneficiary families with members spending more time at home in the area of intervention. Special attention will be put on specific needs of vulnerable beneficiaries and their participation will be encouraged for the design and implementation of activities. Activity 3: Protective materials at household level. PARC will provide protective assets to 80 households residing in the targeted communities. These communities are often subjected to recurrent IHL/IHRL violations (e.g. harassment/incursions by settlers or IF, stoning/arson, cutting of waterlines) due to their geographic proximity to settlements, outposts and military bases. PARC will develop protective responses together with the local committees and the targeted households to reduce the magnitude or/and likelihood of incidents, through the provision of protective assets (such as fences, tents for water tanks, window shutters, fencing, first aid kits, and fire extinguishers. Emphasis will be given to improve the feeling of safety and dignity of the most vulnerable of the households. Meanwhile, both partners will prioritize assisting households where rehabilitation for the substandard shelter is not relevant due to technical difficulties or due to security reasons where the intervention might put great risks on the families.
