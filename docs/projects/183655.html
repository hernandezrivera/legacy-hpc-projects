Project Summary

The rehabilitation of Abu Nasser valley, which is located in Bani Suhaila, is still a priority for the local municipality and in line with its strategic urgent plan. This was confirmed by WASH cluster focal point in Khan Younis and Coastal Municipal Water Utilities (CMWU), which qualified it as “in bad situation” and considered it as high priority. The valley is located in an area of AL Zana, which is at high risk of flooding that might affect approximately 2,600 people. In addition, in Bani Suhaila, floods affect Al Thelal community, which notably include market streets, shops, passers-by, children, etc. The flooding in this area continues to damage adjacent housing units, and render public access more difficult, especially for pupils in UNRWA schools, both girls and boys, as stagnant water pools are formed. AL Fukhari community is also affected by floods specially the south-east and south-west areas. In total, the action will benefit 8,911 individuals.
With the outbreak of the COVID 19 pandemic in the Gaza Strip, the situation is more critical for older people and people with chronic diseases, who are more exposed to health issues. It is also a burden for women, including female headed-households, as they are under further pressure to protect their children by engaging in more cleaning and washing tasks.
In close coordination with all key stakeholders in the WASH sector, PUI proposes to rehabilitate Abu Nasser valley in Bani Suhaila and mitigate floods in Al Fukhari community in order to protect people and their assets, including small-scale farmers’ lands from floods and harsh weather conditions, and collect rain water, which considered as environmental wealth, and benefit from it for other uses.
The proposed action is to build 1 km of drainage system with rain-water harvesting gullies, and to transfer the collected water to Khan Younis lagoon. It will also support municipalities to evacuate the stagnant storm water from the low level/flood-prone areas in the targeted area, through the provision of heavy machines and needed material. The planned action will be implemented with the Palestinian Environmental Friends association (PEF) and PUI’s local partnered community-based organizations (CBO) in the targeted areas. Local partners have showed vast experience in emergency preparedness and urgent interventions in areas at risk of floods through their staff and volunteers’ network.

1 - Needs

In September 2017, PUI updated its assessment conducted in 2016 on Abu Naseer valley in Bani Suhaila. In collaboration with PUI, the municipality of Bani Suhaila held a consultation meeting with a number of people living and working in the areas surrounding Abu Naseer valley to discuss the issue of the frequent flooding, identify the needs, and propose solutions. The meeting took place in the meeting room of the municipality and was attended by local people living in the area, community leaders, men and women, head teachers of the affected schools, mayor of the municipality, municipality's engineers and PUI's representative. In August 2020, PUI liaised again with the municipality, Khan Younis WASH focal point and CMWU who confirmed that the needs are still unmet, urgent and valid. In September 2021, PUI has been contacted by the local municipality who raised the urgent needs for the development of Abu Naseer valley. The main findings of PUI’s updated assessment for the Abu Nasser valley are as follows:- The change of the course of the valley due to the last wars is the main reason behind the flooding and the forming pools of stagnant water. The pools of stagnant water lead to the spread of insects like mosquitoes as well as infectious diseases; - The flooding of the valley threatens the passers-by, especially children, older persons and people with disabilities; - The recurrent floods hinder the movement of people and vehicles in and out of the affected areas; - The limited access to the schools caused by the flooding of the valley leads to the disruption of the educational process in the surrounding schools; - The flooding causes severe damages to the houses, properties, schools, and other public facilities; - The main facilities affected by the flooding include 3 public schools, 2 UN schools, kindergartens, mosques, poultry farms, car repairing workshops and a primary health care center. The municipalities in Bani Suhaila and Al Fukhari indicated that main challenges faced during rainy season are the flooding - of rain water in the low-level areas; - of sewage maintenance holes; - of the main streets during the heavy raining storms due to the blockage of drainage system caused by throwing solid wastes/garbage into it; - of agricultural, not paved and muddy, roads during winter which makes it difficult for farmers to reach their lands; - and leakage of rain-water into houses covered by metal/asbestos sheets. Furthermore, the lack of fuel needed to operate drainage pumps and water wells especially during emergencies and the lack of the human resources to carry out the routine maintenance works and/or the emergency tasks related to floods were raised by all the municipalities. 9,298 individuals are affected by floods in four localities in Al Fukhari (south east and south west areas), Al Zana, Al Thelal. It is important to note that gaps and risks in terms of shelter, further exacerbated by the C19 pandemic, disproportionately affects groups of people with specific needs and capacities, like people with disabilities (PwD), older persons, people with mental health (MH) issues, vulnerable women and girls, etc. While such groups are affected more severely, they also face increased difficulties in terms of accessing and receiving humanitarian aid in an equitable manner. PUI will seek to consult with varied groups of people in terms of Age, Gender and Diversity (AGD), to gain a greater understanding on how the outlined issues affect them, and the ways they would like to be supported to prevent and mitigate those risks appropriately. As such, planned activities will be tailored, to the extent that it is feasible, to their suggestions. To ensure this level of diverse community engagement is maintained throughout service provisions, PUI will focus on ensuring adequate protection, age, gender, diversity and PSEA mainstreaming by mapping possible barriers, risks and opportunities for different types of people in terms of accessing humanitarian assistance and preventing, mitigating or strengthening them appropriately. PUI maintains a larger protection mainstreaming framework that is contextualized with project-specific activities and mitigation measures guided by community consultations. Considering the immense toll instances of SEA can have on the survivor, the community and the humanitarian agencies as a whole, PUI and partners recognize the ever-present need of preventing, or addressing SEA in confidential, appropriate and survivor centered manner.

2 - Activities

PUI pays great attention to the coordination and takes the initiative to contact all key actors working in its area of intervention to provide a coordinated response to affected people. Before and during the activities' implementation PUI will coordinate and exchange information with the WASH cluster, CMWU, PWA and MolG to avoid overlapping and discuss the best practices for the activities' implementation and assist affected people with improved access to flooding mitigation and prevention measures. PUI will work closely with its local partner NGO and CBO volunteers in the four targeted localities: SE and SW localities in Al Fukhari, Al Zana, Al Thelal localities in Bani Suhaila. The action will benefit to 9,298 individuals (including approximately 4,567 women and girls) among the populations in the four localities: AL Zana: 2600 individuals, AL Fukhari, south-east area: 1084 individuals AL Fukhari, south-west area: 1229 individuals, and Al Thelal: 4,385 individuals. PUI will implement the following activities: Activity 1: Upgrading of Abu Naseer Valley’s infrastructures in Bani Suhila through the construction of a rain-water harvesting system in collaboration with CMWU and the municipality, which should benefit to all the population in AL Zana (487 HHs). PUI in close coordination with the municipality of Bani Suhila will conduct a deep technical assessment of the needs in the targeted area in order to prepare the final designs and BoQs for the required construction works (rain-water harvesting and drainage system). The proposed system will be completed by carrying out the following sub-activities: - Levelling works for the valley with spreading of Kor-Kar gravels to reach the design level; - Supply and install 1km of 500 mm (20 Inch) UPVC pipes, maintenance holes, and concrete gullies (catching underground water basins); - Supply and casting concrete B300 for culvert rooms, ground beams and retaining walls to reinforce the valley sides in order to protect residence homes and assets; - Supply and casting reinforced concrete 15 cm thick ground slab at the beginning of the valley in order to protect the valley’s entrance; - Connecting the proposed rain-water drainage system to Al Qrara functioning rainwater drainage system which delivers the collected rain-water to Khan Younis Al Amal lagoon. Activity 2: Support to two local municipalities (AL Fukhari and Bani Suhaila – 9,298 individuals) in targeted areas to enhance their emergency response capacity through the following activities: - Rent of heavy machines for emergency interventions; a contractor will be hired to support the targeted municipalities with front loader machines to evacuate the stagnant water from the low-level areas and muddy streets, make trenches, and build sand dams to prevent stormwater from reaching home and assets; - Provision of Kor-kar gravels in the targeted muddy areas in order to reduce the possibility of water stagnation and to ensure the smooth movement of people, especially children, PwD and older persons, to their workplaces, schools, and primary health centers. - Provision of fuels to operate the rain-water suction pumps and the tools needed for emergency response activities related to floods. Throughout project activities protection, gender, age and diversity will be taken into consideration in a manner that reflects the unique needs and capacities of the target community. Specially, all planned activities will be followed by safety audits (including GBV safety audit) to prevent and mitigate possible risks associated with service provision, including SEA. As part of the larger PUI Protection Mainstreaming framework, activities that respect gender-equitable norms, by promoting for example female employment in a traditionally male-dominated sector (construction work) where possible, will be adopted. To further ensure minimizing and address the risks of SEA, accessible awareness-raising sessions will be ensured with people from different groups, that might be more vulnerable to the threat of SEA (e.g. girls and women with disabilities, unaccompanied children, etc.).Following the necessary awareness-raising, PUI and partners have a robust reporting mechanism where people can voice their concerns regarding SEA in a safe and confidential manner. SOPs on handlining SEA complaints and dealing with SEA investigations guide the whole process and put the wellbeing of the victims at the center of any action.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Beneficiaries are involved in all aspects of the Action through a shared decision-making process. The community-based protection approach involves a continuous process of self-assessment and analysis of their vulnerabilities and needs, used as an entry point for the elaboration of local protection strategies. This is done through surveying i.e. baseline, need assessments, PDM tool, conducting FGDs, holding on key informative meetings and establishing learning sessions. Starting from the design and planning phases, PUI keens on consulting the local communities, municipalities, and the national authorities to provide their feedback regarding their needs, aspiration and learnings. During the project implementation, PUI staff members will ensure to hand out information cards on the mechanism in addition to the distribution of common speech documents and action leaflets in the local partner CBOs and municipalities premises. During the field visits, and the meetings with beneficiaries and the local community, PUI makes sure to collect all relevant feedback regarding the action to ensure a continuous information on the mechanism in the field. Meanwhile, feedbacks may be provided verbally, through text message, email or phone (using the dedicated address and phone number provided on information cards), in addition to all other available media of the organization. It is worth mentioning, the organization partners are actively participated to disseminate the organization FC mechanism to encourage the community to raise their voices and provide their feedback to PUI. Feedbacks will be analysed by the MEAL team and be aggregated in a dedicated anonymous monthly report. Recommendations analysed will be used by program team through the learning process to improve PUI quality of intervention for ongoing and future actions.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Protection mainstreaming is crucial in humanitarian aid delivery. PUI is dedicated to ensuring do no harm and dignity to all people accessing services regardless of their personal characteristics. PUI is currently developing a Protection and Gender Mainstreaming strategy to reflect on the type of actions required at each stage of the project cycle to ensure protection and gender principles are adequately mainstreamed. Based on this strategy and on the planned community consultations, a project-specific Protection and Gender Mainstreaming action plan will be developed Action onset. Some of the key intervention areas will be: • Action onset a protection and gender risks and mitigation analysis will be drafted and further refined and triangulated with the solicitation of direct feedback from the target community, regarding the perceived protection risks, needs and capacity. The analysis will have a special focus on the risks of GBV and challenges for PwDs. According to the initial Protection and Gender risk analysis, project-specific and nuanced mitigation measures will be adopted to respond to the identified risks and gaps. • Community consultation, through different means (FGDs, Key informants Interviews (KIIs), surveys, etc.) will be conducted throughout the project implementation to monitor the progress of the mitigation measures, as well as to detect any new or emerging protection concerns. All tools, SOPs and materials used during each of the project steps will include protection mainstreaming considerations. Protection and Gender Mainstreaming indicator(s) will be measured and monitored regularly through different data collection sources (observations, consultations, FRM, etc.). • SEA is a GBV issue that might negatively impact all aspects of protection mainstreaming and have lasting consequences in the lives of survivors. To ensure PSEA, PUI has and is currently further strengthening its SEA reporting mechanism. The reporting mechanism is accessible through different channels for beneficiaries, staff and other stakeholders. PSEA training will be provided to all staff members. Accessible and clear awareness-raising and key messaging on PSEA will also be disseminated to beneficiaries. • Beneficiaries with or without specific vulnerabilities in need of more specialized protection or PSS services will be provided with safe and timely referrals to other dedicated service providers. In order to ensure safe protection referrals, a protection and PSS service mapping for the area of operation will be accessed or drafted. At the same time, staff members will be trained on the “Safe Protection Referrals” training module, to include “Communicating and responding to GBV disclosures”. • A protection capacity building plan will be drafted action onset, to reflect on the needs identified during the initial Protection and Gender Risks Analysis, as well as subsequent consultations with staff and beneficiaries. Some of the topics to be included in the capacity building plan will be: Protection, AGD mainstreaming, GBV, child protection, PFA, safe referrals, PSEA and child safeguarding, and inclusion of PwDs. • To ensure all protection and gender mainstreaming activities are at the forefront of the project, selected eld staff members will be trained and mentored to act as protection mainstreaming focal points. They will be tasked with specific protection and gender mainstreaming activities, while also raising the awareness of other staff members and beneficiaries on protection related issues. While the above outline the PUI protection and gender mainstreaming framework, specific project related mainstreaming activities will be planned and implemented together with the target community, to further ensure community involvement. Successful protection mainstreaming efforts should put beneficiaries at the center of service provision, while also ensuring they are adequately involved in their planning and delivery.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

PUI first concern is to protect target groups via tailored action based on identified needs, gaps, gender analysis and priorities for all households’ members. In the context of COVID-19, this concern is higher for all stakeholders including PUI & local partner staff. Before starting the rehabilitation works in the Abu Nasser valley, all target groups will be invited for an information meeting to communicate about the different planned activities (rehabilitation works) to take place. They will also receive information about safety and security practices during the implementation phase, as well as be briefed on the danger of the COVID-19 outbreak and precautionary measures to be taken. PUI is very vigilant in acknowledging and addressing the intersectionality of different characteristics with different needs and capacities. To ensure people with different characteristics and coping mechanisms are able to access assistance equally, specific mitigation measures will be adopted. These mitigation measures will derive from community consultations, with people from diverse groups and different vulnerabilities. The protection and gender risk and mitigation analysis that will be further enriched and complimented throughout the project duration identifies the potential risks and systematizes the provided responses. A specific vulnerability mapping will also take place, to complement the protection and gender analysis. The vulnerability mapping exercise will be participatory and inclusive to all diverse community groups. The outcome of the exercise will be a clearer picture of the kind of groups within the community that might be experiencing added vulnerability, including the prevalence of disability and types, the prevalence of other protection concerns (e.g. unaccompanied children), age and gender groups with specific needs, etc. The community consultations, assessments and information dissemination methods and modes will be tailored to ensure people with different characteristics are included equally. The accessibility of the project consultations, assessment, FRM and PSEA reporting mechanism for people belonging to different age and gender groups, as well as to people with diversities including people with disabilities, will be ensured and monitored throughout the project duration. PUI has developed SoPs to be applied by all due to the outbreak of the COVID-19 pandemic. The SoPs are part of PUI’s contingency plan are updated on a monthly basis taking into consideration the local authorities’ recommendations notably the Ministry of Health. PUI’s staff and local partners’ staff are well-aware of the SoPs and specific training sessions took place so all involved personnel is trained and aware of the importance of applying the safety and prevention measures. The SoPs details all phases of the action and necessary precautionary and preventive measures to be taken into consideration such as the use of Personal Protective Equipment (PPE), social distancing, reduction of gathering to a minimum number in an open space, aid distribution measures, alternative options for monitoring and evaluation, etc. At the same time, the protection and gender analysis will be updated regularly to reflect new emerging needs associated with any enforced changes in the project delivery modalities. These changes in modality are also part of regular consultations with the communities to identify and assess how they might contribute to the access in humanitarian service provision of different groups of people. Accessible information in different modalities for different groups of people regarding COVID-19 will be provided (child friendly leaflet for example). Lastly, the continuation of the FRM, regardless of COVID-19 related changes in programming will be ensured.
