Project Summary

This proposed project aims to stop transmission and prevent spread of the COVID-19 outbreak in Bama, Ngala, Dikwa and Magumeri LGA trough an integrated WASH and Health program in order to limit human-to-human transmission, including reducing secondary infections among close contacts and healthcare workers, preventing transmission amplification events, strengthening health facilities. The focus will be on infection prevention and control household and Health facility levels , risk communication , community engagement and counter misinformation , and integration of WASH activities with health activities to strengthen the service delivery within the existing health system by enhancing minimum standards in handwashing, enhanced water supply, sanitation as well as adapted management of ‘Medical waste , expanding the triage area to maintain acceptable social distancing , training of Health workers on COVID 19. INTERSOS will work with the Communities and other implementing partners within Bama, Dikwa, Ngala and Magumeri LGA, and will encourage community-based surveillance (CBS) whenever it is feasible. Community will be engaged in early detection of COVID-19 cases in sites and surrounding communities.

1 - Needs

COVID-19 is of public health importance because of the ability to spread rapidly through human to human contact. The rapid escalation of the outbreak of 2019-nCOV and its global spread has significant implication to public health security in Nigeria.The humanitarian situation is expected to worsen due to COVID 19, particularly due ongoing stressors experienced by the health system including disrupted health system in many inaccessible LGAs, non-functional health facilities, lack of health care staff, overwhelmed response capacity of international humanitarian agencies and lack of resources. A tightening of pre-existing movement restrictions, economic deterioration coupled with strained public services will further increase humanitarian needs and compound existing operational challenges. The impact of the humanitarian crisis in the conflict affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY states) presents one of the greatest vulnerabilities to the spread of COVID-19. The situation is a complex protracted crisis with huge developmental needs, damaged infrastructure, lack of governance and rule of law outside Maiduguri and major garrison towns. The most vulnerable population groups are children, women, elderly and people with chronic medical conditions. The biggest fear is the effects on the decrease immunity associated with diseases and risks typical of humanitarian contexts such as malnutrition, measles, malaria, HIV etc.The COVID-19 pandemic is placing significant strain on healthcare facilities that are already overwhelmed by lack of capacity as well as high risk disease outbreaks such as cholera, lassa fever, measles and malaria. More than 40 percent of health facilities in the affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe have been damaged/destroyed as a result of the protracted conflict. There have also been significant disruptions to vaccination campaigns and other essential health services for children and other vulnerable groups in inaccessible areas. Funding has also been a major challenge, noting that in 2019, the health sector received only 25 percent of its funding requirements.As per the HNO, 7.9 million people are expected to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2020 of whom the humanitarian partners will target 5.9 million of the most vulnerable people. The capacity of the health system to address the possible impact of the pandemic, remains limited.The Risk assessment conducted by the Borno State Ministry of Health, with the support of WHO identified 2 very high risk LGAs (Jere & MMC) and 6 High Risk LGAs (Bama, Gwoza, Damboa, Ngala, Kala Balge and Monguno).• Population living in overcrowded IDPs camps and urban centers high risk of transmission in big camps in Moguno., MMC, Bama, and Gowza• High endemic region for cholera, malaria due to seasonal patterns, weak water and sanitation practices and infrastructure, social behavior patterns. • Higher infection-to-case ratios and progression to severe disease due to the virus’ interaction with highly prevalent co-morbidities, including non-communicable diseases • Weak response capacity on COVID-19 Case Management support from few international humanitarian agencies with over stretched capacity and lack of adequate resources• Lack of fully equipped Isolation/treatment Centre and functional triage wards for COVID-19 especially in LGAs-need for ventilators, oxygen concentrators, respirators etc.

2 - Activities

INTERSOS will focus on strengthening the preventive and IPC measures in the supported Health facility in Bama GSSSS camp, according to the NCDC and WHO guidelines on COVID 19. Training and sensitisation of health workers on COVID 19(early detection and standard principles of IPC), maintaining the minimum requirements for a functional triage system putting into consideration patients with symptoms that meet criteria for a suspected of probable COVID 19 case.INTERSOS will adopt standard IPC precautions at the health facility including minimum standards in handwashing, enhanced water supply and sanitation , respiratory and cough etiquette, providing appropriate materials and supplies, including PPE and ensuring proper use of the PPEs by the staff working in the health facility, ensuring safe handling equipment, cleaning and disinfection of surfaces and equipment and proper medical waste management INTERSOS will reinforce the existing community mobilizers network in Bama, Dikwa, Ngala and Magumeri LGAs through the Community health volunteers (CHV), Community health extension workers ( CHEWs), local leaders ,religious leaders ,youth and women leaders will be engaged on social and behaviour change communication and sensitisation at the community level on the risks of the pandemic though trainings according to WHO and NCDC guidelines, disseminating of IEC materials in different local language INTERSOS will establish prevention measures at the community and household level on infection prevention that are disease specific, including social distancing, hand hygiene and respiratory etiquette and supporting effective referral of patients presenting with COVID 19 symptoms at the community level.In collaboration with protection team and other actors INTERSOS will engage in active risk mapping to identify high- risk population groups for targeted messaging, frontline social mobilization and risk communication teams will provide clear and equivocal messages focusing on what people can do to reduce risk and actions to take when the think they may have COVID 19 . CHVs will be involved in epidemiological surveillance, alert notification, case investigation and case reporting following national guidelines and WHO’s technical guidance as well as a CBS in which community representatives will also be involved in early detection of COVID-19 cases in the camp and surrounding communities.INTERSOS will recruit, train and deploy heath care workers including Doctor, nurses, to monitor persons received at the self-quarantine facility who are either ( New arrivals to the IDP camp from other towns due to the on-going insurgency, any asymptomatic cases identified within the IDP camp and persons showing early symptoms of Covid 19) and also ensure provision of basic emergency care ( BEC) for seriously ill patients and then activate referral. INTERSOS will work in close collaboration and coordination with actors within Bama, Dikwa , Ngala and Magumeri LGAs including SEMA, and SMOH to facilitate referral of symptomatic patients to the designated treatment facilitiesINTERSOS will ensure community engagement is integrated early in the response with ongoing surveillance, contact tracing activities through community coordinated network to ensure effective community messaging on COVID 19 symptoms and distinction between mild symptoms versus severe symptoms and mild cases be isolated to reduce transmission. The traditional healers within the community will be engaged to ensure the sick people with COVID 19 symptoms seeking care from them are referred appropriately for testing and management INTESOS will work in corroboration with the state surveillance team and will alert the Disease surveillance and Notification Officer (DSNO) of any suspected case according to the WHO case definition.
