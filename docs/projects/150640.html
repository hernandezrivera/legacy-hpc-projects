Project Summary

The objective of the project is to a) To Improve vulnerable women's and girls' access to
income generation opportunities and quality multi sectoral services in Gaza b) To improve
the quality of GBV services provided to vulnerable women and girls., UN Women will capitalize on the achievements and lessons learnt of the last year interventions in addressing GBV needs, supporting partners to improve quality, accessibility and responsiveness of the
services to the needs of those who are most vulnerable. UN Women proposes to
implement this 12-month project to improve displaced and vulnerable women’s
accessibility to multi-sectoral services, protection and economic support in the West Bank
and Gaza. UN Women’s interventions will be carefully tailored to address women’s and
girls’ needs following holistic and woman-centred approaches, where tailored
interventions are comprehensively designed to address the needs of the most vulnerable
groups of women i.e. women with disabilities, women at risk of forcible transfer, women
heads of households, women survivors of violence etc. Through the project, UN Women
will ensure improving the quality of services provided to women and that their voices
inform and shape those services. Through the project interventions, UN Women will also
work on identifying women champions of change who have the ability and capacity to
support other women in their local communities overcome challenges and difficulties The
project responds to HRP Strategic Objective 2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians
living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services, in accordance
with the rights of protected persons under IHL. The project responds to HRP Strategic
Objective 2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met
through the provision of essential services, in accordance with the rights of protected
persons under IHL.

Needs

The humanitarian conditions in Gaza are exacerbated by the intra-Palestinian politicaldivide which has contributed to a serious deterioration in the access of the population tobasic services as measures affecting civil service allowances, electricity supply andmedical payments were imposed by the Palestinian Authority. As of April 2018,approximately 19,200 people were still displaced from the 2014 conflict, with a gap ofover 2,657 of the 11,000 totally destroyed housing units still awaiting reconstruction dueto lack of funds. Higher incidence of intimate partner violence, sexual abuse and forcedmarriage in vulnerable communities such as IDPs, refugee camps, Gaza, and groups, suchas women with disabilities and adolescents, and those with limited availability andaccess to multi-sectoral services are of concern (Müller gender Alert, 2018). The majorityof the violence was of psychological nature, followed by physical violence. Elderly womenare more vulnerable to poverty, disease, and have limited access to services compared toelderly men and the rest of the population. In addition, these vulnerable groups often havelimited knowledge of the services offered by different humanitarian actors, whichcontributes to unequal access to these services (Müller Gender Alert, 2018). An analysis ofdrivers of vulnerability in Palestine had categorized these drivers into main categoriesincluding economic drivers. Women are among those most affected by the unequal burdenof unpaid work, even without including care work undertaken at home – usually bywomen and girls. Women in the oPt are particularly disadvantaged as their limited shareof agricultural holdings, economic opportunities and restricted mobility, constrain theirability to contribute to household food security and increases their vulnerability to GBV.According to recent gender vulnerability analysis, GBV services through health serviceproviders have been negatively affected by the long hours of power cuts, and financialconstraints. This is especially effect women, pregnant women, the elderly and those withchronic illnesses, and GBV survivors as health is the culturally accepted entry point todetect, treat and refer cases. Disability is one main source of vulnerability that leads togender-based discrimination. Women with disabilities are usually victims of two-folddiscrimination; as women and as persons with disabilities. According to a researchconducted by the Women’s Affairs Centre in 201, 59% of respondents (total of 988 womenwith disabilities) have suffered of one form of violence, 31% have reported beingsubjected to neglect and house confinement, 23.8% were denied the right to get marriedand start a family, 91% had never received legal services and 33.9% believed that theavailability of psychosocial support was limited. A recent UN Women’s research (HadeelQazzaz 2018) on GBV sheltering services has already shown that overall, crucial gapswere identified in the overall architecture of GBV Services in Gaza including: - Gaps relatedto GBV services provided by NGOs including a) Capacity Gaps b) Referral andCoordination Gaps c) Gaps in Quality of Services d) Gaps in Stability of Services e) Gapsin ability to deliver some priority services f) Gaps in vision/absence of collective strategy. Against this backdrop and continuing its strategic cooperation with humanitarian actors(mainly sectors and clusters) and CSO partners, UN Women proposes to implement a oneyear project in partnership with specialized partners - selected based on a call for proposal process - from protection cluster partners to a) improve vulnerable women’s accessibility to and bene􀂡t from incomegeneration opportunities and multi sectoral services in Gaza and to b) improve the qualityof GBV services provided to women and girls who are most vulnerable.

Activities

In the framework of IASC Guidelines for GBV Interventions and building on UN Women’sexperience in providing multisectoral and gender responsive services to vulnerable groupsof women, UN Women proposes to implement a one year project in cooperation with CSOpartners that have experience and comparative advantage in implementing projectsfocused on GBV prevention and response in Gaza. With technical support and oversightfrom UN Women, CSO partners will work towards achieving the following results: Output 1.1. 200 Displaced and marginalized women and girls GBV victims havenew sources of income and economic opportunities through Cash-for-Work (CFW) as short-term interventions in humanitarian actions to provide temporary employment opportunities of 4 months for two groups (each group 100 BNFs) in NGOs/CBOs and private sector to vulnerable women post conflict environments, CfW to be implemented by a GBV service provider from protection cluster partners. (Target 200)• Activity 1.1.1: Based on market needs, develop and provideskill-matching cash for work programme targeting 200 displaced women, women withdisabilities and women survivors of violence (age between 20 -50). • Activity 1.1.2 Support, through coaching and training, each beneficiary over the course of CfWimplementation, build a profile that can support them compete in the local market. Output 1.2. 4000 Displaced and marginalized adult and young women, girls and men haveincreased access to multi-sectorial services, including prevention and response to SGBVthrough specialized service providers from protection cluster partners. • Activity 1.2.1: Through the services ofspecialized existed anti-violence centres and GBV service providers, provide GBV case management, psycho social & legalassistance, and referral services to vulnerable women and girls and their families. Thiswill include comprehensive assessment and participatory development of a reintegrationplan that meets their needs. These will include (500 women receiving GBV casemanagement, 100 receiving legal counseling and court representation, 300 receivinghealth and psycho social support and 100 referral services) • Activity 1.2.2: Provide child visitation services for at least 20 families (divorced couples) and their children. • Activity 1.2.3: Expand the services of a Help line conducted currently by UN Women partner “PCDCR” to other GBV service providers, in addition to providing physical rehabilitation services andassistive devices for (100) elderly women and women and girls with disabilities onoperational service providers including those identified by CP-GBV SOPs and referralpathways. • Activity 1.2.4: Awareness raising dialogues with 450 men (HeFor Shedialogues) to promote positive masculinity and positive attitudes in relation to GBV andGBV services • Activity 1.2.5: Provide training and support to 50 women from vulnerablebackgrounds to act as experts and demand better quality multi-sector services • Activity 1.2.6: Organize awareness raising sessions targeting 2000 women, men boys and girls onGBV and GBV and disability services.Output 1.3: Capacity of service providers (50) to offer support services to marginalized women and girls in Gaza strengthened • Activity 1.3.1: Co-design and implement tailored capacity building of service providers (GBV,psychosocial, legal and health) on quality and coordinated services/responses designedto address gender- based vulnerabilities (including disability mainstreaming, referralpathways, GBV case management).

Indicators

UN Women will work closely with CSO partners to make sure the project is implementedaccording to project workplan, logframe and set indicators and targets. The Project willestablish a Project Management Committee (PMC) which will consist of UN Women andall three partners. The PMC will meet on a quarterly basis and is responsible for the overallmonitoring and quality control of the project. The PMC will facilitate planning and settingoverall direction for the project as well as regularly generating inputs from targeted communities through conducting quarterly focus group meetings to provide guidance throughout the project phases and facilitating discussion amongstall partners to help coordinate efforts and avoid overlapping. • UN Women will regularly report to the GBV sub cluster, the protection cluster and the Legal Task Force on project indicators and targets including the below: • The development of clear case management and referral plans at the level of project partners, in alignment with recently developed SOPs (Target: 3 plans)• The creation of a peer review system of professional and ethical standards among service providers, with oversight offered by independent legal, mental health and other professionals (Target: 1).• Number of NGO and governmental actors who actively participate in trainings on GBV case management and referral pathways (Target: 50 Actors)• Number of vulnerable women (survivors of GBV, widows, women & girls with disabilities, abandoned women, and women heads of households) receiving multi sectoral services (GBV case management, psychosocial support, legal support, and health). (Target: 650 women and 250 girls; including 50 women over the age of 59 and 50 disabled women).• Number of women and girls with receiving physical rehabilitation services and facilitative equipment (Target: 50 women and 50 girls).• Number of women and girls with disabilities referred to CPWG members as per child protection referral pathways (Target: 50 girls) • Number of women and men who have an increased awareness on disability, GBV and GBV services (Target:1750 women and 350 men). • Number of women and girls with disabilities who access the Help line for support and referral (Target: 50 women and 50 girls). Monitoring and Evaluation A composite of various M&E approaches, quantitative and qualitative will be used. UN Women will utilize result based management frameworks to manage, monitor and evaluate the execution of project’s activities. UN Women will employ various M&E tools applying its internal oversight mechanisms including the internal monitoring and reporting mechanisms such as the quarterly reporting to its HQ, the regular monitoring activities by its monitoring/project officers. UN Women will regularly convene project teams’ meetings, donor coordination meetings, field visits and regular meetings and discussions will national partners and UN counterparts to assess progress via set objectives, risks and mitigation plans.
