Project Summary

• Better access to clean water by installing 11 deep tube wells and maintaining 90 existing tube wells and tap stands, reaching 9,000 people. AAB will also carry out water quality testing on the standard of contamination-free of the water and apply corrective measures where necessary, benefiting 5,000 people.
• Increased sanitation facilities by constructing 16 twin-pit latrines and 16 bathing spaces for over 600 people. We will ensure the facilities are well-lit for the use of women and adolescent girls at night by installing solar lights. Volunteers, supported by ActionAid, will repair and maintain the existing latrines and bathing spaces, benefiting 5,000 people.
• Improved sanitation and waste management by cleaning drainage networks to benefit 15,000 people.
• Better hygiene practices and waste management by raising awareness of at least 5,000 people and distributing menstrual hygiene materials (including sanitary pads, soap, and sandals) to at least 1,000 women and adolescent girls.
• 425 from Ukhiya and 425 from Teknaf (Total 850 individuals) will be covered in 2025 through MHM kit packages distribution and latrine construction. Besides, AAB will focus on other HC upazilas such as CXB Sadar, Eidgaon, Pekua (which are not included in JRP) for installing DTWs, constructing latrines, distributing MHM kit packages etc.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

• Lack of accessible sanitation and hygiene facilities: Since 2018, there has been a 3% increase in the use of improved sanitation facilities to 11.6%. However, areas of high density are putting pressure on existing Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) facilities. In addition, as per MSNA/ISNA findings, 38% of women in camps are not feeling safe using community latrines at night. Besides, 5% of host community members are using unimproved latrine facilities.• Poor solid and human (excreta) waste management system: Only 51% of refugee households dispose of wastage in designated areas. Only 50% of faecal sludge treatment plants in Camp 11 & 12 meet the acceptable standard.• Inadequate safe water facilities: Whilst the number of refugees with access to safe water has increased significantly between 2018 and 2022, the quality of the water is of concern: 8% of community water sources and 39% of household water sources are contaminated with unsafe levels of E Coli. Cases of Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) and scabies have increased significantly in the past year and there have been incidences of dengue fever in the camps as well. As per sphere standard, a minimum of 20L of water per person per day is required.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

ActionAid Bangladesh (AAB) will arrange solar street-lights adjacent to WASH facilities for avoiding potential threats or violence at night against girls, women, and children. AAB will construct gender-segregated latrines & bathing cubicles and distance of facilities from the shelter will be minimal to make safe & accessible for women & adolescent girls. AAB keeps the provision of distributing MHM kits to host community to address protection mainstreaming issue. Project team will ensure operation & maintenance activities of WASH facilities to keep those functional and to protect from privacy issues & ensure dignity (proper locking system, no leakage on fencing sheet etc.) of women & adolescent girls, while using latrines & bathing cubicles. AAB will consult with women representatives from community to select site or take any initiative regrading WASH services. AAB will also involve women participant to manage solid waste through CfW (Cash for Work) for empowering them economically.

2.3 - Age & Disability

ActionAid will ensure WASH services for all types of gender & age groups via its WASH project at FDMN camps and host communities. 2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Ensuring accountability within the WASH project stands as a pivotal cornerstone. A robust system has been established to uphold this accountability. Volunteers are strategically enlisted for every sub-block, undertaking consistent visits to gather beneficiary feedback and complaints. These inputs are meticulously documented in a dedicated register book, managed, and maintained using KOBO tools. Additionally, beneficiaries hold the option to engage directly by visiting either the AAB camp office or the site management office to voice their feedback or register complaints, fostering a transparent and accessible avenue for their contributions to the project's improvement. The AAB MEAL team handles these inputs. Comprehensive training on CFRM, safeguarding policy, PSEA, SHEA, and child protection is mandatory for all WASH project staff and volunteers. Awareness sessions empower beneficiaries to grasp CFRM principles, enabling active and informed contribution to the project's success. Besides, PDM (Post Distribution Monitoring) can be used for accountability purposes as well.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

All of ActionAid’s humanitarian responses follow strong safeguarding protocols, which all partners are also required to follow. AAB take a survivor-centered approach to safeguarding & PSEA, which gives women and girls a voice to speak out against violence and report complaints. AAB has strong policy about PSEA, Safeguarding & Child Protection and takes zero tolerance action against the abuser. AAB arranges refresher training on PSEA, Safeguarding & Child Protection policy on a regular basis for staff and volunteers. AAB has a hotline number to take complain against PSEA breaches.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

AAB has a plan to do some plantation activities and will produce fertilizer from organic waste through MRF (Material Recovery Facility). AAB will use solar power to run water distribution networks and adopt climate resilient design for constructing new WASH infrastructures. Environment friendly and climate resilient initiatives should be adopted, such as solar powered pumping system, using gravity flow for distribution, using technology to reduce water waste etc. AAB keeps budget allocation for cleaning drainage networks during monsoon.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

AAB will keep a lump-sum budget for emergency preparedness and response, such as tie down the roof of WASH facilities in monsoon. It includes contingency plans as well, like water tracking, purchasing aqua-tabs for crisis moment etc.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

As WASH project in camp covers each and every community people in its territory, so youth inclusion is obviously ensured. Besides, AAB engages youth (18 – 24 years) as paid volunteer from host & Rohingya community. Participation of youth will also be ensured in user & maintenance committee of WASH infrastructures. AAB will include adolescent and youth girls (12 – 18 years) in MHM kits distribution participant list.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Community (Rohingya Refugee) volunteers will be hired to do WASH activities, such as repair-maintenance of WASH infrastructures, water supply, waste management, sludge management, hygiene promotion activities etc. in camp. Community engagement initiatives will be taken to build ownership of WASH facilities among the community so that they can maintain sustainable WASH solutions in case of budget limitation. Low-cost maintenance techniques will be shared to make the community resilient and self-reliant.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, AAB aligns its WASH activities with the priorities of DC Office of CXB, such as installation of WASH blocks at schools and colleges, female friendly WASH facilities, distribution of MHM kit packages etc.
