Project Summary

The overall objective of the project will contribute to Coordination Support Services objective 1: Humanitarian response is implemented through effective coordination mechanisms and supported by the timely allocation of humanitarian pooled funds and Coordination Support Services Objective 2: Provision of humanitarian assistance is enabled through the promotion of humanitarian principles and the rights of Palestinians living under occupation
The project will ensure uninterrupted implementation of the core cluster coordination functions including :
1. Supporting service delivery and coordination
2. Informing strategic decision-making of the HC/HCT for the humanitarian response
3. Planning and strategy development
4. Advocacy
5. Monitoring and reporting
6. Capacity building in contingency/preparedness planning
7. Accountability to Affected Population
In the context of OPt and especially with the recurrent escalations/ emergencies and pandemic, Palestinian authorities lack national coordination mechanisms for addressing the enormous challenges facing the education system; each education system in East Jerusalem, Area C, and the Gaza Strip has different duty bearers and face different significant protection challenges. The Education Cluster will support the relevant duty bearers to ensure that children (age 8-12) 49% of girls, youth (13-18) and teachers in the most vulnerable communities can safely access schools that meet minimum humanitarian standards and provide a protective environment in the context of protracted conflict. All interventions planned through the 2023 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) will be closely coordinated with local communities and meet the requirement of strong protection mainstreaming and community engagement, especially in the needs assessment as well as the implementation of activities.
The Cluster is also planning to strengthen the partners' level of engagement on gender through joint cluster needs assessments on gender-specific needs in the field, joint HRP mid-review and related gender analysis, strengthening partners capacity on data collection and reporting of disaggregated data related to special needs of women, girls, boys or men. Partners will jointly identify the cluster priority interventions and responses based on the gender-disaggregated needs assessments, reports, and testimonies.

1 - Needs

The 2023 HNO for oPt has identified 2.1 million people. also, the education HNO 2023 identified an estimated half a million Palestinian boys and girls are in need of education humanitarian assistance across the oPt, around three-quarters of whom live in the Gaza Strip and 49% are girls. Against the unique contextual backdrop of the oPt and the recurrent escalations and pandemic, there is a continued need for coordinated humanitarian response and advocacy. There is a high demand within the local humanitarian community, as well as in the broader international community, for support to develop a robust, detailed, evidence-based common situational analysis; for the coordination of advocacy and operational humanitarian responses; for the management and monitoring of country-based pooled funds; and for the development and monitoring of strategic needs analyses and response plans. With over 40 implementing partners have participated in the 2022 HRP, and financial requirements reaching over USD 37.2 million targeting 0.467 million people in 2022, there is a clear strategic need for continued coordination of humanitarian action in oPt to maximize achievements, while reducing gaps and duplication in the provision of humanitarian assistance. Education Cluster's unique role in facilitating and coordinating these streams of humanitarian work continues therefore to be of critical importance, especially with the increased education-related incidents in WB and the recent Gaza escalation in the education sector. Humanitarian access to education continues to be compromised due to protracted conflict and occupation. For half a million children (49% girls) in the West Bank, East Jerusalem, and Gaza access to quality education in a safe, child-friendly environment is impeded by the presence of checkpoints, the Barrier, frequent escalations in the Gaza Strip, military and armed group activities and incursions, settler related incidents, as well as the lack of infrastructure. According to the MSNA 55% of boys and 36% of girls who dropped out in the last 12 months was due to conflict-related protection risks on the way to and from schools. In the first half of 2022, 115 education-related incidents recorded affecting 7,906 students (35% girls) and 418 teachers and school staff (24.1% female). The disruption of schooling affects education quality and brings about an array of psychosocial effects, such as excessive stress, fear and distress. According to the MSNA around 57,000 HHs in the Gaza Strip reported that at least one child is showing signs of psychosocial distress, moreover, around 84,000 HHs with school-aged children in the Gaza Strip, Area C, Hebron H2 and East Jerusalem reported that psychosocial support is not available at the school for children if needed, in addition to PSS activities. Also, according to MSNA in Gaza 15.2% of households of the 9.1% of households not planning to enroll school-aged children due to cannot afford school related expenses and in WB 11.9% of households of the 19% of households not planning to enroll school-aged children reported reasons for non-enrolment that child is not interested in school. Through service delivery, advocacy and capacity development, the cluster will seek to mitigate the effects of, ensure preparedness and immediate response to emergencies such as, pandemic, a resurgence of conflict, forced displacement and attacks on schools focusing on the basic schools that have children from age 6-10 and CwDs. The response will focus on advocacy efforts to address the increasing trend of education-related violations, where there is little or no accountability for perpetrators of violations. Authorities lack national coordination mechanisms for addressing the education system's enormous challenges. Each education system, in East Jerusalem, Area C, and the Gaza Strip has different duty bearers and faces different significant protection challenges.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

The needs of individual beneficiaries will be met through response interventions that are not cash-based. Interventions are centered around improving coordination, response, and advocacy at the EiE sector level. Beneficiaries will be reached with direct services; therefore, cash transfer is not relevant to the type of interventions proposed in the project. The cluster will continue to engage with the cash Working Group to find out how best to introduce cash programming in the EiE sector. This will be followed by partners' capacity-building and resource-mobilization efforts.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The education cluster aims to provide a relevant and holistic education response by promoting and integrating all fundamental cross-cutting issues and principles. This will help ensure all interventions planned by partners are protective, inclusive and taking into consideration gender equity. The emergency education strategy in oPt is driven by protection-sensitive activities, which underpin the Sector Response. In addition to school-based psychosocial support, providing protective presence for students and teachers, legal aid and advocacy to help protect schools from attacks, the Education Cluster will work with the Child Protection AoR to link schools with child protection service providers to address protection needs in the most vulnerable schools

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Gender equality and gender-responsive programming is promoted through conducting gender analysis with due care to the different needs and protection concerns in health, protection and education of females and males. All data collected by the education cluster partners should be age- and gender-disaggregated. Projects will address gender discrimination in terms of access to education, as well as the diﬀerent risks that boys and girls face on their way to, from and inside, school. Partners will ensure equal participation of boys and girls in student clubs and ensure that school WASH facilities are gender segregated. Community engagement will be enhanced by the establishment of school-based safety committees, through existing community structures, in some targeted schools; to identify priorities, map existing resources within the community, prepare school-based contingency plans, and coordinate activities in the school. Parent-teacher associations will also be involved in key education advocacy, in addition to collecting feedback from community members to enhance accountability to aﬀected populations with due consideration of gender equity. Student groups will also ensure that students’ voices are integral to the design and implementation of emergency education activities. The Education Cluster will encourage partners to establish feedback and complaint mechanisms, where students and beneficiaries can raise their concerns and participate in projects’ design and share recommendations.
