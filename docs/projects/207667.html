Project Summary

UNICEF will provide support to ensure equitable quality and gender sensitive maternal, newborn, child and adolescent health and immunization services as well as provision of sexual and reproductive health (SRH) services including counselling and referral for GBV and support to pregnant and lactating women (inclusive of children with HIV) with inclusion of people with disabilities. The project will focus and ensure adherence with the Minimum Package of Essential Health services for primary health care in PHCs in Rohingya camps, and comprehensive newborn care support. UNICEF work with other partners both in health sector and other sectors to ensure integrated health services are provided. The project will target 555,820 direct beneficiaries (493,186 refugees and 62,634 people in host communities). The project will be implemented in 6 health facilities in the camps (6 PHCs ) and strengthen newborn health care in the camp through newborn technical sub-committee coordination, government health system and the health facilities in adjacent host communities in Ukhiya and Teknaf (2 union sub centres and 16 community clinics). Following the 2022 Rationalization exercise in the health sector, and further agreements with the SAG, UNICEF will work in 6 PHCs, in camps 5, 8W, 10, 16, 17 and 18. Health posts in Camp 3 and 18 will be closed by December 2023, while Health Post in Camp 16 is almost ready to be upgraded to PHC as per plan, and will be operational in January 2024 to provide 24/7 maternal and newborn care. A special focus will continue to support well functional Newborn Stabilization Unit (NSU) with Kangaroo Mother care, across all the Upazila health complexes, Special Newborn Care unit (SCANU) at the district hospital. UNICEF will continue to play her leading role in supporting vaccine logistic and supply and ensure vaccine availability in all service provision sites in all the camps and host communities in Cox's bazar district. UNICEF will continue to provide technical support, supply and transportation of vaccines and logistic and enhance cold chain capacity and advocacy and social mobilization for strengthening immunization services across all the camps. UNICEF will continue to strengthen the Health Management Information System (HMIS/DHIS2/OpenSRP/OpenMRS) through deployment of technical staff to the office of the Civil Surgeon and build the capacity of service providers on HMIS and support the government in rolling out the e-tracker for improve data collection and monitoring on immunization and maternal health services and reduce zero dose. UNICEF has developed emergency response plan to ensure better preparedness and respond to emergency and disasters including strengthening Infection Prevention and Control measures in all the supported health facilities and support the scale up of outbreak related vaccinations including COVID 19 vaccination program in the camps and host communities. UNICEF will ensure risk communication and community engagement for the promotion of preventive behaviour for disease prevention and mobilize communities to increase uptake of routine immunization, capacity building of front-line workers on communication skills for the strengthening of continuum of care for health services (MNCAH) and managing disinformation related to disease outbreaks through engagement with communities and key social influencers

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The Rohingya crisis due to influx of Rohingya refugees from Myanmar into Bangladesh shifted to protracted crisis. UNICEF and other partners have been supporting the government of Bangladesh specifically the Cox's Bazar district to provide integrated essential health services in line with the Minimum Package of Essential Health Services. Despite the collaborative efforts being made to ensure equitable access to quality health care services, there are still issues that need more efforts and attention. The Health Sector Dashboard for service utilization highlights a high ANC 4 coverage of 86% with a 82% facility based delivery for Rohingya, however in 2023 till October 2023, there have been 68 maternal deaths with 52 of them at health facilities. 159 newborn deaths and 166 still births till July, 2023 highlighting a quality of care concern. The J-MSNA found that home deliveries was still high with 38% in the host and 41% among Rohingya population. Access to health care was high at 89% however barriers to access to healthcare services varied. For the Rohingya, they complained of long waiting time, lack of specific medicine or treatment, and not receiving correct medications. While for the Host community, barriers to care were inability to afford care, distance to facility and long wait time with overcrowding. Persons with disability reported they experienced challenges accessing services in the camp (5%) and host community (2%). Risk communication and community engagement plays a crucial role during disease outbreaks through disseminating life-saving information to the public, build community understanding on preventive measures to prevent spread of diseases and health seeking when appropriate.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project will ensure effective collaboration with both health and other sectors especially Nutrition, WASH, social and behavioural change, education and child protection to promote integrated intervention that also address the other determinants of heath and synergy among the partners in the camps and host communities. UNICEF will ensure effective coordination among partners under the leadership of the government and improve on the relationship with the government, refugees and other stakeholders at all level of the health system both in the camps and host communities.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

UNICEF adheres to the protection principles and ensures access to health services to all and avoid discrimination, ensure safety and dignity of the beneficiaries, meaningful access, being accountable, and ensuring participation of all groups. The human right of all the beneficiaries is protected and system are put in place to ensure that none of the population is abused, neglected, or exploited while seeking health services through training, orientation of service providers and monitoring. UNICEF work together with partners, within the sector and across sectors to prevent and respond to GBV, recognizing the systemic exposure to GBV, while helping to ensure that support is available for all survivors of sexual violence (including violence against children). UNICEF has zero tolerance for SEA and SH. UNICEF maintains environment that prevent and protect from sexual exploitation and abuse, and provide timely, confidential, and effective investigation, assistance, and support to survivors.

3.3 - Age & Disability

The project will ensure that there is no discrimination with regards to age for all age groups, specifically children, and the elderly with fast-tracked mechanisms for access to health services through triage, and follow up support. Persons with disabilities will be targeted in the design, monitoring and implementation phases. Access to health facilities will ensure disability friendly ramps, and prioritization for access to services. The results of the project and achievement will be disaggregated based on age and disability. UNICEF allocates and delivers assistance based on needs and without discrimination. All staffs on the project will be trained and oriented to respect and appreciate other people irrespective of their difference in terms of nationality, sexual orientation, ethnicity, race, sex, language, disability, religious belief, gender identity, political or other opinions. A workplace culture embraces diversity and there is zero tolerance for discrimination. The health facilities and services are accessible to all, and some actions have been taken to improve access and service to the people with disabilities. For example, all health facilities supported by UNICEF have ram in the infrastructure with the availability of wheelchair. Actions have been taken to improve the latrine to be accessible (despite the difficult terrain). Community health workers have been orientated to identify and approach the PWD for further support when needed.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project will ensure community members are provided adequate information on the type of health services available at the various health facilities. Appropriate feedback mechanism/system already established in all health facilities for community members to provide their feedback about the services and all concerns and complaints will be adequately and timely addressed. In UNICEF supported facilities, addressing feedback from communities is crucial, thus we established complaint and suggestion box, exit interview, and linkage with Information Feedback Centres (IFCs) in the camps. In addition, feedback from communities also taken from communities meeting led by communities/religious leaders e.g., Majhi, Imam etc. All feedbacks collected were processed (registered and reviewed) during quality improvement meeting every month. All complaints are then discussed, and the facilities will prioritize the issues to be addressed and take action.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

UNICEF work together with partners, within the sector and across sectors to prevent and respond to GBV, recognizing the systemic exposure to GBV, while helping to ensure that support is available for all survivors of sexual violence (including violence against children). UNICEF has zero tolerance for SEA and SH. UNICEF maintains environment that prevent and protect from sexual exploitation and abuse, and provide timely, confidential, and effective investigation, assistance, and support to survivors. The project will ensure that there is protection against sexual exploitation and abuse through establishing trainings of UNICEF and partners staffs, with preventative messaging, reporting lines and preliminary monitoring and re-assessments of PSEA readiness of partners and response. Any identified cases of PSEA will be reported, investigated and specific individualized and organizational based actions and plans to mitigate and respond to any PSEA allegations and cases.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

UNICEF child-centred approach to reduce disaster and climate risk and building resilience in development and humanitarian setting, both for and with children and young people. DRR and climate change mitigation measures are embedded in the programme and UNICEF ensured that partners trained and incorporated these in programme planning and implementation. UNICEF allocated funds with partners for contingency to which they can use when significant event occurred e.g. cyclone, flood, landslide, fire. This is applied since the beginning of the response to the crisis to strengthen and maintain the resilience. All facilities have waste management in place and the workers received regular training as part of infection prevention and control.
