Project Summary

Zimbabwe is going through one of its worst food security crises, with a total of 5.8. million people (4.1 million rural, 1.6 million urban) estimated to be in need of emergency food assistance. According to the IPC Zimbabwe Food Insecurity report for June to December 2019, at least 65% of the population in Gokwe North district in Midlands Province were unable to meet their immediate food needs between October and December 2019, and the district is classified in emergency phase. In the central and urban part of Gokwe, 35.7% of the population (about 13 873 people) is considered food insecure (ZIMVAC Urban Livelihoods Assessment 2019). These people are facing moderate to large food consumption gaps or meet their basic food needs through depleting essential assets or employing other emergency coping strategies. This situation is largely attributed to a poor 2018/19 season which was characterized by erratic rainfall patterns and Fall Army-Worm pest infestation.
The COVID-19 pandemic is expected to exacerbate food security, nutrition, livelihood, WASH and protection needs. Despite a relatively low number of confirmed cases in Zimbabwe (176) as of May 27, 2020, the pandemic puts at risk the gains made over the past few years. First, the COVID-19 pandemic has exacerbated the need to establish adequate hygiene facilities and handwashing stations overlaid with information and guidance on preventive measures. Improved hygiene has demonstrated its effectiveness in combating the spread of COVID-19 globally. The disruption of food supply chains and an increase in demand for basic food commodities has led to increase prices of some basic commoditieDisruption of livelihoods in rural areas is likely to increase due to limited demand and availability of farm labour, and deprioritisation of labour-intensive crops reducing their production and availability. In urban areas, the movement restrictions are greatly affecting ability to work and is resulting in high unemployment and loss of wages. Consequently, both rural and urban households will have even lower incomes, limited capacity to purchase basic food stuffs and reduced consumption of nutritious diets. The 2020 HRP estimates an additional increase of the rural and urban caseload consequent to COVID-19 by 200,000 bringing the total population of people in need to 4.6million people.
Welthungerhilfe will, guided by the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification, build on its work in Gokwe North and South (urban) under the Livelihoods and Food Security Program to respond to the food insecurity crisis through the implementation of a 6 months project whose overall objective is to improve food security for the most vulnerable households in Gokwe in the context of COVID-19. The first component of the project will meet the immediate food security needs of 1000 COVID-19 returnees in 6 wards in the urban part of Gokwe using unconditional cash transfers, with the goal of ensuring that beneficiaries have access to the whole food basket comprising of 7.5kg cereal, 1.5kg pulses and 0.75kg vegetable oil per person per month. The modality for the transfers will be flexible and determined my market assessments which will be done at the beginning of the project. Beneficiary selection will be done remotely through consultations with local authorities and relevant stakeholders and development agencies also implementing in the area. The project will specifically target the most food insecure and vulnerable households, particularly those with COVID-19 returnees and older person headed households, and households with people with underlying health conditions. The second component of the action will provide support for emergency agricultural production and rebuild household food security for 1 000 rural households in Gokwe North. Activities under this component will include provision of seed packs, training in Good Agronomic Practices (GAP) and awareness raising on COVID-19.

Under this objective, the following activities are proposed: The project will use multiple platforms to disseminate gender sensitive information on COVID 19, health and hygiene. The COVID 19 pandemic is still a new disease and communities need continual messaging on how to prevent themselves from contracting the disease and also how they respond in case they contract the disease. Multi platforms include comic books for children, radio sessions, mobile messaging and drive through campaigns. Messaging will also emphasize on privacy and safety of vulnerable groups such as older persons women and people with disabilities. 2 - Indicators

At the inception of the project a MEAL plan will be developed, which will summarize the main indicators, present data collection tools and methods as well as plan the monitoring visits to the project sites. The plan will explain how the project performance indicators are tracked and analyzed to monitor progress and evaluate its effectiveness. For the tools, a blended design of qualitative, quantitative and rights based participatory approaches will be used. The data collection methodology will include review of documents, individual semi and structured interviews, observations and focus group discussions. For this project, monitoring will take two forms: implementation monitoring, carried out by the project team on the ground, who will ensure that activities happen as planned. In addition, Results-Based Monitoring, supervised by the Accountability (CHS) and M&E Manager who will look into the long-term change achieved through the project activities. Project personnel will be trained in data collection, entry, analyses, report writing, collection of stories of most significant change and disseminating. The following indicators will be measured for success: Number of comic books printed and disseminatedNumber of DWSSC members trained on hygiene, COVID-19, prevention of sexual abuse and exploitation, Gender Based Violence etcNumber of VHWs trainedNumber of handwashing stations set up and at use in public placesNumber of community members trained and showing knowledge on good hygieneNumber of community campaigns held
