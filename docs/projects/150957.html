Project Summary


Years of armed conflict in Somalia has resulted in widespread contamination of explosive remnants of war (ERW) and landmines, the proliferation of small arms and light weapons and a steady rise in the use of improvised explosive devices (IEDs) which has resulted many people to live with disability. Victim Assistance in Somalia is often overlooked with limited services available for the survivors of mines/ERW and EIDs in Somalia. In response to these threats, UNMAS is requesting $200,000 USD for a project that is aimed to embed risk education, disability awareness and referral for mine action services into the functions of the aid community and relevant governmental institutions that are working in support of vulnerable populations such as internally displaced persons (IDP), returnees and those living in areas of conflict and where natural disasters have occurred

Needs

Explosive hazards affect both adults and youth, to various extents. For instance, from January 2017 to April 2018, out of 81 recorded casualties caused by ERW, 49 were boys and 16 girls. This indicates that 80% of deaths and injuries caused by ERW were among children and youth. In Somalia, due to cultural norms, boys are more involved in outdoor activities such as herding livestock, gathering wood and food, or collecting scrap metal, instances when they can come across mines and ERW. Several incidents have occurred because of a child finding an explosive device, bringing it into a residential area and causing it to function due to tampering, thereby often resulting in multiple deaths and injuries. Female survivors of these incidents face additional challenges such as limited access to medical and other services, discrimination and marginalisation including abandonment.

Activities

300 focal points among humanitarian organizations supporting vulnerable groups are provided with Training of Trainer (ToT) on landmines/ERW risk education and on how to refer local populations to mine action service providers (e.g. SEMA, police, survivors assistance organizations) while rising the disability awareness through sensitizing the local authorities. Links and partnerships between people at risk and responders (e.g. Somali Police EOD teams, SEMA and other mine action organizations) are strengthened with a view to improving mine action service delivery.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

In the result of ongoing conflicts and wars that happened in the past since 1977 significant explosive hazards are left behind in Somalia. Landmine contamination is mainly affected communities located in boarder areas between Somalia and Ethiopia while ERW contaminated communities throughout Somalia wherever the armed clashes have happened. Lives and livelihoods of IDPs and returnees are prone to explosive hazards due to movement. Host communities also suffers because explosive hazards block their access to the land thus put their lives at risk of being killed or injured. The project will target IDPs, returnees and host communities that are located proximity of mines and other ERW contamination by providing risk education. The project aims to train focal points among the IDPs and communities on disability awareness raising to promote positive perception, capabilities and contribution of the people with disability in the societies.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The project will take in to consideration the gender aspect of the mine/ERW impacts as the impact of explosive hazards on men, women, girls and boys can be different due to mobility patterns and roles and responsibilities in the society. Overlooking the specific gender and age requirements from the explosive hazards perspective can be devastative. For example vital information could be lost if mine action during surveys misses to consult with youth and people above the age of 40 who have witnessed the wars happened along the border of Ethiopia and Somalia in 1970s. The project will make sure gender is considered during the design and implementation of disability awareness raising campaign. Female and children with disabilities are more vulnerable to discrimination and social exclusion.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Due to the fragile clan based social set up in Somalia, competition between clans over the limited resources and the race of political domination made Somalia more vulnerable for conflict and violence. Mine action interventions can make things worse by overlooking this aspect. The risk of conflict and violence can be mitigated by adopting a community based explosive hazard management. The approach will involve careful analysis of the project location from the conflict and violence perspective. The project will provide specific training to the field personnel who will be working with communities in relation to the project implementation on how to interact with different clans. They will constantly monitor this risk and take appropriate action when needed at the same time the project will seek to improve local cooperation between the communities through liaison and provision of Mine Action services.
