Project Summary

The three existing community centers in Hanano city, Aleppo , Jdidet Artuz, and Homs as well as the newly planned community centers in Kashkoul and As-Suqaylabiyah provide protection services to the most vulnerable people living in the neighbourhoods around them. Target groups of the services are children, their parents, women, survivors of GBV as well as Persons with specific needs. Services are provided by trained staff according to standards and protocols of social work.

Needs

The conflict in Syria grew out of what started as the civil unrest in 2011. Since then several million civilians have been displaced, some have sought refuge in neighboring countries and others have undertaken the journey to Europe in search for a newer, safer home. Specifically in Aleppo, around 30% of HHs have at least one member missing (Humanitarian Needs Assessment Programme for Syria (UN Survey, June 2018). Amongst the civilians that remained in Syria, 6.1 million have been displaced, some of them already several times due to changing front lines (HNO2018). In 2018, 1,250,956 Syrians returned; the top sub-district that welcomed returnees was Jebel Saman, Aleppo (MNM, Dec 2018). 89% of them returned to their own houses, often needing to restart their lives there completely. In this fluid and uncertain situation, children and women bear the brunt of the conflict since many families live in dire needs and parents struggle to provide for their children. Lost livelihoods or unemployment significantly reduce parents’ ability to provide for themselves and for their families. In the meantime, savings are depleted after several years of conflict and poverty is on the rise. Many men have been recruited into the armed forces or have joined opposition groups; many of them have not returned since and have left behind their wives with children in need of protection. Excess male mortality and out-migration, especially those in the 20-35 age brackets, are evidence of this trend (UN Survey,2018 ). We observed in our community centers that elderly often focus on covering needs of their children and support them to cover needs of their grand-children. They don’t claim support even if it is urgently needed. This self-imposed marginalization can be addressed by including elderlies in services offered at community-based protection activities and through providing material assistance to them if they are vulernable. . But the dire economic situation of families has also other consequences, a frequently observed coping mechanism is that boys and girls drop out of school: boys so that they can assist in earning income, and girls taking over the domestic role of their mother (UN Survey,2018 ). This negative coping mechanism severely hampers the future prospects of the youth and of school-going children. It contributes significantly to the overall result that 2.1m girls and boys are out of school and 1.35 million are at risk of dropping out (UN Survey,2018 ). In the community centers already supported by Dorcas, we observe mainly the following child protection risks: Child Labour, Children having Psychosocial Distress, Violence against Children, Child Marriage, Unaccompanied and Separated Children. In As-Suqaylabiyah and in Homs we have also observed negative coping mechanisms such as drug abuse and drug trading done by youth . In As-Suqaylabiyah, we’ve also observed children (below 18) joining armed actors in the region. Due to excess male mortality and out-migration in the age brackets 20 – 35, many women were required to take over the role of caregivers, which exposed them to multiple vulnerabilities that traditionally lay outside their role. Many women had to pay a heavy price for this and were exposed to gender based violence. These harmful coping strategies have led to the significant number of 1,052,505 People in Need (PiN) in need of protection measures in Aleppo (HNO 2019) – the city is at a severity scale of 4 in the protection sector (HNO 2019) and hosts 248,584 IDPs (HNO 2019). Jdidet Artuz accounts for 96,981 PiN and includes 96,733 IDPs (HNO 2018). Qatana sub-district has a severity scale of 5 in the protection sector (HNO 2019) and hosts 226,611 PiN in need of protection services (HNO 2019). Homs city has a severity scale of 5 in the protection sector (HNO 2019) and hosts 401,432 PiN in need of protection measures (HNO 2019). As-Suqaylabiyah sub-district has a severity scale of 3 in the protection sector (HNO 2019) hosts 14,828 PiN

Implementation of activities

Activities will be implemented in the community centers in the respective areas that serve the communities around them. Existing community centers are located in Hanano city, in Aleppo, in Hamidiyeh neighborhood serving the old city of Homs and in Jdidet Artouz at a different location than the center run by GOPA . Planned new locations are in Kashkoul area of Jeremana, where we plan to offer complementary services to those that GOPA offers in their community center in Jeremana - since the assessment of our partner indicated significant gaps in the provision of services there. The other location is As-Suqaylabiyah, where we are aware that no community center exists. For the last two locations we remain open to discussion with the joint protection sector and our local partners to ensure that gaps are covered and duplication of services is minimized. Activities in As-Suqaylabiyah and Kashkoul are implemented through the Greek Orthodox Archdiocese and the Syriac Catholic Committee respectively. Our focus throughout the year is to focus on providing quality protection services, which means that a sizeable part of our intervention will be focused on providing quality capacity building and coaching of local implementing partners. Also the services integrate the different aspects of protection such as community-based protection services, but also group and individual PSS activities for vulnerable beneficiaries identified in the communities, group and individual PSS activities for children, based in the community center following the curriculum IRC has developed for Syrian refugees in the Middle East, case management for children, positive parenting courses for parents, awareness raising for GBV and CP, specialized GBV services in the community center itself and training for GBV providers, which are our local implementing partners as well as health staffs. Dorcas and partners do provide specialized GBV services in the centers. Activities include case management, we do refer to mental health services, as per need. All beneficiaries that attend services will be included in awareness raising activities, on protection issues. We increasingly included home work support to children that struggle to keep up at school. Since we noted that children that are newly integrated into schools often struggle to keep up with their home work and come home with low marks. Due to economic challenges also parents are not able to provide support for home work. For this reason the community centers offer this service in their own facilities to school going children who have low marks. All services will be implemented through trained staffs that follow the social work methodology. Dorcas undertakes regular monitoring, additional training and coaching to ensure quality services are provided to people in need. Nearly half of this project is already funded, for the remaining funding we have ongoing promising discussions with potential donors. All activities in these centers are funded by Dorcas.
