Project Summary

HRO aims to continue providing CP services for IDPs and HC in our already existed community centres in Idleb (Jisr Ash Shugur, Darkosh, and Kafr Jales) and in Aleppo (Afrin) and establishing a new community centre in Aleppo (Afrin) and through outreach visits to the widows’ camps (1 in Janudiyeh and 2 in Darkosh) to target the widowed women and children with different CP activities, also HRO will target those children outside the camps who were separated from their mothers due to exceeding the age specified for them to stay in these camps, as they will be targeted with CP CM, including emergency case fund, CVA, and IPA to reduce the cases of child labor, CAAFAG, exploitation and other issues that they may be exposed to, considering that HRO has its own previous experience in providing CP services in emergency situations and is the focal point of emergency response in Jisr Ash Shugur.
HRO will train employees on (principles of CP, CP CM, FTR, Structured PSS, Inclusion of PWD), HRO will provide services to children, their families, caregivers, and local community, including IDPs and HC and the most vulnerable groups such as young children, UASC children and children with disabilities in addition to those who are at risk of protection. Awareness-raising activities for community and caregivers on CP will be provided including CP services in crisis, parenting skills, early marriage, child labor, importance of education, and any other suggested topics by beneficiaries considering cultural context. Also, women and men will be engaged in parenting programs. PSS structured and semi-structured for children to enhance flexibility and adaptation skills for them, with several structured programs, including Flexibility Program for Children, which is provided to children and parents together, the instant challenge program. CM for children in line with SOPs, including CM of UASC based on FTR standard, and using UNICEF for response, reunification, or alternative care support.
4 community committees for children and adults will be established (10 members each) will be trained on basic CP and identification and referral. Also humanitarian actors will be trained on CP minimum standards. M&E team will be responsible for ensuring confidentiality of beneficiaries’ data, through storing files in a safe place within the facilities, coding names of cases, in addition to ensuring the quality of the activities and applying the accountability mechanisms, considering the gender equality.
HRO will adapt the activities and centers to fit the needs of PwDs and elderly through installing the needed tools such as ramps, toilet rails, extra lighting in the corridors and WCs for those with poor vision, etc. Also, transportation will be provided for PwD to participate in the center’s activities. Elderly and PwD will be targeted in all project activities including (IPA) and case fund.
HRO will also provide CP services to ensure that IDPs and HC are effectively involved in the project lifecycle from design to implementation to feedback through the already established protected feedback channels to help them report suggestions, complaints, and PSEA concerns. 9,040 beneficiaries including (5,876 IDPs and 3,164 HC) will be targeted with the project’s activities including 5,785 Females, 3,255 Males (825 Elderly, 5,245 Adults, 2,900 Children, 70 Young Children, and 452 PwD) through 7 community centers including 23 protection staff (7 outreach teams, 9 case managers, and 14 facilitators).
There will be 1 CP supervisor in each governorate to supervise case management teams ensuring the quality performance, staff wellbeing, and the regular monitoring and improvement of project’s progress.
Also, HRO will ensure smooth coordination with the protection NGO partners local councils, and stakeholders throughout the project period, in addition to considering the WHO standard COVID-19 preventive measures throughout the implementation period.

4 - Needs

As of January 2021, around 13.4 million people are estimated to require some form of humanitarian and protection assistance, including 6 million in acute need, due to a convergence of factors including the loss of essential livelihoods and income, mounting food insecurity, limited access to basic services such as health, WASH, education, nutrition and critical protection services, inadequate shelter conditions as well as the immediate and longer-term effects of the COVID-19 pandemic as indicated in HNO (March 2021), where 25% of them have disabilities and 75% of them are women and children, where children with and without disabilities comprise 44% of the people in need across Syria. As of February 2021, approximately 1.7 million IDPs were living in 1,385 camps or informal sites (including 24,800 people displaced in January and February 2021 alone), Women and children represent 80% of this caseload.According to the Voices 2021, 62% of communities surveyed agreed that adolescent girls between the ages of 15-17 drop out of school to work, while that number jumped to 84% in relation to adolescent boys of the same age.According to CP Situation Monitoring Annual Report 2020, 79.90% of KIs reported children are in need for focused non-specialized services, 44.37% reported for the need of the focused specialized services, 71.79% reported a need for specialized services for caregivers, 85.64% reported that children are engaged in some types of work that prevents them from going to school.HRO conducted a Protection Need Assessment during Mar & Apr 2021, where 70% reported that there is child labour in Jisr Ash Shugur and Kafr Jales and 73% reported that there is school dropout in Darkosh, while 53% reported the same in Afrin.

5 - Implementation of activities

After ensuring the preparedness of the project logistics and recruiting procedures, staff will be trained on CP basics, CP Case Management, PFA, PSS, inclusion of PwDs. Afterwards, teams will proceed with conducting the following activities: CP awareness raising on Parenting Skills, child labor, importance of education, how to gain your child's confidence, how to deal with your child's questions, CAAFAG, the role of the family and the community in CP, and children with disabilities through in-center and field visits by the mobile team. Awareness sessions will be held for children who are aged from 8 to 12 according to the safety train series that aims to raise their awareness about the topics of “cooperation, honesty, hygiene promotion, body is mine, and child labor”, conducting PSS activities (drawing, storytelling, sport, singing, clown activities) for boys and girls in centres and schools, targeting each group of children with 10 sessions of structured PSS activities, conducting CP monitoring assessments to identify CP needs/risks in the target communities, training widows’ camp managers and security and safety officials on CP prevention, referral system, and communication skills, besides training women committees in widows’ camps on CP prevention, PSS, referral system, suicide prevention, and communication skills.Key messages and IEC materials on COVID-19 will be disseminated at the end of each session. Also, in case of severe COVID-19 situations awareness-raising sessions will be conducted online via WhatsApp or one of the social media platforms (based on beneficiaries’ consents).Complaints’ mechanisms are in place to ensure quality, transparency, and access of vulnerable groups to all available services.
