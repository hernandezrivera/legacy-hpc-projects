Project Summary

Over 1 million people have been displaced since the conflict started on April 15th. Of this over 843,100 are internally displaced within Sudan. As of May 18th, 2023, COR reported that the number of refugees who reached White Nile state is 122,000 individuals, 65,000 in Elslam locality, and 58,000 in Jablian locality (all of these numbers refer to South Sudanese). Algaya camp in White Nile is hosting now 29,000 new refugees who fled from the war. At the same time, there are 50,000 refugees from different nationalities who have fled from the war into Aljazirah state (Ethiopians, South Sudanese, and others). As such, this project aims to foster an urgent first emergency and life-saving response, to provide timely multi-cluster life-saving assistance to crisis-affected people to reduce mortality and morbidity and mitigate protection risks and respond to the protection needs through humanitarian action.

1 - Needs

Before the conflict, Khartoum was hosting almost half of the 300,000 South Sudanese facing high levels of vulnerability (BaNVA, 2021). The conflict escalated on April 15th has been exasperating the already difficult situation of the country, causing a large-scale displacement and shattered access to food, water, cash, fuel, health care, and other basic services for millions of people. In addition, the local population is facing high insecurity levels, shortage of water, disease outbreaks, and increased food insecurity and malnutrition. Consequently, most refugees present in the open areas of Khartoum, as well as the local community, fled the war in search of safety, food, drinking water, and essential services. Therefore, in 2023, COOPI will continue to address the WASH, ES/NFI, and FSL, protection needs of refugees and IDPs in the most affected localities of Khartoum and Gedaref, where COOPI has a persistent presence, planning to expand to Port Sudan, White Nile, and Al Jazeera its current operations. In particular, COOPI intervention will focus on the following needs: SHELTER:Shelter continues to represent a major concern in the affected communities of Sudan. Other risks such as torrential rains and floods are likely to affect refugees moving/present, particularly in the newly formed settlements where refugees live in a make-shift shelter, posing high public health and GBV risks. WASH:In the selected States, refugees experience high levels of WASH insecurity across the sub-sectors including latrine adequacy, poor access to clean water and handwashing facilities, and poor access to waste disposal. In the communities of intervention, refugees do not have access to hand pumps or water yards, as hand-washing facilities are non-existent or out-of-service. The conflict has negatively impacted the WASH infrastructures in the targeted location with limited or no maintenance to the existing WASH infrastructures while some water plants in Khartoum and North Darfur have been destroyed. As a result, communities are forced to buy water, leading to negative coping mechanisms due to inflation of over 300%. The likelihood of communicable disease outbreaks is very high. FSL:The current conflict in Sudan is worsening the food security of a larger proportion of the population. According to FAO analysis, the worsening food security situation has resulted in the movement of populations from better Food Security Phases to worsening phases. It is estimated that 40 percent of the 17, 580, 000 people (which is 7,032,000) in Phase 2 are now in Phase 3. Currently, the total number of people estimated to be in need of life-saving humanitarian assistance is 19 million according to the WFP report. A large number of the refugees previously living in Khartoum are now moving into Gedaref State daily due to the current conflict. On the other hand, Ethiopian and Eritrean refugees that once resided in Gedaref are also returning to the Gedaref.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

COOPI promotes environmentally friendly and risk avert mechanisms in its WASH and shelter/NFIs response projects and livelihoods diversification interventions do have impact on the health of the public, environment and natural resources. Interventions to preserve and protect the environment will be included from the design phase, implementation and monitoring of each program intervention. The mitigation measures could include but not limited to: analysis of the potential impact of key interventions and discussion with community leaders and sector experts; regular joint water and sanitation facilities monitoring and supervision; emphasis on environmental education and capacity building for increased environmental awareness among beneficiaries and implementing partners; solid and liquid waste management; actions to avoid water pollution due to excavation, backfilling and concreating, hauling and damping of earth materials; controlling formation of stagnant water pool to avoid soil erosion and breeding mosquitoes. The Water User Committees shall be trained on water conservation to promote prudent use of water resources. Additionally, COOPI staff will work closely with the community to ensure that local materials used in construction are sources sustainable and where possible use of by-products of agriculture such as harvested crop stocks and other by products are reused in construction of latrine superstructures. Youth, women, men and others will be organized into water users’ committees, hygiene promoters, natural resource management groups, shelter committees where appropriate and tailored training will be provided to contribute positively to the management of natural resources and peaceful co-existence. Furthermore, climate change, agricultural development andpopulation growth have made water resources extremely important to Sudan’s economic development and social cohesion; COOPI will focus on finding alternatives from fuel powered water pumps to systems which are affordable, able to scaled, environmentally sustainable and climate smart.The project activities will take into account impact on environment and local resources by attempting to monitor the over-use of material (e.g., construction material, water, etc.) as well as the waste production after kits' distribution and other activities Furthermore, COOPI will continue monitoring markets and opt for purchase of locally available material wherever is possible.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

COOPI adheres to the Core Humanitarian Standards in all programmes and ensures Protection and the avoidance of negative impact (CHS 3) in integrated in project design and implementation. COOPI has in place and organizational safeguarding all programs so that activities (i) prioritize safety, dignity and access, in addition to avoiding causing harm, (ii) ensure equality and meaningful access, (iii) includemechanisms for beneficiary accountability, and (iv) emphasize participation and empowerment to meet SHF minimum requirements. This is mainstreamed through awareness sessions, training, feedback mechanism, and follow up. For example, where COOPI will implement shelter assistance on provision of durable solutions, a minimum due diligence tools will be set in place in order to check land tenure or ownership and prevent further risk of eviction or conflict. In addition, participatory consultations andfeedback systems are set up to ensure the intervention is appropriate and does not exacerbate underlying issues or conflict, nor causes this. In line with the SPHERE standards, the project will ensure that targeted beneficiaries have safe access toassistance and services based on need and without discrimination. Specific emphasize is put on protection from GBV and CP risks, through conducted gender- and age specific assessments and including different age and gender groups in project design and implementation. Efforts will also be made to accommodate people with specific needs, ensuring access to services. The roll-out of Protection policies is the responsibility of COOPI Sudan's Protection Focal Person. All Project Management Units are expected to get trained and apply such policies in the day-to-day work.Local partners - which are mandatory for INGOs according to HAC regulations - will also be selected based on existing policies, signatory to COOPI’s safeguarding standards and trained on Safeguarding and Protection.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Community Participation: The project adapts a community-based approach where communities are involved in the design and implementation of activities. The project will be designed in a manner that humanitarian assistance will be provided in line and in proportion to the needs identified and documented in targeted areas, in adherence with SHF guidelines. COOPI will involve the beneficiaries and respond to their feedback when planning, implementing, monitoring and evaluating its program. COOPI’ accountability includes four pillars i.e. risk management, capacity assessment and monitoring the performance of implementing partners, planning, monitoring and reporting, and project and partner auditing. COOPI will provide space for communities to shape their own recovery and for COOPI and partners to better deliver on commitments to stakeholders, including the people COOPI serves and the resource partners who make assistance possible. COOPI will ensure timely information sharing among stakeholders regarding targeted communities. COOPI will focus on various approaches to collect information for community-based indicators. Feedback and Complaint Mechanisms: COOPI will establish effective feedback mechanisms and a Community-Based Complaints Mechanism (CBCM) so that COOPI and partners can incorporate feedback and address problems rapidly, including for sensitive issues like protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) by humanitarian or development workers. COOPI and partner staff will be trained and oriented on SEA. Two community accountability focal persons will be selected from each target community (a woman and man) and trained to facilitate the process. In line with COOPI’ Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) policies and procedures, COOPI will consult communities in establishing an appropriate feedback and response mechanism to give each community the opportunity to identify and report issues on program quality and protection concerns. The system will address both sensitive and non-sensitive issues while upholding confidentially and nonretaliation principles. Specifically, COOPI will link project beneficiaries with existing feedback mechanisms in the area for formats like: (1) an existing Hotline establish by COOPI Sudan for all projects, where beneficiaries can make free calls to interact with MEAL Coordinator for any feedback/complaint and receive a response in immediate timeframe; and (2) Active outreach to project beneficiaries by the MEAL Officer, who will take findings from random beneficiary satisfaction visits to regular program management meetings, or (3) suggestion boxes to offer confidential feedback as appropriate.COOPI will implement a standard Remote Project Monitoring System as per COOPIstandard MEAL Procedures and PSEA procedures.
