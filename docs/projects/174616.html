Project Summary

Populations in NWSW continue to suffer severe consequences of the ongoing-armed conflict with the situation exacerbated by COVID-19. NW is 5th most affected region with 18/19 Health Districts affected by COVID (OCHA, 2020). About 3 million people have been affected- 705,800 IDPs and 360,500 Returnees with 1,968,578 people affected in NW, 782,535 in need of assistance with 232,300 IDPs and 204,298 returnees (Humanitarian Response Plan, 2021). The conflict characterized by the destruction of the already fragile economy, massive displacements due to continuous arm battles coupled with COVID 19, is having disastrous consequences on the populations of the embattled region with women and children suffering the most. The conflict has a major impact on the WASH situation of the population as only 50 percent of households in the NW/SW regions have access to improved sources of water (MSNA, 2019). Almost 61% of households do not have access to functional latrines. WASH is one of the most important needs in humanitarian response without which, many people especially children will die from diseases that are usually caused by lack of sanitation, inadequate safe water and poor hygiene. Without access to basic water and sanitation services, and without the practices of good hygiene, the danger of diarrhoea, cholera and other disease outbreak is high. Without enough safe water for drinking, cooking and personal hygiene, it is difficult to maintain good health and fight off illnesses. Without proper sanitation, water supplies can become contaminated and diseases can spread rapidly. This situation is worst among IDPs and returnees where a lack of access to WASH facilities in the areas where they have taken refuge deprives them of the opportunity to make their shelters into homes, and their settlements into communities and the prevention of COVID 19. Responding to the WASH needs of IDPs, Returnees and vulnerable host populations is necessary, as access to water is a key challenge both in terms of quantity and in terms of quality in the NW and SW.
Purpose
Respond to WASH needs of IDPs, Returnees and vulnerable host communities in hard-to-reach areas of the NWSW.
Objectives: Improve access of IDPs, Returnees, and vulnerable host populations to safe drinking water and hygiene services.
To intervene, the project will improve access of IDPs, Returnees and Vulnerable host populations to safe drinking water and hygiene services by ensuring children and families have access to WASH services and adopt good hygiene practices and essential WASH services integrating COVID-19. Specific activities will include distribution of individual hygiene kits, family WASH kits with aqua-tabs for water purification and jerry cans for storage. This will be accompanied with hygiene promotion, education on water purification and guidance on the correct use of WASH infrastructure that has been set-up for the emergency (if the affected population usually practice open defecation) and the production of local handwashing stations. To ensure equitable access to safe drinking water and hygiene facilities to minimize COVID-19 infections among poor and vulnerable communities, select rehabilitation of non-functional or dilapidated water points/systems will be undertaken. There will be a focus on the rehabilitation of Community Water points/small water supply systems. Water management Committees (WMC) will be revitalized/ trained while respecting COVID19 barriers, to ensure water infrastructure is maintained.
Targets
- 50,000 people sensitized on good hygiene practices/COVID 19
- 25,900 people utilizing improved water services from 10 constructed/rehabilitated water points and revitalized Water Management Committees.
- 50,000 people (10,000HHs) with WASH kits
- 3,500 individuals with hygiene-kits
- 1,000 HHs without latrines with latrine digging kits and squatting slabs for latrine construction.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Water is a key challenge in terms of quantity and quality in NWSW. Few people manage to secure enough water to meet their needs with most communities relying solely on unimproved water sources. Lack of containers to fetch and store water, not enough or too far water points makes access to water further limited. In addition, access to hygiene is very limited as few people have soap and handwashing facilities at home. Few affected families had enough water containers, and soap for hand washing explained by the fact that access to the market is limited, especially outside urban centres, and in conflict-affected regions in NWSW. The situation made worst by poor knowledge and attitude about hygiene practices as an overwhelming majority reported that few people or nobody usually wash hands with soap, especially for communities living in the bush and in rural areas, as well as displaced populations. Reports also show an overall poor sanitation situation with limiting access to sanitation being the unavailability of latrines, and the fact that the existing ones often do not ensure privacy and gender separation. As a result, open defecation is a widespread practice, especially among communities living in the bush and rural areas (Shelter and WASH clusters, 2018). The situation improved a bit with interventions and as at 2019, 50% of households in NWSW had access to improved sources of water with 39% of households having access to functional latrines (MSNA, 2019). The precarious WASH situation is even more concerning considering the health conditions faced by affected populations and their access to healthcare. Water-related diseases are widespread, with many reports of malaria, respiratory diseases and watery diarrhea as main health issues affecting children. Access to healthcare remains limited, with reports that people do not visit any health service provider when children are sick, especially those living in the bush or in rural villages as health facilities either are closed or ran be CHWs who are often times not trained. WASH is one of the most important needs in humanitarian response without which, many people especially children will die from diseases that are usually caused by lack of sanitation, inadequate safe water and poor hygiene. Without access to basic water and sanitation services, and without the practices of good hygiene, the danger of diarrhoea, cholera and other disease outbreak is high. Without enough safe water for drinking, cooking and personal hygiene, it is difficult to maintain good health and fight off illnesses. Without proper sanitation, water supplies can become contaminated and diseases can spread rapidly. This situation is worst among IDPs and returnees where a lack of access to WASH facilities in the areas where they have taken refuge deprives them of the opportunity to make their shelters into homes, and their settlements into communities and the prevention of COVID 19 difficult. This project will improve access of IDPs, Returnees and Vulnerable host populations to safe drinking water and hygiene services by ensuring children and families have access to WASH services and adopt good hygiene practices and essential WASH services integrating COVID-19. This will be through the distribution of individual hygiene kits, family WASH kits with aqua-tabs for water purification and jerry cans for storage. Accompanied with hygiene promotion, education on water purification, production of local handwashing stations and construction of gender sensitive family/public latrines. Select rehabilitation of non-functional or dilapidated water points/systems to ensure equitable access to safe drinking water and hygiene facilities and minimize COVID-19 infections among poor and vulnerable communities. Rehabilitation of community water points/small water supply systems and revitalization of Water Management Committees to ensure water infrastructure is maintained will be undertaken.
