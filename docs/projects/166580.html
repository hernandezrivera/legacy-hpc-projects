Project Summary

On 31st December 2018, China alerted the World Health Organisation (WHO) about cases of pneumonia in Wuhan City, Hubei Province. On 7th January 2020, authorities confirmed that they had identified a novel coronavirus as the cause of pneumonia. The virus was named COVID-19. On 30th January 2020 WHO declared the outbreak a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC) under the International Health Regulations (IHR 2005). By 7th March 2020 cases of COVID-19 were confirmed on every continent. On 11th March 2020 COVID-19 was declared a global pandemic by the WHO.
As of 29 March, 634,835 confirmed cases of COVID-19 and 29,891 deaths (Case fatality rate 4,7 %) were reported from more than 200 cases countries globally. In Africa 39 countries had reported a total of 3005 confirmed cases and 51 deaths. Affected African countries include at least seven SADC member states including South Africa, Zambia, Eswatini, Namibia, Tanzania, Seychelles, the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) and Zimbabwe. In Zimbabwe, as of 21st of May 2020, there have been 51 confirmed cases with 4 deaths, 18 recoverie. Given the fact that the epidemiology of COVID-19 is dynamically evolving it is essential that Zimbabwe is prepared to contain spread of the outbreak within the country and safely respond to eventual increase in caseload.
Owing to a myriad of factors Zimbabwe is ill-prepared to respond to the COVID-19 Pandemic. The macro-economic situation is stark with inflation and shortages (food, water, fuel, electricity) dominating the landscape. The spiraling inflation has put soap and water out of the fiscal reach of the majority of the populace, who also do not have access to clean water. The country’s health system is currently under duress with shortages of critical staff, high levels of turnover and absenteeism. Furthermore, there are shortages of medicines, personal protective equipment (PPE) and many health care facilities (HCFs) are dilapidated and lack adequate water and sanitation (WASH) facilities. On 19th March 2020 the Government of Zimbabwe declared COVID-19 state of emergency and banned all social gatherings and is promoting social distancing as well as hand-washing with soap and as an alternative using alcohol-based rub as an effective method for curtailing the spread of COVID-19. However, the general economic situation in Zimbabwe has significantly eroded the coping capacity of families to purchase soap and other hygiene items. As of 19th March 2020, the informal market rate is USD$1 to ZWL$44. Zimbabwe is also experiencing a drought and the resulting low levels of water in reservoirs across the country has affected water supply. The lack of access to and affordability of water and soap means that the potential of COVID-19 to spread rapidly in Zimbabwe is very high.
In this regard, Zimbabwe FCTZ proposes to support government efforts in combating the COVID-19 spread through the implementation of a 6 months project focusing on infection prevention and control as well as risk communication and community engagement. The response will target communities in 7 districts namely Chinhoyi, Chegutu, Gutu, Kadoma, Kariba and Makonde to support HCF to ensure availability of critical WASH infrastructure, providing critical hand washing facilities and supporting mass media campaigns.

Associated response plan

Zimbabwe Humanitarian Response Plan 2020

Plan fields

1 - Activities

• Support the setting up of 162 hand washing stations at various public places. Hand washing facilities will be set up at fruit and vegetable markets in high risk areas. In areas where there is running water efforts will be made to increase number of hand washing stations to avoid over crowding. Where there is no running water a volunteer will be put in place to oversee the filling up of the hand washing facility and ensure its safety for at least 4 months. Disinfectants will be mixed with water to protect users and soap will be provided in place of disinfectants where this is feasible. Hand washing facilities will also be established at business centres, bus termini and communal taps and hand pumps, where people come together in groups. Efforts will be made to install enough hand washing facilities to avoid crowding. • Support mass media campaigns on COVID-19 prevention using various channels of communication (billboards, radio shows, radio jingles etc) taking into account literacy levels and access of different groups to reach an estimated 143 857 women, children and men. This activity will avoid getting into groups• Support the health system to ensure availability of basic water and sanitation services in key prioritized health care facilities. Technical assessments will done for HCF and priority will be given to those most in need basing on the priority list provided by authorities. WASH infrastructure will be enhanced to ensure availability of basic needs • Providing access to critical hand washing facilities including soap and alcohol-based sanitizers through distribution of 7 973 hygiene kits. The project will distribute hygiene kits to vulnerable women and children including people living with disability to last a household up to three months. • Ensure field and front-line staff engaged in WASH humanitarian responses have adequate access to PPE and practice key risk reduction practices like social distancing. Based on the safety first principle the project will ensure that front-line staff are protect through provision of PPE and share information risk reduction. Efforts will be made to protect volunteers supporting the project by offering services at community level.
