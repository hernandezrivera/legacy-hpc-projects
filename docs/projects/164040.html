Project Summary

The main goal of the intervention is to contribute to improving access to education through supporting of six schools in Aleppo governorate in Atareb sub-district (three schools in Batabu and three schools in Jeineh) villages. According to HNO 2020 Severity Scale, Al Atareb sub-district was classified as 5, these villages are stable relatively and away from the clashes. Batabu is inhabited by 25,500 persons of residents and 12.500 persons from IDPs and Jeineh village is inhabited by 12000 persons of residents and 7.500 persons from IDPs and among them the newly displaced families from Idlib. The intervention will be through repairing and maintenance of classrooms such as (doors, windows, desks) provision of new teaching and learning materials and furniture (chairs, desks, whiteboards, cupboards, tables, solar units and fuel for heating) in addition to provide teachers and education personnel with incentives. These activities are related directly with HRP Education Sector’s objectives which promote and increase access to quality education for boys and girls in different ages after providing the needs and educational equipment. This support will improve the educational environment and secure access to disabilities to have and benefit from the educational services.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2020 there were 6.6 million children are estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance in Syria in 2020. The protracted crisis has had a pervasive impact on the lives of millions of children, adolescents, and youth. Violence, displacement, poverty, and a lack of positive engagement opportunities, also, there were 2.45 million children aged 5–17 out of school and 1.6 million children at risk of dropping out, considerable barriers remain for children to engage and continue with their education. MASRRAT conducted need assessment in Aleppo in Atareb sub-district - Batabu and Jeineh villages. the assessment included visits to schools and held many meetings with local councils, educational bodies, and school administrators. Atareb area includes 86851 children from boys and girls (3-17 years) in need of the education services and 2895 teachers from males and females in need to support in education fields such as educational tools, equipment, and salaries or incentives. Based on the need assessment, all the necessary needs of these schools were limited to the inefficiency of the schools in accommodating more students due to the large shortage of desks, furniture and other logistical supplies in addition to no salaries or incentives for volunteer teachers in these schools thus led to a shortage of staff and a decrease in the quality of education.The Schools in Batabu need to support three schools (one elementary, one middle and one high school) and include 1948 students (980 from HHs and 968 from IDPs and 81 teachers from the volunteers without salaries or incentives since the beginning of the school year.The schools in Jeineh that need to support three schools too and all of the elementary schools include 1408 students (1164 from HHs and 244 from IDPs and 53 teachers from the volunteers without salaries or incentives since the beginning of the school year.

5 - Implementation of activities

MASRRAT's team will sign Memorandum of Understanding (MOUs) with the local councils and Education Directorate. The process of engaging the Local Council will start with advertising about the project in the communities of intervention, explaining the mechanism of selecting the schools, and the number of potential beneficiaries under this project. MASRRAT will coordinate with the other I/NGOs, Education Directorate and local councils in the field in order to ensure the accuracy of implementation and to avoid duplication of aid provision with other humanitarian actors in the area. The main activities will be as following:-Provide teachers and education personnel with incentives (150 $ for every person for 4 months) -Provide maintenance and running costs (maintenance the desks, doors, windows, fuel for heating, paint, cleaning materials, stationary)-Provide schools with school furniture (desk, chairs, tables, cupboards, blackboards, laptops, printers, solar units) The disabled children will have access to schools and WASH by preparing a special stairway, a path for the disabled, support the schools with special toilets, providing schools with movable whiteboards and allocating special places for placing wheelchairs. MASRRAT will conduct the M&E policy to evaluate the project processes. Providing information about the project effectiveness and sustainability, and the lessons learned which will be effective in upcoming interventions. M&E will be carried out using different tools (Surveys, direct observations, questionnaires, complaints and PDMs) to receive the feedback directly from beneficiaries which might be reflected in the project implementation. The project implementation will be done through M&E officers in Hama governorate, the work-plan will be as the following:• Verify the number of equipment need rehabilitation • Verify the number of rehabilitated schools and equipped educational directorates.• Monitor the process of the rehabilitation of the schools

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The main points that the intervention will emphasize the following:-The intervention will present a high priority of the safety and security and dignity of the crisis-affected population. - Reduce physical, psychological and social risks (Do No harm) and minimize and mitigate as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the intervention which can increase people's vulnerability.- MASRRAT will promote access to services and assistance in Aleppo rural with two Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSS) centers which will be open for female beneficiaries who are willing to participate in the center's activities.- The affected population will have access to the intervention services equally as appropriate with the need and without any discrimination. children with disabilities males and females.-The project design aims to minimize and mitigate the negative coping strategies in the community of intervention such (selling assets, early marriage, and child labor). -The project will include awareness-raising to help the affected population to better-cope with the current situation and understand the negative consequences accordingly.- Assistance and services will be provided basing on the needs with no discrimination, Priority will be given to the vulnerable schools. - The intervention will facilitate participation and empowerment by supporting the development of self-protection capabilities and helping people to claim their rights in (shelter, food, health, water, sanitation, and education) especially the women groups. - the project also has designed depending on using gender analysis results which involved the gaps, impacts of the project on gender equality, access to control over resources, assets, benefits, the effects of the gender division of labour and decision making through the location of living and level of education.- The project is served the different needs of men’s/boys’, women ‘s and men’s with their priorities and strategic and practical needsç

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk management process designed to include all of the project life. It includes processes for risk management planning, identification, analysis, monitoring, and control. Many of these processes are updated throughout the project life-cycle as new risks can be identified at any time. According to the assumptions: the targeted locations of the project are considered relatively safe as there wasn't any kind of conflict for a period of time, and the targeted locations are accessible, in addition to, all materials and required inputs are available in the local markets inside Syria. MASRRAT team will take into consideration all precautions according to the following Potential risks: 1- Interference in schools selection processes Mitigation: At the beginning of the project, there will be a clear explanation for the mechanism of selection of the schools according to the needs and vulnerability and the approval of the local council and local authorities on these standards criteria, which in turn limit from the interventions processes. 2- High prices of materials and changes in exchange rates.Mitigation: price freeze for the prices with the vendors3- lack of some material and tools. Mitigation: Import from other regions inside Syria or from Turkey4 -Deterioration of the security situation in the project’s areas.Mitigation: Postpone the implementation process until the stabilization of the security situation.
