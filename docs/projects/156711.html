Project Summary

The purpose of this project is to provide much-needed rehabilitation, maintenance, and furnishing of schools in selected communities in Quneitra. The overall objective of the project is to ensure that students have access to a stable and appropriate educational environment to increase the probability that they have the opportunity to complete their education.
The project will 3 high schools, and vocational school in Qunaitra, reaching a total of 2700 boys and girls.

Needs

A report by No Lost Generation (NLG) indicates that as of April 2018, around 2.08 million school-aged children remain out of school inside Syria. According to the report there are several issues that perpetrate this situation including protection related issues and a lack of safety and security. The Humanitarian Response Plan outlines that the priority is to bring students back to the formal education environment with a special focus on those who are most vulnerable. This requires that schools in former conflict zones are equipped and have the capacity to provide a safe and secure environment. Particularly vulnerable are girls and young women as the conflict and lack of access to school has increased the incidence of child marriage and child labor. According to the World Bank Group (Educating Girls, Ending Child Marriage 2017), for each year that a girl completes her secondary education, she may reduce the chances of child marriage by 5% or more, however if the girls do not have access to quality, secure, and safe educational facilities their likelihood of attending school is greatly decreased.According to the NLG (2018) report, there is a significant shortage of quality and functional classrooms which has resulted in double shifts (sometimes more) in the schools that are functional, indicating that the rehabilitation of classrooms a priority to improve the learning environment. The HNO (2018) reports that more than 1 in 3 schools are damaged in Syria and the targeted area of Al-Kashniyyeh has been categorized as having severe needs.HELP conducted a comprehensive needs assessment of several schools in Quneitrah and found that there was substantial rehabilitation and maintenance needed for the schools to be able to function within standards and provide a safe and secure environment. Bombardments and conflict have destroyed much of the school infrastructure and theft and tampering have also added to the decline in the quality of the infrastructure. Furthermore, schools lack basic furnishing and will need to comply with the needs of vulnerable groups such as girls and those who are differently abled, ensuring that schools are accessible to everyone. The project aims to ensure that these schools are rehabilitated, maintained, and furnished to ensure that a total of 90 classrooms are prepared and have the capacity to provide a quality structural learning environment for students. - Ein Eltineh (Al-Khashniyyeh) High school (1000 student, 32 classrooms) - Qseibeh (Al-Khashniyyeh) High school (600 students, 18 classrooms) - Mashara High school (500 students, 20 classrooms) - Jbata Elkhashab vocational school (600 students, 20 classrooms) HELP conducted a comprehensive needs assessment of several schools in Quneitrah and found that there was substantial rehabilitation and maintenance needed for the schools to be able to function within standards and provide a safe and secure environment. Implementation of activities

Activities Implementation:The HELP Help team will supervise all project activities while contractors implement the project. HELP's Syria team will coordinate and manage the activities on the ground in Qunaitrah and report to the program management team in HQ. The activities will begin with a validation of the needs assessment to ensure that the most needed interventions/rehabilitation are being conducted. Once this is completed, the procurement process will begin for obtaining materials and in parallel, the contractors who will be conducting the rehabilitation will be recruited. Once all required materials are procured, the rehabilitation will take place in the 4 high schools and 1 vocational school, totaling 90 classrooms rehabilitated. Basic furnishings will also be procured and installed into the classrooms to establish classrooms that are prepared for students to attend the school. Project Management During Implementation:Project tolerances will be put into place to ensure that there is clarity as to which decisions the field team can make and when the changes or decisions need to be escalated to the program team. This helps to ensure that only authorized, assessed, and needed changes are made to the project and that decision making is done through appropriate channels. This method further enhances the accountability and transparency in the project. Project management tools will be used to clearly articulate roles and responsibilities and to track progress against planned at the activity and output levels. Risk registers and issues logs will be regularly updated to reduce impact on project activities and better ensure a smooth implementation. Control procedures for operating are in place in the form of procurement and financial procedures and will ensure against fraud and corrupt actions. Project activities will be closely aligned and coordinated with clusters and other agencies operating in Quneitrah by way of regular meetings and coordination in order to prevent duplication and better ensure maximum coverage of interventions for beneficiaries. Protection Risk Analysis

Risk/Threat: The rehabilitation and furnishing of these specific schools may cause tension between stakeholders whose schools are not being rehabilitated.Mitigating Measure: Engage local stakeholders to ensure that the reasons behind the selection of these schools is clear. Continue to monitor the area and risks throughout the life of the project to ensure that this risk doesn’t become an issue and take action in a timely manner to address the risk should it become an issue.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk/Threat: Risk of theft of school rehabilitation/construction materials such as construction materials, furniture (during delivery/storage/ after installation). Mitigating Measure: -MOUs to be agreed with local authorities/councils to ensure local ownership of materials and rehabilitated/new facilities. Risk/Threat: Extreme weather conditions have an affect on rehabiliation activities. Mitigating Measure: Plan rehabilitation for dry months to reduce the probability that weather will affect the implementation and timeline of activities. Risk/Threat: Project team turnover Mitigating Measure: Implement a comprehensive protocol for project documentation and handover procedures to mitigate the impact on the project timeline. Risk/Threat: Local authorities are uncooperative resulting in a delay of activities. Mitigating Measure: Engage authorities early and often through formal (MOUs) and informal means to ensure their buy-in and support for the project. Risk/Threat: Currency fluctuations in the local market affect the budget and procurement of materials. Mitigating Measure: Monitor the market prices and establish control mechanisms that indicate the procedures for escalation of the issue to management and donor
