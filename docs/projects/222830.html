Project Summary

In 2024, South Sudan continued to face severe protection challenges, particularly in regions impacted by displacement, conflict, and human rights violations especially in Jonglei, Upper Nile and Unity States. This project will specifically identify and address a significance role in protection needs in 2025 through monitoring, risk prevention, mitigation-and assessments including severe mental distress caused by cycle of war and violence in South Sudan to returnees, IDPs and resident communities in the proposed project locations leaving women and children vulnerable to sexual abuses and psychological distress, livelihoods disrupted, separation of children, child recruitment and households looted. This project aims to prevent and mitigate protection risks through community engagement including protection monitoring in hard to reach areas targeting 12,200 beneficiaries, 5,000 for general protection programming (1,300 girls, 900 boys, 1,700 women, and 1,100 men), 7,200 beneficiaries for HLP activities (1,700 girls, 1,550 boys, 2,150 women and 1,800 men). Through UNCDR experiences in the protection field monitoring, risk prevention, mitigation and assessment including HLP related concerns will be in place. The conflict in Sudan has worsened protection needs, with hundreds of thousands of refugees and returnees crossing into South Sudan, many reporting serious human rights violations & trauma during their journey. Over the last five years 81 GBV related incidence including the twenty nine underage girls were recorded in Ayod, Nyirol, Maiwut and Panyijiar alone. Women & girls being exposed to rape forced marriages and other forms of GBV due to the fact that displacement makes women and girls particularly vulnerable to exploitation and abuse due to their social and economic status. Women have been reported exposure to the sexual assault in several locations across South Sudan including Ayod, Canal/Pigi, Nyirol, Maiwut and Panyijiar counties when it comes to accessing services and assistance. They face similar risks when walking long distances to collect water and firewood. According to the report and individual/group focus discussion during UNCDR Assessment in January 2024 in Ayod as mentioned above, UNCDR wanted women and girls to have safe spaces for sharing their experiences and accessing support, link them with services providers and dissemination of life-saving messages through awareness raising/community mobilization. UNCDR has both static and mobile presence in Ayod, Maiwut & Panyijiar as protection partner dealing with protection monitoring, risk prevention, mitigation & assessment, we had conducted the report between 15-19 of January 2024 in Ayod and 12-16 of Feb 2024 in Maiwut including surrounding villages and interviewed a good number of boys, girls, and women who expressed their basic needs and protection as well. Based on the discussion and observation, It was clear stated that more efforts are needed to ensure that most vulnerable and persons with specific need must be reached wherever they are. People had to walk a long distances about 3-4 hours in order to access humanitarian assistance. In accessing the areas of intervention, UNCDR will consider the following three;
1. Physical access (air, road and water) according of the site and urgency of the response, areas most affected by the flooding/rainy season require careful attention and planning in order to not jeopardize the service delivery. 2. Negotiated access: UNCDR has traditionally operated in hard to reach locations and has thus endured humanitarian support reaches populations, in need upon the principles of neutrality, impartiality and non-discrimination. In the past years, UNCDR relations with local authorities and elders have been forged based on transparency communication, while being very clear on the principles of confidentiality as it concerns. 3. 1. Needs

Approximately 83% of women in need have survived SGBV, and 80% of children are at risk of exploitation, violence, or neglect (2024 Protection HNRP). The humanitarian situation in South Sudan is worsening, driven by cumulative and compounding effects of years of conflict, sub-national violence, food insecurity, the climate crisis and public health challenges. Despite recent positive developments on the implementation of the R-ARCSS, violence and insecurity remain a persistent reality unless programs are scaled up through both static and mobile field based intervention in the targeted counties. Insecurity and violence have disproportionately affected vulnerable people including children, older persons, survivors of gender-based violence and conflict-related sexual violence, and people living with disabilities in Ayod, Canal/Pigi, Nyirol, Maiwut and Panyijiar. According to Protection Cluster's Protection monitoring assessment conducted in October 2024. Out of 11.29 million affected populations (IDPs, Returnees, Residents), it is predicted that approximately 9.18 million people (81% of the affected population) are exposed to at least one of the top five protection risks. Further analysis shows that 98% of returnees (840K people) are the most protected risk-exposed population group, followed by IDPs at 81% (1,58M). The children continue to face protection risks including neglect, abuse, exploitation, child labour, forced recruitment, SGBV, and lack of access to services. Protection concerns are especially high for girls’ risks for early marriage, early pregnancies, and denial of education particularly for girls and children living with disabilities. Decades of conflict, poverty and poor access to services made persons with disabilities particularly fragile. The Persons with Disabilities (PWDs) are often stigmatized, marginalized, excluded and left unable to access the support needed to live in dignity as equal members of society. Women, the elderly, the person living with disabilities, women, and child-headed households are the most affected/exposed to all sorts of protection risks of physical and psychological torture. To meet the growing needs of the conflict-affected population, there is a need to scale up protection activities through an integrated mobile response to support UNCDR static activities on the ground. UNCDR already has both static and mobile presence in Ayod, Maiwut and Panyijiar counties since 2021, but in June - 2023 UNCDR had expanded its operation to filling in uncovered general protection gaps by 2024. We established new field office in Canal/Pigi and Nyirol with a team of 7 staffs each who had already been deployed (6 females and 8 males’ staffs). As protection partner dealing with protection monitoring, risk prevention, mitigation and assessment, we had conducted an assessment between 15 -19 of January 2024 in Ayod and 12-16 February 2024 in Maiwut including surrounding villages and interviewed a good number of boys, girls, and women who expressed their basic needs and protection as well. Based on the discussion and observation, It was clear stated that more efforts are needed to ensure that most vulnerable and persons with specific need must be reached wherever they are. People had to walk a long distances about 3 – 4 hours in order to access humanitarian assistance. There is also a need to strengthen the existing CBPN, local area land committee where the members can be trained on basic human rights, HLP rights, and peace-building for a peaceful coexistence of the IDPs, returnees, and host community. This project will intend to carry out HLP activities to promote and claim the rights of women and group at risk, also facilitate free legal assistance. However UNCDR will therefore arrange and encourage broad based community dialogue regarding HLP among women and men. Capacity building of the local authority aims to strengthen the local capacity to prevent, mitigate protection risk and resolve the land disputes.

2. Project activities

In line with the Protection Cluster strategy for 2024, UNCDR will enhance Integrated Protection Monitoring and Assessments through static presence and mobile responses to returnees, IDPs and vulnerable resident communities in Jonglei, Upper Nile and Unity States: Activities. 1. Protection monitoring and assessment through mobile response to deep field locations to identify the protection need of the conflict-affected IDPs, Returnees, and Host community. 2. Enhance the case management activities and facilitate the referral of the beneficiaries to access humanitarian assistance and provision of psychosocial support services to the conflict-affected women, men, boys, and girls. 3. Advocacy and awareness raising to sensitize the community to refrain from harmful cultural practices such as forced and early child marriage, gender-based/domestics violence, and this activity including dissemination of live-saving messaging and information. 4. Provision of targeted assistance (IPA) to the most vulnerable individuals to cover the protection-related needs which is an effort to prevent negative coping mechanism. 5. Provide in-kind and cash support to individuals identified to be severely vulnerable and exposed to protection risks. 6. Training of CBPNs and local authorities on home-grown prevention and risk mitigation measures including how community leaders can peacefully address potentials conflict among the community. 7. Establish or Support the existing community-based mechanism for long-term protection outcome. 8. UNCDR will work with protection actors, development and peace partners as well as authorities and existing community based structures and implement activities designed to improve community resiliencies, PSEA, peace-building activities and trainings through AGD approach that aims to transform youth to be agent for change in their communities. HLP Activities: 1. Support the person with HLP related issues to access alternative dispute mechanisms including mediation, negotiation and arbitration. 2. Facilitate the referral of a person with HLP needs to NFIs and other humanitarian partners to access the needed assistance. 3. Provide support and Capacity building on land registration and policy development. 4. Conduct HLP training for the community members comprised of IDPs, returnees and host community on human rights, HLP rights, local and international legal frameworks including traditional customary laws. 5. Provide options with HLP issues on legal remedies particularly claims of lost documents, institutions, and land registration. 6. Provide support and capacity building on land registration and policy development. 7. Provide Legal counseling and psychosocial support including community sensitization and awareness-raising on HLP rights and alternative dispute resolutions. 8. UNCDR will use IEC materials including T-shirts and posters for social messages and awareness-raising purposes.
