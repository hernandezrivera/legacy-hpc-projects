Project Summary

IOM will expand its Health and Nutritional programme to a more systematic and comprehensive approach with nutrition community mobilization volunteers and utilizing the rapid response mechanism. Access to quality emergency health care and nutritional services remains a challenge, especially for internally displaced persons (IDPs) and migrants at reception centers and arrival camps. IOM intends to enhance quality access to comprehensive services through the integration of emergency mobile health care and nutritional services at the deep field locations with other health services at out post. IOM will also continue working with the health partners in outbreak prevention and response efforts, capacity building of health, nutritional, health information management, and emergency referral and support systems. As part of the health promotional and preventive activities, IOM will partner with UNICEF and local NGOs to implement health outreach through health promoters and community volunteers. Under this action, IOM will also strengthen its collaboration with the State Primary Health Care Development Agency (SPHCDA) and will continue to support the State Ministries of Health.
IOM will partner with the Borno State Ministry of Health and Borno State Primary Health care Development Agency in providing health needs in the hard-to-reach areas. IOM will work with the agencies to develop a response strategy primarily targeting the migrating population, persons living with disability, and refugees including vaccination against vaccine-preventable diseases among these vulnerable populations.
In coordination with the Nigeria Centre for Disease Control (NCDC), the Federal Ministry of Health and related departments, World Health Organization, and the Health sector in Maiduguri; IOM will leverage on its health capacity to contribute to the government’s effort to respond to epidemics.

1 - Needs

The protracted crisis in Northeast Nigeria has led to a meltdown of socioeconomic and development structures, which has contributed to the destruction of health facilities and an incapacitated healthcare sector. More than 42 percent of healthcare facilities in the BAY states are non-functional due to the conflict. Additionally, the quality of care is unstable due to the volatile security situation and lack of standardized health care packages. An increase in quality service provides enhanced technical capacity for the healthcare providers, an emergency health response team and the establishment of a nutrition rapid response mechanism are thus noted as critical issues by the agencies responding to the crisis. Furthermore, gaps in referral services have been strongly tagged by Health Sector partners currently providing the responses at reception centers, camps and primary healthcare centers (PHC) in host communities, as a key need specifically the inability of patients to receive critically needed health care or timely access to emergency care in hospitals. Health facilities were targeted during the conflict and as a result, many health facilities across BAY states are still non-functional or partially functional while those that are functioning do so with the minimum capacity to deliver standard health care packages. In hard-to-reach areas communities face serious challenges in accessing health services due to various reasons, including lack of medical staff, unavailability of essential emergency drugs and supplies, security barriers, food transportation issues, malnutrition, and damaged/destroyed health facilities. Critical gaps also remain in functional primary and secondary health services. Other common challenges in accessing health services, especially to persons with various disabilities are related to high direct and indirect costs associated with healthcare, including the price of quality medicines. The disruption in the supply chain of medicines and medical supplies has compounded the problem. The dire living conditions of these IDPs together with the often-poor water and sanitation conditions contribute to increased health risks and higher vulnerability to the spread of outbreak-prone communicable diseases, such as cholera. IDP returnees in newly accessible areas or IDPs living in “Satellite camps” have reportedly limited or even no access to health care services. There is an urgent need to expand the provision of integrated basic PHC services through mobile emergency health and nutrition services to prevent further deterioration of the health status of affected populations and to reach the most vulnerable and disabled persons. Health services also need to be made available and accessible at the different levels of care, supported by a well-functioning referral mechanism, at and between the community, primary and secondary/higher care levels. While the Ministry of Health has made efforts in integrating emergency health services at the PHC, there is a need to further this approach to ensure increased access to emergency health and nutrition services.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Set up 3 Mobile Health Teams at the selected locations that will provide an integrated and standardized primary and preventive healthcare service package, including referrals. Activity 2: Identify and train local community health volunteers and influencers on health promotion and community-based health interventions. Activity 3: Support the national disease surveillance system and contribute to the control of disease outbreaks and other public health events through the training of healthcare workers on epidemic reporting pathways and IDSR. Activity 4: Refurbishment and equipping of 10 selected public health facilities to resume their function and to provide integrated primary health services. Activity 5: Conduct training for PHC workers with the use of the WHO and UNICEF sector tools as a guide for service delivery. Activity 6: Strengthen referral and provision of specialized Health services in collaboration with the state’s tertiary and secondary health facilities to vulnerable persons living with disabilities. Activity 7: Conduct an assessment to determine the availability of Emergency Health care and referral services in the selected locations. Activity 8: strengthen emergency health care services provision at the deep field locations through capacity-building activities in close coordination with the State Ministry of Health Activity 9: Vaccination of Migrants, IDPs, and refugees at points of entry, reception camps, and transit centers against vaccine-preventable diseasesActivity 10: Operation of a mobile outpatient therapeutic feeding program for integrated management of acute malnutrition in collaboration with UNICEF using the rapid response mechanism
