Project Summary

This project is proposed in response to the Shelter Sector Objective 2: Strengthen the resilience and cohesion of vulnerable communities by improving housing and related community or public infrastructure. Hence; this project is designed to meet the dire need of returnees’ population in based on HNO 2022, DEZ population 279440, Pin 197584, acute PiN 169920, at risk PiN 27664, and severity score is 4. Rescate had finished Water and sanitation rehabilitation in 2021 in Al Omal and Air Port neighborhoods. Noting that the area is eventually underserved, there are ~500 houses, yet interventions were limited to rehabilitation of ADRA 89 houses with 52 solar street lights.
The objective of the project is to rehabilitate damaged houses for vulnerable returnees, there are to date around 265 HHs returned to Al Omal and 150 HHs returned to the Old Airport district. Rescate designed a response to rehabilitate damaged houses in the two areas as a 2022 intervention for 175 damaged houses where some houses are used by three families at the same time due to acute vulnerability. However, Rescate is planning to add 100 houses in 2023. At this stage; Rescate is submitting the proposal to rehabilitate 175 houses in Al Omal and the Old Airport areas. Focus on the Old Airport area with 100 houses because most inhabitants are fresh vulnerable returnees who are in dire need for this intervention. However, the Omal area residents returned since 2018 the reason Rescate is targeting only 75 damaged houses in Al Omal for the most vulnerable who were unable to rehabilitate their homes. Rescate conducted field assessment and detailed BOQs including market studies with updated estimation based on the SWG average costs and works to be conducted. In coordination with the municipality, a desk will be set at the Mayor office to review beneficiaries’ HLP dox. Rescate will submit the intact files for MOLAE especially Rescate has MoU so the approvals are facilitated. Shelter sector standards and buildings’ common areas will be applied to the works, with prioritization to the vulnerability score (FHHs, CHHs, elderly, families with PWDs) in addition to those who lost their jobs to COVID-19.
Due to current unstable currency and market prices, Rescate will encourage contractors to submit proposals by giving the awarded contractors a down-payment of 30% to purchase materials in mitigation to market prices changes.

4 - Needs

The intervention is designed to meet the operating plan that is being developed by inter-sectoral coordination priorities, Rescate worked in the area on WASH project and had community and FGDs for this project. The city council, governorate and the communities in Al Omal and the old airport neighborhood provided that the areas inhabitants are vulnerable returnees living in dire needs being low-income day laborers (83% under poverty line HNAP). The residents are unable to rehabilitate their homes due to lack of resources and/or physical inability to move. The areas suffer of low intervention, based on actual field survey ADRA 89 houses + 52 street solar lights + debris removal 20,000 m3. UNDP: Rehabilitation of the electricity grid + 50 lights.Rescate team prepared the detailed BOQs for 175 houses as field assessment is completed, with the mayor’s support in the targeted area; There are 415 returning families but due to limited funding coupled with the community vulnerability being low income HHs and do not have enough financial means to rehabilitate their houses. The mayor confirmed that residents visit his office on a daily basis to inquire about new projects. Rescate M&E focuses on recording weaknesses, and a Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) will be set in place to allow beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on Rescate work, as well as the contractors’ work, and to rely any arising issue, including potential PSEA or GBV issues.

5 - Implementation of activities

This project will contribute to the strategic cluster objectives by supporting vulnerable returnees to live in dignity in their homes. The solutions are rather durable to assist inhabitants to live in decent life sustaining standard. The project has direct contribution to the Shelter/NFI sector by improving the housing conditions to the most vulnerable people in the selected and approved areas in DEZ. This will be done through rehabilitation of the houses & upgrading quality standards by including WASH component in each building. Activities are: - Coordination with MOLAE & Governorate. - Procurement procedures, contractors will be awarded to carry out the rehabilitation works as per the standard procurement and evaluation policies. - Site supervision & monitoring on materials and works, M&E, Field visits, beneficiaries’ feedback on assistance provided - Data collection and beneficiaries registration in collaboration with governorate committee - Final receipt of the works from contractors at each house is concluded with the governorate committee - Post evaluation for 175 rehabilitated houses, beneficiary satisfaction surveys conducted upon receipt of each lot of houses, after warranty period - Final closing reports financial and narrative submitted to Donor and sector 4Ws.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Rescate has a transparent approach for the applicants & beneficiaries’ selection. Monitoring would be followed by analysis, effectively disseminated for the use of all actors. Safety and dignity will be enforced and transversal during all the activities of the project. The principles of Do No Harm enforced as Rescate will have a conflict sensitivity analysis, first understanding the context of the intervening and identifying the relations between different groups that will be targeted. Rescate will act upon understanding these interactions in order to avoid negative impacts. Rescate will ensure elderly or people with other illness, efforts to ensure that these groups benefit and their needs are foreseen, such as direct outreach to them by Rescate volunteers and additional equipment (handrails, and toilet seats) are installed. The project enables equal and impartial access since assistance will be provided on the basis of assessed vulnerability/need and NOT on the beneficiary’s application only (will avoid discrimination based on gender, age, and community). In order to support self-protection, Rescate will adapt to SOPs during the intervention. The objective of this is to ensure that people have the life-saving information and PPEs to protect themselves and others (from the virus and to reduce its impact on health and social life). GBV is strongly mainstreamed in this project; Safeguarding accountability by setting claim and reporting mechanisms are in place.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk of sexual exploitation: Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse Likelihood: Low – Impact: High Mitigation measure: All Rescate staff and partners, and contractors, are required to sign the Code of Conduct which directly refers to the Protection of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and to Gender Based Violence (GBV). As part of on-going efforts to improve accountability, Rescate is committed to implement a beneficiary Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its project to allow beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on Rescate work, as well as the contractors’ work, and to rely any arising issue, including potential PSEA or GBV issues. All PSEA and/or GBV issues will be addressed first by the Project coordinator, who will share the concern with the Head of Mission based in Damascus, in order to establish a solution or way to proceed to enhance the protection of the PSEA/GBV survivor. When needed, the Head of Mission will scale the issue to ONG Rescate Headquarters in Spain, where the Director, in consultation with the gender specialists’ team, will look for potential solutions and/or an appropriate way to proceed.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Operational, one of the possible risks is miscommunication with different stakeholders’ groups. This event could have major consequences. Resources and response capacities being limited and far below known needs, there will always be perception of biased selection of response activities, thereby creating or exacerbating tension. Risk of increased prices due to heavy inflation. Mitigation: already accounted for the increased inflation in the budget, Contractor shall have 30% down payment to purchase materials under Rescate observation to quality, quantity and availability Fuel shortage and procurement supply delays are the Contractors’ responsibility and mitigated to ensure immediate storage of the purchased materials. Approval delays and Covid-19 restrictions are calculated in the project duration; hence schedule works in a manner to avoid mixing workers. Rescate will be working in strong coordination with ST, stakeholders & communities to facilitate focused response with strong need-based approach. Rescate had reviewed reports by OCHA, and the Sector on 1 conflict analysis before planning the response, ensured that a comprehensive needs analysis is conducted with stakeholders & is documented and made transparent, 2 during implementation, make sure those in similar situation of need, at least within the geographical areas, are reached equally, 3 keeping stakeholders & communities regularly informed of the project and its implementation progress.
