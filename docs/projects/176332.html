Project Summary

CASS in partnership with Africa Development Aid (ADA) plans to implement a multi-sector Health, WASH, livelihoods and Cash project in Ayod and Fangak aimed at Covid-19 prevention and mitigating vulnerable households from the impact of COVID-19. During another project implementation in 2020, the project proposed area was hit by floods resulting from over flooding river Nile hence the increased vulnerability in the target communities. This proposed project will respond in target communities of Ayod and Fangak County based on relative vulnerabilities.
CASS and ADA conducted two needs assessments in the two counties of Ayod and Fangak through focus group discussion (FGD) targeting IDP households in October 2020. These assessments have informed this proposed intervention in addition to the unmet needs in the ongoing project. Based on the needs assessment, the critical urgent needs in Ayod and Fangak will include:
• Knowledge of how to prevent COVID –19. The assessment uncovered generally poor hygiene practices which include limited hand washing, habit of spitting, no use of masks, no practice of social distancing.
• Livelihoods support. Large-scale flooding, which occurred between September and October 2020, destroyed crops and caused livestock disease. The cumulative effects of these shocks compounded by COVID-19 measures have disrupted livelihoods and deepen chronic humanitarian needs. In particular, it has disrupted trade, leading to the depletion of much needed commodities in markets in Ayod and Fangak, where majority of the population depend on cattle keeping, farming and fishing.
• Food needs. Food security situation has been compromised by floods, covid-19 including lack of proper nutrition for malnourished children and pregnant/lactating women due to the limited access to food sources and purchasing power.
• Non-food needs. People are unable to meet their immediate needs due to flooding and COVID-19 restrictions on movement, scale down of job opportunities, loss of jobs and unemployment. Most shelters and basic non-food items were destroyed or carried away by the floods
• WASH needs. Due to massive floods, most existing functional boreholes are submerged by floods while other water sources remained contaminated. The displaced communities are in urgent need of access to safe and clean water
The population in these targeted counties are vulnerable to COVID-19 due to frequent population movement between and from Bentiu Protection of Civilians (PoC) site and the arrival of IDPs and refugee returnees, pose a threat for transmission. Furthermore, the current flooding situation has also forced people to live in concentration areas (higher grounds that are not flooded) and this exposes them to the risk of catching Covid-19.
It's important to note that CASS and partners will conduct a detailed needs verification assessment at the beginning of the project to verify the needs.

1 - Needs

A rapid assessment involved 1,703 IDP households (out of an estimated displacement of 9,000 HH) in Ayod County and 400 IDP households in Paguir Payam (Fangak County), out of an estimated 1,500 IDP households. Household surveys, focus group discussions (FGD) and key informant interviews were conducted. The main respondents (stakeholders) were local government authorities, local community chiefs, household heads, with a central focus on representation of the gender diversity, geographical location, impairments, and other vulnerabilities e.g. older people and heads of women or child-headed households. A summary of the findings revealed that, in Ayod, WASH needs have increased due to the unprecedented floods which partly submerged entire villages, including homesteads, local pit latrines, and boreholes, contaminating household water sources. Local communities resorted to either using canoes to travel from home to ‘bush toilets’ or resorted to open defecation in waters far from their living quarters. Coupled with open disposal of household wastes in the surrounding flood waters, contamination of water sources was huge; creating high risk of water-borne diseases, fertile breeding grounds for malaria-causing mosquitoes. In terms of livelihoods, the team observed that the 2020 expected household farm harvests were generally destroyed including remaining 2019 harvest in storage facilities. Growing crops were inundated by floods, while households were repeatedly displaced, with livestock either impacted by flood-based animal illness and death or they were moved to higher grounds far from previous home settlements. The net effect was disruption of household livelihoods and assets. Partial or destruction of homesteads created demand for shelter and non-food items for the affected populations. Huge mosquito presence and their fertile breeding grounds fed by massive, but stagnant floods resulted in reported increase in malaria cases in the health facilities; with some cases treated locally from home. The floods dislodged many ground reptiles, notably snails and snakes, with few reported incidences of snakebites. These stated humanitarian needs are similar in Pagil Payam (Fangak County). The main difference in Paguir is that there are more higher and dry grounds to which displaced populations fled (to schools and churches) than in Ayod County.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Christian Aid and partner conducted a rapid assessment of market including needs assessment in the IDP concentration areas in Ayod and Fangak Counties in October, 2020. This follows the first round of cash distribution aimed at informing the second round cash distribution that was conducted in Jan, 2021. Based on these interventions, the local traders have responded well by ensuring alternative commodities supply through the river from Juba especially after the flood disrupting the Bor market supply. This project will continue to work with the existing market traders by informing them of the new project through the established project implementation committees that are responsible for community mobilisation and activities implementation as well as through the community accountability meetings.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Christian Aid and partner have been conducting monthly market price monitoring in Ayod and Fangak since September 2020 to date. This follows an earlier conducted market assessment that determined that traders have the potential to supply basic household commodities from Juba and other close supply markets into the project area when demand arises. Traders are willing to supply goods so long as there is cash in the community. On the other side, other service providers such as Kush and Cooperative bank have also been able to conduct direct cash distributions in these locations in another ongoing project that ended in Jan 2021. This is to ensure that security risk related in moving large sums of cash needed in distribution points is secured. In terms of cost, Christian Aid’s experience suggested that it would be cheaper to provide cash than in-kind assistance or vouchers. However, Christian Aid identified significant security risks with direct cash transfers by staff to recipients in this environment, and hence decided to transfer this risk to a bank while staff engage at community level processes. Other factors that Christian Aid considers include;Dignity: Multi-purpose cash will enable beneficiaries to have control over what goods and services their households need most and urgently; this guarantees a dignified choice than receiving in-kind and recognise beneficiaries as active participants in providing for the welfare of their families after a disaster. Women Empowerment: The availability of cash gives households a sense of restored power over their immediate situation. In addition, evidence the last project cash distribution shows that receiving cash can at least temporarily empower women within the household especially the health of pregnant and lactating mothers and their children under 5. Supports or revitalises local trade: Injections of cash with local traders benefits the recovery of trade and the local economy. Cash-based support for livelihood rehabilitation and food access can potentially also provide a link between relief and longer-term economic recovery and may offer the opportunity for beneficiaries to increase income and create savings.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Christian Aid (CA) has one of the most progressive partnership policies complemented by strong poli-cies that safeguard against fraud, bribery, prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), coun-ter-terrorist funding sanction compliance and employee code of conduct. These policies have been successfully cascaded and operationalized by all local partners in South Sudan. Africa Development Aid (ADA) is a longstanding partner of Christian Aid South Sudan with assured identity, legal credentials and in good standing with all CA partnership requirements. As part of Christian Aid standard due diligence process, the partner had undergone a more recent Partner Organisational Capacity Assessments (POCRA) in 2019. All POCRA’s are revised periodically to ensure CA’s partners remain compliant and standards are upheld. The partner is compliant with several CA policy requirements including required commitment to Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Code of Conduct. CA has built in support for training and accompaniment of the partner on accountability and PSEA under the project to complement training it received from Tearfund in 2019. CA training on safeguarding and community accountability approaches, including how to engage communities, how to collect feedback and how to share information, will boost the partner’s capacity to implement effective rollout of feedback and complaints mechanism that is built as part of this project. CA has corporate Whistleblowing and Safeguarding (SG) policies in place. All CA staff sign up to Code of Con-duct and PSEA. An annual refresher training is conducted as a continual commitment and compliance. Line managers are diligent in ensuring the applications and commitment are upheld. CASS office has two staff trained as Trainer of Trainers (ToT) in PSEA and accountability. All CASS staff have been trained in PSEA and accountability. During the implementation of the project, beneficiaries will be provided with information on expected behaviours as well as feedback and complaint system. CASS has recently established a dedicated toll-free number to support programme-wide beneficiary feedback and Complaints mechanism. CA has existing feedback systems, and these will be tailored to the sensitivity in the COVID context.

CASS and ADA will use a Community Based Accountability approach adapted to the COVID-19 protocols.Accountability Assessments will be undertaken with the new two target communities and further the work in the other three locations in Ayod to design their preferred feedback/ complaints and information sharing mechanisms which will be used to share information ensuring communities are adequately informed of their rights and entitlements including key project information on eligibility criteria, timelines, expected staff and volunteer behaviour, the Code of Conduct including how communities can engage in the delivery of this re-sponse.CA will develop 500 IEC materials in the local language to share with communities which will explain expected staff behaviour, what constitutes abuse and how to report when CoC is breached.The digital feedback system COMPASS will be deployed to key community and partner staff to capture, ana-lyse and generate reports on community feedback, complaints, including rumours, fears and misconceptions on COVID.COMPASS works in on and offline environments, synching to the global database enabling collection of in-formation directly from hard to reach communities. 7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

CA has long experience with remote project management in South Sudan due to the context in country. In light of COVID-19 these experiences have been tailored and will be applied in this project to ensure CA can deliver on its commitment to quality, learning and accountability. It involves use of mixed approaches, including use of appropriate internet-based technology and or its toll-free number 664 to enable real time accompaniment support during project implementation. Compass will allow the voices of people and communities to influence programme interventions; allowing needed response adjustments based on real-time community feedback. Through this engagement, this project will also attempt to address sexual exploitation and abuse, misbehaviour, wellbeing of CA staff and community. We also follow "do no harm" approaches and therefore we do a comprehensive conflict analyses before projects to identify and mitigate the risks and potential negative impacts of our interventions. Through the established Project Implementation Committees and the Cash Distribution Committees, the project will establish a Community Accountability and Feedback platform for community conversations. These community conversations will harvest project impact as perceived by the community and ensure necessary adjustments are made during implementation. In addition, it will also monitor trends on unintended impacts of the response in Real-time and adaptations made to mitigate against negative impacts.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

All Christian Aid interventions in conflict/fragile states are designed and delivered through a Conflict Sensitivity approach and follow a comprehensive risk management and oversight system. The project is informed by a set of risks identified, assessed for likelihoods of occurrence and measures to mitigate against to ensure project implementation is not impacted. Overall, Christian Aid has strong measures to guide against operational risks related to security, personnel behaviour in the field, financial and quality of delivery of programme. Country office level security management plan ensure staff are supported to under their work with high degree of safety and security, staff behaviour is managed through line management for high quality performance, behaviours in line with the CoC and PSEA in their line of duty. Funding and Reporting Agreement with the partner sets the framework for grants management in accordance with the donor requirement. This is complemented by M&EL plan to ensure high quality implementation of the project. Risks to staff is constantly being reviewed and managed in accordance with country level security management protocol and guidelines. Financial risks are managed in line with CA's due diligence process guided by several policies including anti-corruption and anti-diversion policy and other screening processes assures partner identify, legal credential and standing on commitments. Due to volatile nature of South Sudan context, all risks assessed in relation to implementation of this project will be routinely reviewed to ensure Christian Aid can continue adapt it approaches during the project implementation. For instance, all project staff signs and adheres to PSEA, and all staff are aware and sign up to safeguarding including the Code of Conduct. This information is provided to communities and is systematically monitored by both Christian Aid and partner teams during field visit.
