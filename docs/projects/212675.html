Project Summary

In February 2023, a series of devastating earthquakes struck southern Turkey and Syria, causing widespread damage and affecting thousands of lives. The affected governorates, including Aleppo, Lattakia, Tartous, Hama, and Idleb, experienced significant loss of life, injuries, and displacement. According to the Situation Report (OCHA, 8 March 2023), over 4,500 deaths, 8,700 injuries, and 7.8 million affected individuals were reported in north-west Syria.
The aftermath of the earthquake resulted in extensive damage to many homes, rendering thousands homeless and leaving more than 10,600 buildings completely or partially destroyed in north-west Syria. Approximately 60% of partially destroyed buildings were located in Idleb and Aleppo governorates. Dorcas conducted a rapid needs assessment on 8th and 9th February 2023, revealing that 86% of respondents reported damage to their homes.
The shelter project in Aleppo focuses on rehabilitating houses affected by the February 2023 earthquake. Led by a project manager, Dorcas has been actively engaged in shelter rehabilitation and plans to continue its efforts in 2024, supporting more individuals in returning to their homes. The project involves contracting contractors to execute necessary rehabilitation work, ensuring houses are made habitable and safe for occupancy. Additionally, a well-trained team is responsible for monitoring rehabilitation progress to ensure quality and effectiveness. Through these efforts, Dorcas aims to contribute to the restoration of living conditions for affected households in Aleppo, facilitating their return and recovery.
Dorcas has already appointed a technical consultant to oversee project assessment, monitoring, and quality control. Repairs will be conducted by contractors who collaborate with hired staff, including plumbers, electricians, and painters, some of whom participated in Dorcas' livelihood project. A post-evaluation will be conducted to measure beneficiary satisfaction and the overall impact of the project.
Dorcas aims to address the urgent housing needs of earthquake-affected households. The target is to rehabilitate a total of 280 housing units requiring minor repairs of damaged houses, along with 10 larger units in need of repair of common areas. These efforts are designed to make homes habitable and safe for occupancy, facilitating the return and recovery of affected families.
Dorcas will conduct a comprehensive protection risk analysis as part of its project planning process. This analysis will identify potential risks and vulnerabilities faced by beneficiaries throughout the shelter rehabilitation process. This includes risks such as exploitation, abuse, discrimination, and lack of access to essential services. Dorcas will prioritize the safety and well-being of beneficiaries by implementing safeguarding measures and ensuring that vulnerable groups receive appropriate support and protection. This may involve providing safe spaces for women and children, offering psychosocial support services, and establishing referral mechanisms for individuals at risk of harm.
Dorcas will face various operational risks that could impact the effectiveness and sustainability of its interventions. Some potential risks include delays in procurement and contracting, unforeseen logistical challenges, security threats, and technical issues during rehabilitation works. To mitigate these risks, Dorcas will adopt a proactive approach, including detailed planning, monitoring and oversight.
