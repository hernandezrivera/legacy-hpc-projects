Project Summary

The purpose of this project is to reduce excess child morbidity and mortality through the delivery of lifesaving curative and preventive nutrition services to crisis-affected IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities in Khartoum, Gedaref, Blue Nile, Al Gezira, White Nile and Red Sea states. This action will specifically target boys, girls and PLW with essential nutrition services. The services to be implemented will include lifesaving treatment, prevention activities, capacity building for healthcare providers, health infrastructure improvements, and referral support to stabilization facilities. This plan has been revised to incorporate anticipated increased malnutrition caseloads in prioritized locations following the escalation of armed conflicts in Sudan since April 15, 2023, including the recent surges in December 2023in Al Gezira state. The IRC will support screening for malnutrition at the service delivery points and, in the community, deliver treatment for severe and moderately acute malnutrition, infant and young child feeding (IYCF) counseling to pregnant and lactating women (PLW). The IRC will deliver services directly through the deployment of trained staff, support existing MoH efforts to deliver services and utilize existing local capacity by engaging with local CSO to deliver and scale up services. The IRC will build MoH capacity to improve skills in nutrition screening, growth monitoring, SAM and MAM treatment as well as follow-up services. In addition, the IRC will provide infant and young child feeding (IYCF) counseling to pregnant and lactating women (PLW), micronutrient supplementation to reduce the incidence of malnutrition in the communities. This will be closely integrated with water and sanitation (WASH) activities to ensure good hygiene and reduction in WASH-related diseases.
The IRC will work closely with protection colleagues and local partners including community health volunteers (CHVs) to identify communities and children at risk of malnutrition and scale up screening services. GBV screening will be conducted at all nutrition sites to identify at risk individuals and refer for services. The IRC will refer survivors of SGBV to IRC supported health facilities and other actors providing SGBV services.
The IRC is a global leader in the delivery of quality nutrition services and innovations in humanitarian settings to improve nutrition outcomes including the development of the combined protocol to scale up lifesaving nutrition treatment. The IRC has the requisite staff capacity in country to lead nutrition activities, and already involved in providing nutrition services in Khartoum, Al Gezira, Gedaref and Blue Nile states. The IRC technical excellence unit provides technical support to country teams to ensure quality delivery, with regional Technical Advisors for nutrition based in Nairobi in regular contact with the country teams. Even during the ongoing crisis in Sudan, the IRC maintains adequate capacity in the country and within the region to deliver quality nutrition services, as well as integrate services with health, protection, WASH, and livelihoods.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

In designing its programs, the IRC makes every effort to address protection mainstreaming in the technical design. In all aspects of the project, from design to implementation and monitoring, the core protection principles of ‘Do No Harm’, meaningful access, safety and dignity, and participation will be mainstreamed. The IRC health team works closely with protection colleagues to identify protection risks, vulnerabilities and referral pathways for services. The action will ensure availability of essential nutrition services through training of health facility staff and preposition of nutrition supplies. Providers will be trained on ensuring privacy and confidentiality including in the health information system (HIS).

4 - AAP & PSEA
4.1 - How will the project share information and collect community feedback? Please select as many as relevant.

Face to face with group consultations with aid workers,Face to face with community leaders with aid workers,Phone calls,Complaints / suggestions box

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

The health team will conduct regular client interviews to collect feedback from caretakers on their experience during the visit to the health service point. This will cover waiting time, quality of services, medications received, their perception on how well they were treated, and what can be improved at the facility, among others. The IRC also generates additional feedback through the protection work in the community with the feedback shared with the health team for action. Throughout implementation, the IRC will leverage its MEAL team and feedback systems to ensure community involvement in the design and re-design of the action. The IRC Protection team will conduct outreach and awareness to ensure that beneficiaries are aware of the IRC Code of Conduct, prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse policies, child safeguarding policies, process for providing feedback, and the principles of beneficiary feedback (including confidentiality, non-retaliation, and accountability).

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Overall, the IRC has zero tolerance for bullying, harassment and sexual exploitation and abuse. The IRC has the following policies in place: Child Safeguarding Policy; Beneficiary Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) Policy; Harassment Free Workplace Policy. All allegations and investigation information received is maintained in a confidential centralized case management system. Cases are evaluated and prioritized when received taking into account, among other things, whether the alleged conduct is ongoing or historical. The IRC will ensure that all new staff hired for IRC Sudan are trained on and understand the seriousness with which the IRC takes such issues. The IRC will also participate in the UN-led PSEA network, ensuring communities and staff understand PSEA and how to access reporting channels. The IRC also has a neutral department called the global Ethics & Compliance Unit - ECU, who is responsible for all major complaints, including SEA and abuse complaints.
