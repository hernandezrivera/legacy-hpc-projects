Project Summary

The overall objective of the proposed project comprises of three core elements - Risk Education (RE), Non-Technical Survey (NTS) and explosive hazard (EH) clearance. Each element aims to increasing the safety of Internally Displaced People (IDPs), voluntary returnees and host communities in Kobane, Menbij, Raqqa and Deir-ez-Zor governorates, enabling safe and sustainable returns and thus saving lives. The RE will be delivered to to targeted women, children, adolescents, youth and their caregivers including person with disabilities. Pre and post surveys will be conducted before and after the RE sessions considering questions that is related to person with disabilities. This is in line with the HRP Protection objective: “Reduce the impact of explosive hazards [ related to strategic objectives 1, 2 and 3] and will contribute to addressing the high levels of explosive hazards contamination across Northeast Syria (NES).

4 - Needs

Explosive ordnance (EO) contamination continues to be a widespread protection concern in Syria. Based on MSNA 2021, one in three communities in Syria is estimated to be potentially contaminated. This reflect that one third of total communities was estimated to be potentially contaminated. Based on the findings at governorate level exposure EO is 87%; Deir-ez-Zor is 63% and Ar-Raqqa is 51% respectively. In terms of perceptions, men and boys are significantly more exposed to EO accidents, while young boys and girls under 12 are on average in terms of exposure, whereas adult women are more likely to be exposed. This concludes that entire communities are vulnerable by indirect threat and long-term consequences, exacerbating existing vulnerabilities. While children are affected physically by EO accidents, there is also affected psychologically and economically. Men and boys, particularly adolescents, are more frequently exposed, comprising more than 80 per cent of victims of reported EO accidents in the past five years. Therefore, it is essentials that all groups in communities including children affected by EO must receive awareness about the risks and how to avoid them. Most of the contaminated land are agricultural lands and access roads followed by private properties, other public buildings, schools and hospital. According to the findings of MSNA, 37 % of agricultural land, 24% of private property and 8% of private shelters/ properties are contaminated with explosive hazards.

5 - Implementation of activities

Risk Education: DCA, in partnership with RMCO, will provide age and gender tailored RE sessions to at risk- populations, specifically targeting IDPs fleeing or retuning to highly contaminated areas. RE sessions will provide targeted populations (women, children, elderly and people with disabilities) with knowledge and skills to adopt safer behaviours with regards to Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) and Improvise Explosive Devices (IED). Community Focal Points (CFP) will be trained by DCA on sharing key simple safety messages to their communities, thus increasing the reach and sustainability of the action including people with disabilities. The CFP includes local authority representatives, community leaders, teachers, farmers, public sector, employees, healthcare, and members of women’s councils. Non- technical Survey (NTS) and Clearance: DCA/ RMCO will conduct NTS to gather information about the level and categorisation of explosive contamination in residential dwellings, stabilisation facilities, critical infrastructure and other areas based on the EH contamination. These surveys will include information about the impact of the contamination and will help to prioritize the project clearance activities strictly based on humanitarian needs. Clearance teams will focus their activities in these targeted areas and critical infrastructure, such as schools, based on the data gathered during the NTS, to help save lives and enable safe, sustainable and voluntary returns.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Community threshold of acceptable risk increases as a result of living in contaminated areas for a longer period of time, thus leading to more accidents. Likelihood: Medium Impact: HighMitigation Measures: DCA Project staff will ensure that RE session include topics on effects and consequences of risky behaviour leading to explosive hazard accidents. DCA Project staff with community members will discuss safer alternatives and encourage behaviour change. Protection Risk: Once marking of contaminated areas begins, communities mistakenly assume any area not marked must be safe. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High.Mitigation measures: During NTS, Community Liaison (CL) DCA project staff will inform community representatives and members that unmarked areas may be contaminated with explosive hazards and will instruct or remind them how to recognise warning signs and dangerous areas. RE sessions will also include the message about confirmed and suspected hazardous areas.Protection Risk: During the clearance and survey of houses, land, and other properties there is the risk of an unintended detonation. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High.Mitigation Measures: Coordination with local authorities, local tribal leaders, Mine Action Office (MAO), Self- Administration (SA) and other security officials will be made before clearance and technical survey is conducted to ensure residents are aware of activities and not within the corresponding danger areas.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Protection Risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by DCA staff members or partner staff members.Likelihood: LowImpact: HighMitigation measures: DCA Project staff will communicate regularly and clearly with the community about the provision of available services and who services are for. DCA PSEA focal point in coordination with HR will conduct training and refreshers to project staff and partner staff on the Code of Conduct, Child Safeguarding and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). DCA will establish a complaint box that will allow staff, partners, or community members to raise complaints confidentiality. These will be addressed in a timely and appropriate manner by the member of complaints handling team or management staff depending on the nature of the complaint. DCA follows principles of Do No Harm across its project activities.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Safety and security risks in project location impact project implementation, causing delays and changes in operational areas and threaten the safety and security of DCA project staff and beneficiaries. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation Measures: DCA has a designated security focal point who actively monitors the security situation in project locations, sets up security Standard Operating Procedures (SOP), and conducts regular security- related training for staffs. Project Management remains flexible and changes project locations and modalities as and when required. Hibernation, relocation, and evacuation plans remain in place.Risk: Closure of border Semalka and Peshkabur Border Crossing Point by KRI authorities and SA.Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation Measures: DCA Syria programme has well established office in Erbil with national and international staff. DCA will coordinate with border crossing officials through liaison focal point and continuously monitored the situation for access.
