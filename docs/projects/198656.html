Project Summary

Due to the recurrent operation of insurgency in the Northeastern region of Nigeria, which has rendered a large number of the population vulnerable, the three states of Borno Adamawa and Yobe have been crossly devastated, with thousands of people affected leaving them without homes or means of livelihood, while being exposed to diseases and health challenges.
This project is aimed at strengthening the Provision of Primary Health Care Services and bridging the gaps in Cholera Response to IDPs and Host Communities in Selected LGAs of Adamawa State (Girei and Yola North LGAs)
It will also focus on strengthening the readiness and response actions for Cholera Outbreak response in Adamawa State. AGUF in partnership with the SPHCDA plans to reach the communities selected in Adamawa through the provision of an integrated package of health services to children under five years old, women and their families in IDP camp clinics as well as facilitation of referral services to secondary and tertiary health institutions. The funds will be used for addressing health needs including scaling-up of the emergency health services delivery, medicines and supplies in these overburdened health facilities to effectively cater to vulnerable populations most of whom are women and children.
AGUF will support Adamawa State Primary Health Care Development Agency (SPHCDA) and the State Ministry of Health (SMoH) to provide emergency integrated PHC services which include: treatment of common diseases (malaria, RTI, diarrhea, etc.), provision of antenatal care and safe delivery services, immunization and referral services in IDP camp and host community clinics.
AGUF will work with the Adamawa State MOH, SPHCDA and other partners to address the critical gaps hindering access to emergency primary health care services including addressing emerging challenges and critical needs. These needs include: inadequate number and distribution of health care workers, renovation/reconstruction of health facilities because most of the facilities have been damaged, poor storage capacity and dilapidated warehouses, poor logistics management system, lack of emergency drugs and supplies and absence of referral systems due to lack of ambulance and security.
In Adamawa state, AGUF in partnership with the SPHCDA and the State Ministries of Health will ensure that basic supplies and commodities required for effective Cholera OBR available and prepositioned to reduce mortalities associated with the outbreak.

1 - Needs

Adamawa state is faced by multiple events of cholera outbreak, monkey pox, covid-19, flooding and other priority diseases. These conditions in combination with the insurgency, communal clashes, and the activity of Shillah boys have further weakened the healthcare service provision in the state. Currently in Adamawa State, there is no single on-site healthcare facility providing healthcare services to IDPs in camps since June last following the pulling out of UNICEF due to lack of funding. Services to the IDPs in the state is based on activities of the HTR MTs which are in just 5 LGAs and supported by World Health Organization, thereby putting the IDPs at more risk of morbidity and death. For over 5 years now dedicated services for referral is no longer available in the state for IDPs, so these vulnerable populations engage in out-of-pocket spending and on most occasions 1 out of 6 cannot afford their bills. Many IDPs have been left to their fate and in most instances the outcome is bad. Based on the round 39 DTM Adamawa State has an IDP population of 221,253 who have no access to adequate healthcare services, poor WASH situations, inadequate nutrition with cases of malnutrition and lack of tangible livelihoods. These serves as triggers for communicable diseases.Based on ADSEMA report this year on the flooding, all the 21 LGAs have so far been affected by the floods, but the worst hit are Girei where over 3,000 displacements and 10 deaths were reported (ADSEMA/OCHA). Floods continue to serve as public health threat to already the weak system on ground. In all the LGAs currently having cholera outbreak, floods at different levels have occurred due to poor housing conditions and poor environmental sanitation.While Adamawa State has reported suspected cholera outbreaks in 2001, 2005, 2008 and another confirmed outbreak in 2009, 2013, 2017, 2018, 2019 and now 2021. The year 2009 witnessed the worst incidence of cholera outbreak in Adamawa State with the outbreak involving 15 of the 21 LGAs with over 3,443 cases and 247 deaths (CFR7.2%) reported. The affected LGAs were Maiha, Fufore, Michika, Madagali, Shelleng, Girei, Lamurde, Hong, Gombi, Song, Demsa, Numan, Yola-North, Mubi-North, and Mubi-South with these last2 LGAs worst hit. In Adamawa State, a total of 474 cases have been confirmed as of October 04, 2021, with 22 deaths (CFR - 5.6%). Most of the cases were reported from 9 LGAs of Lamurde, Yola North, Yola South, shelling, Song, Numan, Fufore, Gerei and Gombi LGAs Presently Lamurde LGA accounts for the highest number of confirmed cases. This year 2021 Guyuk and Lamurde LGA are currently having ongoing cholera outbreak. 104 suspected cases have so far been reported with 2 deaths CFR 1.9%. There are currently 15 confirmed cases of monkey pox in Adamawa out of 97 suspected cases with no case fatality for now since January last year. Majority of cases were reported in Yola North prison. Currently 6 LGAs have reported at least one confirmed case of monkey pox. Sample collection and shipment, low knowledge gap among HCWs and lack of adequate risk communication for this disease declared as a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC) needs urgent attention in the State. Covid -19 still a PHIEC has been seen to be of increase in recent times due to a mutant variant while the dry season is gradually coming. Although Adamawa State currently has the highest covid-19 vaccination coverage of 38% as compared to the other BAY states, more needs to be done to reach 80% especially with the coming of the 3.0 strategy for scaling covid 19 coverage. Adamawa currently has 1,342 confirmed cases of covid 19 with 39 deaths (CFR 3.2%)

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Protection mainstreaming has become a norm in AGUFs day to day activities to ensure equity and equality in terms of service delivery. As a core value in planning and implementation of all programs and activities, AGUF, advocate, employ “do no harm” approach which implies taking cognizance of protection risks or potential violations of rights of members of target communities or simply put, DOING HARM PRINCIPLE to intended solace to community members or target beneficiaries. AGUF staff have been trained on protection issues by Protection actors and can identify and refer all cases of GBV, child abuse etc. Through the proposed projects in the areas, and so we will ensure that adequate Do No Harm analyses are being conducted and that participatory methods in all aspects of the project cycle are implemented. All services to be provided will be designed to ensure safe and easy access for all taking into account vulnerable person with specific needs (women, children, elderly, etc.). Our monitoring tools will also include protection mainstreaming indicators as well as questions related to protection mainstreaming in all potential data to be collected so that humanitarian assistance is delivered in a safe, accessible, accountable and participatory manner. AGUF, also ensure that marginalized groups Women, Children and people living with disabilities) in the communities are carried along in process and outcomes of all interventions.Thus, in the course of executing this project, due care will be taken towards ensuring that fairness is being done to gender equity. This means due consideration is given to the inclusion of issues pertaining to the life and the life chances of men and women.On the side of disability, AGUF has always allow for representation of persons living with disability in the course of planning and implementation of its programs and activities. The design and delivery of activities should also factor in the special needs of people living with disability.Regarding conflict sensitivity, AGUF is always conscious of the local norms and conflicts dynamics. We will therefore work with all the relevant stakeholders to identify any key concerns and factors that could lead to resistance/spoilage.in summation, AGUF will employ the core humanitarian standards (CHS)which does not only looks the community as beneficiary but as partner: it recognizes the need to ensure people affected by crisis are not only informed and consulted about the core humanitarian but at the center of decision making process.
