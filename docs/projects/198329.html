Project Summary

This project aims to promote a protective learning environment for 118,000 children living in the Northwest and Southwest regions of Cameroon.
1,475 teachers will be trained in psychosocial support to ensure that the children in their care are in the moral, psychological and social conditions that enable them to attend classes, improve their coping skills and thus improve academic performance. This training will be coupled with training on conflict and disaster risk reduction, after which the beneficiary schools will be expected to work on risk reduction plans that include actions to be taken by communities to minimize risks in the school environment so that children can learn in a safer and less harmful environment.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Traumatic situations experienced by children, as well as their parents and teachers, have had a detrimental effect on their lives and have seriously affected their moral, intellectual, psychological, and social well-being. The training of teachers and other education personnel aims to equip them with the necessary skills and tools to enable them to know the children at their disposal well, to detect the signs of trauma in children, and to provide them with the necessary support or referral depending on the case and the availability of specialized structures for care. This inevitably contributes to the improvement of the learning environment as well as to the protection of children in the school environment. All teachers who have lived through this situation and who supervise internally displaced children traumatized by the events they have experienced must have the necessary skills to provide good supervision of the children. In 2022, a total of 358 teachers, including 230 women, were trained in psychosocial support, and enabled 53,017 children, including 30,533 girls, to be taught by a teacher trained to benefit from the skills acquired through this training. This figure represents 2% of the total number of teachers in the two regions concerned. More teachers need to be trained to improve teaching and learning conditions in the targeted schools.The psychosocial support training will be coupled with conflict and disaster risk reduction training, after which the training participants will return to their respective schools to work with communities on risk reduction plans that will enable school communities to identify the hazards and risks children face in their schools and on their way to and from school, to implement actions to reduce or minimize them. At the same time, children will be provided with the necessary guidance on appropriate behaviors and actions to take in the event of a hazard to minimize the risk.1,475 new teachers will be trained to enable approximately 118,000 children to be taught by a trained teacher and to study in a protective learning environment.
