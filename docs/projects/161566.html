Project Summary

The project is intended to alleviate WASH emergency needs dictated in Pulka (Gwoza) and Dikwa in Dikwa LGA of Borno State by improving hygiene and sanitation practices and water services that will reduce the spread of water-borne diseases among IDPs, returnees and host communities. The project consists of components that promote adequate Hygiene and sanitation knowledge, attitudes and practice through hygiene promotion campaigns, which include proper latrine maintenance, garbage disposal, support for key WASH items and dignity kits for women and adolescent girls through multi-purpose cash (MPC) support with different modalities. This is targeted at supporting the immediate needs of IDPs and returning families that have nothing to fall back to and 10% vulnerable host community members in consonant with the principles of Do No harm . It has been evident also that not much attention has been given to the energy part of the Water, Energy and Food Chain. this, in turn, will have a multi-sector effect on the population, which has a great impact on the nutritional level and the resultant effect on low productivity in livelihood activity.
To support the food security and other multi-sectorial effect, beneficiaries will be supported with cash for food, and complement other food beneficiaries with condiments/supplements (vegetables, fruits, meats,.
eggs and vinegar) and fuel which will be provided through a voucher program of identified suppliers consideration with the safety of the environment and as well supported with energy-saving stoves and also train them with alternative technologies.
Trained members will be supported with start-up kits (production hand kits, casting moulds, T-shaped 1 inch pipe, stencils by 30mtr by 30mtrs wooden plants) which will add up to a livelihood component that will support them even after the interventions as a form of sustainability, as the beneficiaries will continue to share learning with non-beneficiaries.
CRUDAN will ensure that staff, volunteers and partners (eg Contractors, suppliers, etc)) adhere to the organisation's codes of ethics and sign for Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA); adhere to it in the execution of all aspect of intervention. CRUDAN will also ensure AAP issues are incorporated in its interventions.

1 - Needs

CRUDAN’s needs assessment conducted in Dikwa and Gwoza in June 2019, and in addition with other secondary information, identified the frequent influx of new arrivals, as a result some of the major needs identified , food needs is considered most critical, as those who are having access to food assistance, would have to share with unregistered/new arrivals, which made it inadequate and difficult to meet up with the food needs. For others that still have access to food support, such where not complemented with condiments. It was established that most of the new arrivals hardly comes in with items or any belongings, and among the critical things the need is a cooking stove and utensils that will help them prepare their food, because this many have to take the risk of going into the bushes to gate firewood, causing harm to the environment, and tendencies to fire incidences as the put in fires in open space with stones given it the tripod stand. Another source of concern in the findings, is selling of items by the beneficiaries at a devalued price in other to buy what the actually need and are happy with. Some beneficiaries felt lack/or limited support for livelihood made them continues dependent on humanitarian aid, which the felt that if they are supported to use their previous skills in reviving their livelihood base, they will be less dependent.Because of functionality of the makes and for the purpose of dignity and given the power of choice, most of the beneficiaries preferred cash and/or voucher support. The project intends to support Women, Men, Boys, girls and people living with disabilities with Food, Energy Saving Stoves, and livelihood grants. The modality of delivery is through Cash in Envelopes and Vouchers There are some water facilities in Gwoza town but not enough to serve the whole communities of 8 wards, the presence of actors providing food support in Dikwa is not enough considering the influx and families have to wait for months before been considered to receive their food supply: even those that receive, the ratio is not enough for the family size and does not contain all food nutrients. New arrivals do face great challenges in meeting up with basic household amenities (eg. Sanitation Facilities), and other WASH NFI’s. Pulka and Dikwa town rely mostly on water trucking as the limited facilities cannot serve the population, and for the available facilities does not meet the population need and takes longer to get. Accessing safe water is difficult through the digging of boreholes due to Pulka town’s geographical landscape, with reticulation of water points from a high yield position that will change the narrative and in Dikwa generally most available WASH facilities in the camp are damaged (e.g. Latrines), also very few water facilities are available which are not enough to support the large population (UNHCR September 2019). Protection as a cross-cutting theme given the high number of women, youth and children Open defecation is witness mostly among children, Absent or poor performance of sewage and waste and weak sanitation and hygiene conditions aggravates the risk of spreading diseases. Some protection issues include a negative coping strategy of managing resources by marrying off the girls to reduce family burden. Head of families Male or female, most especially Male, could become ashamed for not meeting up to family needs. There are cases of GBV and other psychosocial issues that need greater support for but case and clinical management and as well as regular follow-up
