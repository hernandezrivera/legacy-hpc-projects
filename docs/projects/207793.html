Project Summary

1. Community based health activities:
-Awareness activities for vulnerable (pregnant, disability): Vulnerable people are more likely to face challenges in access to health care as well as to timely and correct information. Therefore, awareness will be conducted focusing on them and their family. CVs provide the necessity of ANC/ PNC checkup, facility delivery, danger signs, family planning and GBV. Even if a pregnant understands the need and willing it, her family may not allow it. For this, awareness will provide including her family, not only a pregnant. If target family have specific difficulty, CVs will support them to solve it through their counselling. Other needed topic also be provided. The topics of the awareness will be selected based on disease trend at the camp and the health/ hygiene topics that are specific to the vulnerable individuals. Awareness will be provided in forms of group sessions and individual counselling. Group sessions are conducted by the CHWs/ CVs and aims to facilitate interaction among participants by sharing challenges and success and discuss solutions on how they can maintain their health. Not only the vulnerable individuals and their families, but their neighbors will also be invited to create an opportunity for the community to develop a better understanding of the vulnerable individuals and think how they can be supported within their community. Individual counselling will be conducted through home visits by the CHWs/CVs, who will provide specific awareness and tailored advice to the individual needs. Even if pregnant and her family are willing to facility delivery, it may not be practicable due to lack of transportation. To avoid this situation, CVs support to make delivery plan including preparing transportation. And CVs assist to transport to visit facility on their needs. It will be contributing the safety delivery and prenatal and postnatal life.
-Training for community Volunteers (CVs) : In order to promote health at the community level, it is essential to develop the capacity of the community people and engage them as actors in the awareness activities for a sustainable and realistic intervention. The selected CVs will receive comprehensive training to obtain the knowledge of maternal health and hygiene that is necessary to conduct the awareness activities and home visit as outlined above. In addition to knowledge, participants will be trained in practical skills such as how to make and stretcher using surrounded items, so that they can assist in emergency transport.
2. Health promotion support through the health facility
-Provision of healthcare services: The center has been more focused on SRH services along with general services for both the FDMNs and Host communities to cover the delivery gap among camp14. Ensuring 24/7 ANC/PNC and safe normal delivery including USG support with availability of all support and adequate trained SRH staff. Identifying GBV and ensure confidential and safe space within the health facility. Patients requiring further care will be referred according to the referral pathways. Pregnant found in CVs above activities are basically brought to the facility for ANC/PNC check-ups and deliveries. And if CVs find difficult case in their activity, they can consult to the facility’s medical staff. Cooperation with facility enables early detection of abnormalities and smooth consultations.
-Facility based health awareness sessions: Medical staffs will organize awareness sessions in the health facility to the patients based on the patient trend and/or the seasonal trend in the camps, necessity of ANC/PNC check up and facility delivery to enable timely health education and promote behavior change. Arranging family planning awareness for both male and female in the facility. Increasing the demand of family planning commodities through reducing social stigma, barrier myths, misperception about family planning among the FDMNs and host communities.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Although 5 years have passed since the influx, the circumstances surrounding the refugees are yet challenging. The total population of FDMNs and Host Communities (HC) in need of assistance is projected to be more than 1.5 million (BGD Rohingya Response Host Community Population 2023-25). The camp continues to observe spikes of communicable diseases such as skin diseases, AWD, which are caused by the congested and unhygienic living conditions in the camp. The uptake of SRH services has improved with increased facility delivery rates and utilization of modern family planning compared to the initial stages of the displacement. However, variations are observed among each camp due to the availability of services and geographical access. For instance, the facility delivery rate in Camp 14 is 66% (HS Coordination meeting presentation of Dec 21, 2022) which is lower than the overall rate of the camps. In addition to the health issues that existed from the beginning of the crisis, the increased needs of NCDs and mental health has been recognized. Similar trends are observed in the HCs, where ongoing negative effects caused by the influx is recognized and service accessibility is challenging. In order to cater the health needs of the refugees and HCs, partners have been providing services under the leadership and coordination of the health sector. Given the protracted nature and financial constraints, service delivery must seek for medium and longer term solutions considering efficiency and sustainability. The Health Sector Strategic Plan 2023-2024 strives for strengthening health system capacity to enable efficient, effective and sustainable delivery of quality health care and ensuring equitable access to essential health care at all levels including the community level. Among the refugees and HC, there is a tendency of recognizing that medicine is the sole solution for health, rather than understanding the importance of practicing healthy behaviors and preventive measures. Health education is essential at the facility level as well as at the community level so that beneficiaries understand that behavior change is the key along with timely health seeking behavior to promote their health and well-being. Despite efforts of health sector partners, not all the beneficiaries have equal access to health care. Among the refugees living in the camps, 4% are elderly persons, 4.29% have at least one specialized needs, and 1% have disabilities (UNHCR Population Fact sheet as of September 30, 2023). Other reports indicate a higher prevalence of disabilities, with 12% individuals having a disability and 35% of the households have at least one member with a disability (Age and Disability Inclusion Needs Assessment, REACH Initiative, 2021). Despite that vulnerable individuals such as persons with disabilities, elderlies and pregnant women have higher health related needs, there are more likely to face barriers in accessing health due to the distance and lack of transportation options in the camp, which may disturb their access to health care when needed. Vulnerable individuals are also reported to have less opportunity to attend community meetings and are less likely to be visited by NGO workers implying that they do not receive necessary information in a timely way. Access to basic water, sanitation and hygiene is also challenging, which hampers their practice to maintain hygiene. A community based approach through CHWs/ community volunteers to deliver correct and timely health awareness to promote health and behavior change and enable timely health seeking behavior is needed. In line with the Health Sector Strategic Plan, our project will provide awareness for health and hygiene with special focus on vulnerable individuals and provide essential primary health care services to ensure that beneficiaries including those with specialized needs are able to receive health care when needed, where not only the FDMNs but also the HCs will be beneficiaries.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Our PHC has been operating in Camp 14 in coordination with the Health Sector ever since the Rohingya influx. We also participate in the camp level coordination with the CiC and Camp Health Focal Point and have coordinated well with them. Since we have been providing essential health services through our PHC and conducting awareness activities and home visit in the community for a long time, our PHC is well recognized and accepted in Camp 14 and the surrounding host community. Our PHC is especially known as the only facility that provides delivery service in Camp 14. Despite the challenging geographical features of Camp 14 where there are many uphill and downhill, pregnant women have been coming to our PHC to deliver their child as a result of our extensive community activities to aware them about the importance and safety of facility delivery. New mothers who delivered at our PHC are satisfied with the care they received and have recommended their neighbors and families to deliver at the PHC, and have also come back to deliver their next child. In this way, we have built good relationships with our patients and the community, and have contributed to promoting safe facility deliveries, which is encouraged by the sectorAlthough our PHC was not recommended as the result of the rationalization exercise, the ranking was “green” indicating that we have been providing quality health care services. The recommended PHC in Camp 14 is not providing facility delivery and is not planning to incorporate this service. The sudden discontinuation of our service will mean that there will be no facilities available in Camp 14 that can provide facility delivery, which will not only confuse the beneficiaries, but may lead them to give up the thought to deliver their child at the facility, which is a concern that must be avoided. Our PHC will provide delivery service in Camp 14 to fill the gap of the lacking function of the recommended PHC, along with seeking alternative solution.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

This project focus on vulnerable people in the community such as pregnant women and disabilities who have difficulties in physical access to health facilities and limited access to correct health information. This project will support vulnerable people to obtain the correct knowledge, educate surroundings not only vulnerable people to understand each other’s. This will reduce the difficulties they have and protect their rights to health-seeking behavior regardless of their vulnerability.

3.3 - Age & Disability

The list of vulnerable people created by the project includes pregnant female and persons with disabilities, but will also include husbands of pregnant female and their families, as well as family members and neighbors of persons with disabilities. The understanding of husbands and family members is essential for facility deliveries and health facility visits, especially for female in Rohingya culture. In addition, even if pregnant and her families want to have an facility delivery, this is sometimes not possible due to a lack of co-operation for transport. While it is important for vulnerable people themselves to gain knowledge to cope with each difficulty, the understanding and cooperation of those around them is essential for a solution. By learning about the difficulties of people in different situations and how to support them, it is possible to build a diverse society in which it is easier for diverse people to live.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

At the beginning of the project, all household are surveyed and a list of beneficiaries is prepared. During this process, we'll explain the selection criteria and process to community to making consensus. During the awareness session, encourage participants to speak up and encourage discussion among themselves. After the awareness session, feedback is obtained from the participants. Based on the content of discussion and feedback, the project will consider to revise content of the awareness session or decide the topic of the next session.Patient feedback of our facility is collected through suggestion boxes and interviews. All data gathered will be disaggregated by sex and age for analysis and monitoring. Based on the date, the facility suvice will be developed.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

In addition, to ensure that sexual and reproductive health and rights are protected, including unwanted pregnancy, unsafe abortion, gender-based violence (GBV), and decision-making in the use of maternal and child health services, through awareness activities for women who are particularly vulnerable, such as pregnant women and those with disabilities, the target population will be given access to necessary information and services.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Due to difficulties in physical access to health facilities and limited access to correct health information, vulnerable people in the community such as pregnant women, people with disabilities who have higher health risks are more likely to be left out in case of emergencies. CVs will identify the vulnerable individuals and provide them with the necessary information to promote health and well-being, and about available health services accessible from their community, how they can get there, whom to contact, so that they can be supported in case of emergencies. CVs will serve as the key person in their community that the community people can rely on when they need health related information. By identifying vulnerable, this will contribute to confirming the people’s safety in a disaster and providing emergency information without any missing. In addition, support to discuss on transportation methods like man power with neighbors on a daily basis will facilitate evacuation.
