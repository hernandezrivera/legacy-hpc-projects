Project Summary

The overall goal of this project is to scale up activities aimed at effectively decreasing suffering, morbidity and mortality related to malnutrition among vulnerable populations in Fangak and Nyirol Counties. This is pegged on the fact that acute malnutrition has been a protracted problem in the targeted counties and thus one that requires sustained investment. The relevant literature reviewed reveal that the needs are substantial and widespread, and it is apparent that there are numerous challenges associated with availability and access to nutritious foods which require urgent interventions.
This proposed CMA response will, therefore prioritize life-saving treatment services targeting children under five years of age, PLW, and chronically ill persons as the most vulnerable groups to acute malnutrition due to their increased biological and physiological needs. Similarly, prevention activities that address the immediate and underlying causes of malnutrition will be enhanced. Further, this response will expand on nutrition specific interventions, as well as reinforce integration and linkages with other sectors to address the key drivers of malnutrition: food insecurity, sub-optimal child care and feeding practices, morbidity, and lack of safe water and sanitation. It will prioritize the unreached payams in Fangak & Nyirol where high prevalence of acute malnutrition, conflict and internal displacement coexist. The project will also enhance activities around nutrition monitoring, analysis and utilization of early warning information for timely coordinated response decision making.
Using static and mobile outreach approaches the project will scale-up services in the designated 12 OTP-TSFP sites. The response strategies will be (1) carry-out case-finding screening using the MUAC method (including malaria testing for SAM patients) from static units and through outreaches to PHCUs and admitting under 5 SAM and MAM patients and PLW MAM patients to SC, OTP and TSFP services (2) conduct mobile outreaches to inaccessible locations beyond the reach of static services (3) deliver MIYCN services to PLW and care-givers of children U23 months through counselling MtoM support groups, including vitamin A supplements and de-worming (4) fully integrate nutrition, health and WASH services, and partially integrate FSL, Education and Protection activities. SAM with medical complications and requiring malaria treatment will be referred to PHCCs.
In order to improve this project’s responsiveness to evolving and varied needs of the affected population, CMA will adopt a people-centered approach by mainstreaming protection, Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP), disability inclusion as well as emphasis on gender and age considerations. In order to build a ground for sustainability, this project will seek to strengthen local capacity, including County Health Department at county level, project staff and community nutrition volunteers through targeted training. Since insecurity creates risks for PLW and U5s, CMA in collaboration with the targeted communities will implement protection practices to reduce risk of SGBV. By applying IASC guidance, measures to identify specific needs of men, women, boys and girls and mainstream gender and protection will be adopted and utilized.
In this project, CMA will provide mechanisms through which affected populations can receive the information they need to make informed decisions on accessing nutrition services, provide feedback on the nutrition interventions, share concerns and submit complaints. CMA will work closely with all stakeholders e.g. local authorities, community leaders, beneficiaries etc. to ensure their inclusion in program decision-making and participation in planning, implementation and monitoring the delivery of nutrition services.

1 - Needs

The emergency this project will address is life threatening acute malnutrition (GAM ≥15%) and will target Fangak and Nyirol Counties in South Sudan. Fangak County is an administrative area in Fangak State with five administrative payams namely Manajang, Paguir, Mareang, Old Fangak and Phom. Similarly, Nyirol is an administrative area referred to as a county in Eastern Bieh with six administrative payams namely Chuil, Pultruk, Nyambor, Thol, Padding and Waat. Before the reorganization of states, the two were part of the greater Jonglei state. Ironically, the situation analysis report by World Vision (July 2019) indicated that in early 2019, the highest proportions of population in IPC Phase 3: “Crisis” and higher were in Unity (67.8%), followed by Jonglei (64.1%) which has the highest number of people estimated to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. These high levels of acute food insecurity continue to be driven by the cumulative effects of the national and localized conflicts e.g. cattle raiding and intercommunal conflict involving revenge killings and population displacements which contributed to insufficient crop production, disrupted households’ access to natural food sources including wild foods, fish & livestock. In some counties such as Nyirol, Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes, while in some counties such as Fangak, Crisis (IPC Phase 3) outcomes have persisted due to broadly seasonal flooding which destroyed crops, limited food and income sources among displaced households and disrupted delivery of assistance and market functioning (S. Sudan IPC Snapshot - Sept 2019)). During January to April 2020, food security situation is expected to deteriorate throughout the state, and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity are expected in some counties such as Nyirol as household food stocks start to deplete and access to livestock products and wild foods reduce seasonally during this period. In Six counties including Fangak, Crisis (IPC Phase 3) outcomes are expected to persist given availability of some harvest, fish, livestock products, and access to markets and HFA. Overall, an estimated 61.1% of the state population are expected to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) during the January to April 2020 projection period (SS- Food insecurity and under-nutrition (IPC, UN) (ECHO Daily Flash of 13 September 2019). Similarly, the nutrition situation in the country is projected to deteriorate. IPC projections estimate that approximately 1.3 million children under 5 will be acutely malnourished and in need of treatment in 2020 - a 50% increase in the malnutrition caseload compared to the previous year. This is expected to be exacerbated by the effect of heavy rains which have inundated shelters, water sources, crops and crucial productive assets in many areas. Besides damaging essential service infrastructure like schools and health facilities, impacting women and children in particular, they have rendered many roads impassable, constraining people’s access to markets and humanitarians’ ability to move across affected areas. Thousands of families have temporarily relocated from worst-affected to safer areas. Guided by the above analysis, therefore, the needed services include SC, SAM and MAM services, MIYCN services, Prevention of malnutrition through provision of specialized food, behavior change communication, prevention of micro-nutrient deficiencies and strengthened capacity to monitor the nutrition situation. There is also need to ensure early detection and treatment of girls and boys under five years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition and promotion of community participation for a responsive nutrition service delivery. The project will use a combination of responses - RRM/ICRM, static/mobile, survival kits ensuring timely delivery of supplies.

2 - Activities

In order to contribute to the reduction of morbidity and mortality arising from malnutrition in Fangak and Nyirol Counties, this project has proposed several interventions/ activities. All these are aligned to the objectives and are meant to achieve the same as defined below:1. Increased equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific service delivery for children, adolescents and women in Fangak and Nyirol by the end of 2020.1.1 Prevention of malnutrition through provision of specialized food focused on under-five children and PLW 1.2 Prevention of malnutrition through behaviour change communication with focus on caregivers of children 0-23 months1.3 Prevention of micronutrient deficiencies through vitamin A supplementation focused on children 6-59 months1.4 Train nutrition workers and community health workers on the MIYCN counselling package1.5 Conduct individual MIYCN counselling for pregnant and mothers and caregivers of children 0-<23 months and with MtoMSGs 1.6 Promote community participation for a responsive nutrition service delivery by raising awareness of beneficiaries on their rights/entitlement in the nutrition sites and by establishing a community complaints and feedback mechanism in OTP/TSFP sites 2. Increased equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for the early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for the most vulnerable and at risk children 6-59 months & PLW.2.1 Establish and maintain SCs, OTPs and TSFP facilities for quality and timely nutrition services 2.2 Deliver early detection and treatment of girls and boys U5 years of age affected by SAM 2.3 Deliver early detection and treatment of children 6-59 months affected by MAM 2.4 Deliver early detection and treatment of PLW affected by moderate acute malnutrition2.5 Train health and nutrition workers of OTP sites on management of severe acute malnutrition to attain the “very high” composite performance score for management of SAM 2.6 Train health and nutrition workers of TSFP sites on management of moderate acute malnutrition to attain “very high” composite performance score for management of MAM 3. Increased equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and joint programming targeting vulnerable groups in Fangak and Nyirol Counties3.1 Conduct malaria testing and treatment and provide ITNs for 6-59 month children admitted for SAM treatment 3.2 Provide WASH NFI kits to children with SAM at all OTP facilities 3.3 Conduct safety audit/assessments at nutrition sites at least twice per year to identify and address women and children’s protection/safety needs3.4 Establish kitchen garden at OTP/TSFP sites for demonstration3.5 Mainstream child protection and GBV risk mitigation through training of health, nutrition and WASH workers on SGBV and the referral pathways (GBV/CP)3.6 Conduct joint educational sessions delivering nutrition, health, WASH, FSL and protection messages routinely from OTP/TSFP centers and through case-finding outreaches3.7 Integrate child stimulation and ECD in the provision of care for children with SAM 4. Strengthened nutrition information system for evidence-based response to the malnutrition crisis 4.1 Through monthly and quarterly meetings, conduct ongoing consultation, review and evaluation of nutrition programme performance with local authorities, other county-based humanitarian actors, community leaders and affected beneficiaries of nutrition services 4.2 Training nutrition staff on preparation and analysis of monthly NIS reports on results of SAM, MAM programs for dissemination to nutrition cluster, donor partners and humanitarian actors, including analysis of impacts on affected populations 4.3 Deliver nutrition reports on routine admissions to county and state authorities as well as nutrition cluster and donors in a timely manner

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

In this project, it is acknowledged that a durable solution is achieved when beneficiaries can enjoy their human rights without discrimination of any kind and when these legal rights are fully recognized and supported in the delivery if this project. In addition to the foregoing, and in as far as this project will be undertaken under the umbrella of UNOCHA, and in support of and in respect of authorizations provided by the GOSS, and as CMA delivers the assistance offered by the donors in respect of these authorizations, then the rights of beneficiaries will be legally recognized under this project. Understanding that achieving durable solutions includes recognition of the human rights of beneficiaries, this project will be implemented in collaboration with government representatives at county level (recognizing that government actors must be supported to take a leadership and coordination role in promoting and facilitating solutions), and with other humanitarian actors (UN Agencies, INGOs, local NGOs) working in Fangak and Nyirol Counties. This project reasonably assumes that the inclusion and subsequent participation of many actors is the surest way of ensuring that the beneficiaries' rights are subsequently legally recognized.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

In this project, it will be acknowledged that a durable solution is achieved when IDPs no longer have specific protection needs that are linked to their displacement and such persons can enjoy their human rights and entitlements without discrimination resulting from their displacement. Therefore, working towards durable solutions means diminishing gradually the needs and vulnerabilities of displacement affected communities, while strengthening their capacities, skills, and increasing their resilience, so that displaced persons can increasingly enjoy their rights. It is further noted that the IASC Framework identifies three options to achieve this goal, which are underpinned by the principles of safety, dignity and non-discrimination: right to return of displaced persons to their place of origin or habitual residence; acceptance by host populations to settle and integrate in areas where displaced persons have sought refuge; and the right to seek settlement elsewhere in the country. In recognition of the above, in this project CMA will work very closely with the community leaders, local authorities as well as INGOs and NNGOs whose interventions cover the protection sector and coordinated by the protection cluster. This will help ensure that all the displacement-affected populations will have access to, and adequately benefit from the project. Further, the project will actively seek to engage both men and women of displaced population in this project so they have opportunity to participate in planning, implementation and evaluation of project activities, ensure displaced populations are aware of and have access to the complaints and feedback mechanism, and sufficient protection to enable free access to project services and benefits.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project will be focused on nutrition in collaboration with health and WASH service providers as coordinated through the Nutrition, Health and WASH Clusters, respectively, and through the MOH primary health and nutrition care system. In some locations the collaborating partner will be CMA also delivering health and nutrition programming. In other locations, other collaborating humanitarian partners will be delivering the health, education, FSL and protection interventions, all of which operate under the locally available government systems. This includes state, county and payam authorities representing both the GOSS administrative system and the various sector ministries. Further, as an active humanitarian actor in both Fangak and Nyirol counties, CMA will collaborate with, and participate in the existing forums for state and county coordination involving representatives of both government and humanitarian actors, to ensure effective and timely coordination with all humanitarian actors delivering programs in the targeted locations of this project. Through these links, the project expects to foster durable solutions in the context of WASH interventions. As has been the practice of CMA throughout its involvements in South Sudan, at the community level, the project will link with the traditional leaders and authorities, local opinion leaders and women leaders, ensuring links with and inclusion of, displaced and disabled populations. The project will also link with and engage the various structures of committees and representative of both traditional groups, church groups and groups formed through Nutrition, Health, WASH, education, FSL and protection interventions so as to quickly and thoroughly increase equitable access to integrated preventative and lifesaving emergency nutrition services for crisis affected IDP and host populations in Fangak and Nyirol Counties.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

CMA has provided nutrition services in Nyirol County since November 2015 and health services since 1997. Therefore, CMA boasts of a 20-year history of health FSL and nutrition work in Fangak and Nyirol counties. During this period, CMA has always ensured that its project work align with all national objectives and all sector protocols as defined through the various sector cluster programming mandates and the sector ministries and department of the GOSS. As in other sectors, with this nutrition project, CMA will ensure that activities and implementation approaches adhere to the technical protocols of the Basic Package of Health and nutrition Services (BPHNS) as prescribed by MoH, and with all guidance provided by the Clusters in relations to social protection, inclusivity, dignity and respect for all groups of target beneficiaries without discrimination and by addressing human rights, and reducing vulnerabilities and risks of the populations served. This project will also work within the framework of a multi-sector integration approach bringing in humanitarian actors delivering nutrition, health, FSL, education and protection interventions as promoted by GOSS, and sector ministries as well as the operational agencies of the UN, INGOs, local NGOs working in Fangak and Nyirol Counties. Similarly, in both Fangak and Nyirol Counties, CMA will use the existing channels to ensure effective and timely coordination with all humanitarian actors delivering programs in the targeted locations of this project. Complementarity will be achieved as the WASH project will be fully integrated with nutrition and health services and by coordinating and collaborating closely with other humanitarian actors delivering education, protection and FSL projects /interventions. This approach is expected to deliver strong and durable results through the WASH project.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

In target counties, protection of vulnerable populations remains an ongoing concern. Currently, there is no conflict between opposing military forces nor any inter-tribal conflict. As such both counties demonstrate a safe and stable environment for the delivery of humanitarian services. Challenges occur within communities and ensuring that all populations who need humanitarian services feel free and safe in accessing them. In relation to the Nutrition sector, SGBV is the most critical and common protection concern, especially among IDPs, and children, adolescent girls, women, and disabled people of host and IDP populations. These are the most vulnerable groups of concern. Understanding that a sufficiently safe and stable environment at the community level is essential for sustainable humanitarian impacts, it is also evident that durable solutions that provide a safe environment need to be anchored in the political-will of leadership at all levels of community and government. However, humanitarian actors must also implement measures and act in a do-no-harm approach to ensure all populations have unimpeded access the Nutrition services. In seeking to address long-term safety concerns, this project will implement the following measures:1. work closely with the community leaders, local authorities and NGOs in the protection sector to coordinate appropriate and needed protection measures;2. ensure facility-based safety audits are conducted as per Nutrition Cluster protocols and implement any required changes to approaches and install gender-appropriate modifications during the nutrition project implementation.3. engage affected populations of host and IDP populations including people with disabilities so that they participate in the planning, implementation and evaluation of Nutrition activities delivered under this project.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

A sustainability strategy is known to protect and improve a community’s resilience, empower beneficiaries with assets and can be a springboard for improved quality of life and sustainable development hence most important in delivering durable solutions. To ensure a lasting improvement, this project will promote integration with health, FSL, WASH, education and protection interventions. Further, it will adopt a collaborative framework involving community leaders, other humanitarian actors, local authorities, government departments and the sector ministries at county, state and national levels. In order to advance sustainability, the project will focus on the following:• Whenever possible lessen dependency on external food commodities by promoting use of locally available nutritious options; • Through nutrition education and protection messaging support local efforts to integrate IDPs by making productive resources available to them to engage in FSL activities • Through WASH messaging, reduce the impact of poor quality drinking water and poor hygiene and sanitation practices including promotion of latrine use;• Whenever possible, link MAM clients with FSL programmes for assistance to regain productive livelihoods as fishers and as farmers;• Through community-based analysis of gender roles in food production and consumption, support the empowerment of women being the key actors involved in shaping the nutritious quality of the food consumed by the children and themselves when pregnant. • Promote multisectoral coordination which has the propensity to create synergy and continuation even after project completion. • Emphasize on aggressive sensitization and training of the key stakeholders on key project deliverables to embed skills and facilitate continuity at the end of the project.• Focus on the documentation of lessons learned and best practices achieved at implementation stage and share widely to promote continuity and/or replicability.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

In this project, CMA will provide mechanisms through which affected populations i.e. most vulnerable community members e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls can receive the information they need to make informed decisions on accessing nutrition services, provide feedback on the nutrition interventions, share concerns and submit complaints. CMA will work closely with all stakeholders e.g. local authorities, community leaders, beneficiaries etc. to ensure their inclusion in program decision-making.These structures will enable affected populations to participate in planning, implementing and monitoring the delivery of nutrition services. CMA’s field teams will undertake FGDs and KIIs as part of the planning and monitoring process, conduct monthly meetings with local, IDP and leaders of Mother to Mother support groups (MtoMSGs) to report on nutrition programming and obtain feedback, and conduct discharge exit interviews with a sample of the beneficiaries to obtain feedback from those who receive nutrition services. Through these regular consultations, the key stakeholders will have the opportunity to effectively participate in planning and monitoring and learn their entitlements for accessing nutrition services and the processes for implementing the full complaints mechanism. Therefore, all nutrition sites will have a feedback and complaint mechanism established as a pillar of project approach to the AAP with an in-built mechanism that the project will consult with users (namely most vulnerable community members e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls) on an individual, KIIs and FGD basis as well as through community meetings to establish a viable, transparent and effective mechanism for delivering feedback and complaints and to seek input to finding resolution to complaints.
