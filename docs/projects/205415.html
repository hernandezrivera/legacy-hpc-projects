Project Summary

This project will be implemented in the 3 Counties of Kapoeta East, North and South to provide quality and equitable integrated health care services to improve demand, access and utilization to the most vulnerable population in Eastern Equatoria State Counties targeting 11,782 (7,838 males & 3,944 females), under 5 years (177 boys and 204 girls), 5-17 years (2,691 males & 1,721 females), 18- 60 Years (4,741Males & 1,892 Females) and above 60 years + (229 males & 127 females) in need for humanitarian Emergency health care services through provision of lifesaving emergency assistant in line with national and international humanitarian standards, and also the epidemiological profile of disease outbreaks, disease burden seasonality, and trauma-related emergency requirements. Prevention, preparedness, response readiness, response, and mitigation tailored to activities with special attention given to providing vulnerable groups including women, children and persons with disabilities in context of IDPs, Returnees and Host Communities.
Organization for Peoples’ Empowerment and Needs (OPEN) has over 8 years of experience in implementing Clinical health packages in both emergency and development in the Ten Counties of Eastern and Western Equatoria State and support community based surveillance, COVID-19 vaccination, EVD outbreak responses, EPI routine vaccination, Risk Communication and Community Engagement, support point of Entry at Nimule and Pogee with funding from USAID and also supported other health interventions through Christian connection for International health (CCIH), World Relief International, Save The Children, and World Vision International to improve access to essential health services that contribute to the overall reduction of the high maternal mortality rates and under-five mortality rates and mitigate the transmission of vaccine-preventable diseases and including other infectious/zoonotic diseases.
With this current appeal, OPEN will strengthen support direct primary health care service delivery and health staff including infection prevention and control (IPC), WASH measures and other appropriate prevention management activities complementing the ongoing Community based surveillance in the 10 Counties.
The response plan will provide quality and equitable health services to the targeted vulnerable population, including maternal, child, adolescent, sexual and reproductive health, common and chronic diseases, neglected tropical diseases, MHPSS (Mental Health and Psychosocial support) and GBV (Gender Based Violence). This response will go along with Universal Health Coverage principles and prioritize prevention, preparedness, response, and mitigation for vulnerable groups. The response will also continue EVD (Ebola Virus and other zoonotic Diseases) prevention, preparedness, and readiness activities, as Western Equatoria faces the threat of EVD and other viral hemorrhagic fevers. The response will support health facilities and community-based systems to serve people in need to strengthen disease surveillance and reporting system in a realization of the three health cluster objectives: -
1. Objective 1: Improve equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services including maternal, child, and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment of common illnesses, disability, MHPSS, and GBV health services to crisis-affected populations.
2. Objective 2: Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases.
3. Objective 3: Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms.

1. Needs

The Population in the three Counties of Kapoeta East, Kapoeta South and Kapoeta North in Eastern Equatoria State are pastoral communities and notably the trends of movement is seasonality drives with reported cattle rustling including road bandits, Food insecurity (the food security situation projection under (IPC Phase 4)) acute food insecurity in Counties of Kapoeta North, Kapoeta East and Kapoeta South as classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) according to FEWSNET: Additionally, the counties are experiencing internal displacement resulting to lack of food due to failed rains, poor agricultural practices and insecurity caused by cattle keepers, and reliance on pastoralist livelihoods, scarce/very low access to water for household and livestock use that led to over 4,500 IDPs in Kapoeta North from Jie Payam of Kapoeta East and therefore, distance, insecurity, and inadequate numbers of functional health facilities across the Counties due to closure of Health pooled Support Primary care units also impact on people’s ability to access medical facilities, care and appropriate medicines in hard to reach areas. Also, lack of enough healthcare workers was commonly reported as low pay and security concerns affecting health workers and consistently malaria remained the main reported cause of death and much higher proportions (between 80%-86%) reported malaria as the main cause of death in all the Counties. These combination factors with low access to health facilities are likely to result in poor health outcomes. The risk of waterborne disease and possible epidemic outbreaks remained relatively high. Furthermore, the Needs contextual analysis from, IRNA and EPI Reports indicated that the incidence rate for common diseases remains immense. Hence, due to the high disease burden resulting from common diseases, epidemic outbreaks, pandemics and neglected diseases, the population targeted for humanitarian healthcare services is based on multiple drivers including, displacements, malnutrition, high disease burden from vaccine-preventable and communicable diseases including Seasonal outbreaks of common diseases including cholera and measles with continued endemicity of malaria is posing a threat to a health system.References:https://fews.net/south-sudan-acute-food-insecurity-classification-april-2023-september-2023 South Sudan IRNA Report for Kapoeta East March 2023-2.pdfhttps://reliefweb.int/report/south-sudan/south-sudan-key-message-update-scaling-assistance-needed-prevent-emergency-ipc-phase-4-or-worse-outcomes-july-2023 https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/immunization-coverage

2. Project activities

The intervention will provide quality health services to people affected by health events, including maternal, child, adolescent, sexual and reproductive health, common and chronic diseases, neglected tropical diseases, MHPSS (Mental Health and Psychosocial support) and GBV (Gender Based Violence). OPEN’s response will also improve sexual reproductive health services of EmONC (emergency newborn and obstetric care), family planning, PMTCT (prevention of mother to child transmission), Clinical Management of Rape and supplies for vulnerable women and girls. The intervention will use referral mechanism to enhance efficiency, integration, and quality of care. The response will follow Universal Health Coverage principles and prioritize prevention, preparedness, response, and mitigation for vulnerable groups. The response will also continue EVD (Ebola Virus Disease) prevention, preparedness, and readiness activities and other viral hemorrhagic fevers.The intervention will also strengthen technical, operational, and field coordination with WASH, FSL, Nutrition, Child Protection, GBV partners through County and State Ministry of Health support while also support disease surveillance and reporting system.
