Project Summary

MBAs will be established in IDP camps in Afrin and Harim district
to provide warm and safe environments in winter conditions as well as a cool space during the summer season. The MBAs are designed for infant feeding support, maternal nutrition, and MHPSS in a safe, private and non-stigmatizing space where IYCF services are provided. MBAs are also a tool to address PSS needs of mothers as they space offers a calm environment where a mother can rest, bond with her baby, engage with other mothers, and get direct help from skilled IYCF-E counsellors for particular feeding difficulties, affecting the well-being of her young child and the family. If the context allows for time and repeated/follow up visits, the nutrition services will aim to include re-lactation support to increase mother’s breastmilk for those who wish to return to breastfeeding. The selection of the MBA sites will be carefully decided according to precise security studies to guarantee the safety of the mothers, their babies and the staff who will be operating in those sites.
As applicable, SR will use the MBA-space as the entry point for referrals to additional services for health needs, SGBV, case management, etc.
Both in the nutrition centres and MBAs nutrition staff will receive training on SR’s global Building Brains common approach for subsequent use with caretakers within the nutrition spaces. What happens in the earliest years of a child’s life has a significant impact, with 90% of brain development taking place before a child’s fifth birthday. Children who do not receive adequate health, nutrition, protection, care, and stimulation in their early years are more likely to do poorly in school and earn less, entrenching a lifetime of unfairness and poverty. The Building Brains evidence-based approach supports caregivers to provide responsive care and opportunities for early learning from birth to three years. Caregiver sessions will aim to change and develop caregiver competencies and behaviours, with a focus on three areas:
Play: Playing games with cups, kitchen items, cloth, and games outside.
Early Communication: Reading books and talking about pictures; Word builder; Story-telling.
Responsive Care: Attachment; Bonding; Calming and Soothing; Protecting young children from accidents; Routines.

4 - Needs

Nutrition cluster in coordination with UNICEF, PAC Team, and ATWG members conducted the 2022 Standardized Expanded Nutrition Survey (SENS). This nutrition survey had been conducted between 02 – 13/ 06 / 2021, the survey covered 9 Districts from 2 governorates (Idlib and Aleppo). Used the Standardized Monitoring and Assessment in Relief and Transition (SMART) methodology to assess nutrition status among children aged 6-59 months and the Standardized Expanded Nutrition Survey (SENS) guidelines to assess anemia, maternal malnutrition, and IYCF practices. Chronic malnutrition is evident in most sub-districts throughout Syria – notably in the northwest of Syria (Aleppo, Idleb). Chronic malnutrition is also on the rise with almost half a million children stunted (low height for age) in 2022 and face losing their future due to hindered learning and physical developments, as well as early deat h. While at least one out of eight children in Syria, is chronically malnourished this ratio is worse in areas affected by displacements, returns, and over-burdened communities such as Aleppo (1 out of 4 children). Stunting in Syria is largely caused by poor infant feeding practices, access to quality diversified diet, and repeated illnesses. To prevent chronic malnutrition, strengthening optimal infant feeding practices and maternal nutrition throughout the first 1,000 days of life is critical not only to the survival of children in Syria, but for their future.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

In line with the Nutrition cluster, SR will ensure " Don no harm "principles are applied to identify and mitigate against potential protection risks that may arise from the implementation of the Nutrition sector strategy and related activities. We will ensure that SPHERE standards are strictly followed and adapted to the context during each part of project implementation, especially during the emergency Nutrition services when the risk of GBV is higher. Measures will also be taken to minimize exposure of the most vulnerable groups (women, adolescent girls, children, people with disabilities, female‑headed households) to greater protection risks, and particularly to GBV. Nutrition staff will be trained on key GBV concepts and referral pathways to properly support survivors. Women, adolescent girls, and people with disabilities will be consulted during the design, implementation, and monitoring phase of each project, with a need to put the necessary mechanisms in place to facilitate feedback from users. We design our projects with an emphasis on meaningful access, safety, dignity, and non‑discrimination so that all potential beneficiaries will benefit from inclusive service provision to the greatest extent possible.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

SR will create a risk register, in which it will identify relevant risks, conduct their analysis and their evaluation by identifying mitigation measures. This will allow to have understanding of the risks and agree on the internal controls to be applied and monitored. Overview of the risks and internal controls: The risk of fraud and misappropriation of assets and supplies due to inadequate financial controls, the lack of segregation of duties and personal integrity. This will be mitigated by having robust internal procedures with the adoption of checks and balances allowing the prevention and detection of fraud. A thorough stock management process is established with a field capacity to spot-check warehouses and conduct stock counts. A whistleblower hotline is available to all project stakeholders and they are aware of it early in the project to report any suspected act of fraud or corruption. An emphasis will be put on the risk of mishandling Personally Identifiable Information (PII) related to project beneficiaries, staff, contractors, vendors, etc. SR will ensure to adopt data protection and privacy measures and ensure compliance throughout the implementation. Particular attention will be given to data protection in case management due to the sensitive nature of its related activities. COVID-19 may have a negative impact and affects Programme implementation. To prevent the spread of COVID-19, staff are trained on COVID-19 protocols issued by Nutrition clusters
