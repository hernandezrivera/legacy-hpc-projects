Project Summary

Medair will provide MPCA to vulnerable, conflict-affected populations in Ninewa, Kirkuk and Salah Al-Din governorates, with accessible, functioning markets as well as the possibility for safe cash delivery. One-off cash distributions, multi-month assistance and value vouchers will be provided as appropriate and determined by market functionality. MPCA interventions will be designed with flexibility in order to respond to the changing context; therefore, smart cards, money transfer agents and mobile money transfer will all be considered as potential modalities for distribution. Medair will follow Cash Working Group (CWG) guidelines and standards including the Standard Minimum Expenditure Basket (SMEB) and guidance regarding time and frequency of distributions. Medair has previous experience using mobile money transfer services with AsiaHawala in Tooz district, in Salah Al-Din governorate (2017) as well as with ZainCash in Hawiga district, in Kirkuk governorate (2018). Market assessments and regular monitoring will be conducted to ensure that cash is the most appropriate intervention in each target location.

Needs

Since the successful operations to retake previously occupied areas in 2017, there has been a reduction in displacements and impact on conflict affected populations. However, there has been an increase in instability, with isolated attacks on local authorities, checkpoints, and key infrastructure. Additionally, IDPs have not returned to their areas of origin at the rate previously anticipated by the government and humanitarian community (IOM DTM); therefore there remain high needs among protracted IDPs in and out of camps, in addition to the needs of returnees, host communities and stayees. These issues require a continued capacity to respond to emergency needs which arise as a result of these attacks or other potential shocks, in addition to continuous programming targeting highly vulnerable populations. Conflict affected populations remain vulnerable with little to no access to social protection services, and vulnerabilities to protection violations remain high. Those highly vulnerable men, women, boys, girls, older persons, persons with disabilities or chronic conditions, and those facing human rights violations will need priority support in order to bridge a period from their displacement to a more sustenance solution. Medair will provide MPCA to highly vulnerable IDPs, returnees, and other vulnerable groups with accessible, functioning markets as well as the possibility for safe cash delivery. According to the 2018 HRP, cash has been consistently preferred by beneficiaries for its flexibility in meeting immediate needs. The proposed locations are those that Medair has an existing presence in Health, WASH and Shelter/NFI sectors. Though the proposed project will target multiple locations throughout Ninewa, Kirkuk and Salah al Din governorates, many of the anticipated MPCA needs will likely be similar across locations and programming will be designed to fit within Cash Working Group (CWG) guidelines and standards, such as the SMEB. Medair will undertake community-specific needs assessments throughout the upcoming year in order to precisely identify needs and inform targeted project design. Medair plans to be responsive to changing needs and priority locations as the context develops. Providing MPCA will not be a standalone activity. Medair will integrate these activities with ongoing Health, WASH and Shelter/NFI programming in order to provide a more comprehensive approach to maximize the impact of its activities, and provide more cohesive multi-sectoral needs assessments to all appropriate clusters. Medair will seek links with protection actors, particularly HLP actors to respond appropriately. Though it is anticipated that returns will continue to areas of origin, there are also IDPs, many in protracted displacement, that may not be able to return due to safety and security reasons, and that are in need of continued support and assistance. In Kirkuk and Salah al-Din governorates, returnees are continuing to occur; however there remain IDPs with restricted movements due to insecurity and ethnic tensions. Medair will look into expanding MPCA activities in Kirkuk governorate as needs arise. In Ninewa governorate, in Sinjar, Mosul and Telafar districts returnees continue to be slow, and those that have returned to their areas of origin are met with limited access to basic services and livelihoods options. In Sinjar district, some families are returning to their areas of origin in the villages by the Syrian border or in the north of Sinjar. The need of assistance remains high for the returnee groups with lack of basic services and destruction to home in areas of origin (Medair RNA in Ain Hessan, Fao, Bir Jary, and Ayadyea on September- November 2018, REACH Intention survey August 2018). Returns the south of Sinjar mountain are slower due to the ongoing decontamination activities, high levels of destruction and ongoing insecurity and ethnic tensions. If needed, Medair will consider decreasing MPCA activities in Ninewa due to the high number of agencies working in the area, in order to scale up in Kirkuk governorate.In the upcoming year, Medair’s response plan will focus on post-emergency phase interventions and will implement a targeted approach including the usage of the socio-economic vulnerability tools that are familiar to Medair staff (CWG tool) and other tools under development (New taskforce CWG tool, etc.).Households will be targeted and assisted based on socio-economic vulnerability using standard CWG tools.

Protection Risk Analysis

In areas of intervention, protection risks will be analyzed and mitigation measures will be developed to incorporate into project design and implementation. Overall protection issues may include unequal access to benefits and services due to gender, age, ethnicity, and religion (among others) and other associated discrimination by authorities and armed groups. Beneficiaries may have difficulties with identification documents, forced evictions and returns, camp closures, lack of services, restricted movements and insecurity. MPCA intervention-specific issues may include the risk of increasing social tensions between beneficiaries and non-recipients of services and distributions. Additionally, MPCA distributions are not sustainable, and are typically delivered via single/multi distribution in times of need. There may also be discrepancies between the targeted beneficiaries and the most vulnerable in the target community, with some of the most vulnerable remaining unreached. Additionally, there may be risks associated with distribution of cash, and risk to staff involved in project implementation.

Mitigation Measures

All activities will take into consideration protection concerns ensuring a “Do No Harm” approach. Protection considerations surrounding employment of female staff include ability to travel (cultural impediments) safely. Staff will be supported as necessary to ensure safety at work. Beneficiary vulnerability criteria and selection will be coordinated with other actors and will be monitored to mitigate against abuse, including the potential use of the vulnerability assessment tool being developed by the MPCA CWG. To mitigate against unequal access to services and to ensure that the most vulnerable are targeted through the intervention, Medair will work closely with local authorities to identify those most in need while cross-checking against surveys and focus group discussions. Distributions will be conducted via safe money transfer modalities such as smart cards, money transfer agents, and mobile money transfer depending on the context in order to reduce risk of fraud and theft. Where applicable, female target groups will be consulted prior to intervention and attention given to location of meetings and scheduling to ensure equal opportunities for participation. Background checks will be undertaken to screen staff for acceptability for household visits, and monitoring activities will measure household sentiments of safety during any distributions and interactions with project staff. Where possible field teams conducting surveys will consist of female and male members, to ensure participation and leadership opportunities for women, also to have the ability to reach all beneficiaries (overcoming cultural barriers such as the limitation in women and men interaction). Partner field staff, suppliers, and contractors working on the project sign Social Accountability Minimum Standards. Additionally, Medair adheres to PSEA guidelines and regulations, and all staff receive this information on induction. Medair has a zero tolerance policy on sexual abuse and related issues, also communicated to all staff.

How is your project accountable to affected population

During project design, the team will work with the communities to get their feedback in needs assessments and vulnerability assessments. Throughout the assessment, implementation and monitoring and evaluation phase, as part of its complaint response mechanism Medair monitors a hotline that beneficiaries can call in to express complaints and feedback. The team listens to each message responds accordingly. If a complaint is severe, this issue will be elevated to senior management, and if necessary Medair will involve external actors to respond appropriately. At distributions there will be a feedback mechanism in the form of a box that beneficiaries can put complaints and other feedback into. The team will review each one and respond accordingly. Medair works hard to ensure beneficiaries are responded to and feel valued and heard in the process. Where possible field teams will consist of female and male members, to ensure participation and leadership opportunities for women, also enabling Medair to reach all target group beneficiaries. Households will be consulted prior to intervention as to their specific needs to ensure a tailored response. Focus group discussions will ensure community participation prior to community type interventions. Post distribution monitoring surveys will be conducted to evaluate project satisfaction and gain feedback on outcomes. Where applicable local councils and community leaders will be consulted for their advice/approval prior to project start. From initial assessments to beneficiary selection stages and evaluation, beneficiaries will be provided information on how to contact Medair's complaint hotline, provide feedback, and issue complaints. Medair also includes complaint/feedback boxes in distribution communities. Medair’s internal logging system for complaints and feedback allows follow up by senior managers if issue are not tracked or dealt with in a timely manner. Please see the Medair Accountability to Affected Populations document for additional information.
