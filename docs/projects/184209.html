Project Summary

Estimated that 6,058,603 people (38 per cent) in the severe category” lack access to safe water and proper sanitation services and are highly vulnerable to AWD and other water related conditions.
ack
of adequate WASH services to the population is a main cause of spread of Acute Diarrheal Diseases (ADD),
leading to malnutrition in the country and contributing to increasing the existing stunting rate of 25.3% in
children below 5 years of age. It is also a cause of relapse (ADD) when treated patients and children
returning to their communities where safe water and sanitation coverages are limited.According to JMCNA, 53% of the IDPs face challenges in terms of access to WASH services in Somalia (53% as highest pertaining to access to soap and handwashing facilities). Furthermore, issues related to access to WASH
facilities for vulnerable groups including persons with disabilities, elderly, women, boys and girls is an
ongoing challenge. It requires availability of reliable data, extensive consultations and community engagement on planning and design while considering unique needs of each group.
To meet the most urgent identified WASH needs in the targeted areas WRRS proposes the following WASH Humanitarian Response Plan activities.
WRRS proposes to provide Emergency water trucking/Voucher with temporary distribution systems for 3 months to 2,00 vulnerable IDPs HHs in Baidioa IDPs(600HH), In Kismayo (600 HH), and 400HH in Afmadow district, Household Water Treatment and Safe Storage (HWTSS) for 3 months for households targeted with emergency water trucking/voucher, regular Water quality monitoring, sampling, testing, analyzing and HHWT/chlorination treatment for 40 community water points across the targeted locations. Extension/Rehabilitation of 6 pipelines for IDPs in Baidioa, Kismayo, Dhobley and construction of water kiosk each 1.5km long and to ensure access to sanitation facilities will Construct 250 Emergency, desludgeable, gender sensitive, disaster responsive communal latrines with hand washing facilities and are lockable from inside in the following location and will dislodge &rehabilitate 250 desludgeable, disaster responsive communal latrines with hand wash facilities in Baidioa IDP and Kismayo IDPs, conduct 48 vector and pest control/solid waste collection campaigns in Baidioa (16), Afmadow(8 campaigns) and Dhobley(8 campaigns), Kismayo(16 campaigns). will Provide 100 sanitation kits including dustbins in IDP camps of Baidioa, Kismayo, Dhobley and Afmadow. The project will Provide hygiene kits to 4000 households (1000HH in Baidioa, 1000HH in Kismayo, 500HH in Diinsoor, 500HH in Afmadow and 500HH in Badhadhe). The kits contents are 2 water containers @20l capacity, 5 pcs of Bar soaps(@800 grams), 1 pact MHKs & 100 HHWT accompanied by hygiene promotion activities and community capacity building activitiec
The project will also strengthen the capacity of the in line ministries within the regional and Federal Government of Somalia States, community leaders and water committees/technicians for better coordination with government authorities and communities and sustainability of the constructed/ rehabilitated WASH infrastructures. In addition, the project will nurture community participation in good hygiene practices through training and community sensitization campaigns.
The project is aligned with protection mainstreaming to ensure safety and dignity of affected populations without causing harm. Affected populations and all relevant stakeholders including men, women, boys, girls, elderly and people with disability will be involved in decision making throughout the project activities/cycle. Furthermore, the project will be a part of an WRRS integrated emergency response program and resilience building, where possible, efforts will be made to integrate, health, nutrition, shelter, CCCM and protection activities.
WRRS will be working closely with the cluster during the response.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

WRRS will ensure an inclusive feedback mechanism that will include all beneficiaries will be put in place which will help to evaluate selection criteria and overall services gaps. e. In all water collection points, a mechanism will be established that will give priority to those who cannot stand in line for long periods, such as older persons, persons with disability, unaccompanied children, pregnant and lactating women. Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance or violence during/after registration or distribution: a. Water locations will be put in central, visible places and not more than 500 meters away from settlements. Location will be decided following consultations with women and girls. This will help to protect them from risks of all forms of GBV when accessing services. Separate toilet facilities with internal locks and secondary enclosures will be constructed for women and men with clear labels. In locations where threats are anticipated, frequency of hygiene kits distribution will be modified and door-to-door distribution considered, to avoid sexual abuse and extortion while carrying kits. Close coordination with Shelter/NFI partners to put solar light installations at WASH community centers to deter any risk of violence against women and girls e. Distributions of water will be done during day light to allow beneficiaries who often women and girls reach home on time.WRRS will ensure the risks to beneficiaries are reduced as humanly possible by ensuring strong community consultative, engagement approaches from bottom up at all steps of project implementation. WRRS will give particular attention will be given to people with disabilities, elderly people, minority groups, unaccompanied children, women and children in designing, positioning, beneficiaries selection process to ensure their views, opinions are listen to and incoporated in project designing and planning. In addition WRRS will Coordinate with civil society specializing in working with persons with impaired mobility or disabilities to help identify such individuals and use them as a resource to improve service delivery, train staff and for the referral of cases. WRRS will ensure WASH staff and committees should be representative of all groups within the community(e.g. gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, disability) and all staff and members should be given protection mainstreaming training.As part of transparent accountability frame work actions will WRRS will be transparent with the affected populations by providing them with accessible and timely information on selection criteria for targeted assistance, organisational procedures and processes that affect them. WRRS has qualified M&E and protection staffs are experience in Setting up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions. WRRS will ensure provision of information to the affected communities through various communication means to reach the broader community and to account for different literacy levels eg. door-to-door, poster, radio,social media, use of pictograms).This will be done cross -sectoral community awareness on how WRRs works during project implementation to ensure community knowledge on their rights, participation project ownership and sustainability of humanitarian interventions offered,promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Eg. place feedback surveys in hygiene kits, feedback box at WASH facilities, phone lines. To mitigate issue of risks of explosive and hazard contamination in project targeted area WRRS will ensure WASH facilities and distribution sites are far from military settlements, arm conflict bases sites, do distribution where possible at household level or cash modalities to reduce risk exposure to targeted benficiaries.
