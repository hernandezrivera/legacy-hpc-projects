Project Summary

Adequate and quality nutrition is a child’s basic human right, with well-nourished children having a better chance of survival and longer-term growth, development, health and productivity. WFP Sudan aims to prevent and treat malnutrition by providing specialized nutritious foods to children under five as well as pregnant and lactating women and girls (PLWG) among refugee, IDP and vulnerable host communities. Geographical targeting of WFP’s treatment program as part of comprehensive Community Based Management of Acute Malnutrition programming is informed by a comprehensive analysis of national nutrition survey results (such as S3M II) and Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) results with a special focus on vulnerable, shock-affected populations.
The proposed response from WFP is in line with the Nutrition Sector Strategy and with the Multi-Year Humanitarian Response Strategy of Sudan. Through this project, WFP will be responding to the nutritional needs of targeted beneficiaries under Outcomes identified by nutrition sector with particular focus on Do No Harm and Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) principles, WFP Sudan strives to enhance protection mainstreaming.

1 - Needs

The nutrition landscape in Sudan is characterized by persistent elevated levels of acute malnutrition and micronutrient deficiencies. The political instability and deterioration of the economic situation are impacting the nutritional status of most population in targeted affected areas particularly among children under-fives, pregnant and lactating women. An estimated 3 million children under five are suffering from wasting: of these approximately 2.4 million with moderate acute malnutrition (MAM). The urgency of implementing this rapid response plan is driven by indicators of extremely high levels of malnutrition, mortality, food security and livelihoods in many localities and states entire Sudan. Based on S3M II which has been implemented by FMOH at the end of 2018, GAM prevalence among population is 13.6%. Although this result is under the emergency thresholds, the need for continuation of the services is due to high prevalence rates at specific localities and states which currently targeted under WFP operations. WFP approach is aligning with nutrition sector priorities to promote a nutrition specific and sensitive intervention to reduce maternal and child under-nutrition and lifesaving response to vulnerable communities. WFP provide nutrition services using Community-based Nutrition Integrated Platform which composed of Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programme aiming at provision of Ready to Use Supplementary Food (RUSF) for treatment of MAM, and community mobilization by Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs).Food-based prevention of malnutrition (FBPM) activity supports PLWGs, and children aged 6-23 months —targeting the first 1,000 days of life— critical to prevent episodes of moderate or severe wasting as well as contributing in reduction of stunting. FBPM is a targeted approach to identify vulnerable population on the verge of falling into actual malnutrition and providing them with nutritious food till they are outside the critical zone and recovered, thus addressing an immediate outcome of food insecurity amongst those most vulnerable. Furthermore, caretakers of these infants and youngest children, as well as the breastfeeding mothers and mothers-to-be are provided with health, nutrition and hygiene information, specialized nutritious foods and access to services.Following a rapid onset shock, in areas where community-based management of severe acute malnutrition (CMAM) is not in place, there exists greater risks for acutely displaced children falling into malnutrition. To address this, WFP implements an emergency blanket supplementary feeding programme (e-BSFP) for all PLWs, and children aged 6-59 months in crisis-affected populations, complementing general food distribution. The e-BSFP is triggered only for new displaced / crisis affected populations that have been assessed as being food insecure and requiring General Food Assistance.WFP provides micronutrient powder (MNP) to complementary food prepared at home in order to improve the micronutrient profile of food given to young children during the weaning period so as to tackle the hidden hunger. The goal of the intervention is to prevent micronutrient deficiencies amongst children 6-59 months and improve feeding practices through Social and Behaviour Change Communication (SBCC).

2 - Activities/Outputs

WFP will coordinate with key nutrition stakeholders to meet the sector strategy using different methodologies and address the critical needs of malnourished individuals through the below suggested platforms: a. Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programme (TSFP) provides treatment for children aged 6-59 months with moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) and acutely malnourished pregnant and lactating women (PLW). TSFP also admits children that suffered from Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) discharged from Outpatient Therapeutic Programs (OTP) for follow up in order to prevent relapse. Active case finding of acutely malnourished children and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) takes place at community level. Community health workers or community mobilizers carry out active case finding, identify and refer acutely malnourished children to the TSFP or OTP center according to their nutritional status. The objectives of TSFP are to:• To prevent morbidity and mortality associated with acute malnutrition• To prevent MAM cases from developing SAM • To prevent relapse of individuals who have been treated for SAM • To improve birth outcomes and maternal nutritional statusb. Food-based prevention of acute malnutrition is a prevention programme aimed at mitigating the malnutrition and the associated risks related to mortality, morbidity for overall child development. The programme is implemented in Community-based Nutrition Integrated Programme (CNIP), in conjunction with the treatment of MAM in high burden areas. This activity will be implemented in an integrated manner with other complementary interventions. The objectives of the programme are:• To prevent acute malnutrition in children 6-23 months and pregnant & lactating women (PLW)• To allow early detection of MAM cases and refer them for treatment.• To improve maternal nutrition with protein-energy supplementation in pregnancy and in support of exclusive breastfeeding.• To increase access and participation in child survival interventions such as de-worming, vitamin A supplementation, and routine immunization or vaccination campaigns. 1. To contribute in reduction of stunting. c. Home fortification is the addition of micronutrient powder (MNP) onto complementary food prepared at home in order to improve the micronutrient content of food given to young children during the weaning period. MNP is added to the food after preparation and just before feeding the child. The goal of the intervention is to prevent micronutrient deficiencies amongst children 6-59 months and improve feeding practices through Social and Behaviour Change Communication (SBCC). The product used consists of sachets of 1 gram of MNP (Vitamino) used daily to fortify complementary food at home. The MNP formulation contains 15 micronutrients as per the WHO/WFP/UNICEF joint statement. The specific objectives are: • Increase intake of micronutrients in children aged 6 to 59 months. • Bring about positive change in feeding practices and food hygiene especially for infant and young children.d. Blanket Feeding Programme is one of WFP’s nutrition emergency response which aims at mitigating the impact of shocks on the nutritional status of vulnerable groups (children from 6-59 months, Pregnant and Lactating Women) in emergency situation. The objectives are • Prevention of acute malnutrition through provision of Specialized Nutrition Foods to children from 6- 59 months and PLW• Increase access to treatment services of acute malnutrition• Improve knowledge among caretakers/mothers of targeted children on the benefits of prevention and treatment programmes.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

In general WFP’s approach is Accountability to Affected Populations composes of: A) Project design and planning phase: With a particular focus on Do No Harm and Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) principles. According to the international standards TSFP implemented in the areas where GAM rates above 10% or between 5-9 with aggravating factors, in Sudan many localities are above these range based on S3MII conducted in 2018. using the GAM prevalence’s at state and/or locality levels as entry points, WFP’s targeting approach for the nutrition interventions, mainly the Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programme (TSFP), which is been guided by those key aspects: 1. All localities with GAM > 10% to be assisted with the Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programme. 2. All localities with GAM < 10% and food insecurity of 50% or higher to be assisted with the Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programme. 3. All localities with GAM < 10% and food insecurity less than 50% to be transitioned to the government with assistance provided for the implementation of non-food-based approaches such as the NIPP circles. 4. Other elements such as SAM prevalence can be considered when making prioritization of localities. WFP is ensuring Social, and Behaviour Change Communication (SBCC) and Nutrition Sensitive approaches are mainstreamed across all nutrition activities. Special focus is placed on strengthening community outreach and radio use as these are the most preferred methods of communication by the beneficiaries. Targeting location(s) Targeting The selection at location done in coordination with MOH and other relevant partners on ground, the key issues to consider for site selection are priorities by nutrition sector based on need and vulnerability, existing or planned SAM services, presence of SMOH or other qualified CPs, CBOs and willingness of community leaders on the project idea. WFP targeting a total of 2,740,146 of beneficiaries for both treatment and prevention programmes, with 19,365 nutrition supplies (RUSF=13,343 MT, MNPs=28 MT, Plumpy Doz = 5,994 MT for both FBPM and e-BSFP). WFP monitoring its activities using the programme performance for individual outcomes (cured, defaulters and mortality) as well as monitoring visits on regular basis to different nutrition sites to ensure beneficiaries are getting right food right time. WFP planned to integrate MAM services within OTP at areas where applicable to ensure smooth referral and continuum of care, the current overlapping is 67% and expected to increase this portion to 85% by end of 2022. WFP has internal system to follow up supplies starting from procurement up to final distribution point and also applying prepositioning exercise for the remote areas and during rainy season for the inaccessible areas.
