Project Summary

This project is divided into 2 parts : the first where the project will respond to health care gaps in rural Dar'a by rehabilitating and equipping primary healthcare facilities, as well as providing basic healthcare services and training professional healthcare staff. This is a two-pronged approach making healthcare more accessible as well as of a higher standard.
Dorcas will repair and equip 3 PHCs in Daraa (2 PHCs level B/ 1 PHC level C). The Ministry of Health were consulted in this process and have provided an indicative list of needs and works to make the facilities safe, sanitary, and functioning once again. As part of the re-equipping process, clinic staff will be trained by suppliers on how to use and maintain the equipment. Dorcas will also engage in training of PHC staff. Based on MoH requirements, training programs will be standardized as much as possible while also being tailored to each HF’s needs. Training will focus on relevant topics linked to key service gaps in the current health system such as RH/GBV and integrated management of childhood illness (IMNCI) and any other health problem that is relevant. Once the facility is up and running, and staff are fully trained, Dorcas will ensure that patients are seen, treated and referred, and receive proper follow-up. Dorcas will also engage its MHPSS team to work specifically on the issue of mental health, providing mental healthcare services to the population in need within the catchment area of the facility.
This project falls in line with Dorcas’ ongoing work in South Syria, where a current projects endeavors to attain the same objectives within underserved areas. The project will draw on Dorcas’ vast experience in the sector, region, and lessons learned from previous projects. This project will also keeping in line with Dorcas’ approach of using social work methodology through much of its programming, and ensuring that gender and protection are not only mainstreamed, but also centralized in project planning design, implementation, and evaluation.
The 2d part that will provide support to PHCs in Aleppo Governorate Dorcas aims to give continuity and expand the health services to support the affected population and to respond to the essential and basic needs of the population by the provision of health services in 5 health centers; Support exists out of medicine and equipment supplies.
In addition to Dorcas and it is partner experience in operating a mobile clinic , Dorcas jointly with it is partner will operate a fully equipped mobile clinic in Idleb Governorate that provides consultations, medicines. Protection will also be mainstreamed through early detections and referrals pathways in all interventions.

4 - Needs

According to the 2021 HNO, today, 13.4 million people in Syria are in need of humanitarian assistance - a 21% increase compared to 2020 - with needs increasingly being exacerbated by economic decline. The decade-long crisis has inflicted immense suffering on the civilian population who have experienced massive violations of human rights law. For the past 2 years , this has been coupled with a global pandemic the COVID-19 pandemic has amplified the needs and has brought the need of accessible and quality healthcare to the forefront. Vital infrastructure has been destroyed, resulting in a lack primary and secondary health facilities. The quality of health care has been further affected by the deterioration in the functionality of medical equipment due to the lack of spare parts and maintenance shortages of drugs and medical supplies. Medical staff have deliberately been targeted, resulting in severe human resource shortages.In October 2021 & December 2021 , Dorcas conducted MSNA in Aleppo and a rapid assessments in both Aleppo and Idleb and found that healthcare is one of their top 3 needs along with the food. In Aleppo, it is apparent that there is a main gap in availability of medicine, regular availability of painkillers (18%) and antibiotics 18%, 15% of respondents have expressed the lack of availability of drugs to treat chronic diseases. For Idleb, 76% of respondents to this rapid assessment revealed that they faced difficulties when trying to access health services

5 - Implementation of activities

In Daraa, Dorcas will repair 3 PHCs. The MoH has provided an indicative lists of rehabilitation requirements, and works to make the facilities safe and sanitary. Repairs include: rehabilitation of doors/windows in facilities; roof waterproofing; repair of walls, floors, roofs , repairing fencing; painting and re-wiring electrical connections; removing and demolishing works for damaged materials and existing debris inside and outside the HF. Other possible works include WASH improvements and protective measures such as partitioning rooms or establish privacy for services such as breastfeeding counseling and ensuring secure comprehensive access for people living with disability. Dorcas will work closely with the Health Sector and will provide oversight to engineering firm to ensure that rehabilitation standards comply with WASH and protection standards. Before any work can take place, Dorcas will first need to get permission to work within the area and conduct the activities.In order to repair the centers, Dorcas will follow a multi-step process: signing contracts with engineering firm, signing contract supervising engineer, assessment of PHC, completion of BoQ, selection of contractor..Provision of health equipment and supplies and personal protective equipment. As for Aleppo, Dorcas will provide medicine and medical equipment to PHCs as well for the mobile clinic in Idleb in addition to capacity building, trainings and monitoring of the health staff in all areas.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

While planning and carrying out activities, special attention will be paid to ensure that gender, age and diversity are mainstreamed throughout the project. Dorcas maintains a gender- and conflict-sensitive perspective in designing activities, and will identify beneficiaries in a participatory approach through: consultations with beneficiaries, internal discussions and capacity building of staff. Cases referred will also be checked . Dorcas will also ensure that healthcare services provided at PHCs, including mental health services, are gender and age appropriate. This means that specific health care services will be provided for mothers and children. All Dorcas staff will be trained on the internal Code of Conduct and asked to sign it and be compliant to it in addition to the CP and PSEA policies. Beneficiary data will be collected and segregated by age, sex and gender whenever possible. Male and female protection staff will be involved throughout the project cycle to ensure that urgent protection issues are addressed and to provide beneficiaries the opportunity to raise any concerns. Dorcas has a strong experience in delivering protection programs and its team is well trained on Case Management SOPs and minimum standards.Dorcas' interventions will be guided by the non-discrimination principle to ensure direct access to services for the most vulnerable groups that will be identified through rigorous assessment.Internal mechanisms to report misconduct will be established


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Dorcas / partners staff are well trained and continuously monitored, Policies and procedures in addition to a complaint mechanism with complaint box, hotline, flyers, pamphlets, etc.. are in place and to ensure that the beneficiaries are not exposed to any risk and in order to handle any complaint confidently. Dorcas/partners are well-known & trusted; individuals and families understand that Dorcas has the ability to provide a multitude of protection services, as well as many humanitarian services like health and others. Working in conversation with the community, Dorcas develops many proposals and activities based on information received from its feedback systems and the needs of the community expressed by those coming into the centers. Dorcas’ two-way beneficiary feedback system was established to ensure that beneficiary populations have a voice in the planning, activity implementation, and evaluation of the project. Feedback and suggestions are listened to as part of the protection risk monitoring and subsequently included in the implementation. This also includes a process to confidentially handle allegations of sexual exploitation and abuse in an effort to ensure that beneficiaries are protected against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Dorcas’ long term presence within the community also ensures that the most vulnerable, such as the elderly, children, PWD, and widows are reached through its services
