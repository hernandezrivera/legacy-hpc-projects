Project Summary

To contribute to improved living conditions and to sustain the lives of those most affected by the crisis in NW Syria through the rehabilitation of 200 unfinished buildings/apartments for host IDP families in urban areas; the installation of 50 "Better Shelters" for off-camp IDPs. The selection of IDPS will be based on the targeting of the most vulnerable households, with an emphasis on those with specific vulnerabilities including persons with disabilities, those with elderly adults, female-headed households, and households where income is generated through child labour, or there is a high risk of early marriage for young girls.
The project will also aim to distribute 2,000 winterized NFI kits including olive pomace stoves and 1 month’s supply of olive pomace for heating in the coldest winter months

4 - Needs

Shelter needs in Syria are widespread and acute resulting in significant living standard consequences and affecting physical and mental well-being and protection of affected populations.Overall shelter severity has increased across Syria, with a corresponding increase in People in Need (PiN) from 2019 by 20 per cent to 5.65 million people. Of this,3.8 million (69 percent) are in areas of severity 4 or higher. Geographically,these areas are concentrated in Idlib and Northern AleppoSince December 2019, the intensification of the conflict between opposition groups and groups supporting the Syrian government have led to the displacement of 831,920 IDPs from opposition controlled areas. 167,822 of these IDPs (20% of the total) have relocated to the 4 Turkish controlled districts (Afrin, Azaz, Al Bab and Jarabulus) in the North of Aleppo Province.The huge scale of the displacement has exceeded the remaining available shelter in these areas, leaving the most vulnerable families without adequate shelter and vulnerable to the cold winter weather, lack of privacy and additional protection risks. 81% of these IDPs are women and children. (170,881 women; 240, 519 boys; 260,193 girls). These women and children are particularly vulnerable to increased protection risks from inadequate shelter when they are displaced. Many of these IDPs have already been displaced from other parts of Syria exhausted their savings.Due to the protracted nature of the crises and the lack of a political solution to the conflict, these IDPs need longer term shelter solutions in semi-urban areas where they can access education, health care, markets and employment. Concern and partners are trying to support IDPs in off camp locations rather than support expansion of the camps in order to reduce dependence on humanitarian aid and reduce the time needed for families to become self resilient in their new location.

5 - Implementation of activities

In response to the influx of IDPs and acute shelter needs in Turkish controlled districts of Northern Aleppo, Concern will work with partners to implement the following 3 shelter activities to provide shelter for IDPs who are staying outside of the camps. 1. Distribution of 2,000 winterized NFI kits including olive pomace stove and 1 month’s supply of olive pomace for heating in the coldest winter months [Activity 1 – Sub-Activity 1.1.1 & Sub-Activity 1.1.2](1.1.1 Provision of non-fuel core and essential NFIs/1.1.2 Provision of fuel for cooking purposes)[Activity 2 – Sub-Activity 1.2.1](1.2.1 Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs including winterization, fuel, and summarization/) 2. Rehabilitation of 200 unfinished buildings/apartments to host IDP families in unfinished buildings in urban areas. (Activity: 1.4.2 Upgrading of unfinished buildings) 3. Installation of 50 "Better Shelters" for off camps IDPs The selection of IDPs for these shelter activities will be based on targeting the most vulnerable households, especially recently displaced IDPs and households with specific vulnerabilities including: female headed households, those on dependency rations, persons with disabilities elderly adults, those children and adolescents at risk of having to work to generate some form of income for their families (child labor), and young girls at risk of early marriage. Concern partners will do 100% verification IDP households referred by community leaders and local councils. (1.3.1 Installation of tents)

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Abuse of Authority by distribution actors (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion)Concern and partners have a code of conduct which controls the expected behavior of the project staff and restricts any abuse of their authority in relation to distributions. A complaints response mechanism exists in both organizations and will be shared with programme participants so that they can communicate with the senior management of Concern and partners and report any breach of the code of conduct. 2. Risk of physical harm at distribution points (security, safety)Concern will work with partners to document the standard operation procedure for managing NFI distributions in order to ensure that vulnerable IDP families are able to safely receive the NFI packages and that the rights and dignity of IDPs are respected throughout the process.3. Crowded living conditions and lack of adequate sanitation facilities can lead to increase in GBV and sexual exploitation. For all 3 shelter activities, Concern and partners plan to focus on the off camp IDPs in order to avoid creating pull factors towards camps where protection risks are more likely to occur. For the rehabiliation of unfinished buildings, Concern will ensure that each apartment has functioning WASH facilities and that sphere standards in terms of the amount of square metres per household and the number of people per toilet are respected.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Risk from IED's in the programme area (being in the wrong place at the wrong time)Concern will support partners to ensure that the partners security plans are appropriate for the level of security risk and that partners have appropriate duty of care policies to protect their staff in the event of any security incident. 2. Eviction of IDPs from rehabilitated unfinished shelters by the building owner. Concern and partners will sign agreements with the building owners before starting rehabilitation of unfinished buildings which state that the IDP families will be allowed to live in the rehabilitated apartment rent free for one year after the rehabilitation is completed. These agreements will be co-signed by a local committee who will be responsible to ensure that the building owners honor these agreements. Concern partners and the local committees will receive HLP (housing land and property) training to ensure that the buildings being rehabilitated are owned by the person signing the MOU and to mitigate the risk of IDPs being evicted during the project. 3. Access restrictions to the geographic area due to active fighting and/or shifting of front lines would prevent the humanitarian actors to provide timely emergency relief to the affected population.This project has been designed to cover all 4 districts of Northern Aleppo which is currently controlled by Turkish backed forces. In the event that the conflict restricts access to one of these districts then the project has been designed with enough flexibility to move the project activities and project team to another district.
