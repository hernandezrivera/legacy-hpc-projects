Project Summary

The protracted conflict in Eastern Ukraine is entering its sixth year, and multiple attempts at ceasefires having yielded little respite from ongoing violence. Conflict has compounded pre-existing economic and social vulnerabilities which challenge the ability of the population – particularly the most vulnerable groups, including women – to achieve economic prosperity or even economic security.
In GCA, the food security situation has overall not improved over the past 2 years; food insecurity levels remain high compared to pre-2014. Within 5km of the line of contact (LoC), the number of food insecure people ranges seasonally from 30,000 to 50,000, noting widespread erosion of coping strategies during and following winter.
The humanitarian community, including ACTED, has been engaged in a large-scale effort to meet the basic needs of the most vulnerable, particularly in the 5km area where 49% of HHs have received international assistance in the past 12 months (REACH 2018). On the one hand, it is essential to reach those who have not yet received assistance yet are in need. On the other hand, noting that the conflict continues to protract, it is also essential to assist some of most vulnerable in restoring their subsistence capacity, and decrease conflict-affected communities’ dependence on humanitarian aid through livelihood activities.
ACTED therefore proposes an intervention to increase the coping capacity of some of the most vulnerable conflict-affected populations within 5km of the LoC in Eastern Ukraine.
First, ACTED will address levels of food insecurity among conflict-affected populations just following winter, when household resources are depleted; and during the next winter, when food insecurity levels are observed to be highest. The project thus aims to reduce food insecurity of the most vulnerable conflict-affected individuals within 5km of the LoC. Targeting settlements with access to functional markets, ACTED will provide unconditional cash transfers to 6,500 individuals at the value of 825UAH/person/month in 2 distributions: 3 months of assistance in spring 2020 to 2,400 individuals (in 1 tranche), and 3 months of assistance in winter 2020 to 4,100 individuals (in 1 tranche).
Second, ACTED will distribute subsistence grants to support the most vulnerable HHs restore their livelihoods, targeting specific gender and age categories based on vulnerability analysis (women, heads of households who are unemployed but ineligible for pensions, single mothers…). ACTED will target the most vulnerable within 5km of the LoC: where this caseload is today almost exclusively targeted under short-term (e.g. cash) assistance programmes, ACTED will look to restore livelihoods through cash/voucher/in-kind grants (depending on beneficiary preferences and access to markets/inputs in given locations). 80 beneficiaries will be supported thus with grants valued at 1,200 USD. Based on the results of a livelihoods assessment conducted by ACTED in September 2018, ACTED expects that approx. 30% of its livelihood grant beneficiaries will be over 50 years old (noting pre-pensioners’ particular economic vulnerability given the economic situation in Donbas, but also cognizant of older people’s possible limited ability to engage in livelihoods activities: ACTED will encourage home based production for this group, and ensure at assessment stage that selected beneficiaries have the capacity to participate in the programme).
Ultimately, by increasing access to food and restoring livelihoods, ACTED will increase coping capacity.

1 - Needs

As the conflict continues to protract and economic recovery lags, the food security situation remains urgent in Eastern Ukraine. Provision of and access to basic services and markets have been disrupted by and especially close to the line of contact (LoC), and Ukraine is one of the most mine-contaminated areas globally (HALO Trust), which hinders farming methods on which communities in Eastern Ukraine may otherwise subsist. Food consumption remains low compared to pre-crisis, noting that food security needs are highest and most acute closest to the LoC. Within 5km, GCA side, 14% of HHs are food insecure, vs. 11% within 20km, and 9% beyond 20km. Food insecurity is also driven by negative coping strategies (REACH 2018-19). There is widespread erosion of coping strategies during and following winter, when food insecurity is highest: 21% of HHs demonstrate poor Food Consumption Scores in winter (REACH 2018) – vs. 16% in summer 2018 and 14% in summer 2017. Socio-economic vulnerability is high close to the LoC: average income in the 5km area is UAH2000 (REACH, 2018), equivalent to 61% of actual subsistence level (2018 average was UAH3263 (Ministry of Social Policy)). Average income for ACTED’s food insecure beneficiaries is significantly lower at UAH1305 and falls to UAH684 for HHs with unemployed pre-pensioners (40% and 21% of actual subsistence level, respectively): pre-pensioners have the greatest proportion of HHs with moderate or severe food insecurity (REACH 2018). HHs that are single-headed, have a disabled member, are woman-headed, have a member with chronic illness, or are headed by a person above 60 are also more likely to experience food insecurity (REACH, 2018). Single-headed HHs are 40% more likely to be moderately or severely food insecure than the general population. Women have fewer economic and livelihood opportunities, and are more likely to be dependent on men breadwinners; compounded vulnerability of single women-headed HHs causes further strain on food consumption and socio-economic opportunities. Based on REACH data and ACTED program experience, out of the general population within 5km of the LoC: - Pensioner and pre-pensioner headed HHs are most likely to be food insecure (18% and 16%); - Women headed HHs are 2.5 times more likely to be food insecure than men-headed HHs (20%); - HHs without children and single-member HHs tend to be more food insecure (17% and 21%). - Single-headed HHs are 2.5 times more likely to be food insecure than dual-headed HHs (20%). - Over 20% of HHs with a member with a disability are food insecure. With private investment unlikely in a still hot conflict area, employment and/or entrepreneurship opportunities are limited. Incomes are unlikely to rise, given economic downturn: unemployment doubled in Donetsk and nearly tripled in Luhansk from 2013-17 (WFP, 2018). As those living close to the LoC are older (41%), 59% female, and often retired or unemployed over the age of 50, they struggle to find livelihoods opportunities. Moreover, entrepreneurship grant schemes often impose age limits on beneficiary selection and are unlikely to target areas close to the LoC due to the higher risk of business failure. Most development and recovery programmes are unlikely to target older rural and small town populations within 5km. Yet, according to ACTED’s livelihoods assessment (September 2018), residents of areas close to the LoC, including the most vulnerable (elderly women living alone, unemployed single mothers), show resilience despite an increasing gap between incomes and actual subsistence level. Most HHs, women in particular, have always engaged in small trade, handicraft, vegetable/fruit gardens or value addition even if the conflict depleted their ability/capital to do so. With support, they could restore their livelihoods, enhance their economic security and reduce their dependency on aid.

2 - Activities & outcomes

Outcomes 1-Access to food is increased for the most vulnerable 2-Livelihoods are restored for most vulnerable Cash-for-food: ACTED will distribute unconditional cash transfers (UCTs) to 6,500 vulnerable people living within 5km of the LoC. Selection will be based on ACTED’s methodology, using HH status (looking at gender, age and other vulnerability indicators e.g. disability) to target a HH level food security assessment. Potential beneficiaries are assessed using a CARI based selection methodology adapted to the Ukrainian context by the FSLC with support from ACTED. Based on needs (see above) , ACTED will target: - HHs of elderly, esp. single isolated elderly (60+); - HHs with members with disabilities/chronically ill; - Women headed HHs; - HHs with unemployed members of pre-pension age (esp. 50-59); - HHs with multiple children, esp. headed by single parents; - HHs of/with IDPs. UCTs are an appropriate modality as markets in GCA are functional and communities along the LoC generally have access to food products. ACTED-led market monitoring under ACCESS has shown stable upwards price trends, seasonal patterns in availability and prices, and minor fluctuations in availability of goods. In 2018, 97% of ACTED’s beneficiary HHs spent 55% of the assistance on food on average, resulting in 82% of beneficiary HHs improving their FCS. UCTs are also a beneficiary preference, as over 70% of HHs within 5km of the LoC prefer cash (REACH 2019), and 98% of ACTED’s 2018 beneficiaries were satisfied with a cash modality. Feedback from groups facing additional barriers in accessing markets indicates that they prefer cash over in-kind: they have support networks/can pre-order from shops to ensure that goods are available; etc. ACTED works through Financial Service Providers (FSPs) covering all locations within 5km, incl. through home delivery. ACTED can thus implement in hard-to-reach isolated areas and reach beneficiaries with limited mobility/domestic care burden, esp. vulnerable elderly. ACTED will distribute in line with FSLC guidelines: UAH 825/person/month(until revision). Beneficiaries will receive 3 months of assistance in early spring, and 3 months in the beginning of winter. Food expenditure patterns are similar among women and men. Among ACTED 2018-19 food security beneficiaries, women headed HHs spent on average 51% of the assistance on food, vs. 53% for men headed HHs. HHs with elderly spent less (47%) suggesting other high needs (health). HHs of pre-pensioners spent the most of any category (58%). As traditional gender roles can still create additional obstacles for women and girls, ACTED will measure satisfaction of women in men headed HHs with HH expenditure. Livelihoods grants: ACTED will support 80 most vulnerable HHs, targeting women/single headed HHs with dependents, and HHs headed by vulnerable older population/HHs of unemployed including pre-pensioners aged 50-59, based on needs (see above): careful consideration will be given to the latter’s ability to engage in livelihoods activities. Grants will be provided through cash (using FSPs), vouchers, or in-kind support. To mitigate gender/protection concerns, for grants to HHs headed by men, ACTED will monitor the satisfaction of women HH members with spending/investment decisions through a dedicated indicator. Community input will be sought in community meetings and by distributing materials to describe the program, disseminate information on ACTED’s complaints and response mechanism (CRM), and ask for referrals. Local stakeholders will verify preliminary lists for UCT beneficiaries as per vulnerability profiles targeted under the project. For livelihoods grants, ACTED will follow-up regularly with beneficiaries to ensure ability to use the grant efficiently (e.g. through referrals to social protection or complementary capacity-building programs/services) and evaluate activity effectiveness post-intervention.
