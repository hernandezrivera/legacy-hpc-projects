Project Summary

Despite significant progress during the last two years in providing safe and systematic access to learning opportunities, more than 30 per cent of Rohingya children and youth aged 3-24 years old still require access to education. Since last year a whole new crop of children entering ECD age will have difficulty to access any education due to COVID-19 restriction on top of land scarcity.
Due the COVID-19 outbreak, Children are mostly affected in current situations and they have little scope to learn. Few initiatives like caregivers lead education in home has introduced but need more guidelines and orientation how the children will engage themselves in education.
Sectors reports shows the gaps a large number of early grade of student out of schooling both in Rohingya community and Host Community.
Out of schooling student the major percentage is a girl. So, SKUS has designed the project prioritizing the girls. To ensure girls schooling the project has kept parents awareness session specially for girls admission.
According to the education sector gaps analysis, there is a gaps for the access to the learning facilities for the children aged 3-5 at camp 5, 8w, 14 where is no land for established learning center due to scarcity of the land. So, only community based home based learning system can cover the gaps with the quality of learning and contribute child development.
The project aims to established of 70 Home based Community facilities Established and the delivery of quality early Learning and child development through ECD to 2100 Rohingya and Host community children (3-4 years old). Among the 70 ECD center, 50 will established at camp 4, 8W and 14 in Rohingya Camp and 20 will be established in host community in Haladia Palong, Jaliapalong and Ratna Palong.
SKUS wish to increase the number of children living with disabilities about 2% in all its facilities, especially the ECD intervention to work on a more inclusive education culture from an early age. SKUS will conduct a survey to ensure their involvement through awareness session, enrollment and referrals. SKUS will established the centres for easy access to special need children. SKUS will provide training to all facilitators and staffs on inclusion.
As a gender sensitive and child friendly organization SKUS will focus on gender sensitive activities like: PSEA training, awareness session for gender mainstreaming, training on child rights, child protection, child safe guarding etc. SKUS will be performing awareness-raising and training activities for parents, caregivers, teachers and community members on child rights, the need for protection, positive coping mechanism and the importance of education, including for girls.
SKUS has the contingency plan to help the young children in emergency.
SKUS will established good professional relations with the local administration and government as well as others service provider at Rohingya Camp and Host Community and engaged the all stakeholders in its all activities.
For better implantation and ensure quality, SKUS will follow and collaborate with the sectors and sector partners like: education, WASH, SMS. SKUS will ensure related govt. officials involvement all through the project life time.
In addition to the education, SKUS already build good relations Protection (CP and GBV Subsector) , Health sector and Wash sector, MHPSS working group, ECD working group and will continuous for smooth project operation. As per Child Protection quality benchmark, SKUS gained highest mark through peer to peer assessment for its tremendous CP work at camp 17 and 16.
In project life time SKUS will conduct a large number of training for capacity building of staff. Therefore they will be able to implement the project smoothly. It will help them to their career development.

1 - Identified Needs

The Rohingya are an ethnic group from Myanmar. They lived mainly in the northern part of the My-anmar Rakhine State, which borders on Bangladesh. Almost all Rohingya are Sunni Muslims. For decades, Rohingya refugees have fled to Bangladesh because of political persecution. Since August 2017, an estimated 720,849 Rohingya refugees have arrived in Bangladesh’s Cox’s Bazar District from Myanmar, fleeing a military crackdown in Myanmar’s Rakhine state that has been characterised by widespread reports of violence against civilians and crimes against humanity. According to the Education Need Assessment report 2019, the approximately 485,000 Rohingya children now residing in Cox’s Bazar are almost solely reliant on the national and international nongovernmental organisations (NGOs) for the provision of education services. Since the start of the response, sector partners’ efforts to provide education services to refugees have been constrained by a number of factors, including a lack of space for facilities in camps that are already heavily congested, as well government restrictions preventing any formal or non-formal education programmes being offered to the Rohingya.The access and learning environment For children aged 3-5, 64% of girls and 64% boys were reported as attending learning centres at least four days per week in the 30 days prior to assessment, with attendance rates across camps varying from a low of 44% in camps 2W and 2E to 87% in Camp 22. Study findings suggest that the attendance gap for this age group is linked both to facility coverage, and to perceptions among caregivers that very young children should not be attending education: only 76% of assessed facilities offered services for the 3-5 age group, compared to the 99% of assessed facilities providing services for children aged 6-14.
