Project Summary

In 2022, UNHCR will target affected populations including; newly displaced populations, IDPs currently living in protracted situations, refugee returnees, and affected host communities with adequate shelter and basic household items. This will be done through the provision of Non-Food Items (NFI kits), Emergency Shelters, and where feasible transitional and durable shelter solutions through different modalities including in-kind and through cash.
The Somalia Joint Multi-Cluster Needs Assessment (JMCNA) 2021, reports the living standard gap of Shelter and NFIs to be at 84%. According to the same assessment, the most commonly reported priority need of more than half of the affected population is still shelter or housing due to poor shelter conditions, lack of space, poverty and lack of security of tenure. The critical indicators that were considered in the calculation of the severity of the POCs in the sector were access to safety of enclosures, technical performance of shelters and the access to vital household NFIs. In addition to these indicators, others that were also considered were living conditions including overcrowding, functional domestic living space, occupancy status, and security of tenure.
In response to the identified needs in the HNO 2022, UNHCR plans to distribute NFI Kits to 56,800 households in kind/cash, provide emergency shelters to 20,000 households, transitional shelters to 800 households and permanent shelters to 600 households. The project will target 340,800 individuals with NFI kits, 120,000 individuals with emergency shelters, 4,800 individuals with transitional shelters and 3,600 individuals with permanent shelters. NFI, emergency shelters and transitional shelter assistance will be provided in cash or in kind and the response modality will depend on the availability of items in the local market, access to the locations, user preference and associated protection risks. Permanent shelters will be provided in kind through implementing partners.
As per the HNO, it is reported that a total of 237 IDP sites hosting 593,058 individuals were congested with associated health risks and fire outbreaks. These overcrowded sites are not planned, leading to the increased risk of fire outbreaks, spread of diseases including COVID 19, relatively higher GBV related incidences and flooding due to the absence of adequate drainage systems. In response to this, through an ongoing inter-cluster initiative between the Shelter Cluster and the CCCM Cluster, UNHCR will provide settlement planning and infrastructure support in 49,500 individuals in sites to be identified by the shelter cluster basing on the level of risk and feasibility of the project. This infrastructure support will be done to ensure site decongestion through settlement extensions and implementation of basic site planning principles where feasible.
UNHCR will carry out Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) and ensure the utilization of the Complaints, Response and Feedback Mechanism for each project. The PDM exercise will be conducted 4-6 weeks after NFI and ESK distribution to analyze the impact of the projects, level of beneficiary satisfaction and establish the lessons learnt for future programming.
UNHCR will target a total of 469,200 vulnerable individuals of the 3.6 million persons identified as in need of shelter assistance. Of the targeted individuals, 84,456 women, 65,688 men, 218,849 children, 18,768 elderly and 93,840 disabled individuals. UNHCR will also incorporate 93,840 vulnerable individuals from the host community and 375,360 vulnerable POCs as part of its target group. The targeting will occur based on UNHCR and Shelter Cluster assessments for those most in need and highly vulnerable. Activities will be implemented in Bari, Banadir, Bay, Lower Juba, Gedo, Mudug, Hiraan, Nugaal, Middle Shabelle, Lower Shabelle and Woqooyi Galbeed regions.
UNHCR will implement and monitor the activities through the support of Implementing Partners.

1. - Projects activities

1. Provision of 56,800 NFI kits. To enable access to basic household items and provide a sense of dignity, NFI kits will be provided to affected population such as vulnerable and disaster affected IDPs and host communities and refugee returnees. Depending on the household composition and size, each household will be provided with one NFI kit comprising of 1 plastic sheet, 3 blankets, 2 jerrycans, 1 solar lamp, 2 sleeping mats, 1 mosquito net, 1 kitchen set. In case the assistance is provided in cash, each household will receive $100 through mobile transfer to procure NFI items from the local markets. 2. Distribution of 20,000 Shelter kits. Shelter kits will be provided to affected population such as vulnerable and disaster affected IDPs and host communities and refugee returnees. 120,000 vulnerable individuals will receive Emergency Shelter Kit (ESK) in kind or where cash assistance is recommended, UNHCR will provide cash ($237 per HH) which they will utilize to procure the Shelter Kits from the local markets. Partner technical staff will ensure that shelters are built according to the agreed standards through regular inspection visits to the project sites. 3. Construction of 800 transitional shelters. Using an owner driven approach, where feasible, 800 transitional shelters will be constructed to support the reintegration of returnees, and targeting affected population such as vulnerable and disaster affected IDPs and host communities with long term security of tenure. 4. Construction of 600 Permanent SheltersIn line with the operational strategic direction of implementing more durable solutions, UNHCR will construct 600 Permanent Shelters in reintegration sites with guaranteed security of tenure as a pre-requisite. This will be done in collaboration with the government, local authorities and other key stakeholders to ensure that the projects are in alignment with existing national and cluster plans and that the beneficiaries will have guaranteed access to other basic services. 5. Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM), Complaints Response and Feedback Mechanism (CRFM) and Market Assessment. PDM exercise will be conducted for all activities within 4-6 weeks of implementation of the proposed interventions for all activities to analyze the impact of the project, the beneficiaries’ level of satisfaction, feasibility of cash-based response and establish the lessons learnt for future programming. The beneficiaries will be made fully aware of the beneficiary selection process, CRFM and the scope of the project through community mobilization and sensitization. 6. Infrastructure support: Decongestion of existing sites through an existing inter-cluster initiative with the CCCM cluster, targeting 49,500 individuals will be done by undertaking basic site planning and expanding the sites where feasible. This will also help in minimizing the spread of diseases including COVID 19, mitigate risk of fire outbreak, reduce GBV related incidences and flooding due to the absence of adequate drainage system. UNHCR will use an AGD approach.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

In locations with functional markets where there is sufficient availability of construction materials and basic household items, beneficiaries prefer cash. UNHCR will assess the protection risks and will use cash where the protection risks are minimal. According to PDM reports carried out by UNHCR Partners on NFI distribution in particular the most recent in Jowhar, when asked about their preferred modality of assistance, the majority (97%) of the respondents indicated cash assistance with only 3% in favour of in-kind support. On the other hand the PDM report on NFI distribution in Hudur, when asked about their preferred modality of assistance, the majority (47.3%) of the respondents preferred in-kind distribution to cash modalities. UNHCR uses Cash-based interventions to provide protection, assistance and services to the most vulnerable. UNHCR has strong experience and existing mechanism, operational standard operating procedures and strategies in planning, designing, implementing and monitoring Cash-Based Intervention. Cash-based interventions as modality of protection delivery, provides dignity, flexibility and choice for persons of concern, as they allow people to decide and address their own needs. Beneficiaries are able to purchase items of their choice, size and quantity. If a house needs of repair, the beneficiary can purchase the required material and undertake the repair. Cash-based interventions help to increase protection outcomes by enhancing; protection mainstreaming by ensuring the safe and dignified delivery and contributing to protection benefits, in areas such as child protection and SGBV and reducing negative coping mechanisms by meeting basic needs. Moreover, cash-based interventions improve cost efficiency in programming. Particularly in the current situation of COVID-19 pandemic, Cash-based interventions enables and enhances the efficiency of program delivery. In addition to this, cash-based interventions have multiplier effects, directly benefiting the local economy as affected populations purchase goods and services in the local market but also promote peaceful coexistence and social cohesion with host communities.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

UNHCR Somalia continues to have a framework agreement with Standard Chartered Bank (SCB) since 2014 to implement CBI interventions which is executed through Al Amal Bank inside Somalia. The project will be implemented through this agreement and UNHCR’s CBI SOPs and the Shelter Cluster CBIs for Shelter SOPs. UNHCR and partners have successfully implemented cash-based responses in Baidoa, Dollow, Kismayo etc. where markets are functional. This is further strengthened and confirmed by the Joint Market Monitoring (JMM) of Shelter Cluster in August 2020 in twelve urban locations across the country, indicating that markets are functional. Shelter Cluster with WASH Cluster and REACH conduct joint market monitoring every 3 months and that considers market functionality.
