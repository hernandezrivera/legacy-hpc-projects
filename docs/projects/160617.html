Project Summary

Initiative for Peace Communication Association (IPCA), a proactive national leading child protection in emergency programming is undertaking a child protection project with an aim to provide structured and non-structured static and mobile case management and psychosocial services to vulnerable girls and boys including children with special needs from the IDPs, Host communities and Returnees population in Kajo-keji, Morobo, Lainya and Juba County in Central Equatoria State.
This project key activities include, i) establishment of six (6) CFS in Morobo and Kajo-keji County, ii) provision of static and mobile community-based case management and psychosocial support services to vulnerable girls and boys, iii) provision of dignity kits to school going adolescent girls and child survivors of SGB, iv) establishment and strengthening of two community-based child protection networks at County level, v) establishment of ten child protection committees at the Payam level, vi) conduct data collection on grave violations of child rights, establishment and strengthening of community-based child protection referral pathways in Kajo-keji, Morobo, Lainya and Juba Counties, vii) conduct sexual reproductive health education to adolescent girls & boys.
This project expects to provide vulnerable with comprehensive case management and psychosocial support services and mitigate risks for children in Central Equatoria State.
The project will collaborate with the Ministry of Gender, Child and Social Welfare, UNICEF, TdH, TiTi Foundation and SSUHA health pooled funded project for clinical case management at hospital and health centers. This project duration is 12 months at a cost of USD$300,000.

1 - Needs

The protracted violent conflict in Lainya, Morobo, Juba and Kajo-keji County in Central Equatoria State continues to negatively impact on the protection environment for children and their families.Displacement and lack of critical social services for children means a compromised childhood for many children in Central Equatoria State. An Inter-Agency report for Central Equatoria State presents urgent needs for child protection lifesaving services for survivors of rape, sexual assault, child labor, CP-GBV, Children Associated with Armed Forces and Groups (CAAFAGs), Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC), psychological distress, physical assault, forced child marriage or psychological/emotional abuse.The needs assessment report also presented need to expand awareness on explosive remnants, sexual reproductive education and outreach activities on child rights and data collection on grave violation of child rights based on The Child Act 2008 and UN Security Council resolution 1612 and 1888.Based on this emergency child protection needs, IPCA is responding with child protection prevention and emergency lifesaving services which include comprehensive Case management, structured and non-structured psychosocial support packages, establishment and strengthening of community based child protection structures, FTR, reintegration and rehabilitation of released CAFAAGs, expansion of awareness on explosive remnants, sexual reproductive education, creation of space for children with focus on girls and children with special needs, child labor, recruitment of children into armed forces, safety Audit, referrals, post rape services, forceful marriages and other CP-GBV services in Juba, Lainya, Morobo and Kajo-keji counties in Central Equatoria State.

2 - Activities

1. IPCA will provide timely case management services to identified vulnerable children, case management support target to benefit 300 vulnerable children, the services will include CBI, PFA, PSS per UNICEF pack, and Caring for Child Survivors of Sexual Violence, referrals and child-friendly components of MH GAP-HIG for MHPSS. 2. IPCA will establish protection incidents documentation, monitoring and reporting system in to inform protection response in Central Equatoria State. 3. Conduct awareness raising on mine risk education at schools, community levels and at the CFS centers. The awareness sessions to reach 6000 children in Juba, Lainya, Morobo and Kajo-keji Counties. 4. Provision of static and mobile community-based psychosocial support services to 2600 survivors of child GBV, sexual assault, physical assault, child labor and psychological distress to 6 locations including (general PSS/therapeutic recreation) level 2, (targeted PSS) level 3 and referrals for level 4 to specialized PSS service offered by qualified psychologist/ social worker (1 per site) and 2 community based PSS worker as per UNICEF PSS Guidelines. 5. Establishment of six (6) CFS, 3 in Morobo and 3 in Kajo-keji County to benefit 600 vulnerable children, the CFS will be situated in Payam with high needs, life skills, integrated PSS, recreational and educational activities including sexual reproductive health education and dignity kits will be provided to adolescence girls in the CFS. 6. IPCA will provide legal aid services to victims and survivors of GBV in Lainya, Morobo, Juba and Kajo-keji County. 7. Conduct awareness raising on child soldiers, child labor, sexual exploitation and abuse and other child rights abuses based on the South Sudan child Act 2008 and UN Security Council resolution 1612 and 1888. The target is 6000 children to 6000 children, women and men. 8. IPCA will develop a comprehensive GBV prevention campaign to support all project activities and further raise awareness amongst target communities with regards to the established SGBV services. 9. Strengthening two community-based child protection networks: IPCA will strengthen two existing/establish new ones to play critical roles in community mobilization, engagement and awareness interventions including coordination of child protection services, referrals and monitoring of child rights at Payam levels. 10. IPCA will conduct training of community CP actors in CPiE Minimum Standards, including comprehensive case management, PFA, PSS (UNICEF pack), and Caring for Child Survivors of Sexual Violence and child-friendly components of mhGAP-HIG for MHPSS in emergencies in Morobo and Kajo-keji Counties 11. Conduct participatory monitoring and evaluation of the project together with partners, local authorities and beneficiaries to enhance accountability. 12. Provision of Dignity kits to school going adolescent girls and child survivors of SGBV, the dignity kits will be distributed to vulnerable girls in school and at CFS. The dignity kits target to benefit 3000 adolescent girls to reinforce girl child education. 13. Conduct data collection on grave violation of child rights based on the South Sudan child Act 2008 and UN Security Council resolution 1612 and 1888. 14. Conduct sexual reproductive health education to adolescent girls & boys from the age of 10 to 18 years, in schools, community and CFS centers. 15. IPCA will strengthen and coordinate referral pathway in Kajo keji, Lainya and Morobo Counties to ensure timely coordination of child protection response in all payams of Kajo-keji, Lainya and Morbo.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

This project needs assessment, design and implementation plan is based international accepted humanitarian principles including “Do No Harm” This project is community based and community driven with project management committees at the community levels to establish an engagement platform to interact with beneficiaries on issues of rights, access and quality of the services to collect beneficiaries’ views on issues of rights, access and performance of the project. The project will utilize the community project committees and the project monthly stakeholders meetings to share views on rights and performance of the project to inform the project programming and other future interventions. This project will establish community help and complaints desks, phone-lines and email for beneficiaries to share information on issues of rights and other concerns that affect their participation in the project implementation and issues that block them from benefiting from the services of the project. To augment this the project will design monitoring tools with clear benchmarks and indicators to measure impact on the beneficiaries.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

This project needs assessment, planning and design had incorporated needs of the IDPs population in this project locations by sex, age and vulnerability, so that each group of beneficiaries get assistance based on their needs. The key IDPs concerns identified in the project needs assessment are distance from schools, health facilities, water points, cultural norms, religious norms, mobility for people with disability and sexual exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries seeking for employment and humanitarian assistance. This project will pay close attention to needs of each group and will also coordinate with other partners to refer other issues which could not be immediately addressed by this project.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

This project will partner with National and state Ministries of Gender, Child and Social Welfare across the project locations, UNICEF, Save the Children, Child protection Sub-clusters and protection partners in this project locations to buy-in ownership of the project for sustainability. This project will establish community structures (community protection committees) and connect them to other partners for additional support. This project will work with identified local partners throughout the project to develop/strengthen self-sustaining structures and partnerships at the community level to allow for successful exist.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

This project falls in the center of South Sudan Child Act, South Sudan Development Plan. This project also incorporated Kampala convention on IDPs. This project will follow accepted humanitarian principles ensure that vulnerable population from the IDPs, returnees and host communities equally benefit from this project. This project will extensively incorporate right based protection to protection status of the communities in a conflict sensitive approach.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

This project will conduct beneficiaries’ safety audits to inform the project activity programming. It will establish community structures (community project management committees) to keep in close engagement with beneficiaries on issues of safety and the way forward. This project will engage local authorities and other non-state actors in the project monitoring to engage them on issues of beneficiaries’ safety in the project locations. This project will also establish beneficiaries help & complaints desks, phone-lines and email for beneficiaries to share information on protection concerns and others issues that affect their participation in the project implementation and issues that block them from benefiting from the services of the project.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Throughout the proposed project, the project will seek local buy-in and ownership of all activities to ensure sustainability. This project will establish community structures (community protection committees) and build their technical capacity and connect them to under partners and State Ministry Gender, child and Social Welfare to build direct coordination of child protection programming at the community levels. By building their technical capacity, the project will continue to develop and strengthen local child protection actors that can continue to undertake community child protection initiatives at the County and community levels. This project will work with identified local partners throughout the project to develop/strengthen self-sustaining structures and partnerships at the community level to allow for successful handover after the formal conclusion of project activities. In addition to sustainability at the local level through local ownership, this project will nurture partnerships to serve as advocacy platform for support and funding by other donors and local authorities.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

The project needs assessment captured broad project risks including security access, presence of explosive objects, gender inequality, sexual exploitation and abuse, influence of authorities, and violence against women, girls and other vulnerable, when seeking for services like water, education, markets, health, farmlands and humanitarian aid. Such assessment is conducted to inform the project of the risks that beneficiaries with focus on women, girls and people with special needs will face in the project implementation. This project has designed risk mitigation strategies that address security access, presence of explosive objects, gender inequality, sexual exploitation and abuse, influence of authorities, and violence against women, girls and other vulnerable, when seeking for services like water, education, markets, health, farmlands and humanitarian aid. Above all, IPCA will utilize its project logical framework development expertise in simulating a comprehensive log frame that underlines its key components, including assumptions and risks to determine the most appropriate project risks to be mitigated.
