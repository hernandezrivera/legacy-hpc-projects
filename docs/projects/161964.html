Project Summary

Save the Children (SC) aims to continue access for children affected by the displacement crisis to safe, protective, inclusive and age appropriate learning and development opportunities in the camps and host communities under Ukhia and Teknaf of Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh through establishing and maintaining 106 Learning Centres (LCs) incl. 3 in host communities, 140 Community Based Learning Facilities (known as home based learning spaces) and 10 Cross Sectoral Facilities (known as Girl Friendly Spaces) for informal early learning and basic education, enhancing knowledge of parents and caregivers on how to support their children’s learning, development, and well-being, and improving the quality of teaching through continuous teaching-related support. Inclusive aspects of programming including: gender-sensitive, child-sensitive, disability-sensitive as well as integration between sectors including child protection will be mainstreamed in all activities to ensure that different needs of girls and boys will responded to, and they will equitably benefit from the interventions. Special attention will be paid in capacity building of staff on key teaching and Social Emotional Learning (SEL) issues. All the learning facilities will also provided with gender segragated WASH points while physical infrastructure, materials and methodologies will be strengthened to ensure access and equity for children living with disabilities. Being a sector co-lead, SC will continue to play a key role in advocacy, increased quality and sustainability of education services for the displaced Rohingya in Bangladesh.

Needs

ACCESS TO EDUCATION: Top barriers to accessing education, identified by parents, include: distance to the learning facilities and safety for children aged 3-5; responsibilities in the household for children aged 6-14; lack of adequate learning materials, and inadequate WASH facilities. Furthermore, while the response as focused on children aged 3-14 years, 92% of adolescents and youth remain without access to learning opportunities. Rohingya adolescent girls are less likely to attend learning centers due to security concerns in the camps for adolescent girls, in addition to a lack of female bathing facilities which is leading to a lack of basic personal hygiene for adolescent girls (and women) some of whom who are inexperienced with Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) and therefore feel uncomfortable to leave the shelter during menstruation. Due to restriction of permanent or semi-permanent structure, temporary constructions are made for LCs with bamboos and tarpaulins, most LCs are already in need of renovation and this is predicted to incur significantly during the recurrent monsoon and cyclone season (April-October). Land scarcity is a key issue severely hampering the response, and requires innovative approaches like double stories facilities, and home based facilities to meet the vast education needs. QUALITY EDUCATION: While there has been some progress on standardizing curriculum for students, learning outcomes are unlikely to improve without the provision qualified and well trained teachers, adequate materials, and creation of the social and emotional learning (SEL) environment. Appropriate teaching learning materials, language of instruction, assessment system are also some of the constraints for ensuring quality education in the camps. The 2017 JRNA reported that only 21% of Rohingya teachers arriving after August 2017 had higher than secondary level education. So, the recruitment of sufficiently educated facilitators with proper understanding on child development remain a challenge in the Rohingya camps. The situation is further complicated by the low levels of teacher competencies with the majority either under-qualified or unqualified, with insufficient ability in basic child-centered methodologies including mastery of subject contents and SEL technics leading to poor learning outcomes. ALTERNATIVE LEARNING MODALITIES: Rohingya adolescent girls (aged 12-18 years) are restricted to access schools due to a lack of qualified female teachers and insecure routes to school. Additionally, 40% of parents have stated (JRP 2019) the learning materials are not appropriate. There is a need to strengthen engagement with parents, caregivers and communities in order to assess the ways in which learning materials and approaches can be more appropriate while also strengthening learning outcomes. There is also a need of providing additional classes for adolescent girls in cross-sectoral facilities (such as girls friendly spaces) in order to support girls' access to learning in line with social-cultural norms of segregation for unmarried girls after puberty. ADDITIONAL VULNERABILITIES: Children living in insecure, overcrowded spaces with one or no caregivers face risks related to SGBV and trafficking into prostitution, particularly for adolescent girls. Adolescent girls are particularly susceptible to early or forced marriage and sexual abuse, while boys face heightened risks of child labor, exploitation and human trafficking. Adolescent boys who are out of schools have increased probability of engaging them into anti-social activities, extremism, bullying, sexual harassment and soon. COMMUNITY ENGAGEMENT: In order to achieve high enrollment and attendance rates in education facilities, sessions with parents, caregivers and Community Education Committees (CEC) are needed to engage the communities in promoting education.

Activities or outputs

Since the beginning of response, SC has been providing safe, protective, inclusive and age appropriate learning for children from refugees and host communities. In 2020, SC will further consolidate its existing presence across Ukhia and Teknaf and will prioritize – a) the continuity of education in protective environments through existing 106 LCs, b) continuation of alternative education modalities for hard to reach children, especially early childhood learners and adolescent girls through 140 alternative spaces and 10 cross-sectoral facilities, c) continuous in-service teaching-related support training for 429 teachers, and d) support 10,196 parents and caregivers and 1,360 Community Education Committee (CEC) members to engage and participate in the education of children. This programming is strategically integrated within SC’s larger humanitarian response in order to provide children with access to essential services. Teachers will be trained to identify and refer children to SC's wider child protection, psychosocial support, health, and nutrition response activities. SC will be mainstreaming wider inclusion practices across all sectors on issues related to Gender Based Violence and Disability. Activities will lead to improved gender equitable, gender sensitive, GBV preventative, GBV responsive, and disability sensitive programming. To ensure gender equality, SC’s response will include targeting adolescent girls as they face a distinct set of barriers in accessing education. SC is also committed to ensuring that at least 50% of teachers are females and that 50% of teachers are from the Rohingya population. To mitigate the risk of reinforcing existing inequalities between women, men, girls and boys, or the risk of creating new gender inequalities, the initiative will seek to engage female and male stakeholders in a gender-responsive and culturally sensitive manner and ensure alignment with SC’s Child Safeguarding Policy and Do No Harm principle. SC will provide training on disaster management to CECs to raise awareness on natural disaster risks. SC will also communicate with the Education Sector its Emergency Preparedness Plan to ensure alignment to sector standards. SC co-leads the ISCG’s Education Sector with the Government of Bangladesh and UNICEF, and co-leads the Education Sector’s Standardization Working Group, and co-leads the Education Needs Assessment Working Group. SC will develop research and/or advocacy papers on key EiE issues in coordination with the Education Sector at Cox’s Bazar and national levels. SC with support from UNICEF and UNHCR initiated teachers’ professional development (TPD) framework, package and training of the Trainers to contribute to improving quality of teachers to deliver quality education in 2020. Rolling out of TPD in 2020 with innovative solutions of curriculum, language of institution, teaching -learning materials and appropriate assessment system will enable the system to improved learning outcomes of the children. Accommodating more children, transitioning from present 3 shift-LC to 2 shift-LC when the students promote to the upper grades, innovative solutions are required. SC plans to construct 6 two-story LCs as per GoB approval of semi-permanent structures, apart from the other solutions of community based/home based learning facilities for adolescents and early years’ children and peer learning opportunities.
