Project Summary

As detailed in the 2020 HRP, the shelter situation has continued to deteriorate due to the cumulative impact of the crisis and 5.65 million of people are still in need of shelter construction or rehabilitation. The bulk of the needs to which the action aims at responding have been assessed in the course of the implementation of TGH projects in Hama. TGH geographical coverage in the Governorate allowed gathering information from local stakeholders as well as through ﬁeld observations in various localities.
The hereby action aims at providing life-saving and life-protecting assistance to vulnerable Syrian in rural Hama, responding to acute needs in the Shelter sector. It aligns with the second sector objective which aims at “Reinforcing an enabling protection environment and community cohesion by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure”.
The targeted location i.e. Harbanifse sub-disctrict has been selected according to the needs identiﬁed as explained earlier and TGH signiﬁcant operational presence in the area that allows close monitoring of the humanitarian needs, identifying at local level the priorities. Indeed, TGH has been working in the area under three sectors (Education, protection and WASH) with an integrated and community based approach. This proximity with the community enables TGH to monitor and identify needs and will allow a further step towards TGH strategic objective by increasing the access to the most-in-need individuals facing speciﬁc shelter needs in emergency settings. Indeed, a full-time team of engineers is based in Hama in addition to the coordination team based in Damascus.
As a response to the needs identiﬁed in the area, this project includes the rehabilitation of damaged private house through the provision of emergency shelter kits (including doors and windows) to 170 vulnerable households private house (HH). This emergency shelter kit approach, suggested by the UNHCR, has already been discussed with local stakeholders especially with the Hama Governor who seems willing to facilitate the implementation of those kind of project in the Governorate. Noteworthy is that, although not fully developed under this sector speciﬁc project description, the targeted location has also been selected to be part of a more comprehensive response. Indeed, Harbanifse sub district has been and is currently targeted by TGH providing WASH, Education, and Protection lifesaving services. This synergy between TGH projects will aim at increasing the impact of the shelter activity described below. Protection mainstreaming will be ensured; guaranteeing a meaningful access and a sound accountability, further fostered protection risk analysis and mitigation measures in every step of the project. It is noteworthy that, TGH already initiated at Damascus level the procedures with Ministry of Local Administration and Environment (MoLAE) and with Hama Governorate and Municipality stakeholders to have the necessary authorizations for the project implementation. This is the first shelter project TGH will implement in Syria, It will aim at giving a comprehensive answer to the identified needs since TGH is already active in WASH, Education and child protection in the area. Those three first sectors are considered as life-saving in the current context of this area. Answering to shelter issue is part of TGH life sustaining strategy and would contribute to give an integrated answer to the identified needs as well as ensuring a complementarity in the service since TGH already rehabilitated water point and school in the targeted locality.

4 - Needs

Needs are dire in this rural underserved area which witnessed population returns and is prone to new ones. Rural Hama targeted localities have so far received little humanitarian assistance in comparison with the scale of needs as only 4 INGOs are currently working in this governorate. More particularly, Harbanifse sub-district is range 3 in the sector severity scale with 9,552 people in need of Shelter assistance. Doors and windows are critical to ensure a protective environment. This specific need was shared by the community in several occasions during TGH assessment. The population resilience remains very poor in a context of increased poverty, deteriorated economy worsened by sanctions, isolation from the international scene and Syrian pound depreciation. Vulnerable people, especially those living in rural areas are exhausted by the dead-end job market. This lack of access to basic services including safe and adequate shelter increases the vulnerability of households and forces the adoption of harmful coping mechanisms. The recent and current hostilities in Idleb and north East Syria i.e. intensive bombardment destroying available services in villages and cities, indicate that security situation can deteriorate further and lead to an increase of displacements and needs. In North Hama and Idleb, the offensive of Syrian Army Forces (SAF) from April 2019 led to massive displacement towards northern Idleb and Aleppo governorate (around 300.000 individuals as of end of December 2019). Although, no displacements from Idleb to Hama governorate have been reported in December, the probability of new arrivals in Hama remains high and will be closely monitored by TGH.

5 - Implementation of activities

Act-1.1: Support to sustainably repair/rehabilitate housing and related community/public infrastructure and facilities to owners/tenants/returnees/host families (can include: materials, cash, voucher, cash-for-work, labour, etc.)Act 1.1.1: Provision of emergency materials for damaged houses. TGH will distribute kits composed of windows and doors to 170 vulnerable HH. Missing doors and windows will be replaced, if possible, with the same type of material as the previously used materials. Other openings that will not be targeted for rehabilitation will be closed with plywood. The selection of beneficiaries will follow two components: -Beneficiaries vulnerability such as women-headed HH with or without children or families with dependent elderly.- House selection: TGH will ensure that the pre-selected beneficiaries’ damaged houses are in safe accessible area and access (or will access in a near future) minimum WASH services but also that they are structurally safe. The distribution modality (direct distribution; voucher etc.) will be chose furtherly after depending on feasibility. Activity 1.1.2: Evaluation and follow-up of input provision among beneficiariesAt the beginning of each project, TGH team conducts FGDs to explain the objectives of the project and its different steps. It avoids creating misunderstanding during the activities’ implementation and tensions within the communities. The process and beneficiary selection criteria will be clearly explained to the population. Once selected, TGH will involve the beneficiaries during the assessments to understand their material type needs.In addition, a PDM will be carried out a month after the distribution to assess the satisfaction of the beneficiaries. This survey will be carried out on a representative sample of the beneficiaries with the KoboCollect tool. This survey allows gathering beneficiaries’ recommendations to take into account to improve future interventions.

B) Risks of theft of assets and earnings. TGH complaint mechanism will allow to receive feedbacks and identifying response optionsC) Selection criteria is influenced by local authorities, may contribute to nepotism and risk of most vulnerable being left out. D) Lack of documents to prove property rights. Likelihood medium; Impact: highMitigation: Explosive hazard awareness will be delivered by TGH contractors, workers. No major security incident targeting TGH staff occurs. TGH authorization to operate in Syria remains granted. Syrian laws ruling humanitarian organizations remain favorable. Field access remains granted.
