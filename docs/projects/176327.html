Project Summary

Humanitarian needs in South Sudan in 2021 are mainly driven by the impacts of years of conflict including spikes in sub-national and community-level violence during 2020 resulted in more than 2,000 civilians killed and people displaced. On top of this, COVID-19 has had a devastating and multi-faceted socioeconomic impact on people, including closure of education institutions, severe economic contractions, spikes in prices of basic commodities, loss of livelihoods particularly in urban areas, increased protection risks and child marriage, and disrupted access to basic services. The already serious humanitarian situation has been compounded by severe flooding, affecting approximately 1 million people each year in 2019 and 2020 (UNMISS report June 2020). In this context, this project aims to improve access to education in emergencies for out of school young girls (above 14 years) by minimizing risk of contamination of COVID 19 virus in different counties of Upper Nile state and Pibor in Jonglei state. It has mainly three components; a) Training to TVET teachers on operating TVET following blended teaching-learning approach (face-to-face cum distance) – 40 teachers (1,500 TVET learners); b) 200 adolescent girls dropped out from school and victims of teenage/early pregnancy/marriage or at risk of joining armed forces receiving entrepreneurship training, functional literacy and tool kits to start business; and C) Organize Radio programme on education including TVET, Agriculture and livestock sector (5,400 learners with 50% female).
In total, 7,100 adolescent girls (45%) and boys (55%) will be benefited from this project in Upper Nile and Jonglei states.

1 - Needs

Impact of COVID 19 pandemic and four incidents of intensified conflict during the year 2020 has disrupted education by pro-long closure of schools and livelihoods. The closure of schools for long term resulted in increased risk of school dropouts, with more girls ending up victim of early marriage and pregnancy. The loss of assets has created incentives for people to engage in armed violence. Two consecutive years of unprecedented flooding have caused destruction of property, infrastructure, crops and displaced people and livestock. The government together with relevant partners prepared Education and TVET Response Plan during and post-COVID 19 situation that requires training to teachers to adopt new measures of response plan and alternative teaching-learning methods to minimize footprint of learners in a classroom and TVET centers for continuity of learning following the WHO COVID 19 infection control and prevention measures. Although there is no specific quantitative study conducted in Upper Nile State (UNS) and Pibor, Jonglei state after the COVID 19 pandemic, humanitarian needs generally seems to have increased across the states in 2020-2021. Ongoing inter-communal violence in Ulang and Nasir in south-eastern UNS led to displacement, food insecurity and protection concerns. Elsewhere within UNS and Pibor flooding, economic instability and the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic have caused a broad increase in humanitarian needs across the state including of education.According to the quarterly assessment conducted by REACH in 2020, access to education services fell dramatically in Malakal and other counties of Upper Nile State following the closure of schools due to the outbreak of the COVID-19 pandemic. By June, KIs from almost all assessed settlements (97%) reported that there was no access to education services and has since remained high. It is likely that many schools will struggle to reopen, given that schools are yet not prepared to improve COVID-19 preventive infrastructure and other facilities to adopt social distancing. The rate of return to school is also likely to be lower for girls due to the pro-longed closure of schools forcing someto join child labour or engage in unskilled economic activities, early marriage and pregnancy. According to the report of IPC Global Support unit- December 2020, tens of thousands of people are likely to face famine conditions in Jonglei state’s Pibor county, where many families are experiencing high levels of acute malnutrition and even death. Agriculture/livestock sector knowledge and skills to out of school youth can contribute to minimize food insecurity risk. According to HNO 2021, only 25 per cent of girls in South Sudan receive a secondary level education, 50% early marriage is common, with half of girls getting married before the age of 18, and the literacy rate for females (age 15+) is 19% compared to 35% for male (age 15+). Some 2.4 million children are out of school in 2020. The majority of them are girls. Children in rural areas and IDP, refugee and returnee children are most vulnerable. About 60 percent of IDPs and returnees live in settlements where no more than half of the children are attending primary education (UNESCO OOSCR 2018). For TVET schools and centers, majority of centers are over-crowded than the classroom / workshop capacity created challenges to maintain social distancing measures after announcement of re-opening of education institutions for candidate classes in September 2020 and at high risk of contracting COVID 19 virus. COVID 19 has also exposed girls to different forms of gender-based violence including forced marriage and early and unintended pregnancy including teenage pregnancies. Education needs remain high among South Sudanese spontaneous refugee returnees.

2 - Project activities

Output 1: Equip TVET teachers with blended teaching learning approach (combination of face-to-face and distance learning) to respond COVID 19 infection risk and other similar emergencies- Training to TVET teachers on operating TVET following blended teaching-learning approach (face-to-face cum distance) – 40 teachers (1,500 TVET learners; 40% female); and Output 2: Improve access to inclusive and protective lifesaving education (non-formal) for out of school and conflict-affected young girls, particularly those rescued and released from armed forces and armed groups- Establish temporary learning spaces for out of school girls or school dropouts between the age of 14-17 years, with focus on girls rescued from armed groups (10 TLC); - Provide life-skills, functional literacy, entrepreneurship training and business tool kits to 200 girls (14-17 years) rescued from armed groups and at risk of joining arm forces, and victims of early/teenage pregnancies due to school closures.- Establish referral mechanisms to link appropriate need-based services related to psycho-social support, back to formal learning in school, and enrollment in existing long-term TVET interventions; - Organize Radio programme on secondary level education including TVET, and agriculture and livestock sector targeting out of school and school dropouts between age of 14-17 years (5,400 learners with 50% female), focusing on refugee, IDPs and host community children and ensure access to connectivity including radio sets through referral mechanism.- Prepare and air through community radios advocacy and awareness raising radio jingle/message focusing on preventative measures for COVID 19 pandemic. - Develop audio-visual document on impact of programme at the community level.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

One of the interventions of the project is to provide life-skills information to the targeted beneficiaries. It includes awareness raising and protection information about PSEA. The radio awareness intervention on COVID 19 will also include PSEA related information.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Depending on types of disabilities, different tools will be used to ensure collecting feedback from individuals living with disabilities. For example, interview will be conducted with participants of visible disability and questionnaires will be used for the persons having hearing and speaking disability.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will mobilize existing community based radios and organizations, NGOs, faith based organizations, youth groups, community leaders to reach the targeted vulnerable population to provide information about the project. Likewise, feedback mechanism will be set following different methods for different group based on their literacy level and existing connectivity means such as an individual interview, FGD on periodic basis and live response from beneficiaries on radios. Also conducive environment will be provided to avoid any influence or pressure and receive fairfeedback from the beneficiaries. The project will collect information on persons with disabilities through secondary data and verify it collecting primary data following snowballing method at the local level to participate them in the programme. Individual interview and observations are the key tools among others used for collecting feedback from such target group. For teacher training, the project will develop tools to conduct pre and post evaluation of participants that will contribute to improve quality and management of training.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

In addition to the feedback mechanism mentioned under 7.4.1, a complaint box will be put in place at the each of temporary learning centers of the life-skills and functional literacy classes. All feedback received from participants through different means will be analyzed on periodic basis and addressed in consultation with the beneficiaries, government and the project implementation and management team. the project implementation strategy will be reviewed if needed to address the genuine feedback.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Often girls are reported abused on the way to/from school/TVET centers. The project aims to address practical as well as strategic gender needs such as promoting recruitment of female teacher, information and services on comprehensive sexuality education, psycho-social and mental health support through coordination and referral mechanism. There is an issue of early marriage of girls and being pregnant even before the age of 14. UNESCO will coordinate with protection cluster and other relevant clusters to collect information on local service providers and set-up referral mechanism. In case if the services are not available, the protection cluster Coordinator will be informed and seek resource mobilization to mitigate the gaps.
