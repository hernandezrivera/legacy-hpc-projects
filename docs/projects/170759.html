Project Summary

In line with CARE Lebanon´s Emergency Response Strategy, CARE will engage with experienced local partners to provide essential repairs to the homes of the most vulnerable households directly affected by the Beirut Blast, subject to minor and medium damage (window and doors blown out, or damaged sanitation facilities such as bathrooms, kitchens and toilets). Both categories, minor and medium damage, will be considered in this intervention.
CARE will prioritise women and girls and carry out all work with gender and protection mainstreamed. The repairs and rehab will be achieved through a combination of cash programming and technical support. Furthermore, CARE´s intervention will encompass restricted cash for shelter in the form of rent for those whose houses are seriously damaged and un-inhabitable, including protection referrals. This component will mitigate evictions and support those most at risk of Housing and Land Property Rights violations, linking to the NRCs legal support unit ICLA. Targeted groups include direct affected population by the Beirut Blast, including vulnerable families already impacted by the economic crisis, migrant workers, female-headed households, elderly and those with specific needs.
The shelter implementation modality also involves engaging local youths in carrying out the assessments repairs and rehabilitation under the supervision of engineers, through a cash for work modality directly managed by the partner. This intervention will benefit Beirut based vendors from being chosen suppliers for the project subsequently boosting the market. This approach will both address the urgent needs of the targeted beneficiaries, ensure that supplies and services are procured and delivered at local level, and create income generating activities which should boost an already devastated economy weighed down by hyperinflation and months of lockdown due to the coronavirus pandemic. It will thereafter ensure sustainability and long-term impact beyond the duration of the project.

Plan fields

1 - Needs

Assumed needs are repairs/rehabilitation for damage category 1 - 2 through cash or technical support (categories are defined by the UNHCR/the Shelter Working Group).

2 - Activities

-Shelter Rehabilitation of minor (180HH) and medium level damage (70HH) to homes for the most vulnerable, through local contractors or cash grants. Shelter sector partners will upgrade units in substandard residential/ non-residential buildings to minimum/ humanitarian standards (in accordance with shelter sector guidelines, SPHERE, UNHCR SoPs and other shelter standards). -Engage local youth and/or migrant and domestic workers to take part in repairs through cash for work (in-direct livelihoods benefits, enhance the youth's capacities and income opportunities). - Cash for shelter: Rent for displaced families (minimum 3 months for 80HH) followed up with referrals to CARE's shelter partners or external shelter/HLP rehabilitation support from other INGOs. (HLP referals for families at threat of eviction – link to NRC/ICLA or others for HLP support) CARE Lebanon is a core member of the Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) network and part of the Protection Working Group, as well as an active member in the Shelter, WASH, Livelihood, and Food Security Working Groups. CARE integrates gender and social inclusion throughout its programs in targeting vulnerable populations and ensuring their meaningful participation. As part of the assessment and throughout the entire response, CARE will focus on the specific impacts on women and girls who are often disproportionately affected in natural disasters. Pre-existing inequalities are likely to be magnified and exacerbated, and women and girls are at increased risk of many types of Sexual and Gender-Based Violence (SGBV). Their voices are often also the least heard when it comes to emergency needs and a say in the time of support they require. Recognizing the ways in which gender relations are organized helps to ensure the delivery of an effective response that meets everyone’s distinct needs and identifies the main gender issues in the affected areas, with a focus on the priority sectors of the humanitarian response. The project will promote women and girls’ participation in the leadership of the emergency response through inclusive recovery planning processes, facilitating women’s (and girls’ where appropriate) full participation in community roles and as equal members of community structures. Staff, volunteers, and partners will be trained by CARE´s PSEA advisors on CARE’s procedures and policies relating to whistleblowing, PSEA, fraud, misconduct, protection from harm, and GBV. At partner level, CARE will ensure that the partners’ capacities increase to be able to mainstream building back safer approach, disability, and PSEA. Customized support will be provided based on the findings of the Organizational Capacity Assessment conducted for the partners. Training on Humanitarian Principles, the Core Humanitarian Standard, and Do no harm is organized for all partners at the start of the collaboration. CARE will mentor its partners in the application of technical standards across the interventions.
