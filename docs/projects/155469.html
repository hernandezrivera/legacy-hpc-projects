Project Summary

Tribal districts of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa remain one of the most underdeveloped regions of Pakistan enduring decades of unrest, crises, poverty and underdevelopment. The region is marked by poor nutrition indicators, with around 20% global acute malnutrition rates (severe acute malnutrition of 5%) – far above the 15% emergency threshold, Tribal region qualifies for the emergency nutrition response. Additionally, across provinces and regions, Tribal Districts have the highest level of stunting (57.8%) with irreversible intergenerational negative health consequences and perceived low future productivity. The trends indicate an alarming rate of acute malnutrition, which may further deteriorate in the absence of nutrition specific and sensitive interventions to specifically address the needs of girls and boys under five years of age and pregnant/lactating women (PLW). Other issues include insufficient scale of vitamin-A and multi-micronutrient supplement distribution for girls, boys and PLW and unhealthy feeding practices within families, driven both by food insecurity, limited awareness and social norms.
According to latest estimates based on population census data for 2017 and prevalence data for wasting and stunting , an estimated 267,600 (20%) girls and boys are wasted (SAM= 65,000 and MAM=200,000), around 350,000 (57.8%) girls and boys are stunted while around 150,000 malnourished PLW are in need of nutrition services. The Nutrition cluster is planning to address around 40 per cent of the total needs in 2019, targeting 25,000 SAM girls/boys for therapeutic treatment/inpatient stabilization care and 80,275 MAM girls/boys and around 42,000 malnourished PLW for supplementary feeding.
Very high prevalence of immediate and underlying causes of under nutrition coupled with poor governance structure, fears of insecurity and unavailability of female care providers increases the risk of morbidity and mortality among girls and boys. The local social value, poor health seeking behaviour, lack of dedicated financial and human resources for nutrition and the gender biased environment further limit the access of women and children to essential care services, putting them at greater risk of malnutrition and associated morbidity and mortality in the targeted return areas. To treat the current case load and to avoid further deterioration of the malnutrition situation in these areas, there is a critical need for nutrition programs and other basic nutrition services to mitigate the risk.
Major nutrition related outputs from the Pakistan Transition Plan - Humanitarian (2019) includes establishment of (a) inpatient stabilization care services for treatment of sick acute malnourished girls and boys (b) outpatient therapeutic feeding services for severely acute malnourished girls and boys (c) targeted supplementary feeding services for moderate acute malnourished girls and boys and PLW (d) Skilled counseling support for mothers/caretakers on promotion of appropriate maternal, infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN) practices and (e) micronutrient supplementation for girls, boys, PLW and adolescent girls.
Objectives
Objective 1: Enhance access of target communities and ensure availability of gender responsive nutritional treatment services for severely acute malnourished girls and boys and targeted supplementary feeding services for moderately acute malnourished girls and boys and pregnant and lactating women.
Objective 2: Enhance access of mothers/caretakers in target communities to skilled counseling support for appropriate maternal, infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN) practices, early childhood stimulation, health and hygiene services – particularly focusing on 1,000 plus days and early years of child life.
Objective 3: Enhance access of girls and boys less than five years of age, adolescent girls and PLW to micronutrients from fortified foods, supplements and multi-micronutrient preparation for addressing micronutrient deficiencies.

Activities

1- Support establishment/operationalization of inpatient/outpatient nutritional treatment/ therapeutic feeding and targeted supplementary feeding services for acutely malnourished girls and boys less than five years of age and pregnant and lactating women. 2- Support inpatient/outpatient treatment of around 25,000 severely acute malnourished girls and boys less than five years of age and targeted supplementary feeding of around 80,275 girls and boys 6-59 months of age and around 42,000 pregnant and lactating women as per national protocols. Additional complementary activities Provide skilled counseling support at each nutrition sites and random communities on improved MIYCN including support for promotion of exclusive breastfeeding; appropriate complementary feeding and maternal nutrition. Distribute multi micronutrient powder for use by girls and boys (6-59 months of age) and multi micronutrient tablets and/or Iron Folic Acid for use by pregnant and lactating mothers and adolescent girls along with messages on hygiene promotion and use safe drinking water and sanitation. Support formation and capacity building of mother support groups comprising positive deviant mothers, grandmothers and PLW for promotion of appropriate MIYCN practices in their surrounding communities. Support formation and capacity building of voluntary male support groups for the promotion of improved MIYCN practices and identification/referrals of acutely malnourished individuals for registration in appropriate feeding programs. Train female and male HCPs (including program managers, practitioners, facility and community based health care providers and outreach workers/LWs) from the Department of Health and partner CSOs on emergency nutrition packages including CMAM, IYCF-E and NiE. The nutrition interventions will be implemented jointly by UNICEF, WFP and WHO in partnership with Department of Health Tribal Districts & CSO partners under the overall guidance of the Humanitarian Country Team (HCT) and Tribal Districts Nutrition Cluster adhering to national and internationally recommended minimum standard of care for a population affected by an emergency.The target communities will be involved in the provision of services (Local recruitment) and through formation and capacity building of mother/fathers’ support groups in the target communities. The project design includes delivery of the nutrition services package at health facility and community level through community based Lady Health Workers (LHWs) in target districts where available and Community Health Workers (CHWs) recruited through CSOs where there are no LHWs. LHWs/CHWs belongs to the same communities, are well versed with the local context and fully accountable to the local health care system and communities. Additionally, the Nutrition Assistants at each health facility level will be supporting formation of Mother Support Groups (MSGs) comprising 8-10 volunteer lactating mothers (positive deviant), pregnant ladies, grandmothers, local LHW, local school teachers and adolescent students. Members of the MSGs will be oriented and updated through monthly meetings by the Nutrition Assistants/Local Health Facility Care providers on community MIYCN counseling package. Counseling session on MIYCN through Nutrition Assistants, LHWs and MSGs entails group based and individual nutrition counseling based on positive deviance approach. The positive deviants are identified from the success cases with support of the communities and elaborated for other mothers to adopt similar behaviour and healthy nutrition practices. This leads to enhanced information on benefits of timely initiation and exclusive breastfeeding and improved dietary diversity for children and mothers. The fathers’/male support groups formed/oriented through outreach worker at each health facility level will support dissemination of information on how/where from to access nutrition services.
