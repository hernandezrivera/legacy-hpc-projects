Project Summary

The purpose of the project is to improve food security, economic and social well-being of vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host families in the target area.
The purpose of the project is to improve food security and, the economic and social well-being of vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host families in the target areas. To build the resilience of vulnerable households, NRC will complement food assistance with livelihood activities using a market-based approach that refers to local traders so to improve access to income and revive/strengthen the local economy. The intervention will be geared towards supporting the most vulnerable displacement-affected households and at the same time building their capacity in food production, food transformation and marketing.
NRC will identify project participants using household-level socio-economic vulnerability assessments. The assessments will prioritize the most vulnerable households, taking into consideration, the age and gender of the head of household, the number of pregnant and lactating women, the number of elderly people, and the number of people living with disabilities and chronic illnesses, as well as other relevant vulnerability indexes such as food consumption score, number of meals per day, possession (or lack of) productive assets.
Market assessments and cash feasibility surveys will confirm the modality of each distribution, and whenever possible, NRC will prioritize cash-based intervention.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

In the crisis-affected regions, which have experienced six consecutive years of reduced agricultural production compared to the five-year average, household food stocks are below pre-crisis levels. Most poor households that still have stocks of staple foods have enough to support only minimally adequate food consumption for up to one month. In the Momo, Lebialem, Meme, and Menchum divisions, which are most affected by food insecurity and where production was lower than in other divisions and compared to the five-year average, poor households exhausted their stocks shortly after harvest in October, four to five months earlier than usual, and are therefore entirely dependent on food from the market. In most reference markets in the North-West and South-West regions, staple food prices remain above seasonal averages, and are rising as households deplete their stocks of produce and become increasingly dependent on the marketShort-term, pendular movement continues within the two regions. Often people move back and forth between their home and their place of refuge. As they hide in the bush, people build huts and cultivate plots of land. In 44 per cent of cases, it is the entire household that undergoes such displacement. In the rest of the cases, men hide in the bushes alone, while their families remain in the village or move to safer urban areas. according to OCHA situational report of July 2023, Ongoing clashes, armed groups’ activity and military operations continued to put the population in harm’s way and cause frequent pendular displacements to nearby bushes and towns for safety and resulted in humanitarian needs. Despite the challenges, humanitarian partners continued provide assistance to the most vulnerable In a multi sectoral needs assesment carried out by IOM in september 2023, majority (70%) of assessed households reported the average duration of food stocks in the household was for less than a week. Another 23 per cent of assessed households indicated that food stocks would last for 1 to 2 weeks, while 7 per cent of assessed households have food stocks lasting for more than 2 weeks .Displaced individuals are forced to resort to coping mechanisms due to a lack of income, the majority of households (76%) will rely on cheaper food. Sixty per cent (60%) of households reduce the number of daily meals, while 47% rely on friends and family for food and 34 per cent of assessed household reported that adults reduce their consumption so that children can eat Access to the population in need in the North-West and South-West regions remains difficult and volatile, largely due to underfunding, insecurity, physical access challenges, and administrative impediments. However, access in the North-West and South-West is possible if resources are available. The need for additional resources for access negotiations increases the operational costs in a humanitarian crisis which is already hugely underfunded, further limiting the reach of humanitarian organizations to assist people in needIn the socio-political context of the North-West and South-West, the Food Security Cluster will consider the most vulnerable people identified by the October 2023 Cadre Harmonisé analysis, and focus on IDPs, the host population, and those left behind with no access to livelihood activities due to insecurity. The Sector will support immediate access to food to improve the food security status through the provision of emergency and time-critical agriculture assistance and increase access to income-generating opportunities through the rehabilitation, creation and/ or maintenance of sustainable livelihood assets. In line with the “do no harm” approach, the Sector will assess the market and security situation to determine the feasibility of CVA, which would be the preferred response modality. To implement this project, NRC will coordinate with the FAO and continue to actively participate in the FSL Cluster in NWSW (including through monthly reports).
