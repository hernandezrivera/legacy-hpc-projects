Project Summary

The Women’s Protection and Empowerment program (WPE) is composed of Partnership and Capacity Building (PCB) component and Protection Provision Services (PPS) component and adheres its activity to women 18-50+ years and adolescent girls 13-19 years old. The PCB component works directly with pre-selected partners inside Syria while also building partners capacity through specialized trainings for future sustainability, proceeding knowledge and skills development, and coaching and mentoring protection awareness and understating for prevention and intervention measures. The PPS component is implemented directly through IRC and IhsanRD includes five WPE mobile teams and one static center in Idleb ,two static centres in Aleppo working directly with women and adolescent girls inside NWS Syria delivering response and risk mitigation activities. The response activities’ case management , IPA, Problem Management plus for women and adolescent girls and focused psychological services with internally and external safe referral (for women and adolescent girls) at a predetermined (safely selected location, accessible, safe, comfortable and welcomed by the community) in Women and Girls Safe Space (WGSS) , delivering Psychological First Aid (PFA),individual counselling and PSS focused groups sessions.in addition to the response activities IRC and IhsanRD provided prevention and risk mitigation activities as following: My Safety My Wellbeing session for adolescent girls, New Mothers Support Group for new mothers and mothers to be, safety Audit , structured and non-structured GBV awareness sessions, life skills sessions , vocational and skilled focus sessions, life skills sessions and cash for protection .
The WPE program focus is to ensure women and adolescent girls are supported emotionally, psychologically and physically while provided further knowledge on GBV, GBV forms, risks and prevention. As the IRC – WPE acknowledges the significant protection concerns women and adolescent girls in Syria face, WPE rounds its functionality in order to directly and indirectly adhere to the prevention and intervention measures required to ensure the increased safety measures are deliberated to women and adolescent girls. The IRC works closely with GBV sub-cluster working groups in Turkey as one of the major protection actors in the humanitarian field. Under the IRC, WPE – PCB component, the program supports sub-cluster raised protection capacity needs, protection knowledge and protection management training material. IRC and it's implementing partner (Ihsan RD )are an active member on the GBV sub cluster and accordingly ,All activities will be implemented with close coordination with the GBV Sub-Cluster and will follow the GBV Sub-Cluster guidelines ,SOPs and tools, with focus on the GBV case management guidelines and GBV M&E tool kit, GBV awareness tool kits, cash for protection ,Individual Protection Assistant (IPA) and case fund GBV criteria.
Accountability and Learning (MEAL) team, supported by the WPE team will conduct GBV need assessment to assess the needs in the community and design the activities to be in line with the assessment result. In addition, the MEAL team will conduct focus group discussions (FGD) and key informant interviews (KII) to understand community’s perceptions of IRC’s services. Moreover, A data quality audit will be conducted on a quarterly basis to ensure that data is complete, correct, and consistent. The audit will also verify source data against what was reported. the MEAL team will also conduct satisfaction survey with the beneficiaries and the MEAL team will use GBV M&E Toolkit as guideline in designing MEAL activities and tools.

4 - Needs

Given the situation in Syria (Northwest Syria) in particular, and as we head into the tenth year of the continued conflict, there are very limited services and deliverables to individuals in need which reflects on the inability to cope with the current situation As indicated in the NWS GBV Trend Analysis Report (April-June 2020), all reported GBV incidents involved females, 79.8% of which were adults (above 18) and 20.2% were adolescent girls (12-17). Of these, 38% were psychological/emotional abuse incidents, 24% referred to denial of resources, opportunities and services and 26% were incidents of physical assault. Rape and sexual assault account for 6% whereas early and forced marriage counts for the remaining 5%. The occurrence percentages of the incidents facing females in NWS vary regarding the age of GBV victims and are committed by different types of perpetrators. The types and percentages of perpetrators are related to the type of incident itself – 22% of the physical assault cases were perpetrated by an intimate partner and 6% were by family members. The data showed that 3% of rapes and sexual assaults were committed by intimate partners, 1% by family members and 2% by others. The highest percentage (16%) of the denial of resources and opportunities services were perpetrated by family members and 9% by intimate partners. While 20% of the psychological/emotional abuse was by intimate partner, 13% by family members, and 7% by others.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Security situation of Syria remains challenging while administration of activities in the affected areas. Given the sensitivity and the importance of services be delivered in a safe and dignified manner, IRC works with its safety and security dedicated departments and ensures locations are sensitively determined to ensure the utmost safety measures are taken for beneficiaries and staff, this is run by collaboration between the Jordan remote team and Syria field staff. Management is run through Jordan remote team. Coordination is conducted to ensure service provision and delivery meets the minimum standards and ensures do not cause further harm to beneficiary focus. IRC works with GBV sub-cluster for recommendation to cover areas in need that lack protection services for targeted vulnerable group based on need identified as women, PWD, elderly and adolescent girls. WPE reaches local council within the community inside Syria for further support within the community. Humanitarian Access Unit (HAU), coordinate with program field management to identify risks that may impact IRC to manage, mitigate and prepare contingency plans. IRC follows its Security Management Plan (SMP) and (SOPs) for NWS to support further needed assistance and safety measures to be taken for beneficiaries and operating staff. Feedback and Accountability Mechanism (FARM) reinforced by IRC assists beneficiary accountability and credibility. IRC is reinforcing Client Responsiveness (CR) for its programs to support beneficiary involvement to ensure services provided actually address required, raised and necessary needs. 7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Protection of our beneficiaries is of paramount importance to the IRC particularly as it may be perpetrated by humanitarian workers, including IRC employees, volunteers, interns, Board Members, incentive workers, partners, and all those that work on behalf of our mission. IRC is guided in its preventive efforts by the UN Secretary-General’s Bulletin on “Special Measures for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Sexual Abuse,” published in 2003 and incorporates the Bulletin’s six core principles. IRC’s Beneficiary Protection Policy further expands on these six core principles to include a prohibition on all types of exploitation and abuse of our beneficiaries. IRC understands the unequal power dynamics between employees and beneficiaries and therefore has created code of conduct, PSEA policies and deedback and complaint mechanisms. Moreover, the IRC considers that: Exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries, whether of a sexual nature or not, by IRC Workers constitutes an act of gross misconduct and are therefore, individually or together, grounds for immediate termination of employment. IRC workers are obliged to create and maintain an environment that is free of sexual exploitation and abuse. When an IRC worker develops concerns or suspicions regarding abuse or exploitation by a fellow worker, whether employed by IRC or another agency, he or she must report such concerns utilizing the IRC’s Global Reporting Guidelines.
