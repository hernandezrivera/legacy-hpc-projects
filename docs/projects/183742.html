Project Summary

The proposed project will work towards increasing access to basic and specialized services for the most affected population of people with disabilities living in Gaza, area C of the West Bank, besides persons with lasting injuries due to great return of March (GRM) and last escalation of May 2021 in Gaza, by providing multidisciplinary rehabilitation services; through outreach, including but not limited to, basic chronic rehabilitation services, post-operative and psychological services. Due to COVID19 situation, the proposed project will include the modality of tele-rehabilitation, besides activation of operational partners' centers under COVID19 protective measures that will definitely include transported beneficiaries to those centers. Moreover, the proposed project will aim at covering the transportation fees of people with disabilities or trauma patients with lasting injuries to non-rehabilitation and mainstream services including: medical, protection, MHPSS through the local referral network in the five governorates. That shall come with special consideration to COVID19 situation by providing family hygiene kit for affected persons with disabilities and trauma patients to avoid the contamination and exposure to the virus. The package of services will be based on pre-identification and assessment of individuals' needs by the combination of well-trained and skilled multi-disciplinary rehabilitation professionals. The project will promote accessibility through the implementation of home adaptation for the beneficiaries who developed permanent disabilities or having long-term disability with limited participation due to barriers faced at home. This ultimately will improve community participation for the targeted population through progressing of their independency, and regain well recovery. At the level of prevention, and detection the project will increase the awareness raising on disability mainstreaming for healthcare professionals. In addition, to increase mobile teams’ competencies on Gender-based Violence (GBV) detection including boosting referrals mechanism for persons with disabilities to increase their access to the GBV services required as per their identified needs and demands. Nonetheless, the project will work on a holistic approach to ensure proper and better access to other needs beyond project deliverables for the targeted beneficiaries through the coordination mechanism of local referral networks and in particular by continuing the support of the rehabilitation taskforce. This will ensure standardizing of rehabilitation protocols and information sharing among actors and preparedness through the capacity building of rehabilitation professional on minimum standards or rehabilitation and emergency rehabilitation.

1 - Needs

HI conducted three main assessments in 2020: 1) A Rapid Needs Assessment (RNA-March 2020), 2) In-depth sectorial assessment (DSA – August 2020) both conducted in the Gaza Strip, 3) a Rapid Needs Assessment (RNA – April2020) in West Bank. In Gaza, whereas the assessments show extensive and multifaceted needs and gaps. The following priorities prevail: 1) the need to ensure continuous provision of multidisciplinary rehabilitation services in order to cover the gaps among people with disabilities and persons with injuries (past and new cohort). The majority of the participants of the RNA highlighted the need to receive long-run physiotherapy and occupational therapy treatment, assistive devise provision and the relevancy to receive psychosocial services, particularly for children with disabilities and their families. They also stressed the importance to enhance affordability of the rehabilitation service provision, as medications, medical equipment and assistive devices result expensive. Transportation cost represents an additional burden. The RNA flagged also the need to access information of available services and receiving a proper referral. In the aftermath of the Great March of Return (GMR), coupled with the overwhelming COVID19 situation, hospitals along the Strip are not able to provide the required comprehensive care and rehabilitation for persons with injuries and person with disabilities. The lack of support could increase the susceptibility to disability. Therefore, it is crucial that services are provided to ensure the continuation of care (trauma pathway) and recovery. 2) The need to strengthen the coordination among rehabilitation actors: the DSA study revealed a defect in coordination among different implementing actors. Although efforts are under way, the need to distribute the caseload of beneficiaries to the various actors and to avoid duplication of service provision is still valid. The Patient Allocation Tool (PAT), which allows tracking trauma patients at the postoperative and rehabilitation stages, is still not properly used, nor used from all. Part of coordination efforts should also aim towards the improvement of a unified referral mechanism. Currently, the referral network lacks of feedback system, of adequate referrals documentation, a long list of referrals brings to long waiting time that could cause further complications for the patients. In addition, one of the main concerns by people with disabilities and persons with injuries was the affordability of the service, in particular the transportation cost to benefit from centres' service provision. Long-term rehabilitation treatments for some conditions is necessary to ensure quality and good level of recovery. Finally, even once the referral has succeeded, remains concerns about the quality of the service. 3) The need for Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS). A factsheet produced by disability working group (DWG) -August 2020, reports that COVID19 pandemic has increased the difficulties facing persons with disabilities particularly in the Gaza Strip, and made their conditions more complicated. A rapid need assessment conducted on May 2021, 1407 persons with disabilities from the IDPs (677F-725M) were identified in the shelters, 1 102 persons (556F-546M) with disabilities and injuries in needs of different Assistive Devices to improve their functional mobility and independence to enable them access to basic needs. Moreover, 534 Persons (233F-301M) in needs of rehabilitation services geographically divided: 188 in North Gaza, 146 in Gaza, 16 Middle Area, 101 Khan Yunis and 83 in Rafah Governorate. In addition, 1 213 NFIs are needed to guarantee access to basic needs, the items identified are 177 medical mattress, 155 wound dressing, 596 hygiene kit, 153 kitchen kit and 132 other NFIs.In West Bank, during the confinement, a rapid assessment was conducted for people with disabilities in the Southern areas. The community workers have registered 1 590 urgent needs for 647 persons with disabilities in Bethlehem and Hebron. Among them, 538 medical supplies and consumables are required for 363 persons with disabilities. 418 needs for sanitizers and hygiene materials are registered for 244 persons with disabilities; 38 needs for mental health for persons with disabilities; and 70 needs for medicines for 68 persons with disabilities.

2 - Activities

The outcome of the project is to ensure the availability, accessibility, acceptability and quality of essential lifesaving health, and rehabilitation services to vulnerable communities and reduce vulnerability of persons with injuries and persons with disabilities; especially women, children and elderly. Output 1: Persons with injuries and people with disabilities have increased independence through equitable access to basic health, rehabilitation and psychosocial services by the multidisciplinary outreach and center-based services in line with the continuum of care. The activities proposed to address the output 1 are the following. Activity 1.1: Provision of multidisciplinary rehabilitation services using center-based, homebased and/or remote tele-rehabilitation to 1,300 people with disabilities and 200 persons with lasting injuries who have limited access to services in Gaza and West Bank. Activity 1.2: Training and follow up of at least 1,500 family members and caregivers (1,000 females and 500 males) on basic rehabilitation therapeutic exercises to support persons with disabilities and persons with injuries in Gaza and West Bank. Activity 1.3: Purchase and donation of 500 assistive devices for the targeted beneficiaries as per their identified needs in Gaza and West Bank. Activity 1.4: Purchase medical materials and nursing disposables (150 kits) to support postoperative care and wound dressing services for persons with disabilities and persons with injuries in Gaza. Activity 1.5: Support independency through reasonable accessible works for 100 houses for persons with disabilities in West Bank and Gaza to enhance mobility and daily life activities. Activity 1.6: Technical support to partners’ rehabilitation teams though monthly technical trainings, quarterly exchange meetings and monitoring field visits in Gaza and West Bank. Output 2: Support the coordination efforts through the rehabilitation taskforce to ensure continuum of healthcare through referrals and sharing information. The activities proposed to address the output 2 are the following. Activity 2.1: Support standardize rehabilitation intervention and boost referrals for persons with lasting injuries and persons with disabilities in Gaza. Activity 2.2: Provide technical training to rehabilitation mobile teams on early disability detection and referral in collaboration with protection cluster (GBV working group) in West Bank targeted governorates. Activity 2.3: Conduct awareness raising sessions on disability mainstreaming in collaboration with disability working group (DWG) in Gaza targeted governorates and in coordination with BASR in West Bank targeted governorates. Activity 2.4: Provision of financial support to vulnerable people in need of acute center-based health and rehabilitation services in Gaza and West Bank targeted governorates. Output 3: Support the urgent needs of affected population to mitigate COVID19 impact through the provision of essential materials and awareness campaigns. The activities proposed to address the output 3 are the following. Activity 3.1: Support partner’s rehabilitation centres with PPEs to ensure the continuity of service provisions to people with disabilities and chronic diseases. Activity 3.2: Purchase and donation of 800 hygiene kits to support affected population of persons with disabilities and persons with lasting injuries to minimize contamination and exposure of the virus.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The ‘Do No Harm’ principle has been considered at different levels in relation with both the services and the individuals, by considering gender differences, by promoting local community ownership of the project through local partnerships. A psychologist will be part of the rehabilitation service team and will be trained to adequately answer psychological needs of affected persons. The tools used for the need’s assessment will be tailored to suit both Gaza and West Bank contexts and specificity of persons with injuries and people with disabilities
