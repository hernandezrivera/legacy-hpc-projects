Project Summary

UNFPA's GBV interventions will contribute to outcome 1 and outcome 2 of the 2017 – 2019 Sudan Multi Year Humanitarian Strategy and contribute to saving lives and reducing the social and economic impact of humanitarian emergencies through addressing the needs of population affected by emergency situation in Darfur, South Kordofan, Blue Nile and Abyei area, the intervention include GBV service provision focus in the high-need areas, the newly accessible areas and new displacements within the recent two years.
The project will support existing and new establishment of women centers in targeted localities through implement activities package include; environmentally friendly rehabilitation/ construction of the centers and provision of GBV services within these centers such as; awareness raising on GBV related issues and information, life skills training, and psycho-social support include individual and group counseling.
The women centers will also serve as safe space for gathering and an entry point for 60,000 women and girls to discuss a range of issues of concern and to receive information on protection and GBV related issues.
The project will ensure access of GBV survivors to health services and psychosocial support and counseling through provision of individual and collective GBV services targeted 15,000 girls, boys, women and men. Support community protection networks will provide the linkages between the community and GBV services and facilitate referral of GBV survivors to access medical treatment and PSS support .
In addition, capacity building for 1000 front line respondents – service providers and community members on CMR, psycho social support, referral pathway, legal aid, national and international legal frameworks and protection policies supporting GBV survivors.
The community level consultations will also create a sense of ownership and agency of the population over issues that affect their lives.
Support existing national and sub national level coordination mechanisms at national and in 4 Darfur states lead by UNFPA to advocate for and respond to GBV concerns, in addition to UNFPA participation in joint needs assessment missions to provide timely response and interventions.
UNFPA will Procure and distribute 35,000 personal hygiene kits and sanitary supplies to the new arrivals and most vulnerable women and girls of reproductive age including IDPs, GBV survivors, and females heading households.
Total of 30,000 individual will benefit from community protection networks outreach activities and information .

Needs

Women and girls are disproportionately affected by the impact of the ongoing humanitarian situation in Sudan due to gender inequalities, combined with other factors such as discrimination and disadvantage including age, ethnicity, marital status etc, breakdown of protection mechanisms, destruction of essential services and economic structures and the lack of recognition of their crucial contribution to the survival and recovery of crisis-affected communities. The position of women and girls is further diminished due to their lack of access to and control of resources and viable coping strategies needed for survival as well the protection of their basic human rights and recourse to justice. The deterioration of gender-based power-relations during crises, heightens exposure to gender-based violence (GBV) in all its forms. Furthermore, impunity of perpetrators and barriers to justice and redress are even more difficult in situations where protections mechanisms are not functioning well. Women and girls at risk represent respectively 25 % and 30 % of the displaced population, this percentage is higher in situations of new displacement. Girls are vulnerable to early marriage, exclusion from education due to domestic work, and trafficking. Single heads of households (38 % of women and girls), survivors of gender-based violence (GBV), women in prison and pregnant and lactating women are also at risk. Adolescents are one of the groups most likely to adopt risky behavior and coping mechanisms, which often puts them at greater risk of abuse and exploitation.IDPs, returnees and host communities continue to face GBV risks in areas of conflict in Darfur’s Jebel Marra, South Kordofan and Blue Nile. Women’s financial status force them to work in unfavorable and insecure environments that expose them to violence. The current harsh economic conditions in the country may also be exposing vulnerable women and girls to further risks. Lack of GBV awareness among communities makes it challenging to ensure prevention, mitigation and access of survivors to the relevant multi-sectoral services where available. Specialized lifesaving GBV services i.e. clinical management of rape (CMR), psycho-social support (PSS), including individual counseling, case management and livelihood support are unavailable in over 80 % of affected localities and where available their quality needs to be strengthened. By the end of 2017, the gap in CMR services in Darfur was 86 % in East Darfur, 83 % in South Darfur, 81 per cent in North Darfur, 79 % in Central Darfur and 72 % in West Darfur. In 2017, 307 cases of GBV were reported to the Special Prosecutor for Darfur Crimes with the majority involving minors (286 cases). Access to justice for GBV survivors is very limited due to the community lack of awareness and stigma, lack of legal aid, transport costs, legal fees as well as weak coordination and referral mechanisms

Activities

1. Support existing national and sub national level coordination mechanisms to advocate for and respond to GBV concerns. These coordination mechanisms already exist and well functioning lead by UNFPA at national and 4 Darfur states2. Provide timely and coordinated emergency GBV services to IDPs and other affected persons with specific needs exposed to neglect, violence, exploitation and/or abuse (through participation in joint needs assessment missions, identification of needs of GBV survivors and coordination with GBV actors to provide timely response. 3. Ensure access for persons with specific needs and victims of protection incidents to aid, social services and legal remedies through referral mechanisms (with special focus on access of GBV survivors to health services and psychosocial support through training of service providers on CMR, PSS and provision of medical supplies (PEP kits) for health facilities) 4. Strengthen capacity of national institutions (including law enforcement authorities and administration of justice) and protection service providers in relation to national and international legal frameworks and protection policies such as Form 8 and Circular 2 for GBV survivors. 5. Strengthen capacity of affected communities to identify GBV risks/incidents, to provide adequate response and to work and working with Community protection networks .6. Establish/ support the existing women women centers in 60 localities targeted 7. Procure and distribute personal hygiene kits and sanitary supplies to the most vulnerable women and girls of reproductive age including IDPs, GBV survivors, and females heading households8. Train protection humanitarian actors on performing rapid GBV assessments and implementing minimum emergency GBV response.
