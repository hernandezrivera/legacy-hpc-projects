Project Summary

The 2018/2019 poor agricultural season resulting from drought and mid-season dry spells and the prevailing macro-economic environment characterized by food shortages, high food prices and fuel shortages have adversely impacted on access to Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) services in both rural and urban areas of Zimbabwe. The drought has resulted in depletion of water in dams and other water bodies and the water table going down. Increased poverty has contributed in communities failing to maintain boreholes.
According to the November, 2019 Zimbabwe rural WASH information management system (RWIMS), Bikita has a total of 1011 water points of which 36 % (206 partially functional and 157 not functional) are either partially or not functional. 11 % of the population have access to safe sanitation, 89 % have no toilets, 60% have pots, 79 % have refuse pits. Chivi has a total of 1139 water points with 38 % (225 partially functional and 206 not functional) being partially or not functional. Around a quarter (28 %) of the population have access to safe sanitation, 72 % have no toilets, 63% have pot racks and 79% have rubbish pits. Mwenezi has 913 water points of which 35 % are either partially or not functional (167 partially functional and152 not functional). Only 19% have access to safe sanitation, 81% have no toilets. 61% have pot racks and 73% have refuse pits.
CARE is proposing to support rehabilitation of 490 perennial boreholes and establishment of 12 (4 per district) solar powered piped water schemes on high yielding borehole. All rehabilitated boreholes will be quality tested. Refresher training will be given to village pump mechanics and water point committees. From the RWIMS Sanitation statistics, proportion of people practicing open defecation is high across the three districts. Project will intensify hygiene promotion as a strategy of minimizing the outbreak of diarrhoeal diseases. Community Health clubs will be strengthened to spearhead hygiene promotion in selected communities. WASH Hygiene and dignity kits will be distributed to the selected vulnerable households, women and girls including those living with disabilities. Accountability mechanisms and systems that safeguard against sexual exploitation of women and girls will be put in place. Gender will be mainstreamed across all activities and gender disaggregated data will be collected. Implementation of the proposed interventions will be integrated and coordinated with UNICEF managed WASH in schools in the all 3 targeted districts and Austrain Aid funded WASH project being implemented in Chivi in addition to other projects being implemented by CARE in the districts. CARE is requesting for USD4 201 904 funding to successfully implement the proposed activities.
