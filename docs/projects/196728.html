Project Summary

After 11-year crisis of in Syria, there is an increased risk of disease outbreaks due to population movement and the erosion of public services and infrastructure. The COVID-19 pandemic and the worsening economic situation are further stretching and disrupting the already fragile health system in Syria, thus affecting the availability and quality of health services, as well as the physical and mental wellbeing of the population.
More than 15.3 million people in Syria are estimated to be in dire need of life-saving health assistance in 2023, an increase of 3.2 million from 2022. This includes 6.8 displaced persons, 2.2 million children under 5 years – including expected live births –, 4.2 million women of reproductive age (15-49 years), and more than 700,000 elderly people who will demand integrated and lifesaving health services in Syria. Under-funding of response agencies has also left the 438k Palestine refugees living in Syria in acute need. The economic crisis also added to the vulnerability of Syrian children and women who are not able to pay for the essential health and nutrition services. (Reference: FINAL DRAFT 2022 HNO Sector Template Health Sector 23 November). A cholera outbreak that started in August 2022 has also added to the vulnerability of the Syrian population especially in the northern governorates.
This proposal from UNICEF aims to support the provision of basic lifesaving curative, preventive and promotional health services and health seeking and care practices, while enhancing early recovery and resilience of the sector by contributing to:
strengthening service delivery to vulnerable population who cannot afford paying for health services, specifically children,
supporting community health delivery platforms, with major focus on community engagement through volunteers to include and integrate them within the health facilities and be part of the community health system to ensure sustainability of those voluntary services for best delivery, including information, supply and monitoring & evaluation. this will help in improving the quality of services for the most vulnerable. The safety of the health workers who are providing maternal and child health services (including newborn care) is also considered through activities which ensure that all health workers are empowered with knowledge on diseases prevention and health promotion. It is also conceived with a view to promoting the complementarity of the contributions of other sections and multi-sectoral integration within UNICEF & other key sector partners. The project will provide health services to boys, girls and women with disabilities on an equal basis.
UNICEF will be working through local NGOs who run 36 fixed health centers and 47 mobile teams across all the 14 governorates, including in NES and NWS and will focus to support the implementation of the immunization programme for children under the age of 1 year, and supplementary immunization activities (national immunization days and catch-up campaigns) for the CU 5 years. More than five million women and children will directly benefit from the package of services, focusing on the highest severity districts as defined by the Health Cluster/Sector and in integration with UNICEF prioritized high severity sub-districts across its seven sectors. Programme effectiveness shall be enhanced by applying a conflict and risk informed lens to planned interventions, and the promotion of the participation of affected populations. The accountability towards affected population is integrated throughout the programme cycle, UNICEF started to collect information from the affected population on the quality of the services through hotlines, SMS and complaint boxes in fixed clinics of NGOs.
UNICEF will work in 258 sub districts, including 170 with a severity scale of 3 or higher.
NICEF will monitor the project through programme monitoring visits & financial spot checks by UNICEF staff, and by third-party monitors

3 - Needs

The rapidly spreading cholera outbreak, COVID-19 pandemic, low vaccinations, combined with deteriorating economic conditions, increases in poverty across the country, and the water crisis in north Syria, are expected to further stretch already weak health systems. Access to basic health care services especially for PWD will be affected negatively and increased morbidities /mortalities may occur among vulnerable communities. Major projected needs for 2023 are outbreak prevention, control and response measures; access to health services (especially for PWD) such as reproductive health and safe delivery, child health, including routine immunization and NIDs, nutrition, care for communicable and non-communicable diseases; mental health and psychosocial support; emergency services, including trauma and referral especially for PWD; and physical rehabilitation at every level of the health system. COVID-19 vaccination, preparedness, community health services, including outreach and engagement on integrated health messages, and management of severe acute malnutrition with medical complications remain essential components of comprehensive health services. With recurrent disease outbreaks, particularly cholera, the key areas in 2023 to be addressed throughout Syria, particularly in the NES, are access to medicines and medical supply shortages, limited health care worker capacities, WASH in health facilities and surveillance systems. Rehabilitation of HCs will increase access to PHC.

4 - Implementation of activities

UNICEF will continue to implement through NGOs to run 36 fixed centers & 47 mobile teams in NES and GoS areas. IPs will provide pediatric & maternal check-ups to treat common illnesses, screen malnutrition, provide medication & refer complicated cases including PWD. IPs will educate, empower communities with knowledge and skills on healthcare through behavior change interventions. UNICEF will support 50 MOH hospitals (CTCs) to respond to Cholera outbreak, implement vaccinations, newborn care, maternal health, IMCI, other PHC projects, and support awareness raising as integral part of all projects including RCCE for Cholera, Including PWDCoordination with UNFPA/WHO is done through joint activities with the same NGOs. UNICEF is: A) reporting monthly to the sector to provide better coordinated response, avoiding overlapping and reaching the most vulnerable groups, B) UNICEF has a concrete data collection system & verified calculation methods to report on the beneficiaries reached, their needs and satisfaction & geographical level. All IPs are trained on PSEA and accountability towards affected population. 50Female doctors are available to ensure maternal care is accessible to vulnerable women. UNICEF has four complementary and triangulated monitoring modalities: partner monitoring; third-party monitoring (TPMs); and programme officer monitoring. In NWS, TPMs provide on-site verification & beneficiary interviews. UNICEF uses a WOS info platform to consolidate & analyze data.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Violence against health workers: 255 attacks on 197 HF occurred (Jan 2018 - Dec 2020) Mitigation: report on attacks (SSA, MRM, and others). Utilize Syria Humanitarian Notification System. Advocacy with all parties IHL. Gender Based Violence: women & girls at acute risk of sexual and GBV, health facilities are an entry point for survivors. Mitigation: affected population should have access to RH services of MISP. HW to be trained on providing survivor with first-line support, clinical management of rape and safe referrals. Training health workers should be in synergy with CP section & UNFPA. Ensure confidentiality of all patients. Sexual Exploitation & Abuse: risk of SEA persists. Mitigation: Enforce PSEA policy for all IPs with zero tolerance, with a documented commitment. Establish community-based complaints mechanisms, awareness-raising on SEA with training, monitoring, especially for mobile medical teams, and encourage female workers.Duty of care: it is obligatory to provide physical, mental safety, security & wellbeing to staff.Special Considerations for C19: ensure equitable access to care, creating at-risk settings (isolation, quarantine..), exacerbation of risk (such as lockdown measures), health workers are under tremendous strain at the frontlines.Healthcare for Vulnerable Groups & Equitable Access to Health Services: work in high-severity districtsSafe Closure of Facilities and Protecting Data: HW trained on safe withdrawalCommunity Engagement & Participation.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse:During the course of their work, aid providers come into contact with vulnerable people. Humanitarian workers have access to goods & services that put them in a position of power over the community. Healthcare providers should strive and commit to preventing sexual exploitation and abuse both on organizational and programmatic levels.Mitigating measures:- UNICEF PSEA policy in place forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and inclusive to accountability measures.- UNICEF has effective and comprehensive mechanisms to ensure awareness-raising on SEA amongst personnel, with continuous training on the topic.- UNICEF trained all IPs on PSEA.- UNICEF has effective recruitment & performance management of all staff.- UNICEF is participating in UN impartial assessment of needs and risks, and understand the vulnerabilities & capacities of different groups, and established reporting and feedback mechanisms through hotlines and SMS.- UNICEF Provides information to communities & people affected about the programmes it is implementing and what UNICEF intends to deliver as well how to report concerns & complaints.- UNICEF established community-based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance and referral. - UNICEF ensures proper investigation and follow up of each reported case, including referrals to specialized GBV service providers where applicable and implementing measures to guard against retaliation or discrimination.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Guard against fraud: UNICEF implement programmatic visits & financial spot checks on regular bases to all IPs. All partners have signed on UNICEF rules to keep their records & financial documents for 5 years after project completion. All partners are assessed and classified for their "risk", and all high-risk IPs are asked to take corrective action & checked more regularly and reassessed later. Ensure humanitarian principles are upheld and services delivery is delivered equally without discrimination through regular monitoring and orientation of all IPs on those principles and continuous follow-up & feedback from beneficiaries. Community volunteers are trained on how to deliver in a professional unbiased way and they are selected from the same community to avoid any contact with check points. Devaluation of the local currency and price increase: This led to increased cost of all commodities including medicines & medical consumables, this will continue for 2022-23 due to the economic crisis and the continuous sanction on Syria. Mitigating measures: UNICEF will increase the salaries for health workers and specialists working with the IPs in coordination with other UN agencies to ensure continuity of services in high-severity areas, and consistency of rates among different agencies. Violence against Healthcare Providers: Data on attacks against HW documented 255 attacks on 197 HF. Mitigation measures: Reporting on attacks through SSA & MRM, advocacy with parties on IHL.
