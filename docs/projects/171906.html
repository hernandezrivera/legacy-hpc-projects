Project Summary

After seven years of conflict, frontline communities in direct vicinity of the Line of Contact (LoC) such those located in the area covered by theVolnovakha raion are bearing the brunt of the protracted crisis. The resources of communities acting as administrative centers in Government-Controlled Areas have been thinly stretched to provide an array of key services (social services, justice, counselling, administrative) for local populations living both in Government (GCA) as well as residents of Non-Government-Controlled Areas (NGCA) of Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts who cross the line of contact (LoC) to seek services in GCA. As COVID-19 has added further pressure on communities and local authorities, increased needs for e.g. Psycho-social support (PSS) have been identified and reported as a priority by local representatives.
ACTED, in partnership with Right to Protection (R2P) and the Red Cross (RC), aim at supporting the resilience of communities in three prospective Hromadas, totalling 67,000 individuals, located in Volnovakha raion (Myrne, Olhynka, Volnovakha) through capacity-building and provision of protection services for vulnerable populations, including Internally Displaced People (IDP) and host communities. Activity design has been informed by the experience of partners in the target areas and, in line with Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) principles, community inputs have been sought and inputted into project design and modalities. Community participation will take place during the entire duration of the project, through public communications, transparency for targeting and eligibility, and partners will maintain Complaint and Response Mechanisms (CRM) to ensure that community members, including people who were not supported, can voice their concerns and receive information in return. Data collected by partners will be disaggregated by gender, age, special needs and type of vulnerability for accountability to affected populations and to inform project modalities.
The proposed intervention includes Capacity-Building of Local Authorities (LAs) to address the needs of the vulnerable population and strengthen provision of essential services such as community-based PSS in cooperation with the Local Ukrainian Red Cross Society (URCS) branch and heads of settlements. The project will contribute promote the development of volunteerism, self-support and social cohesion in the conflict affected communities, thereby contributing to preparing for a humanitarian exit in GCA
Planned PSS and social services activities will be implemented in synergy and contribute to improved resilience for vulnerable people. The services provided by trained local volunteers with the support of the local URCS Branch and regular consultations with LAs before, during and after the project, together with activities aimed at improving access to and the quality of social services, will enhance capacity of LAs to take responsibility for and work with communities to find durable solutions to strengthen social cohesion, well-being and other significant protection concerns.

1/RC activities will aim at strengthening protection, including prevention and mitigation of rights’ violations for conflict-affected people, including IDPs. RC jointly with LAs will provide PSS and outreach support as well as community spaces for older persons, persons with disabilities, and people with conflict-related trauma.
RC will improve social cohesion and resilience in Volnovakha communities, including IDPs, through community-based protection interventions and identification of durable solutions.
2/R2P’s component will contribute to building capacity of LA representatives. R2P’s approach will address disruption of services networks and increase the CAP satisfaction with basic social services, including vulnerable groups (single women, people with disabilities, elderly) host communities and persons who cross the contact line at EECP Novotroitske.

1 - Needs

Preliminary findings from the 2020 REACH Trends Analysis for 2020 indicate that 11% of all assessment respondents (from communities located 0-20km from the LoC in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts GCA) required legal assistance for the issuance of documents (REACH, 2020), and 8% of all respondents reported requiring legal assistance at the time of the assessment (summer 2020) for access to justice, court proceedings and lawyers’ services. Only 17% of respondents reported being able to access and afford civil and criminal justice if needed, demonstrating that counselling and legal assistance remain key sectors for which focal populations require additional support.According to IMPACT’s Hromada Capacity and Vulnerability assessment, sService providers in Volnovakha raion tend to report higher than average pressure on their facilities from NGCA residents than the average in GCA communities (57% of respondents in Volnovakha report experiencing this, versus 42% for other similar locations assessed) and other concerns have arisen and pose additional challenges to local provision of services, particularly in relation to the stress induced by COVID-19 on the population and the subsequent increased need to for PSSpsychosocial support. With regards to the population, 20% of all respondents in Volnovakha raion and 26% of respondents in peripheries of Volnovakha raion reported being either dissatisfied or very dissatisfied with the provision of social services in their community (IMPACT HCVA, 2020). Additionally, 79% of respondents considered that ensuring appropriate level of social, medical and educational services for the population, including those for vulnerable groups was among the top five priorities for the development of their community.Under the REACH Initiative, a joint venture initiative of between ACTED, and IMPACT Initiatives, and UNOSAT, conducted has conducted many assessments a series of assessments in Eastern Ukraine to which can be used to inform the humanitarian community, including the followingdesign and implementation of protection-based interventions in the target area and for the target population:- Multi Sector Needs Assessment/Trends Analyses run every year for 2016 – 2020 and include a dedicated section on Protection needs.- Joint REACH/Protection Cluster Protection Assessment of Isolated Settlements Along the LoC, (2019).- REACH, Interagency Vulnerability Assessment (2016).- Volnovakha Capacity and Vulnerabilities Assessment (2018).- IMPACT Area Based risk Assessment (2020)- REACH Area-Based Assessment (2017) In Volnovakha raion, ACTED and IMPACT also implement activities focused on assessing and supporting interventions that address local recovery and planning needs, specifically looking at local capacities for service delivery - as part of the UN Recovery and Peacebuilding Program (UNRPP).program focused on provision of services to a) address the current gap in area-based needs assessments available at the settlement level b) support local capacities for service delivery. Under the 3P Consortium, ACTED and partners R2P and RC also support authorities in Volnovakha raion for improvement of WASH servicesto increase water safety and raise awareness of, prepare for and mitigate the impacts on preparedness toof natural and man-made disasters.The Red Cross is also active in the area with a local branch, and projects activities since the start of the Conflict, and carried out in October 2020 a Field Assessment to consult and survey 41 Local Authorities and Community Representatives. The consultation was focused on access to health and social services, and sustainable options from the perspective of local representatives. Majority of respondents confirmed that the population has faces gaps in access to social or health facilities/services and that these problems can be compensated by providing transport for the vulnerable population, as well as providing social services to those in need at home.
