Project Summary

In South Sudan, the Ministry of General Education and Instruction’s policy intention is that all children of school-going age in South Sudan, regardless of circumstance or setting conditions, they should have
access to education services. Unfortunately, over 2.8 million children of compulsory school going age are not registered in pupils/secondary schools & are not receiving suitable education across the country (MoGEI, 2023).
In the targeted, Early Childhood Education (ECE) programming not available for children from the poorest households and those communities have the least access to ECE services. ECE Centers are non-existent in most parts of the county and if they do, they are privately owned. This means that thousands of young children miss out on good health, nutrition, responsive caregiving, security and safety and opportunities for early learning. They are not provided with the tools they need to develop and learn, to help them establish a
foundation upon which they can build their futures and realize their fullest potential.
In the selected counties, there is increasing urbanization where informal settlements spring up without proper infrastructure, making it difficult for formal educational institutions to reach these populations. Families living in these areas also face economic pressures that force them to priorities immediate survival over long-term educational investments. Here, the concept of ‘hard-to-reach’ communities extends beyond remote and geographically inaccessible areas to include urban informal settlements where access to services is limited by poverty.
Pre-primary education is not free or compulsory, and there is an uneven distribution of access to services. A 2024 assessment found that students in South Sudan had poor literacy skills. Girls are particularly affected by school closures, and there is a cultural disdain for girls' education. Early gaps in cognitive, linguistic, and socio-emotional skills jeopardies a child’s capacity and motivation to learn in school and low levels of school readiness lead to costly inefficiencies in the education system as disadvantaged and under-prepared
children are more likely to repeat grades and drop out of school before completing the primary cycle (2019, World Bank & UNICEF).
The ECE Policy of the Ministry of General Education & Instruction (MoGEI) harmonizes and regulates ECE standards and programming, calling for concerted efforts to address the pre-primary education disparities that persist across the country (UNICEF, 2023). The 2-year cycle program serves children aged 3–6, providing 4 hours of instruction each day, 5 days a week, ensuring that children have access to comprehensive early childhood education services.
YOUR AID FOR HEALTH (YAH)’S intention is to successfully deliver quality and equitable Education in ibba and Tambura County for all school going age children.
The overall goal is to prepare the youngest generation by creating a safe and empowering environment for 2,000 (1200 girls & 800 boys) children, 160 (120 female & 40 male) teachers and ECE facilitators, 80 (54 female & 46 male) PTA/SMC members, and 10, 000 ( 6,000 female & 4,000 male) parents and caregivers in 4 ECE Centers to learn about health and hygiene, basic education, nutrition, and social and emotional development through a combination of fun educational games and structured classroom work.
YAH is rooted within the community with the potential for community-driven solutions in enhancing early childhood education and care among marginalized populations by collaborating with local stakeholders to better understand sustainable and impactful early childhood development strategies across the County. ECE is not only an investment in a more democratic and a more equitable society, it is also an investment in a healthier, more literate and, ultimately, more productive population.

1. Needs

Inclusive education is a fundamental right that ensures access to quality education for all individuals, irrespective of their backgrounds, abilities, or socio-economic status. However, marginalized communities, including but not limited to ethnic minorities, low-income families, children with disabilities, and girls, often face significant barriers to accessing and receiving quality education in ibba and Tambura Counties. These barriers include discrimination, poverty, cultural biases, lack of resources, and inadequate infrastructure in the counties especially the on going community conflict in Tambura that has left so many children with no access to education.The consequences of exclusion from education are far-reaching, perpetuating cycles of poverty, social inequality, and limited opportunities for marginalized individuals, Ibba and Tambura is under the cluster priority area 4 and under the intersectoral priority area 3 hence the need for agent support to rescue the young children in the county who are the future of this country. To break these cycles, it is crucial to improve and strengthen quality and inclusive education systems that address the specific needs and challenges faced by marginalized communities in Ibba and Tambura. 2. Project activities

Conduct baseline assessment: Collaborate with local communities and stakeholders to identify the specific barriers and challenges faced by marginalized communities regarding access to inclusive education.Orientations: Orient State and county officials on the implementation of the ECE planDevelop inclusive education local policies: Advocate for the development and implementation of inclusive education policies at the local and national levels, ensuring that they address the unique needs of marginalized communities.Enhance infrastructure and resources: Improve physical infrastructure, including school buildings, classrooms, and accessibility features, to ensure that learning environments are inclusive and conducive to learning for all.Curriculum adaptation and development: Work with educators to adapt existing curricula and develop inclusive teaching materials that reflect the diversity of learners and promote inclusive values.Teacher training and support: Provide training programs and workshops for educators to enhance their understanding of inclusive education principles, teaching methodologies, and classroom management strategies.Parent and community engagement: Establish platforms for parent and community engagement to foster a supportive environment that values and supports inclusive education.Develop and implement ECE implementation plan: with a focus on providing access to ECE learners and teacher training.Provide mentorship and training: for 80 Parent Teacher Associations (PTAs)/School Management Committee (SMC)Support provision of ECE learning materials: (ECE Kits, recreation kits, and other supplementary learning materials for 160 teachers and 2000 learners.Establish or install play areas: in ECE centers including talking walls in existing structures Establish county level ECE coordination body: that includes Partners and community representativesPayment of Teachers incentives: Incentives can motivate teachers to perform better by rewarding good performance. This can include spending more time on teaching and preparation, or showing up in the classroom.Monitoring and evaluation: Implement a robust monitoring and evaluation framework to measure the impact of the project, identify areas for improvement, and ensure accountability.
