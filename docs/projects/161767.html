Project Summary

The UNICEF Child Protection project, in line with the Minimum Standards for Child Protection and Gender-Based Violence in Humanitarian Actions, will focus on strengthening access to quality preventive and responsive services that address the needs, rights and dignity of children and their caregivers’ in the conflict affected states of Adamawa, Borno and Yobe. This project will ensure that child protection in emergencies response prioritizes the most vulnerable and at-risk children and their families.
The project is designed with the understanding that investment in the well-being of conflict-affected children and women in North-east Nigeria through coordinated response is instrumental in protecting their rights and contributes to the achievement of SDG 16, in particular targeting 16.2.
Building on previous experiences and lessons learned, this project aims to prevent violence against children and women. The project will empower affected communities to break the culture of silence and bring about significant change in social norms through awareness raising. In addition, project actions will help strengthen the social service workforce to improve the quality and coordination of response. UNICEF will specifically target children affected by or at-risk of abuse, exploitation, neglect and violence against children. The general community, community leaders, families, parents and caregivers will also be targeted.
Results will be achieved by providing integrated child protection case management services for children affected by conflict including unaccompanied and separated children and delivering quality socio-economic reintegration services to children formerly associated with armed groups as well as other vulnerable children and their families.
Conflict-affected children will have access to community-based mental health and psychosocial support services (MHPSS) including and structured recreational activities and adolescents’ life skills education. The project will also contribute to strengthening capacity of service delivery to GBV survivors. Child survivors of GBV will in addition have access to comprehensive minimum life-saving GBV services including health, psychosocial and safety services.

1 - Needs

After ten years of hostilities between the Nigerian Security Forces and non-state armed groups (NSAGs) the humanitarian crisis in North-east Nigeria continues. Civilians bear the brunt of a conflict that has resulted in widespread displacement, lack of protection, destroyed infrastructure and collapsed basic services. More specifically, the 2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview estimates that the protection and well-being of 1.8 million children and adolescents (0.96 million girls and 0.84 million boys) in the north-east remain at-risk – mainly due to the complex and protracted conflict, with greater vulnerabilities anticipated in inaccessible areas. Community resilience to protect children has been weakened, resulting in children and families adopting negative coping mechanisms. Most children have been exposed to one or more of the following: violence, loss of friends, family members and neighbours, accumulated stress, deterioration in living conditions, inability to provide for one’s self and family, increased military presence, divisions in societies, and lack of access to services. It is estimated that over 6,500 children have been separated from their families and are either unaccompanied or separated. These experiences have a significant impact on the psychosocial wellbeing and development of children and have both immediate and long-term consequences for children, families and communities. They can also hamper their ability to access other services, such as education, further damaging their life chances. These children are in need of psychosocial support to build their resilience, help them to recover from their experiences and rebuild their lives. A smaller percentage of children suffer acute levels of distress and/or mental health issues which require more specialized psychological or psychiatric interventions. In addition to the general needs of children impacted by the conflict, there are thousands of girls and boys have been subjected to grave violations of their rights: they have been recruited, used, abducted or held by NSAG during which time they may have witnessed, experienced and sometimes participated in physical and sexual violence. At least 4,000 children have been recruited and used by NSAGs in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe. There is an urgent need to provide community-based reintegration services to children and youth formerly associated with armed groups including survivors of conflict-related sexual violence.

2 - Activities

UNICEF will implement the following activities in close collaboration with the state governments, international and national non-governmental partners as well as traditional, religious and community-based networks: Key activities under Sector Strategic Objective 1 Identification, regular follow-up and service provision for children at-risk of, or affected by, protection concerns, including case management for former CAAG, UASC, and GBV cases; Children deprived of parental care are provided with timely, reliable and safe alternative care arrangements with prioritization of family-based solutions through identification, assessment and support to foster parents including supervision during the provision of foster care;  Family tracing and reunification for unaccompanied and separated children; Key activities under Sector Strategic Objective 2 Psychosocial services for children and caregivers of vulnerable children through structured and age-specific creative, recreational and social activities for children and adolescents; Educate caregivers through positive parenting lessons for caregivers of vulnerable children or vulnerable caregivers; Raise awareness at community level on child protection with specific focus on relevant child protection issues including information sharing on gender-based violence (GBV) and protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA);Key activities under Sector Strategic Objective 3 Case management will be conducted for all girls, boys and young people formerly associated with armed groups, UASC and GBV cases and this will include documentation, follow-up and family tracing and reunification.  Provision of center-based interim care services for girls, boys and young people formerly associated with armed groups; Community-based economic reintegration support for girls, boys and youth formerly associated with armed groups and other vulnerable children and youth within the community; Community-based social reintegration support for girls, boys and youth formerly associated with armed groups and other vulnerable children and youth within the community; Support to girls, boys and young people formerly associated with armed groups and other vulnerable children and youth within the community to access education; Awareness raising for children and community members on the grave violations against children including Explosive Ordnance Risk Education with a focus on preventative actions; A system developed to identify and report on grave rights violations as described under the Security Council Resolution 1612 mechanism; Use of verified and documented information to undertake advocacy with key stakeholders for timely release of children from administrative custody;  Provide confidential and dignified professional case management services for GBV survivors (clinical management of GBV, mental health and psychosocial support) and Strengthen existing or establish functional community-based complaint mechanisms for SEA reporting and survivor assistance.
