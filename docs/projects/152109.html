Project Summary

In its ninth year, the crisis in Northeast (NE) Nigeria shows no sign of abating. Despite continued scale up in humanitarian assistance, significant gaps remain, as thousands of people affected by the conflict have not been reached. The WASH sector estimates that 3.4 million people urgently need sustained, equitable and safe access of appropriate WASH services. This includes 1.1 million IDPs, 664,860 in host communities, 867,233 returnees and 818,170 people in hard-to-reach areas.
To continue to support conflict-affected families and meet these humanitarian needs, CRS proposes WASH support for conflict-affected families in Borno State for 69,670 people (20,901 women and 22,294 girls, 12,541 men, 13,934 boys) through provision of Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) support. CRS proposes to work in Jere, Kaga, Magumeri and in Gubio which are part of the areas identified with high and medium severity status. This will be done through rehabilitation of boreholes, chlorination of water supplies, construction of new sanitation facilities, rehabilitation of sanitation facilities, desludging of latrines, hygiene promotion interventions with distribution of WASH none food items (NFIs).
To minimize potential conflicts among groups and encourage communal cohesion CRS will continue to target vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host populations, selecting those presenting the greatest needs. During the first phase of the project, CRS supported 70% IDPs, 20% returnees, and 10% host community households

Needs

In its ninth year, the crisis in Northeast (NE) Nigeria shows no sign of abating. Despite continued scale up in humanitarian assistance, significant gaps remain, as thousands of people affected by the conflict have not been reached. The WASH sector estimates that 3.4 million people urgently need sustained, equitable and safe access of appropriate WASH services. This includes 1.1 million IDPs, 664,860 in host communities, 867,233 returnees and 818,170 people in hard-to-reach areas. The recurrence of cholera outbreak in the Northeast in 2018 has had an impact on the vulnerable population already at risk and thus a need to put in place preventive mechanisms and mitigation against future outbreaks through appropriate interventions. According to Borno State Situation Report dated 24th October 2018, the cases reported were 5350 with 73 associated deaths. Children, women, girls and the elderly are particularly at risk. Due to gender roles, women and girls are primarily responsible for activities such as cleaning latrines, fetching and handling untreated water, caring for the sick and preparing food. While the elderly are often unable to seek support and treatment, children - and particularly malnourished children - are more susceptible, with two- to four-year-olds having the highest rates of infection. Open defecation rates are as high as 75%, while CRS’s rapid WASH needs assessment found extremely low knowledge of key hygiene practices (hand washing, safe water storage, and open defecation). The situation is compounded by the lack of purchasing power for hygiene items such as hand washing soap. Overcrowding, coupled with poor hygiene practices, is fueling communicable diseases as evident from a number of children with skin problems. Furthermore, the practice of open defecation poses security and dignity concerns particularly for women and girls. Women and girls expressed in FGDs and to program staff that they are typically uncomfortable leaving the privacy and security of their compound to access public latrines or bathing spaces. As a result, many resort to the use of unsafe shallow pit latrines close to their shelters. In Gubio, while most compounds have access to a latrine, these are mostly non-improved pit latrines and insufficient in numbers. No public bathing places exist. In Magumeri, WASH needs are mostly unmet, with sanitation infrastructure almost entirely lacking from IDP settlements. Limited water supply, poor sanitation and hygiene in over-congested settlements, leading to water-borne illnesses. The already existing facilities are put under pressure from the protracted nature of the crisis multiple displacements from hard to reach areas with pregnant women, children under five and lactating mothers being most affected. Sand storms and flooding have affected water supply systems where solar panels were brown off, sanitation facilities super structures brown off and water sources contaminated with some communities fetching water from surface pools.

Activities

Construct 350 latrines and 200 bathing spaces (sex segregated) either in single units or in blocks supporting, 6000 people to access improved sanitation. Hand washing stations outside all constructed latrines, 3,200 families to safely continue to use latrines by supporting the desludging of up to 700 communal/shared latrines and decommissioning 100 latrines used by IDPs and vulnerable host Rehabilitation of water supply 11 water points to support 18,721 individuals (3,742 HHs) to access potable water through the rehabilitation and re-construction of nonfunctional water points. Where possible, boreholes will be equipped with hybrid powered systems. These systems will be installed in places where there is no alternative back up such as a hand pump and the tank storage capacity is not able to meet excess water demand. All water distribution systems will be fitted with flow meters to measure the amount of water supplied per day.Operation and maintenance will be carried out on all existing and new water supply systems constructed This will include daily chlorination of all CRS managed water points by committee members, monitoring of Free Residual Chlorine (FRC) levels at water collection points and at HH level and regular maintenance to ensure continuous functionality and sustainability of the systems. Aquatabs will be distributed to HH fetching water from handpumps and those buying unchlorinated water from water vendors.Establishment/ revitalization of water management committees (WMC): Water management committees will be established or revitalized, to support the running of water supply systems. The committees will consist of 50%-50% men to women ratio and will be trained on make minor repairs, simple operation and maintenance, hygiene promotion, community mobilization and conflict resolution, maintenance fund management and basic book-keeping.Hygiene promotion will target behaviors around safe disposal of feces including those of children, the 5 critical times of handwashing with soap or ash, water safety, food safety, flies and rodent control and environmental hygiene. Regular clean-up campaigns and messaging at water points will be conducted regularly. Hygiene promotion will be monitored through spot checks on message uptake and quarterly surveys will be carried out to track behavioral trends. CRS will pre-position cholera supplies and carryout cholera prevention and response activities in all locations with a focus on hotspots and will coordinate with key stakeholders, including relevant government ministries responding to the outbreak,Through a voucher system, 2,200 families will be able to purchase articles such as jerry cans, multipurpose soap, torch, potties for kids and those with physical challenges. Water containers and soap will form part of the compulsory items to increase water collection and storage capacity of the HH and improve personal hygiene. Based on best practices, lessons learned, and Cluster minimum standards, CRS has integrated a standalone, locally appropriate female hygiene kit(FHK), in response to women’s and girls’ request for these items. To this end, CRS will distribute FHK to 3,000 women and girls of reproductive age.
