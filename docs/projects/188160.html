Project Summary

With an estimated 12.4 million people in need of humanitarian health services this year, the already-disrupted health system in Syria remains under tremendous strain from concurrent emergencies: the ongoing crisis, the COVID-19 pandemic and the worsening economic situation in the country, all of which pose increased risks to health. At the start of 2021, the Health Sector remains focused on three response priorities defined through health system, monitoring and community feedback. The IRC will deliver comprehensive lifesaving primary health care services and support to secondary level health facilities through direct and partner implementation. The IRC health
services will reach an estimated target 132,900 vulnerable conflict-affected individuals in Raqqa, Hasakeh and Deir ezZor through 6 health care interventions including 4 static primary health care clinics (four will deliver BEmONC services), 5 mobile medical units (MMU), community health outreach and two COVID treatment centres. The targeted location includes 2 health centers in DreEzZor governorate 8 in Raqqa governorate and 9 in Hassakeh. The IRC will work in 2 static health facilities in Der Al-Zor to provide primary health care services via partners relief international in Al-Raqqa and Der Al-Zor. The static health faculties with BEMONC activities in Der Al-Zor will be supported by the Local health authoritysecondary health care (hospitals) support will be provided via partners in Al-Raqqa, Al-Hasaka and Der-Alzor, 2 MMUs in Al-Hasaka (Derek MMU, and Al-Sor MMU in Al-Hasaka Local health authority. The MMUs support will be direct support via IRC. The two isolation centers in Al-Hasaka and Qamchli will be supported by partners with local ministry of health. The IRC will be scaling nutrition health services to include extra areas as Raqqa PHCC, and MMU units in Der Alzor rural areas (Shagra). The IRC primary health care intervention integrates community health and reproductive health services and they are provided in line with the essential health service package for northern Syria and WHO guidelines for Syria. This includes prevention and management of communicable and non-communicable diseases (NCD)s, integrated management of childhood illnesses (IMCI), reproductive, maternal and child health, clinical care for sexual assault survivors (CCSAS), provision of pharmacological and nonpharmacological mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), health education and risk communication and community engagement (RCCE), and COVID-19 risk mitigation measures with strict adherence to standard infection prevention and control measures (IPC). The IRC will also continue support with COVID-isolation units and its work with implementing partners. The IRC will adopt two referral mechanisms: Internal NGO intersectoral pathway (Health- Protection-ERD), at the health facilities level between the health components (Health- MHPSS- Reproductive health-community health) and external referral pathway from the MMU and static clinics to secondary health care centers. The referral between the IRC component is tracked and documented through referral tracking tools and focal points from each component who are following the case through regular meeting. For the external referrals, the IRC uses the ambulances to transport the beneficiaries to the secondary health care and receive documented feedback about the case through accompanying medical personnel. The IRC will also contribute to disease surveillance through systematic collection, analysis and reporting of health data and weekly reporting through the Early Warning and Alert Reporting System (EWARS) to inform and support outbreak preparedness and response. In light of the COVID-19 pandemic, the IRC will aim to increase support to COVID-19 isolation units and integrate its screening, triage, and referral protocols
throughout its health interventions. The IRC will expand (RCCE) across its health programming.

4 - Needs

Acute shortages of health care staff and functioning health care facilities, Unsafe water and poor hygiene practices among displaced people in shelters increase the risk of water- and foodborne diseases. Unvaccinated children, Increasing incidence of communicable diseases, Sub-optimal referral services, inadequate antenatal care services, Lack of mental health and psychosocial support services for both children and adults2 are key barriers to health access, particularly for non-communicable diseases which account for 45% of all deaths. COVID- 19 has put additional stress to coordinate, prepare and respond to outbreaks while continuing routine primary health care. Women of reproductive age and children under five make up more than 35% of the population, with ongoing disruptions or access issues for basic SRH services. With 498,480 women expected to be pregnant in 20213 the gaps in BEmONC & CEmONC services will have devastating impacts on maternal and neonatal outcomes. There is a high incidence of mental disorders among NES communities. Psychiatric disorders range from mild to severe, as are PTSD, depression, anxiety, psychosis, high number / high incidence of convulsions and epilepsy in all age groups and genders. Out of 16 public hospitals, only 1 is fully functioning. No district in NES meets the emergency threshold of at least ten beds per 10,000 people nor the IASC standard of 1 hospital per 250,000 people. There is great need for medicine, PPE and supplies.

5 - Implementation of activities

The IRC will deliver Primary health care across Ar-Raqqa, Al Hasakeh, and Deir-ez-Zor by static PHCCs, MMUs and local partners with essential health services to WHO guidelines. The IRC will integrate primary, reproductive, MHPSS and community health across service delivery points to ensure curative and preventive services and continue to build on its mhGAP MHPSS services. Each facility will be equipped with a MHPSS counsellor, psychoeducation, psychotropics by psychiatrists, and referrals all conducted to IASC standards. IPC and quality assessments conducted at facilities with refresher training for staff. The IRC will support 24/7 BEmONC services and work with partners to create a network for BEmONCs centers linked to CEmONC facilities. CCSAS services will be provided for GBV survivors with the existing protection programs. Provision of life saving pharmaceuticals, PPE, and medical supplies will be provided to all supported facilities. The IRC has an internal referral system between health, integrated protection and ERD for referral follow up. Ambulance services will be provided to support external referrals and followed up. Primary and secondary health inter-sectoral and between service referrals will be provided. Will provide secondary inpatient pediatric care services delivered in Al Hol camp and support 2 COVID-19 isolation units. The IRC MEAL implements spot checks, data audits, data review and satisfaction surveys with beneficiaries to maintain conditions.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Service delivery during security situations: Mobile services in temporary locations while facilities are evacuated and relocated. Risk: Community stigmatization and discrimination of children, survivors of sexual violence receiving specialized services such as case management or health services Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigation: Use community acceptance strategy, including non-specialized services as entry-points and trust-building. Ensure specialized services are properly messaged to community. Embed specialized services in broader service-provision to de-stigmatize, using confidential and discrete spaces. MHPSS: There will be awareness sessions and psychoeducation on MH conditions and services, to reduce stigmatization and support awareness of specialized services. Risk: Access barriers due to physical impediments as well as access restrictions Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation: Conduct inclusion analysis, deploy MMUs to reach; deploy community outreach teams to support referrals and integrate health service delivery. Risk: COVID-19 and impact on health and wellbeing. Likelihood: High, High mitigation: will work with its health facilities to create a safe and clean environment at all health facilities-- IPC control measures and PPE adherence for staff and clients access the health facilities. Will conduct screening and triage to ensure clients do not have nosocomial infections of COVID-19. The MHPSS services will be available to reduce stress and anxiety.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk of sexual exploitation and abuse and sexual harassment of women and girls’ clients by staff and volunteers. Likelihood: Medium/Low Impact: Very High Mitigation: IEC materials for beneficiaries explaining reporting channels for beneficiaries to report PSEA and ASH. The IRC adheres to a strong internal code of conduct related to prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) andsafeguarding beneficiaries, especially children, as well as a safe environment for reporting that allows for anonymity. Each year, all IRC staff attend a full day IRC Way Code of Conduct Training that includes discussions on The IRC’s commitments to beneficiaries and The IRC’s zero tolerance for sexual misconduct. NGO will work with partners utilizing their existing beneficiary mechanisms to establish common standards for beneficiary feedback and ensure protection from sexual exploitationand abuse and accountability to beneficiaries and other stakeholders. This will include capacity building activities with partners to reach these standards, as necessary. The IRC is a member of PSEA networks inside Syria and provides regular updates on the number of staff receiving PSEA training and number ofcomplaints received through feedback and complaint channels.
