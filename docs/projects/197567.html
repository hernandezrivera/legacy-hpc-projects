Project Summary

Infectious diseases are major causes of high morbidity and mortality in humanitarian crises. In 2021, Syria experienced several major outbreaks from emerging and re-emerging diseases, including COVID-19, acute diarrhea, and Leishmaniasis. Also, the current outbreak of COVID-19, which reported 50,674 cases and 2,944 deaths (a high case fatality rate (CFR) of 5.8%) as of 15 January 2022, remains active and has been threatening the country.
The principal risk factors contributing to the emergence and rapid spread of epidemic diseases in Syria include acute and protracted humanitarian emergencies resulting in fragile health systems, increased population mobility, climate change and draught, environmental deterioration, weak surveillance and limited laboratory diagnostic capacity, and decline in affordable health services.
These epidemic threats, compounded by protracted complex emergencies, remain potentially devastating to development in Syria, through decline the economic situation, increase poverty, increase avoidable medical costs, loss of manpower due to immigration, and difficulties in access to health services. The need to prevent, quickly detect and respond to any infectious disease outbreaks that pose persistent threats to global health security remains a national priority and requires proper preparedness and effective surveillance system in order to deliver effective and systematic response to health emergencies.
The Early Warning and Response System was established in response to the Syrian Crisis, and EWARN in NWS, with the objective of addressing the increased potential for communicable disease outbreaks. Although EWARS has become well established in the country and had largely replaced the main national routine surveillance system, however, new concerns about the spread of infectious diseases, especially emerging diseases such as COVID-19 or pandemic influenza, have emphasis the importance of enhancing the early warning and rapid response surveillance system in addition to the existing routine surveillance system and build integrated surveillance system.
This project’s aim is to reduce morbidity and mortality due to preventable infectious diseases among general and vulnerable populations (IDPs, refugees and host communities) by enhancing emergency preparedness and response capacities in Syria.
Specific objectives:
1. Enhance surveillance system for timely detection and responses to outbreaks and improve the availability of descriptive disease epidemiology data, outbreak early detection and response, provide data required for assessment of disease prevention and control interventions, in addition to improve the information system of surveillance by using a web-based interface with integration of other vertical surveillance systems to detect unusual events that might signal an outbreak.
2. Enhance the country’s capacities for timely laboratory confirmation, as outlined in the international health regulations (2005), and to facilitate the transfer of safe and appropriate diagnostic technologies, practices and training to laboratories in the zones of emergence of these diseases.
3. Support prevention and control of epidemic prone diseases through the provision of lifesaving medicines and vaccines for effective case management especially for children and women
4. Strengthening multisectoral health emergencies and outbreak response coordination and community engagement in the surveillance and response to disease outbreaks.
The project will be implemented through close coordination and consultation with MoH, health directorates at the governorates and district levels, as well as other health partners and NGOs. the project will also contribute to strengthen the capacity of health partners through capacity building and technical support activities for surveillance and lab officers. and will ensure the availability of reliable disease surveillance data, and secure treatment and case management of Infectious disease

3 - Needs

The continued humanitarian emergency along with COVID-19 pandemic and the economic crisis in Syria have severely disrupted the country’s health system. Low immunization coverage due to limited access to health care services adds to vulnerability, especially for women, children, and displaced populations; measles cases, Leishmaniasis, and acute diarrhea outbreaks have continued to be reported. Currently disease surveillance in Syria is managed by early warning surveillance system. This system requires transitioning in order to establish a new functional integrated routine disease surveillance system for notifiable diseases as the country starts to recover from conflict. In 2021, The main health concerns in Syria included: COVID-19, water borne diseases, measles, and leishmaniasis. Laboratory capacity in Syria is still one of main challenges for confirmation of the outbreaks. public health laboratories are no longer functioning in most governorates. Only the Central Public Health Laboratory in Damascus is capable of testing for cholera, measles & bacterial meningitis. Moreover, laboratories experience shortage in reagents and supplies, and there is no specific budget assigned by MoH for public health laboratory activities. In NWS,. WHO’s support for response measures included COVID-19 interventions, provision of essential medicines, financial support for investigation visits and collection and transport of specimens, raising awareness, and providing training.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. There is a protection risk for surveillance workers who may be exposed to disease infection, physical security/ violence during data collection from EWARS sentinel sites and investigations of alerts, including laboratory specimen collection in insecure areas. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium Mitigation measures: a) Assess and analyze the security situation of the concerned areas before planning field visits/investigations, b) Ensure the use of secured transportation routes and vehicles for field investigations, c) Build relations with local communities to increase acceptance of humanitarian workers, d) support the surveillance and rapid response teams with needed personal protective equipment PPE. 2. Disclosure/mishandling of patient names and information leads to stigma and discrimination against patient/family/community. Likelihood: low. Impact: Medium. Mitigation measure: use unique codes for cases to ensure the confidently of data, training for RRTs. 3. Nature of RRTs/lab workers jobs could lead to stigma/discrimination. Likelihood: low. Impact: Medium. Mitigation measure: engagement of community leaderships to advocate the tasks of RRTs. 4. Relatedly, for RRTs and laboratory staff: burn out/stress. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation measure: Increase the number of trained RRT and lab personnel, activate the RRTs at district level, involvement of community and health partners In surveillance activities mainly In hard to reach areas.

- Difficulties in conducting verification of alerts and case investigations, including specimen collection and referral for laboratory analysis, mainly in hard-to-reach areas.
