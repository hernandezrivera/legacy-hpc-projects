Project Summary

Medair’s proposed intervention will provide life-saving and sustainable shelter interventions at household-level to 1,650 target beneficiaries (excluding overlap) by providing rehabilitation of housing for vulnerable conflict affected households and additional 165 upgrades for People Living With Mobility Impairment (PLWMI). The intervention builds on the experience, expertise and relationships from Medair’s previous project cycles to reach the most vulnerable and in-need, with protection mainstreaming and sustainability at the core of its programme.

Needs

There has been widespread damage to homes in many parts of Medair’s target governorates, i.e. in Deir-ez-Zor city more than two thirds of the apartments are damaged and >18% in Aleppo. Medair’s previous house-to-house assessments in newly accessible areas such as Dar’a identified households living in damaged or makeshift housing including tents. There has been a shift in priority from MoLAE and the shelter cluster during the past 12 months; from shelter upgrades to approvals-to-implement granted only for owner and tenant-occupied rehabilitations. Despite serious concerns, identifying and addressing HLP issues is a challenge: beneficiary lists (with correct documentation only) require pre-approval by authorities, and a MOFA directive (August 2018) specifically prohibits INGOs any direct communication with beneficiaries related to legal assistance and counselling. At the same time, other protection issues exist even for households without HLP issues. Medair’s most recent assessment in Dar’a shows 99% HHs with at least 3 household vulnerabilities which include at least one of 7 targeted for protection concerns: disability, chronic illness, elderly, FHH, PLW, CU5, > 3 children under 12 years. The lack of adequate shelter (particularly locked doors or adequate lighting) potentially poses both privacy and safety issues for women and children who are more vulnerable to the threat of violence or abuse. For the disabled and the elderly, inadequate hygiene and sanitation facilities, lack of shelter with adequate insulation, or lack of appropriate adaptations such as handrails or ramps result in the loss of independence and the need for help for even the most basic activities, posing dignity issues, and, as well, the risk of other health concerns. Medair will therefore target vulnerable households for rehabilitation (with special attention to those households with a PLWMI or elderly.

Implementation of activities

Medair will carry out 330 shelter rehabilitations (categorized “green” as structurally sound) as per sector recommendations, responding particularly to the protection needs of the most vulnerable. This will include adaptations for up to 33 household with members with PLWMI and the elderly. Detailed technical assessments help tailor the intervention according to protection needs including, but not limited to, adequate lighting and lockable doors and windows, modification of existing sanitation facilities, widening doorways and access ramps and paths. To implement these activities, Medair will work through local contractors, supervised by Medair recruited site engineers on a daily basis with oversight of Infrastructure Officers. Technical input will be provided by a National Technical Advisor and Global Advisor (based in Switzerland) to ensure compliance to sector and international guidelines as well as quality according to Medair’s international standards.
