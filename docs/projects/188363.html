Project Summary

The problem to be addressed is that the livelihoods of communities are being negatively affected by a combination of weakened local currency, the knock-on effects of the C-19 pandemic, a poor agricultural season, climate change, and water crisis impacts. To reduce the negative impact on the affected population, on markets and services, a resilience portfolio has to be adopted through a variety of key Livelihood Economic Recovery interventions that will assist to make the vulnerable household more self-reliant and reinforce their access to livelihood opportunities. Taking into account the real protection needs of women, girls and GBV survivors through merged them within the livelihood's intervention through referral mechanism.
The project targets 1,400 HHs (8,400 individuals) to reinforce and enhance better access to income-generating opportunities and to have community resilience strengthened. 300 farmers access ways affected by years-long conflict, will be slightly rehabilitated to improve access to farmlands. 600 women with previous experience will provide with SMEs and skills training, then deliver cash grants. Moreover,500 CFW workers (semi-skilled & Unskilled) will implement activities related to rehabilitation of the access ways, public infrastructures such as public bakeries, irrigation canals, water, sewage, and drainage services. CFW can foster social cohesion and community ownership in addition to supporting markets through communicating clearly how the CFW benefits the whole community, even if there are more IDP participants, Hire skilled workers from the community as CFW supervisors and trainers and pay them at labor market wage rates commensurate to their expertise, Consider incorporating into the CFW design apprenticeship schemes targeted to the local youth among the most vulnerable resident HHs, Conduct town hall meetings as part of community engagement, circulate key messages through pamphlets, posters, social media, mobile messages.

4 - Needs

MEAL team carried out a Household Economic Profile in Nov 2021. A total of 100 household surveys were conducted. The assessment was designed to discover the predominant prior livelihoods profiles, skills, interests, and limitations of the targeted populations and how the ongoing conflict has changed them. In summary, the following were the key findings;  The study reveals the continued degradation of the economic situation. The economic situation significantly worsened.  Due to the scarcity of available jobs and the harsh economic climate, respondents predominantly reported taking up poorly-paid and irregular daily-wage employment.  Cash for work scheme is preferred, where beneficiaries are linked with the dominant agricultural value chains activities, and so create short-term work opportunities.  Focusing on building the resilience of vulnerable people with previous experience by strengthening their livelihoods through small business grants. Cash modality allows for a more normalized market environment and protects beneficiary dignity.

5 - Implementation of activities

The action will provide structured support to 600 SMEs in the target locations. For many who do not have access to land or livestock assets. For those who have had to relocate and have left behind assets, or where there is increased demand for their services but do not have the ability to restart due to a loss of capital, the action will provide conditional cash transfers (CTP). 4 Business training will be delivered to BNFs under C-19 precautions which will particularly focus on (a) basic profit/loss accounting and (b) marketing and (c) preparation of the monthly turnover report. SR will conduct 1 SME training, where all the participants from the VT will be invited to attend SME training for 5 days which is provided to enable BNFs to prepare business plans that include all aspects of managing the expansion or establishment of small businesses.Also, provide temporary employment (CFW) to 550 HHs run by unskilled/semi-skilled workers, who will be assigned to workgroups. CFW will include green water harvesting techniques, agro-access road rehab, and primary irrigation canals. 500 unskilled workers, employed for 20 days, supported by 50 semi-skilled (agro & construction technicians). Labor wages be harmonized with other agencies and the local market rate and are anticipated to be 5-8 USD per day. coordination and harmonization, especially at the area level with other aid providers via clusters gap analysis figures and associated communications channels (skype groups).

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Women and girls being excluded from the program design, implementation, and evaluation of activities. Mitigation: As per our protection mainstreaming policy needs of women and girls are constantly considered, with specific dedicated staff, questions, and action/responses targeting the most vulnerable and the gender sensitivity. Risk: Boys and girls (under 18 years) are excluded from the design, implementation, and evaluation of activities. Mitigation: Needs of boys and girls are constantly considered, with specifically dedicated staff (part of SR child protection mobile team) with adapted assessment tools and action/response targeting the young and the gender sensitivity. The CRM is adapted for under 18 years, ensuring the feasibility of a safe and secure feedback mechanism, as well as adapted follow-up to address the issues. Risk: Older persons and persons with a disability (PWD) are part of the design, implementation, and evaluation of activities. Mitigation: Program staff is regularly trained in inclusion and adequate program response tailored-made for PWD. Risk: Increased sensitivity between host communities and IDPs. Mitigation: Based on a community approach, social activities are conducted based on communities’ initiatives, ensuring inclusive participation of all groups. SR ensures the involvement between the host community and internally displaced persons once the project starts in order to mitigate any tension within the community.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Clear standards of conduct, technical and behavioral competencies for all staff, consultants: SR will ensure that staff have the technical knowledge and skills, and behavioral competencies to deliver commitments to affected population, and understand what accountability looks like in practice, and to abide by SR’s Code of Conduct CoC which includes: Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, Prevention of Conflicts of Interest and Fraud, and Child Protection. All staff members are obliged to follow SR’s CoC as outlined in their job contracts and measures will be taken should they be found in breach of it. However, malicious complaints will be identified as such and our staff protected from false allegations.SR will consulate a PSEA field officer who will make appropriate recommendations to management on enhancing prevention strategies. This could include collecting and analyzing information on actual/potential risk factors for vulnerability to sexual exploitation and abuse and elaborating measures to address them. Make sure that PSEA materials and posters are visible and in place in all locations. Receive complaints, reports, and questions about alleged acts of SEA committed by the organization’s personnel. And track all PSEA-related activities and ensure that all information on PSEA-related activities is shared with relevant persons in the Organization
