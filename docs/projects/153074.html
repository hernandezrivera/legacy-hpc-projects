Project Summary

Oxfam’s immediate objectives are to save lives, meet the immediate food needs and protect the livelihoods of the most vulnerable groups of women, men, boys and girls in the targeted counties.
Extreme food insecurity persists throughout South Sudan and increased humanitarian assistance is needed to prevent the loss of lives and their livelihoods. An estimated 1.7 million people remain internally displaced in South Sudan, many of whom have been displaced more than once. Another 2.5 million people have fled South Sudan to neighbouring countries since December 2013.
A collapse in national economy, reduced crop production and dependence on imports seriously deteriorate people’s ability towards food security round the year, putting millions of lives at risk. An estimates 6.1 million people are at risk of severe food insecurity during last lean season according to Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) report of September 2018.
Most of the targeted counties in Jonglei are at critical stages regarding food insecurity like Uror (IPC phase 3!), Nyirol (IPC phase 3!), Pibor (IPC phase 4) and Twic East (IPC phase 4) during January – March 2018. On the other hand, Malakal under Upper Nile state is a bit instable in terms of population movement from PoC to Malakal town. The population movement is also a critical state in Wau under Western Bahr el Ghazal. Moreover; there is a potential risk of catastrophe in Pibor (20% population which is 20,000 people) and Wau (0.2% that means 1000 people) from January -March 2019.
The driving forces are a below average rainfall, constrains of humanitarian assistance delivery and high possibility of continued armed conflict, and upcoming dry season from January -March 2019 it is highly likely of increased people being classified under IPC 4. Additionally, there has been infestation of crops by fall army worm across the entire region, which will contribute to low yields for the harvest this year. Households are facing significant constraints to meeting their basic food needs. Prices remain extremely high and with limited income, most households are unable to purchase sufficient food from markets. Conflict has also prevented many households from engaging in typical livelihood activities, such as farming, lowering food available through own production. Households throughout the state are facing food consumption gaps. Levels of malnutrition are expected to decline in dry (January – March) and lean season (July -August) and remain at ‘Critical’ (GAM(WHZ)>15%) levels in many counties.
Oxfam has been working in Jonglei, Upper Nile, Western Bahr el Ghazal and Lake states for long time. A total of 55,000 people will be targeted through different means of Food Security & Livelihood interventions in Jonglei ( Uror, Nyirol, akobo, Pibor and Twic East), Upper Nile ( Malakal) ,and Western Bahr el Ghazal (Wau) and Lake states ( Cuiebet). The affected population are IDPs, returnees and host communities.
Based on the analysis of relevant assessment reports the basic interventions are General Food Distribution (GFD), Conditional food distribution through Food for Asset, unconditional and conditional Cash Transfer Initiatives, Seeds and tools provision either direct distribution or voucher programming, capacity building initiatives to improve livelihoods, increasing agricultural production linking with market to ensure food and nutrition security.

Needs

The food security situation in the targeted counties are very critical in terms of food consumption, dietary diversity, reduced coping strategies, level of hunger and livelihood coping strategies. The following are the plan as par contextual needs and organizational mandates: Without humanitarian assistance in Uror and Nyirol counties there is a high risk that the population may slip into catastrophe from October 2018 to March 2019. Therefore, Oxfam has been implementing a 60 days General Food Distribution (GFD) in Uror and Nyirol now; on the other hand a 90 days GFD is implementing in Akobo which is characterized with poor road network, revenge killings, cattle raids predominantly conducted by Murle armed youths from neighbouring Pibor county-Boma state. GFD will be continued for next year also in association with World Food programme in these counties in partnership with World Food Programme. Conditional General Food Distribution is also being included for next year. It will be mainly targeting beneficiaries at the household level using Payam level geographical targeting in the 16 Food Distribution Points of Greater Nyirol, Uror and Greater Akobo Counties. This is because the nature of works to be undertaken under the conditional GFD will be related to environmental health cleaning. It will also include awareness / Sensitization trainings which includes personal hygiene both at home/Individual level and waste management awareness. Agriculture activities will lead to increased local food production and this should eventually lead to reduced number of months’ households depend on food assistance and small skill constructions works (shelters at the GFD sites and pit latrines to be used by the beneficiaries during the GFD). In addition; there is a plan for recovery of livelihood through different initiatives in Nyirol and Akobo. Food Security situation in Pibor county is deteriorating day by day where the key drivers are continued internal conflicts, cattle raiding, flooding and economic shocks. Food Consumption and dietary diversities are very poor and people are adopting emergency coping mechanisms according FSNMS round 22. The GAM rate is 20.7%. Considering the huge needs to prevent the elevated risks of catastrophe (IPC phase 5); immediate and short terms interventions are being considered under Food Security and Livelihoods. In Malakal and Twic East there is a need of restoration of livelihood and there may be a risk of returnee’s influx which require appropriate recovery support. Oxfam’s plan is to continue livelihood and resilience program with a crisis modifier component. Wau county is classified under emergency (IPC phase - 4) in September and the forecast is same for October 2018 to March 2019. Approximately 1000 people are at catastrophe from September 2018 to March 2019. The Acute malnutrition rate is very critical found in August 2018 and the GAM is 14.7%. Therefore, immediate support of Cash through different means, seeds & tools support and income generating options together with training were proposed. Cuiebet is one of the counties in Lakes state affected by extreme critical levels of acute malnutrition and food insecurity because of market disruptions, high food prices, intercommunal conflict, irregular rain pattern and early onset of lean season. So, food assistance through FFA and GFD are some of the immediate options to save live, prevent further deterioration of the food insecurity situation, protect assets and improve the livelihood of vulnerable people.

Activities

The following are the proposed activities in the targeted counties from 1st January – 311st December 2019: Activity 1: Food Assistance (40% of the targeted population) – Provision of food to the most vulnerable people where it will cover both unconditional and conditional General Food Distribution (GFD). Oxfam has been implementing General Food Distribution in Uror, Nyrol and Akobo counties from 2014.Activity 2: Provision of food assistance to people through Cash Programming, cash voucher or commodity voucher food distribution (10% of the targeted population where 70% conditional and 30% unconditional). Unconditional transfers will be targeted at vulnerable households such as the elderly, child-headed households, chronically ill, disabled, or pregnant women.Activity 3: Cash for Work/Food for Assets/ Food for Literacy work (10% of the targeted population) will aim to develop community assets, e.g secondary dyke rehabilitation to control flood events with the environmental objective to combat land degradation and promotion of agricultural activitiesActivity 4: Livelihood promotion for on farm & off farm production (40% of the targeted population) - Promote cereal production, livestock and fishery for improved household own food productivity which is linked to improved household food consumption and/or sale of surplus.• Strengthening existing grassroots formal and informal extension service agents targeting 225 people.• Fishery training & support - Distribution of fishing nets, hooks and fish drying ovens to support people to support market-led fishery. • Horticultural training & support – Distribution of nutritious and high value vegetable crop seeds and farm tools to target people and support market-led vegetable production.• Cereal training & support – Distribution of cereal seeds and farm tools to people and support short-term cereal production• Goat training & support – Distribution of ready-to-mate goats to people and support short-term meat and milk production, promotion of women friendly off-farm income generating activities.• Establish market linkage between producer’s groups and traders for buying and selling their products. Across the activities 50% women's engagement will be ensured to meet their specific needs and rights. A bottom up community participation approach will be followed with a view to beneficiary section, program implementation, monitoring & evaluation as well.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Oxfam adheres to the humanitarian accountability principles (HAP) and internal accountability policies and procedures. Oxfam`s previous and ongoing projects implemented and ensured accountability to beneficiaries in the entire project cycle.As part of ensuring OXFAM’s accountability to the beneficiaries, basic information like the amount and types of items each beneficiary is receiving, the distribution place, time etc. are being shared to the targeted groups through banner, Project Implementation Committees, help desk at payam and Boma level, local media and megaphone. Community Protection Committees (CPC) and Project Management Committees (PMC) are also playing a key role in providing relevant information to the community people regarding their entitlement, rights and communication channel.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

All these messages are context specific and tailored for the particular group of people including gender and age groups. For unconditional support these messages are very important for beneficiaries targeting, taking extra care during distribution, and monitoring of outputs. In most cases, local languages are being used to disseminate these messages with a view to have better understanding. Banner is being used for wider sharing with local community, authority and other stakeholders.Oxfam has also identifying and training the Project Management Committees (both female and male) to conduct regular focus group discussions with the aim to identify contextual needs/problems at early stages, and to solve these on regular basis. The focus group will be conducted fortnightly during the first quarter of the project and the periodicity will be reviewed afterwards.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project will carefully consider the protection issue related with sexual exploitation and abuse through protection committee and its set up mechanism. The Protection Committees are the local key entity to gather and coordinate this issue with Oxfam and other stakeholders. Oxfam defines misconduct as sexual exploitation and abuse, harassment, fraud, aid diversion, bribery, or other malpractice in the workplace. We use the UN definitions of sexual harassment, sexual exploitation and sexual abuse. Oxfam has a zero-tolerance policy towards sexual exploitation, abuse, and harassment. Oxfam International has a number of global policies and tools that are implemented in all Oxfam countries, including the following: Code of Conduct; Policy on Whistleblowing and Complaints; Child Protection Policy; Antibullying and Harassment Policy; Aid Diversion and Abuse Policy; Sexual Diversity and Gender Identity Rights Policy, Common Approach to MEL and Social Accountability (CAMSA); Humanitarian Handbook (which highlights standards on protection, safe programming, Complaints and Feedback Mechanisms) etc.In November 2017, a dedicated Global Safeguarding Taskforce was set up to implement measures to prevent and investigate sexual misconduct across Oxfam, with an approach that is victim/survivor centred and fosters the right organizational culture and working environment. Oxfam has a worldwide network of safeguarding leads and focal points, based in countries where we work. Trained and committed staff are available who can receive disclosures, provide support, and take forward investigations and other responses. Oxfam South Sudan has a dedicated Safeguarding Global Team, Country Safeguarding Taskforce, Safeguarding Focal Points for Field site and distribution team. Oxfam has policies, procedures, guidelines and SOPs for ensuring reporting of any misconduct including safeguarding.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Oxfam has been working with existing Community Protection Committees (CPC) and or come up with new Project Management Committees (PMCs) comprising of a membership of 50% women and 50% men (not more than 10 people made up of beneficiaries’ representative). The CPCs/PMCs are providing community level engagement and coordination. The CPCs are being utilised as a channel for Communicating with Communities on themes such as beneficiary targeting, identification, verification, voluntary services, Complaints and Feedback Mechanisms (CFM)s. They are assisting together with Oxfam in organising, managing distributions, provide information on entitlements, rights, community protection, as well as influencing for local peace and security given the nature and complexity of conflict situation in areas of operations. The mandate for the committee is to link the teams to the community and vice-versa. They are also part of capacity building initiative to lead the community. Oxfam did consultation with 1600 Community Protection Committee members directly to know the needs of specific groups to design for next year. The success of the mentioned modalities is also largely depending on the feedback received from different stakeholders, community engagement, enhanced community based mechanisms like CPCs/PMCs, and also beneficiaries understanding of the project. To reach out to the community, Oxfam conducts humanitarian negotiation and mediations with relevant stakeholders at different levels. Negotiation meetings are conducted with all key stakeholders in the community, community leaders i.e. chiefs, commissioners, local government. During each of the meetings adequate time allocated to respond to questions that the community leaders and beneficiaries may have in order to ensure that all are satisfied and knowledgeable about how the project will be conducted.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Oxfam has been ensuring open and clear accountability and transparency of programming through announcement of targeting criteria, program activities and use of community village committees. Discussion will also be made with the targeted community to set up agreed compliant and feedback mechanism and regular complaints and feedback will be conducted, referred to the relevant person in OXFAM and responded on time. Different modalities like setting complaint boxes, hot line and committees will be utilized to capture and addressing the complaints from the ground level on time. The accountability and complain mechanisms search to receive positive or negative feedback about what we are doing, to ask for information/ clarifications (about any possible topics such as FFA approach, targeting criteria, food ration, timing of distribution, quality of items, when we will visit their village whether we can help them with something else). Beneficiaries will be able to give us suggestions or raise complaints/things which could be done better.Toll Free line which beneficiaries use to call directly to WFP office (for Food Assistance Project) and the complaint/feedback is recorded and immediately forwarded to the person responsible for respond and resolution of the complaint received. All the complaints forwarded to the responsible person will be entered into a complaints data base and the status of each complain updated as closed or pending, depending on the resolution of the complaint. The number will be showed in banners and sign post located at the project site to ensure that it is accessed by all people.The information collected through the accountability and complaint mechanism will be analyzed in the Accountability Monthly Meeting (AMM) which will involve the field teams. From the AMM, an accountability report including recommendations and measures to improve the implementation will be shared with the key stakeholders.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

To check whether the distributed are utilized for the intended purposes, Oxfam GB will conduct quarterly PDM along with onsite distribution monitoring. The Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) exercise will help to gain information on project coverage, beneficiary satisfaction with the assistance provided, food security dynamics and utilization of the assistance. Household surveys, focus group discussion (FGD), key informant interviews (KIIs) will be conducted as part of the PDM exercise. Wherever possible, the final PDM reports will be shared with all key stakeholders. Key recommendations for future programming will be discussed and any immediate corrective action required will be taken. Qualitative data will be collected to measure the outcome level indicators using the most significant change (MSC) technique involving the local community. Story gathering process will be an on-going process that will run throughout the project life and then final selection and verification of the success story will take place in the last months of the project. Some of the stories will be included in the quarterly reports.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Oxfam applies ‘safe programming’ using the protection risk reduction approach (protection mainstreaming) in order to promote conflict sensitivity, Do No Harm, and actively contributing towards existing protection threats (deprivations, violence and coercion) in the area of operations. The project action will be informed by regular risk assessments; identification and mitigation of risks; supporting and being sensitive and inclusive to the most vulnerable people and or people with special needs; do information dissemination, awareness raising, and campaigns on rights, entitlements, services available and protection from sexual abuse.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

To maximize the use of limited resources, evidence based prioritisation of needs will be strengthened through weekly and quarterly needs analysis.Access barrier of different ethnic groups and its power relations will be assessed on regular basis as the context keep changing. According to the key findings; a proactive approach will be integrated into all interventions to address the barrier to access issue. Attention will be given to all groups of people considering gender, age and those with disabilities for meaningful, informed and effective participation. Emphasis will be put on reviewing the approaches in place including feedback mechanism to ensure two-way feedback between humanitarians and the affected populations and better understanding of how humanitarians are engaging communities and who they are engaging.Women groups will be placed in the leadership roles in the Distribution committees for decision making like Community Protection Committees, Project Management Committees.Information dissemination and awareness risings will be done to sensitize women, men, girls and boys on their rights, entitlements, services available-how, where and when to access those services (including SGBV services); humanitarian code of conduct; and protection from sexual exploitation and abuse as well as on other general misconduct.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project has been measuring physical and financial access of target beneficiaries to acquire items, food/cash. Based on favourable context the relevant approaches will be adopted to make safe accessibility of them. At the ground level agreeing a minimum package and common outcomes; better information sharing and prioritisation of needs to ensure cost effectiveness’ in reaching beneficiaries with limited resources for addressing barrier issue as well. Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Distance from the distribution centre, market, crop land and fishing ground are the considering factors to ensure safety of target beneficiaries. Cash transfer risk analysis is always being done to know whether this is feasible or need some precautionary measures.Market monitoring, understanding of financial infrastructure, ensuring an inclusive and appropriate targeting and information management will also be internalized. Emphasis will also be given on understanding potential security risks and engagement with communities. Strengthening community based protection mechanisms to be done through basic consultations with targeted communities and beneficiaries on principles of engagement, general safety, identification and layout of distribution sites, basic facilities (like drinking water, shade, and latrines; crowd control; special attention to vulnerable groups; participation and information dissemination; and complaints and feedback mechanisms). Some consultations will be based on existing Community Protection Committees (CPCs) in areas of operations and lessons learnt from previous interventions.
