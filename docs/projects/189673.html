Project Summary

Following the signing of Revitalised Agreement on the Resolution of Conflict in South Sudan (R-ARCSS) on September 12, 2018, citizens started returning to their homes unaware of dangers of unexploded remnants of war. MTT proposes to educate returnees, IDPs and host communities within the former Jonglei State about the dangers of the remnants of war and unexploded landmines. The risk education aims to protect civilians and humanitarian actors from lurking dangers in their immediate environments. The teams will focus on the two main approaches to reach people in Canal/Pigi and Twic East counties, with key EORE messages in all areas of operation. This will be through primary and secondary approaches. First will be the primary approaches where the most at-risk members of the community will be made aware of the lurking dangers in their day to day activities. The most at-risk members of the community are ordinary people living in or close to contaminated areas or other suspected hazardous areas. Secondly will be secondary approaches in which MTT will work closely with the most influential stakeholders of the community. These are opinion leaders who are agents of change in their communities. They include religious leaders, teachers, group leaders or representatives. They will use the availed EORE information to reach out to the community. The project will enhance the capacity of the community to identify and report any suspicious objects they will come across. Channels will be established to share the information with NMAA and UNMAS. Other stakeholders involved will be other humanitarian actors within Education, WASH, Nutrition, Child Protection and Health clusters. The project aims to reach the majority of the population with EORE.

1 - Needs

Canal/Pigi and Twic East have been battlegrounds for the civil war, both before and after independence of South Sudan. All along the two counties have been risky for the population. Civilians came across two land mines in Pigi town, one on January 25th 2018 and another on February 2nd 2019. They alerted the authorities who called G4S – the clearing group to clear them from the town. Fresh returnees need to be educated of the need to beware of unfamiliar objects they may come across in their day to day activities. The risks cut across all community members of all ages and genders.

2 - Project activities

• Team members provided with refreshers trained EORE in Twic East and Canal Counties, Jonglei State • Conduct safety briefings to 160 selected members of the community (80 men and 80 women) on mine and ERW safety. • Provide EORE sessions to at-risk populations (IDPs, Host and returnees) that will be (disaggregated by sex and age) benefiting from EORE activities • Collect data related to landmines and ERW through information gathering from the communities’ members and report to UNMAS• Provide regular updates on weekly and monthly progress and obstacle during the project to UNMAS and NMAA.
