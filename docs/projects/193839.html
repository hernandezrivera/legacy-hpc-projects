Project Summary

This project aims to address the urgent needs of specific group of patients in Gaza, those who suffer from complex Limb Reconstruction (LR) injuries as a result of the various Israeli offensives but also who are in need of a combination of plastics and vascular interventions.
This project is in rhythm with Strategic Objective #2 and Health Cluster Objective #1.
The complex limb injuries have placed high demands of the Ministry of Health to deal with, as these cases require years of treatment and are financially overwhelming. In addition, you cannot treat patients from an orthopedic perspective without having proper vascular and plastics interventions involved. The skills' set available in Gaza are only able to meet a tiny proportion of these numbers and complexity of injuries.
This project will aim to reduce the suffering of the trauma patients in need of LR, Plastics and Vascular interventions via bringing medical missions from the UK, tele-medicine support, supplies, and via the blood bank services through community campaigns in collaboration with our local partner PMRS. The intervention is coordinated with other key stakeholders serving LR in Gaza such as MoH, WHO, MSF-F, MSF-B, MDM Spain and others.

1 - Needs

This project is in rhythm with Strategic Objective #2 “The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL.” and Health Cluster Objective #1 “Ensure the availability of acceptable and quality essential healthcare services to vulnerable communities in oPt”. The nature of the protracted emergency in Gaza forces complex limb injuries on the patients. This is evident in all the assaults that were waged against Gaza. The complex nature of the injuries that Gaza patients face is a combination of Limb Reconstruction (LR) injuries, Vascular injuries, and need a plastics intervention too. All those three areas are scarce and in need of huge interventions to increase the local capacity and be able to respond to patients’ needs. All the aforementioned categories are characterized by large numbers of patients due to the protracted emergency, lack of specialised staff, and lack of supplies needed inside the venues of the MoH. If we are to talk about patients’ numbers first, according to MAP’s database (records of 2022), each LR outpatient clinic records an average of 30 patients (average 89 cases per month with almost 8.7% patients being females and 23% being children), with an average of 6 new patients and 8 Great March of Return (GMR) patients every clinic and this rhymes with HNO stating that there is at least an average of 50 patients monthly. Medical opinion indicates that the nature of the injuries that the LR unit sees requires plastics interventions and at the early stage, when stabilizing the wound and before becoming a LR case, vascular interventions are key. As for human capacity and skilled staff, LR services are lacking hugely. Currently, there is only one LR expert surgeon working at Naser’s unit and were he to leave or become unable to work, the service would cease. Furthermore, in the event of an escalation, the unit would be rapidly overwhelmed insufficient to meet the huge influx of patients. As for plastics, according to MAP’s most recent assessment done in 2022, there are only 13 plastic and burn doctors working in the two burn units. Only two of them have accredited Masters’ degrees in plastic and reconstructive surgery. This demonstrates a chronic lack of human resource capacity of plastic and reconstructive surgery in MoH facilities. In Gaza, there are only 0.7 plastic doctors per 100,000 citizens while there are 4 in Greece, 5 in Italy and 2 in Turkey for every 100,000 citizens.Regarding vascular capacity, comparing the number of doctors supporting VS in Gaza to international standards, it was found that US recommended 1.4 VS per 100,000 population, on the other hand, UK recommended 1 VS per 100,000 population. For Gaza if we assumed that there are 26 doctors working in VS departments (not necessarily VS specialists), regardless of their level of training, it would be found that the ratio is 1.3 doctor per 100,000 population. However, if we take into consideration only the VS specialists then the ratio will decrease to 0.6 per 100,000 (MAP’s vascular assessment, 2021). When it comes to other team members needed for the 3 sub-specialties, it can be fairly stated that majority of the services do not have dedicated nursing staff or other health categories needed (aside from the burns’ unit in Shifa and Naser’s LR unit). According to a study conducted by the Palestinian National Institute of Public Health in 2019, Palestine is believed to be short in the number of specialist doctors in the most critical specialties based on the leading causes of death and the top reasons for which patients get referred outside of governmental health facilities, vascular surgery was one of those specialties. For all specialties in Gaza including the three that are the subject of this concept, staff are lacking technical skills and their knowledge is outdated and all are in need of external support to be able to provide the health services needed inside Gaza. This is a feature of Gaza’s health care system with the blockade that has been imposed on it for more than 15 years now. When it comes to supplies, for LR to start, MAP and WHO both pour into the Nasser unit huge resources to keep the unit functional and operational. LR supplies are vastly expensive if we are to take into consideration quality of the service which is a must according to the health cluster objective #1. Yearly, the unit is supplied with instruments and supplies worth around 500,000 USD to maintain its services. As for Plastics and vascular, the situation is a bit the same. MoH suffers from huge gaps in supplies related to vascular and plastics surgery. While for the later, MAP and MSF-F are trying to bridge some of the gaps in plastic surgery supplies; vascular surgery is left alone, and MAP has started a procurement process worth 300,000 USD for the units in Gaza, but this will only serve and last for few months.. To add, all hospital services in Gaza are suffering from shortages in blood units. In a culture where blood donations are scarce and happen only when patients’ families are required to compensate the units that the patient will use; it is a must that community blood donation campaigns are supported and encouraged in Gaza. As per the MAP ongoing project with PMRS, the campaigns that we facilitate add up to 30% of the yearly supply that the hospitals in Gaza need. All the above reasons are compounding the suffering of patients in Gaza and are main causes of increasing the distress of the disenfranchised. According to the MSNA of 2022, 99.8% of households surveyed reported a barrier to care and to dig deeper, the main reasons for barriers to health included: the high costs associated with care, unavailability of treatment, and scarcity of medicines and supplies (76.8%, 22.1% and 19.4% respectively). These all confirm the assessment above.

2 - Activities

Overall, the activities of this project will target 784 unique patients:Patients already seen in 2022 and being followed up during local and mission clinics: 424 including (37 females, 99 children and 14 PWD)New patients entering the service: 360 (avg 6/wk * 60 clinics)Medical expats in addition to the local team will deliver approx. 650 consultations through the outpatient clinics and will conduct 145 complex surgeries. Furthermore, the educational component of the project will help build the capacity of 54 healthcare workers from across the health system of surgeons, nurses, and physios. (28 under LR, 10 plastics, and 16 vascular. The numbers include at least 1 female nurse at Nasser unit). It should be noted that MAP’s approach to work in niche health areas is always multi-disciplinary and focuses on all health practitioners inside the units that we target including but not limited to doctors, nurses, physio, radiologists, etc. As such, we bring multi-disciplinary teams from the UK to work alongside their local peers and MAP has as experience of almost 7 years in MDT approach. The activities are summarized as below:- Conduct 10-12 medical missions in LR, Vascular and Plastics, - Procure the needed items and supplies and donate these to targeted units in Gaza, - Conduct medical MDT 6-8 symposiums for 30 attendees of ortho/plastics/vascular related health care providers, - Provide online learning platforms for the orthopaedic/plastics residents in Gaza (35 residents), please note that MAP is currently establishing a residency programme in plastics in collaboration with the PMC and we are expecting to have 10 residents from multiple years join the programme), - MAP to follow up with the cases using EQ-5D-5L assessment form - Maintain the project’s database - Conduct bi-weekly online clinical discussions with MoH and other relevant partners to address 1-2 cases every meeting. 2*2*12 = 48 cases, - contract PMRS to deliver 14 blood donation campaigns per month, the contract with PMRS will include provision of supplies and awareness activities too. MAP has designed the project activities to meet the different needs of women, men, boys and girls. But MAP’s core MDT team that has been trained by our partner expats includes male and female physios that help in the examination of female patients and ensure their dignity. In addition, the visiting teams have a reasonable balance of male and female practitioners which takes into consideration cultural differences, social variances and related gender dynamics. At least every quarter, a pediatrician LR specialist examines the children’s cases. As for protection, please note that protection of data and beneficiaries has been one of main pillars to the work of MAP in Gaza. Extra care is taken into consideration treating patients’ data, soft copies are stored in an online safe place controlled by our London IT manager with access limited to few MAP staff only, hard copies are secured in metal cabinets with keys made to only two staff members, data is shared online without any unique identifiers such as ID or names but via unique codes, no data is taken from patients unless they provide full written consent and patients can even ask MAP to alter their names and not to publish photos. Needless to mention that the project ensures the full inclusion of people with disability especially that most of the limb reconstruction patients suffer one form of disability. MAP, with its’ partners, ensure that the ortho/plastics and vascular services are accessible and the place for consultation is adapted for Persons with Disabilities. Accountability:In terms of accountability to beneficiaries, please note that MAP has a dedicated team combined of in-country and over-seas staff dedicated to Beneficiary Feedback Mechanism and Accountability. Currently, MAP has a dedicated WhatsApp number (00970594151748) on a device monitored by the BFM focal point in Gaza, an e-mail (FeedbackGaza@map-uk.org) for those who wish to send e-mails to MAP, online surveys and feedback channels (such as this one – LINK – developed recently for the oPt-HF project we are currently implementing), and MAP has also a page on our website dedicated to feedback and complaints in both Arabic and English. The tools were chosen carefully to make sure that we reach as many people as possible regardless of the literacy level and also to make sure that there are secure ways of reporting without the feel of being exposed. The algorithm at the organisation has a triage system that makes sure that complaints are all handled in complete confidentiality but also ensuring that serious complaints would reach the highest levels of management in the organisation to examine and investigate.For this project in specific, we will use phone calls to receive feedback from CDS staff about the deliveries and any feedback they might have. We will also circulate a satisfaction survey that will be used to seek people’s feedback on the project. Just to give an example of how we take into consideration participation from patients, a FGD with patients was conducted in May 2022 to inform the patients that MAP is developing its own feedback channels, to consult with them about that and at the same time to collect what they think about the services provided. In summary, it can be concluded that participants were generally satisfied with the services they were provided, and even asked for the health staff to be involved in such future sessions.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

MAP is neutral and does not lean toward any group over the other in the context we live in. MAP is impartial, and we provide aid to those who need it regardless of any other consideration such as gender, age, religion, etc. MAP is an independent organisation and is not affected by commercial powers.
