Project Summary

Ravaging flooding in Kapoetas and its environs have affected at least 8,000 people as they continue to wreak havoc among vulnerable populations particularly women and children who are faced with deteriorated living conditions and heightened risks of water borne diseases and malaria; and likely resurgence of cholera, acute watery diarrhea, typhoid among others. This is in addition to submerged water points further contributing to few and overstretched available facilities, destroyed health facilities, shelters and cut off access to most vulnerable (South Sudan Floods Update, Sept 2019; OCHA Briefings, September 2019). Kapoeta, which is a medium ranked cholera hotspot with long term recurrence as per the recent Cholera Situation Analysis and Hotspot Mapping 2019, presents key health concerns which have been exacerbated by the current flooding.
This coupled with the projected deteriorating food insecurity early 2020 with disrupted livelihoods, depleted food stocks, inflation and livestock disease outbreaks and cyclical inter-communal violence, which are likely to further high malnutrition levels particularly among children under 5 years and pregnant women, coupled with poor access to safe water, hygiene and sanitation coverage (IPC, September 2019).
Majority of the population particularly women and girls are further exposed to cases of gender based violence and harassment in an effort to access water, sanitation among other services from distant places.
Humanitarian needs are projected to increase if no formidable political solution is obtained and likely increase in bureaucratic impediments. Pilgrims of Hope (PoH) is a National NGO that has been working in the Eastern Equatoria and proposes actions, which are in line with the WASH Cluster HRP 2020 strategy, to respond and support 16,000 beneficiaries (3000 men, 3000 women, 5000 boys and 5000 girls-includes people with special needs and youth) through WASH and Nutrition, GBV and Health linkages in Kapoeta South; WASH and Health in Kapoeta North. These initiatives will address and respond to high Global Acute Malnutrition rates, increasing cases of protection concerns particularly those affecting women and girls while accessing WASH services through GBV mitigation and prevention and control of water borne diseases such as cholera, acute watery diarrhoea, typhoid, eye infections.
PoH will in addition ensure appropriate cross cutting issues are mainstreamed by ensuring environmentally friendly approaches through trainings and sensitizations, engagement and involvement of all gender categories to promote gender equity, robust and participatory beneficiary feedback mechanism that will ensure Accountability to Affected Persons including advocating for protection of vulnerable people and minorities including people with disabilities, protection among others.

2 - Activities

• 60 dysfunctional boreholes and water points in key community areas including health facilities, OTP sites, GBV risk areas, schools and market centers are rehabilitated and maintained fully functional to ensure equitable access to safe and sufficient water to most vulnerable• Rehabilitation of 100 safe and lockable latrine facilities and equipping them with hand-washing facilities to enable practice safe, sanitary, dignified and gender appropriate. sanitation. • Provision of WASH NFIs, including MHM kits requested from WASH Cluster Pipeline among admitted among 3,000 households SAM at OTP/SC till the household level • Mobilize and train 300 hygiene promoters/ Health/Nutrition workers/volunteers trained on the WASH minimum package of activities• Carry out hygiene promotion campaigns among 16,000 beneficiaries on appropriate hygiene practices to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases. • Provide timely WASH minimum package of interventions targeting 2,000 HHs on an ad-hoc basis, adapted to the specificities of the related-outbreaks, at an institution level as well as for the wider community in Kapoeta South and Kapoeta North Counties.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

PoH implementation strategy will ensure, recognize and uphold beneficiaries’ rights and dignity in addition to ensuring these are within the legal frameworks within the laws of South Sudan and the Humanitarian Principles.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Beneficiary perspectives including any issues raised on implementation/ complaints will be addressed through two-way communication feedback, as part of beneficiary accountability. The project will in addition prioritize displaced populations in the target locations including those affected by recent flooding, although other groups will as well be targeted including people living with disabilities.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Pilgrims of Hope has been working in liaison with line ministries including Water and Irrigation, Health among others and various clusters, in line with the framework for sustainable provision and support towards the Global WASH Action Plan. This is in addition to closely working with other actors including collaborating efforts with the Cholera Task Force and Ebola Task Force.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Pilgrims of Hope proposed actions are in tandem and aligned to the 2020 WASH Cluster Humanitarian Response Plan strategies and objectives that will aim at supporting and responding to acute WASH needs with linkages in health, GBV and Nutrition; in addition to being a local organization, in line with the Grand Bargain and localization initiatives, linkages and coordination with Partnership for Resilience Framework and conflict sensitive programming and inter-cluster synergies for holistic response.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Targeted locations have in the recent past been marred by armed clashes including increased criminality and ambushes and cases of gender based violence. Pilgrims of Hope will continue to monitor these trends in liaison with other stakeholders in addition to working closely with the Conflict Sensitivity and Resource Facility. This will ensure appropriate mitigation measures are in place to ensure protection of of men, women, girls and boys including people with disabilities.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Pilgrims of Hope proposed actions will be linked with line ministries, Relief and Rehabilitation Commission and local leadership, in addition to tapping into existing local capacities including health/ nutrition/ hygiene promoters and water user committees among others. These will be engaged across all stages of implementation including involvement of targeted beneficiaries to foster sustainability upon project period expiry.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

To reduce the risk of beneficiaries and staff falling victims to crime or violence, abuse including sexual exploitation and abuse, exclusions based on gender and disabilities, long distance travelled to access services; active risk mitigation measures will be adopted. This includes situation monitoring and implementation of minimum-security standards. Coordination and working with local leadership and line ministries and targeted beneficiaries has also fostered good working relations that will ensure unhindered access to targeted beneficiaries. This is in addition to conflict sensitivity with power increasingly turning towards humanitarian resources during operational implementations. Active internal communication and consultation mechanism has been put on place to monitor and respond to trends and incidents of that nature. Externally, collaboration with UNOCHA and the NGO Forum provides opportunity and mechanisms for redress and putting in place mechanisms for feedback and complaints with appropriate actions and mitigation measures instituted.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Pilgrims of Hope as in the past will continue to ensure feedback provision among targeted beneficiaries via the adoption of consultative approaches in the implementation; ensuring and engaging affected people (boys, girls, men and women-including youth) in the processes of implementation and decisions that affect their lives, by ensuring there is an ongoing, two-way communication and dialogue throughout all stages of the project cycle. This will be in addition to installation of appropriate information dissemination and reporting and feedback channels about the project, including Payams to be targeted among girls, men, boys and women.All attempts will be made to ensure participation of relevant targeted groups (boys, girls, men and women including youth and those with special needs). This will in addition ensure principles of beneficiary empowerment, popular and active participation and rightful and unalienable entailment. The action will include a responsive feedback and complaints mechanism based on local traditional systems. However, parallel mechanisms are in place to validate and interrogate the system, especially in respect of responsibility and accountability of personnel of partners and the organisation. These will take into account gender, diversity and age. Men, women, boys and girls are consulted jointly and separately during project review meetings and post distribution monitoring exercises.
