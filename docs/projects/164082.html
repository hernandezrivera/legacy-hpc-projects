Project Summary

This action has been designed to address the need for a more integrated comprehensive response across northern Syria while taking into account the unique needs across the country and population per HNO, HRP and cluster objectives So2 So3. The proposed action aims to build community capacity to contribute to achieve a greater level of resilience, mitigation protection risks, and negative coping mechanism and participate in economic life for 16,883 HHs including (86,040 individuals), through three proposed value chains:
1- Supporting Olive farmers through the proposed olive value chain
2- Support wheat farmers through the wheat value chain
3- Supporting livestock breeders through the Livestock value chain
And the proposed three value chains connected to each other’s through the provision of fodder for targeted animal keepers from wheat farmers as well as providing targeted women group with required olive and dairy products.

4 - Needs

WATAN assessment methodology:Eleven Watan Field Researchers carried out Key informant interviews and focus group discussions in Aleppo and Idleb governorate during the month of March 2019. Watan field researchers interviewed 91 community members in Idlib 58 (Ariha, Harim, Idlib Center, Maaret ALnoman, Jesr Al Shegor) and Aleppo 33 (Afrin, Al-Bab, Azaz, Jarablus, Jabel Samaan).:- 72% of participants reported that there is a serious issue of food in their locations. In Idleb it was higher, reaching 89%. Our field researchers asked more questions about the type of issues and found that: No Food No Market 53%, No income 40%, Loss of agriculture land 28%, no physical access to market 27%, loss of agriculture tools 15%. In addition, some participants reported that there is a lack of cooking facilities. - Only 33% of the participant also reported that the main sources of food are subsistence production. 76% reported that the main source of food is from local markets and imported from other areas. 65% mentioned receiving food assistance from humanitarian organizations, which mostly goes to IDPs. - The main source of income was reported as the following, 87% from agriculture, 30% from Agro-pastoralism, 8% from fisheries, 82% from small business/trading and 88% from daily work.inline with HNO 2019 concerns and to gain better understanding WATAN main findings were:regarding wheat production:- Lack of pesticides and the spreading of insecticides, there were companies to fight them by the government - Imported substitutions - Lack of marketing information - Damaged silos - Malfunctioning mills regarding olive:- Crop diseases and high prices of fertilizer, pesticides.- Expensive processing inputs - Unavailability of quality labsregarding livestock:- New Livestock diseases from Iraq- Lack of veterinary services (medicines and vaccines) - low production breeds - Lack of pastures and fodders

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

WATAN in all of its projects apply do no harm principle through Pay attention to gender differences, Checking references in recruiting staff and volunteers and build the capacity of new personnel from the local and/or affected community, providing follow-up supervision and monitoring to ensure that interventions are implemented correctly, use local cultural practices to support local people, Use methods from outside the culture where it is appropriate to do so.Also, WATAN carrying out activities and using an approach aimed at facilitating individuals and communities to achieve respect for rights in safety and dignity through community-based protection approach. WATAN using the GAM “ gender age marker” to tackle whether or not a humanitarian project is designed to effectively respond to the different needs of men, women, girls, and boys within the affected population. Also and in order to target people with special needs, WATAN always includes at less 5% of them in the BNFs list and targeting them through appropriate modalities.WATAN In its project separate between meal and female and people with special needs have special services such as chairs, water tank. WATAN on a regular basis conducting FGD with the local community.Also WATAN publishing PSEA material and inform BNFs and non-BNFs about them rights and have complained mechanism in place as well as building the local capacity in terms of protection issues through conducting appropriate training such as PSEA As for WATAN’s staff, all of its staff once hired WATAN to conduct a PSEA and safe gardening as well as a code of conduct and humanitarian principles training. Also, WATAN an active member in the protection cluster and always applying the referral mechanism.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

• Loss of cash or assets (operational): the risk owner: the team leader in each site - This continues to be a threat to all areas of operation inside of Syria - mitigation measures: daily inventory, control the treasury better, involve many people in this process to extend the circle of suspicion.• Unforeseen Changes in the Conflict- Risk owners: Senior Management Committee - each office - Conflict is such that an organization cannot safely access locations, staff gets besieged in one area of control or operational assets are lost. Mitigation measures: Unlikely Moderate Stable Local information shows an increase in conflict levels, as we as direct reports from staff on the ground. “Continue to gather information from a range of sources. Develop a contingency plan to work in an alternative location if the conflict continues. Continuous efforts by the access teams to gain new assurances and connections to secure humanitarian space.• " Kidnapping / Abduction - risk owners: Security Management/Program Management/Individual - risk mitigation measures: traveling in the day time - transportation in convoys - high coordination on movement tracking - deterrence through local authorities. • Exposure of sensitive information - Risk owners: "Security Management/Program Management/Individual Responsibility/Executive reinforcement of policies" - Communications with anyone inside of Syria are very likely compromised and we must assume that leakage of electronic data will occur. Results will include a Threat to the detention of individuals, reputational harm and compromise of community members or partner safety. Mitigation measures: strict procedures on staff in case there was information exposed. Less visibility of WATAN in critical environments.
