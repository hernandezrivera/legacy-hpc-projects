Project Summary

TITI Foundation Shelter and NFI response will provide time-critical and life-saving assistance to populations facing greatest needs due to displacement through i) improved access to safe, appropriate emergency shelter and life-saving non-food items to newly displaced and the most vulnerable populations in secondary displacement and ii) improved living conditions of highly vulnerable IDPs in protracted displacement, returnees and host communities unable to meet their S/NFI needs. The response will be targeted based on assessed needs and the context. The response will support populations in Lakes state; Cuiebet, Rumbek East, Rumbek Center and Rumbek North; Central Equatoria; Yei and Kajokeji; Eastern Equatoria; Magwi with repair and replacement materials, construction of shelters in case of influx, as the main SNFI partner.
TITI Foundation aims to focus more on cash-based interventions in locations where security is more stable with functioning and accessible markets. This will give beneficiaries more flexibility in meeting their needs and also contribute to market recovery. TITI will consult with beneficiaries prior to distributions to ensure that shelter and NFI materials are as socially and culturally accepted.
The targeted beneficiaries will be 18,500 individuals (3500 elderly individuals, 9,000 women and girls, 6,000 men and boys) in need of reinforcement of shelters and the new arrivals in Kajokeji, Yei in Central Equatoria, Magwi in Eastern Equatoria from Uganda Camps.
More distress and havoc has affected the community with Flooding causing more harm hence Internally displacing people from Rumbek East, Rumbek Center, Rumbek North; Maper town, and Cuibet. The effects of communal conflicts have been very drastic in the areas of Jonglei and Lake states with sporadic attacks that are a result of revenge killing and cattle rustling. Gender-based violence cuts across all four states hence the need for TITI Foundation to ensure adherence to the Do No Harm principle and to reduce community tensions. The most vulnerable host community or returnee populations will also be selected as vulnerable and not exclusive IDPs. Beneficiaries will be selected based on strict criteria identified with the participation of the communities to ensure needs are properly identified. The most vulnerable will be prioritised including PLW, households with the largest number of under 5 children, child-headed households, disabled, elderly, widows, orphans, and vulnerable female-headed households.
TITI Foundation will conduct a rapid distribution Monitoring and PDM after two to four weeks of distribution to ensure satisfaction on the needs of the community beneficiaries are of high quality and responded to their intended purpose.

1 - Needs

The number of internally displaced people across South Sudan increased from 1.7 million in November-December 2020 to 2 million in September 2021. Without political stability and peace, people may continue to be affected by violence in 2022 with subsequent displacement. Localized outbreaks of sub-national violence will likely continue to drive displacement and destruction of shelters and household items. People lost their houses and properties in the 2021 floods. Another year of sustained flooding will cause further destruction of shelters and depletion of household goods and may further drive temporary and permanent displacement.The lack of flood-resilient construction and continued depletion of natural resources for temporary and transitional shelter continues to drive vulnerability, placing households in a continuous pattern of emergency need without the opportunity for recovery. areas with functional markets lacking essential shelter materials and NFIs. This presents particular challenges to displaced people who have faced multiple displacements as their resources likely have been depleted and they face competition for items with local communities.In addition to the destruction of shelter and NFIs, three consecutive years of flooding have led to an increase in longer-term displacement and relocation of communities from areas that have been completely inundated and are prone to flooding in the rainy season. This will exacerbate existing issues related to the security of tenure among communities and may drive non-displaced people or those who do not rely on humanitarian shelter and NFI support to seek managed sites to access resources. This decreases the resilience and coping mechanisms of affected people and residents of formal sites continue to remain extremely dependent on humanitarian support to meet all basic needs. South Sudan continues to receive returnees from neighboring countries. The pace of returns held steady in 2021 before increasing sharply in October with more than 60,000 returns registered in that month alone. Returnees usually arrive with minimal resources after walking for days or weeks and need shelter and NFI support.The multiple displacements and compounding factors created a situation where affected populations do not have access to the market, nor the means to obtain basic household NFI items, hence the call for external support through this project In 23 counties, more than half of the affected people requires emergency shelter support, particularly in Jonglei, Lakes, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Unity and Upper Nile states, areas with the highest concentration of displaced people. TITI Foundation will target 18,500 individuals newly displaced people, IDPs, host communities and returnees unable to meet their SNFI needs. The intervention will applying the WHO COVID-19 guidelines and measures to mitigate the spread of the virus to the vulnerable HHs hence avoid causing more harm to the population. The measures will highly consider social distancing, hand washing with soap, clean water and the use of face masks. TITI Foundation interventions will be based on multisector needs assessments preceding any particular response. This will allow TITI Foundation to respond to the specific needs of the displaced communities, being fully accountable for through identification and assessment of their needs according to the cluster strategy. TITI Foundation Emergency Preparedness and Support has an extensive experience in conducting Emergency NFI response activities in Jonglei, lakes, Central and Eastern Equatoria States.

2 - Project activities

The project is in line with the S/NFI Cluster objectives to Improve access to safe, appropriate emergency shelter and life-saving NFIs to newly displaced or vulnerable populations in secondary displacement and the living conditions of highly vulnerable IDPs in protracted displacement, returnees and host communities unable to meet their S/NFI needs to rebuild lives through coordinated, integrated and targeted S/NFIs solutions. TITI Foundation will conduct a targeted assessment based on needs to support the most vulnerable IDPs, host communities and the returnees to improve their living conditions and ensure privacy and dignified conditions. 1. TITI Foundation will use its two response modalities to respond to emergency shelter and NFI needs for the vulnerable population across Lakes, Eastern Equatoria and Central Equatoria in correlation with the protection team:a) CVA interventions will be based on functional and accessible markets through conducting rapid market needs assessments, needs analysis among the beneficiaries and provision of conditional or unconditional cash support; this will be combined with the In-kind (reconstruction and rehabilitation) needs analysis in areas that are hard to reach.2. Conducting beneficiaries' identification, verification and registration based on the most critical needs through TITI Foundation emergency response team in collaboration with the host community leaders, local authorities and beneficiary’s leaders. Based on the communicated criteria to the Leaders, the TITI static team will disaggregate the population by gender, age and vulnerability. 3. Cluster approach will be utilized by the static team during the distribution of the Emergency Shelter, NFIs items from the Cluster Pipeline. 4. TITI will largely focus on CVA projects where there will be voucher distribution of unconditional but restricted commodity vouchers for locally available shelter, NFI materials. Titi CVA project staff will enhance sustainability through mobilization of Locally available materials for shelter, repair and rehabilitation as per the prescribed standards; Formation of Community Lead distribution committee comprising of both men and women for transparency.5. Quality post-distribution monitoring: Post-distribution monitoring (PDM) s for up to 50% of our distribution will be conducted, depending on a location accessible and security within a month after our NFIs distributions. PDMs assesses beneficiary satisfaction, quality and quantity from the NFIs items distributed. Feedback of the PDM reports will be shared with the beneficiary community, partners and NFI clusters.6. CFM/Communication and Community Engagement; in case of emergence of complaints or feedback that can be channelled through both static response team and CVA for shelter (Reconstruction & rehabilitation) project. Effective messaging around the associated potential impacts of responses on protection, environment and usage of the distributed materials, translation of all IEC materials to local languages will be a priority.

5 - Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)
5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cost effectiveness is a fundamental aspect in the use of the cash or voucher due to the many challenges and dynamics in a community when it comes to transportation and local market destruction with in-kind commodities; Beneficiaries are able to make a dignified choice and meet their most pressing needs if the Cash and Voucher system is applied.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Titi's presence in the region provides its beneficiaries with an upper hand in terms of Market support interventions where market functionality was affected by the crisis and local markets were not able to cope in a timely manner to support access for basic needs. Titi will not blindly deliver humanitarian assistance in areas where markets are disrupted, rather it will consider whether to stimulate the local market to recover more quickly or asses the populated zones and trigger a temporary market from the outset of the emergency.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Information is context specific as beneficiary entitlements will be different in each targeted location. Shelter and NFI assistance is focused on repair and reinforcing shelter structures as well as replacing damaged NFIs. The focus will be on entitlements for newly displaced and resettling IDPs where assistance is to support a more permanent shelter and recovering from displacement. Information will also be on the voucher fair process. Given security risks and flooding information will be on a “need to know” basis to minimize information circulating such as on distribution dates or value of vouchers provided.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

FGDs will be conducted and information collected through writing. The documented feedback will be evaluated and a comprehensive response done within the shortest time possible with actionable timelines on intervention met.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Vulnerability and deep poverty status of the beneficiaries exposes them to risks that are undesired hence the need for PSEA. Titi will cascade the PSEA to the Authorities, Traditional Leaders and beneficiaries. There are considerable protection risks in the project locations including GBV. For the responses based on displacement patterns, prior to assessments, security will be assessed and information triangulated to make decisions on whether a response will put beneficiaries at risk or not. To minimize risks, beneficiaries prior to distributions will be informed of risk reduction measures that they can take such as travelling in mixed groups and during daylight hours. Distribution sites will be identified in safe locations such as church grounds or health centers in consultation with communities who have strong local knowledge of the safest locations. Friendly Helpdesks during distribution days and the CRM will be strengthened to identify protection cases and subsequent referrals will be made to Protection partners such as DRC, UNHCR, the Red Cross movement or MSF, UNHCR or Voice for Change.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

At the inception of the project, Titi will provide sensitization explaining details of the programme, community involvement in the targeting process, what assistance beneficiaries are entitled to and the CRM. Following beneficiary selection, beneficiaries will be consulted on design of shelters and NFI kits to ensure materials are appropriate to the needs. Stakeholders and community feedback meeting and sessions will provide avenues for sharing of the findings and development of solutions that are long lasting, this will be used as learning avenues that will strengthen the relationship of the program at the community level.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Titi Foundation will assess security and information elicited to make decisions on whether a response would put beneficiaries at risk or not. Based on its “Do No Harm” principle. Titi’s staff are responsible for implementing the Code of Conduct, Child Safeguarding Policy and Anti-trafficking in Persons Policy. During the assessments, specific needs of women, men, girls and boys as well as PSNs will be considered to ensure the composition of shelter and NFI kits meets all needs. During the distribution of vouchers and kits, households will nominate one member to receive assistance, usually, a senior female as this can encourage joint decision making. To reduce protection risks, beneficiaries will be sensitized on measures to take while travelling in mixed groups and in daylight hours. Distribution sites will be in safe and accessible locations such as church grounds or health facilities and engage in the mobile nature of the response to reduce travel times. Information Helpdesks on distribution days and the CRM will be used to identify protection cases and make referrals to partners handling Protection. PDMs will be used to collect information on protection risks and the information will be used to inform future distributions.Women as per social and cultural norms have limited decision-making roles as decisions are made by men. This includes women’s rights to property as they cannot own the property or land they work and can impact where they can relocate to in event of displacement or resettling. Disabled have limited means to access shelter and NFI materials as they lack mobility and means for shelter construction. In addition, women with disability are excluded from decision-making processes, 48% of women with disabilities reported never having access to the decision-making process through community participation and engagement mechanisms (IOM, 2018). Titi will ensure that specific needs of vulnerable groups are identified by including representatives of those groups in the discussions held around the project implementation. Specific FGDs will be organized to ensure freedom to speak and raise concerns by those vulnerability groups.
