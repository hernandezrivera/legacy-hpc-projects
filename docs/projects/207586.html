Project Summary

The project is to concentrate on building coherence between host and refugee communities in camps 24, 25, 26 and 27 and Hnila Union Parishad of Teknaf upazila of Cox’s Bazar. Social cohesion will be strengthened through dialogue platforms for conflict resolution, through improved access to protection services and increased knowledge about protection problems, as well as through increased safety in the immediate surroundings of the community members. The project is designed to serve 9,995 direct beneficiaries (8,500 RRs and 1,495 HCs) consisting of: (Community Protection Facilitators (CPFs) and supervisors (133), Community Protection Group (CPG) members (9,995), Dialogue Forum members (open), Adolescence club members (450) and Risk and Vulnerability volunteer (780). Apart from this, 50000 indirect beneficiaries will be benefitted as a spillover effect. Among the project participants 50% will be women and in Disability and Inclusion sector standard will be followed. Sustainable outcomes will be achieved through the participatory nature of the project, especially facilitated through the Community-based Monitoring Committees. Community members and local authorities are involved in the design of the new structures (dialog fora, CPGs, Risk and Vulnerability Mapping Volunteer - RVMV), which will increase their ownership to maintain these after the project end and they will at the same time be capacitated to do so. Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), environmental and Covid-19 aspects and protection concerns are considered because volunteers will be specifically trained to identify such risks together with their communities and to develop plans in a participatory manner to overcome the identified risks and vulnerabilities. Furthermore, the RVMVs are trained to become first responders in case of an emergency and are equipped with tools accordingly. All core activities will be implemented in a participatory manner where group members can decide about the contents discussed and the actions to be taken. In addition, two so-called Community-based Monitoring Committees one for RRs for respective authorities; one for Host Communities (HCs) and respective authorities which are formed they will not monitor in a technical sense, but will meet four times per year to assess the project process and to propose adaptations where needed. Representatives from all beneficiary groups within the project will be chosen in a participatory manner to sit in the Monitoring Committees. Project will form Community Protection Groups (CPGs) with 50% women and girls’ members and other forum and platforms will also ensure women participation. At all level project will rightly inform through sessions about women and girls rights, pessimistic effect of early marriage, GBV and SEA. It is expected that CPGs with direct support of CPFs and other project team members will develop stronghold on protecting GBV and SEA. Moreover, they will rightly educate to use Complain Response Mechanism (CRM) system either of this project or any other organization. The project will build communities capacities and strengthen community engagement to ensure accessibility to life-saving information and knowledge on rights and available resources to communities. In addition, as per Union Chairman of Teknaf Union, he recommends to extend other wards of Hinla.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"According to the J_MSNA 2023, Child Labour, Child marriage, GBV and people joining criminal groups are the main protection issues. The barrier analysis on the accessibility of protection services provides valuable insights into challenges reported by community members seeking to report safety or security incidents or access protection services in the 12 months preceding data collection. Notably, a substantial majority in both the host community (88%) and the camp (80%) did not face any issues. For those encountering challenges, common barriers included problems not being resolved to the household's satisfaction (4% in the host community, 6% in the camp), uncertainty about where to report (2% in the host community, 5% in the camp), and a lack of understanding about the reporting process (2% in the host community, 3% in the camp). Interestingly, privacy concerns or overcrowding were not reported as barriers in the host community but were mentioned by 3% in the camp. A small percentage acknowledged that older persons (2% in the camp) and females (percentage not specified) faced challenges in reporting or accessing protection services. It is crucial to recognize that these findings are based on respondents' perceptions and may not fully capture the actual experiences of those reporting protection issues or the extent of challenges they encounter, as emphasized in the provided disclaimer.The survey on psychosocial distress within host communities and Rohingya camps revealed notable insights. Overall, 33% of households in host communities reported signs of psychosocial distress or trauma, with 67% indicating no such signs. In the Rohingya camps, the prevalence was slightly higher, with 38% acknowledging distress and 62% reporting the absence of such indicators.Breaking down the data by age and gender, a nuanced pattern emerged. Among boys aged 3-17, 1% in host communities and 6% in camps exhibited signs of distress. Similarly, for girls in the same age group, the figures mirrored those of boys, with 1% in host communities and 6% in camps. However, the disparity increased among adult populations. Adult men (18-59) showed higher rates of distress in host communities (43%) compared to camps (33%), whereas adult women's distress levels remained similar in both settings, with 42% in host communities and 42% in camps. These findings underscore the importance of considering age and gender differentials when addressing psychosocial well-being in both host communities and Rohingya camps.This is a project, which is the continuation of FIVDB’s protection projects in the area since 2020. This project addresses those identified barriers and protection issues, by strengthening community based protection mechanism and engaging youth in ensuring protection services for the community people. Youth clubs associated with the project will ensure entertainment for the people through sports and theatre which addresses the psychological stress issues to a large scale , while the case management ensures addressing individuals victims being supported.

2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

"The project has been implementing since early 2021 till the period of Dec 2024. The project has adopted approach to strengthen working relations with administration, camp management and other actors etc. working in the camp and host community areas. Moreover, the activities have been designed contributing to each other activities in terms of capacity development, sustainability and achieving targets. To attain the project long term goal i.e., strengthening “Peaceful Coexistence” or Social Cohesion among the host and Rohingyas communities the design of activities has been done such a way that they are integrated with communities as such towards end of the project Forums, Clubs and community groups which are the main interventions will function altogether i.e., host and Rohingya communities will function jointly and will move towards Peaceful Coexistence. The design of the project is to strengthen community capacities of different types of community groups and sustain its functions for attaining Peaceful Coexistence and Protection Services. The groups are capacitated as such they themselves under this project will coordinate and keep continue functioning and sustain the approach. Moreover, they also will coordinate with other service providers to attain protection services and continue. In Spite of this, project will search and approach to other donors to attain funds for this concept and also will work closely with other actors functioning in the same localities to support the community groups for their capacity strengthening as such they can functions independently.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Project has appointed Community Protection Facilitators (CPFs) who resides in the localities where the target households/beneficiaries reside. These CPFs have been capacitated through training, coaching and mentoring to know the protection related needs of the beneficiaries. They are also being interlinked with the protection services providing institutions as such they are well acquainted with the institutions and well known about the process of referrals. There are numbers of CPF Supervisors to properly monitor the referral systems and provide necessary support and skills to better support the beneficiaries and respond to protection needs. However, always put focus that no way there should be any adverse effect of the interventions. Moreover, ensures participations in all stages of the beneficiaries while designing the mainstreaming concept in project interventions.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

"The project has formed two Community Based Monitoring Committees (CBMCs) one with host communities and another with Rohingya Refugees to monitor the progress and accordingly to report. The members of the committees consist of direct beneficiary groups, teachers, religious leaders, representatives of local government, of camp management (site management of CiC offices). They monitor activities and share their findings on regular basis to the field team. The committees are independent for monitoring the activities which have been implanting in their communities. Project has detail activities and implementation strategy with the CBMCs for their capacity building in terms of independent monitoring. Moreover, the committees also meet quarterly with project team including senior management of the project and share their findings and accordingly next course of actions are being set. The project also all support and services to the beneficiaries’ displaying in banners, festoon, handbills and to total communities and welcome any queries and questions and accordingly clarify to them. Moreover, project also has developed Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) system to ask about their grievances. CRM issues have been handled confidentially. Under the Protection Objective 2 and Activity # 2 i.e., Protection Monitoring there are numbers of other regular activities those have suited well with this objective and activity # 2. The propose project is continual of a project have been implementing since January 2021 till April 2024(including NCE period).

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

"FIVDB is an active member of PSEA network and maintains regular coordination with the network. FIVDB’s PSEA policy is developed incorporating core principles adapted by the IASC on PSEA and Convention on the elimination of all forms of discrimination against women (CEDAW). FIVDB has participated in a PSEA assessment conducted by UNICEF in 2023 and has been declared as high/full PSEA capacity organization. FIVDB ensures PSEA reference check of all staff during recruitment and incorporates knowledge, attitude and practice (KAP) questions regarding PSEA in staff recruitment interviews and written tests. All staff receives a mandatory training on prevention of sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment, gender sensitivity and orientation on all the policies of FIVDB. FIVDB is currently planning to conduct a KAP survey every year with the staff, volunteers and project participants to mainstream PSEA at the beginning of every year. All the facilities of the project will have PSEA and safeguarding IEC materials including awareness posters for all beneficiaries regarding their rights and FIVDB’s complaint hotline number. All the facilities will be installed with visible and accessible complaint and feedback box. The complaints and feedback will be responded and followed up regularly ensuring community engagement. FIVDB will take necessary actions regarding SEA complaints that are in line with the SOP for the SEA complaint referral. FIVDB has a dedicated gender cell to deal with all SEA complaints and take necessary actions. FIVDB ensures gender balance in gender cell to ensure accessibility of persons of concerns to place their complaints and issues. Regular risk assessments will be conducted by the organization to address and prepare a mitigation plan for the identified risks for the project and community participation will be ensured in the risk assessment. Regular monitoring of PSEA practices will be ensured throughout the project based on developed checklist.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, the project has Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and Disaster Risk Management (DRM) elements. Project has formed a significant number of Risk and Vulnerability Mapping Volunteers groups. These groups consist of members from all sections of the communities as such they can properly identify climatic risk and vulnerabilities of the populations and the areas. Accordingly prepare action plan. To address the identified Risk and Vulnerabilities the project will coordinate with other service providers, government’s relevant departments for capacity building of the target groups, resources mobilization towards reducing the identified risk and vulnerabilities. The activities will also closely monitor the progress and will further integrate required resources mobilizing from different internal and external sources.
