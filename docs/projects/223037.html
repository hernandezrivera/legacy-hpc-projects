Project Summary

This project aims to address the critical vulnerabilities faced by adolescent boys and girls in Dikwa, Jere, Monguno, and Gwoza by integrating robust case management systems with comprehensive and inclusive education programs. The intervention targets children at risk of abuse, neglect, exploitation, and child labor, ensuring they access quality education in a safe and supportive environment while addressing the underlying protection concerns.
To bridge the gap in education access, the project will establish and expand formal and non-formal education programs tailored to the needs of out-of-school children, particularly girls and children with disabilities. These programs will provide scholarships, learning materials, and school feeding initiatives to encourage enrollment and retention. By integrating case management, the project will empower community members, teachers, and child protection workers to identify and respond effectively to cases of abuse and exploitation. This includes the establishment of child-friendly spaces and referral pathways, ensuring children receive psychosocial support and essential services.
Inclusive education will be prioritized through capacity-building efforts. Teachers will be trained in adaptive teaching (learner centered) methodologies and equipped with learning materials designed for diverse needs. Infrastructure improvements will make schools more accessible to children with disabilities, fostering an inclusive learning environment that accommodates all students. Concurrently, the project will engage communities through sensitization campaigns emphasizing the importance of education and the rights of children to protection and development. Community-based child protection committees (CBCPC) and Community Education Committee (CEC) will be established to strengthen local safeguarding efforts and sustain community engagement.
Recognizing the systemic challenges in conflict-affected areas, the project will advocate for enhanced policies and funding commitments for inclusive education and child protection. Coordination among stakeholders will be strengthened to deliver integrated, sustainable solutions that address the multifaceted needs of children at risk. By weaving together education, protection, and advocacy, this initiative will safeguard and empower vulnerable adolescents, fostering a foundation for resilience, growth, and development in some of the most challenging contexts.

1 - Needs

The northeastern region of Nigeria is currently facing an acute humanitarian crisis, driven by sustained armed conflict, widespread displacement, and systemic infrastructural challenges. This crisis has severely disrupted educational systems and significantly escalated the risks of abuse, neglect, exploitation, and child labor. The region is marked by extensive human rights violations, including killings, rape, and other forms of sexual violence, which have resulted in the denial of educational opportunities for nearly 2 million children, as highlighted by the Education in Emergencies Working Group (EiEWG) in 2023. The most vulnerable populations are marginalized and displaced children, particularly adolescent boys and girls, who encounter compounded barriers to accessing quality education. The scarcity of qualified educators in these areas exacerbates the crisis, leaving these children without the necessary academic support. In addition, the ongoing conflict has disrupted social structures, exacerbating the risks of abuse, neglect, and exploitation. Harmful traditional practices, such as early and forced marriage, continue to undermine girls’ educational opportunities, limiting their access to socioeconomic advancement and perpetuating cycles of vulnerability. In response to these challenges, a comprehensive, integrated approach that aligns education and protection is imperative. This approach should focus on safeguarding and educating children at risk, while advancing inclusive education systems in conflict-affected areas such as Dikwa, Ngala, Monguno, and Gwoza. Strengthening inclusive education programs is crucial to expanding both formal and non-formal educational opportunities tailored to the specific needs of vulnerable children particularly girls, children with disabilities, and those from marginalized communities. To ensure equity, teachers must receive targeted training in inclusive pedagogy, gender sensitivity, and the effective use of adaptive learning materials. Moreover, enhancing protection mechanisms is a critical priority for safeguarding the well-being of children in these regions. Establishing child-friendly spaces, improving referral systems for psychosocial support, legal aid, and medical care, and providing specialized training for education and protection workers to identify and address risks of abuse, neglect, and exploitation are essential steps. Rehabilitating and equipping schools with accessible facilities will further ensure that children can learn in safe, supportive environments. Community engagement and awareness campaigns will be instrumental in transforming harmful cultural practices and shifting public attitudes towards education and child protection. Raising awareness about the value of inclusive education and children’s rights to protection is essential for garnering local support. The establishment of community-based child protection committees (CBCPC) and Community Education Committee (CEC), will empower local stakeholders to take an active role in safeguarding children. Advocacy for increased funding, the strengthening of policy frameworks, and improved coordination among humanitarian actors are also crucial to ensuring long-term, sustainable interventions. By addressing these systemic gaps and fostering collaboration across key stakeholders, it is possible to mitigate the adverse effects of the crisis, provide a protective environment for vulnerable children, and ensure their access to education, safety, and dignity.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members by implementing multiple, accessible feedback mechanisms. These will include community meetings, focus group discussions (FGDs), and one-on-one interviews with a diverse range of vulnerable individuals, including women, girls, people with disabilities, and marginalized groups. Feedback will be gathered in a safe and confidential environment, ensuring that all participants feel comfortable sharing their experiences and concerns. Additionally, the project will establish complaint and feedback mechanisms, such as suggestion boxes, helplines, and community feedback committees, to allow for anonymous feedback. Regular monitoring visits will be conducted by community representatives to ensure continuous engagement. The feedback will be analyzed, and the project will adapt based on the input received, ensuring that the needs and concerns of the most vulnerable are addressed throughout the implementation.
