Project Summary

The impact of over 50 years of continued military occupation in the West Bank on the protection of Palestine refugees is wide-ranging. Palestine refugees face a range of serious protection threats, including: threats to life; liberty and security; destruction or damage to homes and other property; forced displacement and restrictions on freedom of movement and access to natural resources, markets and other livelihood opportunities.
This action is based on UNRWA's protection mandate for over 843,931 Palestine refugees in the West Bank. Particular focus is given to the needs of Palestine refugees living in 19 refugee camps, where the Israeli Security Forces conduct repeated military operations. The action has two core objectives to (1) monitor and document IHL & IHRL violations affecting Palestine refugees, using this as a basis to engage in targeted protection advocacy to promote accountability and enhance the protection of affected persons, (2) to respond to the acute needs of Palestine refugees exposed to the risk of displacement and/or forcible transfer through the provision of cash assistance and an integrated case management response.
In relation to the first objective, the aim is to enhance the protective environment for Palestine refugees exposed to protection threats through protection advocacy - through persuasion, mobilisation and denunciation, and based on monitoring and documentation of protection incidents and related analysis. The tools and approaches are determined by the HCTs protection advocacy strategy. The aim is to promote both protective policies and practices as well as the accountability of duty bearers.
In relation to the second objective, this action responds to protection threats by mitigating their consequences and reinforcing the resilience of affected persons. UNRWA will provide dedicated case management services to affected persons; also facilitating access of these persons to cash transfers depending on the identified needs. Cash assistance addresses immediate needs linked protection threats, whilst case management services improves access to other UNRWA services and specialised partners, and reinforces the resilience of affected persons.
This action works directly towards two out of three of the strategic objectives of the HRP:
Strategic Objective 1: The rights of Palestinians living under occupation, including those living under the blockade and other restrictions, are protected, respected and promoted in accordance with IHL and IHRL.
Strategic Objective 2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian context of the West Bank including East Jerusalem remains directly tied to the impact of the Israeli military occupation now in its 53rd year. Protracted socio-economic, and protection challenges continue. Representing one-third of the West Bank population including East Jerusalem, many of over 843,931 Palestine refugees registered with UNRWA experience challenges marked by continued: (i) violence and force displacement, administrative and punitive demolitions; (ii) restrictions on access to land, services and markets, particularly for those living and/or owing land in Area C and in the seam zone; (ii) stagnant economy and lack of employment opportunities that erodes the purchasing power of Palestinian refugees thus classifying them as one of the poorest segments of Palestinian society; (iii) food insecurity that particularly acute amongst refugees residing in camps. In 2019 the situation for Palestine refugees remained volatile. An increase in demolitions and resulting displacement has been the outstanding protection threat thus far in 2019. At least 438 structures were demolished by Israeli authorities in the first 9 months of 2019, 118 of which were refugee-owned. This is the highest number for the reporting period since 2016, which was a record year. In particular, East Jerusalem is targeted, accounting for over one third of demolitions. Further, at least 551 Palestinians have been displaced as a result, 202 of whom were Palestine refugees, including 103 children. Refugees are disproportionately impacted, accounting for 36% of the displaced but only 25% of the population. The excessive use of force and its impacts on Palestine refugees continues to be a major protection concern. Between January and end September 2019, 4,290 Israeli Security Force (ISF) operations took place in the West Bank, 400 of which occurred in refugee camps. There have been 25 Palestinian fatalities in that period, 10 of whom were refugees, over 2,500 Palestinians have been recorded as injured - the majority following exposure to tear gas, but also a substantial after being shot with live ammunition. Palestine refugee camps are hot-spots for confrontations and violence. Through its monitoring and documentation, UNRWA continues to identify multiple concerns regarding the excessive use of force. Military operations - particularly in refugee camps - are either initiated as, or develop into, violent incursions characterised by deployment of live ammunition, the excessive tear gas use, damages to refugee homes and property. This precipitates severe psychological stress, physical injuries and at times also fatalities. With respect to both conflict related violence and displacement, children are usually particularly affected by these traumatizing practices and the psychological impact can be quite severe on development. Young men are exposed to conflict-related violence including physical injuries or traumatizing psychological experiences, in addition to detention. There is evidence that sexual and gender-based violence is increasingly used within Palestine, as a negative coping strategy to address feelings of powerlessness and hopelessness, prevalent as result of the occupation and humanitarian crisis. Research published in 2010 and 2017 established that men who reported experiencing occupation related violence were more likely to perpetrate intimate partner violence and sexual harassment. Displacement and property damage has a particularly detrimental impact on women and girls. Following house demolitions, families often move in with extended family and relatives, often giving women less control over their time and the household and care-giving responsibilities, in addition to overcrowding, dependence and an increased risk of family discord and violence. Elderly household members are also particularly vulnerable to these practices, as they may have endured repeated incidents.

2 - Activities

Outcome 1: Enhanced accountability and improved practices of duty-bearers vis-à-vis their obligations under International Human Rights Law and International Humanitarian Law to Palestine refugees in the West Bank. Activity 1.1: Incidents of IHL and IHRL violations in refugee camps, Bedouin and Herding communities, East Jerusalem, Barrier affected communities and communities affected by settler violence are systematically and thoroughly documented. Activity 1.2: Where consent to do so is secured and when necessary, documented IHL and IHRL violations are addressed on a systematic basis with relevant authorities during meetings, calls or through protest letters/letters of concern. Direct interaction with relevant Duty Bearers to address violations of international law. Different platforms of engagement are used in this process; from real-time interventions through telephone contact, to scheduled meetings and official letters. Activity 1.3: Donors and other international stakeholders are sensitized to the impact of IHL & IHRL violations on Palestine refugees when relevant and possible (through briefings, field trips to affected areas, presentations at relevant forums abroad, etc.). Activity 1.4: Mobilization activities focus on addressing briefings to diplomats and lobby organizations, reporting to Human Rights stakeholders, field visits for politicians and diplomats to emblematic communities, background briefings for journalists, and briefings for relevant forums abroad. Activity 1.5: Public advocacy initiatives on protection issues affecting Palestine refugees in the West Bank. Outcome 2: Refugee households impacted by protection threats are better able to cope. Activity 2.1: Refugee households affected by demolitions/evictions & damages following military operations/settler violence are identified and assessed by UNRWA. Activity 2.2 Following assessment, services (case management, emergency cash assistance and individual service referrals) are provided to affected Palestine refugees in line with identified needs. Activity 2.3 Monitoring of cash assistance and case management services. In terms of the role of the beneficiaries in the design, implementation and evaluation of this project this can be summarized as follows: At the design phase for Outcome 1, beneficiaries have been directly involved the design of the activities, including through participation in committees, focus group discussions and surveys to develop dedicated advocacy strategies at the community level, UNRWA ensures that the inputs of vulnerable individuals are appropriately represented in this process. These strategies are the basis of action taken under this project. During the implementation routine engagement with community stakeholders is carried out to ensure the appropriateness, impact and relevance of activities. In addition at the closure of the activities, the community is directly involved in providing to the evaluation that UNRWA undertakes and uses for its future programming cycles. In addition in 2019 UNRWA carried out 15 community-level protection assessments in communities that are subjected to a coercive environment, in order to better prioritise protection work. At the design phase for Outcome 2, beneficiaries were involved at the community level in terms of identifying the drivers of vulnerability and risk for those affected by protection incidents that are responded to, this included direct involvement in the piloting phase of eligibility tools. In the implementation phase beneficiaries are directly involved as they are the drivers of their individual case plans with are developed in coordination with the social workers, furthermore they have opportunities to raise concerns and complaints through a dedicated free-to-call hotline. In addition beneficiary inputs are provided in terms of their assessment of whether the assistance provided met their needs, and about additional needs that were outstanding.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

In UNRWA's Protection Strategy (2012), the prioritisation of safety and dignity is explicitly address as a key protection standard where UNRWA programmes deliver services in a manner that ensures the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and staff. UNRWA ensures that its actions do not cause unintended harm or result in reduced protection of beneficiaries, communities or staff. On a biannual basis all of UNRWA's activities are subject to a protection mainstreaming audit, which assesses the integration of protection principles in UNRWA's work. This audit included extensive interviews and focus group discussions with beneficiaries, and sought to assess UNRWA's application of the four main protection principles, including the principle of Do No Harm. The most recent protection audit was completed in 2019, and a number of recommendations were developed, which are now being followed up with the support of a dedicated Protection Officer, funded under this action. UNRWA has a well established process during its monitoring and documentation procedures of obtaining informed consent (and assent in the case of minors) for recording and use of information that affects them. This consent process was further enhanced in 2018 with the introduction of additional guidance to field workers and training. In 2019 UNRWA developed a new information management system for the collection of sensitive protection monitoring and documentation data; specifically the system now separates out biographical data from incident related data, with individual cases being only referred to through generated case code. In 2019/8 UNRWA developed a dedicated feedback hotline for the use of all beneficiaries under this project, this hotline is available free of charge to beneficiaries both in East Jerusalem and elsewhere in the West Bank. A response feedback mechanism has been developed internally to deal with complaints and to redress concerns raised. UNRWA is further working on improving in this regard and is due to introduce a community feedback mechanism using a mobile application in the coming months. Under Outcome 1, for the purposes of ensuring that briefings involving affected persons, are carried out in a way that prioritises the safety and dignity of these persons, UNRWA has developed an internal guideline for ethical practices in the context of briefings, field visits, communications and others. This includes a checklist to guide all staff working in this capacity. Under Outcome 2 UNRWA has developed, in coordination with the West Bank Protection Consortium, a dedicated vulnerability assessment tool in order to prioritise assistance to the most vulnerable refugees. This tool was pioneered by UNRWA and has since been adopted by other members of the Consortium and is to be applied throughout the West Bank. Under Outcome 2 of this project, within the Case Management response UNRWA ensures that vulnerable individuals can access assistance by external referrals, UNRWA has developed dedicated case management SOPs and internal/external referral pathways, to ensure that the comprehensive needs are met. In the planning of its advocacy approach and strategy UNRWA engages with a range of community representatives, including protection committees, village councils, camp resident committees – as well as holding numerous focus group discussions including for women, children, youth and the elderly in communities that we are working with. Concretely this engagement has led to adaptations in the approach to UNRWA’s assistance, including developing community-led protection initiatives, expanding the provision of case management services and adapting the modality of assistance provided. These discussions have led UNRWA to adapt its advocacy approach and strategy, and to enhance its prioritization of thematic issues and communities. The feedback provided contributed to the prioritisation of thematic advocacy issues to be pursued within the Action.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNRWA as a service provider is dedicated to the application of the humanitarian principles in all of its service delivery, and takes it responsibility in this regard very seriously. Specific measures are taken to ensure that UNRWA's services are neutral and impartial in nature, including through regular monitoring throughout its operations. For the purposes of this project, Under Outcome 2 UNRWA has developed, in coordination with the West Bank Protection Consortium, a dedicated vulnerability assessment tool in order to prioritise assistance to the most vulnerable refugees. This tool was pioneered by UNRWA and has since been adopted by other members of the Consortium and is to be applied throughout the West Bank. UNRWA takes measures to ensure that its assistance under this project is available to all persons, and takes dedicated action to ensure that individuals with specific needs are reached. UNRWA takes actions to ensure that persons with disability, with movement and access restrictions, the elderly, women and children are targeted during outreach and communication - and also are key stakeholders in all community interactions and in accountability processes. Services delivered are tailored to the needs of individuals, including for those with specific needs, the case management services delivered are developed in coordination with the affected persons and are guided by their specific needs. Specific needs according to age, gender and disability are identified at the initial assessment stage of case management, for those affected by damages to residential property, and those exposed to displacement. The individual case plans are tailored to respond to these needs, for example provision of services linked to mobility issues amongst the elderly. In terms of the work in advocacy UNRWA ensures that in its documentation and monitoring dynamics including gender, age and disability are factored into needs analysis, trends analysis and UNRWA conducts a number of thematic briefings per year to focus on the specific needs of vulnerable groups. UNRWA has developed dedicated field briefings on disability, gender and GBV, children and the elderly for this purpose.
