Project Summary

The protection environment in South Sudan remains unstable characterized by localised communal violence, revenge killings, GBV and widespread lack of basic services. Housing, land, and property (HLP) remained one of key protection concern. Coalition for Humanity in its current protection projects in Upper Nile and other states has documented incidences where women are denied access to land unless they have first born male child. This has affected mostly widows, IDPS and returnees who have nowhere to turn to in order to rebuild their lives.
Coalition for Humanity will integrate closely its GBV and general protection projects in all project locations proposed here in order to provide maximum benefits to the people affected by the conflict. With Protection of civilian sites (PoCs) transitioned to IDPs centers most people will return to their original homes. It is also expected with the current phase of peace agreement implementation, relative calm is expected and this will means people will want to return to their former homes to start their lives once again. With challenges around accessing land, it will be a big challenge to many households and this project aims to provide an integrated support in coordinated efforts with GBV, General Protection and shelter and NFI cluster. With strong presence in most of the proposed project locations, CH has implemented other projects apart from from protection/HLP leveraging on its local context knowledge and acceptance by the authorities and communities. It has wider coordination strengthen and mainstreaming of key activities in both protection and other relevant clusters. The project activities will be achieved based on feasible modality include static and mobile response as context permits. With local partner HDA, both will combine local expertise and presence to deliver the proposed activities.
The project will envisage to conduct among other the following key deliverables;
Targeting a total caseload of 20,000 beneficiaries, the project will undertake the below activities.
1. The project will reach women, men, girls and boys through awareness raising activities on HLP issues in targeted urban and peri urban centers
2. The project will women, men, girls and boys and provide support with legal counselling to identified people in needs
3. Through pro bono legal services, the project will support women, boys, girls and boys with legal aid

1 - Needs

Access to housing, land, and property (HLP), is an important issue that has exacerbated conflict and suffering during the many years of conflicts in South Sudan. Many households have moved and lost their land while displaced with particular worrying situation for women headed households. The implication brought about by the culture and questions around land ownership has worsening over the last year. People in PoC sites are slowly returning to their former homes that are occupied by others or do not have economic means to acquire new ones. The HLP is one of key protection concerns that requires urgent attention to addressAccording to Humanitarian need overview conducted by UN OCHA, it highlighted that the formalization of land laws and the creation of mechanisms of their implementation remains a gap and creates an obstacle for people who want to return. Widespread lack of literacy and insufficient legal ownership documentation made it difficult for many people to understand and effectively advocate for themselves. This issue contributes to attempts of improper land-grabbing that can drive further conflict and displacement. Women and people with disabilities disproportionately struggle to assert rights to housing and land. Given that Housing, land and property (HLP) rights continue to be a growing key protection issue across South Sudan, an estimated 1.5 million will be affected by HLP issues in 2021, in addition 743,000 children are indirectly affected of HLP issues. During the years of conflict, ownership claims and control over land and property has played a defining role which was likewise marked by large-scale destruction of housing, land and property. Spontaneous returns of both IDPs and refugees are expected to increase in 2021 as the government moves forward with the implementation of the R-ARCSS. This will result in a rise of HLP issues in areas of return, especially because of illegal occupation and grabbing of houses and lands, which has potential to contribute to sub-national and localized violence along with conflict based on identity lines in some areas. In urban and peri-urban areas, there has been an increase in the risk of evictions as a result of the COVID-19 pandemic. Country-wide restrictions to movement and trading has negatively impacted livelihood activities and resulting in reduced income for many households, leading to a failure to pay rent, which could lead to a rise in the number of evictions. Despite the legal provisions recognizing the equal rights of women to land, recognition and protection of those rights remain limited throughout South Sudan. Women’s land rights remain largely conditional. Dispossession of widows, daughters, and divorced women is common. HLP rights are cross-cutting issue and require multi-sector approaches. Coalition for Humanity has identified the above needs, will work closely with implementing partners, the Protection Cluster, leading the national HLP Technical Working Group (HLP TWG), in collaboration with the Shelter/NFI Cluster in establishing comprehensive programs on HLP and shelter. Identification of persons in need will be accompanied by services such as legal assistance and case management, but also provision of shelter kits, cash for shelter, etc.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Our project staff, volunteers and consultants sign PSEA policy together with our code of conduct that prohibits any unwanted behaviour with beneficiaries at all times. Beneficiaries are also provided with information sessions in order to understand what could constitute PSEA violation and how they can report. As part of our overall efforts, we support other organizations to understand PSEA in work place and how to enforce. We also target local leaders across our project areas.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

our project staff engage special groups especially individuals living with disabilities and illiterate by doing focus groups discussions and encouraging them to write in any language they know rather than English. Coalition for Humanity will hold community dialogue days to allow community to give feedback on project implementation and any challenges. A door to door survey on beneficiary satisfaction will, be conducted with special focus on people with disability, the enumerators will interpret questions in local language and record findings, to enable illiterates community members to participate

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Before this project initiated series of consultative meetings were held with beneficiaries to define our feedback mechanism Once the project is operationalised, feedback and complaint mechanism will be put in place including boxes where beneficiaries can share their feedback about some of the services and sensitive information.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback will help us understand areas that require improvement and how best to adapt so that our assistance go a long way in meeting the expectations and needs of beneficiaries. It is a learning process that we encourage so that our project implementation bring out the meaning, engagements

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Coalition for Humanity has elaborated process of involving local authorities, beneficiaries mostly women and girls at the early stages of responses. We carry meaningful engagement to ensure that feedbacks are taken on board to inform the actual response. We mitigate risks such as interference, and inclusion of different disadvantaged groups by having clear selection criteria for our beneficiaries selections.Coalition for Humanity will embrace the protection principles, all services will be taken closer to the people to avoid long distances covered by beneficiaries. The organization will maintain high levels of confidentiality in reporting, survivors will not be captured by their true identity, they will be provided with confidential space and key focal persons to receive information. support to survivors will not be broadcasted. All staff, enumerators, and implementing partners will sign PSEA code of conduct and gender policy in order to commit to protect the community against exploitation during project implementation. There is a risk of minority, people with disability, women, girls and boys not participating effectively in the project. Coalition for Humanity will conduct focus group discussions targeting people with disability, boys, girls, women separately to make sure their needs and concerns are captured. Coalition for Humanity has a PSEA code of conduct, all field staff are trained on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, Community leaders are also trained on PSEA to raise awareness in the community so that beneficiaries can know they have a right to humanitarian aid without their dignity being compromised.Community members will be part of the referral pathway, being represented by various leaders. awareness be created about available redress mechanisms for various HLP concerns Coalition for Humanity will conduct forums, community dialogue days to capture feedback from community members who are not able to use suggestion box. a survey will also be conducted door to door to find out beneficiary satisfaction. Coalition for Humanity will work with locally based staff, with indigenous knowledge about the project area. The security situation will be closely monitored, with updates to partners and the clusters.
