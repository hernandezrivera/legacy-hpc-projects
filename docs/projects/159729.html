Project Summary

The project contributes Improvement of food security, reduction of risks, vulnerabilities and building resilience to future shocks and stresses to crisis affected populations in Hiran, Lower Shabelle and Banadir regions in South Somalia. The Specific Objective (Outcome) of the project is improved nutrition and food security and increased resilience for 5,200 vulnerable community and IDP households or 31,200 men, women, boys, girls, people with disabilities and minorities. The proposed project will provide:
Banadir:
(1) Immediate food security and livelihoods assistance to 2000 IDPs n stress and crises for a representing 12,000 women, girls, boys and men including people with disabilities and minorities to access essential food items through food vouchers for three months. In addition.
(2) The project will provide women IDPs and vulnerable host communities as income generation; 10 grinding machines and 10 oil processing machines benefitting around 200 IDPs with female headed HHs with malnourished children or HHs with disabilities. (women are regrouped in to 10 and each 10 will receive 1 grinding machine or 1 oil processing machine) representing 1,200 men, women, boys and girls.
(3) The project will train 200 women on operation & maintenance of grinding machine (100 women) and oil processing machine (100 women) and income generation practices.
Hiran and Lower Shabelle regions:
(1) Restocking to 1,000 rural HHs ((Each HH will receive 5 goats), representing 6,000 men, women, boys and girls including people with disabilities and minorities, who have lost their animals due to the drought/floods and have no alternative livelihood assets to survive.
(2) Farm input to 1,500 HH farmers and agropastoralists in Lower Shabelle and Hiran regions for recovery and resilience, benefitting around 9,000 women, girls, boys and men affected by crisis to protect their assets and generate income for their households.
(3) The project will train 300 farmers and 100 pastorals on the best farming technologies, animal husbandry.
(4) WARDI will rehabilitate through CFW 25km feeder roads affected by floods in Hiran and L/Shabelle regions, benefiting 1000 HHs or 6,000 women, girls, boys, and men to rehabilitate productive assets for a period of 3 months.
(5) WARDI will construct/rehabilitate 10 reinforced concrete culverts in Hiran region affected by floods to make easy the movement of vehicles and donkey carts to the main markets.
During the project implementation, WARDI will maintain regularly:
(1) Consultation meetings with women, men, girls, boys including people with disabilities and minorities.
(2) The visibility of the project activities; communication and coordination mechanisms with the FS Cluster partners in the target regions, the Local Authorities, FS cluster and OCHA.
(3) Keep the project documents in safe custody with good quality.
(4) WARDI project staff will undertake supervision and monitoring of the project activities on daily basis during the project implementation.

Project Activities

1. With the community stakeholders including women, people with disabilities and minorities, and Local Authority support, select and register the 2000 IDP HHs and vulnerable host communities (12,000 men, women, girls and boys). Identify & select Food Vendors to supply planned food items near their settlements and ensure the location and access routes to shops are safe. Distribute the planned food items equivalence to US$100/HH to the 2,000 registered HHs at the selected vendors’ shops on monthly basis. Undertake monitoring & supervision of the food distribution events at each shop.2. Provide 10 grinding machines and 10 oil processing machines as Income Generation Activity to 200 women headed IDP HHs who have malnourished children or people in disabilities, benefiting 1,200 men, women and children. Each 10 women are grouped and will provide 1 machine. WARDI will also provide the 20 women groups US$500 as start-up.3. Train the 200 IGA women on operation & maintenance of grinding machine (100 women) and oil processing machine (100 women). The training also included literacy/numeracy.4. Distribute farm input to 1,500 farmers in Hiran and L/Shabelle, benefiting around 9,000 men, women and children to start their farming activities destroyed during the recent flooding and not received any external assistance and provide 1,500 ha machinery land preparation (3hours/ha) to the target 1500 farmers, benefiting 9,000 men, women and children, including people with disabilities and minorities. 5. Provide Restocking to 500 HHs, who lost their animals during drought and floods (300HH in Hiran & 200 HHs in L/Shabelle) with 5 shoats/HH, benefitting around 3,000 men, women, girls and boys. 6. Conduct training to 150 farmers (50 men and 100 women) and 50 pastoralists (25 men and 25 women) on best farming technologies and methodologies and animal husbandry respectively. 7. Rehabilitate 35 km flood affected feeder roads in L/Shabelle and Hiran regions with CFW through voucher to 1,000 skilled and unskilled workers (700 in Hiran and 300 in L/Shabelle), benefiting around 6,000 men, women and children. Each unskilled worker will receive US$5/day and the skilled workers will receive US$10/day for 3 months. The beneficiaries will take directly from the nearest money vendor to avoid risk of looting. 8) Construct/rehabilitate 10 reinforced concrete culverts in order to make easy the movements of the vehicles and donkey carts to the main markets with out disturbing the rehabilitated irrigation canals. WARDI will maintain regularly: The visibility of the project activities; communication and coordination mechanisms with regional FS Cluster partners, the Local Authorities and OCHA; Keep project documents in safe custody with good quality.

Mitigation Measures

2a. Target communities always compose of different categories or groups of people whose needs are different, including men, boys, women, girls, elderly, disabled persons, minorities etc. During WARDI intervention, WARDI will consider the needs of these people's rights in an equitable and fair manner, because each and every person has full right to address his/her human rights to live in peace, prosperity with dignity. When targeting the project beneficiaries, special attention will be given to the Elderly, disabilities and minorities. In many cases, also unaccompanied ones by any others; female-headed households, pregnant and lactating women (PLW), families with large number of children, widows with no partner/husband, orphans families, as well as disabled persons, male or female members of the target households will be given an account. WARDI will take these actions during the project implementation: 1. Prioritize safety & dignity and avoid causing harm 2. Promote equitable and impartial access to all 3. Ensure accountability to affected populations, and 4. Strengthen participation and empowerment of the target beneficiaries. Hence, regardless of age, Gender, status, origin, or clan or sub-clan of the beneficiaries, resources will be availed accordingly, in order to let them meet their own rights as HUMANS. Failure to do so, means lack of Transparency, Accountability & Justice for all.3c. WARDI will make sure: The location and access routes to the distribution points and services are safe. Such as Identification for central locations - beneficiaries should not have to traverse long distances or pass through conflict zones to access distribution points; Avoid placing distribution points near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire or violence. Weapon bearers should not be present inside distribution points; Distributions should take place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall; Ensure the mode and frequency of distribution minimises safety risk – be aware that beneficiaries may face theft, intimidation, sexual assault and extortion whilst taking their rations home; WARDI will work in coordination with protection actors to put in place measures to prevent and respond to physical/sexual violence arising from food distributions; both host communities and IDPs will have access to the services to avoid tensions between IDPs and host communities.4d. WARDI will hold at the inception of the project, community mobilization, and sensitization meetings with all stakeholders to officially launch the project. During the sensitization meetings, the project objectives, implementation strategies, scope, beneficiary selection criteria, beneficiary entitlement and roles and duties of each stakeholder including M&E roles will be discussed and agreed. WARDI will develop elaborate beneficiary complain and feedback tool, project beneficiaries and stakeholders will be given 1 day workshop on how to use the complaint /feedback system to enhance transparency and accountability. Community ownership of the project will be done through formation of structures such as engendered Beneficiary Representatives, and Camp Relief Committees. WARDI will establish engendered committees (women participation will be strictly adhered) that will have hand-in-hand working relations with the project team and beneficiary communities; both sides will exchange any information related to the management and implementation of the project activities. Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) will also be used to make sure that the beneficiaries are satisfied with services provided and complaints can be channeled to the right direction. there will be direct contact between community leaders and project team as technique of CFRM to act accordingly in case of complaints. WARDI will adhere the principles of "do no Harm" through not creating any partiality and conflict.

Please provide further explanation

The project activities include rehabilitation of productive assets like irrigation canals destroyed by recent floods to boost crop production in the area as well as clearance of roads linking villages to the main market to improve access to traffic and human movements in Lower Shabelle. The rehabilitation of canals and roads with CFW contribute to income injection to the communities to improve their livelihoods and benefit from their potentiality to address their own needs in a productive and sustainable manner. Both canals and roads are communally owned, used and maintained. After the project ends the communities will sustain the interventions in a participatory and supportive manner by contributing individual capacity in kind or in cash. The project will also boost the livelihood assets for resilience and recovery to the farmers and pastoralists through provision of agricultural and livestock support and Income generation activities provided to the project beneficiaries. This will support the beneficiaries to start their livelihood activities and not depend any more to external support. WARDI will also improve their capacity to manage the small resource they received through training, in order to adopt the new technologies and methodologies for farming and animal husbandry and other income generation activities, which is included in the project services. Cash transfer will stimulate the market growth and more suppliers will restock their goods to meet the demand. Suppliers will be vetted during registrations to make sure that prices are constant over the period of implementations. Cash for Work (CfW) stimulates recovery of the local economy by creating short-term work, reintroducing income flow, supporting local businesses through the purchase of construction materials, and encouraging households to purchase food and non-food items locally. CfW will be used during rehabilitation of damaged community assets while maintaining the dignity of the affected population through meaningful community engagement in the selection and implementation of projects. CfW will provides short-term employment opportunities for large segments of the affected population and helps provide economically vulnerable groups with a means to sustain themselves. CfW will help prevent the sell-off of assets and accumulation of debt that can frequently result from the short-term economic pressures caused by conflict or disaster. Vouchers will be used during emergencies where households will require food ratios.
