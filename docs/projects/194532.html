Project Summary

The war in Syria continues to have a profound impact on people across the country and at least half of the people affected by the crisis are children. The humanitarian situation in the country remains severe as the decline of the economic situation exacerbates the effects of the conflict. The education sector is impacted by low availability of education services, few functional schools, shortage of teachers, extremely low teachers' salaries. The targeted areas are expecting large numbers of returnees to further stretch the education sector’s capacity. The impact of the COVID19, Cholera, rapid devaluation of the Syrian Pound and the international sanctions are putting additional strain on the population and local administrations.
According to the HNO 2023, at national level average, self-reported attendance of the children between 6-17 for the 2021-2022 academic year was 88% with 12% reported to be non-attending, while in Idleb it reaches 15%, in Raqqa – 25%, in Aleppo – 16%. Attendance starts to decline from 11 years old at steeper rate for boys through their teenage years. The main reasons why they do not send their children to any form of learning 48% of households cited those economic related issues were the most prominent reason why children were not in school, 27% of households reported that schools were not able to accommodate the child/ren’s.
Thus, FCA projects are focusing on the pockets of low attendance to schools and offers tailor made solution specific per area.
All of the areas that FCA program is covering area are marked as 4 or above on the severity scale. Most of the target location are newly opened returnees areas with the high demand for education services and often presence of operational schools is a motivating factor for the families to return to the area.
The project directly contributes to HRP priorities both in terms of geographical prioritization and by combining soft (non-formal education, protection etc) and hard components (school rehabilitation, school furniture, students kits etc) in education focusing on quality education.
Access to education related activities:

3.3. Recreational activities in schools

Quality of Education related activities

4.2 Teachers' training
4.3. Support for children with disabilities through aids and teacher training.
Efforts will be made to link parents of OOSC to the livelihood program of FCA or other partners in the area.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

R1: Learning facilities or their surroundings are structurally unsafe or do not provide sufficient protection from the elements (rain, snow, cold, dust, etc.) or from public health risks including Covid-19. M1: FCA will make sure that schools buildings and grounds are safe and protected. This will include stairs, walls, roofs, flooring, safe electric installation, water, etc. All School grounds should be fenced. All construction works will be supervised and monitored by technical staff (engineers and MEAL officer). FCA will ensure Child Safeguarding measures are in place before work start. R2: Tensions within the community or between the organization and community due to feeling of being unfairly treated, insufficiently consulted or concerns with the project. M2: Consultation with beneficiaries and local structures, transparent process to be followed. FCA will advocate with sector/donor that that donor conditions do not undermine the ability to response adequately according to do no harm principles. R3: Unequal treatment of people with disabilities that cannot access education without additional support. M3: FCA will start cooperation with two disabilities organization that will help to develop methods and provide assistance to the children in need. We will also provide specific aids to CwD to be able to access education to compensate for the disability.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

R1. Aid workers or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they should be serving, particularly children. The risk may be heightened for children with disabilities, those unaccompanied, orphans and younger children. M1.Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policies should be in place, practised and monitored. Polices will cover standards of behaviour for the organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and include accountability measures. Polices should address risks at the organizational level and the program level. Trainings and refreshers on PSEA should be done systematically. M2. FCA staff and targeted communities should have access to safe and effective reporting systems including knowing when they will get a response to their report and having the right to report anonymously. M3. Targeted communities and staff of the learning facilities should be aware of the organization’s policies and procedures. M4. All FCA staff sign PESA as a part of the job contract.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R1: Risks related to approvals. M1: MoU with the MoE has been signed in Jan '21 and MOLA in June '23. It creates background for smooth cooperation with DoEs in the respective governorates. R2: Delays for national staff to access selected areas of interventions. Access restrictions on international staff. M2: In case FCA is not able to reach the target population, and related delay in start-up and implementation of activities a closer coordination and engagement with SARC and MoE will take place. R3: Risk of insecurity, conflict and violence. M3: FCA will regularly security SOPs and provide refresher training for all staff. In the event that the conflict breaks out, security contingency plans already in place will be followed. Evacuation to safer areas will be evoked when deemed necessary. In case of inaccessibility as a result of insecurity, activities will be moved to safer areas while trying follow beneficiaries to their areas of refuge. R4: High inflation rate. M4: In case the price of goods or services will increase, FCA will inform the donor on the difficulties to follow the agreed plan and proposes revisions. FCA will closely monitor the exchange rate of SYP and will make sure that impact of the exchange rate on the project activities is minimal. Joint action through DINGO. R5: Transfer of funds to Syria. M5: Request donors to send funds directly to Syria. Consultations through DINGO. R6. Increased returnees/IDP caseload. M6: close monitoring of the population movement
