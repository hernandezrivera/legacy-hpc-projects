Project Summary

Community Action Organization (CAO) proposes to address the main causes of malnutrition through lifesaving nutrition interventions linked to health, FSL, and WASH to foster a meaningful impact on communities' resilience and prevent childhood morbidity and mortality related to malnutrition among vulnerable children and women, CAO's proposed response will focus on both treatment and preventative approaches, through Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) and Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) approaches. CAO Emergency nutrition programs will align with cluster objectives and approaches. I) Blanket Supplementary feeding program, ii) Community mobilization to increase access and coverage of CMAM services, iii) Promotion and provision of MIYCN counseling services and social behavior change, iv) Improving program quality through advocacy for integration with health, WASH and FSL v) Treatment of children and PLW with acute malnutrition through Outpatient therapeutic programs, vi) Maternal dietary supplementation, vii) Promotion of optimal IYCF through mother-to-mother support groups. viii) Improving Dietary Diversity through household kitchen gardening and chicken rearing piloted in select locations, ix) Improving nutrition information management systems and operations research to ensure an evidence-based response, x) Building capacity for frontline nutrition workers and institutions at all levels through on-the-job training and monitoring.
CAO will ensure localization is realized during the intervention, by collaborating with existing community nutrition structures, such as Mother to Mother support Groups, Father support groups, health and nutrition workers, and building their capacities. CAO will conduct supportive supervision. The service will be delivered through static and outreach/mobile facilities for remote and hard-to-reach areas in the targeted project locations. CAO will work with MOH, UNICEF, and WFP to secure the timely provision of Nutrition supplies necessary for Nutrition Service delivery. CAO will establish 5 OTP/TSFP sites in New Fangak and 3 OTP/TSFP sites in Canal/Pigi Counties.
The intervention will target a total of 2,300 children U5 with SAM cases, 9,600 children U5 with MAM cases, 8,900 Pregnant and breastfeeding women (PBW) with MAM cases, 15,940 MIYCN and 10,925 with VASD services. CAO will deliberate efforts to enhance coordination and integration with the Health, WASH, FSL, and clusters to address the drivers of malnutrition through a multi-sectoral approach that aims to reduce vulnerabilities, prevent malnutrition, and build resilience among vulnerable people. The nutrition sites will also promote kitchen gardening, screening and treating malaria, managing diarrhea, and mainstreaming child protection and gender-based violence risk mitigation. CAO will also support hygiene interventions to improve nutrition outcomes and reduce the risk of hygiene-related morbidities. CAO will monitor the nutrition situation and the needs of the affected people through KAP and SMART surveys, monthly nutrition program data collection and analysis, and joint monitoring visits. Supportive supervision and monitoring visits will enhance service delivery and engage with the community and other stakeholders to gauge evolving needs. CNVs will be supported to undertake household visits, conduct regular screening of malnourished children and women to ensure timely detection and treatment, and report monthly. Data collection tools and analysis will be strengthened to enhance the monitoring of integrated services. Accountability to affected populations will be enhanced via community awareness mechanisms, enhancing community complaints and feedback mechanisms in the nutrition facilities by establishing a well-attended private complaint and feedback desk and conducting community focus group discussions with the community.

1. Needs

The acute food insecurity and malnutrition situation in South Sudan is deteriorating as a result of the economic crisis, repeated widespread flooding conflict, displacements, and insecurity. According to FSNMS Round 30 and SMART surveys' findings, the national Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels remain above the WHO emergency threshold of 15%, with 2.1 million children aged 6 – 59 months at risk of suffering from acute malnutrition and 1.1 million PBW at risk of acute malnutrition in 2025 and will need urgent treatment. The nutrition situation among vulnerable populations (U5 and PBWs) in South Sudan remains dire. Currently, acute malnutrition rates in 49 counties exceed the emergency threshold of 15%, with one county in Upper Nile projected to reach IPC-AMN phase 5. The impact of severe food insecurity with many individuals and households in emergency IPC phase 4, floods, displacement, inter-communal conflict, erratic climatic hazards (ongoing flood impact in the primary rainy season), poor macroeconomic with massive economic downturn with an inflation rate of more than 400%, disease outbreaks, and poor WASH conditions at household and community level, and a loss of livelihoods continue to aggravate the current worse nutrition indicators and the entire humanitarian crisis in Sudan Jonglei state, especially Fangak and Canal/Pigi county, is one of the states currently experiencing the effect of flooding and the influx of South Sudanese returnees, which continues to drive high humanitarian needs, putting additional strain on the host households who are sharing scarce resources with them. In the current IPC food security situation, about 1,112,000 people, 53% in Jonglei state, are in the crisis stage (IPC Phase 3+) until November 2024.According to the 2025 IPC for Acute Malnutrition analysis (IPC- AMN), Fangak County is currently projected to be in the Critical stage of IPC AMN Phase 4. High prevalence of disease burden, inadequate/optimal sub-IYCF practices with only 4.6% of children 6-23 months receiving the required food quality at the required frequency, acute food insecurity, severe flooding, and sub national level violence and conflicts continue driving mass displacements of vulnerable people. These have all been linked to the critical IPC AMN phase 4 and IPC phase 5 levels in Fangak County. In Addition, the severity of acute malnutrition among children and PBWs in Fangak and Canal/Pigi counties is likely to deteriorate with the two counties classified as IPC AMN Phase 4 (Critical) to IPC AMN Phase 5 (Extremely Critical) during the lean season (April to June 2025) (SS IPC Dec 2024 to Mar 2025). High prevalence of diseases (49.4% of children). Low national level minimum Dietary Diversity at 16.5%, and Minimum Meal Frequency at 7.5%, with only 2.1% of children aged 6-23 months receiving Minimum Acceptable Diet (MAD); severe food insecurity with most HHs classified as IPC Phase 3+, Low access to health services exacerbated by effects of flooding which destroy health and nutrition facilities; and WASH facilities resulting into Poor WASH practices.The operational context of the two countries makes it difficult to tackle the determinants of malnutrition effectively, given that they face yearly floods. Fangak County is vast & complex to access, with only 5 nutrition facilities accessible through the river, a task that also remains hard. While access challenges can be tackled given the available resources, inadequate funding has not enabled the sustainability of CAO's proposed nutrition plans to conduct outreach nutrition services as should be the case. Given the operational presence of CAO in Fangak and Canal/Pigi counties, an integrated approach will be possible to tackle the immediate and underlying causes of malnutrition while coordinating with stakeholders and strengthening CHD efforts. CAO will address malnutrition issues among children U5 and PBWs, targeting returnees, IDPs, and the Host community.

2. Project activities

Activity 1. Provide SBCC on MIYCN to Protect, support, and promote optimal breastfeeding and complementary feeding practices. Activity 2. Provide micronutrient supplements to young children and women. Activity 3. Screen and treat girls and boys under five years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by severe and moderate acute malnutrition. Activity 4. Integrate child stimulation and early childhood development services in providing care for children with acute malnutrition. Activity 5. Work with the WASH Cluster to ensure the provision of WASH kits to children with acute malnutrition and sensitize communities with hygiene and sanitation messages. Activity 6. Work with FSL Cluster to promote chicken raring, establish kitchen gardens, promote seed multiplication for vegetable gardens, and support fish preservation. Activity 7. Collaborate with the health cluster to ensure the prevention, screening, and treatment of malaria and the management of diarrhea during nutrition service delivery. Activity 8. Work with the Protection Cluster to ensure mainstreaming of services and referral of child protection and Gender Based Violence (GBV) cases identified in the nutrition sites. Activity 9. Link with Cash and Voucher Assistance programs to benefit nutritionally vulnerable children and women. Activity 10. Collaborate with health cluster in the management of diarrhea, including providing Zinc and ORS to children with diarrhea. Activity 11. Undertake community engagement activities while designing, implementing, and monitoring nutrition services to strengthen Accountability to Affected Populations. Activity 12. Conduct regular program and field monitoring and supportive supervision to strengthen evidence-based decision-making. Activity 13. Conduct nutrition coordination meetings with the government, implementing partners, and donors. Activity 14. Lias with GBV actors in the project areas to ensure the existence and utilization of referral pathways Activity 15. Conduct protection/GBV safety audits in all the nutrition facilities to enhance protection and prevent GBV Activity 16. Work closely with the CHD while continuously providing technical capacity building and operational support
