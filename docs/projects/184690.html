Project Summary

Among the various underlying factors, food insecurity remains a major driver of malnutrition in Somalia.
Based on the latest projections by FSNAU, from October to December 2021, food insecurity is expected to further deteriorate among poor rural, urban, and displaced populations due to the impacts of anticipated, below-average 2021 Deyr season rainfall, continued insecurity and other food security related risk factors, including rising food prices and cost of living, declining availability of milk for both consumption and sale, and a likely reduction in agricultural employment opportunities during the forthcoming Deyr season. It is therefore estimated that around 3.5 million people across Somalia are expected to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes between October and December 2021. In 2021, flash flooding was particularly felt in Belet Weyne district. Based on a trend analysis of recent years, it is estimated that up to 450,168 additional civilians will be displaced by floods in 2022 (HNO, 2021).
In agropastoral livelihood zones, where the main shocks include erratically distributed rainfall and conflict, poor households have experienced substantial crop losses and low income from agricultural employment. With few alternative sources of food and income after current food stocks are depleted, they face moderate to large food consumption gaps through late 2021. In riverine livelihood zones along the Shabelle, seasonal floods inundated farmland, destroyed crops, and displaced local populations, leading to significant crop losses and the loss of income from agricultural employment. The past floods destroyed parts of the existing irrigation infrastructure. This in turn reduces the expected yield of agricultural production. Protective structures along the river are inadequate or have been destroyed. FAO's Food Security and Nutrition Analysis Unit (FSNAU) assesses the current Estimated Nutrition Situation (GAM) for children under 5 years of age in the entire Hiran region and along the entire course of the Shabelle River as "critical" (Projection February - April 2021). From the data available, Belet weyne has 52,448 people in need who are stressed and 166,084 people who are severe and in Afgooye they are 102,715 people stressed and 218,270 who are severe. These group of vulnerable people will need immediate assistance in terms of food security. Furthermore, people with disability have be left out and they face acute food insecurity. Wardi will use inclusion criteria to meet requirements of disable persons. Wardi has experience in undertaking food security program in both regions which include provision of livestock such as goats and chicken distribution.
The Specific Objective (Outcome) of the project is improved food security and increased resilience for 5,200 vulnerable community or 31,200 men, women, boys, girls, people with disabilities and minorities. The proposed project will provide:
1) Farm input (seeds, fertilizers, tools) to 1,500 HH farmers and agro pastoralists in Lower Shabelle and Hiran regions for recovery and resilience, benefitting around 9,000 women, girls, boys and men affected by crisis to protect their assets and generate income for their households.
2) Restocking to 1,000 rural HHs (Each HH will receive 5 goats), representing 6,000 men, women, boys and girls including people with disabilities and minorities, who have lost their animals due to the drought/floods and have no alternative livelihood assets to survive.
3) The project will train 300 farmers on the best farming technologies, animal husbandry.
4) Wardi will construct/rehabilitate 10 reinforced concrete culverts in Hiran region affected by floods to make easy the movement of vehicles and donkey carts to the main markets.
5) Distribution of food rations in form of food vouchers to 500 vulnerable IDPs and host communities in the target area.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Target communities always compose of different categories or groups of people whose needs are different, including men, boys, women, girls, elderly, disabled persons, minorities etc. During WARDI intervention, WARDI will consider the needs of these people's rights in an equitable and fair manner, because each and every person has full right to address his/her human rights to live in peace, prosperity with dignity. When targeting the project beneficiaries, special attention will be given to the Elderly, disabilities and minorities. In many cases, also unaccompanied ones by any others; female-headed households, pregnant and lactating women (PLW), families with large number of children, widows with no partner/husband, orphans families, as well as disabled persons, male or female members of the target households will be given an account. WARDI will take these actions during the project implementation: 1. Prioritize safety & dignity and avoid causing harm 2. Promote equitable and impartial access to all 3. Ensure accountability to affected populations, and 4. Strengthen participation and empowerment of the target beneficiaries. Hence, regardless of age, Gender, status, origin, or clan or sub-clan of the beneficiaries, resources will be availed accordingly, in order to let them meet their own rights as HUMANS. Failure to do so, means lack of Transparency, Accountability & Justice for all. WARDI will make sure that the location and access routes to the distribution points and services are safe. Such as Identification for central locations - beneficiaries should not have to traverse long distances or pass through conflict zones to access distribution points; Avoid placing distribution points near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire or violence. Weapon bearers should not be present inside distribution points; Distributions should take place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall; Ensure the mode and frequency of distribution minimises safety risk – be aware that beneficiaries may face theft, intimidation, sexual assault and extortion whilst taking their rations home; WARDI will work in coordination with protection actors to put in place measures to prevent and respond to physical/sexual violence arising from food distributions; both host communities and IDPs will have access to the services to avoid tensions between IDPs and host communities. WARDI will hold at the inception of the project, community mobilization, and sensitization meetings with all stakeholders to officially launch the project. During the sensitization meetings, the project objectives, implementation strategies, scope, beneficiary selection criteria, beneficiary entitlement and roles and duties of each stakeholder including M&E roles will be discussed and agreed. WARDI will develop elaborate beneficiary complain and feedback tool, project beneficiaries and stakeholders will be given 1 day workshop on how to use the complaint /feedback system to enhance transparency and accountability. Community ownership of the project will be done through formation of structures such as engendered Beneficiary Representatives, and Camp Relief Committees. WARDI will establish engendered committees (women participation will be strictly adhered) that will have hand-in-hand working relations with the project team and beneficiary communities; both sides will exchange any information related to the management and implementation of the project activities. Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) will also be used to make sure that the beneficiaries are satisfied with services provided and complaints can be channelled to the right direction. there will be direct contact between community leaders and project team as technique of CFRM to act accordingly in case of complaints. WARDI will adhere the principles of "do no Harm" through not creating any partiality and conflict.
