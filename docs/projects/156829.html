Project Summary

HRO will implement the project activities through establishing a center in two places; the first location will be in North-West of Aleppo (Darat Izza city), and the second location will be in Ariha city in rural Idlib. The focus will be on reducing risks and strengthening protective factors for children to ensure their physical, social and emotional development; thus contributing to the cluster priority of protecting children and mitigating the effects of violence and displacement.
HRO staff will be wholly responsible for the implementation, supervision, and management and monitoring of the project. HRO will seek to link CFSs to other services such as education, health and social services to enhance referral of children in need of special assistance.
This component will target 10,076 (1158 men, 1158 women, 3880 boys, 3880 girls) within the activities of raising awareness in child rights, child protection and prevention of family separation, training community leaders on PSS in CP and GBV, CPMS and ERW, PSW manual,and PFA,along with establishing child protection networks, Provide semi-structured and structured psychosocial support activities to vulnerable children, provide specialized services to CAAFAG and other vulnerable children, Trace and reunify CAAFAG & separated children with partners/caregivers, Provide follow visits to CAAFAG & other vulnerable children.
Also, HRO will deliver another component in Al Madiq Castle, Hama countryside, for empowering teachers and headmasters to help children in schools to overcome traumas, losses and psychosocial adversities along with child protection activities. This empowerment will be achieved throughout training sessions and workshops where teachers will gain the tools, skills, and methods of dealing with students and to activate as much as possible of their brain space for educational purpose rather than being sunk with ideas of loss and failure that resulted by the current events. The most important skills that should be delivered to teachers and headmasters are skills for integrating PSS and psycho-pedagogical components in their regular work with the class (i.e. in lessons) and in other job related activities (meeting with parents, etc.), skills for providing individual support to children in need, skills for working with parents, skills for coping with their own traumas, losses, and job related burdens and adversities throughout the self /Staff care activities. The in-school module is supposed to contribute to the social wellbeing and social reconstruction of the covered communities as a whole.
During six months, this component will be delivered to 250 teachers, headmasters, and instructors where the training will be within the first four months, where the fifth and sixth month are for delivering supervision for the trained personnel.

Needs

Children throughout Syria continue to face pervasive violence and a range of protection risks. Grave child rights violations remain a significant concern, including in areas where hostilities have declined, with children at risk of killing and injury, recruitment and use in hostilities, torture, detention, abduction, sexual violence, attacks on schools and hospitals and denial of humanitarian access by parties to the conflict. During the first 9 months of 2018, the UN verified 2,354 such violations against children, in 13 of 14 governorates of Syria , illustrating persistent trends of violence though not the full scope of conflict-related violence affecting children . This included more than 1,300 verified incident of killing or injuring of children . Respondents in assessed communities reporting explosive hazard contamination in Syria identify adolescent boys and pre-adolescent boys as the groups most at risk of death or injury from accidents, with incidents reported while children were playing and going to school .The ongoing or recurrent conflict environment experienced by many boys and girls over the past seven years has profoundly affected their psychosocial well-being – weakening their sense of security, purpose, control and significance, or exercising social agency, and causing profound distress. Many are without civil documentation to prove their identity, access basic services and safeguard their rights. It is estimated that more than 2.6 million children remain displaced and separation from caregivers is a reported child protection issue in all 14 governorates of Syria putting children at risk of exploitation, abuse or neglect . Events leading to family separation indicate how related protection and child protection issues increase vulnerability of affected boys and girls, with death or disappearance of caregivers, disappearance of children, separation due to displacement or conflict, child marriage and child recruitment among cited reasons for family separations . Respondents in those assessed communities where discrimination in the delivery of humanitarian assistance emerged, identified unaccompanied girls as the most likely group to face such discrimination .The conflict and its impact on children and families have fuelled negative coping mechanisms such as child labour, early marriage and child recruitment, putting adolescent boys and girls at particular risk, while straining the capacities of families and communities to protect children. Child labour is reported to occur widely in all 14 governorates of Syria . Child labour interferes with children’s schooling by depriving them of the opportunity to attend school, obliging them to leave school prematurely, or requiring them to attempt to combine school attendance with excessively long and heavy work. Child marriage is reported in 12 out of 14 governorates mainly affecting adolescent girls, though adolescent boys are also at risk . Girls and boys are married early as a household negative coping strategy to “mitigate” protection risks and to respond to the worsening economic situation of families. Recruitment of children, particularly adolescent boys, by all parties to the conflict continues to be reported in 12 of 14 governorates of Syria and the use of children in the conflict has increasingly normalized. In the first none months of 2018, the Syria MRM verified 631 incidents of recruitment and use of children (8% boys) , with 92% used in combat roles, and around a quarter under the age of 15. Payment of stipends and material incentives, family and community influence, as well as the need for protection, survival, desire for revenge, status and identity, and intimidation are cited push and pull factors of recruitment and re-recruitment.

Implementation of activities

A.1.1: Train 116 CP staff, 10 CPNs and community leaders on PSS in CP, GBV, CPMS and ERW, PSW manual & PFAA.1.2: Establish 10 CP networks (CPNS)These Interventions will aim at addressing vulnerabilities related to child labour, child recruitment, child marriage and family separation. HRO will coordinate with other service providers to ensure that children formally or informally released by armed groups are provided with appropriate reintegration services in their communities. This project will work with leaders, Community Based CP Networks (CBCPNs), families, and youth groups to prevent recruitment and change norms that favour children’s participation in armed forces or groups.A.2.1: Provide structured PSS activities to 1000 vulnerable children (500 girls & 500 boys) in CFSA. 2.2: Provide semi-structured PSS activities to 5000 vulnerable children (2500 girls & 2500 boys) throughout-reach teamsIn the child friendly space (CFS), dedicated time and space will be created for adolescent girls where they can be together. HRO will ensure that a female facilitator is available to work with the girls, who can offer confidentiality, warmth and advice. HRO will seek also to link CFS to other services to enhance referral of children in need of special assistance.A. 3.1: Document and provide specialized services to 100 CAAFAG and other vulnerable children (separated & unaccompanied children, GBV survivors, children with disabilities and MHPSS)A.3.2: Trace and reunify 30 CAAFAG & separated children with partners/caregiversA.3.3: Provide follow up visits to 30 CAAFAG & other vulnerable childrenChildren associated with armed groups (will be approached by HRO case managers), and children with complicated mental health problems will be referred to other service providers.Children involved or at risk of hazardous labour will be approached by HRO case managers to comprehensively be managed by working with children and their caregivers to promote minimal standards and involve children in HRO CFSs.A.4.1: Deliver ERW awareness to 1000 children & 1200 adults using IEC materials.A.4.2: Conduct awareness raising events to 600 children & 1000 adults on child rights, CP and prevention of family separation targeting IDPs and host communities.HRO will deliver risk awareness to IDPs and local community in schools and communities. Community outreach staff will use posters and leaflets to deliver sessions in targeted areas and groups.Risk education activities are very important for IDPs and local communities who will hopefully return back to their places after the cease fire, and will benefit from RE activities to avoid further harm.HRO will also be involved in raising awareness on prevention of recruitment of children by armed groups, children involved in hazardous labour and prevention of family separation. The main target is the parents, caregivers and community stakeholders such as religious leaders, local council members, teachers, medical point’s directors, and etc. HRO will train case workers to identify and respond to the needs of such children. HRO will use the CP sub-cluster SOPs and referral pathway to provide the needed services through case management.A.5.1: holding trainings for education personnel, teachers and headmasters , (PSS, CP, GBV, close management, self-care): Where the following steps will be followed:- Distributing candidates into groups- Conducting pre-test for the trainees- Starting the process of training by two training teamsA.5.2: conducting supervision for the trained personnel HRO teams will provide pro bono consultations to the trainees. Moreover, each headmaster will implement a staff care plan which is developed to target the school teachers. Protection Risk Analysis

1- Risk: Unavailability of transportation means for people with disabilities to attend the session, especially the awareness raising sessions and PSS sessions.Mitigation: Providing means to facilitate their attendance such as wheelchairs.2- Risk: Unavailability of mechanisms and policies to document the CP cases, where they are exposed to be loss and breach of privacy.Mitigation: Providing templates, from the CP sub-cluster, to document cases and keep data safe. Also, case workers will be trained on these policies and procedures to protect data.3- Risk: Inability of the team to deal with people with disabilities and vulnerable groups such as teenagers and elderly.Mitigation: Training the team on the method of receiving all ages and specifying programs to these categories. As for the people with disabilities, they enjoy the priority to attend all of these sessions.4- Risk: The team is not aware of risks related to PSEA.Mitigation: Training the team on PSEA.5- Risk: The team and beneficiaries are not aware of the importance of accountability and having high quality services.Mitigation: Establishing complaint mechanism, familiarizing beneficiaries with the method of reporting complaints, familiarizing the team with the importance of this mechanism in order to develop the sense of accountability. 6- Risk: When conducting referral for a case, the survivor may be exposed to a higher risk due to location of the referred service such as relief, medical, or inability of the service provider to deal with the survivor, or even abusing them.Mitigation: Higher level of coordination with the service provider prior to referring cases, along with raising the awareness of the provider on how to deal with survivors cases. Also, providing transportation means and accompanying the referred case to the location of the service provider.7- Risk: Difficult access to the centers to which children are referredMitigation: Provide transportation for children and their families to reach the centers referred to it, or child protection case managers will go with them to the referral centers.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption 1: We assume that the security situation in the defined area is staying the same.Risk 1 : Changing the security situation that interfere with implementation of the project.Mitigation Strategies: We will establish a response plan that can be activated according to the situation.Designing the activities in a flexible way where we can change the way of conducting the activity according to the situation so as to achieve the wanted results. and we can focus on more suitable activities like doing PFA . Stopping the activities for short period. moving the center to another place if deemed necessary after negotiation with OCHA. Assumption 2: Local council and authorities are continue to allow for our work, as the case right now with other projects.Risk: The local authorities decide to prevent us from implementing the project or part of it.Mitigation: as preventive measures we will coordinate with the local authorities and negotiate with them about the best way to implement the project and discuss the importance the project that will help. Assumption 3: The attitudes and needs of the population stay the same as we found in the needs assessments .Risk 3: changing in the attitudes or priorities of the population that will affect their willingness to benefit from the project activities.Mitigation strategies: Will work on awareness raising through out the project period and will get the feed back from the beneficiaries about the services and adapt accordingly . Assumption 4: There are experienced people in the targeted communitiesRisk 4 : Due to lack of experienced people in the targeted communities, IDP may be recruited based on their previous experience.Mitigation Strategies: equality in opportunities among host community and IDPs will be ensured throughout selecting people who are residing in the targeted area regardless of being IDP or from host community. Job vacancies advertisements will be shared with the local councils. Assumption 5: The materials will be purchased from local vendors in the target areaRisk 5 : Risk of triggering sensitivity with the local vendors during the procurement and purchasing materials from outside the targeted areas.Mitigation Strategies: All materials that are available in the targeted area should be purchased locally in accordance with HRO & SEMA procurement policies. Assumption 6: the political and security conditions doesn’t change in the targeted areas Risk 6 : Change of the political and security conditions in the targeted areasMitigation Strategies: If the GoS does assume control of an area that then prevents HRO from being operational, HRO would most likely make the necessary arrangements to move staff, equipment and structures to opposition-held areas where mental health needs are not covered.
