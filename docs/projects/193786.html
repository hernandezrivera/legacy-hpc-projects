Project Summary

2.11 million Palestinians live in Gaza Strip. The Multi Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) 2022 indicates that the Gazan population consists of 8.2% female-headed households (FHHs) and 21% households (HHs) living with at least one member with a disability. The same assessment shows that 59.8% of targeted HHs has any kind of damage or defects at their shelters, of which 69.7% reported having a disability and 58.5% are FHH (60% Female and 53.5% Male over 60 years old, while 57.6% female and 61.6% male from 18-59 years old).
In partnership with the Gaza Culture and Development Group (GCDG), the project aims to strengthen HHs’ living in sub-standard shelters to access dignified shelters and immediate essential needs by creating a safe, accessible, and healthy environment. This project will support 561 individuals (276 Females including 104 girls, 143 adult-women, and 29 older women, in addition to 285 Men including 112 boys, 142 adult-men, and 31 older men) living in sub-standard shelters in the governorate of Khan Younis and Gaza. The MSNA 2022 identifies both locations as the highest governorate with stressful or severe shelter conditions. Vulnerable groups such as persons with disabilities (at least 100 beneficiaries of the targets), persons with injuries, older persons, and single FHH (46 single FHH) living in substandard shelters will be prioritized and identified from the waiting lists of the MoSD. The project will promote the active participation of beneficiaries in the shelter-upgrading and shelter adaptation process at all stages and will provide space for technical exchanges with other shelter cluster members on accessibility and barrier-free environment topics. HHs members in need of external service will be referred through the established protection and GBV referral systems in the Strip.
Following the capacity building by the project team, the project end users will carry out the shelter upgrading, and adaptation process based on the Self-Help Approach by receiving three cash transfers via banks system to cover expenses: 1) Advance payment to start-up work; 2) Second instalment after verification visits confirm progress; and 3) Final payment upon completion. Inclusive participation of women, men, girls, and boys will be guaranteed in the application of the self-help approach methodology; a method that also enforces the ownership of the beneficiary. The project intervention will apply the shelter minimum standards for upgrading, SPHERE standards, and ISO international standards for accessibility, encouraging the participation of the most vulnerable HHs members in the final decision-making. Furthermore, 80% of the targeted HHs in need of essential NFIs will be supported through in-kind or vouchers (according to market functioning) that may contain winterization, IPC, dignity, hygiene kits, and/or kitchen set and functional assistive devices. A sensitization activity on the use of the items will be provided during the distribution to ensure the correct use and maintenance. The project’s impact will be closely monitored on the operational level by HI. At the same time, the use and dissemination of clear information regarding the HI inclusive feedback and complaint mechanism guarantee accountability to the affected population.
The proposed intervention fully aligns with the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) 2023 and the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2023. The action also supports the achievement of 2023 HRP Strategic Objectives, specifically objective 2: “Ensure acutely vulnerable Palestinians under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank have access to essential services”; and specific objective 2 of the shelter cluster: “Vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns are supported to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacities” and Cluster Objective 3: “Increase capacity to respond to natural or manmade hazards in a timely, efficient and effective manner”.

1 - Needs

The MSNA 2022 highlights that the Gazan population consists of 8.2% FHH and 21% HHs living with at least one member with a disability. According to the Gaza Protection Consortium (GPC) baseline, 72.3 % of the targeted group in the assessment - of whom 25.2% are FHH and 44% have at least one member with a disability - reported that either they do not feel at all safe or somehow feel safe and dignified in their current shelters, 45% reported a lack of information and 59.5% reported limited access to the needed services. According to the MSNA 2022, 60.3% (20.6% HHs reporting disability, 54.6% FHH) of Gaza households confirmed having at least one household member unable to find work. It also indicated that unemployed members are at 54.2% female and 50% male above 60 years old, 54.8% female, and 37% male between 18-59 years old. In May 2022, the Shelter Cluster Dashboard reported 1.688 destroyed Housing Unit (HU), 916 HU severely damaged, and 59,2260 HU with different amounts of damage. In 2020, MOPWH reported that 21,500 existing housing units across Gaza, classified as substandard and do not meet the minimum requirements of living space, weather protection, hygiene facilities, and privacy, require different degrees of rehabilitation. The MSNA 2022 mentioned that 59.8% of targeted HHs reported any kind of damage or defects at their shelters. Among them 69.7% reported disability and 58.5% FHH (60% Female and 53.5% Male over 60 years old, while 57.6% female and 61.6% male from 18-59 years old). In more detail, in one hand, the same report highlighted that, 127,783 FHH in Gaza Strip live in shelters with some kind of damage, of whom 80,835 FHH are living in stressful shelter conditions (11.2% reported disability), 46,023 FHH (10.3% reported disability) are living in severe shelter condition and 925 FHH (8.3% reported disability) are living in extremes shelter conditions. This was confirmed by HNO, 2023, as women and girls living in substandard, overcrowded, unsafe shelters with a lack of privacy and limited access to hygiene facilities are highly exposed to the risk of gender-based violence (GBV). On the other hand, the same study also highlighted that 40,494 persons with disabilities in Gaza Strip live in shelters with some kind of damage or defect, of whom 25,121 live in stressful shelter conditions, 15,051 live in severe and 322 live in extreme shelter conditions. The same report highlights that 67,270 HHs from Gaza and Khan Younis governorates (40,478 in Gaza, 26,792 in Khan Younis) live in stressful or severe shelter conditions as the highest governorates in the strip. The GPC baseline assessment 2022 reported that 80.6 % of assessed households do not have any source for heating in the winter season, 6.5% lack a bathroom, and 14.5% said that housing was their priority need for assistance. Moreover, fully or mostly unable to meet their basic needs, and 97.7% are fully or mostly unable to cover their non-food items needs like house maintenance, hygiene items, rent, and other needs.

2 - Activities

The project aims to strengthen HHs’ access to dignified shelters and immediate essential needs by creating a safe, accessible, and healthy environment. The project is composed of 2 outputs; the first output - sub-standards shelters of vulnerable HHs have been rehabilitated and adapted based on shelter minimum standards - will target 100 shelters in stressful or severe conditions; the 100 shelters will be identified out of 150 sub-standard shelters nominated by the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) according to its waiting list in both Khan Younis and Gaza governorates. This will be in line with the Shelter and HI criteria, including household vulnerability assessment, shelter vulnerability assessment and socioeconomic aspect as family size, single FHH, number of persons with disabilities living in the house, and effect of barriers on participation. HHs members needing external service will be referred either by the cluster mechanisms, including protection and GBV referral pathways or the local referral networks established by HI in 2009. The targeted 100 sub-standard shelters will be upgraded and adapted based on shelter minimum standards and physical accessibility standards in full participation of the HHs members. Technical drawings and BoQ will be developed and priced by the project team; then Self-Help Approach will be followed through restricted cash assistance (on average 2400 USD through three instalments). The self-help approach will include training and coaching that ensure the participation of the most vulnerable groups within the targeted HHs. It will also have a topic about the existing feedback and complaint mechanisms. The size of each grant will be as much as possible tailored to the actual individual needs; the project interventions will take into consideration the shelter's exposure to hazards and risks, and the conditions of the shelter structures such as stairs, balconies, external/internal doors, ventilation/window, roof and walls, internal wall and floor surfaces, water supply, kitchen, toilets/showers, electrical connections, lighting system, and accessibility. Furthermore, the restricted cash will support the HHs to conduct temporary actions to protect their shelter from harsh weather (mainly winter extreme) until the completion of the project interventions. Moreover, the intervention will be gender sensitive in terms of ensuring the existence of private spaces in bedrooms, toilets, and bathrooms. The implementation of the action will also be used as practical case studies for the other Shelter Cluster members to guide key actors in the different phases of the accessibility intervention. Post-distribution monitoring (PDM) will be conducted to observe and analyze how the restricted cash was spent and what needs were met. Best practices and lessons learned will be shared with the clusters during the regular monthly meetings. The second output -access of vulnerable households living in substandard shelters to functional devices and NFIs were secured through the provision of essential standardized Shelter NFIs kits and functional assistive devices, which will target 80% of the targeted HHs. NFI items will be selected based on the Shelter's minimum standards and HI's previous experience in responding to the needs of young, elderly and children needs. It may include winterization, hygiene, infection protection and control kit, family dignity kit, cleaning kit, and/or kitchen sets based on the identified priority through either in-kind or voucher approaches that will be decided based on market functioning; 30% of targeted families will receive functional assistive devices to ensure proper function and independency of HHs’ members with disabilities.

3 - Indicators

HI adopts a monitoring system that will ensure proper tracking of progress and capture any challenges in the implementation tor the requested support on time. The assigned Project Manager will be in charge of the M&E system with close support from the Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) unit. This system comprises: I) a monitoring tool (PMBox) developed at the project’s inception phase and regularly monitored during the project’s implementation period. This tool tracks the progress of different activities, achievements and indicators; it also allows for identifying the challenges and delays within the project activities and identifying potential solutions. II) A project review meeting is scheduled and conducted every four months according to HI standards. During this meeting, all the departments and the project team discuss the progress of the project’s activities and the achievement of results. Additionally, recommendations for improvements are identified and followed to ensure the quality and timely implementation of activities. III) Technical monitoring can be provided by the Regional MEAL and Technical Units based in Jordan, as required. IV) Field support visits conducted by HI project team to partners and beneficiaries, on a regular basis, to monitor and support the activities’ implementation and outcome achievement. The project monitoring will use and apply the following tools: Post Intervention Monitoring (PIM) and satisfaction surveys. The M&E of the project is designed in accordance with HI Planning, Monitoring and Evaluation Policy, which is derived from HI institutional policies on Child Protection (CP), Protection Against Sexual Exploitation Abuse and Harassment (PSEAH), Prevention of and Fight Against Bribery (PFAB), Code of Conduct (CoC), and Safety and Security (SaS). The M&E system complies with IASC guidelines on the inclusion of Persons with Disabilities in Humanitarian Action through the application of Common Humanitarian Standards (CHS) Commitments 4 and 5, SPHERE Standards, and Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP). Concerning indicators, the project will be monitored against the following set:HI will measure progress against the following set of outcome results indicators: Outcome, # of women, men, girls and boys protected and have improved access to adequate shelter; % of women, men, girls and boys are satisfied with assistance provided; % of targeted women reporting shared decision making on restricted-cash transfer use. Output 1: % Of FHH, elderly, people with disabilities who report improved access to safe and dignified shelter after the intervention; # Of women and men members of targeted HHs engaged in orientation sessions on self-help approach; # of exchanges meetings conducted with shelter clusters members; # of referral conducted to external service providers. Output 2: # of NFIs/vouchers distributed; % of women, men, girls and boys are satisfied with NFIs/vouchers provided; % of women and girls reporting NFIs/vouchers contents meet their descent immediate essential needs; % of persons with disability reported improvement in their daily activities.A Feedback and Complaint Mechanism (FCM) exists and will allow different stakeholders to raise their concerns through different available modes of reception such as complaining via: a phone line, email, boxes, HI staff, and data collection exercises. Data confidentiality protocols are in place. All HI Palestine staff have received training on the HI safeguarding approach by the Safeguarding focal point. HI also has internal (via intranet, email) and external (website, email, post) complaints and whistle-blowing mechanisms with the clear investigation and disciplinary procedures. A PSEAH toolbox is available on HI’s intranet to implement the existing policy. Beneficiaries with and without disabilities are able to fill complaints if needed.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Accountability to the involved stakeholders will be encouraged and assured through a complaint mechanism, which involves a direct communication flow among beneficiaries, steering committee structures, and HI. Specific focus will be on providing appropriate opportunities to amplify the voices of groups with protection needs including female-headed households and persons with disabilities, not only to articulate their own needs but also to give them space to contribute to project decision making and evaluation. HI had an accountable complaint and feedback mechanism that is inclusive, gender sensitive, and accessible to the different vulnerable groups. The system offers different means to address complaints or feedback in writing, verbally, to complaint email, phone line, website, or directly to focal point from HI. The beneficiaries will be informed about how to access and provide/submit complaints and feedback during the activities of orientation sessions as well as the printed protection and complaint leaflets via different format will be distributed. Based on the analysis of the received complaints by a technical committee lead by the MEAL unit, corrective actions will be taken in full respect to the privacy and transparency principles, The project team will take seriously the received feedback to informs the design of the next phases of the interventions or it may indicate the need for an immediate change in approach and taking corrective action

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Beneficiaries are selected from the Ministry of Social Development waiting list, in order to protect final beneficiaries and limit potential negative impact, capacity building on the slef-help approach will be complemented with technical support to ensure the DO No Harm Principle.Prior to the implementation of the activities project staff will be invited to attend a refresher training session by HI Technical Protection Officer on the overall protection principles and on how to detect GBV. This should improve their knowledge and tools to conduct risk analysis that identifies risks, risks level, likelihood and mitigation measures that will be taken into consideration in advance.During the project, HI teams will comply with detailed HI policies and procedures regarding protection of beneficiaries such as the Child Protection Policy, a Policy on Protection of Beneficiaries from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) to prevent possible harm. Also, it ensures that there are clear ways in which issues that arise are handled to protect the beneficiaries, the image of the organization as well as the donor. For purposes of adhering to professional ethics and in line with HI policy for the prevention and fight against bribery, HI does not tolerate any form of corruption while conducting its activities and has measures in place to avert related risks and consequences.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

HI will ensure the meaningful and appropriate access of vulnerable groups such as female headed households and households living with disabilities to assistance without any barriers or discrimination and consider their protection and safety concerns during the implementation of activities. For this reason, the social vulnerability assessment of the shelter cluster was adapted to meet the specific situation of female headed households and persons with disabilities by considering the Washington Group's short set of questions and analysing the physical barriers that may hinder full access to aids by different target groups disaggregated by sex, age, and disability. Moreover, accessibility is at the core of the project and will be ensured to all beneficiaries considering their needs and gender through coaching to team members to respond to the specificity of beneficiaries’ needs, thus, raising awareness about the project interventions will depend on different tools to ensure access of all community member in spite of the different capacities. The project design has considered gender and protection principles by emphasizing the participation of women as well as further responding to women and persons with disabilities needs. This will be integrated through 1) orientation sessions on the project activities with the project beneficiaries following rights-based approaches when engaging with communities; 2) Dissemination of the feedback mechanism under HI and partner responsibility; 3) The use of accountability community engagement mechanisms: such as dissemination of beneficiaries’ selection criteria, clarity of the selection process, an announcement to the community of activities etc.; 4) Carrying out safe programming training to project staff that ensures continuous identification of risks; 5) Selection of beneficiaries will be cross check with shelter cluster and related stakeholders to assess applications and do the joint decision making on the most qualified beneficiaries.
