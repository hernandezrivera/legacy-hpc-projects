Project Summary

The action seeks to address vulnerable populations within 0-20km of the contact line prioritising hard to reach and enclaved areas that have limited accessible livelihood opportunities and reduced purchasing power at the household (HH) level. As such, the project will target vulnerable households with multipurpose cash grants (MPC) based on existing vulnerability criteria, revised in July 2020 to include newly emerging COVID 19 vulnerable groups in line with MPC and CWG Ukraine guidance.
IOM will target vulnerable households as per previous projects and as outlined in the June CWG guidance on transfer values. As such, the action will benefit single headed households with a focus on female headed households (FHHs), multi child families, HHs with chronically ill and disabled members and HHs with unemployed members of pre-pensionable age. However, in areas where IOM has conducted recent COVID 19 MPC interventions, IOM intends to conduct FGDs (where possible given COVID 19 measures) with community members from across different age/sex groups and vulnerable categories as well as local SIs and MoSP line ministry representatives to review existing vulnerability criteria with affected communities.
Geographical locations of intervention will be in line with other IOM activities to facilitate an area based approach where possible. Target areas to be prioritised will be those that are hard to reach and isolated communities. However, as the current response is addressing COVID 19 as well as the impact of the conflict, it is likely that MPC will be delivered in areas further away from the contact line. IOM will reach out to SI and local SES branches regarding the selection of locations of intervention and to identify vulnerable HHs against the revised vulnerability criteria.
MPC amounts will be delivered in line with revised CWG transfer value amount of 1,150UAH/person/month.

1 - Needs

Since the onset of the conflict, many vulnerable households (HH) have been forced to survive in increasingly difficult circumstances. Many areas close to the LoC were cut off from their traditional market centres where they would seek social/health services, basic needs and often livelihood opportunities. Subsequent inaccessibility resulted in HHs having less access to income generating activities and affordable services and goods which substantially increased the HH vulnerability. According to the data of the Ministry of Social Policy of Ukraine (MoSP), since 2014 almost half of the 1.4 million registered internally displaced persons (IDPs) moved to government-controlled areas (GCA), leaving their homes and livelihoods. The unemployment rates in the Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts increased from 9 % and 8 % respectively in 2014 to 14 % and 15 % in 2019. Unemployment rates in the Eastern Conflict Area (ECA) have soared as a result of COVID 19 as reflected by April 2020 State Employment Service (SES) data on unemployment in Donetsk and Lugansk oblasts, recording an increase by 68% in Donetsk and by 34% in Lugansk, compared to the end of April 2019. MoSP data on the actual subsistence level (ASL) for Ukraine, when compared to humanitarian partner data on income levels, also highlights severe HH expenditure capacity concerns. MoSP ASL calculations, which are reflective of a HH’s minimum expenditure basket, was estimated at 3,846 UAH in April 2020, an increase of just over 1,000UAH since January 2020. CWG income data analyses put the average income for vulnerable HHs at a total of 2,058UAH/person/month which amounts to only 53% of the ASL. With the clear increase in ASL from January to April and the increased expenditures and loss of income as a result of COVID 19, vulnerable families are going to struggle considerably to meet their basic needs. Adding to the issues of HH purchasing power in ECA is the high percentage of pensions, over a 3rd of the overall ECA population, with the percentage representation increasing to 40% in contact line communities according to Proliska. Elderly HHs survive on pensions that are barely sufficient to cover basic needs and are inadequate in addressing the additional burden of inaccessible services and basic goods. Lack of opportunities, insufficient income and access to services have become pronounced during COVID 19 as transportation links failed. This cut people off from essential services and goods, while prices of basic goods and hygiene items rose (NMS round 17 & IOM COVID 19 Rapid Impact Assessments, RIAs) and livelihood opportunities were lost. In contact line communities, a protection monitoring report by Proliska in late March noted that 71 of the 119 contact line settlements surveyed had lost public transportation links, 54 did not have local food shops, 70 did not have access to medical services and 57 didn’t have access to pharmacies. IOM RIAs conducted with contact line communities in April 2020 identified increasing food insecurity with over 50% of people switching to cheaper food sources with an additional 40% either using savings to purchase food or reducing food intake to manage other essential expenditures. With reduced access, an increased share of HH expenditure will be used for transportation to reach essential services, to pay for more expensive goods made available by mobile shops, or families might be forced to further forfeit their right to food security and access to basic essential services.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

Outcome: Vulnerable conflict-affected persons residing within 20km along the contact line in Donetsk and Luhansk regions (GCA) are able to access essential commodities and services Output 1: Vulnerable households benefit from cash transfers to cover their essential and urgent needs Activities to output 1:  Outreach to local SES, Sis and community networks to review vulnerability criteria and identify HH for possible MPC support;  Conduct assessments with identified HHs, verify and notify multipurpose cash beneficiaries; Carry out cash distribution for target beneficiaries; Conduct PDM of the cash transfers to assess the efficiency and effectiveness of assistance provided. IOM intends to provide 8 months cash assistance in 3 rounds in line with previous cash distribution modalities. Feedback from beneficiaries has confirmed that it is preferred to receive the cash in three installments as this helps with managing household spending over time. The project will ensure that both men and women benefit from the project through their representation in the target groups, including the elderly (60+); disabled (1, 2 groups); HH with chronically ill members; single headed HH; families with 3+ children and families with unemployed members of pre-pensionable age (50-59yrs) in line with CWG June Multipurpose Cash guidance document. The project aims to target also male single headed households where possible although recognising the heightened vulnerability of female headed households. IOM will, where possible, implement MPC as part of a multisectoral area based intervention. This hopes to maximise the impact of the MPC in communities where IOM is conducting livelihoods activities with the hope of contributing to local economic recovery through cash injections into markets with new absorption capacity.

3 - Indicators & targets

Outcome: Vulnerable conflict-affected persons residing within 20km along the contact line in Donetsk and Luhansk regions (GCA) are able to access essential commodities and services  % of beneficiaries that reported that the transferred cash increased their access to essential commodities and services (Target: 75%); % of beneficiaries with reduced use of existing negative coping mechanisms due to the assistance provision (Target: 50%); % of beneficiaries satisfied with the procedure and the modality of the cash transfers (Target: 90%). Output 1: Vulnerable households benefit from cash transfers to cover their essential and urgent needs  # of disbursement rounds in target areas throughout the project implementation period (Target: 3 rounds); # of households benefitting from multipurpose cash assistance (Target: 1,000 households: estimated 2,100 beneficiaries, including 1,470 female and 630 male; as well as out of them 210 children, 1,050 adults and 840 elderly). The average size of the target household is 2,1. To ensure accountability to the population, IOM operates a number of feedback mechanisms which allows beneficiaries to contact IOM by phone, email and global accountability platform. Information on the mechanisms are distributed to beneficiaries of IOM projects and a dedicated team are responsible for investigating claims and providing feedback to complainants. Periodic monitoring and post distribution monitoring by IOM M&E teams are further mechanisms through which beneficiaries can provide feedback. Feedback is incorporated through lessons learnt processes to ensure that programmes going forward are better able to meet the needs of the populations. As described, intended beneficiaries will be consulted during developed of components and implementation to better tailor deliverables to the outlined needs.
