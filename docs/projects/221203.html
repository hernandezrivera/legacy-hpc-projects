Project Summary

It will be obvious should we receive news of Cholera and other water related disease outbreak in Njikwa Sub Division soon. Njikwa has been highly hit by the crisis as fierce fighting left trails of destructions and pains in the communities and in the hearts of the people with all social services shutdown especially in central town of Njikwa Administrative area, Ngwo, Oshie and Konda villages including Muslim Fulani Communities in the “Hill Country”. The Water systems in these communities were highly vandalized with untold damages ranging from breaking of pipes, taps and catchments points putting the population more vulnerable to sicknesses like Cholera, Typhoid, Malaria and other dirty water associated diseases. There are 10 water collection points including 3 standard catchments that are dilapidated in these highly affected villages.
About 10 000 people have limited or no access to safe drinking water, sanitation, or
hygiene services. As of July 2024, typhoid and malaria affected all the villages in the
sub division with almost 80% of the population having traces of malaria or typhoid. A
good number of cases of cholera noticed and managed at local levels. Between June
2020 and July 2024 over 200 people died in Njikwa from what the local people called
"high fever" - Typoid and Malaria; Many children died of high running stomach and
vomiting - "diarrhea" as community health worker reported, many people complained of belly bite each time they drink water. The toileting system had collapsed as many people fled, with open defecation very common.
The Comprehensive WASH support project intends to improves access to safe and
adequate water supply and basic sanitation infrastructure for 2000 households with a
population of 10 000 people, 10 health facilities in the Health District, 17 schools in community learning spaces. 10 water collection points will be rehabilitated, fenced and 300 taps and100 wash basins will be provided and others rehabilitated. 50 sample community latrines will be built in public places to encourage the households to construct theirs under the supervision of trained Community WASH Committees. The project builds resiliency through complementary hygiene practices to maximize and enhance health-seeking behaviors among the most vulnerable communities.
Rollout typhoid and bar cholera outbreak project will support and improves sustainable environmental health conditions of vulnerable communities in Njikwa by improving water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) infrastructure and service delivery. The project addresses the key challenges to long-term sustainability by enabling (a) behavior change, (b) community ownership, and (c) local capacity to manage
infrastructure.
Community Water, Sanitation and Hygiene Committees (C-WASH-C) will be created in each community as a water management board for sanitation and hygiene issues in the
communities of Njikwa municipality. This project aims to train 30 Hygiene and sanitary inspectors and first aiders from 9 villages. 30 Community based water maintenance personnel with be trained on basic water repairs and maintenance and provided with basic plumbing tool kits and hygiene kits. They shall organize regular clean up campaigns, do repair works on broken pipes, drinking points, inspect homes and the latrines, disinfection latrines and slums clearances. 4 community volunteers shall be trained for chlorination of the water systems. The project will also target 2000 vulnerable girls and women for the sensitization on good practices on menstrual hygiene and to provide and train them on the use of reusable sanitary dignity kits.

1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The most urgent need for safe and clean water in Njikwa now is the protection of watercollection points and repairs of broken pipes as they are attractive points to cows andother animals in 4 crisis affected communities. Recently, cows have invaded 6 waterpoints and catchments broken and more pipes destroyed for the quest of water as the dryseason approached. Catchment areas need to be fenced and broken pipes repaired. Thiswill go a long way to evade cholera and typhoid outbreak that are associated with unsafewater. Cows and pigs stray in to the leaking areas where the drink, bath, sleep anddefecate. This goes directly into the broken pipes. It will not be strange now to receivenews from Njikwa of cholera outbreak as the people are most vulnerable now than before.The crisis has left a trail of destruction and pains in the community with most socialservices shutdown especially in central town of Njikwa Administrative area, Ngwo,Oshie and Konda villages. The Water systems in these 4 communities were vandalized with untolddamages ranging from breaking of pipes, taps and catchments putting the population vulnerable to sicknesses like Typhoid, malaria and other dirty water associated diseases. There are 10 water collection points in the 4 villages that have all broken down and waternot chlorinated for 7 years. The fighting has caused significant damage to essentialinfrastructure, including water and sanitation systems and health facilities. Access tobasic needs has reduced with increasing rates of food insecurity, malnutrition, thereemergence of Typhoid, Malaria and Cholera. About 10 000 people have limited or noaccess to safe drinking water, sanitation, or hygiene services. Typhoid and malaria havebeen the major health challenges faced by people of affected villages in the Sub Divisionwith almost 80% of the population having traces of malaria or typhoid. A good number ofcases of cholera noticed and managed at local levels. Between June 2020 and July 2024over 200 people died in Njikwa from what the local people called "high fever" - Typoid andMalaria; Many children died of high running stomach and vomiting - "diarrhea" ascommunity health volunteers reported, many people complained of belly bite. No medicalreport was available to attest to the cause of the deaths since the health services were notfunctional to determine the actual cause. The toileting system of the people had collapsedas many people had displaced, you could see open defecation. Water catchments havebeen abandoned with broken pipes. All regular technicians fled and no one to clean orrepair them. In other places water points are not cleared or drained. No knowledge offiltering water before drinking. No chlorinated water for over seven years. Typhoid andmalarial very prevalent. The communities use mostly pit latrines of which many havebeen abandoned and have collapsed, the people do open defecations in back yardbushes. The Mbororo Fulani community in Njikwa is highly affected and threatened for supporting the government. They need of CRI and NFI. Women have resorted to the old traditional methods of managing menstrual issues. Some use pieces of clothes in turns while others use untreated traditional leaves and back of trees exposing them to diseases and infections. The project targets 2000 including 200 IDP, 225 Returnees vulnerable girls and women to sensitize on good practice on menstrual hygiene and provide reusable dignity kits. 2000 households of 10 000 people including 2000 IDPs and 1000 Returnees, 4 health facilities, 8 community schools need safe and good drinking water. 10 water collection points need rehabilitated, protection and 300 taps, 100 washbasins be provided and others rehabilitated. 50 community latrines needed as samples to the communities. There is need to create Community WASH Committees as WASH Inspectors. Community based water maintenance personnel trained and provide plumbing and sanitation kits
