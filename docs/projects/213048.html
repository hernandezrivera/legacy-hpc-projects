Project Summary

The crisis, which has lasted for more than ten years, has also caused more than one million people to flee their homes. The economy has also suffered irreparable damage since the crisis began, with GDP falling by 60 percent, while increases in commodity prices are at an all-time high. It is estimated that more than 90 percent of the population now lives below the poverty line. The COVID-19 pandemic has also accelerated this economic downturn by further reducing income generation opportunities. It is estimated that more than 50 percent of the population is unemployed.
This plan aims to provide key and complimentary services to cover the immediate needs of the most vulnerable newly displaced groups, supporting their resilience and preparedness.
This plan responds to the needs of the most vulnerable groups including women, men, boys, and girls of all ages, both IDPs and host-vulnerable communities as well as people with disabilities.
It is to respond to the Sector priorities by providing shelter and NFI support to reach the most severe communities, the main outputs of this plan are:
1- Providing Pilot Shelter Units - 96 units, for 96 households "500 individuals" to support hosting the newly displaced households who do not have safe and secure shelter.
(Aleppo Rural and Idleb Governorates)
2- Rehabilitation works for 820 (houses), 820 households "4,100 individuals" including the most vulnerable host and displaced families.
(Aleppo Rural and Idleb Governorates)
3- Collective Centers Rehabilitation works for 400 vulnerable IDP households, "2,000 individuals".
(Aleppo Rural and Idleb Governorates)
4- Provide complementary services to 30 camps "1500 Households/7,500 individuals":
- Providing core and seasonal and supplementary NFIs 1500 vulnerable IDPs, "7500 individuals".
- Distribution of 1,000 winter clothes for 1,000 households "5,000 individuals" for the displaced families, returnees, and the vulnerable host community (Complementary).
(Aleppo Rural and Idleb Governorates)
- Distribution of 1,000 heating materials for 1,000 households "5,000 individuals", including Stoves and Fuel to cover the needs in IDP sites within the harsh winter conditions (Complementary).
(Aleppo Rural and Idleb Governorates)
5- Installation of new/ replacement of existing tents for 830 vulnerable IDP households, "4,200 individuals".
(Aleppo Rural and Idleb Governorates)
6- Implementation of site-level infrastructure improvements for 3,125 vulnerable IDP households, "15,700 individuals".
(Aleppo Rural and Idleb Governorates)
Based on a technical assessment proving the availability of essential services (such as markets, public services, and infrastructures…), also, taking into consideration HLP rights, WATAN will be operating in vulnerable communities following the Protection, vulnerability, and prioritizing the most in need Reference for Northwest Syria guidance and the AAP framework.
Also, in line with humanitarian principles, this plan's impact is supporting the reconstruction targets for the affected communities to avoid raising violence within the crises hosting communities that produce more armed conflicts and fewer protection conditions.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Based on the primal HNO figures and the weekly and monthly updates from CCCM Cluster - XB, WATAN conducted a survey to better understand the needs and status of IDP households. Implemented in 60 camps, the field survey included questions related to how IDPs cope with winter conditions, and imminent threats to vulnerable categories, such as women, children, and the elderly. Women accounted for 20% of the survey sample. Since the conduct of the baseline survey. Approximately 40% of IDPs are currently residing in Idleb and Aleppo Governorates (23% and 17% of the total IDP population, respectively). , of whom 51% are estimated to be children and 25%, are women. Idleb governorate has the highest burden of displacement, with IDPs representing almost 53% of the total population. In the context of the 6th of February Earthqoueck; the dire conditions in Northwest Syria have deteriorated, exacerbating the reliance on humanitarian assistance and diminishing positive coping strategies. At the same time, the preparations for the approaching winter season are expected to put a further strain on people’s finances, especially in light of rising costs. The prioritization of locations is: - Concentration of displacement (IDPs and returnees) - Presence of new displacements (IDPs and returnees) - Absence of humanitarian actors (cross-checked with updated 4Ws) - Accessed constraints to household items such as NFIs and Shelter materials.
