Project Summary

This project aims to monitor the quality of water collected from the different water points accessed by IDPs and affected population in the North West, Southwest, Littoral and Western regions of Cameroon with a focus on the bacteriological and physico-chemical properties of the water sources. Combined with the challenges brought to IDPs and affected population, the impact of Cholera outbreak among others water borne diseases has had a toll among IDPs and host communities. Water points continue to face pressure from a mass of IDPs into the many localities in Southwest, Northwest, West and Littoral regions. The monitoring of water sources and water points on a monthly basis is one of the priority cholera infection control measures which EPDA will like to cover. The results of this regular monitoring, water samples collection, testing and analysis will provide state of water quality in terms of safe and contaminated and will also inform strategies and solutions to be undertaken in order to curb the spread of the cholera especially in IDPs sites and host communities. Proposed solutions will focus on improving water points, hygiene and sanitation promotion and improving practices. The project targets 180 water points that are accessed by over 9,000 people. Samples from these water points will be collected and analysed twice in one year as it should be done during the dry season and rainy season. This project will also include a component relative to capacity building of water users at community level on key skills such as water point management, financial management of small community water schemes, cholera response, flood controls, and PSEA among beneficiaries.
This project will also focus on determining the water points responsible for the proliferation of water borne diseases in order to propose control and treatment measures.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Cholera cases in Cameroon have increased significantly since early 2021. On October 31, 2021 Cameroon’s health authorities declared a cholera outbreak. According to national SITREP number 26 published in August 2022, the situation kept persisting as more suspected cases were identified with the cases prominent in 4 regions namely, Centre, West, Littoral and South West regions of Cameroon. Forty-eight (48) health districts have been affected since 2021 with 10,724 cases notified, 211 deaths recorded with lethality rate of 2% and gender ratio of 2:1 for male/female respectively. The distribution of cases and deaths by health district in the South West region as of August 18, 2022 showed that, the most affected districts were Limbe with 3194 cases reported, 33 deaths and lethality ratio of 1.03% followed by Buea with 1023 cases reported, 13 deaths with lethality ratio of 1.27% and Tiko with 766 cases reported, 17 deaths with lethality ratio of 2.22%. This has only compounded the deteriorating situation of the socio-political crisis in the Northwest and Southwest regions which started late 2016. Many people have fled their communities of origin for safety and have now settled in Bamenda, Limbe, Tiko Buea, part of Littoral and Western regions. The increase in population has also increased the pressure on water resources and sanitation with increased tendencies of open defecation due to insufficient latrines. Open defecation may also account to a large extent the spread of cholera. The monitoring of water sources from where IDPs and host population carry potable water is important and necessary to attack the causes and curb cholera outbreak resulting to high morbidity and mobility rates. This project will address the causes of cholera in IDP settlements as well as host communities by looking at the physic-chemical and bacteriological parameters of water points. This project will make use of project materials such as multi-parameter, GPS, 500ml of test tubes, coolers, anemometer, alcohol, cotton and a stop watch, and transport vehicle which will enable the following results to be attained:- 180 water points monitored with water samples collected and analysed twice (rainy season and dry season) in one year distributed as follows: Northwest - 30; Southwest – 110; West – 10 and Littoral – 30.- 3 water analysis results produced and shared: 60 in the first quota, 60 in the second quota and 60 in the last or third quota.This project will focus on water quality and target households, disadvantaged IDP host communities in urban and rural areas. A community approach shall be used mobilizing water users, sensitizing and collecting water samples as well as negotiating access.
