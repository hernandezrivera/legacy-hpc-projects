Project Summary

During the last three years, Cameroon has been trampled upon in a terrorizing Socio-political conflict, known as the “Anglophone Crisis” which is one of the world’s most neglected displacement crises. A peaceful strike, which began by teachers and lawyers in late 2016 but in a short time escalated into a violent revolt against the marginalization of the English-speaking regions of the country. The campaign to declare a symbolic independence of the Southern part of Cameroon from the rest of the nation officially, triggered a violent fight between the Cameroon army (Military) and Non -state armed groups called as “secessionists”. Through protest activities, such as “ghost town,” economic and social services have been paralyzed. Brutal killings, Kidnappings, sexual assaults on young girls and women, burn-down villages and massive destruction of properties, and hospitals close-down have regrettably resulted to more than 450 000 people who are displaced, 780 000 children deprived of basic education and thousands of people currently hiding in the bushes, have access to no humanitarian relief according to the report published in June 5th, 2019 by The Norwegian Refugees Council on the world’s most neglected displacement crises
The current deepening Socio-political crisis is a growing concern affecting many patients in the Southwest region of Cameroon to access health care services promptly. This crisis has been ongoing for the past three years, and it is uncertain when it will end. On “ghost town” days, residents and IDPs who live in the cities (Kupe-Maninguba, Mamfe, and Indian) have no option but to stay in their homes fearing to be attacked by unidentified persons or killed by a stray bullet. There are also no-go zones where commercial taxis abstain to travel to because of tha t, the population is left with no means to move from one end to another whether to their workplaces or appropriate health care facilities, especially in cases of emergency.
It was reported by the Yaoundé Emergency Center director Dr. Louis Joss Bitang A Mafok that 365 people dead on arrival were recorded in 2018 ( The 1 p.m. CRTV Radio news, on December 28, 2018) and when we met with him, he made us to understand that one of the main reasons is that most emergency cases occur in schools and those attending to victims merely rush them to the hospital without providing any basic life-support treatment. Considering that this situation is not isolated to the enormous need of Pre -hospital emergency care in Cameroon as a whole, this figure may allegedly be higher due to the Socio-political conflict that erupted in the Northwest and Southwest regions.
The lack of well-established emergency care services in the Southwest region of Cameroon has resulted in many dead at school level on arrival to health care facilities especially in this crisis period , even though Doctors Without Borders (DWB) has been serving within their capacity to offer some free emergency care transport to victims within some parts in these communities in order to bridge the huge gap between specialized health facilities and the victims’ location, it is obvious that there is still a lot to be done at the school level. This is undisputed because at the school level, simple prevention of risk is not yet common to all students and pupils and basic first -aid gestures.
