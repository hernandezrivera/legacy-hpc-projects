Project Summary

Under this project proposal, CMD plans to offer support to resumption/recovery of crops and fisheries production for sections of farming populations engaged in crop and livestock activities in Ayod,Fangak, Nyirol, (Jonglei) and Kapoepa South and North (Eastern Equitoria) aside from Livestock treatment and vaccination planned for Uror, Nyirol and Ulang. The project aims to increase production of food; nutritious vegetables and fish as rich protein sources in the locations during the dry season and expand the livelihood activity options among the vulnerable, increasing dietary diversity for the Agro pastoralists and fisher. In Renk County they project will upscale Fisheries interventions (production, preservation and value chain analysis, seed production and provide support to seed fairs using locally sourced seed; seed security & multiplication strengthening/ Support market functionality e.g. assist traders to re-supply markets/ local procurement & aggregation of produce for WFP assistance.
Farmer groups will be formed and trained on crop and vegetable farming; traders trained on seed-voucher system. This project will benefit the vulnerable households in most vulnerable; Child-headed, Elderly and Female-headed households will account for the 40 .5 % of the households under IPC 3 and 4 suffering under food insecurities and malnutrition. 90,466 people in 12,924HHs are targeted (42,519 through receiving food, non-food items and vouchers. 47,947 people will be reached through distribution of farming tools and fishing kits and guided to start and upscale production of food nutritious vegetables and fish. 310,000 livestock are planned for treatment and vaccination; particularly in Nyirol,Uror, Ayod and Ulang counties. Overall, 4,133 people within the communities will be capacitated to ensure they are strengthened to sustain activities beyond this project.
The activities will be conducted by CMD FSL Team comprises of FSL specialist who will conduct assessments, prepare and carry out post distribution monitoring on the usability and long term impact of items distributed e.g. seeds, fishing and farming tools. CMD will involve the beneficiaries on all the stages of the intervention to provide response best suited for the needs of particular community served, taking into account vulnerabilities of each represented group: men, women, boy and girls. Strong focus will be made to ensure that the most sensitive groups (e.g. persons with disabilities, pregnant and lactating women, and unaccompanied elders/minors) in the community will be served appropriately to their needs and in the manner not exposing them to additional stress. CMD will carry out complementary programming in the counties i.e. Nutrition and Health, working closely with nutrition and health partners, HHs with SAM cases attended to at the SCs will be provided with kits.
If the current drivers of food insecurity worsen through the end of 2019, and in the absence of humanitarian assistance, there is a heightened risk of emergency as large populations are already experiencing severe food insecurity.

1 - Needs

August 2019 IPC findings flag Counties in Jonglei, Upper Nile and Eastern Equitoria states as hosting populations with critical and Emergency food needs (IPC phase 3 & 4); one step below the Famine level (IPC 5) and one of the worst in South Sudan. Humanitarian situation in Ayod, Nyirol, Uror, Fangak Counties (Jonglei), Renk, Longechuk and Ulang (Upper Nile) and Kapoeta South and North (Eastern Equitoria) remains critical (GAM prevalence above 9.9%). Livelihoods disruptions have been recurrent driven by protracted conflict and displacements devastations, which has contributed to insufficient crop production. Households have lost livelihoods through climate shocks, with floods and pest infections seasonal rampant. Most of the population has missed planting seasons consecutively for three seasons; host communities and IDPs have been left food insecure and the malnutrition levels alarming. If the current drivers of food insecurity worsen through the end of 2019, and in the absence of humanitarian assistance, there is a heightened risk of emergency as large populations are already experiencing severe food insecurity. High SAM and GAM rates in the counties have been attributed to deteriorating food security, destruction of health, water, hygiene and sanitation facilities, high morbidity and extremely poor diets along with underlying issues of suboptimal infant and young child feeding practices (IYCF) Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) and PLW’s cases. For instance, ayod has GAM rate of 16.2%; SAM rate of 3.6%, MAM rate 12.6% and Prevalence of malnutrition PLW is 34.2%. In projection period of August to December 2019, and in the total absence of humanitarian food assistance, an estimated 353,498 people (67% of the population) will face Crisis acute food insecurity or worse. Disruption of markets due to total cut off of supply routes and chains, and hyperinflation means populations have to depend on humanitarian Aid (WFP 2018). Settlements within these counties are using extreme coping strategies such as only children eating, and spending days without food.

2 - Activities

As per the FSLC priority activities for 2020, CMD will aim to increase production of food; nutritious vegetables and fish as rich protein sources in the locations during the dry season and expand the livelihood activity options among the vulnerable, increasing dietary diversity for the Agro pastoralists and fisher. The following activity types are planned;1. Distribute food items 42,519 people or 7,086 households during the lean season to the most vulnerable affected households. This will improve the physical and mental well-being and reduce morbidity, mortality & suffering (HRP SO1 and FSL CO1). 2. Provide complementary livelihood support by distributing seeds and tools to 7,991 households (47,947 people) in order to boost crop production (2020 HRP FSL CO2) and address dietary diversity - the major underlying cause of malnutrition - with vegetable production. CMD will deliver practical training on basic agronomic practices, agribusiness and post-harvest handling to build capacity and resilience thatwill in turn enable communities recover and absorb shocks (2020 FSL CO3 and HRP SO3) and establish kitchen gardens at sites near boreholes to encourage vegetable production during the dry season. 3. Livestock vaccination; disease prevention & treatment especially zoonotic e.g. RVF planned for 310,000livestock in Nyirol, Uror and Ayod.4. Support to seed fairs using locally sourced seed; seed security & multiplication strengthening/ Support market functionality e.g. assist traders to re-supply markets/ local procurement & aggregation of produce for WFP assistance in Renk. 5. Training & capacity building: Both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production (farmer field schools, demonstration plots), income generation (business skills), vocational training etc. 4,133 people and groups planned. CMD will integrate Health/Nutrition activities through screening and referral of malnourished children and lactating and pregnant mothers to receive packages of farming/or fishing kits in Ayod and Fangak, knowing one of the major causes of continuously high malnutrition rates is the difficulty accessing the target locations. Since CMD has staff already on the ground in these locations, we will can complement the nutrition/Health program with screenings and referrals, take advantage of Health/Nutrition sites to distribute supplements and transport urgent cases to the nearest treatment centre.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Selection of beneficiaries has been based on community composition; Host (53%) and IDPs (47%). The targeted counties host some of the largest number of IDPs in Jonglei, Upper Nile and Eastern Equitoria states according to IOM DTM with majority of these female and children moving from Upper Nile, Unity and other parts of Jonglei. All activities will be designed according to cultural and society norms, considering the specific needs of IDPs. Access to services will be assessed and obstacles to equal access addressed. CMD staff has been trained on Gender and Accountability for Affected Persons in emergency. Vulnerability dimensions shall be considered during service delivery and beneficiaries accorded respect and treated with dignity.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

CMD will work in coordination with local agents including national and state governments to adopt best practices and standards in relation to food insecurity. Of particular interest, the project will work with the National and state Ministries of Agriculture to strengthen their participation in community farming, pest control, disease surveillance in livestock, market analysis and food management/utilisation.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

This project complements the National Ministry of Agriculture objectives which also plays the oversight responsibility and works in collaboration with partners. This plan works in close consideration of IDP trends in the targeted counties, flagged as hosting some of the highest displaced persons in the country.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Protection objectives will be met through ensuring the project contributes in GBV safety and psychosocial well-being of all groups. A special focus of the project will be on the rights of the women, children and the elderly to be protected from the worst forms of physical and sexual violence, child abuse and child labour. The project will keep in perspective a rights-based approach to social protection and fight exclusion and discrimination against marginalized groups. This means the project will focus community dialogues on protection risk factors and GBV prevention measures through community gatherings and meetings that are socially inclusive and engaging local leadership. CMD takes a serious and proactive approach to ensure all of its activities are safe for community targets. This approach is outlined in its Beneficiary Protection Policy, which holds staff and volunteers to a high standard of responsibility for ensuring the safety and protection of the beneficiaries with whom they interact. The organization has a PSEA policy alongside a child safeguarding policy to prevent harm to children. These include PSEA procedures apply at staff recruitment, induction/training, performance management, whistle- blowing, discipline and grievance, programming guidelines, partnership arrangements and complaints/reporting. In addition, the project will promote implementation of activities that respect the environment and encourage local/inclusive procurement of equipment and materials. It will exercise constant awareness and sensitivity at the local level based on “Do no harm” principles. By focusing on inclusion and practical rights, it is expected that the project will contribute to social justice and social peace. Activities will be implemented where access can be ensured; and psychosocial support will be employed to provide appropriate care, protection and social integration.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

During and at the end of the project, CMD expects at least 85% of beneficiaries targeted to have recovered from shocks related to food insecurity. Majority of the community farmer groups shall undergo different food production trainings to ensure knowledge sharing within the community. During project implementation, beneficiaries will be encouraged to consume locally available food and to cultivate short term maturing crops which are common food e.g sorghum, maize and groundnuts .The Organization also anticipates involvement of other partners i.e. UNFAO and other agriculture partners to provide seeds and tools for the beneficiaries affected by conflict to improve the general food status of communities in the targeted communities. The project exit strategy is based on the project statement that is to build the communities’ capacity to #1) cope with recurrent shocks and stresses, and #2) produce food through home-grown solutions to enhance household livelihood. In this way the communities will be able to continue such programming in collaboration with other stakeholders such as the Ministry of Agriculture could take over various producer groups organized and established by the project after the end of this project. Continuous assessment of the local capacity needs to inform capacity building activities of the project will ensure that the project activities are handed over to capable and knowledgeable persons.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

The first working assumption of the project is that the political and security situation remains conducive to the project. The associated risk is that the security situation may deteriorate in a way that the continuation of the project is no longer possible. To mitigate against this likelihood, CMD will sensitise key stakeholders and capacitate them in a way that enhances the likelihood that the protection of vulnerable groups will be improved, even in the absence of the project. There may also be a situation of difficulty for women to participate in the intervention. Their active participation in the social protection of vulnerable children and youth may be undermined and effectively undercut. CMD will engage in community outreach to explain the project’s objectives and importance of engaging women and girls. A third assumption is that there may be issues with legitimacy and functionality of central government in parts of the targeted Counties. Direct assistance at local levels including, if viable, working with local authorities may be impeded. The project will not address issues which require changes in policies and legal frameworks.In this early recovery and transitional context, CMD’s life-saving intervention will use food assistance, fish and crop production and livestock treatment and vaccination to assist in the restoration of food security systems, to encourage the return of internally displaced persons (IDPs) and refugees, and to promote social cohesion among communities. The project will focus primarily on IDPs and their hosts to encourage adaptation and integration. All the Counties are accessible by air from Juba and by road from Bor, Malakal (South Sudan) or Gambella (Ethiopia). All project locations remain safe for carrying out project activities.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

CMD actively seeks the views of the affected populations to improve policy and programming, ensuring that feedback and complaints mechanisms are streamlined, appropriate and robust enough to deal with (communicate, receive, process, respond to and learn from) complaints about breaches in plans and shareholder dissatisfaction. 80% of CMD staff is recruited on project location among the affected population. In order to strengthen feedback mechanisms from communities CMD provides suggestion boxes in all CMD field offices to maximize on inputs from communities. Positive and negative feedback are incorporated into lessons learnt for project improvement. All CMD led assessments will have potential beneficiaries taking part. We emphasize transparency in project implementation by directly involving the community in every stage of the project to ensure clear understanding of objectives of the project, expectations and stakeholders. CMD has incorporated the Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations (CAAP) into all relevant statements, policies and operational guidelines including incorporating them in staff inductions. Information will be made available to local communities in local languages through community mobile megaphone radios, forums such as church meetings amongst others. Staff, volunteers and consultants, both national and international, are provided with adequate and timely inductions, briefings, and clear reporting lines that promote positive organizational behaviors and enable staff to understand their responsibilities, work objectives, organizational values, accountability commitments, key policies and local context. CMD works with partners and other stakeholders to ensure the needs of the most vulnerable are addressed while upholding all standards of Do No Harm.
