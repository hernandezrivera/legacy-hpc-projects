Project Summary

Despite legal protection, sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) –including domestic violence, rape, and sexual abuse –remains widespread throughout Somalia as a result of the continuing insecurity, weak rule of law, gender inequality and large-scale displacement, although reliable data on numbers and frequency are not available and abuses are likely to be under-reported. SGBV is particularly prevalent in camps for internally displaced persons (IDP) and in conflict zones. Additionally, the HNO 2021 still cites the dire need for specialized GBV services including legal aid, community sensitization, PSS, survivors’ shelters and child-friendly spaces, inadequate local GBV actors especially in rural areas. This limited access to essential services has had its toll on the physical and mental health of some of these population groups with special needs such as the elderly, people with disabilities and GBV survivors who cannot often access mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services in local health facilities.
The proposed project is targeting the most vulnerable IDPs, host and hard-to-reach communities in Beledweine and Buloborte districts. Training of health care providers on Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) will be conducted at health facilities supported by Mercy-USA to respond to reported cases of rape; construction of women center to enable women to get together and discuss issues affecting them and use the center as a place to conduct their IGA activities and get informal education on numerals; dignity kits will be distributed to 10% IDP population of the vulnerable women and girls of reproductive age; solar lamps will be bought and distributed to the identified women and young mothers where their security is deemed threatened at night; an Income Generating Activities (IGA) will be conducted to women, men, boys and girls on basic business skills to help enhance the coping mechanism. The trained community on psychosocial support and rights of children will be there to support SGBV survivors and issues affecting children in the community through Community based approaches, therefore the trained frontline service providers will be able to respond and offer psychosocial support to Sexual Gender Based Violence (SGBV) survivors, unaccompanied and separated children will be gathered for by the community through identification of caregivers within the community in line with survivor centered approaches and using the existing referral pathways. SGBV survivor will be supported through service accessibility, also through outreach in the hard-to-reach areas and livelihood project. Gender Based Violence Information Management System will be used to inform actors on the different types of GBV occurring in the community and it will help in GBV program.
Mercy-USA will ensure that people with disabilities participate in the implementation of project activities by ensuring that they are represented in all community meetings. During project implementation, segregated focus group discussions will be held with the community to identify gender inequalities, discuss all the genders' needs, and understand the risks faced by people with disabilities in accessing GBV services. Activities will target to address these needs during program implementation. Access to people with disabilities will be ensured and increased through outreach activities and behavioral change communication campaigns.
During routine and regular program monitoring and evaluation, Mercy-USA staff will collect disaggregated data by disability, age and gender to monitor the impact of programs on vulnerable groups. Mercy-USA, during both monthly and quarterly reviews and analysis of data, will seek to monitor the inclusiveness of programming by comparing achievements versus available population data.

1. - Projects activities

Improve timely access, availability and utilization of multi-sectoral services to vulnerable and conflict affected women, girls, men and boys to strengthen coping mechanisms and live a dignified life in Beledweine and Buloburte districts. The main activities include: • Distribution of dignity kits to be prepositioned and distributed to vulnerable women and girls in reproductive age (15 - 49 years) in Beledweine and Buloburte to uphold dignity in the community and to facilitate their access to social services. • Train medical staff on clinical management of rape (CMR) and survivor centered approach • Identify, rehabilitate and maintain 1 existing and 1 new women center to up scale the weekly group psychosocial activities among GBV survivors and other vulnerable women and girls • Support to 2 GBV one stop centres • Procure, transport and distribute solar lamps to the most vulnerable women and girls with special needs to mitigate protection risks • Training of staff on psychosocial support using the Minimum Standards of Child Protection and Gender Based Violence, signing of code of conduct and use of Do No Harm principle • Train frontline service providers (Case managers, police, community leaders, community support teams) on GBV, referral pathways, survivor centered approaches and psychosocial first aid. Frontline service providers will be training for effective response and support to survivors of GBV and enhance proper coordination in support to the survivor needs • Training of Case Managers who will respond to the needs of women and children Women with disabilities have been described as being doubly marginalized due to their disability and gender. They may be more vulnerable to poverty and social exclusion and often have limited social, political, and economic opportunities and lack of access to basic services. Women with disabilities may also be at greater risk of sexual and physical violence and abuse. Girls with disabilities often experience discrimination in access to education and family life. Mercy- USA will ensure GBV survivors with disabilities have equal access to services ranging from mental, psychosocial, counselling, and case management. Mercy-USA will ensure that people with disabilities participate in the implementation of project activities by ensuring that they are represented in all community meetings. The project will consult with people with disabilities about location, design, safety, and facilities' appropriateness. Additionally, mobile services will be provided (integrated with other sectors) to ensure access to services. Mercy-USA will provide home visits as more appropriate for disabled female care-givers to access information about GBV and services available and consider the use of mobile phones as an important method of communicating about available services, but also for seeking assistance when a person with disabilities has experienced violence (i.e., through a hotline).

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

• Mercy-USA, guided by internal PSEA and Child Safeguarding Policy and international best practices, will safeguard gender justice and equity and promote gender equality. • Mercy-USA will carry out mapping exercises on a semi-annual basis to understand marginalized communities in the areas of operation and their needs. • Data collected for program implementation will be segregated in gender and disability, ensuring that the program reaches marginalized communities. • Mercy-USA will ensure that formal confidential feedback and complaints mechanisms are accessible to all community members, especially marginalized communities. • The project will engage women, girls, boys and men in all consultations to identify safety and security risks regarding the location and distance of health facilities. Emergency clinical treatment and referral services will be available to survivors of GBV. Awareness-raising for GBV prevention will be done as part of the sexual and reproductive health related information provision, involving women, girls, boys and men. The project will ensure accessibility to services for populations vulnerable to GBV, such as women, children and persons with disabilities. • Mercy-USA will have three forms of confidential feedback mechanisms to ensure that all community members are reached, including illiterate people, those who cannot physically travel, and finally, those who do not have resources to feedback. The follow mechanisms will be in place: • Suggestion boxes •Hotline numbers • Focal persons at the community level. • Community mobilization will be intensively carried out to ensure the uptake of the formal feedback mechanisms established. • Mercy-USA will continuously monitor and evaluate the effectiveness of the mechanisms established and make improvements continually.
