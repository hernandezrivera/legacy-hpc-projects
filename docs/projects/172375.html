1 - Needs

Given the current security situation in Libya that has greatly affected animal health due to lack of vaccines, drugs and veterinary services, the spread zoonotic diseases is expected to increase. Trans-boundary Animal Diseases (TADs) are one of the biggest obstacles to livestock production and development in Libya. This is in addition to existing production systems which are characterized by an extensive farming system-mainly nomadism. The most important endemic TADs are Foot and Mouth Disease (FMD) in cattle and sheep and goats, Bluetongue,Pest of small ruminats (PPR), Bovine brucellosis, Brucellosis in sheep and goats and Rabies (dogs). For the poultry sector, the Newcastle disease is one of the most important. Several other diseases such as contagious respiratory bronchitis (IB), inflammation of the larynx and trachea gastro ILT, chronic respiratory disease, Salmonella, and E. coli are notified. These diseases are a big threat to food security, nutrition, livelihoods and internal or international trade. In addition to the outbreak of a number of plant diseases (green pits scale, date palm weevil, leaf, stem and yellow rusts in cereals.... ) In order to control and reduce the spread of zoonotic diseases as well as plant diseases, FAO plans to provide support to 1000 most vulnerable returnees and non- displaced households through vaccination , treatment of livestock, as well as treatment of plants. This will safeguard and protect the livestock and plants, which are one of the main food and income sources of the affected population in the identified districts. The protection is to ensure that the most vulnerable segment of the affected communities, especially pregnant or lactating women, those with children under 5 years of age get access or continue to access animal products (milk products and meat) for a healthy growth and life. The project will be implemented within the framework of the Libya Humanitarian Response Plan in collaboration and partnership with Ministry of Agriculture, Animal and Marine (MoA) and NGO partners on the ground.
