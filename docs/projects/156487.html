Project Summary

The proposed project contributes to strengthening the resilience of the population of Ar-Raqqa, and Der- Azzor as the measures will decrease their vulnerability and invest in strengthening structures that will help the population after project end to meet their needs. In addition, the activities have the potential to reinforce each other.
The intervention will cover the WASH needs as below:
Water:
- Water station rehabilitation.
- Water networks fixing.
- Concrete water tanks constructing
- Water stations operating.
- Water safety plan applying.
- Water trucking
- Water tanks distributing
- Water in health facilities and schools
Sanitation:
- Waste collection
- Landfill rehabilitation
- Waste management operation cost.
- Sewage and drainage system rehabilitation.
- Toilets and showers constructing and fixing
- Cleaning the rivers watercourses with all needed to fix the roads on.
- Sanitation in schools and Health facilities
Hygiene:
- Hygiene kits distribution
-Hygiene awareness
-Hygiene in schools and health facilities
The project will target men, women, boys, and girls from the three governorates, both host communities, and IDPs from within cities in those governorates as well as the surrounding areas or other parts of the city who live in the targeted neighborhoods.

Needs

Since late 2017, following the war on ISIS in Raqqa and othe parts of NES, the region has witnessed the further arrival of a returnees and IDPs.The increasing number of the population in the region has led to a strain on resources and services in the region. While humanitarian assistance is reaching both IDP and resident populations, major gaps remain. In order to address such gaps,a need assessment by Mercy Corp was conducted in Raqqa in October 2018 showed the following findings:• An estimated 45% of the assessed population has access to water; however, only 35% of those living in Wahdeh and Tas-heeh neighbourhoods have access to adequate water. Tishreen and AlThawrah neighbourhoods also have a smaller percentage of population (40%) with access to water. • The main source of water is municipal supply; however, the majority of the key informants reported that there was no evidence the water was being treated by the municipality, meaning that the quality of water is a concern. • The data reveals that 100% of HHs have access to basic latrines; however, there is a need to improve the sanitation infrastructure and sewage system. Based on the Humanitarian Needs and Response Priorities November 2018 WASH response report in NES: The WASH response is focused on emergency water supply and NFI distributions, rehabilitation of critical WASH infrastructure and household level water treatment through distribution of aqua tabs. There is a high reliance on water trucking across many areas due to an absence of functioning networks- including in Deir Ez Zor, Raqqa, rural areas of Tabqa and Menbij and in Southern Hasekeh. There is a gap, across NES, in a robust response and focus on sanitation, hygiene promotion and water quality, resulting in many health concern’s - examples include an outbreak of ABD in Deir Ez Zor and typhoid in Hole and Areesha camps.WASH out of camps - Partners are primarily targeting informal settlements, collective centers, unfinished buildings and small villages with water trucking, aqua tab distributions, provision of NFIs, such as hygiene kits and water tanks. The wide spread of communities poses a challenge in identifying people in need and the installation of WASH facilities. The winter season will pose new challenges for access e.g. smaller trucks or tractors will need to be used increasing the number of trips. - These communities are reliant on support in the absence of functional water networks and while private water trucking exists, the cost is prohibitive to many households. - There is a gap in sanitation and hygiene promotion in these locations, leading to a risk of outbreak of waterborne disease. - Security and access remain a serious challenge: 186 settlements are hosting more than 106,000 people in need of water, sanitation, hygiene and NFIs in DZ, across the North, East and West Line, with acute needs in the East Line where access remains difficult. Water Supply Infrastructures rehabilitation - While there is a lot of work on rehabilitating water supply infrastructure, mostly in Ar Raqqa and Der Ez Zhor governorates, a lot remains to be done along the Euphrates. Coordination between Humanitarian and stabilization actors is crucial to increase the efficiency of the response. - Humanitarian and stabilization partners have been targeting areas where quick fixes have enabled the restoration of water supply to large catchment areas. While those quick fixes have had a positive impact, there is need for additional repairs to ensure sustainability and the prioritisation of quality of supply, as much as quantity. Most of the water stations are using surface water where recent rains and runoff have increased the turbidity of the raw water. This will remain a challenge as long as the rehabilitation will not include the reparation of the treatment systems.

Implementation of activities

Overall objective: To contribute to the resilience of conflict-affected people in Ar-Raqqa and Deir Ezzor governorates through improved quality of life, and health status Sub-objective 1: Improved access to safe drinking water sources for community members through strengthening the Ar-Raqqa and Der Azzor Water Units in the operation, maintenance, and management of water systems in select neighborhoods - SR will rehabilitate the water station, water networks fixing, water tanks constructing and distribution, clean water trucking, rehabilitation the water system in schools and health facilities, in addition to operating the water schemes. Sub-objective 2: Access to improved sanitation facilities enhanced for community members and school children through rehabilitating existing infrastructure. - SR will rehabilitate sewage and drainage systems, toilets and showers constructing, cleaning the rivers water courses, in addition, to support the local communities to be able to run the project after SR finish the intervention. Sub-objective 3: Environmental hygiene strengthened through support to communal waste disposal services in the operation of waste collection vehicles and waste collection campaigns - waste campaigns, - waste collection -fixing the waste collection vehicles - landfill rehabilitation. Sub-objective 4: Beneficiaries have increased awareness and adoption of good hygiene and sanitation practices through hygiene promotion measures. -hygiene awareness -hygiene kits distribution This project is linked to the UH-funded sector “reconstruction/rehabilitation of social and productive infrastructure”. The proposed project supports the priorities of the Humanitarian Response Plan HRP 2018 as follows: •WASH Objective 1: Sustainable water and sanitation systems are maintained and/ or restored to improve public health, including through the repair/ rehabilitation/ augmentation of water systems, support to operation and maintenance, support to wastewater systems, and support to solid waste management •WASH Objective 2: Most vulnerable groups receive life-saving assistance to reduce WASH-related morbidity, including through WASH technical training and capacity building for local authorities

Protection Risk Analysis

Syria Relief staff will participate in protection working groups, and child protection and GBV sub-clusters to take lessons learned for mainstreaming of protection in also WASH projects. Measures to protect the safety and dignity of beneficiaries will be contextualized to the political and humanitarian situation. In order to reduce risks of Sexual and Gender-Based Violence, considerations will be made with respect to the location and design of WASH facilities in order to make sure that there is no sexual exploitation occurs through the implementation process and this issue is going to be managed and monitored through the complaint mechanism and roles of the SR MEAL staff that will always be available over the targeted locations. Moreover, SR team will be coordinating closely with other NGOs in order to develop a special tool for monitoring the issue related to this topic. For example, latrines in schools, clinics, hospitals, and GBV centers will be lit and lockable. SR will focus on protection of beneficiaries especially women, and children from sexual abuse and harassment in schools, camps, and communities. In this regard monitoring and reporting tools will be developed and implemented and during the project cycle. Finally, NCA will organize training for partners during the project period on the UN Security Council Resolution 1325 and develop and simplified checklist to report the progress during the project implementation. SR will focus on enhanced participation of women in project design, implementation and monitoring of activities. All proposed activities are in full alignment. They have also been designed to be culturally appropriate, inclusive, and confidential. In this project, Syria Relief will ensure the safe access to services for all genders and age groups at all times. This measure will protect beneficiaries. Likewise, by installing complaint mechanisms in the targeted locations, which will be checked by Syria Relief staff during the time of the project, we will allow any protection issues related to this project to be known and seek immediate advice from the senior project staff in Syria for a response.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: - Withdrawal of American troops and the possibility of GOS entering the area.- Turnover of staff - Delays in the procurement process because of possible prices fluctuation - Landowners refusal for signing the MOUs for rehabilitation of WaSH infrastructure due to frictions with local councils. - Delays in project implementation due to the restriction on transporting goods inside NES - Violent armed conflict between groups opposite to SDC and SDC foces - Possible involvement of GoT in NES aeas - Violation of humanitarian principles or code of conduct by staff/ organization loss of confidential data (Data security) - Noncompliance with donor requirements in line with a signed agreement - corruption, mismanagement of funds and fraud Assumptions and Mitigating measures: -Constant Security monitoring and analysis of Security Risk Environment and Political Developments which impact on Access - Cross border SOP’s in place. - Registration in Syria with the local authorities. - Compliance with KRI laws- Continuous monitoring of the political and armed developments, which may affect NGOs. - Contingency plans to respond to changes in status quo. - Constant monitoring of the security situation with particular attention on OAG’s in AoO and areas bordering AoO. - Information sharing with other present organizations - Develop the security and contingency plan in syria - Develop a plan for alternative fund transfer routes. - Constant dialogue and advocacy with donors, and NGOs forum. - Transparency in finance and funding arrangements. - Record staff concerns and address as per HR policy - ensure that field and Iraq staff are aware of project steps - Ensure staff well-being - Map potential suppliers and contractors. - Develop alternative supply chain routes in both Syria and neighboring countries. - Develop detailed procurement plans and initiate procurement early. - Regular meetings with LCs and beneficiaries represents
