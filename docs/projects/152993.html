Project Summary

Based on the September 2018 IPC analysis, it is expected that 6.1 million1 people in South Sudan (59% of the total population) face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity at the peak of the lean season (July – August), of whom 47,000 are in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5) and 1.7 million in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). Unity state where the project falls has 71.3% or 214,000 people in emergency or catastrophic phases of the IPC classification and a further 395,000 in phase 3. This project is therefore developed to address the food insecurity issues.
Mercy Corps aims to contribute to building the resilience of the crisis affected populations through support to beneficiaries to improve their livelihoods performance . The project will also contribute to sustainable, equitable production, income and employment for the vulnerable through market-led production.
Mercy Corps appreciates that emergencies affects people differently. In South Sudan women, children and elderly are the worst affected with the conflict and economic shocks. As such in terms of targeting or selection, women, child headed households, elderly, PWD and the chronically ill will be highly preferred during the process. All activities including targeting, post distribution monitoring, evaluations will engage women separately to ensure adequate contribution to issues affecting them. All reports will be presented with information dis aggregated by gender. The project will target a total of 10,830 households that comprises approximately 65,000 individual beneficiaries.
The proposed project will contribute to the Food Security and Livelihoods cluster objectives 1 and 2 namely:
- Cluster Objective 1: Unconditional food assistance: Provide emergency regular life sustaining and life-saving food assistance to prevent famine, improve food consumption, improve dietary diversity and improve coping strategies for the most vulnerable population
- Cluster Objective 2: Food production for the most vulnerable households: Increase vulnerable household food production to protect the erosion of livelihoods through agricultural seeds and fishing inputs.
Cash for work grants to most vulnerable households with labour ability will avail temporary income for vulnerable households to meet their immediate food requirement while community common resource properties like dykes are rehabilitated to combat flood events.
Cash programming will be complemented by vegetable farming and staple crops inputs as a means to diversify their diets. Programme design is based upon market survey and vulnerability assessment and the FSNM September 2018 report high quality risk analysis with assessment of multiple types of shocks and stresses people face.
Livelihood promotion:
- Support vulnerable households towards short-cycle vegetable food production systems for home consumption and sale of surplus by distributing livelihoods assets e.g. vegetable seeds and tools, pro-resilience livelihood business startup grants aimed at diversifying livelihood options.
- Where possible, livelihood activities are supported to improve household income aimed at immediate improved household purchasing power of diversified food of choice and working capital of other livelihood options.
- Communities are supported in preferred livelihood supply chain management and livelihood value chain development aimed at strengthening their resilience to the causes and effects of the common disasters.
Through this project, Mercy Corps is planning to target the most vulnerable men, women, boys and girls in Rukona and Panyijiar for IGAs through distribution of livelihood inputs to support beneficiaries during the dry season hunger gap, which will be from February – April 2019 (three months earlier than normal). The
business start-up grants will be complemented by low input vegetable gardening and staple crop production(from March to May) as a means to promote household self-reliance and immediate food provision through own food production for both home consumption and sale of surplus.

Needs

Over the years, conflict has devastated the livelihoods of millions of South Sudanese. The South Sudan IPC analysis of January 2018 indicates that there are 5.3 million people (48% of the population) who are estimated to be facing Crisis and Emergency (IPC Phases 3 and 4) acute food insecurity, out of which 1 million people are facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Compared with the same time last year, this reflects a 40% increase in the population facing severe food insecurity in the post-harvest season. Worsening food insecurity is primarily driven by protracted conflict and displacements in Unity State, which have contributed to insufficient crop production, disruptions to livelihoods and persistent macroeconomic deterioration. Livelihoods have been further eroded by climatic shocks, such as prolonged dry spells and flooding, and pest infestations (e.g. Fall Armyworm). Of extreme concern is the anticipated worst-case scenario of large-scale Catastrophe leading to Famine (IPC Phase 5) which is reported likely in protracted absence of humanitarian assistance and conflict-related restrictions to population movement, and according to the IPC September 2018 outlook, counties of greatest concern include Panyijiar in Unity State, South Sudan. Therefore, delivering humanitarian assistance linking relief and early recovery livelihood activities is needed urgently to save lives in the proposed geographical target of Unity State. However, even with the levels of humanitarian assistance in May-July 2018, Panyijiar County remained in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and will require large-scale assistance to prevent extreme food security outcomes and loss of lives. In a context of chronic conflict, economic shocks, poverty, climate extreme shocks of frequent flash floods within the flood plain of River Nile are further undermining food security and livelihood viability in Unity State of South Sudan. Given the extreme circumstances the people in Unity State live under and the fact that most of these households lost much of their livelihood assets during the crisis, has led many to resort to negative coping mechanisms to deal with this reality and to support their families. Mercy Corps’ experience in Unity State, revealed that the use of local adaptive and high value open pollinated varieties of vegetables which are well accepted for their palatability, drought tolerance and high demand in market would ensure a sense of project ownership and project sustainability beyond project exit. Mercy Corps interventions on fishery and vegetable production support, in addition to promoting trade and dietary diversity would also enable IDP’s and host communities to access food varieties close to home. Based on market assessments and community need assessments in Panyijiar carried out by Mercy Corps, basic and good agricultural skills training which can facilitate the broader market linkages on topics such as food producer cooperatives, basic business management and market linkages etc. were desirable, as well as more practical trainings on market-led production i.e, household/low-input nutrition gardens and market-led fishery. Mercy Corps has identified people within Unity with vocational technical skills on vegetable processing that beneficiaries have expressed an interest to learn. These identified potential beneficiaries can undergo refresher training to prepare them as Trainers of Trainees of vocational skills like low external input vegetable producers and fish preservation techniques. In addition, the food production would enhance trade between the ethnic groups segregated within Panyijiar County supporting positive co-reliance and interdependence in addition to strengthening household resilience through skills development and household income generation.

Provide breakdown by women, men, boys and girls

women (300) , men (100), boys (80) and girls (120)For the purpose of this project, definition of vulnerable household: one that has one or more of the following characteristics:• chronically ill headed household, who are not able to work; child-headed (child is < 18 years); elderly headed (≥ 60 years) with children < 18 years; disabled (mentally and physical challenged) headed household; widow/widower headed households; single mothers; households with high dependency ratio (≥ 3 i.e. 3 dependants per healthy adult); households with acute malnourished child(ren); households with inadequate productive assets; poor and unemployed semi-skilled youths, households with low food consumption scores (FCS <35). The vulnerability criteria will be defined in a participatory manner with the concerned communities according to community-led wealth ranking system adopted to suit both SPHERE standards and local vulnerability context. The vulnerable female headed households and widows, families fostering children, and households whose crops failed and have limited access to income will receive specific attention. Care will be taken to ensure that child headed households, and the elderly are not left out of the beneficiary selection process. These include men and women who are elderly or single due to conflict related issues and are alone with children and other dependants, and who may be overlooked. Beneficiary targeting will be conducted following community based participatory beneficiary targeting, where the communities by themselves will take the lead in beneficiary targeting starting from the identification of vulnerability criteria, actual beneficiary targeting and endorsement of the list. Thus, beneficiary targeting will be accomplished through rigorous involvement and participation of community. Together with the FSL Cluster, specific criteria of vulnerability to malnutrition have been identified and represent key criteria for prioritization for the FSL cluster as well: - New arrivals IDPs are recognized as extremely vulnerable and therefore will be prioritised. - Among the new arrivals children under-five and PLWs will be recognized for targeting- Elderly (60+yrs)

Protection Risk Analysis?

Briefly describe how

Mercy Corps is committed to ensuring safe programming and mainstreaming protection at all stages of this project. Mainstreaming protection in the project will significantly mitigate the impact of exclusion of marginalized groups, particularly women from decision-making processes andin ensuring that we are better able to meet the needs expressly identified by our female beneficiaries, as well as those people with specific needs. Mercy Corps will create avenues to enhance women’s opportunities to decide and manage community activities by ensuring that they are consulted and targeted for participation. Mercy Corps has a strong community based approach throughout project implementation. Throughout programmes, it has succeeded in finding opportunities for women to participate in activities not traditionally targeted at them, The integration of protection staff in the project team will help ensure the implementation of key activities beyond basic safe programming and Do No Harm approaches.Complaints mechanisms will also be closely linked with protection committees and local GBV referral partners to ensure that any complaints related to GBV can be addressed with a survivor centred approach.Given the complex nature of displacement and conflict in South Sudan in addition to already existing high level needs in our areas of operation, Mercy Corps also engages with and targets host communities. This helps to support conflict sensitive programming and social cohesion from the start of the project. Not only are host communities benefiting from our project, but we also work to support host and displaced communities working together to manage potential sources of conflict (e.g. water sources) by supporting both communities to communicate proactively and reactively to conflict through different community based structures. A protection analysis, gender analysis, and risk analysis conducted for our programme areas will ensure that we are able to identify potential needs and risks.
