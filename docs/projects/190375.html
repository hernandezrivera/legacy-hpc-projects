Project Summary

The overall objective of these proposed interventions is to increase access to safe drinking water and provide adequate water to conflict-affected communities and promote safe hygiene and sanitation practices in Guto Gida and Sasiga woredas of the East Wollega zone, Oromia region, and Debate of Benishangul Gumuz Region of Ethiopia.
The assessment findings indicated a critical shortage of water and the community depended mainly on seasonal water sources like rivers, hand-dug wells, shallow wells, and springs. The team visited five hand-dug wells and seven springs that are not functional. In Sasiga, Guto Gida and Debate towns, the host community is getting water from improved water sources through pipelines but due to shortages of water and access problems, the IDPs in the surrounding town have to travel to a nearby river or unprotected spring to fetch water. The assessed non- functional spring water schemes are silted up, totally damaged, or/and cannot retain water due to cracks, while the hand-dug wells had faulty hand pumps. The water bureau estimated that households survive with 1 jerry can of water per day, which means 1.5 – 2 liters per person, which is far below the minimum standard of SPHERE. The limited water resource has led to tensions between IDPs and host communities, resulted in protection concerns as girls are traveling 2-3 km to fetch water, increased the prices of water and there is an anticipated Cholera outbreak.
As an immediate response, distribution of water treatment chemicals, promotion on HH water treatment and safe water handling, provision of hygiene kits (soap, bucket, washing basin, water container, women dignity kits) to support community hygiene promotion, sanitation including the construction of emergency semi-permanent latrines at crowded villages in the three Woredas where HH who lost their shelter are temporarily living in a host community & rehabilitation of water schemes are recommended as an immediate solution. As per the information from both woredas water offices and cluster update, there is no active WASH partner in both proposed woredas. After accounting for all the acute needs, the targeted areas are still in need of access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene promotion. FIDO will work with UNICEF focal, partner, and the relevant government offices to ensure that there is no overlap and coordinate for effective resources utilization.
The project proposes to reach 45,922 beneficiaries with a total budget of 369160.14 USD. FIDO’s interventions are in line with the WASH cluster’s suggestion in terms of prioritized location, intervention types, and budget allocation. FIDO will assure close collaboration with the WASH cluster, concerned government bodies, and beneficiaries in implementing and involving them in the decision-making process of the proposed project.

1. - Projects activities

Procurement, transportation, and distribution of NFI items like laundry soaps, body soaps, washing basin, buckets, and jerry cans for 13,145 individuals (325 for Guto Gida woreda and 8630 Sasiga and 4190 for Dibate woreda) or 2,629 HH of IDPs (325 for Guto Gida woreda, 1472 Sasiga woreda, and 832 Debate Woreda). Organize hygiene promotion training to 150 hygiene promoters (20 for Guto Gida woreda and 75 Sasiga and 65 for Dibate woreda. Conduct hygiene and sanitation promotion activities using megaphones to avoid mass gathering. Printing and distribution of 400 posters for hygiene promotion messages displaying hygiene practices. Construct 28 emergency semi-permanent latrines (4.35x2.15x1.5) for two woredas (5 blocks for Guto Gida woreda, 15 blocks for Sasiga and 8 blocks for Debate woreda) of IDP sites in line with the SPHERE standard, with one stance equaling 50 people. Construction of handwashing facilities at each emergency latrine. Digging of 28 solid waste disposal pits (5 pits for Guto Gida woreda and 15 pits Sasiga woreda and 8 pits for Debate Woreda). Procurement of hand tools for latrine and west disposal pit excavation and earth work per communal latrine. Purchase and install 500 mL liquid soaps for 28 hand washing stations for a total of 336 pieces over six months. Final excreta disposal service through permanently sealing latrines (The latrines will be sealed permanently if they fill up or if IDPs leave the site). Duplication of hygiene promotion guideline for hygiene promoters. Conduct Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM).Provide Personal Protective Equipment and hygiene materials (Face masks, hand sanitizers) for 92 hygiene promoters. Provide Personal Protective Equipment and hygiene materials (Face masks, hand sanitizers) for 92 hygiene promoters. Safe and accessible reporting by community members through establishment of complaint handling committees (50% female) and cascading bi-weekly meeting session. Rehabilitation of twelve non-functional water supply sources (seven nonfunctional springs, 1:250 individuals it will serve around 1,750 individuals and five nonfunctional HDW, 1:500 individuals it will be served around 2,500 individuals). Procurement, transportation, and distribution of households' water treatment chemicals. . FIDO proposes to provide three months of water treatment chemicals (about 180 sachets/ 3 months) for each HH. Conduct monthly consultation meetings with beneficiaries regarding the services and activities that affect them. WASH-Cos training (WASH committee will be trained on water management and maintenance of water infrastructure and sustainability in IDP sites. They will be recruited from community members; each committee will have members/woreda).

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

Initially, the project is designed based on the identified gaps. Like any other project, FIDO will create an effective communication channel between the government stakeholders and the affected population prior to the implementation of any of the project activity in accordance with the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) as well as sphere standards. FIDO ensures the participation of community members throughout the life cycle of the project. It will establish a transparent mechanism that allows the community members to provide feedback on the project implementation. FIDO recognizes that the essence of accountability is to respect the needs, concerns, capacities, and dispositions of those with whom we work and to answer for our actions and decisions. Hence, the organization is keen and committed to mainstreaming this accountability frame throughout the project cycle management following the Humanitarian Accountability Partnership Standard. Accordingly, relevant program information, beneficiary selection criteria, and beneficiaries’ entitlement from the project will be made available to communities in a timely, accessible, and accurate manner, communities will be informed and understand and agree with key decisions relating to our intervention. FIDO will devote itself to purposely empowering communities and building their capacity to participate in all components of the program cycle. Communities will be sensitized about their right to provide feedback and complaints and community feedback and complaint procedures that are accessible, safe, and effective will be provided for beneficiaries during the startup of the program and will continue to be communicated. Information sharing and awareness creation about accountability to the affected population will be held during health education activities by using different posters, banners, and printed IEC/BCC materials. These procedures will alert communities on their rights. Joint monitoring with the government and partners will be made to ensure the quality of the project response against humanitarian benchmarks. FIDO will strongly align its monitoring mechanisms with national guidelines/standards and SPHERE minimum standards. It will institute a strong and participatory monitoring system starting from the project inception through the implementation period to regularly collect project information in a participatory manner as well for analysis to allow for timely adjustments when needed. Activity achievement, output, and outcome data will regularly be collected against the project indicators at different levels, and the results will be compared against humanitarian standards/benchmarks.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

FIDO will establish a clear mechanism that allows the community members to provide feedback on the project implementation. Communities will be sensitized about their right to provide feedback and complaints and community feedback and complaint procedures that are accessible, safe, and effective will be provided for beneficiaries during the startup of the program and will continue to be communicated. A complaint handling committee will be established and along with responsible government stakeholders and FIDO, project staff will involve handling any concerns or complaints that arise from the beneficiary and acting accordingly. The committee will be comprised of women, people with disabilities, elderlies to ensure maximum community participation. Aside from the committee, FIDO will also set up alternate ways for feedback and complaint mechanism to promptly respond to concerns of beneficiaries and adjust implementation. Mechanisms to receive this complaint will consider the preferred mechanism of the community but will mainly use Help desk, community feedback suggestion boxes, provision of local stakeholders of contacts of FIDO staff and selected focal representative of the community. FIDO project staff and community volunteers will be trained on humanitarian accountability and its importance to mainstream them on the ground at every level of the project. FIDO will also ensure the collection and utilization of sex and age disaggregated data that adheres to recommended age groupings.
