Project Summary

Increase access to safe and adequate drinking water, acceptable sanitation facilities and improved awareness of safe hygiene practices among refugees and host communities. The target population are IDPS, returnees and affected host communities in East Darfur, South Darfur, South Kordofoan, and Kassala

1 - Needs

The IDPs camps and affected communities of four states are getting assistance through CARE and are currently receiving 15 L/p/d, in line with SPHERE standards. However there is a need to sustain the provision of these services through further construction, rehabilitation and maintenance of facilities. In the new targeted areas, such a Shataya the average is less than 10 l/p/d and comes from unprotected sources. The same multi-sectoral needs assessment has indicated that adequate water supply is one of the biggest challenges for IDPs and affected host communities including Kassala as not receiving safe and sustained access to water. In refugee camps and host communities, FGD participants reported that the water is pumped only in the morning and in the evening, which limits their access to adequate supply. 50% of the host community members still remain without latrines and similar needs exist for South Sudan Refugees.In South Kordofan, three inter-agency assessments conducted in El Liri, Rashad and Abu Jubaiha in October and December 2018, have shown a high number of IDPs and a consequent limitation on basic services provision, impacting all categories of populations. There are currently over 168,084 IDPs, whose majority now lives in Abu Jubaiha, and Rashad localities. At the same time, due to the resurgence in 2016 of the conflict in South Sudan, over 80,000 South Sudanese refugees have relocated to South Kordofan, mainly in El Liri and Abu Jubaiha localities. In El Liri, needs remain particularly high for them as well as for the host communities, who are also constrained in their access to safe water and to health services outside refugee settlements. In Rashad, There are only 11 health facilities functioning for 92,719 people, which is far behind the standards. Most of the IDPs are unable to access the health services because they do not have the money to pay for the services or drugs, while some cannot access them at night due to insecurity. The WASH as a result remains high. In East Darfur localities such as Abukarinka, Asslaya, Eldein still remain with high WASH need

2 - Activities / Outputs

WASH: Operation/Maintenance of water supply facilities; construction/rehabilitation of water sources, borehole drilling/construction; upgrading of water sources including water pipeline networks, upgrading water treatment system; chlorination of water sources; water quality testing; water user committee’s formation/revival & training; Households latrines construction/rehabilitation & decommissioning; Construction of latrines at facilities; CLTS triggering & latrines construction by beneficiaries; Shower construction, rehabilitation & maintenance; distribute water voucher/cash; recruitment & training of hygiene promoters; hygiene promotion awareness campaigns; Garbage collection & safe disposal facilities construction; Distribution of hygiene items & supplies (soap, child potties, Jerry cans, menstrual hygiene kits; etc.).

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

CARE promotes environmental friendly and risk avert mechanisms in its water and sanitation, health and nutrition response programs and livelihoods diversification interventions do have impact on the health of the public, environment and natural resources. Interventions to preserve and protect the environment will be included from the design phase, implementation and monitoring of each program intervention. The mitigation measures could include but not limited to: analysis of the potential impact of key interventions and discussion with community leaders and sector experts; regular joint water and sanitation facilities monitoring and supervision; emphasis on environmental education and capacity building for increased environmental awareness among beneficiaries and implementing partners; solid and liquid waste management; actions to avoid water pollution due to excavation, backfilling and concreating, hauling and damping of earth materials; controlling formation of stagnant water pool to avoid soil erosion and breeding mosquitoes. Health and nutrition facilities measures include cleaning campaigns, quality and monitored incinerators, and refresher training to health professionals, WASH experts and community volunteers. Youth, women, men and others will be organized into water users committees, hygiene promoters, natural resource management groups, health committees, nutrition volunteers, and VSLAs where appropriate and tailored training will be provided to contribute positively to the management of natural resources and peaceful co-existence. Environmental impact assessment will be carried out if interventions require to do so and will be decided with sector ministries.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

CARE uses different strategies in relation to the activities planned to be implemented: as women engage on income generating activities, their domestic work burden, mobility related risks and access and control over resources are well considered. Engaging men and boys, creating awareness on PSEA and others are among mitigation measures planned. Protection at individual, family and community levels are what will help to address the risks associated to the project activities planned.
