Project Summary

Raga, Pibor, and Tonj North Counties are among the hard to reach counties in South Sudan as stipulated by the WASH Cluster and UNOCHA with demanding WASH needs particularly among host communities, internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees. The effect of years of conflict, displacement and lack of basic services has continued to be felt throughout the country with an estimated 7.2 million people in need according to the 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview. The WASH condition is also demanding in these Counties with critical needs in access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene services to the IDPs, returnees and the host community. Peace Corps Organization intends to scale up water sanitation and hygiene services to this vulnerable group of people through an integrated approach in Nutrition, Health and GBV sectors. Provision of safe, reliable and sustainable water will be addressed through rehabilitating water points and developing new protected hand dug wells both at the community and institution level so that the affected population can have access to safe and adequate amount (15L/person/day) of water for drinking and other domestic uses. The rehabilitation of water points will be done after a GBV risk analysis have been conducted in the focus groups discussions with women and girls, conducted by female staffs and co-chaired by a GBV partner active on ground considering the safety and best location thus reducing the risk of occurrence of GBV cases and sexual exploitation of women and girls since they are the primary beneficiary of water. Nutrition facilities such as OTP/TSFP/SC, health facilities such as PHCC/PHCU and protection sites such as women and girls friendly spaces and child friendly spaces will be prioritized by PCO as part of integration among the various sectors. The water point’s rehabilitation will be done by trained hand pump mechanics selected among the locals in the community and the spares from the WASH core-pipeline hence ensuring acceptability, ownership and sustainability. Water Management Committees will take care of these water points and will teach the community on best practices to ensure apron is clean and also fencing the water points to prevent animals from defecating at the aprons.
Sanitation situation will be addressed through rehabilitation of latrines and hand washing facilities to ensure safe disposal of human excreta and hand washing at critical times. The rehabilitation will be done after consultation through FGDs on best siting which is safe and will have doors and locks to ensure privacy, safety and dignity on usage and will prevent occurrence of GBV and other forms of abuse associated with the use of latrines. This project will ensure communities are taught by WASH staffs and Nutrition/Health staffs (trained on WASH minimum package) on how to improve hygiene situation through household visits, hygiene creation and awareness campaigns with emphasis on hand-washing with soap at critical times, safe disposal of human wastes, treatment and storage of drinking water to prevent occurrence and transmission of Cholera and other water borne diseases.

1 - Needs

Humanitarian situation remains critical in south Sudan. The cumulative effects of years of conflict, violence and destroyed livelihoods have left more than 7 million people (about two thirds of the population) in dire need of some form of humanitarian assistance and protection according to South Sudan Humanitarian Response Monitoring Report 2019. Many of these affected population have the highest proportion of HHs that are continually dependent on unprotected water sources, and have limited or no access to latrines and WASH NFIs. According to REACH south Sudan situation overview 2019, high proportion of HHs (74%) reporting having a water borne related diseases, with malaria the most commonly reported (74%), fever (56%) and then by AWD (20%). For the households that can access safe water, a lot of time is spent in the water points with an average of 1200 households per hand pump spending more than 30 minutes and walking more than 500 meters which is below the sphere standards which may be a source of conflict between the host and the visiting community. Women and girls sometimes are forced to walk for long distance through forests and insecure areas so as to access water exposing them to various forms of GBV and additional dangers from armed groups since majority of them are responsible for fetching water. The operational hand pumps in Raga, Pibor, Tonj North Counties are overused and has led to more breakdown of pumps and increased queuing time at the viable water sources causing reduced per capita water consumption (under 15 L/person/day), hence increased consumption from unprotected water sources like untreated rivers and hand dug wells and also reduced time for other essential tasks at their homes. This competition and pressure on the scarce water is a driver to conflict between the host communities, the IDPs and even animals. Access to safe and gender sensitive latrines remains a big issue during both wet and dry seasons and thus open defecation is predominant in these areas of Warrap, Jonglei and Western Bahr El Ghazal Humanitarian Needs Overview 2019 estimates that about 10% of the community have safe latrines and they use it appropriately leaving an approximately 90% of population practicing open defecation. The sanitation and hygiene situation is further worsened by the fact that most of the community do not have access to a hand washing facility, poor knowledge and practices on personal hygiene and environmental conservation practices. Women have also been excluded and sidelined from leadership roles and opportunities which are associated with a little stipend or incentives. PCO intends to address this through women empowerment and to be heading committees. Community members especially the IDPS and returnees may lack capacity building and this project will address this through various trainings that will impact skills such as hand pump mechanics.

2 - Activities

• Training of enumerators and hand pump mechanics who will help the community in borehole mapping and repair of the boreholes and protected hand dug wells to ensure sustainability of the project.• Rehabilitation and repair of boreholes and protected hand dug wells- This will be done by trained hand pump mechanics after a GBV risk analysis through FGDs with women and girls using the in-kind supplies requested from the core pipeline.• Development of new protected hand dug wells where there is no existing borehole to provide safe water to the vulnerable IDPs, Returnees and the hosts.• Rehabilitation of latrines will also be done in or near institutions after a GBV risk analysis has been done and the latrines will have locks and doors to enhance privacy, dignity and security and hence reducing the risk of exploitation, such as sexual exploitation and abuse.• Establishment of hand washing stations and facilities at the rehabilitated sanitation facilities to ensure communities wash their hand after defecation.• Transport and distribution of WASH NFIs requested from the WASH core pipeline to the beneficiaries and monitoring their use. This will be done by WASH staff and also Nutrition/ GBV/Health staffs trained on WASH minimum packages including demonstration on usage.• Selection and training of community hygiene and sanitation promoters/ volunteers to visit households with messages of improved WASH which will help influence positive behavior change to the community.• In schools, WASH club will be established with student members and teachers as patrons. School PTA members will also be formed and trained so as to train children in schools on WASH through Child to Child (C to C) approach• Streamlining GBV Training to WASH staffs after being trained by the protection teams active on ground.• Establishing and training water management committees on best ways of managing water points including guiding the elderly, sick, people living with disabilities, animals and children at the water points to ensure sustainability of the project.• Training of Adolescent girls on Menstrual Hygiene Management both in schools and community level including demonstration on usage of menstrual hygiene management kits.• Training of Nutrition and Health staffs on WASH minimum package as part of integration among the different sectors.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Political dynamic, peace and stability will determine probability of sufficient safe protection environment to enable stable durable solutions, through creating enabling environment. The Government is committed to ending conflict as long term intervention to the conflict affected IDPs, returnees and host communities in the country and therefore Water, Sanitation and hygiene is closely linked with Nutrition, Health, Protection. However this does not stop the linking of the project with durable solutions like building of community owned resources persons and improving population well-being since a healthy nation is a wealthy nation if other factors like conflict are not present.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes. PCO works hand in hand with line ministries, Relief and Rehabilitation Commission and local leadership. These will be engaged across all stages of implementation including involvement of targeted beneficiaries to foster sustainability upon project period expiry. PCO’s strategy of working with local communities and authorities to ensures that local knowledge and skills are driving the ideas and solutions which have defined the design of this project and will continue to influence throughout proposal implementation with a strong self-build approach that can be easily replicated independently by communities. PCO will ensure that activities are handed over to the community and other WASH actors in the targeted locations through the local authorities and through coordination meetings after project closure. The trained volunteer female hygiene promoters and Water Management committees and existing pump mechanics from the relevant ministry of water and irrigation staff will be able to continue giving community support on hand pump repairs, management of sanitation facilities, distribution of MHM re-usable materials to women and adolescent girls and sensitize the community on hygiene promotion messages.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

PCO have developed a tool for feedback and complaint mechanism which we have been using in the previous similar projects and have always proven to be efficient. The local leaders and other community representatives will give their opinions and complaints as well as possible recommendations. These complains, compliments and recommendations from the beneficiaries and the other stakeholders e.g. local authorities will then be screened and processed for further actions by management . Information relating to the project’s response to feedback will be fed back to communities by the Project Management Committee via scheduled consultation meetings, ensuring ongoing two-way communication. PCO have locally recruited and trained Community Hygiene Promoters who conduct household visits and are able to get feedback directly from the beneficiaries and they are addressed immediately depending on the urgency. We also have sessions with the beneficiaries during our field supervisions where we discuss the challenges, project impacts and the areas which they feel requires more concern and the best approach.
