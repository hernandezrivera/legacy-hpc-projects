Project Name

Project Summary

The provision of integrated emergency lifesaving Nutrition services to the vulnerable children U5s; 8,367 Boys, 11,780 Girls, and 10,487 Women (PLWs) through fixed sites and mobile approaches in Fangak (Jonglei), Pariang (Unity) and Renk (Upper Nile) during 2020.
Within a 1 years of implementation, the project is anticipated to utilize $760,000; This project will contribute to the 4 cluster objective; (1) Contribute to the reduction of mortality, mortality and child suffering from protection threat through increasing access and equitable utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific service delivery for Children U5s with severe and moderate acute malnutrition and pregnant & lactating meeting the admission criteria will be enrolled into the CMAM program; Provision of special nutritious foods, Maternal infant Young Child Nutrition, Micro nutrients supplementation and behavioral change communication approach; (ii) To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment; Active and passive screen for malnutrition at community level through the community nutrition volunteers and Maternal Infant Young Child Nutrition Promoter focusing on the prioritized locations of Pariang (Unity) Renk (Upper Nile) and Fangak (Jonglei) by the end of 2020.

(2) Ensure safe and equitable access to critical integrated basic services to enable [number] prioritized target people to meet their basic needs; (1) To increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and joint programming targeting vulnerable groups; through conducting malaria screening and treating, Strengthening livelihood skills with kitchen gardening, and demonstration gardens to support on complementary feeding for children under five both girls and boys.
(3) Regular monitoring of the nutrition situation in the project area implemented and the information used for appropriate actions at the project level; Through monthly capacity building for staffs on how to use the NIS software and Quality of data submitted to National and Nutrition cluster for proper informing and planning purposes.

1 - Needs

As per the latest IPC analysis in May-July 2019, hunger and malnutrition in South Sudan still maintains its abnormally high levels. This trend is attributed to protracted political crisis, characterized with insecurities and displacements without livelihoods; inadequate coverage of social services i.e. health care, nutrition action, education etc. to many population pockets that only serves by nature amidst ignorance. The report further more show that estimated 6.96 million people (61% of the population) are likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity or worse, an estimated 1.82 million people will face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, and 21,000 are likely face Catastrophe (IPC phase 5). In former Jonglei State, food insecurity remains high with an estimated 1.33 million (73% of the population) facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. Coincidentally, it is also the most populated and the most food insecure State in South Sudan during the May-July projection period. Six Counties are in Emergency (IPC Phase 4), namely Canal/Pigi, Twic East, Uror, Nyirol, Bor South and Akobo neighboring with Fangak County. Canal/Pigi is the worst affected, with an estimated 10,000 people (10% of the population) in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). According to a SMART Survey conducted in Canal/Pigi in April 2019, the County has a GAM (WHZ) prevalence of 24.3%, indicative of Critical (Phase 4 on the IPC for Acute Malnutrition scale), that require urgent and significant scale-up and intensification of treatment as well as humanitarian food assistance to address food consumption gaps. Hold the Child’s progressive evidence based programing projects 2020 intervention in communities (i.e. Renk, Pariang and Fangak) which have recently been characterized with GAM rates above the WHO threshold of 15% apart from Fangak with 14.2% and SAM of 4.4% (IMC, 2018 Dec). [1] Fangak is a flood prone County trending with episodes of water borne disease i.e. cholera in 2017 and Hep-E 2018 Week 28 IDSR report. Prone to population movement, a safer home for returnees especially from Sudan, Ethiopia and different PoCs of South Sudan including Juba PoC. The associated increase service needs are anticipated to swell during 2020-21 as communities are anticipate to relate out of PoCs and find safe heavens like Old fangak and hence the need for humanitarian action. [2] Renk County trends with a GAM prevalence of 32.1%), and SAM of 8.5% (May 2019 Smart Survey), a value double the WHO recommended emergency threshold and the highest GAM in South Sudan as of July for 2019 with high caseloads noted to be in Jelhak among other southern Payams. The county has been reported to experience Measles Outbreak in week 23 of 2019 a signal of a very low service coverage; and coupled with conflict clashes associated population movements in search for safety, water and food among other services. [3] Pariang County lies in the inter-communal conflict belt of Unity State like Abiemnhom, Mayom characterized with restricted livelihoods; With IPC classification, Pariang is classified under Phase 3 (Serious acute malnutrition) and listed among the 12 counties in need of urgent attention. there are localized movements in and out of the county across the national borders. The FSNMS report show over 57% of households on Unity state reported a family member who migrated across boarders (the highest in the country) many of whom pass through Pariang. The Low food production among locals amidst high 2019 seasonal floods; low purchasing power and child feeding practices are directly attributed to high propositions of female headed households (65% for unity state) and 20% proportions of household with injured members (According to FSNMS 22). Hold the Child programing as recognized a differential admission trend for boys at 48% and girls at 52% during our 2018 programing and link to the known cause of the gender disparity i.e. social barriers and roles

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes. In situation of displacements as a result of conflict, insecurity among others, Mobile outreach mechanisms will be deployed to efficiently support those cut out of service access for various reasons including the displaced.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project is linked to both government and community systems available in the country. National stakeholders are involved in developing and planning levels to provide a clear understanding and nutritional status of the country. At this process, mapping and categorizing of location is done based on their needs and the available resource. This linkage helps to identify service providers to maximize resources and avoid duplication of service and resources in the same area. Community system is linked with the Boma Health Initiative approach (BHI). This BHI intiative will strengthen community mobilization, malnutrition screening through the trained Home Hygiene Promoters to support and enhance community linkage with health services. This approach will strengthen the existing Community Nutrition volunteers and mother to mother support groups to timely identify and refer malnutrition cases in the community.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Based on the context of the south Sudan, There is ongoing population movements across the country for various reasons including insecurity as a result of inter communal clashes, looking hunger in some parts of the country and search for good clean safe drinking water. This project is designed to provide allocation for IDPs, Returnees but tailored approach to make sure social protection on every child, every person has equal rights to essential health service.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

South Sudan has been experiencing waves of the conflicts and inter communal clashes, Floods, insecurity related to access issues across the country. However, with peace waves in the country, our assumption is that there will be sufficiently safer protection environment and open operationalization without hinges and obstacles during the implementation phase.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes. Sustainability/Handover is an integral element of Hold the Child Programing. This project ushers with stakeholder’s involvement along with CHD joint monitoring and quarterly reviews; other measures i.e. local recruitment including staff and CNVs, thematic capacity building including CHD staff, working with partners within the counties. All project supplies will be secured through cluster pipelines. Further more the project is designed with inclusive beneficiary and stakeholder feedback platforms to integrate local evolving needs to stay relevant during 2020 and establish capacities to community/CHD/partners’ takeover beyond the project cycle
