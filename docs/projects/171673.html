Project Summary

Mercy-USA for Aid and Development intends to set up a project to address the urgent nutrition needs in Banadir, Galgadud, Hiran, Mudug and Togdheer regions of Somalia. Since the collapse of Somalia's central government in 1991, repeated conflict in conjunction with poor food security, limited healthcare and nutrition service availability and has resulted in Somalia having among the worst health-related indicators in the world.
According to FSNAU-FEWSNET-2020-Post-Gu-Technical-Release, 2.1 million people across Somalia are expected to face Crisis or worse (IPC Phase 3 or higher) outcomes between now and December without sustained humanitarian assistance. An additional 3 million people are expected to be Stressed (IPC Phase 2), bringing the total number of people facing acute food insecurity to 5.1 million. Humanitarian assistance must be sustained through December 2020 to prevent Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes for 2.1 million people.
Mercy-USA will provide treatment of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) through IP (Inpatient Therapeutic), OTP (Outpatient Therapeutic) and SFP (Supplementary Feeding) services in line with the Basic Nutrition Service Package (BNSP) at up to 39 facilities and related outreaches. Mercy-USA will train and support nutrition workers to identify, provide lifesaving treatment and follow-up on cases of malnutrition. Mercy-USA will also integrate preventative services including providing Infant Youth and Child Feeding (IYCF) counseling and micronutrient supplementation at MCHs. Community-level social mobilization will be done to promote healthy nutritional practices, including campaigns for micronutrient supplementation, deworming, and improved feeding practices. The Mother-led MUAC approach will be used for referrals.
Mercy-USA will ensure that people with disabilities participate in the implementation of project activities by ensuring that they are represented in all community meetings. The project will consult with PWDs about location, design, safety, and facilities' appropriateness. During project implementation, segregated focus group discussions will be held with the community to identify gender inequalities, discuss all the genders' needs, and understand the risks faced by PWDs in accessing nutrition services. Activities will target to address these needs during program implementation.
Access to children with disabilities and their caretakers and/or disabled pregnant and lactating women will be ensured and increased through outreach activities and behavioral change communication campaigns.
During routine and regular program monitoring and evaluation, Mercy-USA staff will collect disaggregated data by disability, age and gender to monitor the impact of programs on vulnerable groups. Mercy-USA, during both monthly and quarterly reviews and analysis of data, will seek to monitor the inclusiveness of programming by comparing achievements versus available population data.

Project Indicators

Improved, integrated, quality basic nutrition services for children and pregnant and lactating women including IDPs are available in the coverage districts. All the communities have improved knowledge of essential nutrition behaviours and increased capacity to plan manage and monitor recurrent shocks and stresses. Mitigation Measures

Risk that the project does not have sufficient internal mechanisms to combat and prevent child safeguarding and PSEA:• Mercy-USA, guided by internal PSEA and Child Safeguarding Policy and international best practices, will safeguard gender justice and equity and promote gender equality. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. The following mitigation measures will be applied to address this risk. • Mercy-USA will carry out mapping exercises on a semi-annual basis to understand marginalized communities in the areas of operation and their needs. • Data collected for program implementation will be segregated in gender and disability, ensuring that the program reaches marginalized communities. • Mercy-USA will ensure that formal confidential feedback and complaints mechanisms are accessible to all community members, especially marginalized communities. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms. The following mitigation measures will be applied to address this risk. Mercy-USA will have three forms of confidential feedback mechanisms to ensure that all community members are reached, including illiterate people, those who cannot physically travel, and finally, those who do not have resources to feedback. The follow mechanisms will be in place:• Suggestion boxes • Hotline numbers• Focal persons at the community level. • Community mobilization will be intensively carried out to ensure the uptake of the formal feedback mechanisms established.• Mercy-USA will continuously monitor and evaluate the effectiveness of the mechanisms established and make improvements continually. Heightened insecurity that might result in interruption of program activities. Mercy-USA will mitigate this risk by implementing program activities through community acceptance and working closely with local communities to ensure safety of operations. Limited accessibility due to poor roads worsened by seasonal rains. Mercy-USA will select suitable four wheeled vehicles for program operations. Logistics department will develop a detailed travel plan that is season specific identifying the ideal periods of supply delivery. High levels of displacement due to failure of rains, floods, higher malnutrition rates, low food stocks and spike in new IDPs. Mercy-USA will mitigate this risk by tracking humanitarian conditions and advocating for additional funds if necessary to support new arrivals.Criminal threats to staff and beneficiaries in operational environment leading to robberies, destruction of property, attacks on staff, or beneficiaries. Mercy-USA will carry out regular and ongoing staff security updates and establish an active and rigorous evacuation plan detailing roles of local and international staff. Communities are forced to leave their place of residence to access humanitarian assistance (humanitarian assistance as pull factor). The following mitigation measures will be applied:• Mercy-USA will continuously map the location of beneficiaries to better target IDPs and pastoralists ensuring that services are provided at the convenience of the target beneficiary.• Mercy-USA will reach beneficiaries who are located far from health centers through the provision of outreach activities. • Mercy-USA will select CHWs from the local community, ensuring no settlement is left out in the program geographical reach.

Please provide further explanation

Mercy-USA will provide services in facilities that are easily accessible by communities. Services at the facilities will be provided in a confidential environment with care given to support appropriate services for children, the elderly, and the disabled. All midwives hired in the program will be female, and Mercy-USA will endeavor to support a majority of staff, including nurses and community workers to be female in order to provide appropriate services in the local context. Nutrition workers will be trained on basic protection issues involving children, rights for the elderly and the disabled at the beginning of program activities in order to identify and support appropriate care responses and potential referral cases. Educational and counseling sessions held at the facilities will use appropriate pictorial cards and IEC materials to help explain messages to the widest possible audience.
