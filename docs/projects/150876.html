Project Summary

WRRS will Provide access to safe Water, Sanitation and Hygiene for women, men, boys, girls, marginalized, minority and the physically challenged in emergency need in our target areas. We will ensure that the program delivers developing sustainable, gender sensitive and disaster responsive WASH infrastructures and resilience. These interventions will include provision of safe water and for both domestic and animal use, Rehabilitation and protection of existing water source will be enhanced. Just but to include: provision of solar water pumps to sampled boreholes, rehabilitation of shallow wells, provision of water storage containers/bladders within IDPs in Dohbley, distribution of water through water tracking/vouchers and chlorination of the same at facility level and HH. Conflict and flood affected areas will have construction latrines at the facility level and IDP settlements. Gender sensitive communal latrines will be constructed with lighting especially within the IDP settlement. Some of the flood affected latrines shall be desludging and repaired in our target areas. Hygiene promotion in communities and schools, distribution of hygiene kits and sanitation tools. Through our nutrition and health centres we shall teach beneficiaries on best hygiene and sanitation practices as an act of behaviour change, community workers will be equipped with emergency response preparedness and plan as a mitigation measure.
WRRS through the interventions will reach 25,000(9,000 women, 7,000 men, 6,000 girls and 3,000 boys) through equitable WASH interventions in Lower Juba, Baidoa, Garowe and Sool region of Somalia.

Needs

Water hygiene and sanitation concerns have been on the rise ranging from heavy rainy and drought seasons in somalia, with the current floods that were experienced in 2018 being the current ones, this subsequently had followed a prolonged drought that had hit Somalia in 2017. According to REACH assessment, Lower Juba reported 5% severity of AWD where only 0.92HCF/10,000 population. Morbidity rates are high (>20 %) in many parts of the country but highest among population groups surveyed in northeast and central parts of the country. Improved rainfall during 2017 Deyr (Oct Dec) season has eased drought conditions in many parts of Somalia. However, the Deyr rains were mostly below average to near average and the 2018 Gu rains are also forecast to be below average. Large scale and sustained humanitarian interventions and seasonal improvement due to Deyr season rains have reduced the risk of Famine in Somalia through mid 2018 but needs for assistance remain high and critical, an estimated 301 000 children under the age of five are acutely malnourished, including 48 000 who are severely malnourished. Under Five Death Rate (U5DR) rates were also below 2 per 10 000 per day in all 30 surveys compared to 5 out of 31 during Gu 2017. Access to basic clean water, sanitation and hygiene has affected the livelihood structure of the residences and the IDPs in the areas proposed by WRRS to intervene in. In this regards we will work towards quality and equitable service delivery to the most marginalized in the areas and those in hard to reach areas inorder to provide the basic WASH services and build on the behaviour change and response during hazards and shocks like floods and droughts.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Accountability to affected population is a key aspect in the trans-formative agenda This will be enhanced through proper community engagement and ownership to avoid causing any risks to the affected or targeted beneficiaries. . The project intends to involve the community in all stages of the program cycle, so that they understand the aim and the outcome of the entire project and embrace its as it rolls, they will also understand the guiding principle behind the decisions made.The project intends to use already existing community governance structures and use them as health committees. These will be involved in recruitment of staff, community mobilization and monitoring of the project.Their presence will represent the interest and role of the community in successful completion of the project. The project will also have a feedback mechanism , from which complaints and comments from the community will be collected, and fed back to the same. This will be done in a highly confidential manner with very few staff in the management allowed to interact with this process. WRRS will address the complains as they come and embrace the comments for the sustainability and acceptance of the services within the community since these are live saving interventions.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Gender disparities are on the rise in the operational communities, where women usually take a back seat in issues of community development. WRRS through the project, through community mobilization and awareness, will ensure women are empowered and represented to influence decisions and outcomes. A Do No Harm approach will be adopted in all our interventions. Contextual analysis is important in the implementation of this project considering the various potential sources of conflicts in the target locations both at leadership and community level. Regular assessments and community engagements will be done to learn if the project activities, approaches and methodologies are sensitive to the local context. Vulnerable community members generally refer to those disadvantaged with compromised access to resources to meet their daily basic needs. The project staff will ensure that during community mobilization and sensitization, all community members are given equal opportunity to participate.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

WRRS has been working in areas where security and conflict has been a major factor affecting the beneficiaries. Accountability for violations is limited, with traditional or formal mediation and justice mechanisms having been disrupted, or having become disrespected or inaccessible. As a result of general insecurity, inter-clan conflict escalations, indiscriminate attacks as well as military offensives and withdrawals in the context of the conflict between AMISOM and AS, and supporting actors. This is further aggravated regarding violations for marginalized groups where societal inequalities result in reduced protection. WRRS will work closely in collaboration in providing education on Disaster Risk Reduction, and how to go about in areas with explosive remnants of wars.
