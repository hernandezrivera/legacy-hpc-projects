Project Summary

Since 2013, on-going conflict has resulted in 2.48 million refugees fleeing and 1.84 million internally displaced with around 203,000 residing in PoCs (OCHA, Sep 2018). Conflict has impacted on food security, and based on the IPC analysis, by March 2019 it is anticipated that 49% of the population will be at Crisis or Emergency Levels of food insecurity. High rates of morbidity along with poor health, nutrition and hygiene practices, as well has high levels of food insecurity have resulted in high malnutrition rates. Although NBeG is relatively calm, it has suffered indirect impacts of conflict as well as erratic rainfall affecting staple crop performance with 54.6% of the population in NBeG were under Crisis, Emergency and Humanitarian Catastrophe (IPC, Sep 2018). Households in Juba PoC lack the means for income generation and are dependent on food aid to meet food needs. In Unity, lack of access to nutritious foods places households in Bentiu PoC and outside the PoC at severe risk of malnutrition as well as poor nutrition practices. Despite the re-vitalised peace agreement in August 2018, persistent insecurity in many locations means high levels of returns are unlikely from Juba PoC.
While this project will not directly focus on the FSL cluster objective 1, Concern aims to partner with WFP to ensure food access in Juba PoC through GFDs; and in Aweil West (another partner provides GFDs in Aweil North) during the lean period from July-September. This project will contribute to the FSL cluster objective 2 to increase food production and improve dietary diversity. In Aweil West and Aweil North, extremely vulnerable households whose main livelihood is agriculture will be supported with emergency seed provision to improve food production. Seeds will be provided through voucher fairs where beneficiaries will have more choice in selecting seeds according to their preference. Concern will use Community Agricultural Extension Workers and lead farmers through a training of trainer approach on climate smart agriculture and biopesticide production as well as post-harvest handling. Beneficiaries will be formed into groups for block farming to pool resources and share risks. Dry season agricultural support will focus on vegetable gardens with households with members in OTP/TSFP to improve access to nutritious foods. In Juba PoC, Bentiu PoC and Guit, linked with the Nutrition programme, Concern will support MtMSGs to engage in sack farming in PoCs and kitchen gardens outside of PoCs for improving consumption of nutritious foods.
The project will contribute to the FSL Cluster objective 3 though targeted life sustaining cash assistance to strengthen households and community coping capacities by building productive assets and reduce reliance on food aid through conditional cash transfers. With hazards associated with erratic climate in Aweil West and Aweil North such as prolonged dry periods and flooding, Concern will support vulnerable communities with CFA. Communities will prioritise assets such as shallow wells, feeder road and dykes, which will be constructed by vulnerable beneficiaries who will then receive cash transfers to meet emergency needs during the lean period.
The relatively stable situation in NBeG allows for linking with Concern’s longer-term integrated programmes focused on resilience-building such focusing on more training on climate smart agricultural techniques; addressing the high rate or morbidity through improving water and sanitation infrastructure and hygiene practices; increasing iCCM for early detection and prevention of common illnesses; and improving health and nutrition behaviours. In NBeG, beneficiaries of this programme will also feed into these resilience programmes. In Unity, beneficiaries of vegetable production inputs will also be targeted by WASH and MICYN interventions and Juba PoC Concern will coordinate with WASH partners to ensure Concern’s beneficiaries are targeted.

Needs

The on-going conflict since 2013 has resulted in 2.48 million refugees fleeing to neighbouring countries and 1.84 million internally displaced with 203,000 IDPs living in PoCs (OCHA, September 2018). Conflict has impacted on food security and based on the IPC analysis for September, 2018 6.1 million people faced Crisis or Emergency levels of food insecurity during the lean period in 2018. Although harvests in October-December 2018 will reduce the proportion of the population in food insecurity, 4.1 million people will remain at Crisis and Emergency levels. Despite the re-vitalised peace agreement in August 2018, the combined impacts of conflict, poor harvest and erratic rainfall patterns affecting crop performance will mean an early start to the lean season in March 2019. It is anticipated that 5.2 million people (49% of the population) will be at Crisis Levels or worse by March 2019. All of Concern’s projected areas of intervention are facing serious levels of food insecurity by Jan-March 2019: Guit (Unity) and Aweil West (NBeG) counties are projected to be under emergency phase while, Aweil North (NBeG), Bentiu PoC (Unity) and Juba PoC will be in crisis phase with likelihood of worsened situation without the humanitarian food aid (IPC, Sept 2018). Along with food insecurity, high rates of morbidity and poor health, hygiene and nutrition practices will result in high rates of malnutrition. Despite better security context, NBeG has suffered from the indirect impacts of conflict such as decline in the macro-economic situation, flows of food commodities and food price rises as well as erratic rainfall patterns impacting on the staple sorghum performance. Although most households access their food from markets, increasing food prices and limited income for most households made it impossible to access food at markets. Consequently most households resorted to negative coping mechanisms such as begging, charcoal burning and migration which separates families often leaving women with additional incoming earing burdens and neglecting childcare. Concern’s post-planting assessment in Aweil West and Aweil North estimated that while most households planted 4ha for this growing season, crop performance has been compromised by a long dry spell between May-July and was worsened by flash floods that damaged large areas of crops. According to SMART surveys in December 2017, the GAM and SAM rates in Aweil West (GAM - 14.6%; SAM 2.7%) and Aweil North (GAM - 18.5%; SAM 4.8%). As well as food insecurity, poor dietary diversity is factor in high malnutrition rates with only 3% of children 6-24 months receiving food from 4 or more food groups in Aweil West and 2.6% in Aweil North. In Juba PoC, IDP households have limited access to markets outside the PoC, high food prices along with lack of income generating opportunities due to displacement status results in households being particularly vulnerable to food insecurity in the absence of humanitarian assistance. Concern’s SMART survey in December 2017 show an increase in the Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence from 11.0%, in October 2016 to 12.8% in December 2017. According to the WHO classification on the severity of acute malnutrition, this result classified as serious. Along with food insecurity, poor nutrition practices are a cause of malnutrition with only 15.4% of mothers reporting adequate dietary diversity.In Bentiu PoC and Guit, malnutrition is also a result of poor nutrition, hygiene and health practices. Only 11.9% of children 2-23 months in Bentiu PoC and 6.9% in children 6-23 months who are received minimum diet diversity and most households lack the means to have access to nutritious foods. While food assistance is provided to conflict affected and vulnerable populations by other partners, there is a need integrate with Concern’s nutrition activities to promote consumption of nutritious foods through the nutrition prevention activities implemented in the targeted communities.

Activities

The project will be in line with the FSL Clusters Objectives:Emergency food assistance: This will be provided under a separate project in partnership with WFP where households in Juba PoC and Aweil West will receive GFDs. Emergency seed provision to improve food production: In Aweil West and Aweil North, Concern will support extremely vulnerable households with provision of seeds to improve food production. Households will be targeted based on community-defined criteria such as having disabled, elderly or chronically ill in a household, household with 3 or more children under 5, households with a member in OTP/TSFP and female headed households. A market assessment will be conducted to ascertain the availability of seeds preferred by beneficiaries; understand supply chains and identify if traders can meet increase in demands. CWG tools will be used and the information collected will be used to identify potential positive or negative impacts of the project on markets. Seeds will be provided at seed fairs where participating traders will be selected as per Concern’s procurement procedures including not only price, but quality of seeds, inspections of shops and stores and capacity to meet increased demand. Vouchers will be provided to beneficiaries who will be able to purchase cereals and legumes that they will be familiar with for cultivation. Community Agricultural Extension Workers and lead farmers will be trained by Concern and County Agriculture Departments and they in turn will provide training to beneficiaries on climate smart agriculture and biopesticide production. Following seed fairs, PDMs will identify satisfaction levels of beneficiaries with the programme. For dry season, focus will be on provision of inputs for vegetable gardens, targeting also MtMSGs with the aim of promoting dietary diversity and income generation. Trainings will focus on vegetable production and MIYCN including cooking demonstrations. In Juba PoC, Bentiu PoC and Guit, FSL activities will be part of Concern’s Nutrition programme. MtMSGs will be supported with inputs for sack farming in PoCs and kitchen gardens to produce vegetables and provide cooking demonstrations. Hygiene practices will also be incorporated into trainings. CFA to provide immediate food access and community assets: Concern will improve access to vulnerable households in Aweil West and Aweil North during the lean period from July-September 2019 with conditional cash transfers through CFA. Households will receive cash transfers for constructing community-prioritised assets to build resilience of households and communities. Targeting will be done using community defined criteria. Households must have an able-bodied member to participate (those without able-bodied members will be selected for GFD). Existing community structures with equal female/male membership will conduct hazard mapping and identify DRR measures. Cash transfers will allow vulnerable households to meet immediate needs during the lean period without having to resort to negative coping strategies such as selling productive assets.Linking with resilience programming: The context in NBeG is fragile but calm, allowing for linking emergency FSL interventions with longer-term programmes. These programmes focus on promoting climate smart agriculture such as conservation agriculture, use of fast maturing crop varieties and natural resource management cope with prolonged dry periods. The programmes are integrated with Health, WASH and Nutrition to reduce the impacts of illness, poor hygiene, health and nutrition practices and gender inequalities on malnutrition. Beneficiaries of this project will be targeted for Concern’s resilience programmes in NBeG, specifically those who have household members in OTP/TSFP. In Juba PoC and Unity, while the context does not permit longer-term development, integration with WASH and Nutrition will contribute to addressing causes of morbidity and malnutrition.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Prior to the beginning of the programme, sensitisations will provided to beneficiaries detailing targeting criteria, entitlements to agricultural input assistance, the fair process for seed fairs monitoring as a mechanism to provide feedback, the CRM and safety measures beneficiaries can take during fair days. Concern will consult with beneficiaries to understand preferences for seeds prior to the procurement processes for selecting traders for seed fairs and vegetable kit distribution to ensure seeds provided are those that beneficiaries are used to cultivating. Prior to fairs, Concern will hold a meeting with traders to explain the fair process and the importance of adherence to Concern’s policies on preventing exploitation and abuse such as the Code of Conduct and Programme Participant Protection Policy. On the days of vegetable kit distribution and fairs, information is available on entitlements to assistance is available to all beneficiaries on clear banners in the appropriate language or pictograms. Information will be provided on the voucher process, the monetary value and how beneficiaries can use the vouchers to purchase seeds. Given low numeracy levels and high levels of illiteracy, Concern staff will be in the fair to assist beneficiaries with calculating costs and ensure they have correct change.

Information will be context specific as the project for inside Juba PoC, Bentiu PoC, Guit, Aweil West and Aweil North. As the majority of staff are recruited from the local areas including inside the PoCs, they will be able to communicate messages that is understood by communities. Juba PoC will be direct vegetable kit distribution for MtMSGs so information on entitlements will be provide. In Aweil West and Aweil North, emphasis will be placed on targeting the most vulnerable for both CFA and emergency seed provision. However, men’s groups will also be supported to analyse how their roles impact on their spouses’ and children’s nutrition.

Concern conducted an evaluation of its recently completed multi-sectorial emergency programme, which had FSL components to obtain beneficiary feedback on how the programme impacted on their food security. Concern also regularly discusses with farmers during the season on staple crop performance such as sorghum to understand farmers’ perspectives on food security for the next few months and triangulating this with its own rainfall measurements. Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

In South Sudan, there are considerable protection risks in the project locations so and protection will be considered at all stages of the programme cycle. South Sudan currently ranks 187/189 countries in the 2017 HDI and the country loses 36.3% when the value is discounted for inequality (HDI reduced from 0.388 to 0.247). The figure reflects the strong patriarchal society and culture in South Sudan and women play secondary roles in the community. This places them at a disadvantage in household decision making such as on livelihood activities and child nutrition. It also exposes women and girls to risks including early/forced marriage and sexual and domestic violence. Although laws in South Sudan provide for the protection of women and girls against discrimination, abuse and GBV, they are often misinterpreted or ignored. In 2017, the Protection Cluster reported 3,585 GBV cases (95% female, 19% children); increasing by 35% from 2016. This is in particular in locations affected by conflict such as Juba PoC and Unity. In Juba PoC, 34% of women had been exposed to intimate partner violence with 70% of these not seeking formal help, and 33% of women being survivors of sexual violence (No Safe Place, 2017). Displacements due to conflict or hunger expose households and individuals to high protection risks with violence, GBV, forced recruitment, abductions, killings, property loss, and mental health risks.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Despite the revitalised peace agreement in August 2018, recent trends show high risks of insecurity in programme locations such as Guit, as well as overcrowding and tensions in the PoCs. The erratic climate such as prolonged dry season or heavy rains can impact on delivery of assistance. In consultation with communities, distributions and trainings will be planned in line with seasonal calendars. Distributions and trainings will be done prior to rainy season and climate smart techniques contribute to minimising impacts of dry periods through soil water conservation. In NBeG, as many men have migrated to search for employment, women have additional responsibilities in addition to their household tasks and childrearing such as earning incomes. For poorer female-headed households, they are often required to hire causal labourers to do what are traditionally men’s tasks such land clearing and preparation. Women as per social and cultural norms have limited decision-making roles as decisions are made by men. This includes women’s rights to property as they cannot own the property or land they work and can impact on whether they can engage in food production. Poor and extreme poor households lack the means to acquire productive assets such as agricultural inputs and livestock to improve food security and are often excluded from community decision making and social networks. Disabled and elderly lack physical means to engage in agricultural production as they lack mobility. In addition, women with disability are excluded from decision-making processes, 48% reported never having access to decision-making process through community participation and engagement mechanisms (IOM, 2018). Concern will ensure specific needs of vulnerable groups are identified and such groups are empowered, through including them in discussions on project planning and ensuring that the modality of assistance (GFD, seed provision, cash transfers) is appropriate to their needs.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

With considerable protection risks in South Sudan, Concern needs to consider risk mitigation measures at all stages of the project cycle. Prior to the beginning of the project, Concern will assess security and triangulate information to ensure risks are minimised implementing the Do No Harm principle. Concern’s staff are responsible to implement Concern’s Code of Conduct, Programme Participant Protection Policy, Child Safeguarding Policy and Anti-trafficking in Persons Policy. Included is mandatory reporting of exploitation or abuse. Concern Equality Adviser based in HQ will review project design. Specific needs of and risks to women, men, girls and boys as well as PSNs will be considered when Concern conducts needs assessments such as if poorer farmers can engage in agricultural production and whether disabled can access productive assets. Concern has been working in NBeG for nearly 20 years and Unity and Juba for over 4 years and has built up a strong relationship with local authorities and community leaders ensuring strong acceptance of Concern and this ensures Concern can safely access communities and many risks are reduced improving beneficiary access to distribution sites. Concern will continue to inform community leaders and authorities of the project. However, given the context, security risks mean that Concern has Standard Operating Procedures in place which are updated according to the security situation. Protection measures include planning to avoid threats, departure and curfew times, communications, storage, hibernation and evacuation procedures. Deterrence measures will be last resort in the event of severe threats to staff/beneficiaries including suspending the project until safety is guaranteed. The Area Coordinators are in charge of implementation of security rules in the areas under their responsibility while the Country Director has overall responsibility for managing security in Concern’s programmes.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Due to the considerable protection risks in South Sudan, Concern needs to consider risk mitigation measures at all stages of the project cycle to minimise protection threats. Given different inequalities of various groups, specific needs of and risks to women, men, girls and boys as well as PSNs will be considered during assessments to ensure the correct modality of assistance is provided based on inequalities such as mobility, gender and socio-economic status (such as unconditional cash transfers for disabled, seed provision to food insecure farmers). During assessments, Concern will identify protection risks and measures for risk mitigation for beneficiaries. Before distribution of cash transfers and fairs, Concern will communicate with community leaders to ensure sites are safe for distributions. Prior to distributions, beneficiaries will be sensitised on measures they themselves can take to reduce risks such as travelling to sites in mixed groups and during daylight hours. Households will be required to nominate one person to receive vouchers or cash transfers, usually the senior female in the household as this encourages joint decision-making. Distribution sites will be located in consultation with communities in safe locations such as church grounds or health facilities with shade, water points and sex-segregated sanitary facilities. Help desks on distribution days and the CRM will be used to identify protection cases and make referrals to Protection partners. During distributions and fairs, people with reduced mobility will be provided with casual labour to transport commodities.
