Project Summary

Responding to the increasing demand in the overburdened communities as a result of the continuous arrival of tens of thousands of forcefully displaced people. The project proposed interventions seek to improve the humanitarian situation for the war-affected population by providing transitional temporary shelters for the IDP families, which will provide more security and privacy to the inhabitants, prepare the settlements/campsites with the needed intervention to improve the inhabitant IDP families’ quality of life and distribute winter cash assistance for the in-need families.
The project will be implemented for the affected population in Idleb governorate (Dana, and Salqin sub-districts). 19,500 people will benefit from all activities (3,432 men, 3,822 women, 5,148 boys, 7,098 girls) while coordination with local councils, shelter centers’ management, and other actors in the area is the first mandatory step for all outputs; the following shows the project implementation steps:
• Distribution of 3,000 cash for winter assistance to 3,000 IDP HHs in Salqin Sub-district, 150$ per HH.
• Campsite preparation with all infrastructure needed Dana sub-district, 450$ per HH for 300 HHs; the activity will include removing obstacles and leveling the land, strengthening the soil with backfilling materials, graveling the site to leave it in the best shape possible, providing all the infrastructure and services needed as, sanitation, drainage, water systems, electricity system, fencing, facilities etc.
• Construction of 300 transitional housing units with all infrastructure needed in Dana Sub-district, 2500$ per HH with all needed facilities (connections to WASH facilities and kitchen areas) within the unit.
• Providing 300 house-based solar systems with all needed connectivity in Dana Sub-district, 800$ per HH.
As the document “dignified and safer living conditions” shared by SNFI cluster, the design of the sheter unit will include:
o Ensuring internal living spaces’ security and privacy by providing doors and windows for the houses
o Ensuring the beneficiary’s safety against life-threatening issues.
o Ensuring a healthier environment by promoting hygiene and maintaining the WASH facilities in the house
o Providing illumination and a light power source in each room by providing the needed electrical points (lamps, switches, wires...etc.) and connecting them to the local power source
o Ensuring better living conditions for people with special needs by providing special architectural elements (metal grips and handles, metal railings, special needs people suitable toilets...etc.)
IRD will coordinate with the local authorities and target the most crowded communities and/or camps.
To be accepted by the project criteria, HHs and property owners should agree on the terms of assistance and sign a contract that allows the BNF to stay for at least one year.
Valid HLP documents that verify the ownership of the properties must be presented.
Coordination at all levels and with all stakeholders will be continuous throughout the project duration, and the implementation will be monitored and eventually evaluated.

3 - Needs

Shelter and NFI needs in Syria are widespread resulting in signiﬁcant living standard consequences and affecting physical and mental well-being of the affected populations. Overall, shelter severity is still noticed across Syria in 2022 according to the latest reports shared by the HNO. The number of populations in-need for shelter according to the same report has reached 5,503,225 people by Oct 20th 2022, of those, around 3 million (53%) are in areas of severity 3-5. Approximately the same figures are found in the NFI need, but more than 76% are considered in 3-5 severity areas.Geographically, these areas are concentrated in the northwest of Syria, where 34% from the population are reported living in sub-standard shelters with low access to electricity/lighting, insulation from heat and cold, deterioration of shelter integrity, in addition to lack of privacy. In conjunction with this, 38% are facing increasing risks of eviction.The overall NFI PiN has increased by 5% to 5.7 million (from 4.9 million in 2022). Winter clothing, mattresses and blankets have witnessed a significant increase in inaccessibility in 2022 when compared to 2021, Such items are crucial to ensure the well-being of the most vulnerable, particularly during harsh winter seasons when access to basic NFIs is considered lifesaving.As the SNFI 2023 recommendations, the activities were chosen to meet the SNFI HLP requirements and to respond to the increasing needs under both sector objectives 1&2.

The intervention will include removing obstacles, land leveling, graveling, water network, drainage, sewage network, electricity, fencing, etc.A 3: Construction of 300 transitional housing units with connection to WASH facilities and kitchen areas, IRD will identify 300 IDP HHs, who live in inadequate shelter or tents and verify their info. The housing units will be served with interior WASH facilities and kitchen areas and connect them to the nearest services networks if available.A 4: Providing Solar Energy Systems for 300 HHs with all needed connectivity, IRD will identify 300 IDP HHs, who suffer from the lack of available power sources and provide them with the needed solar energy system power source.IRD will still consider the HLP availability as a must in all shelter-related activities to avoid any conflict in the area and to preserve the right of the owners and the BNFs.IRD will evaluate all the activities through its MEAL team to measure the satisfaction and impact on the BNFs and targeted communities.IRD will sign MoUs with the LCs to ensure the facilitation of activities. As well as signing the needed protocols with AFAD if needed.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

IRD adopts a zero-tolerance approach towards sexual exploitation and abuse. And it is committed to the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse both within IRD and within the framework of IRD programs and their beneficiary.To this aim, IRD’s Policy Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse: • Encourages prevention; • Promotes detection; • Sets out a straightforward investigation procedure; • Sets out a mechanism of sanctions IRD fully upholds principles of freedom of speech and protection of victims of sexual abuse and exploitation. As such, IRD has developed mechanisms that can contribute to preventing sexual exploitation and abuse such as • Signing of the Code of Conduct by the staff in order to clarify unacceptable behavior and in particular sexual exploitation and abuse; • Information to the beneficiaries, staff, and all stakeholders about the existing Complaints and Response Mechanisms - CRM which can allow them to send their concerns and complaints in a confidently way without fear • Implementation of training and awareness sessions to train and empower the teams, particularly for staff exposed to working with minors or the most vulnerable population; • Identify risk factors on an ongoing basis and in particular throughout the project cycle; • Put in place evaluation procedures for IRD staff, partners, and stakeholders by carrying out due diligence, particularly with regard to their ethical commitments and past behaviors.
