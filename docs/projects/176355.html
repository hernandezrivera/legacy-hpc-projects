Project Summary

The proposed project will provide training to the vulnerable households to enhance fishing, crop and vegetable production mainly for consumption at the household level and sale of surplus at local markets in selected payams of (Bor town, Kolnyang, Anyidi, Makuach, Jalle and Baidit) in Bor South County of Jonglei state and (Nyikabur, Tukoro, Yokara, Bori, Bokana and Lojora) payams in Terekeka County of Central Equatoria state which will create extra income for these vulnerable households. These payams are chosen due to their suitability for fishing, crop and vegetable production.
SAADO will target 9,000 households in Bor South County and 7,000 households in Terekeka County in this proposed response with emergency fishing, crop and vegetable kits support. The 2 locations have been selected because of the needs prevalent in these counties according to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) January 2021 which shows both Counties in IPC 4 extreme levels of food insecurity and the existence of ongoing food security and livelihoods supported by FAO-ELRP, Cash for Seeds, Livestock vaccination and treatment and WFP supported Food for Assets programming by SAADO in the proposed areas. The emergency livelihood kits will be obtained from the FSL cluster recommended FAO core pipeline.
SAADO will put in place a mechanism for effective and participatory community vulnerability mapping in the selected payams and bomas and will use a comprehensive beneficiary selection criteria developed, discussed and agreed upon with the communities to include Men, Women, Boys and Girls.
During community consultations at various levels, men, women boys and girls will be consulted to fully understand and appreciate the impact of the current crisis on vulnerable men, women and children that would further inform our proposed response. “Do no harm” and AAP principles will be applied throughout the duration of the project, so that any possible negative implications of the project at community level are addressed before they occur. This will be done through integrating a strong Communicating with Communities and Engagement (CCE) feedback mechanisms.
The proposed project will build upon and complement SAADO's 11-year experience in implementing food security and livelihoods programmes in the proposed counties of Terekeka and Bor South Counties including; Terekeka County: General food distribution (GFD), School Feeding Programme (SF), Food for Assets Project-2020 to 2022 (FFA) supported by WFP, Emergency Livelihoods Response Programme (ELRP) 2020 to 2023; Cash for Seeds project 2020 to 2023; Livestock vaccination and treatment supported by FAO 2018 to 2021. In Bor South County: Emergency Livelihoods Response Programme (ELRP) 2020 to 2023; Cash for Seeds project 2020 to 2023; Livestock vaccination and treatment supported by FAO 2018 to 2021.
SAADO’s proposed emergency response will also complement the lifesaving, livelihoods and nutrition activities carried out by other FSL partners in the two counties such as ADRA in Terekeka County and VSF-Germany in Bor South County of Jonglei state. The proposed activities are necessary and sufficient to achieve the stated cluster objectives for these interventions based on the relevance of each activity in increasing access to emergency food security and livelihoods support for the most vulnerable households in the targeted communities. The indicators selected are SMART and SAADO has in place a strong monitoring and evaluation system and mechanisms to measure these indicators and to ensure the intended results are achieved. The means of verification, such as monthly reports, distribution lists and regular field visits are strongly credible and are currently used by the organization in its MEAL processes across its field offices in the country.

1 - Needs

An estimated 168,900 of Bor South’s County women, men and children will not have sufficient access to food and livelihoods in 2021 according to the humanitarian needs overview report January 2021. It is projected that out of the 298,200 people in need in the County,65,400 people are projected to be in IPC Phase 5 while 103,500 people will be in IPC Phase 4. A combination of factors has led to this situation as the communities were hit hard by the triple shock of intensified conflict and sub-national violence, a second consecutive year of major flooding, and the impacts of COVID-19 and the subsequent lockdowns in 2020, and a protracted macro-economic crisis resulting in record high food price spikes.The sub-national violence in Jonglei state has displaced thousands of people and resulted in hundreds of casualties as violent clashes and reprisals have played out between Murle, Dinka Bor and Lou Nuer communities. The conflict has been as a result of persistent Murle attacks into Dinka and Nuer areas and large-scale coordinated counterattacks in March 2020, by thousands of Nuer and Dinka in Pibor which involved the theft of a significant number of cattle.To compound this dire situation, an estimated 1,066,000 people have been affected by flooding in areas along the White Nile since July 2020 with nearly 90 per cent of those affected in Jonglei state. Abnormally heavy rainfall from July to October 2020 which led to the overflow of the Nile, Pibor, Sobat, Lol and other rivers, topography with flat land and minimum gradient and the collapse of the main dyke along the White Nile has led to massive flooding in Bor Town according to OCHA report December 2020. The report indicated initial displacement of more than 33,000 people into Bor Town’s higher ground from flooding. The NOAA/NCEP Global Ensemble Forecast System report also cites that the production loss due to flood damage is estimated at 26,892 Ha of cultivated land which translates to an estimated loss of 11,916MT of cereals with an estimated 6,000 heads of cattle, 36,150 goats and 5,400 sheep reported to have died. According to the Food Security Outlook for South Sudan (FEWSNET, January to April 2021) Emergency (IPC Phase 4) is likely in the flood-affected county of Bor South where it is likely that high proportions of the population either have large food consumption gaps with most households depending on humanitarian assistance. To buttress this further, acute food insecurity remains high in severity and scale due to years of conflict that have driven asset losses and significantly weakened the economy, as well as large-scale floods and periodic conflict that caused crop and livestock losses. According to FSNMS data, a ‘Critical’ level (GAM WHZ 15-29.9 percent) of acute malnutrition exists in Bor South County state that is classified as Extreme (IPC Phase 4) or worse, attributed to poor dietary quality and quantity and to the impact of flooding on disease incidence, morbidity, and access to health and nutrition services. The humanitarian situation in Terekeka County remains fragile due to a multiplicity of vulnerability drivers including intercommunal violence, floods, population displacement, commodity price fluctuations, and pre-existing vulnerabilities according to the UN Office for the coordination of humanitarian affairs September 2020 report. Currently, Terekeka County is experiencing a renewed period of sub-national conflict which has displaced hundreds of people as violent clashes and reprisals have played out between the Yungwara and Kobura sections of Mundari community according to IRNA report October 2020. Terekeka County also experienced unusually heavy rains and the overflow of the Nile River and its surrounding Jor and Kote lakes which caused flooding in low lying areas of Terekeka County washing away planted crops.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

SAADO’s proposed project will utilize the established beneficiary communication and accountability (BCA) mechanisms and project management committees (PMC’s) to provide information to the beneficiaries as well as address issues around prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Whenever such issues are raised SAADO’s protection staff will link the survivors with protection implementing partners as part of strengthening the referral pathways. The project management committees will also publicise and manage a complaints and response mechanism which will allow beneficiaries to provide feedback and anonymously report grievances and complaints related to PSEA. The PMCs will use project-specific risk-based protocols to respond to complaints. The project management committees will be trained to follow SAADO’s PSEA policy standards and guidance for reporting and investigation, and any other requirements as per national law.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

SAADO’s proposed project will ensure tailored transparency and feedback mechanisms such as open meetings, verbal, written and pictorial notifications of decisions and actions; consultations with women and men (separately where necessary to ensure distinct gendered voices are heard), consultations with different age groups and persons with special needs, and confidential complaints mechanisms (such as a complaints box) will be used.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

SAADO’s proposed project will establish Beneficiary Communication and Accountability (BCA) mechanisms for communicating information relating to the project, as well as soliciting feedback, grievances and complaints from beneficiaries and will take into consideration issues such as traditional decision-making structures, access to communications, local culture and literacy. SAADO’s commitment to accountability will be clearly demonstrated in ensuring that the project beneficiaries in the targeted counties have a genuine voice in the assessments, design and decision-making process that inform the priorities, modalities and trajectories of this proposed project. In addition, as part of their orientation training and continual development, the project personnel receive instruction on why AAP is prioritized, how it is operationalized, and what their roles and responsibilities are in making it work. In each target area SAADO will ensure that communication with the community and key stakeholders is timely, accurate, appropriate, effective and understood. Tailored transparency and feedback mechanisms such as open meetings, verbal, written and pictorial notifications of decisions and actions; consultations with women and men (separately where necessary to ensure distinct gendered voices are heard), consultations with different age groups and persons with special needs, and confidential complaints mechanisms (such as a complaints box) will be used.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

A core component of SAADO’s beneficiary accountability and communication in this proposed intervention will be the regular dissemination of project-related information and updates to community members (beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries). Communities will be encouraged to provide regular feedback through monthly community consultation meetings facilitated by established project management committees. Information will be fed back to project management teams who will be responsible for adapting and iterating implementation strategies and workplans accordingly thereby ensuring field realities are reflected in monthly planning hence facilitating flexible and adaptive programming based on lessons learnt and emerging priorities and needs. In addition, information relating to the project’s response to feedback will be fed back to communities by the project management committees through scheduled consultation meetings, thereby ensuring ongoing two-way communication.
