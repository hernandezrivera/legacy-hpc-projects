Project Summary

Despite relatively better seasonal rains of the last Gu’, and the overall nutrition situation continuing to improve due to a combination of improved food security conditions, reduced outbreak of diseases and sustained humanitarian interventions. However, high level of acute malnutrition tends to persist in several population groups due to underlying/structural causes and the adverse impacts of the 2016/2017 drought persist in some areas, most notably among pastoralists who lost most of their livestock and people who became destitute and were displaced. Insufficiency, irregularity of rainfalls and poor groundwater quality are the main causes of lack of water and displacement of communities. The quality of the groundwater is poor to moderate (with only 40-50% suitable for drinking water) with the majority of the water sources having excessive levels of salinity (70%) when compared to the United Nations, World Health Organization (WHO) standards for drinking water (1,500 µS/cm) and due to this pastoralist communities residing in this area and their livestock are suffering from shortage of water supply and many times children suffer from diarrhea caused by the salinity of the water . Lack of water treatment systems in most rural areas and the limited knowledge on household water treatment and safe storage comprise some of the underlying causes of displacement and AWD/cholera out breaks as well. Furthermore, there is an existing preference for surface water sources considered to be “sweet” water as opposed to saline groundwater supplies even though the former is unsafe. There is limited understanding of the risks around contaminated drinking water. More than 80% of people neither know nor use recommended methods of household water treatment and safe storage such as boiling, solar disinfection, filtration and chemical disinfection. Unavailability of chemical disinfectant in the local market is one of the barrier identified from the problem tree analysis . According to the IRC recent assessment – May 2018, almost 50% of the available toilets in Galkacyo IDPs are not functional due to collapsing or filled and might endanger the life of women at night .117 cases of diarrhea were reported the last two weeks prior to the assessment. This project will address underlying causes of AWD/cholera and displacement though improving water quality and quantity and sanitations facilities

Needs

Throughout much of the past 2 years, Somalia has experienced drought with considerable population and livestock movements in side Somalia. Outbreaks of Acute Watery Diarrhoea (AWD) and displacement have necessitated emergency response and a major water trucking operation to provide water to the most vulnerable communities. With many of the seasonal water sources failing due to poor rains, and other due to higher salinity, there has been considerable reliance on a limited number of high-yielding water schemes whose ongoing performance is critical to the drought response. This limited access to good quality water sources contributes to the displacements or high AWD/cholera prevalence situation due to the use of nearby available contaminated surface water sources that was harvested last season. This project will address both salinity and quality of the contaminated water through domestic desalination facilities and “Sawyer 181” water treatment facility at household level with capacity to reduce turbidity 91% and 99% of the microbial and filtering of 8-10 liters per hour. As women mostly deal with household responsibility including water fetching therefore after installation of the desalination facilities women will be able to sell the purified water and will eventually improve their economic status. The IRC will also improve the water retention capacity of Berkads to upcoming rainy seasons through rehabilitation and cash for work approach. Construction new sanitation facilities as well as dislodging filled sanitation facilities through cash for work to improve the sanitation coverage in the Galkacyo IDP settlements. The project will take several measures to address any gender disparity by ensuring women and men are actively involved in the design and targeting of project activities, in particular (but not limited to) the siting and design of sanitation facilities. Activities

Activity 1.1.1: Undertake distribution of domestics desalination machines to women groups in areas were access to potable water sources are not possible due to higher salinity problems.Activity 1.1.2: The IRC will rehabilitate and improve 18 Berkads to increase their holding capacity in areas of great need. The Berkads will be strategically located along routes of emergency water trucking to additionally benefit this lifesaving activity when required, serving as water reservoirs. These will be rehabilitated through a Cash-For-Work subsidy approach to increase community involvement, ensure their participation and improve their livelihoods. Activity 1.1.3: The IRC will introduce a point of use water treatment for target households with during emergencies (AWD and cholera outbreaks), as well as support the installation and utilization of sawyer181 for households whose primary water sources are Berkeds and hand dug wells. On top of this, the IRC will also promote household the safe Water chain will be ensured through regular cleaning of water containers and safe water storage.Activity 2.1.1: Construct/rehabilitate institutional sanitation structures. To promote latrine access, the IRC will work with beneficiaries through cash for work to construct or rehabilitate institutional sanitation structures. The IRC will consult with communities to select sites that are in well-lit areas that are a short distance walk from where the targeted population resides. Activity 3.3.1: Distribute hygiene kits for displaced, school going girls and vulnerable households. Vulnerability criteria for distribution will be set by community representatives (at least 50% women). The hygiene kits will contain clean water containers, Sawyer 181 and soap for hand washing and sanitary towels for menstrual hygiene management.Activity 3.3.2:Pre-position cholera/acute watery diarrhea supplies

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The projects will be implemented in a way which prevents long-term over-exploitation, pollution and degradation of environmental conditions, both at the local level (e.g. by addressing solid waste management, or drainage from water points) and at the macro-level (e.g. by avoiding over-abstraction of aquifers). In general, IRC will strive to ensure appropriate skills and resources to maintain the systems put at the disposal of communities and local governments in order to compensate for diminishing external support - including capacity building, training, establishing Water Management Committees, liaising with local government, all of which are aimed at improving sustainability. There will be continuous information sharing and feedback between the IRC, community leadership, the WMCs and communities via open public meetings and public notice boards. A complaint mechanism will be established and operated throughout the project. The mechanism will involve assigning an IRC focal person to record and follow-up on any complaints received.
