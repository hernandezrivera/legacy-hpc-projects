Project Summary

The project will provide integrated life-saving and recovery health, nutrition and WASH interventions to the most vulnerable IDPs and host communities population in North Darfur.
The project aims to improve vulnerable communities’ living condition and contribute to reduction of morbidity and mortality rates through provision of quality sustainable and equitable integrated WASH, nutrition and health services in Elfasher and Kabkabiya localities in North Darfur state, Sudan.
The project will be provided to about 147,650 people (35,696 women, 30,828 men, 45,431 girls and 35,696 boys), among them about 6,500 newly displaced HHs as a result of the armed clashes.
Project interventions (health, nutrition and WASH) will be integrated to the targeted beneficiaries specially women, children and persons with disability to ensure addressing root causes and contribute in reduction of mortality and morbidity rates, ensure service provision to the most in need and vulnerable populations, ensure effectiveness and sustainability of the project.
WASH interventions include provision of quality and adequate water supply to health facilities, schools and other institutions within the targeted communities, rehabilitation and quality assurance of existed water sources; gender sensitive sanitation activities including construction of latrines at facility level and community level, hygiene services including awareness on water related disease and how to prevent/mitigate its transmission, hand-washing, water purification.
The project will also provide preventive and life-saving Nutrition interventions including provision of CMAM components (treatment of severely and moderately acute (SAM & MAM) U5 children and pregnant and lactating mothers as per CMAM guidelines, referral of SAM children with medical complication to stabilization centers (SC) IYCF components and community based activities including MUAC screening, follow-up malnourished children, disseminate nutrition and health messages.
Health interventions include prevention, promotion and treatment activities, include strengthen health system and improve accessibility to adequate health services through rehabilitation and equipping of health facilities, provision of essential medical and non-medical supplies, capacity building of health staff and community workers, provision of comprehensive minimum basic PHC package and response to medical emergencies and outbreaks.
Integrated to WASH, health facilities will be provided with appropriate and clean water supply, hand washing facility and gender sensitive disability sensitive latrines.
Integrated to nutrition, medical check and treatment of U5 children, assessment of U5 children's nutritional status, educating caregivers on health and nutrition messages and best family practice.
The project will manage anticipated risks if occurred including:
- Insecurity: security hazard are not unlikely to occur including armed groups and inter-communal conflicts, these if occurred would threaten delivery of project, life of personnel, risk of looting and destruction of assets and facilities, in response to that APDHWO will develop risk management plan including contingency plan, closely monitor and respond in accordance.
- Accessibility: network accessibility is absent in some locations specially in Sortony IDPs camp, communication plans will be developed accordingly. All targeted locations are accessible by vehicle except during rainy seasons, this will negatively affect project delivery; in response to this APDHWO will develop risk response plans including increase interval between visits, provide supplies covering more periods, provision of onsite warehouse.
- Economic challenges include inflation and fluctuation of exchange rate; the risk management includes early planning and execution of procurement processes.

1 - Needs

The needs are increasing in North Darfur as well as in the whole country; political instability, economic crisis and inflation and security instability had its negative impact on communities. Population in need in Kabkabiya, Saraf Omra and As Serief localities of North Darfur are estimated at over 135,000 people (25,661 men, 29,713 women, 37,817 boys and 41,869 girls), among them about 22,500 IDPs in Kabkabiya locality (4,275 men, 4,950 women, 6,300 boys and 6,975 girls). Needs for water, sanitation and hygiene: WASH severity in the targeted localities is critical/extreme in Kabkabyia and Saraf Omra and catastrophic in Es Sireaf locality as per HNO 2023; Regarding WATER: the average % of population using surface water in the 3 localities is 39%, and only average 22% of population have access to water comes from an improved water source, provided collection time not exceeding 30 minutes for a round-trip; in SANITATION, total population in need represents 55% of the total population, with 32% of population utilize unimproved facilities and 23% disposing human faeces in open spaces or with solid waste; regarding HYGIENE, about 68% of population are lying under the catastrophic category which is indicated by hand washing with water and soap practice. (Source: Sudan WASH sector HNO and HRP 2023)Other data indicating the need for WASH intervention include: The percentage of open defecation is very high especially in As Serief 57%, Kabkabiya 15% and Saraf Omra 30% (S3M 2018). Lack of improved, safe and gender sensitive latrines within institutions is high as well considering that about 30% of health facilities in North Darfur are lacking partially or completely improved latrines (HeRAMS August 2022 bi-annual report). Good hygiene practice and knowledge is low within the targeted localities with nearly 0% access to handwashing facilities and nearly zero have good knowledge on 5 key handwashing times in the 3 localities. Period prevalence of Diarrhea is significantly high among children in the targeted localities (As Serief 58%, Kabkabiya 45% and Saraf Omra 59%) (source: S3M).Needs for nutrition: Acute and chronic malnutrition rates are high among U5 children in the targeted localities with average of GAM 20%, SAM 4.5% and stunting 52% prevalence rates, these rates are extremely exceeding the country thresholds; reduced accessibility to nutrition services and lack of awareness on mother and young children nutrition, and other multi-cluster causes including (in addition to direct nutrition related cause) increased prevalence of infectious diseases among children, limited access to health services, low water quality and quantity, poor hygiene and sanitation. Focusing the light on mothers’ nutrition practices it found that the average rate of exclusive breastfeeding is only 47%. The immediate life-saving nutrition needs include preventive and social protection nutrition services for children under-two, treatment of malnutrition for children under-five and PLWs and emergency supplementary feeding program (e-BSFP) for U5 and PLWs. Immediate needs include also integrated and complementary Health and WASH services to address root causes of malnutrition.Needs for health: Accessibility to mother and child health services is low in the three targeted locations with average of 32% of pregnant women did not attend ANC, only average 21% received PNC, EPI coverage for 0 – 4 months is about 51% in the three localities.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The project will provide integrated health, nutrition and wash activities at institution and community levels as followingHealth:- Support 8 health facilities (2 secondary hospitals and 6 PHCCs) including equipping with essential medical and non-medical equipment and furniture and provision of medical and non-medical supplies- Availing and capacity building of health staff and community health workers- Provision of in-patient and out-patient consultation and free of charge treatment as per guidelines- Provision of MCH services (continuum of care) including ANC, PNC of mothers and neonate, BEmONC, IMCI, EPI - Referral of emergency cases to the next health level- Timely respond to medical emergencies and outbreaks including training of RRT- Raise community awareness on health related issues integrated to nutrition and hygiene awareness raising activities at community level and facility level WASH:- Availing water supply through Water tracking at health facilities and for IDPs - Permanent and temporal latrines construction and provision of handwashing facility- Local Hygiene promoters training.- Raising community awareness on proper water handling, food handling, vector control, prevention of water-borne diseases, environmental and personal hygiene, proper use of latrines, waste disposals, methods of conducting hygiene promotion, economic issues and social promotion, community mobilization- Distribution of hygiene kits- Hygiene promotion campaign will be conducted to disseminate key hygiene and sanitation messages and will be linked with the dissemination of messages on Child Protection and GBV prevention. NUTRITION: - Increase coverage and quality of nutrition services through establish/rehabilitate and support nutrition centers (SCs, OTPs, and SFPs) as per SMOH/UNICEF/WHO criteria and guidelines; including equipping with basic equipment and furniture, timely availing medical and nutrition supplies, capacity building and training of nutrition workers (staff/CNWs).- Early detection of malnutrition among under 5 children through MUAC screening and referral to the appropriate nutrition service.- Management of malnourished U5 children through appropriate nutrition services (SC, OTP, SFP) as per CMAM protocol and guidelines- Management of PLWs with acute malnutrition- Provide Food Based Prevention of Malnutrition (FBPM) for 6-23 month children and PLW- Provide emergency blanket supplementary Feeding program for children 6-59 months and PLW during emergencies - Micronutrient supplementation through MNPs (home fortification) for children aged 6-59 months per child- IYCF/E message dissemination- Conduct counselling of Pregnant women and caregivers of children 0-23 months on MYCN - Training of service providers on IYCF related interventions- In addition to training sessions on CMAM and IYCF (including on-job training) service providers will be trained on Information Management training and Nutrition surveillance training.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Some of project's activities that might have impact on environment include: rehabilitation and construction activities, medical and non-medical wastes, use of paper, and cooking/boiling instruments.on mitigation of its environmental impacts APDHWO will follow specific measures during the whole project life-cycle:- During need assessment and design:APDHWO will identify targeted communities needs on prevention and mitigation of communities' practice which impact on environment including waste management practice, cooking materials and other practices, also will identify their needs that will be provided by the project and plan to prevent and mitigate its impact during the design and implementation phases- During design phase:Identified activities include rehabilitation and construction activities that will be responded by using of environmental friendly construction materials as much as possible. Disposal of medical and non-medical wastes will be addressed by providing of quality standard waste management facility considering environmental and protection measures. Use of paper is essential for registration, reporting and monitoring activities, APDHWO will use recycled paper, paper will be used on highest need only and the disposal will be through standard waste management facility, all APDHWO staff including field staff and community workers will be oriented on these measures. Cooking/boiling instruments will be used for cooking demonstration and sterilization of medical equipment, APDHWO will provide refilled gas cylinders and will refill it frequently to prevent use of firewood, communities will be oriented on the negative impact of illegal and destructive logging of trees.- Project will provide enhancement activities including provision of quality standard waste management facility, raising community awareness and provision of environment friendly cooking/boiling instruments

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection measures will be mainstreamed throughout the project life-cycle as following:- During needs assessment phase:All community components (women, girls, boys and men) on their diverse religions, ethnicities, tribes and backgrounds will participate in the needs assessment process with no discrimination and ensure empowerment and participation of the most vulnerable groups including PWDs in the needs assessment. Also the needs assessment process will address the needs of vulnerabilities and address any existed barriers to be considered during the designing of project.- Designing phase:during this phase, protection measures for targeted beneficiaries specially vulnerable groups will be ensure to be addressed throughout the project including but not limited to: types of services to be provided, security measures regarding appropriate time and place of service provision, conflict analysis and actualization of do no harm principle.Planning phase:Risk assessment and risk management to identify and manage any anticipated risk including security risks, protection measures during rehabilitation and construction, fencing the facilities and waste management area, gender sensitive facilities including but not limited to health clinics, gender sensitive latrines, selection criteria of community workers, planning for monitoring activities including appropriate feedback and compliant response mechanism. plan for child safeguarding and protection from sexual exploitation and abuse including training of staff and community workers, planning for implementation in protection oriented sphere e.g. children will not adjusted to be alone with adults, community will be oriented on their rights and how to report if any violation occurred. protection measures for PWD including address their special needs to have meaningful access to services.During Monitoring:Meetings with beneficiaries and vulnerable groups individually and in groups to ensure their satisfaction on services and no harm threatening them. Monitoring activities include as well physical check of protection measures as per project planning
