Project Summary

Women/adolescent girls, widows, divorcees, persons with disabilities, older women, divorced women, and women minority clans face heightened risks of GBV with increased displacements multiple displacements and forced evictions due to flooding, droughts, and armed conflicts.
The purpose of the planned intervention is to strengthen access to comprehensive services for GBV survivors and risk groups through promotion, mitigation, and provision of GBV integrated services. The proposed project will benefit 7680 (5000 women, 2450 girls, 130 boys and 100 Men) in Mogadishu (Kahda and Deynile districts) and Afgoye corridor. The project will create community and service providers’ engagement on GBV prevention and response services for the GBV survivors to increase ease access to timely integrated and friendly services for gender-based survivors affected by the crisis in Kahda, Daynile and Afgoye corridor (health, psychosocial, case management and vocational training skills). The intervention will improve GBV response, prevention and provide integrated and comprehensive access to GBV basic services that will build resilience and restore dignity for GBV survivors in the target locations. This will be done through the following activities:- Developing, disseminating and operationalizing integrated referral pathways for GBV survivors to access CMR, PSS and other related GBV services in two safe home centers and establishment of three GBV one-stop centers in Kahda, Daynile and afgoye corridor, Service mapping of targeted areas to contribute to the update of referral pathways, Mobilize PSS counselors to deliver one on one and group psychosocial support and counseling for traumatized women and girls, Support CMR trained actors to mobilize and deliver rape and other services to GBV survivors • Support the operations of 3 GBV one-stop centers to provide services and case management to GBV survivors, Support the operations of 2 women and girl’s safe spaces to deliver first aid PSS services, conduct referrals and mobilization of services for GBV survivors, Procure and distribute dignity, re-usable sanitary kits and solar lanterns, and other basic materials to GBV survivors including vulnerable women and girls and Support transport for referral services for GBV survivors to GBV services.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash is a flexible resource that allows people to spend money according to their needs and priorities. Cash Transfer Program may be a more efficient allocation of resources for both agency and beneficiary. Distribution costs for Cash Transfer Program are usually lower than those for food or non-food items. Transfers of cash also eliminate the possibility that beneficiaries re-sell distributed items to buy preferred goods and services.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

Cash interventions are an appropriate response for providing resources to people only when the market is functioning. In that case, SCC conducted a market assessment to consider a minimum standard for cash interventions. The market assessment was aimed to determine if the preconditions for cash interventions were met. The organization’s assessment has closely checked the availability of the stocks and prices and we have talked to the different traders if they have adequate stock to meet the demand or whether seasonal issues are impacting stock levels in the market and we came to know whether there is often enough to decide if cash programming was appropriate and whether the markets will be badly impacted by an in-kind distribution of goods. the project will be using mobile e-transfers that will be distributed discreetly and allowing for greater dignity and personal security. The provision of cash or voucher is an appropriate option in this project as it empowers the affected survivors to decide on their own on how to meet their own needs using available local resources and improves their status In emergencies. the method is an appropriate response to the survivors meet their immediate needs for food and non-food items, and to support the recovery of livelihoods. distributing cash can often meet people's immediate needs more quickly and appropriately than the direct distribution of commodities. Cash gives for the beneficiaries' choices and thereby preserves their dignity. Commodity distribution often poses logistical and security problems, and - in the case of food aid - it may disrupt local markets.
