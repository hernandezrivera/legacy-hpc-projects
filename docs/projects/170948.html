Project Summary

Explosive weapons pose one of the most prevalent challenges to security and stabilization in South-Central Somalia. The proliferation of explosive remnants of war (ERW), improvised explosive devices (IED), and mines not only threatens lives in Somalia but also limits access to basic services and livelihoods/economic opportunities for most of the population. As a result, explosive contamination represents a serious impediment to stability, security, and ultimately recovery and development. Furthermore, EO cause loss of life and damage to the property creating a climate of fear within which effective humanitarian operations can be prevented. The presence of EO and unsecured Small Arms and Light weapons SALW poses threats to the lives of IDPs, host communities, and returnees, and acts as a source of instability through providing components to build improvised devices and resources for armed belligerents. On an almost daily basis, disparate insurgent elements terrorize Somali communities through the use of IEDs on city streets, targeting AMISOM security forces as well as Somali civilians, which results in deaths and injuries.
Al Shabaab has reoriented its campaign towards asymmetric warfare, with increased use of IED against the military. Flood-affected communities and IDP are highly vulnerable to EO threat: they can encounter IEDs along the roads when intending to return, be exposed to attacks targeting AMISOM and SNA, but also due to the presence of EO contamination in their areas of origin.
In line with IMAS 12.10, Risk education activities implemented as part of the project will seek to reduce the risk of death and injury EO (mine, ERW, IEDs), by raising awareness and promoting safe behaviour.
Most at risk communities will be identified and reached through life-saving messages tailored to specific groups.
Targeted communities will be better equipped to identify the threat and adopt safe behaviours, such as sharing information among their communities and reporting any suspicious item to the Somali authorities.
Life-saving awareness sessions will be conducted by local partners SOWMAC and JUMAN who operate in the area and are accredited by Somali explosive management authority (SEMA).
To promote the sustainability of the project, MAG will build the capacity of local partners through the training of Community liaison officer (CLOs) from JUMAN and SOWMAC and the continuous monitoring, evaluation and feedback of the activities implemented. This will enable them to continue delivering risk education messages, once the project is completed. In addition, it will strengthen their position as key points to collect reports from the community and consolidate information related with EO contamination for future use of HMA stakeholders and Somali authorities. The effectiveness and scope of the intervention will be greatly improved by local partners with field teams who are familiar with the context. In doing so, MAG will contribute to the 2021 HRP Objectives by: • improving risk mitigation, • providing safe access to services for IDPs, returnees, and other civilians affected by conflict, or natural disasters, • improving the protective environment and intervention supporting durable solutions for IDPs, • capacity development • building communities resilience so they are less prone to famine shocks in future

Mitigation Measures

The scale of the problem is striking: While there has been no comprehensive survey of mine and explosive remnants of war contamination in South Central Somalia due to ongoing instability, limited surveys suggest one in ten communities are directly impacted. The scattered nature of the contamination and diffusion of IDPs throughout the country means the most practical way to mitigate the threat is through the delivery of MRE. Funded mine risk education capacity in Somalia is insufficient to reach current numbers of returnees traveling from Kenya into Somalia and the IDPs moving within the country. Last year MAG supported RE teams serving returnees entering Jubaland and IDPs in Southwest State. However, due to a lack of funding, the RE project was halted. RE teams would need to be deployed to ensure that the returnees and IDPs could receive RE training. Returnees who received targeted RE training tailored to the situation for instance, in Gedo and Afmadow regions will be at lower risk of injury or death, leading to a lower number of incidents, and the reduced requirement to support victim assistance in the future Drought remains the largest contributor to displacement, followed by conflict in Somalia, with a complementary approach required to best support the at-risk communities. IDPs in Somalia – while at risk of lack of access to food, water,, and life-saving services – also find themselves in unfamiliar territory that has been scarred by conflict. IDPs travel with limited knowledge regarding contaminated areas and explosive remnants of war in Somalia (as supported by surveys of returnees and IDPs in South West State by MAG partner organizations). The lack of knowledge puts this community at an increased risk of death and injury from mines and other unexploded ordnance, while IDPs that are conscious of the risks but who lack the understanding to respond adequately find their access to necessary services limited. As part of the Protection Cluster, MAG’s intervention will mitigate the risks posed by mines and unexploded ordnance to vulnerable families.
