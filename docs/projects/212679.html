Project Summary

The project aims to provide lifesaving and life-sustaining coordinated, equitable health services to address the dire need for health services for all age and sex groups; boys, girls, men, and women, with attention to people with special needs; physical disability, mental disorders, and psychosocial problems, without any sexual discrimination, among IDPs and host communities in northern Syria.
SEMA will prioritize the reduction of morbidity and mortality through improving access to quality health care (primary and secondary, specialized services, community health, and risk communication) in 19 health facilities.
SEMA will provide the following services at the primary healthcare level: RH services (antenatal care, post-natal care, family planning, normal deliveries, gynecological consultations, and clinical management of rape), Integrated management for child illness, EPI, communicable and non-communicable diseases, Nutrition, MHPSS, health awareness, and basic laboratory and radiology services.
Services at the secondary healthcare level will include gynecologic surgeries and cesarean sections, trauma management, major surgeries, hospitalization services, blood transfusion, hemodialysis, and ICUs.
This program also includes MHPSS, MH gap intervention, and focused-non-specialized services by PSWs within SEMA health facilities, also at the community levels.
SEMA's planned activities will meet specific needs to enable access for people with disabilities to health services under their specific situation.
SEMA will ensure privacy, equity, and easy access to the services as there will be women's waiting areas within the facilities and available female staff to deal with women and children smoothly.
SEMA will provide quantitative and qualitative reports that include disaggregated information about cases/age, gender, and disability. including reporting to DHIS.
SEMA will ensure following the protocols and guidelines according to WHO's eight pillars. SEMA health facilities will be provided with the required PPEs to keep the frontline staff safe and keep the health facilities running as well. Furthermore, IPC measures and tools will be secured and followed to ensure adherence to the WHO standards and guidelines and prevent the health facility from being a potential infection source.

SEMA’s most remarkable experience is the multi-sectoral integration program modality, as this approach not only proved to be cost-effective instead of having a standalone activity but also addresses the diverse needs of the targeted population. Therefore, SEMA is so keen to integrate health services with mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), protection, WASH, nutrition, immunization, diagnostic services, and specialized care for persons with disability, those with communicable and non-communicable diseases, and children with severe acute malnutrition with medical complications.; and gender-based violence (GBV) survivors in need of first-line support. The supply chain of essential medicines and medical supplies, as well as the availability of functional devices and equipment, will be secured also through this action.

Needs:According to the Health Resources Availability Monitoring System (HeRAMS) 3rd quarter report for 2023, there were 629 health facilities, compared to 546 in 2022, with 435 of them functional. The breakdown of functional health facilities is as follows: 74 (17.0%) hospitals, 198 (45.6%) fixed primary health care centers (PHCs), 71 (16.3%) mobile clinics, 45 (10.3%) specialized care centers, and 47 (10.8%) other health facilities. Out of the total reported health facilities, 435 were functioning, 187 were nonfunctioning, and 7 had no reports. In the North of Syria, 243 (55.8%) of the functioning health facilities were in Idlib governorate, 179 (41.2%) in Aleppo governorate, and 13 (3.0%) in Northeast Syria (Al-Hasakeh and Ar-Raqqa) governorates.None of the functioning health facilities met the WHO benchmarks for health workers (more than 22 health workers per 10,000 population), and none of the communities had community health workers meeting the WHO benchmark (at least 10 community health workers per 10,000 people). Additionally, the benchmark for the number of hospital beds per 10,000 population (inpatients and maternity) was not met in any of the assessed districts except Idleb and Jisr-Ash-Shougur.
