Project Summary

This project is designed to create access to basic education and protection services for the vulnerable children and women affected by crises and emergencies in South Central Regions of Somalia, particularly people living displaced camps and other vulnerable populations living in Banadir region. The target population of this project is 6,000 crisis affected vulnerable children (2,400 girls and 3,600 boys). The main misfortunes affecting the target population are either drought currently evolving, or the ongoing conflicts or floods currently affecting populations living along the Shabelle river or all of the above at times.
The purpose of this project is to create the enabling environment for children and use the school attendance as important factor for the prevention of child protection risks, particularly girls. Therefore, the project will employ interventions such as a) construction/improvement of classrooms and child-friendly spaces, b) provision of teaching and learning and recreational materials, c) provision of emergency teacher incentives, d) provision of school furniture, e) provision of school uniforms, f) provision of school feeding, g) provision of safe drinking water, h) training of teachers on child-friendly pedagogy, i) training of community education committees, j) training of teachers on basic child rights and counseling, k) training of community-based child protection committees, l) hygiene promotion awareness to prevent AWD/Cholera, and m) setting up CtC Clubs in schools. These interventions are envisaged to improve children’s access to education services, lifesaving intervention while providing children and women awareness, prevention and protection from abuse, exploitation and violence; such Gender Based Violence, sexual exploitation, early marriage, child labor and recruitment of children into the armed groups/forces.

Project Activities

Up to 2.1 million people in Somalia are expected to face food consumption gaps, high acute malnutrition, or depletion of assets indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes through to December 2019. Current high levels of food insecurity are driven by drought conditions from late 2018 through mid-May 2019, exacerbated by below-average and erratically distributed rainfall during the 2019 Gu’ season (April-June), which began late and finished early (FSNAU FEWS-NET, 2019). Whilst 2.6 million IDPs, marginalized communities and civilians in conflict-affected areas are living in the most vulnerable circumstances, with limited or no access to quality basic services. These populations require livelihood support to prevent a deterioration of their situation (HNO 2019). According to UNICEF, Somalia has one of the world’s lowest enrolment rates for primary school-aged children; only 30% of children are in school, 40% girls. Girls’ participation in education is consistently lower than boys; due to low availability of sanitation facilities in schools, the lack of female teachers, safety concerns and social norms that favor boys’ education. The South and Central Somalia is the most affected with more than 3 million out of school children, drought, food insecurity, poverty and inequality also hinder efforts to get more children in school (ECW). Approximately 4.9 million school aged children in Somalia, and of these, an estimated three million, or more than 60%, are out of school, the majority in South and Central regions. 4.2 million people in need of humanitarian assistance, an estimated 1.85 million are school aged children requiring assistance, with a focus to increased access to quality education, school retention and sustainability (HNO 2019). The protection crisis in Somalia exposes children to threats, violations and abuses, i.e. sexual and gender-based violence, child recruitment, attacks on civilian areas, infrastructure and forced displacement, remain a pervasive feature of the humanitarian crisis in Somalia. While the Protection needs in Somalia result from the lack of access to services, limited awareness of basic rights, and existing gender discriminatory and harmful socio-cultural practices, disadvantaging marginalized groups such as minorities and persons with disabilities, and affecting women and children disproportionally (Ibid). This project targets 6,000 crisis affected vulnerable children (2,400 girls and 3,600 boys) in Banadir region. The focus of this project is to use schools as an entry point for life saving assistance to vulnerable children and integrating provision of food security, WASH and protection services at the school level. The response will enhance the focus on the protective role of education and integrate child protection activities at school level. This project are based on the strategic approaches of the education response which is to boost the retention of crisis affected school children and increased access to education for IDPs children and children in host communities. Thus, the needs this project will be: a) provision of teaching and learning materials and recreational materials, b) provision of emergency teacher incentives, c) provision of school furniture, d) provision of school uniforms, e) provision of school feeding, f) provision of safe drinking water, g) training of teachers on child-friendly pedagogy, h) training of community education committees, i) training of teachers as CtC mentors and child counselors, j) training of community-based child protection committees, k) hygiene promotion awareness to prevent AWD/Cholera, and l) setting up CtC Clubs in schools. This project envisages improving children’s access to education services and lifesaving while providing protection for children and women, from abuse, exploitation and violence.

Mitigation Measures

Mitigation measures2a. To ensure inclusion we will …1) Incorporate the info on community power dynamics into the M&E activities, to identify those unable to influence decisions, vulnerability, exclusions and any barriers to access services.2) Collaborate with civil society working with persons with disabilities to improve access and service delivery.3) Encourage employment of female teachers and teachers with disability to build a more inclusive environment. 4) Identify/address needs of children facing difficulties to access education e.g. girls, children with disabilities, etc.5) Work with children, teachers, CECs, community leaders to secure children’s equal and safe access to education. 2b. To include and consult women, girls and boys …. we will1) Consult with and understand community needs/ preferences for school location, etc., when designing projects 2) Empower the CECs to play an active role in identifying solutions and promoting ownership, and improving the sustainability of the response. 3) Talk to girls and boys separately about the barriers they face and get information about exclusion and marginalization4) Ensure students, teachers, and CECs are democratic and inclusive, representing all groups within the community.5) Carry out girl-education advocacy at community leader’s level and ensure adherence to girl’s education. 3a. To prevent protection concerns … we will1) Engage the local leadership (traditional and political) to advocate for protection of the IDPs2) Consult both the displaced and the host community about education and protection needs3) Assess if humanitarian assistance could exacerbate tension between the two communities. 4) Avoid community tensions, by providing host community access to education services, if need be. 5) Engage CECs, Teachers and Community leaders as watchdogs and first line of a community-based protection and prevention mechanism. 6) Establish reporting mechanisms on protection incidence to ensure timely reporting and adequate response. 3b. To prevents exposure of women and girls to sexual violence … we will1) Talk to girls and boys separately about risks facing children, particularly the violations, exploitations and abuse affecting women and girls.2) Reinforce CECs, Teachers, parents and Community leaders as watchdogs and first line of a community-based protection and prevention mechanism. 3) Establish reporting mechanisms on protection incidences to adequately respond to CP and GBV, incidences4) Establish feedback box schools and a hotline number and respond swiftly to all complaints5) Engage education authorities to develop school rules, codes of conduct and corrective / punishment measures. 3c. To prevent exposure to sexual violence … we will1) Consider the use of underground facilities or mobile education services, while considering the distances children travel to school and their modes of transport. 2) Consider ways to reduce threats, including changing the location, introducing accompaniment, etc.3) Ensure class timing takes place in daylight hours giving children sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. 4) Situate play areas for children in a clearly visible and safe locations5) Ask children directly about the safety of their travel to and from school and their presence at school. 3d. To avoid assistance affecting peaceful coexistence … we will1) Provide host community with access to education when experiencing shortages of basic education services.2) Consult displaced and host communities about education needs and assess if assistance could exacerbate tension or conflict between the two communities. 3) Engage the local authority in all discussions and provide leadership role to avoid tensions.4) Provide shared responsibility to both communities including employments, etc.5) Establish M&E and feedback systems to understand community relations/possible tensions so as to respond to.

Please provide further explanation

The project contributes to the durable solutions in many ways 1)Zamzam Foundation (ZF) is willing to use permanent learning centers (PLSs) rather than TLSs. The permanent school structures were established earlier this year in many parts of south central Somalia. The reason for using PLSs because they are eviction proof, safe and equipped with the brick wall fences, large gates, gender sensitive toilets and a separate toilet for teachers. Most of the class rooms have furniture, though for some schools we may require to provide desks. The ZF model of PLSs provides sustainable IDP schools, contributes to the durable solutions, and is a model that can be replicated by other organizations.2)The project increases community awareness on areas of education, projection and community sustainability/ownership of schools projects. It promotes child protection and other protection risks facing women and children. It also increases community awareness about the importance of education and creates discussion about community role and responsibilities in the school ownership. 3)It ensures participation and enables children to have a say in their future, their schools activities, learning outcomes and rights to protection against violence, exploitation and abuse.4)It engages the local leaders at regional and district level to secure acceptable quota for women and girls, the marginalized people, and persons with disabilities. It promotes inclusiveness, equity and all.5)It will strive to raise girls’ enrollment in schools while capitalizing on the school feeding program, the clean water and the gender segregated latrines lockable from inside as protective measures and also a way to save money for the family if their children are sent to schools. It engages the CECs and community leaders to influence parents.6)It contributes to the revival of the education sector and boosts children’s enrollment in schools though the training of teachers while enabling the community to own, sustain and manage their schools. Besides, the current teacher’s incentive reduces teacher absenteeism and enables children get quality education.7)It increases the number of teacher counselors in each school, and contributes to the management and the healing of children with psychos-social trauma, student’s discipline and provide psychosocial support for all learners though the introduction of the CtC initiative in schools.8)It creates an enabling environment for children by organizing them to form CtC clubs and training them on CLDRR. The skills gained through CLDRR will enable children to get their voice heard at school, identify risks affecting children at school, at community and at home, understand GBV risks and be change agents in child rights, child protection and GBV prevention. 9)It trains and engages community-based child protection committees (CBCPC), to create awareness on child right, child protection and GBV risks, while the provide info on the existing referral pathways and linkages to service providers. The increased awareness on CP will prevent child abuse, exploitation, and violence and bring about a generation that upholds a violence free schools and homes. 10)It provides dignity kits to GBV survivors and women vulnerable to GBV; so as to boost the morale of the GBV survivors and prevent possible GBV risk affecting females living in IDP camps or in rural areas. Besides, it will also enlighten women and the community at large on the mechanisms for prevention and response to GBV cases, referral pathways and linkages services providers.11)It increases children’s hygiene and sanitation skills and contributes to the prevention of AWD/Cholera. Engaging children as community hygiene promoters contributes to the long-term prevention diseases, particularly the periodic outbreak of AWD/Cholera.
