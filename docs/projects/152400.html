Project Summary


This project aims at enabling communication with communities and camp coordination/ management in camp like settings in Leer County. If funded the project will contribute significantly to better camp coordination and camp management in South Sudan by fostering better communication in terms of information gathering and sharing with humanitarian partners, enhanced feedback mechanisms through established complaint desks and more knowledgeable community leaders on humanitarian response and CCCM in Leer County. The project will be implemented with a period of 12 months with targeted beneficiaries of 7000 and budget of $299,989.

Needs

First and foremost, it is imperative to note that nobody wants to create more POCs. It is undisputed that international legal frameworks and protocols affirm that affected civilians must continue to stay in camps or camp-like settings until such a time when conditions are suitable for them to resettle in alternative locations or return to areas of origin. South Sudan being a signatory to some of these conventions is not exceptional. Moreover, some of the most at-risk IDP populations throughout South Sudan include communities living outside camps and or sites with little or no direct support from humanitarian agencies. The context of Leer and Mayendit is unique and disturbing with its panacea partly pivoted on strengthened camp coordination and Camp management. It is unique and disturbing because in late April 2018, armed combatants attacked villages committing crimes against international law as well as breaching serious human rights violations, (Amnesty International, 2018). During these attacks, the combatants deliberately targeted and killed civilians, including women, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities. While many civilians were killed by gun fire, others were burned alive in their homes, hung from trees and rafters, or run over with armored vehicles. Civilians were also hunted down after fleeing into nearby wetlands, or rivers, as soldiers shot indiscriminately into areas where they were hiding and carried out attacks on islands where civilians had sought refuge. During these operations, these combatants systematically abducted and raped women and girls.As a result of these heinous acts, 23,586 persons were internally displaced in Mayendit and 13,200 were displaced in Leer. It is believed three times the number recorded were still hiding in the bush for fear of attacks. Out of the 13,300 displaced persons, 4700 sought for shelter in the TPA. Currently according to the latest head count, the population in the TPA stands at 1379 persons. In Mayendit there are 658 persons with majority of the IDPs returning to their homes or places of origin or seeking alternatives shelters following relative calmness tranquility at the beginning of August 2018 to date. If funded, this project will propel CCCM vision that aspires to ensure that All people affected by displacement in camps and camp-like settings in South Sudan live in dignity; have their rights respected; and access participatory, equitable and quality services that build upon their resilience through all phases of their displacement until a durable solution is achieved through promotion of equal access and needs based assistance to improve quality of services for populations affected by displacement; expansion CCCM services to new and unreached populations; improvement of engagement with and be responsible to vulnerable populations with priorities for addressing protection gaps and ensure local ownership of site management through relevant and meaningful community participation.

Activities

1. Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM): IDP populations utilize CFMs in PoC and displacement sites, which will guide service providers in integrating systems that ensure effective and timely response. Targeting 1000 women, 200 girls, 800 men and 500 boys within 12 months in Leer and Mayendit. Two more complaint desks will be established in Mayendit. It will also involve two complaint desks in Mayendit.2. Population Registration: New arrivals into PoC sites will be registered with priority activities aligning with demographic tracking systems and improving the quality of data collected. This information will be also utilized to advocate to local authorities and the humanitarian community on needs and population flows in the sites. Total of 600 women, 400 girls, 200 boys and 500 men targeted in new registrations in Leer and Mayendit.3. Site profiling: Will be regularly updated and information will be used to advocate to donors, the wider humanitarian community and allow for identification of gaps in a coordinated and rationalized manner. 1 Profiling done in Leer and Mayendit every quarter and information shared.4. Intentions/Perceptions Surveys: HR will ensure intentions of IDPs are established to guide programming at PoC sites, collective centers and spontaneous settlements, as well as inform dialogue on protection led returns and solutions. 1 survey done in Leer and Mayendit every quarter and information shared. 5. Customized common services: HR will advocate to service providers to adapt to the extent possible, all infrastructure of common services such as latrines, roads and bridges for the benefit of Persons with Specific Needs. 1 disability/accessibility audit done in Leer and Mayendit every quarter and information shared. 6. Monitoring and quality assessment of services, using Camp Management Agency monitoring processes in TPA and community based monitoring mechanisms in collective centres /other camp like settings, to identify needs and gaps, hence clear communications on service provision between all stakeholders. It will involve Indicator monitoring; House to House Service Monitoring and Rationalization of service providers. 7. Training of the community on core components of humanitarian response and CCCM. 4 Trainings done in Leer and Mayendit targeting 300 women and 150 men on leadership, gbv, protection and human rights.8. Building capacity of site committees/ community leaders to better manage the TPA. The training will target block leaders, protection committees, women groups and site official in Leer and Mayendit with a total of 150 women and 200 men being targeted in 12 months.9. Conducting outreach activities aimed at better long term camp management and coordination in the TPA. These will involve 2 monthly awareness sessions in Leer and Mayendit with key messages around peace and reconciliation, peaceful coexistence, GBV, governance, human rights and protection. This activity will target 2500 women, 1000 girls, 500 boys and 1000 men. The activity will use public address systems, banners, organized competitions (cultural), banners, T shirts etc with key messages to strengthen community participation and cohesion.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes. The project location has witnessed in the recent past serious Social upheavals/ political instability. This has been due to clashes/Inter-ethnic clashes/Clashes between returnees and resident community members. All these have posed serious protection challenges in these areas. Hope Restoration as the leading local agency in general protection continues to provide life saving services to the PiN/ PSN in these locations. However, with the signing of peace agreement, there s faint possibility that 2019 may have low clashes/ violence witnessed in these areas. Women and girls are at high risks should the status quo remain. Persons wit disabilities especially the physically challenged who may not be able to run away during the violence's are at eminent risk should the events of April/May 2018 resurface in 2019. Rape in these areas has been rampant especially for young girls/ children. Assuming that the process comes to fruition all these will come to pass.
