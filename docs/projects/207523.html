Project Summary

Since 2017, the Non-State Armed Groups (NSAG) had demanded the closure of schools. They threatened or burnt down establishments that have remained opened. As a consequence, as of December 2022, 54% (3518 out of 6,515) (Ref: Cameroon Education Strategy 023-2025) schools are closed, putting 92.517% (955700 out of estimated 1,033,000) children out of schools and 73% (21,128 out of 28,866) teachers not reporting to work owing to the life-taking threat posted on them and children as well as attacks on education facilities.
Schools have been shut down in most of the difficult and hard to reach villages in the Anglophone Regions for the past five years. Parents who had means displaced their children from these villages to attend schools in urban areas in the English speaking Regions where schools were ongoing while others moved to French Speaking Regions. The very vulnerable parents and children who lacked the means to seek education out of the very difficult and hard to reach crisis zones have been out of schools for six academic years, this has led to child recruitment into arm groups, early marriages, early and unwanted pregnancies, drug abuse and high level of juvenile delinquency.
An estimated 1,033,000 school aged children have been affected by the crisis. They were denied the opportunity to learn and develop themselves. As Schools officially resumed for the 2023/2024 academic year, an improvement in the number of schools that resumed as compared to last year has been registered. However, the doors of majority of public schools in some hard to reach areas in the NWR of Cameroon are still closed. As a result, parents and communities have organized learning centers for their children from nursery, primary and secondary levels to enable them enjoy their fundamental right to education while other have sent their children to private schools though facing a lot of difficulties responding to the tuition fee..
According to the analysis of the MSNA Round 9 for the NW and SW regions of Cameroon the number of schools in these Divisions that opened their doors is far below the number of schools available in these divisions (Bui total number of schools 636, operational 56 giving 9%, Ngoketunjia the expected number of schools is 250, operational 20 giving 8%, Momo expected number of schools is 255 operational 19 giving 7%, Menchum expected number of schools 216 operational 89 giving 41%)
Due to the extremely high risk operational environment in the North West Region, for which education is used as political weapon in the conflict, some communities have developed strategies to grant children access to education through learning centers while other sent their children to the closest mission school.
• The project seek to improve access to education by ensuring enrollment and retention of 4800-children in 13 learning centers within 5 Divisions for 2290boys and 2510 girls (3-18years), in the NW(Bui, Boyo, Ngoketunjia, Momo, and Menchum) Region of Cameroon for 12 months in formal and non-formal learning centers.
The specific objectives
• Provide school learning kits to 130 teachers and 4800 children (3-18 years) in 13 learning centers within 5 Divisions in the NW (Bui,Boyo, Ngoketunjia, Momo, and Menchum) region of Cameroon
• Assist 4300 children (3-18 years) with school feeding programs in 13 learning centers within 5 Divisions in the NW (Bui,Boyo, Ngoketunjia, Momo, and Menchum) for 9 months.
• Train 26 Teachers and education Personnel in 13 learning centers within 5 Divisions in the NWR (Bui, Boyo, Ngoketunjia, Momo, and Menchum) on psychosocial support to offer psychosocial assistance in their learning spaces. These facilitators will in turn provide psychical to 4800-children (3600-girls and 1200-boys).
• Actively engage Community participation through creation of feedback committees, hotlines, and complaint and feedback mechanism for 9 months.
• To ensure those 321 girls, 217 boys and 120 adolescence affected by crisis have access

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Access to education remains a challenge with the ongoing Anglophone crises. Since 2017, the crisis has a substantial social and humanitarian impact on children and education in the NWSW. The conflict has also had repercussions for the education. The whole system has failed in providing access to inclusive learning. An estimated 1,033,000 school aged children have been affected by the crisis. They were denied the opportunity to learn and develop themselves, failing in achieving Sustainable Development Goal (SDG), goal 4: ensure inclusive and equitable quality education and promote lifelong learning opportunities for all. The current system is leaving more than 955,700 children behind due to the extremely high risk operational environment in both Regions, for which education is used as political instrument in the conflict; some communities have developed strategies to grant children access to education through learning centers. The creation of learning centers in hard to reach villages in crisis affected Divisions in the NW (Bui, Boyo, Ngoketunjia, Momo, and Menchum) has offered opportunity to many vulnerable children to have access to education and support affected population to meet their basic need of education. Many children have also dropped out of school, some of which are involved into moral delinquency, early marriages, prostitution, and unwanted pregnancies and are willing to either learn a trade or get involved into businesses. The main activities of this project will be • To provide school learning kits to 130 teachers and 4300 children (3-18 years) in 13 learning centers within 5 Divisions in the NWR school learning kits (Chalk, Chats, text books) for teachers, (exercise books, pens, pencils, rulers, slates, math’s sets, calculators) for children - Transportation of learning kids to learning centers - Distribution of learning kits in learning centers. - Purchase of face masks, buckets, soap and hand sanitizers for 13 learning centers - To assist 2650 children (3-18 years) with school feeding programs in 9 learning centers - Purchase of snacks (biscuits) for 9 learning centers - Transportation/ distribution of snacks (biscuits) to 13 learning centers - • To train 130 Teachers and education Personnel in 13 learning centers on psychosocial training. - Organize one day psychosocial training in each Region - Feed 130 teachers and other education personnel during training - Payment of transport allowances to 130 teachers and other education personnel - To create 13 feedback committees, to manage complaint - Creation of feedback committees - Training of feedback members on how to receive and treat complains, provision of scholarships to 945-youths , 5-18years (600-females and 345-males ) that are unable register in formal schools talk less of paying tuition fee • 4300 emergency affected children and youth (3-18 yrs. old) attend learning spaces in affected • 13 learning spaces provided with learning materials in the targeted locations. • 13 learning spaces provided with education kits in the targeted location • 130 teachers/other educational personnel in affected areas trained in psycho-social support. • 500 children (3-5yrs old) taught by teachers trained in psychosocial support services • 4300 children (5-18 yrs old) taught by teachers trained in psycho-social support services.658 girls, boys and adolescence (5-30years) affected by the crisis have access to radio Education programmes and digital learning platforms.
