Project Summary

This project is aimed at supporting the response to COVID-19 pandemic in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe states. The COVID-19 pandemic broke out in China and declared a pandemic in January 2020. It is caused by the 2019-nCOV, a new type of coronavirus that started in China in December 2019. This virus has infected over three million persons worldwide and recorded over two hundred thousand deaths with an estimated global case fatality rate (CFR) of 6.95 percent as at April 28, 2020. The first case in Nigeria was recorded on February 27, 2020, with a total of 1,532 cases and 44 deaths, a CFR of 2.87 percent at April 28, 2020. The index case in the conflict affected BAY states was recorded on April 18, 2020, with a quick rise in the number of cases to 47 cases and 7 deaths recorded with a CFR of 14.89 percent in Borno as at April 28, 2020.
The current pandemic presents a unique set of challenges to the vulnerable populations in the conflict affected North-East Nigeria and will further compound the already challenged and weak health system and its ability to respond to the needs of the population. The effects could be dire, particularly to those displaced and/or living in camps and camp-like settings who are often faced with specific challenges and vulnerabilities with an already suboptimal functioning health service, poor and inadequate shelter and poor hygiene conditions. These populations are often exposed to stigmatization, may face challenges accessing health services and the COVID-19 outbreak threatens to exacerbate these challenges and thereby disrupting protection, human rights and public health services. The BAY states also suffer from a greatly debilitated health system which is currently being strengthened through close collaboration between humanitarian actors and government with significant gains over the past two years. It is therefore critical that the COVID-19 outbreak readiness and response strategies, plan and operations extend to all persons regardless of status and ensures inclusiveness as well as protecting and sustaining the progresses made in health systems strengthening (HSS) through a multisectoral approach.
UNICEF in partnership with state government agencies (SPHCDAs and SPHMBs) and the State Ministries of Health of the BAY states, will support the COVID-19 outbreak response through the procurement and distribution of essential commodities such as PPEs and drugs required for case management and infection prevention and control (IPC). UNICEF will also ensure that sustained integrated PHC services continue to be available to the vulnerable populations especially in IDP camps as they are still exposed to other morbidity and mortality causing conditions which are only going to be worsened by the pandemic.
The objectives of this interventions are
• To mitigate the risk and contain the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic and decrease morbidity and mortality in Northeast Nigeria.
• Ensure continuous and sustained provision of basic integrated PHC services in IDP camps and to underserved populations during the COVID-19 outbreak response.

1 - Needs

In its eleventh year, the armed conflict in the Nigeria’s North-East has significantly disrupted the health services delivery in the BAY states and as a result, the few existing health facilities are currently overwhelmed. While all humanitarian efforts have been deployed to ensure that challenges such as lack or damaged health facilities, insufficient supplies and equipment, inadequate and maldistribution of human resource for health are effectively tackled, overcrowded living conditions in IDP camps with limited access to safe drinking water and poor sanitation significantly increases the risks of preventable disease outbreaks like Cholera, Lassa fever etc.As per the HNO, 7.9 million people are expected to need humanitarian assistance in 2020 of whom the humanitarian partners plan to target 5.9 million of the most vulnerable people. The unforeseen shock, such as the COVID-19 pandemic, is likely to impact current projections. The capacity of the health system to address the possible impact of the pandemic, remains limited and will be stretched with this pandemic.The BAY states are home to a lot of vulnerable persons the majority of whom are women, children and the elderly residing in camps and camp-like settings. Overcrowded settlements and poor nutritional status of these individuals predisposes them to decreased immunity levels with increased incidences of clinical condition such as severe acute malnutrition, measles, malaria, etc. As such, it is expected that a higher percentage of these vulnerable populations face a higher risk of morbidity and mortality when exposed to the 2019-nCOV. It therefore becomes imperative that additional funding is provided to effectively respond to the COVID-19 outbreak because as the number of cases increases, there will be a greater demand for resources to minimize the effects of the viral disease which has brought untold hardships to other locations with better preparedness and response plans and resources.Preparedness activities in the states have since commenced in line with the recommendations of WHO and National Centre for Disease Control (NCDC). Humanitarian partners in collaboration with government MDAs have identified the pillars needed for an adequate response however the following challenges exist:1. Weak community-based surveillance systems in some locations2. Risk of high transmission in overcrowded IDP camps and settlements such as found in Monguno, MMC, Dikwa3. Unavailability of facilities for quarantine and isolation of IDPs in IDP camps or IDP camp-like settings.4. High risk of outbreaks other than COVID-19 due to seasonal changes (rainy season approaching), poor WaSH practices and infrastructure.5. Existing high disease burden and higher infection-to-case ratios and progression to severe disease due to the virus’ interaction with highly prevalent co-morbidities, including different NCDs, measles, malaria, HIV/AIDS, malnutrition and food insecurity issues 6. Higher infection-to-case ratios and progression to severe disease due to the virus’ interaction with highly prevalent co-morbidities, including non-communicable diseases 7. Weak response capacity on COVID-19 Case Management support from few international humanitarian agencies with over stretched capacity and lack of adequate resources8. Weak screening mechanisms at PoE, especially with inadequate or no screening system in place at international borders. 9. Poorly equipped Isolation/treatment Centre and functional triage wards for COVID-19 especially in LGAs, needs for ventilators, oxygen concentrators, respirators etc. 10. Weak health system as more than 40 percent of health facilities across the three states are non-functional or partially functional.11. Risk of higher transmissibility due to larger household sizes, intense social mixing between the young and elderly, inadequate water and sanitation, and specific cultural and faith practices, mass prayer gatherings, large weddings and funerals.

2 - Activities

To adequately respond to the challenges mentioned above, UNICEF in collaboration with our implementing partners will participate in the following pillars of COVID-19 response: Coordination, Risk Communication, Case management and Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) as well as logistics and supplies.UNICEF will leverage on her position as a major stakeholder to ensure that coordination meetings continue to happen and participate actively in the decision making processes ensuring that all decisions arrived at are of high technical value, evidence based and in line with the core principles of fundamental human rights and protection of the affected populations. In the case management and IPC pillars UNICEF will support in the procurement and distribution of PPEs, drugs and essential supplies including medical equipment for the isolation centers, support logistics for ambulances in isolation centers. UNICEF will also support the training of health workers on basic protection protocols in health facilities.In the risk communication pillar, UNICEF will support the production and dissemination of information, education and communication materials, TV and radio jingles on COVID-19 as well as community engagement activities with the intention of improving knowledge, influencing attitudes and promoting hygienic practices and contact tracing as well as surveillance.UNICEF will also ensure that integrated PHC services continue to be available in supported health facilities across the BAY states and maintain other Health System Strengthening (HSS) activities in accordance with public health guidelines. This will ensure that, while all measures are taken to control the COVID-19 outbreak, key services such as MNCH services, RI services etc. remain available especially in IDP camps and other underserved populations.
