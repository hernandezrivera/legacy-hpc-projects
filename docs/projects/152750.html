Project Summary

The proposed project aims to reduce the morbidity and mortality of young children and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) due to malnutrition by increasing access to Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) services and nutrition education (MIYCN/IYCF-E).
The onsite nutrition education to mothers and caregivers on use of treatment nutrition supplies and promoting dietary practices will equip them with knowledge and skills to prevent malnutrition and make healthy food choices within their context.
Currently WRSS has oversight of 11 nutrition sites in Koch County, 2 sites in Bentiu PoC and 6 nutrition sites in Fangak County. This project seeks to support outpatient therapeutic feeding program (U5-OTP), Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP – U5 MAM and PLW) and Mother, Infant, and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) programming in these 19 sites by including community outreach and mobile interventions. The project will also increase the capacity of OTP/TSFP sites through improved infrastructure and maintain the number of Stabilization Centers in Koch and Fangak Counties.
Program integration shall be ensured by establishing kitchen gardening in selected nutrition sites, PLWs beneficiaries linked to receive vegetable kits where possible and they will also benefit from cooking demonstrations. Proper sanitation facilities (latrines), safe drinking water, handwashing stations and ECDE materials shall be mainstreamed across nutrition sites. SAM children will be tested for malaria and other illness (URTI, UTI) and referred/linked to adjacent PHCC/Us for comprehensive treatment. The program shall be sensitive to the needs and safety of women, boys and girls by creating a friendly and sanitary atmosphere.
WRSS will apply an adaptive strategy for accessing and serving the affected populations ranging from static sites to local outreaches to accompanying RRMs and ensuring supervised services continue thereafter. As possible and necessary, nutrition centers damaged during the conflict will be rehabilitated so that services can be normalized.

Needs

With this project, WRSS will address nutritional needs of host communities, IDPs, returnees and other affected women, girls and boys in Koch County, Bentiu PoC and Fangak County. In these areas, national and program assessments have shown deteriorating conditions arising from pockets of malnutrition for both women and children under 5 years. The targeted areas are staggering between “critical” GAM (WHZ) 15.0 – 29.9%) and ‘Serious’ GAM (WHZ) 10.0-14.9%) levels of acute malnutrition according the latest IPC key findings (September 2018 – March 2019). Fangak is painted red (Phase 4) indicating emergency classification. A recent IRRM mission in two locations in Koch County between 15th and 21st September 2018 by UNICEF and partners found proxy GAM of 21.8% in one of the 2 locations where mass MUAC screening was conducted. A snapshot of the current program data shows is showing an upward trend on case admissions and treatment. Currently, in Koch we have a total of 17 SC admissions, 127 OTP admissions and 442 U5 TSFP and 176 PLW TSFP admissions. In Fangak, we currently have 165 U5 OTP, 203 U5 TSFP and 209 PLW in the program. On average, we have continued to receive between 5-10 U5 SAM cases, 10 – 20 U5 MAM and around 15 malnourished PLWs every week in both Fangak and Koch. WRSS proposes to continue putting Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) at the forefront as a life-saving treatment for SAM and MAM children under five years and malnourished pregnant and lactating women (PLW) with children less than 6 months. An educative and preventive component, MIYCN/IYCF will be continued to ensure proper utilization of nutrition supplies and mothers/caregivers are empowered with skill and knowledge for better feeding practices. This is critical to ensure a smooth transition from early initiation of breastfeeding (colostrum feeding), exclusive breastfeeding and complementary feeding and eliminating contextual barriers. The program will identify direct beneficiaries through active case finding in the community and referrals to nutrition centers. CNVs and staff working in the community will screen children under five years and PLW for signs of malnutrition and refer those meeting criteria. Referrals shall also be done between PHCC/Us for detected cases. Treatment at the centres will follow CMAM guidelines. Continued community mobilization efforts has made new and operational nutrition centers (OTP/TSFP) known to community members. The project will ensure defaulter tracing so as to maximize gains. Beneficiaries will be inked to indicate their participation in the program and avoid duplication. The project will deworm children under five enrolled in the feeding program. WRSS will provide Out-patient Therapeutic Care (OTP) to severely malnourished children without complications and Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP) in all nutrition sites both PHCC/U and standalone sites as well as through mobile services. The nutrition staff and volunteers will conduct the screening and monitoring activities with beneficiaries, and will distribute the RUTF and CSB++. If simple complications are diagnosed, the beneficiary will be referred to the nearest PHCC/SC. Beneficiaries already enrolled in the OTP/TSFP program will be screened for complications by nutrition staff and volunteers during each visit; if diagnosed with a complication, the beneficiary will be referred to the PHCC/SC for treatment. OTP/TSFP discharge criteria for this project will follow CMAM guidelines.

Activities

This project will seek to address three of the four front-line responses prioritized by the Nutrition Cluster for 2019. For life saving and contribution toward reducing prevalence of acute malnutrition and death rates among under-five children, pregnant and lactating women. The following activities shall be undertaken.• Operation of Stabilisation Centres (SC), OTP and TSFP Sites shall be maintained.• Screening will be done both at the community and at the nutrition sites on children under 5 and PLWs to identify signs of malnutrition. • Appropriate referrals shall be made to nutrition sites and PHCC/U for treatment. With this the project target treatment SAM 2200 SC/OTP TSFP, 4200 U5 MAM TSFP and 2000 PLW TSFP. • Stabilization centres (SC) will target children under five with severe acute malnutrition with medical complications. The criteria for admission will include: Bilateral pitting Oedema + or ++ or MUAC <115mm WFH<-3Z-score with a medical complication. - OTP criteria of admission (Bilateral pitting oedema + and ++ or MUAC <115mm or WFH<-3Z-score with appetite and clinically alert) will be admitted in OTP.• TSFP will target boys and girls aged 0-59 months, pregnant and lactating women and other vulnerable groups. TSFP under-five criteria; MUAC >115mm<125mm or WFH>-3Z-score and <-2 z-score and appetite/clinically well.• PLW will be admitted with MUAC <23mm during third trimester and <23mm lactating women with infants < 6 months and receive treatment in the program.• Patients discharged as “non-cured” will be visited at their current place of residence to investigate compliance with the course of RUTF and other factors (hygiene practices, etc.). These patients will be referred to a health facility for evaluation. • Upon discharge, patients will be referred to the supplementary feeding program. The project will track children transferred between program components (PHCC, OTP and SFP).• Beneficiary education shall be provided especially for mothers and caregivers of children under 5 in the program on proper use of nutrition supplies.• CNVs will do defaulter tracing and home regular home visits to ensure adherence and reminders on next dates for site visits.• Health and nutrition staff will be trained on CMAM and MIYCN/IYCF-E Guidelines to have better understanding and skill as they work.• WR will work with mother to mother support groups in the community to identify and counsel mothers and primary caregivers of children 0-23 months on MIYCN/IYCF messages. The project is will provide counselling to about 800 men and 6,400 women on IYCF practices. • WRSS will integrate nutrition activities with Health, FSL and Education services to enhance resilience of the beneficiaries. Latrines, safe drinking water, latrine and WASH NFI kits specifically soap will be provided to caregivers of children 0-23 months or at hand washing stations at the nutrition sites. PLWs will be encouraged to establish their own kitchen gardens at home. ECDE will be established at nutrition sites and equipped with ECD kits for learning and play. Children with SAM aged 0/6-59 months will be tested for malaria and test positives for malaria referred for treatment in PHCC/U. • 2 SMART Surveys shall be conducted.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Mother to mother support groups and Community nutrition volunteers (CNVs) are the grassroots information agents in the community. CNVs and Mother to mother support group live in the community, speak the same language and are well known in their neighborhood. They are trained on MIYCN/IYCF messaging, they move house to house wearing branded T-shirts with pictorial description of best feeding practices. These appeals to women, girls and boys and inspires information sharing on printed pictures and messages in vernacular. They are trained on screening to identify signs of malnutrition and to do referrals to the nutrition centers. They monitor and reinforce adherence to proper use nutrition suppliers for admitted cases in the community, they remind them of their next visits to the centers and help with defaulter tracing.Direct beneficiaries, PLWs, mothers and caregivers of children under 5 years are also educated at the nutrition centers on how to use nutrition supplies and they receive information on best nutritional practices, hygienic food handling and food preparation, and are encouraged to establish their own kitchen gardens. WR have used and will continue using participatory approaches encouraging community members to participate in activity implementation. Both at the nutrition centers and community, MIYCN messaging which targets women, girls and boys is relayed.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

In communicating nutrition messages, WR addresses barriers to proper infant and young child feeding that are context specific. Mothers cultural believes that hinder proper mother, infant and young child feeding for example, believes around colostrum feeding and exclusive breastfeeding messages are addressed to mothers of children 0-6 months immediately when they give birth. They get this message on delivery at the health facility, PHCC/U through trained birth attendants and those delivering at home are reached by a network of mother to mother support groups. Messages of better feeding for PLWs and children 6-59 months is also reinforced. Barriers around complementary feeding and continued breastfeeding to 1 or 2 years are addressed. With an aim to improve dietary diversity, beneficiaries are encouraged to use locally available and seasonal foods like beans, vitamin A-rich fruits green leafy vegetables and peanut butters that are locally grown and prepared. Caregivers are also taken through cooking demonstrations and hygienic food handling practices. They get to see and understand frequency of feeding, the amount and thickness of food given to children 6-23 months. PLWs are also encouraged to eat well and desist from overworking themselves. When members of mother to mother support group visit homes, they also get to talk to fathers on how PLWs can be assisted and the involvement of men in ensuring better maternal, infant and young child feeding. These nutrition education messages are barrier‐specific and are delivered directly to mothers by mother to mother support group members and CNVs.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

WR South Sudan’s ability to achieve our aims, in complex and insecure environments like theGreater Upper Nile Region, is linked to our service quality and commitment to ethics. Given the potential for desperation among the beneficiaries, and the power dynamic of those bringing and distributing relief items over the beneficiaries, World Relief (WR) will take a firm, zero tolerance stance towards any staff or volunteers taking advantage of beneficiaries financially, sexually, or physically, or in any way attempting to divert resources or use the system for their personal gain in addition to their wages. The Country Director and Human Resource Manager educate all staff on the Code of Conduct content, which includes the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), which each staff member receives and signs upon acceptance of employment. A copy of the signed Code of Conduct stays with the employee and another copy is kept on record. Already employed staff benefit from a series of HR trainings organized annually to deepen understanding on a number of important HR and protection issues. Line managers explain the content if an employee, beneficiary or local authority have any questions or issues regarding the Code of Conduct or PSEA. In order to manage cases of a breach of conduct if they arise, World Relief will identify protection Focal Points at the implementation site who will receive complaints from beneficiaries or the community. Breaches of the code of conduct will be referred immediately to senior management in Juba (CD and HR Manager) for action. In order to ensure beneficiaries and local communities are able to report complaints, a complaint mechanism will be developed and disseminated through community volunteers in the project.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

With years of static presence in the target locations and existing long-term programming, World Relief in a unique position to implement the proposed project because of the resulting relationships with the communities, beneficiaries, and volunteers. Interactions and dialogues with local staff concerning similar past projects and the needs of their communities have helped to shape the proposed project. Local staff, as members of the community, have unique insight to help shape humanitarian programming. WR's recruitment strategy that prioritizes local staff first and then other national staff and building their capacity has resulted in 97% of employees being nationals, even in senior positions, such as Program Director. WR Staff regularly interact with beneficiaries through normal programming activities in these locations. Likewise, beneficiaries are regularly consulted through monitoring and evaluation activities in existing programming, where they provide their opinions and feedback as well as expressing their needs and those of the larger community. These formal consultations have taken the form of discussion groups, interviews, and surveys, in addition to informal consultations. This feedback has been used in planning the proposed project.Feedback is also regularly obtained through consultation with project volunteers. WR has an extensive network of volunteers in each location, such as Community Nutrition Volunteers, Lead Mothers/Fathers, Community Health Promoters, Water Use Committee Members, Parent Teacher Association Members, and/or Lead Farmers. As community members with specific commitments to humanitarian activities, these volunteers are able to provide learning that is used to tailor the project to the needs of their communities.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

WRSS will develop a feedback mechanism for beneficiaries. Comment boxes be made available in all nutrition sites to allow beneficiaries to provide feedback directly to WRSS. Additionally, CNVs will be able to receive feedback from participants during Home Visits in settings where participants feel comfortable sharing their comments. There shall be regular field visits, the project ensures that unannounced spot checks on different nutrition sites is done. Registers will be checked and discussions held with direct and indirect beneficiaries. Where there are complains or grievances more investigation into the matter will be done with an aim of ensuring transparency on how commodities and resources are distributed and utilized. Discussions will be held with CNVs and Mother to mother support groups on feedback, complains and responses they are getting as they work with the beneficiaries within the community. These are important matters for discussions to gage perception. Views from the beneficiaries and other community members present at the spot during the visit are also collected to gain insight on how the project is implemented.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Participatory approaches shall be adopted to ensure that the community is involved in program monitoring. Discussion with community leaders, men and women will be held to help identify appropriate locations for new nutrition sites to ensure proper coverage. They will be sensitized on project ownership and will monitor and safeguard nutritional commodities getting to their area as they know such is for the treatment of their children. Thus, community members will provide supportive supervision to outreach staff and CNVs to ensure program activities are implemented in the community. They will be able to quantify what, when, where, how nutritional services are done and who has been reached. Where communal work is involved like kitchen gardening, ECDE establishment, community engagement will be important in ensuring that knowledge and skills passed are utilized.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

WRSS has established two layers of community engagement in the project proposed locations. The first layer of engagement with the community is through community level volunteers working in nutrition CNVs and mother to mother support groups. The volunteers will be involved in project design, implementation and monitoring of activities in their respective locations.The second layer is through strengthening and working with community-based committees, which are comprised of members of both genders, elderly, youth, people with disabilities, and of minority groups where relevant. The community level engagement is materialized through conducting regular meeting with the volunteers and community-based committees. This is especially so to ensure that no one or group is left out by service provision and that there is optimum coverage and access by all persons in need. WRSS sector staff and area coordinators regularly conduct meeting at community level and discuss progresses, challenges, future plan and opportunities, community concerns and risks. Feedback is discussed with the management team in Juba and used to adapt the project to meet the changing needs. WRSS also interacts with specific protection actors concerning their findings in the target locations to make sure programming addresses the needs of all community members. Protection will be mainstreamed across this project to mitigate any risk, harm, abuse and exploitation faced by the target population. Consultation with local authorities and line ministries as well as other stakeholders will determine the locations of service implementation, taking into consideration safe spaces that are accessible to the largest number of beneficiaries.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

With similar programming in these locations, WRSS Staff are aware of the barriers to access that different population groups could face during the project. In Fangak, targeted communities may be isolated by hard terrains, swampy island and accessibility to the locations happening only by canoes or boats. Many times, flooding hinder movements of beneficiaries to access nutrition centers. Under these circumstances, staff and volunteer workers (CNVs) will continue with weekly outreach activities to badly affected, stationing in centrally known areas to replenish supplies even doing house to house to do case finding and defaulter tracing. In Koch, well planned schedules, prompt communication and notification to local leaders and other community representatives will continue to ensure staff and CNVs access designated areas of operations safely and that all eligible beneficiaries are able to access and benefit from the proposed nutrition services and activities. These consultations remain important during beneficiary targeting. By so doing, WRSS will mitigate these barriers and endeavor to reach beneficiaries as close to their own communities as possible.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Applying best practices, vulnerable women, girls and boys targeted and those benefiting from the project will be protected during project implementation. Principles of “do-no-harm” will be observed. Beneficiaries will be referred to nutrition sites that are closer to them and schedule of operations well communicated to them. At the nutrition sites, secure and clean latrines, safe drinking water, soap and water for handwashing shall be made available. In MIYCN/IYCF counselling and education outreaches, deliberate efforts shall be made to incorporate men. At the households, father/men involvement will be encouraged to ensure better understanding so that the beneficiary pregnant and lactating women and/or caregivers of children under 5 years may get the required support. A rights-based approach shall be employed empowering beneficiaries to understand, ask for and safeguard their entitlements freely. Both genders, elderly, youth, people with disabilities, and of minority groups, where relevant, communities will be empowered to mitigate inequality and improve their resilience.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Though this is not specifically a protection project, protection will be mainstreamed to address inequalities and any violence, coercion, exploitation, deprivation, or neglect. As described above under Protection Risk Analysis, WRSS has a long history in these locations and staff are very knowledgeable of the local context and what threats exists. Existing multi-sectoral programming has incorporated this learning to ensure protection mainstreaming and the same will be done in this proposed project. Through community engagement with program volunteer teams comprised of local residents, WRSS will be able to recognize and address such concerns. The volunteers will be involved in project design, implementation and monitoring of activities in their respective locations. Additionally, through strengthening and working with community-based committees, which are comprised of members of both genders, elderly, youth, people with disabilities, and of minority groups, where relevant, communities will be able to advise and help mitigate inequality and protection concerns. WRSS will message carefully and thoroughly for the proposed project so that communities and beneficiaries will know their rights and entitlements and be able to claim them freely.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

This project will measure access of beneficiaries to key activities. Known population estimates will be used in setting numerical targets and in the targeting of beneficiaries by geographical location. Known population statistics will be compared against the number of beneficiaries accessing the project’s activities. Access of beneficiaries will also be measured by beneficiary category, with monitoring disaggregated (as appropriate) by gender, age, disability status, vulnerability status, etc. Where the monitoring identifies insufficient access by a beneficiary group, project staff will adjust the project to ensure their inclusion.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

The will ensure safety of pregnant and lactating women, girls and boys coming to nutrition sites by providing safe drinking water, latrines, safe and child friendly spaces. Staff will always ensure that nutritional commodities (RUTF/RUSF) are not expired, that they are of good quality, all sachets are well sealed, are hygienic and meet safety requirements for human use. By promoting IYCF-E messaging, the program safeguard motherhood for children 0-23 months. It is here that exclusive breastfeeding is emphasized for children 0-6 months to ensure healthy babies and after 6 months, timely introduction of soft-solid and semi-solid foods are introduced with complimentary feeding. This ensures children grow well in a safe atmosphere.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Dignity of beneficiaries shall be safeguarded by ensuring that all field staff are trained on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and Do No Harm principles. Respect, integrity and objectivity at work shall be upheld in all project activities to ensure that beneficiaries feel safe and protected. The project team will ensure that all WRSS nutrition sites are free from possible risks and threats so that there is safe access by all beneficiaries including women and children who can be vulnerable. Beneficiaries shall be sensitized on their rights. Their right to be served with dignity , right to aid, right to information, right to give feedback , right to participate during any engagements and through committee representatives.
