Project Summary

The IRC developed the project in line with its global vision, the Strategic Action Plan for Bangladesh 2022-2024, and JRP 2024 objectives. The prioritization of activities and targeted beneficiaries is based on the priorities expressed by the community, protection monitoring data, and client feedback initiatives, JMSNA, assessment report, insights from IRC field teams and a gap analysis. IRC coordinates with the GoB, Protection sector on protection SAG, protection mainstreaming, ATWG, ADWG, GDHWG, PSEA, monthly 5W, quarterly PM updates, and participates in camp-level coordination meetings with the CICs, and other actors. The IRC's Protection project will be implemented in the following camps: 1E,1W,15,16,21, and 25 where IRC has been implementing integrated protection programs since 2017; and expanded its legal services to Rajapalong, Palongkhali and 5 unions in Teknaf.
IRC will use AGD responsive approach, and the project will focus on systematic protection monitoring to unearth rights violation and access concerns and trends, relevant to protection and assistance to inform prioritization, advocacy, program re-design, and decision-making. Collect the AGD segregated data and information through KIIs, FGDs, and HHs interviews, verified, and analyze the data to share with the sectors, donors, at field levels, and other stakeholders that help to reduce the risk for all the camp beneficiaries. provide case management services to individuals at heightened protection risk to ensure they manage and reclaim their rights leading to their safety, dignity and ensuring resilience. Provide IPA support to vulnerable individuals, a process informed by UNHCR profiling, PTE, and the regular identification through protection monitoring including PWD and elderly. Solar lights to be considered for elderly, young women, and persons with disabilities (PWD) to mitigate the protection threats, and coordinate with Energy and Environment network (EEN) and camp management to mitigate protection risk.
Strengthen inclusive and integrated community-based protection structures (CBPs) (50% M and 50% F), including persons with disabilities to handle non-criminal protection cases, and conduct timely referrals to foster access to services for those in need. The project will promote peaceful coexistence within the camps, and between refugees and host community through meetings, community dialogues, and recreational activities; CBPCs will conduct regular protection risk analysis, to identify risks, incidents, and trends, which will inform community-specific action plans, ensuring referral and CBP mechanisms in place. They will consider the environmental aspects during protection risk assessment and formulate action plans. IRC as a key player in the Protection sector, will conduct a multi-sector gender-sensitive assessment on climate change and associated protection risks, continue with disaster preparedness efforts for monsoon and cyclone responses, and identify the negative coping mechanism; provide legal assistance (legal counseling, mediation, support at police station and court) in host communities and accessible to women and adolescent girls; conduct protection mainstreaming training; support IRC, partners, Protection and other sectors to mainstream protection through prioritizing safety, dignity, and do no harm, ensuring meaningful access by all to services, promote accountability, and participation & empowerment.
IRC has a well-established CFRM in office and field levels, is co-chairing the PSEA network, and has focal point staff to create awareness on SEA, child safeguarding, and reporting mechanisms with the support of IRC’s Gender Equality Diversity and Inclusion team; conducts capacity-building initiatives to IRC and other stakeholders. The IRC’s established 2-way communication system to provide life-saving appropriate information both in the host community and camps using a tested method to provide and respond to information needs.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Now in its sixth year, the protracted crisis is a protection crisis with over 931,574 Rohingya refugees in Cox’s Bazar and Bhasan Char where 51.34% female, 44.24% children, 3.93% elderly. The recent JMSNA’23 found 5% of refugees and 2% of host community individuals with disabilities, besides ADI Need Assessment’21 found12%. JMSNA'23 by the Inter-Sector Coordination Group(ISCG) highlights protection concerns as a top priority in the refugee camps (48%), along with low access to services (17%). Refugee households expressed higher concerns(60%) about protection risks compared to host community households(33%). The major concerns included crime, violence, and potential affiliation with criminal groups. The stateless Rohingya live in extremely overcrowded camps, with no freedom of movement outside the camps, restricted access to rights including education, livelihood, and specific protection needs contributes to the cyclical denial of identity, persecution, and displacement. The Concerns included crime and violence(48%), potential affiliation with criminal groups(25%) and restriction of movement(25%).37% of refugee households reported feeling unsafe after dark while 5% will not go out alone in the dark; 50% of hosts reported feeling very safe compared to refugees 22%. The main reasons include presence of criminal groups and unfunctional solar light, only 48% are fully functional, 13% partially and 30% non-functional and 10% missing. 20% of refugees reported the main reasons for leaving the camps includes work opportunities, lack of food and overall deterioration of security situation. According to the ISCG Community Consultations: Adjustments to Assistance indicated an increase in child labor, GBV, trafficking, theft and looting, adjustments due to reduction of assistance. That mostly affected of adjustments for women headed HH(89%), health conditions for elderly(78%), 50% PWD, 60%pregnant and lactating mother, and 38% widow due to their age, physical condition, social security, limited employment opportunities, hazardous alternative work arrangements. Safety and security concerns are significantly decreased in the camps, especially theft, robbery, abduction, and intimate partner violence (IPV), increased disputes and aggressive treatment within families. human trafficking and gender-based violence, pushed into illegal activities to earn additional money or else risk cross-border movements and travel. The protection consortium PM report (June-Aug’23) revealed limited access to services(29.65%), safety and security/incidents of violent nature (25.18%), and HLP violations (24.09%) are the most reported protection concerns, on-going tensions between host and refugee communities, and among refugees, as a main protection concern. The safety and security concerns are extortion/payment of bribes(21%), detention and torture(20%), and abduction/disappearance(20%), along with criminal gangs, racketeering, human trafficking, violence, and abuse. The 2nd quarter PS monitoring report revealed the deterioration of the safety and security situation. Major safety and security incidents included – abduction (172), serious physical assault(161), extortion(137), killing/murder/manslaughter(31)-of which all victims were refugees-, and shooting /gunshots(29). Growing concerns of abductions and gunfights between rival criminal groups continued to be reported almost regularly in various camps. The influx has posed challenges to the formal justice system, impacting its effectiveness and community trust. Delayed investigations, obtaining witness testimony becomes difficult, and hinders the progress of justice. According to the PS Monitoring report, most frequent serious crimes brought before the court through the legal partners includes (attempted) murder, serious physical harm, and (attempted) rape. Additionally, the prevalence of extortion practices specifically exacerbates the challenges in seeking justice for women and girls.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The IRC’s rationalization work is premised on integrated protection programming to respond to protection issues through multi-sectoral response. It increases the project scope or reach, enhances capacity building, promotes internal referrals and timely access to services, and the upholding of protection standards such as confidentiality, stakeholder management, and others. These interconnected processes cannot be carried out by a single team, and it is a highly valued aspect of protection integration. The rationalization strategy draws from the multifaceted nature of protection threats and the complex contexts in which they arise. As such, complementary, coordinated, and collaborative actions by PRoL, CP, and WPE to address protection concerns are required. protection integration is anchored on clear alignment of the interventions and/or approaches under PRoL, CP, & WPE, and the rationalization process by PS. It leads to high-impact, cost-effective programs for people affected by disasters & conflict, and shapes policies and practices in Bangladesh by sharing our learning and experience with others. IRC will follow the sector strategy to cover the gap and reduce duplication. The IRC’s rationalization is driven by five objectives: ensure services are integrated, reduction of existing service gaps, cost efficiency of the intervention and quality of programming. IRC already has a strong presence in the targeted sites and has been closely coordinating with the sectors, government, and areas of responsibility. IRC also has a robust resource mobilization plan, capacity, and relations with donors to cover the proposed interventions. IRC has a separate government liaison team to coordinate with the relevant government entities. The IRC Bangladesh, regional and HQ teams work closely together to provide high-quality protection services following technical standards and the organization has an integrated protection program (CP, GP, and GBV).

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project will prioritize the protection of affected populations and contribute to protection outcomes in humanitarian actions by preventing, minimizing and/or mitigating the consequences of protection risks. By designing and implementing all activities, we will consider protection risks and potential violations and maximize the protective impact of aid programming. The IRC will continue to build the capacity of sectoral partners and its IPs to prioritize safety and dignity and avoid causing harm, ensuring meaningful access, accountability, and participation and empowerment throughout the PCM. A continuous assessment of the project activities, services, plans, monitoring, and evaluation will be conducted with PM lenses to identify existing limitations and gaps of the program and effective plan to overcome identified limitations (PMAP). Regular review on the PMAP will help the program to identify the progress, changes, new opportunities, possibilities of intentional and unintentional harm etc. and take necessary actions to minimize the negative impact of the program, maximize positive impact and mitigate the limitations and gaps including the possibility of causing harm through program cycle and activities. IRC’s PM is ultimately about rights-based, reliable, and quality humanitarian programming that considers age, sex, disability, specific needs, and cultural appropriateness throughout the project. The clients are rights holders or agents of change to be consulted and their ideas will be incorporated in the project. The IRC will follow PM process scorecard to review the project and ensure remedial support to mitigate the risk. The structures to be strengthened, building existing community capacities, increase access and inclusion of the most vulnerable and marginalized groups. Regular community consultations, and risk assessments for men, women, boys, and girls, including PWD, diverse persons to identify and mitigate risks, and ensure safety, dignity, and equity.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

The patriarchal representation of masculinity is strong in Rohingya and host communities. IRC is dedicated to mainstreaming Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) within the project through a comprehensive framework that places the rights and well-being of individuals affected by PSEA at the forefront. IRC recognizes the potential conflict between mandatory reporting obligations and the survivor's right to choose how they address a PSEA incident and pledges to resolve this conflict on a case-by-case basis, taking into account both the survivor's rights and the broader community's safety in alignment with the relevant organization's internal policies. A survivor-centered approach will be maintained, with strict adherence to confidentiality at all stages of the process, ensuring that the survivor's safety and dignity are always prioritized during reporting, investigation, and assistance. The participation, mobility, and decision-making of women and girls are very limited and controlled by men and boys. The deeply entrenched social, and cultural norms support men in all aspects, accepting underage marriage or marrying more than one at a time, having sexual relationships with multiple partners, and confirming physical, psychological, or sexual harassment to women and girls in the name of disciplining. Gender analysis through an intersectional lens and location-specific considerations will be conducted, and measures to empower marginalized people to mobilize and claim their rights. The IRC will reach the age, gender, and diverse population throughout the project and disaggregate the analysis in line with AGD approach. IRC will follow the gender ratio in staff and volunteers’ recruitment and management representation, training on gender mainstreaming and PSEA, and design and implement a gender-sensitive intervention for changing situation through shifting the norms and cultural practices, where women, girls, and divers can raise their voices

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The IRC incorporates Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and Disaster Risk Management (DRM) elements in all its operations. Vulnerabilities and protection risks for women, girls, and children are heightened during disasters. The IRC’s approach involves establishing the Protection in Emergency Response Unit to address protection concerns during disasters precisely. This unit is equipped to respond swiftly and effectively to protect the most vulnerable populations, particularly women, girls, and children, strengthening CBPCs that are pivotal in enhancing community resilience and disaster preparedness. These committees comprise community members, including women, who are trained to identify, address, and respond to community protection risks. build the capacity of committee members and other stakeholders in the community through training and awareness message dissemination in multi-hazard preparedness and response. provide necessary emergency response equipment and personal protective gear to the community-based committee members and others to respond appropriately during emergencies, minimizing protection risks. Fire Safety Assessment & stimulus Drill conducted. By integrating DRR into our Protection sector, IRC’s approach emphasizes preparedness, community involvement, capacity building, and the provision of essential equipment to the clients. This enables to reduction of protection risks and enhances the safety and well-being of the most vulnerable individuals, particularly women, girls, and children, in the face of disasters. work closely with energy and environmental networks to foster climate mitigation and climate adaptation. Protection monitoring, CBP, and volunteers will help to identify the protection risk related to climate adaptation and issues of security due to the lack of solar lights. IPA (solar light) and coordination between CBP and actors will help to an environment-friendly aspect in the IRC locations.
