Project Summary

Al-Hol camp is one of the largest camps in Syria hosting around 58,439 people (as of 30 September 2022), including Syrian internally displaced persons (19,439), Iraqi refugees (30,590) and third country nationals (8,399). Areesha camp is smaller and hosts only Syrian IDPS (around 14,264 people as of 30 September 2021).
Both camps have a functioning COVID-19 treatment facilities (CTF) which operate on 24/7 basis seven days a week. The necessity to have fully functional CTFs stems from the fact that camp-based residents are more vulnerable to transmission of COVID-19 due to overcrowded conditions in the camps, lack of IPC supplies, shortage of potable water, and a range other, camp-specific factors.
Both CTFs are supported through a project funded by OCHA/SHF which is nearing competition (February 2022).
Areesha camp
Total number of infected/suspected patients admitted to CTF in Mar-Dec 2021 reads189.
Average monthly number of infected/suspected patients admitted to CTF reads 19.
Al Hol camp;
In March-December 2021, the CTF in Al-Hol camp served as the main referral point for all suspected and confirmed cases in the camp, admitting 519 infected/suspected patients in total (including 206 IDPs, 254 Refugees, and 59 third-country nationals). On average, around 52 infected/suspected patients per month were admitted to CTF in Al-Hol camp in 2021.
It should further be mentioned that operational support to CTF in Al-Hol has been provided by other humanitarian partners, including ICRC (provision of tents, blankets, mattresses, power generators, site fencing ready-made meals, potable water), UNHCR (tents partitioning), WHO (provision of power generators, medications and medical supplies), CADUS (caravans), UNICEF (water tanks) and MSF (waste burner).
Impact of the closure of CTFs in both camps
If additional funding is not secured and service continuity is not ensure, on average 52 infected patients will not be isolated and treated in Al-Hol camp, and 19 infected patients will not be isolated and treated in Areesha camp. Closure of CTFs in the end of February 2022 will leave both camps without any dedicated COVID-19 treatment facilities. CTF service suspension in both camps (especially in Al-Hol, given its population size) could potentially result in a sharp increase in the number of COVID-19 cases.
the CTFs were designated to receive the mild and moderate cases while the severe cases are referred outside the camp. the project proposal will contribute to provide the necessary requirements of medical, protective supplies and medicines as well the logistical services as well provision of the medical staff that needed to facilitate the provision of the treatment for the potential infected cases at the two centers at both camps.
as well, due to the high pressures on the health facilities of COVID-19 outside the camp in Hassakeh and Qamishli cities, where as of various reports received about lack of availability of spaces for the referred cases from the camps, So that, the need to continue provision of the curative services which linked with the other health partners in close coordination with WHO.
Moreover, The CTFs at both camps play a key role for spreading awareness precaution and preventive education measures to be taken against COVID 19 pandemic in particular by those who are the immediate relatives of the infected people and sharing the shelter with them.

4 - Needs

In coordination with WHO and its Health partners as well with the camp management and administration in both camps, Al Hol and Al Areesha, Al Bir Wa Al Ehsan (BICA) has made a field specific need assessments, which revealed that there is an urgent need to continue the provision of the medical treatment for the potential COVID-19 cases in both camps through supporting the two CTF and to respond to potential emergency needs arising from the COVID-19 pandemic through provision the required logistical support, medical equipment and medicines and provision facilities to protect health care workers from infection by COVID19. As well to continue to spread awareness education on preventive and precaution measures to contribute to combat coronavirus among the camp based population who are the most vulnerable during harsh winter. Al-Hol camp is one of the largest camps in Syria hosting around 58,439 people (as of 30 September 2022), including Syrian internally displaced persons (19,439), Iraqi refugees (30,590) and third country nationals (8,399). Areesha camp is smaller and hosts only Syrian IDPS (around 14,264 people as of 30 September 2021).

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

BICA is committed to mainstreaming the protection considerations at all stages of the project cycle. The project is deigned to make the concerns and experiences of women, girls, boys and men an integral dimension of it. BICA field team will select community volunteers from both genders and rely on their feedback and inputs throughout the implementation period. Men and women will also be separately consulted with regards to the day-to-day logistical considerations, such as regarding the most appropriate locations for gender-segregated distributions points. As a result, women and men have an equal the right and opportunity to participate in decision-making. The risks of PSEA will be identified through publicly discuss and address ongoing violence where men and women will be given a chance to express their different concerns, experiences, needs and capabilities through gender-age including persons with disabilities and survivors of GBV as identified. The assessment phase will highlight different gender- age specific gaps, such as unequal access assistance and services for men and women, in addition to likely protection risks for women, boys and girls. And as prevention measures that: 1. Employees sign code of conduct related to sexual exploitation. 2. Training of employees on procedures related to sexual and material exploitation. 3. Posters that include the complaints numbers. 4. Assigning a specialized team to follow up and verify complaints in order to respond immediately.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Security risk: the security is unpredictable although the areas of intervention is enjoying calm security situation, Likelihood: low. Impact: medium. Mitigation: BICA closely monitors the changes in the overall security situation in targeted areas and adjust accordingly its operations, adapting its protocols, systems and contingency plans, to minimize safety and security risks for its personnel and beneficiaries. Fraud risk: Aid diversion, fraud, and corruption. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: diversification of supplier, triangulation of information, standard monitoring procedures in place, security plan and risks assessments tables updated and followed. Financial risk: Substantive devaluation of USD against the Syrian pound. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: The eventuality of significant changes in the value of the Syrian currency will be closely monitored and, if materialized, timely communicated with OCHA office in Qamishli and options discussed. COVID 19 risk: Vulnerable households including persons with disabilities are mostly affected and in need of support. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: Support the beneficiaries to implement the health protocol and the preventive measures against the challenges posed by COVID-19 and to adhere to wearing masks.
