Project Summary

This project aims to provide life-saving and resilient WASH-IWRM, Education, GBV and Protection to 159,150 beneficiaries, 119,900 (WASH), 17620 (Protection) and 21630 (Education). IAS will target returnees, host communities, and those affected by conflict or recurring natural disasters in South Kordufan, South Darfur, West Darfur, Red Sea and the Blue Nile. IAS will target vulnerable populations such as women, children, and children with disabilities, and the elderly considering gender-sensitive issues. IAS will utilize an Integrated Water Resources Management (IWRM) approach. Activities to be implemented under WASH will include drilling of new boreholes equipped with hand pumps and construction of solar-powered Mini Water Yards.
IAS, within this project will provide life-saving education services for 21,630 beneficiaries (5670 indirect beneficiaries, 15960 direct beneficiaries), in Blue Nile, Altadamoun, Wad Almahi, Kurmuk and Baw targeting 5040 students and vulnerable children, 420 PTAs ( 2 in each locality ,10 member in each), 210 teachers – West Darfur, Sirba, Habila and Jebel Moon targeting 3000 students and vulnerable children, 6 PTAs ( 2 in each locality , 10 members each) 30 teachers -South Darfur, Sharg Eljabel, AlSunta, Kateila, Greida and Um Dafoug targeting 5000 students and vulnerable children, 10 PTAs ( 2 in each locality ,10 members each), 50 teachers –South Kordufan, Dilling, Alreef Alshargi, Talawdi, Alabassya and Abu Jubayha targeting 5000 students and vulnerable children, 10 PTAs ( 2 in each locality 10 members each), 50 teachers -Red Sea 4000 students and vulnerable children in Red Sea, in Haya, Sinkat, Algunub and Dordeib targeting , 8 PTAs ( 2 in each locality 10 in each), 40 teachers. Protection will be targeted 17620 in SK (Dilling, Talawdi, Alabassya, Abu Jubayha and Alreef Alshargi), SD(Greida, AlSunta, UmDafoug ,Katiela, East Jabal mara) and BN (Altadamoun-BN, Baw, Alkurmuk Wad Almahi) localities; intervention will be in GBV and general protection including PSEA through awareness sessions in PSS approach and provide PSS services to children with disabilities with referral mechanism . For the affected population, including children with disabilities. IAS will implement activities aimed at addressing GBV. These will include construction of 6 Women centers (2 in each state), targeting 6000 vulnerable women and girls The project will conduct 28 awareness campaigns (2 in each locality) targeting11200 vulnerable and conflict affected beneficiaries on Gender Based Violence at locality levels. Moreover, IAS will formulate and train 14 community based protection network (1 in each locality, 30 members each) to support the delivery of the PSS services and referral pathway. IAS will mainstream peace building, gender and climate changes mitigation strategies in all the projects.

1 - Needs

Humanitarian partners estimate that about 13.4 million people – 29 per cent of the population – will need humanitarian assistance. This is 4.1 million people more compared to 2020 and an increase of 44 per cent in the year. Overall, the number of people in need in Sudan in 2021 is the highest reported in the past decade. Of the 13.4 million people in need, about 7.3 million need emergency assistance for life threatening needs related to critical physical and mental well-being. Meanwhile, 13.3 million people require life-sustaining support to meet minimum living standards. The Health sector has the highest number of people in need – 9.2 million, followed by WASH – 9 million, and the Food Security and Livelihoods sector – 8.2 million people in need. There are 2.5 million internally displaced people (IDPs) in Sudan. Most of them are in Darfur, South Kordufan, and Blue Nile, which have been the epicenters of conflict over the past 17 years. These are also the areas where 52 per cent of people in need are concentrated. This means that almost half of the people in need are in non-conflict central and eastern parts of the country, whereas in 2018, 74 per cent of people in need were in conflict areas - Darfur, South Kordufan and Blue Nile. (SUDAN HNO-2021)According to Joint Education Need Assessment (JENA 2021) 71% of the schools do not have hand washing facilities, 46% of schools lack of water, soap and hand washing facilities and 22% of classrooms are damaged and 52% reported require major rehabilitation and only 9% of latrines are accessible to Children with disabilities. 66% of households in West Darfur face challenges of access to clean water. In West Darfur, 81% of households reported that females are responsible for fetching water,40% of respondents reported awareness of water committees in their respective areas (MICs BASELINE SURVEY, WD & CD- 2020).An inter-agency assessment in South Darfur in East Jabel Marra established huge needs in WASH; all localities are in urgent need of water and WASH facilities as many boreholes have broken down. Some households were using unprotected water sources, Schools did not have water facilities, most of the IDPs did not have toilets and practiced open defecation in and around their dwellings. Based on the needs assessment that was conducted in the targeted areas, it became clear that Sanitation coverage in the targeted area is extremely low, and many people do not practice good hygiene. There are water-related diseases, there are some communities that know little about the causes of these diseases because they do not have a good background about health Awareness, while most villages do not know the causes due to a lack of health awareness. Part of the reason why sanitation and hygiene practices are poor is socio-cultural in nature: many people in the targeted areas have never been used to good sanitation and hygiene behavior, and prefer traditional practices. (IAS South Darfur State Needs Assessment, Nyala, Beleil, East Jebl Mara & Ed-Elfrsan localities, January 2021)Gender Based Violence (GBV) is a key concern within the communities affected by emergencies i.e. conflicts, tribal clashes/tensions, floods, drought as well as disease outbreaks. According to the MSNA data, 29 per cent of households have access to GBV services with 87 per cent of households preferring reporting GBV cases to the police, while 24 per cent preferring leaders, four per cent preferring social worker, and only one per cent preferring health staff, community midwife, teacher or NGO staff. Also, people are 27 per cent less likely to report GBV cases to the police in South Darfur compared to Khartoum and the other states, which reflects varying levels of confidence in police. (SUDAN HNO-2021).

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Due to our experience and lessons learned in the multi-year DFID funded IWRM projects in Darfur and eastern Sudan, IAS uses IWRM principles to enhance the environment in the design, implementation, and closing of our WASH humanitarian projects. For 2022, IAS will be implementing Hafirs using the community-involved catchment approach, the assessments wil also be based on catchment while coordinating with WES the decisions governing all things related to the intervention, including environmental actions. Rather than continuing to use up existing precious water by drilling more boreholes, this approach guides rainwater (90% of which is normally lost) through the use of sand dams and other subsurface dams to replenish existing wells. The environmental impact for safe water provision is naturally very positive in terms of supporting healthier lifestyles, a better quality of lives, and supporting plants and agricultural projects, etc., but with the use of cement and construction materials, great care will be given to cleaning up after each project site; that each water point intervention area will be cleaner than before IAS commenced the project. Inclusive community/ main actors project design, initiation, and planning meetings will be the stage for environmental protection and planning advocacy. Beneficiaries will be tasked with developing a long-term environmental plan and will sign-off and be accountable to the authorities on their progress. For every water point, IAS will conduct Environment Impact Assessments (EIAs). Taking the data from these surveys, we educate targeted beneficiaries to take the necessary steps to improve the environment. Communities will be involved in cleaning up water points and latrine construction sites. Fences and shrubs will be set up around the area. Conservation of natural resources, especially water, will be central themes in WMC meetings throughout the project, including close-out activities, including inspection, ensuring that the environment activities were carried out and the environment enhancement plan was completed for the future each beneficiary community where IAS is intervening. As well as budgeting for water point maintenance needs, WMCs will be requested to work with the community to budget environmental enhancement activities. Finally, those receiving environmental advocacy and planning training will be encouraged to bring their knowledge into the local schools.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

In emergency WASH responses, IAS will continue to facilitate meaningful participation and engagement of affected communities, including training and messaging throughout the program, including child protection needs assessment, response planning, and monitoring. In carrying out community-based protection services, IAS will pay attention to the choice of locations and targeting of beneficiaries to avoid perceptions that assistance is delivered in a discriminatory or non-equitable manner, conducting community safety audit and mapping of exiting services and ensuring that the results of these two exercises inform programming and advocacy. IAS will maintain transparent, inclusive, and adequate communication with communities on available services, consulting communities and duty bearers in the planning, selecting services, and feedback after service provision. Partners will also be guided on ensuring prioritization of services to groups considered particularly vulnerable, such as young children, children with disability boys and girls, children without adult care, ensuring that programming is tailored to respond to all communities' needs. Child Protection services and response interventions will continue to be based on a quality and integrated approach, including coordination and capacity building with other humanitarian actors to support Protection mainstreaming and a do-no-harm approach as well as principled response. Besides, IAS will build on and integrate, wherever possible, feedback mechanisms to strengthen accountability and support the interagency coordination mechanism to respond to relevant complaints. IAS will continue working closely with partners to monitor beneficiaries' feedback, share information, and conduct community consultations, ensuring adequate accountability to affected populations (AAP). Child Protection training for partners will often include sessions on AAP and Child Protection response. During the implementation of this project, IAS will ensure equal participation of all vulnerable groups, including children with disabilities and other vulnerable populations, in all decision-making processes concerning needs identification. As COVID 19 hits globally, IAS will ensure safeguarding the poor and vulnerable groups, including children with disabilities, through continuous massages that will be provided within all project implementation activities. During latrines rehabilitation, facilities will have enough lights to ensure girls' and children's safety during usage. During WASH activities implementation, IAS will position the water facilities in the appropriate location accessible to women and girls to ensure their safety from sexual exploitation and protect vulnerable from being trafficking and be subjected to join armed groups.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

safety and dignity for affected people, following the guidance of the Global WASH Cluster and the HCT. The minimum obligations are operationalized in the accountability framework developed by WASH partners, which guides partners to 1) provide the minimum information to the public, 2) involve the community in decision making, 3) learn from feedback and complaints, 4) encourage appropriate staff attitude and behaviors. 5) use information from project learning, and 6) develop programs based on participatory assessments. The WASH cluster will continue to guide partners in adopting the framework by sharing best practices and peer learning. The implementation of the framework will be monitored through a self-reporting mechanism, while the cluster will also advise partners on relevant indicators they can include in their monitoring efforts (including post-distribution monitoring). As part of its community-based response, IAS will engage and involve affected populations through, among others, establishing community-based protection networks and committees, including IDPs, women, and youth, to foster two- way communication and feedback with the humanitarian community and mobilize capacities toward the Protection of all members of the community, particularly the vulnerable. Community protection initiatives also involve the community's participation in raising awareness of crucial protection issues relevant to women, girls, boys, and men to prevent or resolve conflicts and foster peaceful co-existence. IAS will also establish feedback and complaint mechanisms, such as protection hotlines that provide information on services, suggestion boxes in child-friendly spaces, safe spaces for women, and regular trends analysis. Other accountability mechanisms include the Accountability and Impact Assessment Framework of the GBV Sub-Cluster, as well as Child Protection Minimum Standards, which supports accountability between partners and meaningful child participation in program implementation. Every WASH intervention will include SEA mechanisms.
