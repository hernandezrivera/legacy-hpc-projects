Project Summary

The proposed project aims at improving food security of conflict-affected communities in Northeast and Northwest Syria, with an approach geared at saving lives with emergency assistance, and increasing resilience at household and community level with improved food production capacity and livelihoods opportunities. This will be achieved through a combination of different interventions at the individual and community-level. At the individual level, emergency responses will be delivered through distribution of in-kind food baskets and Ready-to-Eat Rations, as well as Cash-for-Work and Cash-for-Food schemes, benefiting 51,242 individuals, including recently displaced persons. This emergency response will complement multiple rounds of recurrent, monthly food assistance, which will constitute the core of the intervention, reaching 232,800 beneficiaries in and out of camp through cash, voucher, or in-kind modalities for at least 8 months. In addition, ACTED will enhance food production at HH level, through kitchen and home garden kits, enhancing individuals' food security. Both emergency and monthly activities will be completed by Nutritional Awareness Sessions, targeting all beneficiaries regardless of the modality through which they received support, which will strengthen HHs’ ability to make informed choices regarding nutritional values. ACTED will also target farmers and livestock holders with longer-term support, delivering voucher assistance to redeem seeds, fertilizers, polytunnels and tools, and training on climate-smart agriculture and best practices. At the community level, ACTED will rehabilitate communal agricultural assets, including bakeries and mills, as well as irrigation networks. These interventions will benefit both farmers (who will have increased access to water for irrigation through network rehabilitation) and communities (who will be able to access good quality bread through bakeries rehabilitation). All project activities will be designed in a way that enables resilience to be built at a local level and that builds the capacity of men, women, boys, and girls to access basic items and meet basic needs themselves. Specifically, at the household and community levels, ACTED will support men and women in building their own capacities to meet their basic needs so that they are more resilient and better able to access commodities and services in the future. Lastly, ACTED will work in coordination with local technical department with the aim of building their capacity to provide essential services for communities. This project is estimated to benefit a total of 344,319 individuals. ACTED will rely on a variety of selection criteria depending on the assistance modality, including recent displacement, no access to similar food assistance prior to the intervention, low economic status/conditions, food assistance not covering household needs, absence of regular income source, etc. Criteria such as market accessibility and labor opportunities in each area of implementation will determine assistance modalities between in-kind and voucher/cash. For business grants support, beneficiaries will selected on the basis of application presenting a business plan.

4 - Needs

The 2021 HNO for Syria reports 14.2 million people in need of FSA assistance across Syria, with a significant deterioration of the food security and livelihoods situation over the past year due to the compounded effects of a wide array of factors. Limited market accessibility and unaffordability of goods are causing widespread food insecurity across Syria, while continued conflict, displacement, Covid-19, and the currency crisis, have borne a drastic negative impact on employment opportunities and purchasing power, with the SMEB reaching 180,792 SYP as of April 2021, an unaffordable amount for virtually any conflict-affected household. Regarding agriculture, continued security risks and the absence of functioning infrastructure massively impacted production in Al-Hasakeh, Aleppo, and Idleb governorates, resulting in reduced income opportunities for farming and livestock holding households, as well as seasonal and daily workers, causing both food security and economic livelihood concerns. In particular, Al-Hasakeh governorate, where agriculture formed the core of the region’s economy, is on the brink of the most acute wheat and barley production crisis in over a decade, with major increases in the prices of subsidised goods including diesel crippling productivity, insecurity and military/militia presence limiting farmers’ access to crops, as well as wildfires destroying agricultural assets and yields in 2020. Food insecurity has become even more critical over the last months, due to the large-scale displacement of about 1 million conflict-affected persons in NWS.

5 - Implementation of activities

ACTED will deliver an emergency food response, specifically geared towards the most vulnerable households and recently displaced persons, through Ready-To-Eat Rations and Cash-for-Food or Cash-for-Work modalities with an average 7 USD daily wage. ACTED will assist some 50,000 persons under these emergency activities. Additionally, ACTED will roll out monthly food assistance over 8 months or more, mainly through in-kind food baskets or voucher modalities, depending on market accessibility in each targeted area, with an average 65 USD value per basket/voucher per month, including a scale-up approach based on household size for vouchers. 232,800 individuals are expected to benefit from monthly food assistance across Northern Syria. Cash and voucher value is determined to cover around 70-75% of the SMEB in each area of intervention. ACTED will also enhance long-term food security and resilience to future shocks at the household level through the distribution of 2,000 garden kits with an average 230 USD value, containing organic and mineral fertilizers (Ca+B and 20:20:20 NPK). ACTED will support micro, small and medium businesses with training and grants ranging from 500 USD for home-based and small entrepreneurs or farmers up to 6,000 USD for larger transformative businesses, strengthening market-driven income-generating activities for roughly 1,800 households. ACTED will also support farmers and livestock holders with vocational training, as well as grants for seeds, fodder, and tools. At the community level, ACTED will rehabilitate productive assets of the agriculture value chain and will work in coordination with local technical departments to strengthen their capacity to provide essential services.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Non-inclusion of highly vulnerable groups and minorities needs during project design/implementation - Mitigation measure: The proposed intervention will be implemented in accordance with ACTED’s gender approach, which incorporates gender analysis and considerations into the full project management cycle, from project design to implementation and monitoring. During the community sensitization, ACTED encourages the identification of special needs of most vulnerable groups including women, children, elderly, or people living with disabilities and conducts FGDs and community meetings prior to implementation. Risk 2: Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse - Mitigation measure: All ACTED staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct which directly refers to PSEA and GBV. As part of on-going efforts to improve accountability, ACTED is committed to implement a Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its projects to allow beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on ACTED work, as that of the contractors, and to relay any issue, including SEA or GBV. Risk 3: Cash-based intervention-related risks – Mitigation measures: ACTED selects distribution locations that ensure that highest level of safety and security for beneficiaries. ACTED’s monitoring and evaluation unit monitors cash-based implementation through verification processes and direct engagement with communities (FDG and HH-level interviews), ensuring that selected recipients will use the distributed funds for purposes in line with the objective. ACTED conducts market assessment ahead of cash-based implementation to ensure markets are functioning and accessible.

7 - Does the project include a PSEA strategy ?

Yes

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

All ACTED staff receive induction and regular refresher trainings on the Code of conduct, PSEA, and Child Safeguarding policies. Contractors have the obligation to sign an ethical declaration and respect ACTED Code of Conduct. Contractors are briefed on PSEA before the implementation of the project. ACTED staff will be present to monitor daily the work of the contractors but also the behavior of the contractor employees towards camp residents. Information on the CRM will be communicated to camp manager and community leaders and as much as possible directly to camp residents, especially those who may be directly impacted by the road rehabilitation. Information will also be displayed on the channels available to raise complaints. Mitigation measures are monitored through regular on-site monitoring at the rehabilitation sites. The presence of women staff, in the programme and monitoring teams, will be guaranteed particularly during monitoring and evaluation activities conducted at household-level. ACTED staff, programme and monitors, are trained on protection and gender mainstreaming and on how to refer SEA incidents and/or suspicion internally. All SEA incidents are investigated by ACTED SEA Committee and communicated to the PSEA Network, as part of ACTED responsibility as member of the network.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

• Conflict escalation: movements are suspended in specific areas; relocation of staff to other offices to continue activities • Community acceptance: monitored daily by liaison assistants, regular meetings with local community leaders and Key Informant network in order to prevent any tensions; regular dialogue ensured with all relevant authorities, with support from various coordination mechanisms at area level. • Road closures: ACTED diversified its network of suppliers through tender processes and reinforced local suppliers’ capacities to ensure that items are procured regularly and prepositioned • Procurement related risks: 2-week local advertisement in multiple locations and ACTED’s transparency@acted.org email, analysis of all offers led by Regional office. Evaluation of technical and financial offers conducted separately between field team and Regional Capital. Preparation of bid analysis, and full documentation is then done by Capital. • Recruitment related risks: process entails 2- week advertisement. Longlist prepared by Antakya Area Office and shortlist by Area Coordinator. Selection process is completed after interview records are received and reference check completed by Antakya HR Community and cohesion dynamics: • CRM inside Syria: stakeholders can safely provide feedback and report any project issues or concerns.
