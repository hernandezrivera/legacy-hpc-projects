1 - Needs

The conflict has had a negative impact on Ukraine’s economy. Industrial and agricultural production in Donbass has significantly decreased since the beginning of the conflict. Likewise, unemployment has increased, decreasing household’s income while living costs have remained the same or become more expensive due to scarcity. The cumulative impact of the conﬂict over the past 5 years has eroded people’s coping capacity, pushing them deeper into vulnerabilities. According to the FSLC’s Humanitarian Needs Overview, out of 3.4 million people in need 1,6 million people are severely and moderately food insecure in eastern Ukraine, in addition to 37,000 IDPs residing in other oblasts, who also need food assistance.Between 2013 and 2017 the percentage of the population living below the actual minimum subsistence level increased from 20% in 2013 to 74% in 2017 in Luhanska oblast and from 22% to 66% in Donetska oblast (“Analysis food security and socio-economic situation 29 march 2018”).The most commonly applied coping strategies are crisis strategies. Nearly 20% of HHs in NGCA (up from 3%) and 4% (up from 2%) in GCA has a member that has migrated elsewhere in search of work. Around 61% (up from 46% before) of HHs in NGCA and 31% (down from 35%) of HHs in GCA were found to have reduced health expenditures. Among the stress coping strategies, spent savings is the most common and is applied by 63% (up from 25%) in NGCA and 26% (up from 25%) in GCA. Around 20% of the households interviewed in GCA (up from 10%) and 28% in NGCA (up from 6,4%) purchased food on credit or borrowed food[ ]. Negative coping strategies include survival sex which affects predominantly women and even girls when finding themselves in extreme economic situations due to the conflict and lack of livelihoods opportunities. Female-headed households (57% of total) make up a larger proportion of unemployed households (39% vs 25%), making them particularly vulnerable to negative coping strategies.In 2016 women earned on average only 73.7% of men’s average monthly wage, which has not changed in nearly 20 years. [2] Such gender-based wage gaps create further vulnerability to poverty for women, especially female-headed households. It is estimated that 21% of female-headed households and female IDPs have poor and borderline levels of food consumption and therefore continue more vulnerable in GCAs (FSL Cluster)Level of unemployed youth IDPs is 42% compared to 35% of unemployed IDPs [3] (with 68% of unemployed IDPs being women [4] According to the 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 406,000 unemployed working-age people need livelihoods assistance in Donbass. Among the most vulnerable households (HH) are single-headed HH with children, HH with two or more children and female-headed households (FHH). Youth IDPs are particularly vulnerable to unemployment in Donbass as their education has been most likely disrupted by displacement. Indeed, youth unemployment in Ukraine stands at 20% (World Bank 2018) compared to 9% for the general population, 15% in Donetsk and 17% in Luhansk (FSL Cluster 2017) compared to 9% for the general population, 15% in Donetsk and 17% in Luhansk (FSL Cluster 2017). 48% of IDPs are inactive (IOM NMS 2018), and out of a total of 89% of IDPs who were actively seeking employment but reported facing difficulties, 80% of them were women (HNO 2019). FHH face challenges with 39% currently unemployed compared to 25% of male-headed HH. The conflict has had a significant impact on the agricultural sector, traditionally a major sector for livelihoods in Ukraine. 21,000 rural HH in GCA require agricultural support (HNO 2019).

2 - Activities & outcomes

SCI is implementing successfully such activities for the last four years in GCA and NGCA. Beneficiaries are receiving cash grants through bank transfer.The cash transfer amount, that equals 970 UAH/person/month is in line with the recommendation of CWG. The total amount is calculated for 2436 HH. The transfer amount for one household is 5820 UAH (970 UAH/person/month multiplied for 3 persons of the household for 2 months of assistance). The family composition of 3 persons is calculated based on previous project implemented by Save the Children and implementing partners. Vulnerability criteria: HH with children and members with disabilities, female headed and elderly headed households with children, elderly headed households with children in ward, etc.)Results: Result 1: 7308 (2436 HH) targeted conflict affected boys, girls, men and women have increased capacity to cover their essential basic needs strategies through provision of cash assistanceActivity 1.1: Community mobilization (integrating child protection awareness session and orientation on accountability mechanisms) and beneficiary selectionActivity 1.2 Market price monitoringActivity 1.3: Disbursement of first wave of cash transfers through national post office and/or banksActivity 1.4: Intermediate verification of cash utilization.Activity 1.5 Disbursement of second wave of cash transfers through national post office and/or banksActivity 1.6: Lessons learned session with partnersResult 2: Targeted conflict affected households enrolled in multipurpose cash are resilient against shocks and continue to invest in their children’s survival, learning and protection and reduced negative coping strategiesActivity 2.1: Conduct baseline to beneficiaries prior to disbursement of cashActivity 2.2: Monitoring of cash utilization prior to 2nd disbursementActivity 2.3 Post distribution monitoring and endline after distribution of all tranchesAccess to markets :According to JMM there is no significant changes in availability of financial institutions and markets since the previous round of MM. Working ATMs and banks were reported by 100% respondents in market sub-centers, while in periphery the financial institutions have continued to be mostly non-available. The reorganization of markets has occurred, and new linkages have emerged, which still might lack the capacity to service changing demand. Some locations have limitations in access to construction materials, non-food items etc. Residents of these locations reported that they travel for such materials and items to semi-peripheral markets or market sub-centers. Accountability to Conflict Affected Population• Conflict affected people (male, female, boys, girls & elderly) will have access to mechanisms (social media, hotline) to give feedback• Conflict affected people (male, female, boys, girls & elderly) is to be consulted during the whole project cycle• Information sharing to all conflict affected population (boys, girls, men and women, elderly)Coordination: SCI will engage regularly in the meetings of the CWG and the other Clusters (e.g. FSC, Shelter & NFI, Protection) based in Kyiv and in the sub cluster meetings in Donetsk and Luhansk oblast. SC is working bilaterally with agencies engaged in CTP in the same operating areas particularly on MPC Grants, as well as through the interagency CWG led by OCHA, to ensure that transfer values are both harmonized across agencies, and sufficient to meet basic needs as well as ensuring intended program and protection related outcomes.To mitigate risk of beneficiaries duplication while implementing activities there will be cross-check verification with partners and other stakeholders applied.Partnership: SCI will implement through its existing local partner organizations in GCA and NGCA. These local partners have been already trained and have experience in community mobilization, distribution and implementation of shelter related activities.
