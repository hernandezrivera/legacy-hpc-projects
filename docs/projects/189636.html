Project Summary

Norwegian Church Aid (NCA), in partnership with TOCH South Sudan, proposed to implement one year project to reinforce and scale up specialized GBV services provision including GBV case management, referral and provision of psychosocial support to Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), returnees and host communities in Tonj North, Tonj East, Gokrial East counties of Warrap State and Pibor county in GPAA. The project aims to enhance women and girls’ recovery and build resilience as well as empower communities including men and boys on GBV knowledge on prevention and risk mitigation.
NCA and its partners will utilize the women and girls’ safe spaces
The project will integrate cash and voucher assistance modality programming through case management and dignity kits items provision across the sites to increase women and girls’ choices to access services and flexibility.
The project targets a total of 16176 individuals which account for 2% of the total target of the GBV SC including Pibor: 4555 (1959 women, 1868 girls, 318 men, 410 boys).
Tonj North 5310 (2209 women, 2261 girls, 420 men, 420 boys). Tonj East: 3686 (1143 women, 1844 girls, 331 men, 368 boys). And 2617 in Gokrial East (968 women, 1187 girls, 209 men, 261 boys).
NCA is implementing the people with disability project, and in this project, GBV has been identified as the primary protection risk faced by people with disability. The project also targets 250 elderly persons with critical GBV needs. The project will also support 200 persons of disability from the target population. NCA has GBV programming in 5 states of Warrap (Tonj North and Kuajok), WBS (Jur river), EES (Torit), GPAA(Gumruk), and central Equatoria. In these places, we have women and girls’ friendly spaces where case management counselling psychosocial support are delivered by trained caseworkers as well as referral of GBV survivors to live saving-services such as health care. We also do prevention services through awareness-raising in the community and mainstreaming the GBV programming into the NCA WASH programme, where water management committees are trained to carry out GBV awareness sessions in the communities.
The NCA has also integrated the GBV and peace messages awareness "Tamar" campaigns with faith-based and civil society organizations. In addition, NCA is a member of state and national GBV, NFI and WASH clusters where specific GBV needs, and gaps have been identified.
The key outcomes include IDPs, returnees, host communities, primarily women, adolescent girls, child survivors, elderly and persons with disability, improved access to existing services for GBV survivors through a secure and confidential referral and reporting system, and increased community engagement in GBV prevention and response activities. The intervention aims to attain these outcomes by providing individual and group psychosocial support awareness-raising to the rights holders on GBV, addressing the stigma that GBV survivors are subjected to, capacity building of service providers on effective response to GBV survivors a as well as empowering and strengthening community-based structures such as protection committees, women groups and school clubs in GBV prevention and response. empowering and building resilience to GBV survivors through life skills support services, strengthening safe, confidential and accessible referral systems for survivors of GBV. Through this intervention, NCA will identify and address barriers to reporting with survivors and how they feel service provision can be enhanced. NCA will use an inter-agency approach to implement the project to ensure that comprehensive referral and case management services are accessible to survivors. NCA and partner will liaise with WASH, Health and Livelihood and peacebuilding clusters to ensure that GBV survivors and vulnerable persons of concern have access to the services through referral pathways to include services for persons with disability, child child survivors

1 - Needs

Tonj north, Tonj East, Gogrial East and Pibor are among the counties classified under emergency (IPC Phase 5). According to OCHA humanitarian need report published in February 2022, continued flooding and increased inter-communal violence reported in many states across the country, mainly in Jonglei (Pibor), Lakes, Unity, Upper Nile, Warrap (particularly Greater Tonj) and Western Equatoria (notably Tambura), will likely continue to disrupt livelihoods and trade and contribute to an increase in humanitarian needs in 2022. A recent GBV assessment conducted by NCA in February 2022 show that gender-based violence cases are widespread in Tonj north, Tonj East, Gogrial East and Pibor counties. The NCA report identifies Intimate partner violence as the highest form of GBV then rape, sexual exploitation and abuse, physical violence, sexual assault, forced and early marriages, emotional abuse, and denial of resources as the most rampant forms of GBV in the four targeted counties. The NCA survey further shows that 33 % of the respondents indicated that GBV cases usually go unreported while only 12 % reported that perpetrators of GBV are often taken to the police. Still only 0.5% of the cases (mostly rape) are taken to the hospital while 3% are taken to women safe centres. Cases taken to the faith leaders comprised 30% showing the level of trust the community has bestowed on the religious leaders. In terms of support to survivors, a majority of the respondents (82%) confirmed that survivors of GBV normally seek help from various institutions including the police, and GBV centres. Out of every ten women and girls, at least six had reported being sexually or physically assaulted. Violence against women and girls has worsened due to famine, floods, intercommunal violence, and Covid-19 have heightened women and girls' risks to GBV, especially sexual exploitation. Women and girls face high risks of maternal deaths due to lack of access to maternal care services. The Covid-19 pandemic confined Adolescent school-going girls at home, hence eventually becoming victims of sexual exploitation and early/unwanted pregnancy. Intercommunal violence in Warrap and Pibor have widened the gap of women and girls' access to essential water, food, markets and medical and maternal care services. Additionally, intercommunal violence increases women and girls' exposure to GBV as they provide for the family amidst the conflict. All forms of GBV continue to remain underreported due to stigma and cultural norms, which are deeply rooted in Tonj North, Tonj East, Gogrial East and Pibor counties. The widespread GBV constitutes a significant impediment to women and girls' participation in recovery and development initiatives in Tonj East, Tonj North, Gogrial East and Pibor counties. The prevalence of violence against women and girls is due to the gender inequality exacerbated by displacements arising from recent floods and conflicts. Presently, there are information and knowledge gaps on GBV as communities lack adequate platforms to discuss and address GBV issues. In addition, women and girls in Tonj North and Pibor walk long distances to access markets services and fetch water and collect firewood which exposes them to further risks and threats. The elderly and persons with disabilities are exposed to safety and health risks as they are often left behind during active conflicts and floods. Women girls continually face high incidents of trauma coupled with minimal opportunities for social and economic opportunities.Furthermore, due to food insecurity and scarcity of livelihood opportunities, women and girls are vulnerable to negative coping strategies such as transactional sex and child labour. Menstruation also undermines adolescent girls' school attendance, with the cost of menstrual hygiene becoming prohibitive due to the current financial crisis aggravated by conflicts and the Covid-19 pandemic.

2 - Project activities

(1) To build the capacity of 150 community leaders, religious leaders, women, men and youth groups, especially the age sets, law enforcement agencies, water management committees to address issues of GBV. The training will focus on Tamar and engaging men in responsible practices, including identifying women, girls, men, and boys as champions for change. (2) Provision of survivor-centered psychosocial support focused on healing, empowerment and recovery(3) Provide professional skills training on case management and PSS for 22 frontline NCA staff (counsellors). (4) Provide specialized individual and group psychosocial support and case management services to GBV survivors, women and girls, child survivors, men and boys of all ages, including persons with disability. (5) Provide material and cash and voucher support to highly vulnerable GBV survivors, women and girls of all ages. NCA also intends to engage its WASH emergency programme to train women and girls survivors of GBV and other vulnerable groups in life skills training such as making covid-19 face masks, bread making and menstrual hygiene kits; which NCA has already been distributing to reproduce these materials locally to make income and meet their basic needs. (6) Conduct dignity kit distribution and programming targeting vulnerable women and girls as part of GBV risk mitigation (7) Establish and / or strengthen GBV referral systems and linking GBV survivors to available response services and support NCA will work closely with the GBV sub-cluster at the grassroots level to strengthen and establish safe, accessible, and confidential GBV referral systems by establishing standard operating procedures for GBV referral, including developing standard referral and case management tools for partners (8) Conduct community awareness raising activities on GBV risk factors, mitigation measures and on how survivors can access support and services in line with the “do no harm” principle (9) Implement primary GBV prevention interventions to addresses harmful practices, systemic gender inequality and power imbalancesThe GBV prevention and response initiative will aim to reduce the vulnerability risk through protection activities through TAMAR campaigns and engaging men through accountable approaches. in Tonj North, Tonj East, Gokrial East and Pibor.(10) Implement Women and Girls Friendly Space (WGFS) programming to provide services, information and activities that promote healing, well-being and empowermentin Tonj North, Gokrial East and Gumruk. NCA intends to strengthen provision of psychosocial support services in already established 4 Women and Girls Safe Spaces being supported by other donors and agencies as well as establish three additional ones in the three locations. In addition, NCA will equip Women and girls safe spaces with WASH related services such as hygiene materials and Non Food Items (NFIs). (11) Enhance GBV preparedness, contingency planning and resilience building to prevent and respond to GBV

5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Greater Tonj and Gokrial in Warrap and Gumruk in Pibor have vibrant local markets where dignity kits for women and adolescent girls and elderly women can be sourced. NCA has conducted a preliminary cash and markets survey which has shown viability of using vouchers for targeted beneficiaries to receive GBV services through vouchers. There is a safe mechanism for payment of traders through the existing local Money transfer modalities within NCA which would enable NCA to transfer cash safely. Discussions with Women and Girls has shown that there are no barriers to receiving hygiene materials from shops and that cash vouchers are generally safe. The distances and routes for right holders’ access to markets are safe. Traders are able to guarantee a steady supply of goods based on market demand and have a price range that does not fluctuate beyond a certain threshold. Overall, NCA has established that using cash and vouchers will give right holders better value and choices.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

NCA has considered operational issues of access especially during wet season where costs of transportation of humanitarian materials becomes difficult due to bad roads. The challenges related to costs for transport as well as costs and risks associated with in kind donations and impact on local businesses have been considered. The existing markets and their ability to supply goods from big towns e.g., Juba and Wau have been assessed. The significance of promoting local businesses, the importance of not disrupting the function of markets by introducing external goods which are already in markets have been analyse. In addition, NCA considered the need for sustainable access to GBV services as well as the need to build resilience of communities and markets and factors for safety, market supplies and costs. NCA also recognised that markets are disrupted by road access constraint but also considered that local businesses are more resilient and more adaptive to shocks. We considered the impact of the economic crisis and how humanitarian responses can also play a role in supporting markets for delivering goods.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The feedback from the beneficiaries will be used to improve the quality of the services delivery to align them to beneficiaries needs and priorities. Feedback on breaches of code of conduct will be handled on the basis of outcome of thorough investigations. In addition, NCA monitoring processes are undertaken monthly and quarterly while midterm reviews and valuation are undertaken annually. Complaints response and feedback mechanism is a component of NCA programme monitoring framework.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

NCA has established diversified feedback and response mechanisms that are safe, confidential, culturally acceptable and accessible to all including persons with disabilities at the community level. NCA communicates the availability of these channels through visual aid such as posters, verbal announcements, and group discussion. sessions to capture diversified audience, including children, illiterate, and persons with disability. Messages are adopted to the local languages of the contexts.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

NCA provides information about PSEA in diversified methods to suit the audience of the information, including persons with disabilities and those with literacy inabilities. NCA produces IEC materials such as posters and billboards educating the audience on methods of protection from sexual abuse and exploitation. NCA also has a code of conduct against SEA which is available to all partners and key stakeholders. Women, men, girls, and boys are engaged in focused group discussions to create awareness about SEA and reporting mechanism. During counselling sessions, survivors of GBV are briefed about SEA and preventive measures to SEA. Staff will be made aware about their expected conduct while working at the community level. All NCA and partners staff will be trained in different polices on code of conduct and safeguarding children. Beneficiaries will be informed about their right to complain if any member of the staff or partner staffs does not uphold their values, breach any policies according to the expectations of the community and according to the employment guidelines and policies.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

NCA has a Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) at the country level which highlights communication modalities with communities. The CRM has contact details of focal points at field, country and Head Quarter level where complaints are handled. There is a working email and telephone contacts for sensitive and non-sensitive complaints indicated in project information sheet. In areas where communication by telephone and email is not possible, NCA CRM focal points will consult with beneficiaries on best alternative channels for communication. Before and during project implementation, beneficiaries are provided with information about the project, details about its scope, funding, objectives, including selection criteria of the beneficiaries. At the services point, there will be a feedback and response mechanism desk that is easily accessible and feedback box where beneficiary can drop how they feel about the project. GBV survivors receive feedback during counselling sessions, follow up visits and at the centre during the recreational activities sessions by engaging women.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

NCA has a safe, confidential, and culturally appropriate and accessible reporting channels for staff and beneficiaries. Available options for reporting are clearly communicated to beneficiaries in diversified formats and languages including verbal communications, use of pictorials posters, banners, during focused group discussions, and individual counselling sessions. NCA has trained its staff on the Act Alliance code of conduct and child safeguarding policy and always encourages staff to do refresher on-line trainings monthly. NCA intends to hold refresher trainings for the existing implementing partners on Act alliance code of conduct and child safeguarding policies. has internal reporting channels that are safe and accessible for staff including, emails, the whistle blower sites with accessibility options for persons with disabilities. At the community level, NCA has IEC materials translated into the local languages. NCA has also carried out a conflict analysis of the area of our project implementation which gives us a very good understanding of the social , cultural, and political dimensions . This will help NCA to adopt conflict sensitive approaches such as do no harm policy in the execution of GBV and other programmes. In addition, NCA has a strong working relationship across all humanitarian clusters including government line ministries and GBV sub working group, child protection, general protection, Wash cluster, food security and livelihood, among others, at all levels, through participatory cluster meetings, reporting on GBViMs outcomes, joint cluster needs assessments. NCA participates in establishment and strengthening of the referral systems with its partners and always creates awareness to beneficiaries about the available referral services. Through safe, confidential, and accessible referral systems, NCA has been able to refer survivors of GBV and other vulnerable population groups to service providers for life-saving services like clinical management of rape and safe maternal care services. Periodically, the established feedback and response mechanisms are evaluated for effectiveness and efficiency. Beneficiaries are consulted during establishment of these channels for the best options that suit their contexts, continually NCA seeks beneficiaries ‘feedback about the effectiveness of these channels and necessary changes are adopted based on the gaps identified. NCA has trained its staff on data safety and security as well as confidentiality. A particular staff has been assigned to manage the GBViMs which is password-controlled. All survivor’s information that is in hard copy is kept in files and locked up in a cabinet. Survivor’s personal information is entered using codes with no disclosure of names. Additionally, all staff including implementing partners’ case workers and community mobilizers are trained on case management and GBV case management operating procedures, including the GBV guidelines for case management adopted from IASC and UNFPA GBV guidelines for case management tool kits. NCA is committed to maintaining zero tolerance to corruption and fraud through training and creating an awareness to its staff and implementing partners, contractors, affiliates on the anti-corruption and fraud policy and signing it. The staff are obliged to maintain this standard in their direct service to the communities according to the reporting systems in place for reporting corruption and fraud.
