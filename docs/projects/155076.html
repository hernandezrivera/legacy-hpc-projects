Project Summary


Food Security: Is specific to improve the FS status of the Refugees in camps and most vulnerable host community. Will help to buy nutritious food and other basic things.
Food Assistance: Is about distribution of nutritious hot meal to targeted vulnerable groups such as pregnant and lactating women, children, adolescent, elderly, and sick persons. Each day beneficiaries will receive one meal (lunch). Every day approx. 5000 (500 person/day/kitchen) beneficiaries will receive meals through 10 community kitchens. People supporting the kitchen operations like cook will be from host community and supported through CFW. They will get daily wage BDT 350 per day. ACF guidelines will be put in place and close monitoring and supervision ensured. ACF plans to increase kitchens from 10 to 12.
Cash distribution: Cash distribution comprises of Conditional cash grants (CFW and Livelihood Cash grant):
Rohingya refugee and extreme poor host community people having ability of physical work will be engaged in CFW program for immediate income generation. CFW program will support- WASH facilities construction and rehabilitation, palasiting the hill side, small bamboo bridge construction, community kitchen working, babysitting service, garbage collection and cleaning of common areas. Each CFW beneficiary will get BDT 350 per day and in total BDT 4200 (12 days @ BDT 350/day) in a month. Livelihoods Cash Grants will be provided to the most vulnerable HH living in host villages. This is the 50% of the target of the year 2018. HH assets analysis, Livelihoods option identification and beneficiary business plan development based on Multiple Livelihood Strategy for alternative and adaptive livelihoods options will be done before Cash Grants distribution. IGA business plan development, training and technical assistance will be given. Each of them will get BDT 12000 from this project for one time. Mapping of market actors and linkage establishment with the beneficiaries and initiation of value chain development will be introduced.
Food production: Food production intervention will focus on Home gardening/kitchen gardens, small livestock, poultry and other off farming activities. This target is based on the observation of the investment done by the cash transfer beneficiaries reached in 2018. Training and technical assistance in collaboration from government departments will be provided to beneficiaries. Cash/in kind support (for quality seeds, tools for gardening) will be provided to targeted beneficiaries for homestead based food production. The project will establish the demonstration gardens through Model mothers. For this purpose each model mother will get incentives @ 6100BDT and 03 days training on homestead base food production. These model mothers will train others, in total 2750 (250X11) HH will be trained. Introduction of “Nutritious Food Card” linked with homestead based food production will contribute to increase dietary diversity.
Skills Development: It will emphasize on capacity building to increase the technical skills. Govt. agencies will be involved for technical training. Trainees will be selected based on their existing knowledge, skill and interest and in line with that will be engaged in different IGA.

Needs

In August 2017, increasing violence in Myanmar forced Rohingya population to flee to the neighbouring Bangladesh. WFP food security vulnerability classification of recent vulnerability assessment (REVA) showed that 81.4% of new arrivals are vulnerable (highly vulnerable – 58.2% and vulnerable- 23.2%), 68.7% of old refugees are vulnerable (highly vulnerable – 45.6% and vulnerable- 23.1%) and among the local host community 38% are vulnerable (highly vulnerable – 12.5% and vulnerable- 25.5%). About 50% of the host population has traditionally relied on wage labour in the agricultural sector, but successive disasters (a cyclone, flash floods, landslides) has severely depleted the ability to work in the agricultural land. This has been compounded by the influx of refugees, which has led to both the reduction of agricultural land and a labour surplus, driving wages down, and an increase in the price of basic food and non-food items. Action Against Hunger carried out a Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) in May 2018 with both Bangladeshi and Rohingya cash supported beneficiaries in Ukhiya Upazilla of Cox’s Bazar district. The PDM reveals that the cash assistance was critical for the beneficiaries as it contributed in quality and quantity of food, improved access pregnant women to health centres for delivery, meet the cost of medicines, retrofitting house infrastructure, debt repayment, spend on education for their children, and invest in small business to make cash income. Cash assistance helped reduce the need to rely on negative coping strategies, such as reduce food consumption. Host population do not have access to humanitarian aid. This situation made them vulnerable as like as Rohingya refugees. This is why the humanitarian response must focus on timely provision of food assistance, but also on social and economic empowerment of women and most vulnerable households in the host communities and Rohingya through social safety net program and home based Food production.Low household income (total reliant on humanitarian aid), inappropriate maternal and health practice and limited access to safe water , personal hygiene and sanitation facility are consider underlying causes affecting the nutritional status of Rohingya community. Only 57.5% of children 6-23 month get >3 meal per day (minimum meal frequency) while 12% met the minimum meal diversity. The minimum acceptable diet is low (7.6%) attributed to one of the immediate cause of malnutrition. According to the needs identified through different surveys and assessments, ACF has designed its Joint Response Plan to meet the needs of each target groups through Food assistance (complimentary cooked food),Conditional cash and grants Food production and skill development for meet up the basic requirements including nutritious food. The intervention will target both of the Rohingya Refugees in camps and most vulnerable host community in Cox’s Bazar district.

Activities or outputs

FOOD SECURITY: Food Security intervention will be specific attention to improve the food security status of the Rohingya Refugees in camps and most vulnerable host community. Food Assistance: Nutritious hot meal distribution in the outreach spontaneous sites through Community Kitchen. Distribution of nutritious hot meal to the specially targeted vulnerable groups such as pregnant and lactating women, children, and adolescent, elderly, and sick persons. Each day targeted beneficiaries (Pregnant and Lactating Women-PLWs, children, adolescent girls and elderly persons) will receive one meal (lunch time). Every day approximately 5000 community people (both Rohingya and local (approx. 500 person/day/kitchen) through the installation of 10 community kitchen. The persons working for these kitchens for food preparation, cooking and serving will be engaged from the surrounding community. Cash distribution: Cash distribution comprises of Conditional cash grants (CFW and Livelihood Cash grant). Rohingya refugee and extreme poor host community people having ability of physical work will be engaged in CFW program for immediate income generation. Through the engagement in CFW, they will get BDT 350 per day. Each of them will get BDT 4200 from this project (12 days @ BDT 350 per day) in a month. Livelihoods Cash Grants will be provided to the most vulnerable household living in host villages to ensure a regular income generation throughout the year. This is the 50% of the target of the year 2018. Each of them will get BDT 12000 from this project for one time. Mapping of market actors and linkage establishment with the beneficiaries and initiation of value chain development will be introduced for beneficiaries. Food production: Food production intervention will focus on Home gardening/kitchen gardens, small livestock, poultry and other off farming activities. This target is based on the observation of the investment done by the cash transfer beneficiaries reached in 2018. Training and technical assistance in collaboration from government departments will be provided to beneficiaries. The project will establish the demonstration gardens through Model mothers. For this purpose each model mother will get incentives @ 6100BDT and 03 days training on homestead base food production. Thus, model mothers will become a local trained resource person and will provide training and technical support to the neighbouring 2750 (250X11) targeted neighbour households for homestead based food production. This initiative will contribute to increase dietary diversity of the project beneficiaries. Skills Development: The project will give emphasis more on capacity building to increase the technical skills. Project will seek support from the government technical vocational institutions to engage the beneficiaries to receive training. Action against Hunger will establish linkage with vocational institution to support training of beneficiaries. Project will identify the persons based on their existing knowledge, skill and interest. According to their capacities and skills, they will be engaged in different Income Generating Activities based on their choice for regular income generation profitably.
