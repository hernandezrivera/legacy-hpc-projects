Project Summary

This project aims to support conflict-affected populations as well as medical and social workers in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts by contributing to a reduced risk of infection with COVID-19 and improving access to medical care.
The protracted conflict in Eastern Ukraine continues to have a negative impact on medical institutions (MIs, including hospitals and health care centers) and social institutions (SIs, including those working with the elderly, disabled persons or orphans), which are highly dependent on scarce public funding. In the GCA and NGCA alike, insufficient funding is leading to a lack of essential equipment and consequently reduces MIs’ and SIs’ capacity to provide necessary (and often lifesaving) care to the most vulnerable .
The already dire situation has been exacerbated by the severe consequences of the COVID-19 pandemic. Institutions across the targeted intervention area need personal protective equipment (PPE) to reduce the risk of infections among their staff and avoid undue limitations of their critical work. Furthermore , limited awareness of COVID-19 preventive behaviour and a lack of social distancing increase the risk of transmission for the general population.
Through this project, IOM will provide medical and other equipment to MIs and Sis based on a thorough needs assessment to be jointly conducted with local authorities; In addition, IOM will distribute PPE to MI and SI employees and social workers in the targeted institutions, further to raising awareness on the risks of COVID-19 as well as preventive behaviour among SI staff; and provide health-related equipment and support to Entry-Exit Checkpoints (EECPs) to reduce the risk of infection. IOM has already provided PPE to EECPs and is currently providing medical equipment to Stanitsa Luganska. The proposed activities are a scale up of current interventions in light of gaps identified and the continued threat of COVID 19.
IOM will primarily target SI staff for PPE assistance given their engagement with vulnerable COVID 19 groups including the elderly, people with disabilities and people with chronic diseases. Their ability to act as vectors places these groups at extreme risk of contracting COVID 19. This concern was raised by the Ministry of Social Policy to IOM as the earliest cases within their staff was contracted on a home visit and subsequently spread due to lack of proper equipment.
Further, regional authorities and assessments conducted with SI indicated that there was a need for small scale life support equipment at SIs to assist residents struggling to breathe until appropriate medical care is able to reach them. Due to the nature of the groups in residential care (elderly, individuals with severe disabilities and chronic diseases) they are more vulnerable to the impact and reportedly less of a priority to move as they are already in care.
EECP will also be targeted due to the continued concern over them acting as vectors. Humanitarian partners have consistently raised concerns over the management of crossings and lack of sufficient assistance. This places border guards (SBG) and those crossing at risk which is a high percentage of elderly in normal times. The ability of EECP staff to ensure safer practices and have life saving equipment on site increases their ability to better manage the needs and safety of vulnerable groups crossing the LoC. As such EECPs will be provided with PPE and other equipment gaps identified during previous assessments, these have included defibrillators and other emergency primary care equipment and relevant first aid and trauma care training. IOM assessments identified that despite the presence of humanitarian first aid points the working hours are insufficient and ambulances can take over half an hour to arrive. As such, SBG need both training and equipment to manage the needs outside of humanitarian first aid points working hours.

1 - Needs

IOM’s observations in the field as well as discussions with implementing partners show that the already difficult situation in MIs and SIs has been further impacted by the COVID-19 pandemic and is drastically deteriorating. Field assessments indicate that medical facilities along the line of contact as well as some feldsher points outside urban areas continue to operate in limited capacity. Should the operational abilities of these facilities, which often provide crucial support to vulnerable groups such as elderly and those with chronic diseases, be further impacted, it would mostly affect communities that are already vulnerable . A recent needs assessment carried out as part of HelpAge’s ACCESS IV project showed that more than 73% of elderly people in the GCA already have issues with access to medical facilities and medicines with more of 99% of those interviewed indicating they had existing chronic diseases. Furthermore, 86.97 per cent of older people (64.2 per cent of women) have limited mobility which brings with it its own health concerns. The lack of capacity at medical facilities specifically in the way ofa lack of essential medical equipment (including electronic cardiographs, microscopes, autoclaves, hot-air sterilizers, ultrasonography machines, basic hemoanalyzers, hemodialysis systems) as well as non-medical equipment hampers their ability to support populations. Both large hospitals and remote centers outside urban areas are affected. The former include hospitals designated for COVID-19 response and experiencing severe overloads, while the latter continue to be of significant importance to rural populations as feldsher points provide crucial first aid in case of emergency, and offer examinations and treatment for those who are unable to travel outside their area of residence due to financial or physical circumstances. The protracted crisis, in combination with the COVID-19 pandemic thus further limit the access of most vulnerable community members to adequate medical assistance . Elderly population and people with disabilities are among the most affected by the lack of adequate healthcare and at risk of COVID 19 however, single headed households or large households are also at risk of increased health concerns due to an incapacitated system. The out-of-pocket costs of medical treatments and the absence of modern high quality equipment in the Mis and Sis lead to negative coping strategies such as self-medication or not seeking medical attention at all which increases health concerns later on that the under equipped systems have to manage (Reach protection assessment, 2019). Similarly, a majority of the SIs involved in the COVID-19 response are severely underfunded and unable to procure relevant equipment. The lack of such equipment (including oxygenators , ventilation systems for lungs, testing materials, and no-contact thermometers), has been identified as an emerging need by the facilities and by the local and de facto authorities. Thus, providing MIs and SIs with priority equipment will address imminent healthcare needs of targeted communities and increase the capacity of institutions to provide critical lifesaving services to their patients and communities at large. In addition to this equipment, IOM’s intervention proposes the provision of PPE to MIs and SIs. A lack of PPE among these institutions has consistently been identified by the Health, WASH and Protection clusters and does not only pose a risk to the health of the staff members but also facilitates the spread of COVID-19 to beneficiaries and threatens the disruption of services.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

Outcome: Conflict-affected communities as well as medical and social workers in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts benefit from reduced risk of infection with COVID-19 and receive improved medical care . Output 1: MIs and SIs have their capacities strengthened to facilitate their service delivery to targeted communities Activities for output 1:• Assessment and identification of potential benefitting institutions in coordination with relevant local counterparts;• Procurement and distribution of equipment through an open competitive process;• Post-distribution monitoring on support provided. Output 2: MIs and SIs are equipped with COVID-19 PPE kits to avoid undue risks to staff and interruptions in the provision of support Activities for output 2:• Assessment and identification of potential benefitting institutions in coordination with relevant local counterparts;• Procurement and distribution of COVID-19 PPE, including protective overalls and respirators, through an open competitive process;• Post-distribution monitoring on support provided. Output 3: MI/SI staff and the affected population have increased awareness of the risks of COVID-19 as well as of preventive behaviour Activities for output 3:• Assessment of needs for information and awareness raising on the risks of COVID-19 infection among the targeted MIs and SIs;• Identification of groups of staff at the selected institutions with support from the representatives of the institutions.• Identification of appropriate methodologies and development of curricula on awareness raising for MI/SI staff, e.g. refreshers for previously trained staff, and for the public, e.g. engagement through posters and other printed material;• Carry out awareness raising trainings for MI/SI staff.• Procure and distribute printed awareness raising materials.• Post-distribution monitoring on support provided. Output 4: Newly established EECPs in Luhansk oblast (GCA only) have increased capacity to provide first aid and avoid the spread of COVID-19 through enhanced crowd management and trainings; and border guards in all EECPs have increased access to appropriate PPEs. Activities for output 4:• In coordination with relevant authorities, identify EECPs in need of PPE support and the items and equipment which meet the needs of the EECPs.• Identify best practices on how to avoid the spread of COVID-19 and train border guards• Procure and distribute crowd management and first aid material as well as PPEs through an open competitive process.• Post-distribution monitoring on support provided NGCA DFA have been contacted for assistance however have indicated they are not in need so all EECP assistance will be provided on the GCA side. IOM will target newly established Zolote checkpoint for equipment assistance primarily but also continue engagement with other checkpoints in both Donestk and Lugansk oblast to assist with their life support and PPE equipment needs. IOM will leverage our engagement with State Boarder Guards through our IBM programme as well as relationships established by the Emergency & Stabilitation unit in our previous humanitarian border management (HBM) activities. IOM has already leveraged support to 5 EECPs both with COVID 19 response and HBM training and equipment support in the past. To ensure accountability to the population, IOM operates a number of feedback mechanisms which allows beneficiaries to contact IOM by phone, email and through a global accountability platform. Information on the mechanisms are distributed to beneficiaries of IOM projects and a dedicated team are responsible for investigating claims and providing feedback to complainants. Post distribution monitoring by IOM M&E teams are further mechanisms through which beneficiaries can provide feedback. Feedback is incorporated through lessons learnt processes to ensure that programmes going forward are better able to meet the needs of the populations.

False information about the risks of the virus as well as on preventive measures is also widely spread. Awareness raising on these issues for both the general public and for MI/SI staff, who are especially vulnerable because of the difficult working conditions, would therefore be crucial. The EECPs are vital entry points for numerous IDPs and people living in the NGCA for having access to their pensions and bank accounts, basic health services, education and/or visiting family relatives . The full operation and the smooth processing at these EECPs is crucial for many people on both sides of the contact line. From 2019 to 2020, the number of people crossing the LoC at the five existing EECPs has dropped by 90 percent (Humanitarian Needs Analysis 2021, 2020). It has however been observed that there is an unnecessarily high risk of contracting COVID-19 for those who do cross. This is due to a lack of crowd management at EECP, resulting in insufficient social distancing among those crossing, which include many elderly people. Border guards, in particular in the soon to be opened EECPs in Luhansk oblast, would benefit from such material support as well as best practice trainings on how to prevent the further spreading of COVID-19. In addition, border guards themselves are at risk of contracting and transmitting COVID-19 due to a lack of PPEs at all EECPs.
