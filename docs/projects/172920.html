1 - Needs

The nutrition situation in Sudan has been characterized by persistent high levels of household food insecurity and undernutrition since the first S3M survey conducted in 2013. Based on data available (S3M II 2018) 36.6 per cent of children less than five years of age are stunted (short for their age). The national prevalence rate of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) was recorded at 14.1 per cent , which places Sudan above the emergency threshold of 15 per cent according to the WHO criteria. Over the years, there has been substantial attention to the provision of nutrition treatment services in conflict-affected states, however, most Sudan’s acutely malnourished children (52 per cent) live in nine non-conflict affected states. An estimated 3.6 million children are suffering from wasting annually, of whom around 330,000 SAM and 1.1 million MAM will be targeted through outpatient therapeutic and supplementary feeding programmes, respectively. The deteriorating macroeconomic situation in Sudan is worsening household food and nutrition insecurity for all Sudanese, particularly for the most vulnerable families and children. The Sudan’s food and nutritional security are eroding, deepening a crisis already affecting 14 per cent of the population. Over 574,000 children require treatment for severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and the operational costs of reaching these children are rising. An additional 2.2 million children need moderate acute malnutrition treatment. Additionally, COVID-19 has exposed additional vulnerabilities, with nearly 14,000 reported cases, over 800 heaths, and a disproportionately high case fatality rate outside of Khartoum.according to the S3M (ii) result South Darfur and East Darfur has WFH GAM rate of 16.43%, and 16.88% above WHO cut of point (15%), while South Kordufan and Kassala WFH GAM rate were 8.13% and 8.12% and with aggravation factors of IDPs and Refugees, and the increasing Food security issues they are in high need of Nutrition services support. SENS was conducted for South Sudanese refugees in settlements in South Kordofan State, Sudan to assess the general health and nutrition status of the refugee population, and formulate workable recommendations for appropriate nutritional and public health interventions. Results indicate critical (>15%) global acute malnutrition (GAM), with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) above emergency levels (>2%). The findings also point to the wider effects of chronic under-funding for the response, especially for WASH, health and livelihoods assistance.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project will be will contribute in environmental and natural resource protection; CARE ensures that 100% of supported Nutrition and Health facilities, are implementing environmental protection measures; reliable sanitation services, safe waste disposal Standard Operating Procedures, and maintaining functioning incinerators. CARE will ensure all the relevant staff (especially nurses and cleaners) are trained in infection control and waste management, using the safety boxes to transport waste to the incinerators. in facilities where there is no incinerators, CIS will construct quality incinerator to ensure proper medical waste management. also CIS is promoting planting of trees at health facilities, and operation areas.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

CARE will take extra caution in analyzing the protection of at risk groups, includingchildren and girls. Efforts will include: no creation of new risks, address discrimination,analysis of the most vulnerable, safety and dignity in service delivery, orientation andfamiliarization on humanitarian principles and rights, and promoting empowerment.Furthermore, CARE and partners will exercise principles of protection mainstreaming:meaningful access through consultation and involvement of the communities about thebest way to deliver the services and considerations for vulnerable groups, accountabilitymechanisms and Field visits. CARE and partners will empower communities by training &capacity building & awareness raising about key protection concerns and risks, consultingthem in decision making process, identifying volunteers and establishing and trainingcommunity committees. Strengthening community protection mechanisms and improvingaccess to services through information dissemination on available services, goodselection criteria and identification of the most vulnerable and referrals will be prioritized.Do no harm analysis will be carried for each sector and the activities will be screened notto enhance risks for all especially women, girls and children. staff and volunteers will ensure screening of all children boys and girls during the HH visit and nutrition and health facilities; also Facilities such as latrines and water points, income generating activities and trainings will be analyzed anddiscussed with at risk groups to ensure that the beneficiaries are protected and that theservices are easily accessible and safely usable by all. Protection risks faced by women and girls, and in particular S/GBV risks, will be the specific focus of capacity building forstaff and partners to ensure that they are able to detect cases of abuse. Staff will also beoriented in order to make sure that programming does no harm and that they are able torespond safely and appropriately to protection cases when they are reported, using aconfidential and survivor centered approach to disclosures of violence. An internalmechanism for reporting complaints and ensuring that required measure are in place willbe promoted throughout the implementation period

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

CARE will be working and coordinating with all interagency partners to ensure accountability maintained for the affected people including SEA and abuse complaints. CARE has Measurement in place to protect beneficiaries and communities from sexual exploitation and abuse as the following: - all CARE staff has signed letter that explicitly prohibit SEA of beneficiaries by staff and staff of partners (such as NNGO, contractors, vendors, suppliers, etc.). and CARE has policies and training package that have been disseminated to staff and well understood amongst staff; all CARE staff Has received training and orientation on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse; CARE has also appointed PSEA focal persons at country and state level, whom are doing, regular in person support and follow with the team members. CIS has reporting mechanism in place for reporting PSEA, at field level, CARE Medical assistants in charge of the clinic are the focal person at the Field level, whom work closely with all his/her departments staff to ensure PSEA policy are implemented and any cases related to PSEA are reported timely to CARE focal person at state level.
