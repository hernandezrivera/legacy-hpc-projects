Project Summary

In response to the displacements in Hiran region due to flooding, HIWA is proposing a multi-sectoral response with mobile teams providing combined Protection support services to both IDPs and host communities.
In the protection sector, HIWA will provide emergency protection services to vulnerable, newly displaced boys and girls in Beletweyne, in both IDP and host communities.
To ensure the most vulnerable are reached, HIWA will target 6,100 newly displaced women, men, girls and boys. This project will fill gaps in service provision where government and humanitarian support is currently insufficient. Provision of services in general and protection services in particular have been significantly challenged by the limited access. As a result, HIWA’s multi-sectorial integrated approach aims to support the current situation in Beletweyne while preparing for the possible influx of people following continued prolonged rain.
HIWA will implement activities in Beletweyne methodologies and approaches will be validated and lead by HIWA Protection Coordinator and HIWA field monitoring team to ensure harmony in the conduction of assessments and evaluation of needs. The project is a scale up of existing support being funded by UNICEF based on the successful implementation of child protection services in Beletweyne since 2012.
Through the establishment and the training of community child protection committees and School Management Committees, the resources of the community will be mobilized around the schools. Gender equality will be fostered in the composition of the committees. Each trained committees will be provided with material inputs to sustain the school of their community through specific agreed upon activities.
The awareness campaigns as well as the hygiene and sanitation promotion activities will be designed and organized with the active involvement of teachers, committees members and pupils, taking into consideration the specific needs of girls and boys.

Needs

According to OCHA flash report #2 more than 427,000 people have been affected as of 26 April and of these nearly 175,000 have been displaced as a result of the flash and river flooding in Hirshabelle, South West and Jubaland states as well as Banadir region, according to data collected by humanitarian partners. Assessments are ongoing to determine the impact of flooding in affected areas. In Hiraan region of Hirshabelle State, an estimated 122,580 people have been displaced in Beletweyne town and surrounding riverine villages after the Shabelle River burst its banks and inundated houses and crops. Some of the affected were already internally displaced. Immediate needs include water, shelter, food, The lack of latrines in the new settlements camps are potential protection risk to girls and woman, access is limited due to the large number of people accessing the few facilities, going out at night also poses a higher risk due to darkness in the new settlement. The need for emergence latrines in the target communities particularly in schools and for the IDPs in the new sites has remained unaddressed. The possibility is that the population will stay longer at these sites due to the fear of possible floods reoccurring due to the expected rains. There is also need to rehabilitate the collapsed public latrines and toilets in IDPs where people have started to return. The dirty/stagnant water is expected to dry soon. However, scattered faeces and dump garbage found everywhere in the town and in IDP camp is posing a health hazard to the population. There is urgent need for Garbage collection and cleaning of the town. The proposed food for work could be used to address this plus direct intervention by WASH cluster. While the Households in the IDP sites are benefiting from the ongoing water tracking the need to rehabilitate the drinking water sources is urgent to provide sustainable solution. In the short run the water tracking also needs to continue for at least several months.

Activities

a) Provision of portable solar lanterns to 500 of the most vulnerable IDP households who are most at risk to all forms of gender violationsb) Provision of dignity kits to 500 GBV survivors and foster families supporting unaccompanied and separated children.c)Training of community dialogue leaders on facilitation Skills using UNICEF Toolkit Part 4 (Dialogue Facilitation Skills) and GBV UNICEF toolkit part one 8 Community dialogue facilitators (CDFs) will be trained on how to facilitate peace building dialogue sessions with the peace building and gender dimensions.d)In coordination with sub cluster leads conduct rapid protection assessments in Beletweyne to identify and document urgent protection risks and needs faced by women, men, girls and boys, including CP/GBV related issues, as well as grave violations of human rights especially of children and women, in line with the Rapid Protection Assessment (RPA) mechanism. Beletweyne had been prone to both drought and flooding during the rainy seasons.e)Develop, obtain, print and distribute through teams of CP Outreach Workers, 5000 IEC materials on grave violations against children/CPiE, mine/IED risk, IDTR for UASC, referral pathways and available services in the local language.f)Service mapping in Beletweyne in close coordination with sub cluster leads to strengthen the existing coordination mechanisms and in sites where there no existing structures, HIWA will establish active structures which will be governed by Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs). The structures will be identified through camp leaders and existing projects staffs. This will help in the identifying needs and duplication of services. HIWA will ensure at least 5 community structures exist in the vast IDP settlements in with specific sector task assigned to each structure. 5 groups comprising of 8 members will be trained and strengthened as part of the community based structures establishment.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

First, the increase in gender-based violence, including sexual exploitation and human trafficking, and child protection issues is one of the most serious. This problem is usually linked to the establishment of temporary workers camps or to a significant increase of displaced population coming from other areas, which often occurs due to conflict or natural disaster like floods and drought. During the implementation of the project HIWA has codes of conduct in place, community information and awareness campaigns, as well as the stregthening of the health services are all measures that have been effective in mitigating these threats.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

HIWA works under do not harm principle so as to reduces risks in its project implementation. Always GBV survivors are given maximum security by ensuring confidentiality of their information and this helps in reducing risk exposure to GBV survivors. HIWA has strong presence in the areas of its operation.we have aclose working relationship with the authorities so as to ensure the safety of both the staffs and beneficiaries during service delivery. HIWA has policies in place where every project staff has read and sign those policies like Child protection policy,anti bribery and anti fraud policy, code of conduct and other commitments.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

HIWA has strong presence in the areas of its operation.we have a close working relationship with the authorities so as to ensure the safety of both the staffs and beneficiaries during service delivery. We're focusing our efforts on the most socially vulnerable and on those who are most exposed to violence. Among other initiatives, we are training people on peace building and resiliance, improving access to health care, promoting the necessity to send children in schools for them to learn, and supporting and strengthening the existing community based discussion and dialogues so as to mitigate risks and impact of armed conflict and violence.
