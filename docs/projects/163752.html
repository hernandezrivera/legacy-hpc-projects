Project Summary

Common Operational Datasets or CODs, are authoritative reference datasets needed to support operations and decision-making for all actors in Libya humanitarian response. CODs are 'best available' datasets that ensure consistency and simplify the discovery and exchange of key data.
In Libya and since 2017, UNFPA has been providing UN-OCHA with COD-PS based on best available population data extending technical support to the Bureau of Statistics and Census. Over the last three years, the quality of Libya COD-PS has improved although no recent census has been conducted since 2006.
In 2019, the Libyan government has authorized the Bureau of Statistics and Census to conduct a national demographic survey. Through this project, UNFPA has agreed to provide technical assistance to the Libyan authorities to make sure that the data is collected according to the standard-defined metadata for SDGs and with full disaggregation when relevant to the target, in accordance with the Fundamental Principles of Official Statistics.
The demogrpahic survey will not only update the COD-PS for Libya but will also help bridging the gap between humanitarian and development data and facilitate the multi-year humanitarian planning involving local and national authorities.
The data will be fully disagregated and geo-spatially linked using a coordinate system (especially administrative boundaries) and have unique geographic identification codes (P-codes).

1 - Needs

The humanitarian crisis in Libya is largely defined by an environment of deepening vulnerability for the population at large, with a rapidly deteriorating economy and public sector and recurrent pockets of acute needs prompted by outbreaks of violence, shifts in territorial control of the local power of armed groups.This complex context is rooted in a protection, basic services, economic and governance crisis that has engulfed the country since 2011. The ability of humanitarian community to assess, analyze and response to the needs of the affected population is fruther hampered by the lack of reliable quantitative data that is recognized by local, national and international agencies.In absence of reliable information produced by Libyan institutions, humanitarian responders have made use of diverse sources of data to identify priority needs, and understand coping strategies to plan their interventions. On another hand, documenting protection vulnerabilities especially human rights violations – such as gender-based violence – has proved to be challenging for Libyan stakeholders. The needs of some vulnerable people including women, boys, girls and people with disabilities needs to be reflected through representative sampling and protection-sensitive data collection processes. In order to ensure accountability of national and local stakeholders, stronger leadership and improved coordination, there is a need to ensure that humanitarian action is based on reliable and mutually agreed-upon sources of data and information.There are clearly limitations to the nationally-produced data (especially when it comes to protection-related displacement and migrants and refugees data). Hence, it's crucial to complement the official data with qualitative and vulnerability-based specific humanitarian data for a more comprehensive understanding of people needs and Libyan context.

2 - Activities

The projects consists of:- Provision of technical assistance: Through deployment of technical population and data experts to Libya (national and international) to strengthen BSC capacities to produce quality population data according to international standards.This include technical assistance during design of the demographic survey, data collection and exctraction of the COD-PS.- Coordination between national and international agencies involved in data collection: UNFPA will organize meetings involving the national statistic office, some technical data producers (in Libyan line ministries) and members of the Information management Working Grooup (coordinated by OCHA). Meetings will aim at coordinating data collection processes, setting common standards for different data-sets and agreeing on data sharing protocols.The project is building on UNFPA experience in conducting population surveys in Libya. The selected enumerators (mixed teams of both male and female enumerators) will visit houhseholds and interview both women and men while considering privacy and without exposing women to further harm. Questions will be designed based on previous testing and several rounds of data collection in a way that doesn't expose people-at-risk to further harm. Confidentiality will be ensured starting from the field data collection (anonymized questionnaires) to the data cleaning and analysis with special focus on protection-related data (non-identifiying data). The survey will allow for data to be disagregated by age, sex and disability.The COD-PS will capture all people considering their ages, gender, disability status, and their legal status (libyan/ non-Libyan). The data related to the legal status will be non-identifying considering its sensitivity.

4 - Protection risk analysis

- Risk of excluding some population groups (women, adolescents, non-libyans, stateless people);- Access challenges due to insecurity and political instability;- Risk of overlapping between the demographic survey and other humanitarian/ development surveys;- Risk of divergeance in baselines, P-codes or indicators metadata;- Risk of exposure of people identity or doing harm to some people -at-risk;- Risk of disagreement between different authorities (east and west, local and national);- Risk of sexual exploitation and abuse;

5 - Mitigation measures

- UNFPA has already agreed with Libyan BSC to have all population groups included. During the development of the questionnaire, further consultations will allow for defining the groups-at-risk and the measures needed to include them without doing harm; Enumerators will be selected from the local community and will include both nale and female enumerators;- UNFPA has been able to condcut population surveys in collaboration with the BSC in all areas in Libya (except Murzuq). In case of access constraints, UNFPA will provide technical assistance to BSC to use remote sensing technologies with best possible quality insurance mechanisms;- UNFPA will be actively participating to the IMWG to avoid mis-coordination;- UNFPA will actively seek coordination and consensus building for identifying common baselines and defining boundaries; When official boundaries between municipalities are absent, UNFPA will adopt the nationally and locally recongized statistical boudnaries (admin level 4);- Population data will be non-identifying, and the COD-PS will be anonymized; Data sharing protocols will be established to allow for protection of sensitive data;- The demographic survey is comended by the Libyan authorities. UNFPA will extend the existing agreements with the National Statistic Office. A technical coordination committee will be established involving all key national stakeholders including the Ministry of Local Governance with clear accountability lines;- According to UNFPA policies, all staff members working for UNFPA or its partners should recieve the PSEA training. UNFPA is actively engaged and fully committed to Zero tolerance policy to SEA.
