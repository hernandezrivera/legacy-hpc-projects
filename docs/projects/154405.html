Project Summary

In 2019, the humanitarian community in Iraq is prioritizing contingency planning for any new phase of humanitarian response that may emerge. The UN Humanitarian Country Team (UNHCT) has agreed an overarching strategic objective in relation to improving humanitarian response timeliness and capacity through preparedness-related interventions. To contribute to this objective, the Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) Consortium intends to enhance capacity of existing government structures and local authorities including the Joint Coordination and Monitoring Cell (JCMC), the Joint Consultative Committee (JCC), the Board of Relief and Humanitarian Affairs (BRHA) in Dahuk, the Erbil Refugee Committee (ERC), the Ministry of Migration and Displacement (MoMD), and Ministry of Labour and Social Affairs (MoLSA) and its relevant Departments to enable these to better cope with future emergencies, including taking over the responsibility for and operation of the RRM.
To facilitate this, the Consortium intends to conduct a feasibility study, including a risk analysis and capacity assessment, which will ascertain the feasibility of handover, identify relevant counterpart/s and guide the overall transition. The study will identify government capacity gaps in the areas of contingency planning, emergency preparedness and response, operational planning, supply chain management, prepositioning and logistics, monitoring, information management, and reporting. The assessment will provide a baseline for the capacity development actions, led by the RRM Consortium, to facilitate successful handover to the Government.

Needs

After the end of the military operations in Iraq in December 2017, there has been a significant reduction in new displacements and the overall need for RRM has therefore decreased. Despite reduction in mass new displacements in 2018, the RRM remains relevant in Iraq and has assisted those experiencing secondary displacements and inter-camp movements as well as representing a potential source of short-term assistance, in coordination with other Clusters’ efforts, for most vulnerable families who returning to their places of origin. The RRM has proven to be a reliable and swift-moving means of delivering immediate support to large populations spread over wide geographic areas and, recognising the unique role such a capacity can play in the context of Iraq’s continuing cycles of violence, there is value in supporting the Government to take responsibility for, and maintain, an RRM capacity of its own. In this light, the RRM Consortium intends to hand the mechanism over to the Government. During 2019, the Consortium intends to complete groundwork for transition and exit by building capacity of existing government structures and local authorities to enable them to better cope with future emergencies.

How is your project accountable to affected population

UNICEF is accountable to women, boys, girls and men whose lives it aims to improve and places this responsibility at the core of its humanitarian policy. Continued dialogue between UNICEF staff and affected populations is facilitated to ensure effective engagement of beneficiaries and timely information provision. Participation by IDPs and returnee communities enables them to play a role in decision-making processes that affect them through the design of UNICEF emergency interventions and the review and evaluation of programmes. UNICEF interventions are informed by needs assessment of affected populations to determine the appropriate responses. In addition, UNICEF has a strong working relationship with the Government Ministries and their Directorates at governorate level and ensures that these bodies remain the key duty bearers, alongside local non-government organisations (NGO) and civil society organisations (CSO), in the identification of services and supplies needed in the local area, and that these bodies remain accountable to the children and families they serve. UNICEF will ensure that this message is reinforced through the capacity building initiatives that will be undertaken in this project. Through its co-leadership of the Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) at national and sub-national levels, coordination with the World Food Programme (WFP) and the United Nations Populations Fund (UNFPA), and through in-field presence of technical staff from the RRM Consortium INGO and NNGO partners, UNICEF maintains strong and regular lines of communication both with partners and with beneficiaries, ensuring timely feedback into operational and strategic levels of decision-making. Involvement of RRM beneficiaries is at the ‘post distribution’ stage, where data is collected through a questionnaire administered to a sample group of IDPs to assess their satisfaction with the items they’ve received. Families receiving kits provide feedback on quality and timely assistance received through RRM partners. In 2017, the third-party evaluation conducted focus group discussions (FGD) with IDPs to review and revise kit contents. In addition to this post distribution monitoring and feedback mechanism, RRM partners set up a help desk during the distribution process to collect beneficiary complaints and feedback, which are regularly reviewed and addressed. UNICEF will ensure that these good practices are adopted by the government counterparts that will take over RRM.
