3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Considering the protection issues, the meaning of “Gender-Based Violence (GBV)” is any act that results in, or is likely to physical, sexual or psychological harm or suffering to women/ girls. This includes threats of violence, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of liberty, whether occurring in public or private or family in society. The gender-based violence (GBV) can be in the form as (i) Child marriage, (ii) Female genital mutilation, (iii) Honour killings/ dishonour in public forum, (iv) Trafficking for sex or slavery, (v) Intimate partner violence, (vi) Physical punishment or physical torture, (vii) Sexual, emotional or psychological violence and so on. Globally the most common form of violence is physical violence inflicted by an intimate partner. Violence against women/ girls not only constitutes a gross violation of human rights but also has enormous social and economic costs which affects the development of country and also the society/ family.

3.2 - Gender

Despite positive shifts in perceptions and behavioural practices around women’s economic participation, leadership, and empowerment in both Rohingya and host communities, lingering traditional gendered social norms and resistance to change remain . Among the Rohingya and host communities, 80.6% believe that men should work, and women should stay home for duty bearing responsibilities of children . In both communities, women generally experience barriers to freedom of movement and access to and control over resources/services , with girls’ access and mobility restricted once they reach puberty.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Under the AAP IASC framework, AGRAJATTRA & Tdh will implement activities following three main phases. (I) Taking account: the project team will involve beneficiaries at the beginning of the project to update the baseline data and to share all relevant information related to the project and to involve them in the decision-making process. (2) Giving account: AGRAJATTRA & Tdh will organize a series of consultations focused on sharing information with communities related to the progress of the project activities, especially during multisectoral community outreach sessions. (3) Being held to account: AGRAJATTRA & Tdh will organize formal and informal meetings at key moments of the project (start, annually and end line evaluation) to give the communities the opportunity to assess the project and to give positive and negative feedback related to the project performances.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes the project will have DRR and DRM elements as follows: (i) Reinforcing infrastructures to prevent, avoid or limit floods and protect the population (ii) Always keep an emergency kit ready, as well as enough supplies of food and drink to last the estimated length of the emergency, (iii) Permanent monitoring of the risk of flooding (through weather forecasts, information shared through NGOs groups, Emergency cell communication), (iv) Follow the emergency cell instructions, (v) Follow the Emergency Response Unit (ERU) instruction. (vi) Use the life jackets while travelling by boat in the field, (vii) Keep your gutter and drains clear and unblocked, (vii) Upon flood warning, (viii) Relocate chemicals, paints and insecticides to a safe high place. Switch-off and unplug all electrical appliances from power points, (ix) Beware of streams, drains and area known to flood suddenly, (x) Dry food storage for mitigating DRR and DRM.
