Project Summary

This project aims at building the resilience of the poorest, most-in-need and hardest-to-reach children. It targets 881 communities and 549,712 vulnerable children (50% girls and 2% children with disabilities) with below breakdown:
• IDPs & Returnees Children: 163,000
• Vulnerable Residents: 386,712
The project should provide integrated and sustainable assistance through interventions that promote resilience amongst affected populations and strengthen human security. The whole child approach using the Child Friendly Schools (CFS) framework needs to be clearly reflected with the following five thematic components: (i) Inclusiveness; (ii) Effective Teaching and Learning; (iii) Safe, Healthy and Protective Learning Environment; (iv) Gender Responsiveness; (v) Community and Child Participation.

1 - Needs

Since 2018, an estimated 4.2 million primary and secondary school-aged children in Sudan have been out of school. Additionally, due to COVID19 all schools in Sudan closed March 2020 and remained closed for an extended period with school reopening postponed until at least November, affecting approximately 9 million school children. It is expected that the most vulnerable children, particularly children living in areas affected by conflict, will not be able to return to school or to progress in school unless significant support is provided to mitigate against this long absence from school. In 2021, the UNICEF Education will therefore be scaling up its response to support 549,712 conflict and disaster-affected primary school-aged children (ages 6-13) to resume or continue their education in a safe and protective learning environment. Displaced children, out-of-school children, children from nomadic and pastoralist communities, and children with disabilities are most in need of this assistance.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The activities will be implemented under three outcomes as follows: Outcome 1: More out-of-school children, especially girls, marginalized groups and those in the most vulnerable situations, are enabled to access quality pre-primary, primary and alternative learning opportunities with zero tolerance to hazards To increase enrolment and reduce dropout of children from schools, multitude of interventions will be provided in a customized package based on actual underlying reasons. Target schools will be supported to enroll additional 76,923 Out of school children, to open ALP classrooms for 76,923 children and to establish ECE centres/ pre-school classrooms for 88,100 Children. Also, 10,994 Children with disabilities will be targeted in an integrated manner whether through training of teachers, providing specialized supplies, adding rams to Classrooms and latrines, etc... Schooling supplies will be provided in form of Teacher kits, student kits, Recreational Kits, ECD kits, dignity Kits for girls, uniform sets, books and school furniture for a total of 549,712 children to attend schools. Outcome 2: Individuals, Education providers, parents and communities have improved knowledge, skills and pro-social capacity to contribute to quality learning outcome. Building capacity of 881 communities and civil society to promote, enable and sustain behaviour change related to access to quality education. 13,215 PTA members will be trained on School Improvement Planning SIP before being supported with school grant (max 270,000 SDG per school) for income generating activity that will cover local school feeding, teachers’ incentives (ALP, Pre-School, School), school running costs and at least 50% reduction in school fees. At least 10,000 teachers will be trained on Teacher Preparedness Training Programme TPTP to oversee implementation of COVID-19 school protocols supported by school health clubs in each target schools. Outcome 3: More communities have strengthened mechanisms to protect children from violence, abuse, and exploitation including FGM/C and child marriage for inclusive, safe and protective school environments. The integrated approach of Education and Child Protection will be reflected in all education activities. It targets increasing awareness of 28,263 education personnel and community structures on the CP issues with signing of Code of Conduct (CoC). Moreover, safe referral pathways and complaint mechanisms between CBCPN, PTA and Education personnel will be established with estimated 10,000 cases referred to CP agencies/networks by education staff each month from the target 881 schools.
