Project Summary

The current security and humanitarian situation in South Sudan affected 299,162 refugees in South Sudan as of September 2019. The majority of them originated from Sudan (276,531) followed by the Democratic Republic of the Congo (16,055), Ethiopia (4,409) and the Central African Republic (2,041). They reside in 21 camps and urban locations in Upper Nile, Unity, Western Equatoria, Central Equatoria, and Jonglei regions. South Sudan maintains an open-door policy for refugees and grants those arriving from Sudan, the Democratic Republic of the Cong, and the Central African Republic prima facie refugee status. There are additionally 3,266 asylum-seekers from Ethiopia, Eritrea, Somalia, Burundi, and other countries waiting for a determination on their individual refugee claims. Daily border crossing continues with more than 8,000 new arrivals registered in 2018 (as of September 2019) from Blue Nile and South Kordofan States of Sudan.
Refugees reside in conflict-affected regions where security remains precarious with fragile relations with host communities. The continuous economic decline, weak rule of law, limited development support and lack of social services and infrastructure in refugee-hosting areas as well as the conditions in the refugees’ areas of origin mean that refugees will continue to rely on lifesaving humanitarian assistance and on social cohesion with and acceptance by the host community for protection.
Under the refugee response, all refugees and asylum seekers registered with UNHCR will benefit from various forms of protection interventions from UNHCR and its partners, including individual registration and documentation. Specific protection services, such as SGBV response and child protection will focus on the identified at-risk groups. While food, water, health and education services are available for all refugees living in camps, provision of other assistance will be undertaken in a targeted manner, focusing on persons with specific protection needs and vulnerabilities. This will primarily be achieved through participatory assessments, surveys as well as verification exercise to identify vulnerable refugees and their needs. Impact monitoring will be used to gauge the efficiency of projects implemented and for the purposes of course-correction in terms of better targeting of refugees in need of assistance. The needs of persons with disabilities and the elderly will be further mainstreamed in various multi-sector response services. Vulnerable refugees in urban settings will also continue to benefit from targeted protection interventions and facilitation of their access to national services.
UNHCR and partners will continue the delivery of protection and multi-sectoral assistance to refugees. UNHCR and partners will continue programs ensuring refugees’ access to healthcare services, education, WASH, livelihoods, food and NFI distribution. Access to reproductive health and HIV services will be ensured through complete ANC coverage, health facility deliveries and skilled health workers, as well as comprehensive HIV/AIDS programs allowing refugees to have access to ant-retroviral treatments (ART) through ART centers in all camps and the two referral hospitals.
Initiatives to strengthen SGBV prevention and response mechanisms and child protection will be further promoted. While facilitating traditional justice mechanisms, such as conflict resolution committees and joint customary courts, UNHCR will support the re-establishment of statutory courts in refugee sites.

1 - Needs

By the end of September 2019, South Sudan was hosting around 299,162 refugees, most of whom came as a result of the on-going fighting in South Kordofan and Blue Nile States in Sudan and were settled predominantly in 21 refugee locations and the vast majority of refugees reside in the remote locations of Unity and Upper Nile States. A smaller number of refugees from the Central African Republic, Democratic Republic of Congo and Ethiopia have been displaced on a protracted basis, and are mostly settled in Central and Western Equatoria States. All refugees in South Sudan are in need of international protection, multi-sectorial life-saving and basic assistance to enable them to live in safety and dignity. Ensuring the safety and security of refugees in South Sudan remains a fundamental concern and a major protection challenge. In recent participatory assessments, among main protection challenges refugees indicated insecurity and violence, presence of armed elements, recruitment of children, SGBV, insufficient food and education facilities, limited socio-economic opportunities and skills training as major protection concerns. Maintaining the civilian character of camps will necessitate increased advocacy with the government and other stakeholders. Refugees in camps rely heavily on multi-sectorial assistance and any scaling back of such basic assistance will immediately lead to the deterioration of the humanitarian situation of refugees to emergency levels. Children in urban and peri-urban Juba struggled to access education due to the closure of public schools because of limited government funding. Monitoring reports indicate refugees and the host community are confronted with precarious socio-economic situations with approximately 80% of host and refugee populations facing food scarcity, limited livelihood options, and the gap in the food supply. Competition for scarce resources has led to high tensions between refugee and host communities, further aggravating the situation for refugees.South Sudan remains one of the most complex humanitarian operations globally, with key infrastructure lacking, long heavy rainy seasons causing significant inaccessibility, localized conflict including inter-communal clashes and human rights violations ongoing in some parts of the country. The humanitarian situation has been exacerbated by lack of services including in the areas of education, food, infrastructure and health, and by limited humanitarian access. Of the 200,000 that have returned spontaneously data collected indicate that 52% of them have not been able to reach their places of origin; habitual residence or intended area of return due to continued conflict, with many homes, either destroyed or occupied. Not reaching their area of origin or habitual residence may result in key protection issues such as exposure to sexual exploitation, forced marriage, forced recruitment and negative coping mechanisms among others. Scarcity of resources, such as water, food, basic services and livelihoods may become a source of conflict between spontaneous refugee returnees and host communities.

2 - Activities

In 2020, UNHCR will continue to focus on ensuring access to territory (safety), reception, registration, status determination, and documentation and improving the humanitarian and civilian character of asylum. Among other things, it will be important to support the Government of South Sudan in developing standard operating procedures for the screening and processing of combatants in refugee camps. In line with government policy designed to protect the civilian nature of asylum, UNHCR will also aim to transfer new arrivals (est. 14,000) and the residual caseload in the Yida settlement to Pamir/Ajoung Thok camps. Services should be handed over to communities and local authorities by mid-2020. UNHCR will continue to promote peaceful co-existence with host communities and address the needs of children, women at risk, SGBV survivors, persons with specific needs. Other activities include; promoting access to justice, attaining complementary pathways for durable solutions for protracted cases.UNHCR will increase focus on promoting durable and transitional solutions. Key elements of the strategy include; 1) Focusing on community-based solutions, 2) Promoting peaceful coexistence between spontaneous refugee returnees and receiving communities using quick impact projects and supporting community self-management. UNHCR will continue to co-lead with RRC the coordination and response of systematic returnee monitoring, strengthen regional data collection, build upon intention surveys conducted in countries of asylum and apply a community-based assistance strategy with targeted support for extremely vulnerable. UNHCR will not facilitate, promote or encourage returns to South Sudan as long as UNHCR’s non-return policy remains valid.UNHCR will continue to periodically review its strategy in view of the evolving situation in terms of political stability and security situation in the country. UNHCR will also continue life-saving assistance include;• Facilitation of essential services in the area of health, nutrition, WASH, education and camp management to meet refugees' basic needs. • NFI distribution will target new arrivals and most vulnerable households including female-headed households. Shelter support will be limited to emergency shelter for new arrivals and provision of transitional shelters to the most vulnerable. • Maintenance and upgrade of service infrastructures, including schools, health facilities, water distribution, shelters as well as access roads and airstrips. • Use of renewable energy will be pursued for better sustainability of services and environment protection. • Protection monitoring, analysis, training, and advocacy, addressing various protection concerns related to men, boys, women and girls of different ages and backgrounds. Activities will also focus on bolstering community protection mechanisms. • Multi-sectoral GBV prevention and response, including installation of solar lights, information management, awareness raising, psychosocial counseling and referral services.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

UNHCR will continue advocacy and interventions for legal counseling and legal assistance to survivors of crimes, exploitation and violence, particularly those of SGBV and will closely work with partners, government, traditional refugee and host community bench courts, and community structures. The absence of rule of law and effective formal justice structures in the refugee-hosting areas continues to prevent refugees’ access to formal justice. As disputes are mostly resolved via traditional mechanisms, the provision of legal assistance and monitoring of community-based justice mechanisms remains important. Civil cases are dealt with/by Conflict Resolution Committees (CRC). Local and law enforcement authorities, CRC and customary law courts need continuous training on human rights, judicial procedures, refugee rights and obligations, civil rights and procedures. UNHCR will also continue to sensitize refugees about their legal obligations and training for new members of the customary law courts on human rights, Refugee Act, court procedures so they discharge responsibilities effectively. UNHCR will also continue legal awareness campaign in the host community and support to survivors of SGBV and other violence on legal proceedings.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The presence of refugees has had a huge impact in the area which, has strained the harmony and peaceful coexistence between the host community and refugees. As there is a fragile relationship between the refugees and the host community, it is critical to support resilience and self-reliance to a proportion of the hosting communities. Particular attention will be given to vulnerable girls and women, and other population groups with special protection need in all activities. Given the lack of social services, an increased number of the host population is seeking to access basic services available inside the refugee camps. While UNHCR has extended its services to host populations and included the most vulnerable host communities members in various sectorial interventions, further targeted community-based interventions and community dialogue are needed to address, at times, the tense relationship between these two communities. UNHCR is focusing on self-reliance and boosting environmental activities to mitigate against a breakdown of the peaceful co-existence with host communities, while also continuing to programme for the stabilization of life-saving basic services for the community, in particular with regards to the health of the population.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project will continue capacity building for relevant local and national institutions such as Commission for Refugee Affairs, Ministry of Humanitarian Affairs and Disaster Management, Relief and Rehabilitation Commission, Directorate of Nationality, Passports and Immigration and empowering persons of concern by improving their documentation, education, coping mechanisms, resilience and livelihoods. Under the Refugee Coordination Model (RCM), UNHCR will continue leading and coordinating the refugee response in various locations in the country. The RCM provided an inclusive and participatory platform for all actors and stakeholders in planning and coordinating refugee response and addressing the protection needs of person of concern. Close cooperation will continue with the Commission for Refugee Affairs (CRA), UNHCR’s direct counterpart on refugee affairs, and Co-Chair, National Refugee Coordination forum. UNHCR will continue to work closely with the Directorate of Nationality, Passports and Immigration under the Ministry of Interior to ensure that the appropriate actions are taken in regard to the people at risk of statelessness.UNHCR will strengthen partnership with the Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC) under the Ministry of Humanitarian Affairs for the mapping of spontaneous refugee returnees.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

UNHCR will ensure that protection is central to humanitarian action through protection programming and mainstreaming, including monitoring, assessments, analysis and advocacy. UNHCR will engage in a number of activities aimed at supporting solutions through creating enabling conditions, in line with South Sudan's national framework for return, reintegration and relocation updated in October 2019. Solutions initiatives will be supported in accordance with the guidance and policy documents that were endorsed by the Humanitarian Country Team. The total refugee population from Sudan and other countries is expected to increase in 2020. Main refugee protection priorities will include ensuring access to territory, reception, registration, status determination and documentation; maintaining the humanitarian and civilian character of asylum; promoting peaceful coexistence among refugee and host communities and access to quality education for refugee children which is in line with South Sudan's Refugee Act.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The political landscape in South Sudan shifted significantly on 12 September 2018 when most of the warring parties signed the Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in the Republic of South Sudan (R-ARCSS). By early 2019, the situation was generally stable but fragile. The implementation of the peace deal had fallen behind schedule, and the Integrated Food Security Classification analysis forecasted worsening food insecurity affecting nearly 7 million people. However, security had improved and humanitarian access had expanded to all but a few areas in Central and Western Equatoria where clashes continued between state forces and a handful of armed opposition groups that did not sign the peace agreement. While the situation will require close monitoring and robust analysis, UNHCR maintains cautious optimism that the R-ARCSS will translate into a complete cessation of hostilities during the course of the year, and South Sudan will be on its way to building lasting peace and restoring law and order and basic social services. Although UNHCR does not yet consider it appropriate to promote or facilitate refugee returns, the agency foresees a small scale increase in the number of South Sudanese refugees spontaneously returning in 2020. UNHCR will continue to enhance returnee monitoring at border crossing points and areas of return in coordination with South Sudan’s Relief and Rehabilitation Commission and other partners.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

UNHCR will continue Government capacity building and peaceful co-existence programs to complement existing programs for early recovery and resilience will be initiated to increase absorption capacity in areas of returns as well as improvement of livelihood for ensuring sustainability. Community-based programs, prioritized by the communities to ensure sustainability, will be also continued in 2020. Community self-management structures focusing on advocacy and socio-recreational activities will be supported that include refugee leadership, community outreach volunteers (COV), community-based protection teams and sector-specific structures for education, WASH, child protection and SGBV, as well as youth, women and disability associations and early marriage task forces. COVs have been instrumental in identifying and providing assistance to extremely vulnerable refugees that will be further expanded in 2020 with more gender-sensitive composition.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

UNHCR and partners maintain its offices in the refugee camps since they have greatly increased the interaction between refugees and UNHCR, and also provide effective complaint mechanisms to address persons of concern’s views. Camp interagency coordination meetings are held on a monthly basis. Community Outreach Volunteers (COV) network is also reinforced since they crucial to provide real-time information to UNHCR on issues affecting their lives. In 2019, UNHCR will work with its partners to strengthen community-based complaint mechanisms and expand awareness on PSEA across key areas of operation. Building on the Cluster approach to ensure consistent messaging and centralization of complaint mechanisms will strengthen prevention awareness and integrated response, when necessary. Working closely with the GBV Sub-Cluster and the multi-agency PSEA Taskforce will allow boosting the implementation of the guidelines for the Community Based Complaint Mechanism (CBMC), amplifying, therefore, the efficiency of reporting mechanisms using the methodologies the communities prefer.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Following Age, Gender and Diversity approach the participatory assessment provides the basis for designing protection and assistance programs for Persons of Concern. Annual Participatory Assessment (PA) exercises and consultations with Persons of Concern (PoCs) were undertaken in the course of 2019 which informs protection programming. This will be continued in 2020 involving all actors. The principles of age, gender and diversity, a rights-based, and community-based approach guided the process. PA ensured that refugees are put at the centre of the decision-making concerning their protection and welfare as well as ensured accountability to affected populations. Qualitative data collection was done through FGDs and KIIs and feedback sessions were held with communities to present findings and recommendations.
