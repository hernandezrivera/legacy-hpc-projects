Project Summary

According to Gender-based Violence (GBV) Sub-cluster Response (April – 15 October 2023), Since 15 April, the number of people in need (PIN) of gender-based violence (GBV) services in Sudan have increased by over 1 million to 4.2 million people. The number of people targeted for GBV services has increased to 1.3 million, with an increase of over 900 per cent of people targeted in states that are heavily impacted but still accessible. The number of people in need of GBV services in 2024 is expected to increase to 6.9 million
Status of the Sudanese Women: Women and girls are negatively affected by the on-going fighting and also affected by local unrest between tribes and extended families. Women and girls became more vulnerable to rights violation such as house raids, rape, kidnapping, slavery and assassination. Reports of child recruitments have also surfaced (OCHA Sudan, 2023)Coupled with the lack of protection in most areas of the IDPs; and the lack of awareness of the importance of speaking out fearing of stigma and revenges. In this situations, there is increasing threat of domestic violence, gender based violence (GVB) and sexual gender based violence (SGVB).
In displacement settings, threats and risks of GBV against women and girls persist particularly sexual violence, sexual exploitation, harassment and early and forced marriage. Anecdotally all forms of GBV such as physical violence, psychological violence, denial of resources, and others, remain prevalent. In most cases, some GBV survivors experience multiple forms of GBV.

There are structural and cultural constraints in decision-making mechanisms that impede equal representation between men and women; with regard to emergencies response. During focus group discussions with IDPs young women in different gathering points to discuss their needs and priorities, we witnessed agitation, eagerness and willingness to respond to this catastrophic situation and to combat this trend of violations; also witnessed urgent need for support with life-saving items.
This project will be an opportunity to adopt the vulnerable IDPs communities’ protection, build their capacity to resilience and respond to some of their urgent human needs; and help to sustain their livelihood activities.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Since the beginning of the project, WADI has provided training to the affected population regarding accountability. We have taught our hired staff about SEA and self-care, and we have also provided training to the community about sexual exploitation and abuse, educating them about their rights. We have established accountability mechanisms in our office that allow us to receive SEA complaints through direct communication with HR and a complaint box. Additionally, we have designated focal points in the target area who receive complaints from the SEA and report them to USB by the wadi

4 - AAP & PSEA
4.1 - How will the project share information and collect community feedback? Please select as many as relevant.
4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

Our organization has always cared about the input from the community. This is demonstrated by the way we have carried out projects, from need assessments using open-ended FGD questions to detailed follow-ups with the community at large through pre- and post-evaluations and extensive follow-ups with all population members assigned to two male and female focal points in the target areas who receive complaint boxes for literate and illiterate people. These focal points are then used to receive oral reports from the community, ensuring accountability to the affected population.
