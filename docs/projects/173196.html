Project Summary

Moafa II will seek to improve the nutrition, water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), and health status of 337,038 project beneficiaries (including 157,321 IDPs and returnees) in conflict-affected areas of Jebel Marra and Um Dukhun, Central Darfur State, Sudan. The project will build on the solid foundation of multiple OFDA-funded interventions since in 2016 in Jebel Marra and expanded to Um Dukhun in 2019. The response will extend to newly accessible village clusters in these localities and harder to reach villages within the current clusters. The program will engage in emergency health, nutrition, WASH, and humanitarian coordination to help communities rebound from displacement and conflict.

1 - Needs

Health: Jebel Mara and Um Dukhun experience extremely high disease burdens compounded by seasonal disease outbreaks. The top diseases reported by CRS-supported HFs in these areas are respiratory tract infections, diarrhea and malaria. Um Dukhun is highly prone to outbreaks of diseases especially AWD, chikungunya, hemorrhagic fever and measles since there are limited health interventions and Chad has a similar broken health system. Central Darfur S/MoH remains underfunded and the situation has worsened due to the extreme economic downturn over the last year. Drug supplies are insufficient for the whole state and are prioritized for use in the tertiary facilities. The budget for frontline facilities (BHU and PHCC) is very limited and insufficient to provide drugs, supplies and staffing. The six new communities (Mohamedein, Arshin, Aumo, Deblong, Karakara, and Magan – with a total estimated population of 172,393) have no health services due to insufficient infrastructure, personnel, supplies, and equipment. (IPC Phase 4). Jebel Marra is one of the key areas of concern. Vulnerable communities have resorted to reducing meals, switching to cheaper and less nutritious foods, borrowing, and selling livelihood and HH assets, to the detriment of future generations and risking the creation of poverty traps. HF data from CRS in Jebel Marra and Um Dukhun shows a GAM rate of 15.5% and SAM of 4.6% that is above the WHO emergency threshold. Inadequate feeding practices of young children, poor WASH facilities, high disease prevalence, conflict and recurrent drought contribute to high levels of malnutrition. WASH: CRS' integrated needs assessment, conducted in February 2020, noted insufficiency of water infrastructure to meet demand, poor HH water storage practices, and prevalence of water-borne diseases. Water sources are poorly protected, resulting in water contamination. HH water storage is typically untreated, uncovered, and drinking water is accessed through the dipping method. Handpumps are overused with frequent breakdowns. Communities ration water and women and girls travel long distances, exposing them to increased Gender Based Violence (GBV) risks. Latrine coverage is poor, leading to pervasive open defecation. Villages suffer from severe shortages of water, limited sanitary facilities, and overcrowding. One-third of people consume water from unprotected sources, with an overall water consumption as low as 8 liters per person per day. Across the areas, people spend between two to three hours to access water. For example, a single handpump was shared by 33,750 people in Aumo and by 37,200 in Mohamadein. Livestock: The needs assessment recommended small livestock (poultry meat and eggs) to increase consumption of nutritious foods. Raising local poultry is a common practice in the areas, yet a majority of poorer HHs are unable to access starter birds. As general distribution could negatively impact markets, Moafa II will use a market-based approach through the voucher fair modality for vulnerable IDP and returnee HHs with severely malnourished children or PLW. In collaboration with USAID under the RISING program, CRS is using voucher and fair initiatives including DiNER (Diversity for Nutrition and Enhanced Resilience), to link communities to local suppliers of diverse planting material while providing practical information about nutrition. Lessons learned from this approach will be integrated into Moafa II livelihood initiatives. Given the current unstable economic context in Sudan where prices change on a daily basis, ongoing market assessments will be integrated into operational oversight of the program, rather than conducted as a standalone market assessment.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Goal: Conflict-affected communities in Central Darfur have improved nutrition, WASH and health status (1) HEALTH: Targeted communities have increased use of primary health services; - CRS will support management of 19 health facilities (HF) and 3 mobile clinics. These 19 HF include 13 HF which CRS has supported in past programming (continuation) and 6 new HF, which will be established in new vulnerable areas. CRS will rehabilitate these 6 new HF in these communities.- CRS will manage these HF and mobile clinics with the MoH. CRS will support running costs, using and training MoH seconded staff as well as equipping the clinics with medicine and non-medicine supply and equipment needs. - At the HF, CRS and MoH will treat and monitor communicable diseases, treat non-communicable diseases, and provide reproductive healthcare such as ANC and PNC at HFs and through community-based referral. - CRS and the HFs will also engage in community health activities through health education and promotion, screening, case detection, referral and follow up. This will be done utilizing a team of trained community health workers. (2A) NUTRITION: PLW and CU5 in target communities have reduced levels of malnutrition; - At its HFs, CRS will implement all four components of CMAM and will train HF staff in these using GoS national guidelines and protocols. HF staff will manage SAM cases without medical complications. Nutrition staff correctly diagnose/refer SAM cases with medical complications to the nearest SC- At the majority of its clinics, CRS will have OTP and TSFP. CRS will also establish IYCF corners at each OTP/TSFP to ensure that health services in emergency situations include a focus on infant care and encourage early and optimal breastfeeding- At the community level, CRS and its HF will utilize trained community health workers to promote nutrition behavior change, using the same nutrition messages from the HF/OTP. CRS will also form mother support groups and grandmother support groups to help support appropriate IYCF promotion at the community level. (2B) FOOD SECURITY: PLW and CU5 in target communities have reduced levels of malnutrition; - CRS will target vulnerable HHs with children or pregnant/lactating women diagnosed with SAM with food security support. CRS will give targetted HH vouchers to redeem with local vendors to purchase two poultry birds at the livestock voucher fairs.- CRS will train recipients on poultry production, animal health and safe handling, composting, disposal and use of poultry waste- CRS will carry out awareness campaigns on the importance of consuming animal source foods (eggs & meat) for optimal HH nutrition. - CRS will link beneficiaries to government state MoPER, University of Zalingei, FAO and community animal health workers (CAHWs) for veterinary services and policy support. (3) WASH: Target communities have improved public health and access to WASH facilities, services and materials; and,- For the new HF targeted (6 HF), CRS will construct/develop appropriate waste management systems (incinerator, placenta pit, etc) and construct VIP latrines with handwashing stations- CRS will also improve community water supply by rehabilitating community Hand-Dug Wells and Boreholes. CRS will rehabilitate 20 boreholes (3 will be at HF), repair fifteen handpumps, and five hand dug wells. CRS will carry out water quality control testing (boreholes and HH water supply) and chlorinate contaminated water sources by establishing solid waste management disposal sites for each community, carrying out community engagement activities on the importance and use of solid waste disposal sites, and developing community wide solid waste management plans- CRS will conduct hygiene promotion activities (awareness)

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection Mainstreaming: The proposed interventions will enhance social cohesion and will also empower vulnerable and marginalized groups including IDPs and returnees. This approach will ensure the sustainability of the interventions by basing the activities that underlie them on proven principles for mainstreaming protection. CRS has an existing Safeguarding policy and a Code of Conduct required of all staff, partners, and affiliates, to ensure that beneficiaries are protected from any kind of abuse and exploitation during the conduct of this project. In addition to the gender and protection mainstreaming strategy, CRS will ensure that partners, new staff, and affiliates understand and sign the CRS Code of Conduct before engaging in the project. CRS will also make sure that partners and field staff are trained on the child/vulnerable adult protection (or safeguarding) policy. During sensitization activities, project staff will share information on how to report abuse, as prescribed in CRS’ comprehensive safeguarding policy. CRS will initiate community meetings at an accessible location and time to share information, and field staff will be required to visit any HH unable to attend the meetings face-to-face due to disability or other constraint. In addition to selecting markets/vendors closest to beneficiaries, those requiring additional logistical support - including the elderly and persons with disabilities - will receive a transportation stipend, which will be redeemed with food. Key stakeholders will be sensitized on CRS’ Code of Conduct and reporting abuse. CRS and partners will be working with key Protection cluster members to conduct a protection risk assessment and identify mitigation measures. Overseen by CRS Sudan’s Protection Manager, CRS will undertake a protection risk assessment to identify key risks, determine those most affected according to gender, age, and diversity factors such as disability, and develop mitigating measures or design/adapt project activities in response to the identified risk.
