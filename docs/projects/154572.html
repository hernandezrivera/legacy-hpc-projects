Project Summary

The proposed intervention is complying with HRP 2019 Strategic Objectives for Iraq, and more specifically Strategic Objective 1 “Post-conflict transition towards durable solutions” and Strategic Objective 2 “Strengthening the Centrality of Protection”. It also complies with the Protection Cluster Objective 1 and 2, the Child Protection Sub-Cluster’s operational framework and the Education Cluster Objective 1 and 2.
Based on TGH extensive experience in Child Protection / Education integrated approach in rural, urban and camp settings, the project aims at providing timely, culturally appropriate and gender sensitive child protection and education services to conflict affected children and their families, while strengthening community based mechanisms of risks prevention and response. As such, both immediate responses and long term solutions will be implemented, notably by strengthening systems and service delivery, and building capacities. TGH proposes to focus on Ninewa, Salah Al-Din, Erbil, Dohuk and Suleymaniah Governorates, and notably in rural and urban area, through a mix of static and mobile modalities depending on the context. This two-fold, flexible approach will allow TGH to maximize its population coverage, including in underserved areas, and deploy its teams shortly based on needs and gaps.
Notably, and thanks to teams composed of Child protection, Education and Youth Empowerment staff, the intervention will tackle various risks that affects’ children’s development, resilience and well-being, and provide a comprehensive response. The intervention will be implemented in close coordination with TGH legal partner Justice Center in Ninewa and SAD to ensure children’s access to their rights.
It will directly target children, adolescents but also their parents, and to a larger extent their community. Educational, vocational and psychosocial activities adapted to age, gender and specific needs will be provided by taking into each group’s specificities at all stages of the intervention. TGH will develop activities in schools and learning spaces (such as PSS, trainings of teachers and social workers, awareness events, support and linkage between members CPCs and PTAs). Integration of Child Protection and Education component will also imply smooth mutual referrals between activities based on children’s needs, thanks to mix teams of staff (composed of teachers, case workers, PSS facilitators and Community and Youth Empowerment counsellors). In parallel, the mobile approach in villages will allow to reach families and children that do not access services.
TGH has been implementing child protection activities in Erbil, Ninewa, Dohuk and Suleymaniyah Governorates since 2013. TGH therefore has experience in urban, peri urban and rural settings, as well as in IDP camps (Khazer M1, Salamiyah 1 and 2, and Nimrud camps) where TGH is Child protection focal point (and previously in Nargaziliah where TGH was the Education focal point). In Erbil governorate, TGH has been providing Child protection and education services from 2013 until August 2017 in several localities around Erbil. In Bashiqa sub-district in 2017-2018 and in Mosul until now, TGH has implemented non formal education, child protection and youth empowerment activities (including vocational trainings). Finally TGH is supporting MoLSA/DoSA in all Governorates of Kurdistan to strengthen their capacity in Child protection and notably case management (2017-2018).
Therefore, the proposed intervention, based on TGH expertise, is fully in line with the 2019 Humanitarian Strategy for Iraq.

Needs

Based on People in Need analysis, 2.4 million conflict affected children are in need of education support and 1.1 million children are in need of child protection (in addition to 400,000 adults, for a total of 1.5 million individuals targeted by child protection interventions). Ninewa Governorate concentrates the highest numbers of IDPs, followed notably by Dohuk, Erbil and Salah Al Din, while Ninewa and Salah Al Din concentrates the highest number of returnees categorized with very severe and severe needs (source: OCHA). Districts targeted by TGH interventions are among those where needs have been assessed as the most severe according to the Needs severity matrix, based on the Multi-cluster Needs Assessments: Mosul, Tal Afar, Hamdaniya, Tilkaif in Ninewa, Sumel in Dohuk, Shirqat and Balad in Salah Al Din and Suleymaniyah (including Halabja and Garmian, which are administratively separated). Indeed, the numbers of IDPs, returnees or both categories of population are the highest while child protection risks remain critical and gaps have been noted. Most of intervention currently being rolled out is in camp (80 to 90% according to the latest Child Protection Sub-cluster dashboard, as of 31st October 2018), uncovered needs are acute in rural areas, including in villages and informal settlements, where access to services remains limited. The crisis and subsequent displacements have had a differential impact on girls and boys, on men and women, and on children, adolescents and adults. While return movements towards their places of origin are increasing, children, adolescents and their families, taking account each gender and age groups specificities, needs and capacities, are in need of support to cope with the aftermath of the conflict and subsequent displacement, affecting their life opportunities (disruption of education and livelihoods, protection-related issues) but also their personal well-being (war and displacement-related trauma, loss of protective community mechanisms) in order to support their resilience and reintegration. Access to formal education remain limited due to issue of distance, documentation, disruption of education, capacity of schools and teachers, education related cost. 41% of conflict affected children (IDPs in and out of camps, returnees and affected host community) have no or limited access to education services according to the Education Cluster analysis (compilation of the MCNA and MoE data). Besides support to children, teachers are also in needs of trainings, material support and incentives to be able to perform their role. Urban and rural return zones, such as Mosul, Tal Afar and Tilkaif districts in Ninewa governorates, appear to show severe needs and lack of actors. In KRI, underserved areas such as Sumel, but also Halabja and Garmian in Suleymaniah, where very few actors are present, have been identified. Multi-cluster Needs Assessments results from September 2018 show worrying prevalence of Child Protection issues, affecting boys and girls, children and adolescents, and the districts planned to be targeted by TGH are among the one where child protection risks appear to be the highest (REACH – Child Protection Index), as confirmed by TGH assessments and experience in the area. During the Ninewa HRP/HNO workshop held in September in Mosul, and in consistence with TGH assessments conducted in East Mosul and West Mosul (2018) and the collected data in Tal Afar, Tilkaif and Qayyarah, the main risks that children are exposed to are child labor, child marriage, school dropout, missing documentation, psychosocial distress and risk of exclusion/stigmatization/arbitrary detention for children perceived as affiliated. Children have been indeed particularly exposed to risks related to the conflict, the exodus or the life in IDP camps and at risk situations remain while they have returned. Once returned in their place of origin, lack of basic services and harsh socioeconomic conditions fuel those Child Protection issues or Education related issues. Children with missing documentation remain one of the highest protection concern in Ninewa governorate (in TGH’s areas of operation such as Mosul, Hamdaniya, Telafar and Tilkaif notably - particularly Nimrud camp sees a high number of ISIS families lacking documentation, up to 50%) as well as in Salah Al Din governorate. The denial or challenges to access to civil documentation has a direct impact on children’s rights to access services and notably formal schools. In addition, legal assistance for sensitive cases, such as detention, adoption and guardianship is necessary to ensure children’s needs are being addressed within a comprehensive approach. Justice Center, TGH’s local partner, has supported more than 1,600 children documentation cases (mainly in Salah Al Din, but also in Ninewa) and 50 cases of children detention cases.

Activities

The main objective of this intervention is the improvement of children’s protective environment and access to education, especially in rural and urban areas. TGH proposes to implement an integrated child protection and education intervention, through mobile and static modalities and focusing on the different needs and capacities of all age, gender and role in the community. As such, activities will be adapted to girls / boys / men / women, children / adolescents /parents or caregivers, community members / teachers / authorities. The mobile approach will allow TGH to reach beneficiaries that do not access static services, but also to shortly deploy to uncovered locations where gaps are identified or where recent movements have occurred. Activities will include:- Specialized individualized support through Case management for children at risk, including complex cases of UASC, CAAFAG, SGBV survivor, victim of violence, etc; including legal assistance through the partnership with Justice Center local legal partner. Through mutual referrals of children's cases in need of legal support (detention cases, adoption/guardianship, documentation), and of children supported by Justice center in need of case management, PSS or support to access education, children will benefit from a comprehensive response. Safe identification and referral will also take place in school thanks to teachers’ trainings.- Structured and sustainable psychosocial support, including in schools and learning spaces, targeting the different age groups (Reported under CP indicators only to avoid double counting). TGH implements the Child and Youth Resilience Programme and the Adolescent Girls toolkit. - Youth empowerment activities, including : life skills, support to civic initiatives, employment skills and Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET) for adolescents and youth, in order to support their resilience, access to opportunities and recognize their key role in their community. - Parents sessions that will promote a stronger protective family environment. Some modules are part of the PSS structured Programs that TGH implements, in addition to regular sessions on child protection risks, importance of education, their role to enhance their children’s development and resilience.- Non-formal education activities (including catch up classes and basic knowledge for adolescents) to ensure children can reach the required level to register back to school, prevent drop out and build up adolescents’ knowledge when their registration to formal education is not possible.- Awareness raising on CP and Education through a large set of activities: FGD, large campaign led by the community themselves- Trainings and capacity building of authorities (MoLSA/DoSA, MoE/DoE, police and asayesh), CP and Non CP actors (including teachers and education related personnel), Community members on (depending on the profiles): Child protection basics (including Safe Identification and referrals and child safeguarding), PSS (including Life skills), PSEA, Positive discipline, etc. This activity will aim at building safe and protective learning and living environments.- Development of Child Protection Community-based mechanisms, by setting up or supporting Child Protection Committees, Adolescents/Youth Clubs, PTA and ensuring their empowerment and active role in promoting a protective environment and access to education. Communication with Communities (CwC) is a key component to inform Communities on theirs rights and available services.- Support to school-aged children by providing cash for education, support to transportation, school and recreational kits. This support is based on strict criteria, based on needs and vulnerability, taking into account risk mitigation measures.- Support to teachers by providing incentives and teaching material when the lack of salaries or material represent a barrier to perform their duty As part as this integrated approach (education / child protection / livelihood for adolescents / legal assistance), referral pathways will be set to ensure children and their families benefit from a holistic and comprehensive response. Link with other actors will be maintained to ensure all needs are covered, especially in terms of Mental Health, Special needs, GBV etc. TGH will maintain its active participation to coordination forum (clusters/sub-clusters/working groups) and regular update of service mapping to ensure prevention and response activities are always adapted to needs and contexts and done in collaboration with partners. N.B.1: PSS and CM targets are reported under CP only to avoid double-counting.N.B.2: as advised, TGH did not include its Child Protection activities in Hamdaniyah IDP camps (Khazer M1, Salamiyah 1 & 2 and Nimrud). Indeed, the latter are implemented under UNICEF funding, which is therefore including these activitie under their own HPC submissions. TGH can include them at revision stage if requested so.

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risks are critical in the ongoing context of humanitarian work in Iraq, and most even specifically so forchild protection actors. It is TGH’s inherent role to implement activities aiming directly at preventing and responding to protection risks, especially child protection risks. Some key protection risks identified for the hereby-proposed intervention are the following:- Risks related to data protection and beneficiary privacy (especially for sensitive cases such as CAAFAG, as authorities might request to have access to confidential information).- Risks related to perception of ISIS affiliation among beneficiaries, especially in Sunni communities, with specific risks encountered by adolescents and women. - Unequal access to services between age, gender, social/ethnic/religious groups, etc, that can be caused by tradition, reluctance to gather with peers, distance, security constraints, lack of information, etc- Safety and Psychological risks for the Staff due to dealing with complex cases (children victim of violence, including SGBV, CAAFAG, etc). - Safety risks for beneficiaries and staff due to working in areas still contaminated by ERW and IEDs.- Safety risks for staff linked with regular check-point crossing.

Mitigation Measures

Risk mitigation strategies include a large set of measures:- TGH is mainstreaming protection in all its intervention, including the Do No Harm principles and the Age Gender Diversity (AGD) approach to take into account age, gender and special needs in all its activities, from the designing of the intervention to the implementation, monitoring, project’s closure and evaluation. TGH’s approach is inclusive and promotes social cohesion, especially in locations where different religious and ethnic communities live together, or locations with a mix of Host Communities, Returnees, IDPs or refugees. TGH conduct regular needs assessments to ensure intervention are based on needs, vulnerabilities and risks, and are designed to provide an adapted response.- TGH works in close collaboration with authorities and involve communities at all stage of its intervention, to ensure the acceptance, relevance and adaptation of its activities. TGH operates a regular monitoring of security context and developed Contingency plans. Regarding the risks of ERW/IED, TGH is in contact with both local actors and with UNMAS.- As TGH is dealing with sensitive situations and providing support to complex profiles (for instance, CAAFAG children or families alleged to be affiliated), TGH participates to relevant Task Force to share challenges and discuss common strategies of prevention and response.- TGH is relying on experienced staff (in CP and EiE) and is regularly organizing refreshment sessions for its staff on Code of conduct and Child safeguarding, Child Protection Principles (including confidentiality). In addition, TGH is in the process of training 100% of its staff in PSEA (field and management staff) to promote best practices and knowledge of reporting mechanisms (in addition to disciplinary measures put in place for any staff member failing to comply with the TGH policy). - TGH is regularly working with specialized psychologist to provide field staff with guidance on how to cope with exposure, and to improve staff care mechanisms. - Protection Risk Analysis are done on a regular basis and Risk mitigation measures are set for all interventions.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Accountability to affected population (AAP) is ensured through various mechanisms, such as :- Community consultation at the assessment, design, implementation and project’s closure’s stages, through interviews (key informants, community leaders, etc) and Focus Group Discussions (organized per age and gender);- Regular communication on available services (Communication with Communities – CwC)- Involvement and empowerment by supporting community based groups (CPC, Youth/Adolescent groups, PTA) to design, organize and monitor activities (ex: awareness campaign, PSS activities, civic initiatives, etc) - Set-up of ideas box and complaint feedback mechanisms in order to increase participation and adapt the response. These modalities aim at increasing population’s participation, appropriation and influence on the intervention. TGH provide them with feedbacks following consultation and complaints.
