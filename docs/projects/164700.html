Project Summary

Due to the intensive conflicts and shelling in Southern Idleb and Western Aleppo, massive displacements occurred throughout the past few weeks. The displacement directions were to several areas in the northeast of Idleb and northern Aleppo governorates. The majority of IDPs in Idleb settled in Harim district camps near the Syrian Turkish border. Additionally, the CCCM cluster tracked and recorded displacement waves towards Afrin and Azaz districts in northern Aleppo. To contribute to the overall efforts to respond to the critical needs of the new IDPs, IDA plans to respond to the massive displacement by providing life-saving WaSH services to 150000 IDPs in three areas in Idleb and Aleppo governorates. The target areas are:
1- Idleb governorate, Harim district SY0703
2- Aleppo governorate, Azaz district SY0204
3- Aleppo governorate, Afrin district SY0203
Focusing on vulnerable people, IDA plans to deliver emergency and live-saving WaSH services as follows:
- Waste management and disposal.
- Installment of pre-fabricated WaSH facilities in the camps.
- Water trucking.
- NFI WaSH and hygiene kits distribution.
- Hygiene awareness campaigns.

4 - Needs

IDA has recently conducted a rapid needs assessment in the area, and the report will be released soon. However, according to the preliminary results, health, WaSH, shelter, NFI, and protection are the prioritized services for the people in need in the camp. Data were collected separately from the different groups of people in the camp in terms of gender, age, and vulnerability and needs were concluded in a disaggregated manner. The majority of the IDPs in the camp are women and children. There is a percentage of about 15% of the IDPs who are disabled and have movement difficulties. For WaSH needs, women declared the need for separated WaSH facilities as well as dignity kits. Elderly and disabled people confirmed the challenge to access drinkable water especially during the current harsh winter season and lack of water. access to WaSH facilities was a challenge as well. The entire population expressed their demands to have waste management and disposal in the camp. The most vulnerable people according to the camp management are: widow women, women-headed HHs, people with disabilities, U5 children, and elderly persons.

5 - Implementation of activities

IDA will coordinate closely with the active actors in the area to implement the following activities:1. Support water quality assurance and operation and maintenance of water supply systems2. Repair, rehabilitation and operational support to sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems3. supply and purifying water to the IDPs sites. 4. Support waste management system in the camps through garbage bins. During the project implementation, monitoring team ensures the regular delivering of the activities and verifies the quality and accessibility of the services, through collecting feedback of the affected population and beneficiaries by interview, checklist and surveys. With note that the sampling methodology considers the most vulnerable people.Beneficiaries, mainly IDPs, are strongly engaged in activities, sessions, and in the field coordination meetings as well. IDA has an effective feedback and complaints mechanism policies, and procedures are in place to facilitate receiving the complaints/concerns safely and with confidentiality through multiple channels/tools such as emails, Whatsapp number, complaint boxes located in a visible and accessible place, presented clearly on awareness brochures and materials.Feedback on performance and best practices, challenges, and lessons learned are thoroughly discussed among the project team and M&E department.Furthermore, the gender balance and the recruitment of people from the same targeted communities were taken into account in the recruitment process to overcome and mitigate culture & gender-based challenges in those areas. Non-discrimination. Gender, disability, age, are emphasized in data collection and data analysis throughout the project period.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The team will be trained on how they best deal with serious and life-threatening situations. They will be provided with regular updates and recommendations to assure their safety and security. Coordination with Syrian Civil Defense in areas of implementation will be followed up to make sure their prompt access and response whenever needed. Another coordination with the health facilities, camp managements, and emergency response focal points will be enhanced. In the case of extremely severe insecurity, the work will be suspended based on safety and security focal point instructions with immediate coordination with the donor. Risk management plan considers the following: 1. Attacks on patients and/or project personnel due to a deteriorated security situation. Likelihood: lowMitigation: Immediate cessation of activities and waiting for a change in the situation.2. Destruction of health structures, plundering of furnishings due to a deteriorated security situation.Likelihood: lowMitigation: Suspension of activities; analysis of the situation, rehabilitation and/or replacement of missing equipment.3. Closure of Turkish border crossings for persons and goods, resulting in delays in project implementation.Likelihood: middleMitigation: Stockpiling (medicines, consumables); Sufficient number of medical staff living in Syria.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

To mitigate any risk that could affect the project implementation, IDA will develop Risk Management Matrix to set and plan for any possible risk that could be faced. Access:1- Risk: Access to project location is getting more harder or prevented by the local council or the controlling armed group. Mitigation: IDA has a valid MOU with LC covering project implementation duration. Operational:1- Risk: overlapping of services whether during the planning or implementation phase. Mitigation: IDA is active NGO since 2012, and has active projects in the areas. Also, the locations of these facilities will be shared and reported with the health cluster through HeRAMS system. IDA will manage this issue with the cluster, WHO and local council. 2- Risk: a shortage of needed supplies ( fuel, drugs, medical consumables) Mitigation: IDA has the authorization to deliver any humanitarian assistant through border gates.3- Risk: facility targeting and shifting control-lines. Mitigation: IDA will implement a specific security plan and will make sure the buildings are fortified against targeting. Moreover, IDA will share the locations of the facilities with a de-escalation mechanism to avoid targeting from conflict parties. IDA can evacuate the facilities and shift to other project locations in case of front-lines changes. Financial: 1- Risk: corruption and abuse use of cash. Mitigating measures: IDA will assure that all accounting and financial management practices are in line with IDA financial instruction.
