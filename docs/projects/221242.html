Project Summary

The North-West and South-West (NWSW) regions of Cameroon are affected by a complex humanitarian crisis that has resulted in a system-wide breakdown of infrastructure and services in the most affected areas, especially in the health sector. This crisis has resulted in the displacement of the population within and without the country who are either escaping from a conflict situation or are returning to the community of origin. The OCHA sitrep for October 2024 stated that from January till October 2024, Cameroon registered a total of 583,112 IDPs in the NWSW and 459,605 returnees. These displacements further deprive the populations of basic needs, a burden that is equally felt by the host communities as their limited resources become stretched.
The Ministry of health counterparts shows that in the NWR, 8 of the 448 HFs are non-functional while 7 are functioning with unqualified staff; and in the SWR, of the 308 HFs, 66 (17.6%) are non-functional. Though the situation in the NWR may look promising, the region is characterized by numerous attacks on healthcare. Of the 24 attacks on healthcare registered in the NWSW from January till November 2024, 22 were registered in the NWR alone. This has further deteriorated the availability of staff, especially in the domain of physical and mental trauma care.
The NWSW have equally experienced numerous disease outbreaks including Cholera, measles, yellow fever and mpox that have contributed to fragilize the health system. Though no case of cholera has been registered in 2024, the SW region continues to intensify surveillance activities especially during the rainy season. Presently, the HDs of Bamenda 3 and Wum in the NWR are in yellow fever outbreak, and the regions have accounted for 26 of the 114 suspected mpox cases, 7 (77%) of the 9 confirmed cases, and 1 of the 2 deaths registered in the NWR. Mbonge HD in the SWR is the presently in outbreak as three more cases have been confirmed during the epidemiologic week 46. Both Clads 1 and 2 mpox variants have been registered in the SWR.
This project aims to reduce excess morbidity and mortality of internally displaced populations (IDP), returnees, and affected host communities and Support Early Recovery and Resilience in the North-West and South-West regions through the restoration and improvement of the health system’s components and core public health functions, in alignment with the principles of building back better and sustainable development of Cameroon.
The project will be carried out in all the divisions of the NWSW regions, while putting more focus on areas of high risk like the Manyu and Ndian in the SWR and Bui and Donga mantum in the NWR. In areas with poorly functioning and non-functional health facilities as well as hard to reach zones, mobile clinics will be the priority healthcare delivery approach as this will ensure timely access to equitable healthcare.
The project will also lay emphasis on supporting non-functional health facilities to restart while strengthening the capacities of some affected health facilities to improve the service offer.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The situation in the North-West region and South-West regions has remained volatile and unpredictable for the past 8 years since the onset of the crisis. The combined effect of the numerous outbreaks, and the crisis has had a negative impact on an already fragile health system. Access to essential health services remains a challenge to most of the affected communities due to the complete or partial shutdown of health facilities, the distance to the closest functional health facility, the limited service packaged offered by the health facilities, and the inability to pay for the cost of health care. The persistence of insecurity is causing additional displacements in these regions. The displaced people are at a higher risk due to poor living conditions, and the lack of access to basic services. There is an urgent need to improve epidemiological surveillance of diseases with epidemiological potential. This will ensure early case detection and control In the same way, response to ongoing outbreaks like mpox and measles will be carried out. These will reduce the impact (morbidity and mortality) of these diseases on the populations in these regions. Taking into consideration the negative impact of the crises on the delivery of healthcare, it is imperative that the capacity of health districts and health facilities be strengthened especially in some health districts like Mamfe in the SWR where the district hospital was destroyed, Kumba HD that hosts the mpox treatment center, Buea HD, Bamenda HD, Nkambe HD which host the referral hospitals of the regions and offer physical trauma care to victims of the conflict. This will ensure proper response during outbreaks and response to the emergency health needs of the affected communities. The most vulnerable populations that include persons living with disabilities, pregnant women, and teenagers are the most vulnerable and hence they need assistance to access quality health services. The brutal nature of the crisis has equally resulted in an increase in the incidence of mental trauma cases. . Also, a good number of people are traumatized mentally and need mental health assistance. The project will have a mental health component to take care of the mental health needs of the affected populations.Mainstreaming disability inclusion, accountability to affected persons, and prevention and response to sexual exploitation, abuse, and harassment (PRSEAH), as well as the respect for the humanitarian principles will be ensured throughout project implementation.
