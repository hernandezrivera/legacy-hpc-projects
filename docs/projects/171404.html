Project Summary

The project comes in line with HRP 2021 strategic objective 2 and it falls under the Shelter and NFIs Cluster Objective 2 ”vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns are supported to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacity”.
The project has been designed based on SIF extensive experience in the field and in close consultation with beneficiaries as well as the shelter cluster. It widely integrates the recommendation from OCHA field monitoring visits to previous and ongoing projects by introducing diversified packages for the targeted houses based on the actual need. This will maximize beneficiaries’ participation and satisfaction by the project outputs. The project will provide complementary shelter assistance to the previous and ongoing winterization projects that SIF has been implementing to assist 900 HHs who benefited from the distribution of winterization NFIs, rehabilitation of substandard shelter, and cash assistance for NFIs. The project is a Ten months aims at providing shelter rehabilitation and NFI to protect people at higher risk of being affected from COVID-19 crisis and exposed to harsh weather conditions and protection concerns.
The project will support a total 1000 vulnerable HHs including (group 1: 400 HH at home guarantee identified by MoSD in North Gaza and Gaza and group 2: 600 windowed women headed households (WHHH) from SIF lists) . The priority target will be focused on HHs having PWDs, elderlies, persons with chronic diseases that put them at higher risk of being affected of COVID 19.
The first group is 400 families at home guarantee from MoSD’s lists will receive standard COVID 19 family Hygiene and cleaning kit , enough for 1 month. SIF will implement this activity in partnership with Beit Lahia Development Association (BLDA) and supported by a strong coordination mechanism with MoSD, and shelter cluster coordinator.
The second group is 600 WHHH from Gaza , Dair Al Balah, Khan Younis, and Rafah living in substandard housing conditions exposed to extreme weather and at risk of COVID 19( of which 200 HH will receive shelter rehabilitation and 400 HH will receive NFI and light repair). These HHs have been identified by SIF annual assessment and multiple shelters needs assessment conducted between January and July 2020 focus on seasonal elements, targeting a sample of 348 households. The assessment found a need for urgent rehabilitation and NFIs assistance ahead of the winter /summer to cope with harsh weather and COVID 19 situation. SIF will directly deliver this intervention to this target group based on experience and easy access to potential beneficiaries. The project will assist the targeted HHs through improving hygiene conditions for families lacking basic facilities, such as latrines or kitchens, by providing appropriate hygiene materials, cleaning supplies, awareness materials, and technical consultancies and guidance though rehabilitation, and relevant NFI. With support from SIF project team, each HH will develop an individualized rehabilitation plan and NFI package corresponding to the specific needs and priority of their shelter gaps to make it comply with shelter upgrading minimum standards and NFI standard through cash based approach.
SIF will apply mix implementation modality –shelf help and contracting for rehabilitation. SIF will consult with the beneficiaries on the suitable modality based on the situation of COVID 19 at the time of implementation and their capacity to undertake the shelter repair work. SIF and the partner will comply with COVID 19 protective measures to ensure safety, and do no harm to the targeted beneficiaries’ and other involved stakeholders.
SIF’s protection unit’s (Protection manager/focal point and Counselor and case manager) will carefully monitor the protection concern (GBV, abuse, exploitation, and child labor) by building the capacity of the project’s team, partner, and targeted beneficiaries and through referral.

1 - Needs

The recent shelter vulnerability assessment conducted by MoPWH and concluded in January 2020 in Gaza revealed that 7,000 units need complete reconstruction, in addition to 21,500 substandard units, which need rehabilitation of essential components to provide minimum requirements.SIF carries out multiple assessments for orphans’ households who are enrolled in SIF sponsorship program. From January to July 2020, SIF assessed 348 out of 1,500 (700 from previous list and 800 from new list) women headed HHs identified in SIF database as needing urgent shelter rehabilitation ahead of the winter/summer season. Their needs were assessed by two site engineers who applied an upgrading minimum standard survey. These HHs lack basic elements of protection such as overcrowding home, lack of privacy and safety, and potential exposure to gender and child related protection concerns. The sample includes 14 HHs in North Gaza, 50 HHs in Gaza, 143 HHs in Deir Al Balah, 46 HHs in Khan Younis governorate, and 95 HHs in Rafah governorate. The assessment results show that 22% of the HHs have asbestos roof which is classified as toxic material and 55% have metallic roof. 72% of the surveyed HHs suffers from leaking windows, 61% of the HHs suffers from leaking roofs, 23% have cracks or holes on the external walls, 23% of the HHs lack one room at minimum with insulated roof against heat and cold, 10% of HHs have unfinished wall surface with a minimum one layer of plaster or Tyrolean coat. Those HHs reported that family members are frequently exposed to cold during winter and hot in summer. 17% of the surveyed HHs suffers from unsealed floor which cause the presence of insects and rodents, 11% do not have kitchen with adequate space with solid and cleanable preparation counter and sink in which obstruct the woman to cook food. 61% of the HHs suffer from leaking from broken WASH facilities, and 16% of the HHs’ toilets do not have tiled floors or walls which decreases hygiene. Also 38% of the HHs have a water storage capacity below the minimum standard, and 46% of the HHs do not have either solar or electric water heater impeding daily personal hygiene.The results show serious shortage in households safety elements as 45% of the HHs are crowded which does not allow privacy and safety, 65% of the HHs suffer from unsafe electrical outlets, switches, and fixtures which could cause electrical fires, 75% of the HHs do not have waterproof outlets at the kitchen, toilets, or showers, and 67% of the HHs do not have one Fluorescent / LED light with switch at all house’s spaces. Moreover, 23% of the HHs do not have any emergency power energy for lighting during the electricity cut while 77% have insufficient safe emergency LED and for lighting in which affects children’s school performance. 61% of the HHs’ do not have solid and lockable doors for bedroom and bathrooms thus do not allow privacy and safety. 10% of the HHs has inadequate size of windows in toilets and bathroom, 44% of HHs does not have lockable windows, at 55% of the HHs’ windows lack insect mesh although they are located in Mosquito prone areas and 63% their basic furniture in a bad condition. SIF conducted three focus group with 28 women from Khan younis and Rafah to assess the need of NFI. Women ranked the cooking gas, water heater, and oven as the first priority need, kitchen stove second priority, blanket and mattresses and kitchen tools are the third priority, heater is the fourth, mat and detergents are the fifth priority, and personal hygiene stuff is sixth priority and winter clothes is seventh priority. Through previous shelter projects, SIF assisted 511 HHs (211 HHs with rehabilitation work and 300 with cash assistance). SIF will continue addressing the need of shelter rehabilitation and NFI for the remaining vulnerable HH identify by SIF annual assessment.

2 - Activities

The project will support a total 1000 vulnerable HHs including 600 WHHH from SIF's lists and 400 HH at home guarantee from MoSD's list through four activities. Activity 1: Shelter rehabilitation for 200 WHHH from Gaza, Dair AL Balah, Khan younis, and Rafah live in a substandard shelter condition with average cost $2500 per HH. The rehabilitation work will include adaptation of shelters to meet the needs of PWDs, elderly persons and improving the living area to adequate isolation space for people at higher risk of covid-19 , increase privacy, improve WASH facilities , repair leaking roof, doors and windows to protect the families from extreme weather. SIF will directly implement this activity based on experience and available shelter needs assessment of potential beneficiaries. Beneficiary selection will conduct based on the prioritization criteria adopted by shelter cluster including Household Vulnerability and shelter vulnerability assessment tools (minimum standard), scoring, and ranking. Each HH will develop an Individual rehabilitation plan based on HH specific needs. A diversified plans will be revised and determine the cost of each HH repair works. Activity 2: Provision of essential shelter NFIs, hygiene and disinfection materials and light repair to 400 WHHH living in substandard and overcrowded shelters at higher risk of COVID-19 average estimation $400 cash assistance. Each HH will develop a diversified NFI package in addition to light repair (if any) based on HH’s priority need. For the above two activities, SIF will sign an agreement with the selected beneficiaries that will be enclosed with an individual rehabilitation plan and NFI package that developed by the beneficiaries themselves and determine the amount of the cash assistance each HH will receive. SIF will conduct one day coaching session to introduce the project, agreement clauses, Implementation plan, and provide awareness related to COVID 19 and PESA. HHs will receive shelter rehabilitation through self-help and will be provided by basic technical instruction, and negotiation skills to strengthen their capacity in handling procurement of quality materials and managing rehabilitation work. SIF will adopt mix modality of contracting and or self-help. Two factors will determine which modality will be applied at the time of project implementation i) COVID 19 emergency situation in Gaza Strip ii) Beneficiaries’ capacity and preferences. In the case of COVID 19 situation where lockdown and restriction movement imposed on the ground, most properly contracting approach is more suitable to control safety of the beneficiaries and all stakeholders involved in the work. If the situation allows easy movement, HHs will be consulted regarding their preferred modality -cash for self- help repair or hire a contractor as well as cash for self-procurement of NFI or direct distribution. Activity 3: provision of COVID 19 Hygiene and cleaning kits average cost $60 to 400 families at home quarantine from MoSD verified lists. SIF will implement this activity in partnership with BLDA in Gaza and North Gaza. BLDA will be directly responsible for the implementation and SIF will responsible for overall management, monitoring, and reporting. BLDA and SIF will keep continuous coordination with MoSD to select home quarantined families from the lists verified by MoSD. SIF will provide technical specification of standard COVID 19 family Hygiene and cleaning kit and support BLDA in developing BoQ for procurement. BLDA will undertake the procurement, stockpiling Hygiene and cleaning kits and distribution of the kits to targeted beneficiaries. BLDA will apply COVID 19 protective measures during the procurement, storing the kits and distribution. The distribution will take place upon the updated lists of affected families received from MoSD and in full coordination with MoSD, shelter cluster and local authorities. SIF will monitor the distribution through the presence in the field and through phone call to the targeted families to ensure that the service has been delivered safely, equally, and timely.Activity 4: Provision of protection against PSEA and mainstreaming protection procedures to all the project stakeholders.SIF will carry out series of activities related to PSEA through the following: SIF will conduct brush up training to Project team, Partner’s team covering an introduction to PSEA including SIF’s Abusive Behavior Procedures (ABP), Child Safeguarding Policy, and Code of Conduct. The training will focus on how to report SEA cases with special focus on referral of complaints of SEA, in line with IASC guidance. The training will be conducted by SIF’s HR and Protection focal point.SIF’s HR unit will ensure that all project-related contracts includes a clause on PSEA as part of staff members’ contract, service providers ‘contract, partnership with local partners to be signed as an annex to the contract. The beneficiary agreement sessions, the protection focal point and counselor case manager at SIF protection unit will promote PSEA-related awareness activities with beneficiaries through PSEA related questions and ensure that the level of awareness of rights and information on access to SEA related complaints is well understandable. Printable materials include PSEA information and complaint channel information adequately disseminated to all beneficiaries and annexed by the beneficiary agreement.Also, SIF’s protection team (Protection focal point and Counselor/case manager) will carefully monitor the protection concern related to SEA through monitoring visits, interviews, and FG. In case any SEA case arises during the project implementation, SIF protection focal point will identify the case and refer it either internally to the counselor/case manager at SIF protection unit or through relevant external referral services

It has been built on the use of a results-based monitoring tool PMP where all results will be monitored against planned results and indicators using M&E tools/MoVs relevant to the indicators. The PMP is a very effective monitoring tool that articulates the M&E plan that followed and implemented by the Shelter Manager and the MEAL Manager. All data will be collected timely in line with M&E plan including: Baseline/shelter assessment is established to measure the project objectives targets indicators (using shelter minimum standard with focus on upgrading/rehabilitation elements). The project team (Project Manager and Site Engineers) will closely monitor the project implementation through daily or frequently presence in the field - depends on the modality of implementation that will be applied. Daily minoring report will be produced and shared with SIF Programs Manager. Any problem that may be emerged will be captured and corrective action will immediately take place. In case of contracting modality, weekly meeting with the contractor will be conducted to discuss and document the progress of the project and the quality of the work according to minimum standard. Another layer of monitoring will be carried out by MEAL Manager through frequent monitoring visits in order to capture the impact of the different shelter assistance provided through the project and collect success stories. MEAL field visits’ reports will be shared with the shelter Project Manager and follow up plan will be jointly set to ensure prompt feedback and learning. MEAL Manager will conduct focus groups and individual interviews with sample of beneficiaries to collect their feedback and assess their satisfaction. End line survey is planned using shelter assessment tool in representative sample of HHs that received the intervention to assess results against indicator targets and to measure project success as well as to discuss with beneficiaries their perceptions and experiences in terms of protection from harsh wither, privacy, safety, and dignity. Post monitoring distribution survey by phone call or individual interview will capture beneficiaries’ satisfaction and feedback specifically for home guarantied . Upon completion of the project, SIF will conduct an internal evaluation and learning workshop including all project team and participates from finance and logistic departments. The results of the workshop will be used for improving future shelter interventions.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

SIF takes into account the Core Humanitarian Standard to build inclusive beneficiaries’ communication, participation, and feedback mechanisms over the project cycle including sharing information, listening carefully to targeted beneficiaries and involving them in decision-making to contribute to effective intervention and improve the quality of the delivered services. Beneficiaries’ feedback will support a real-time adaptation of the project to precisely fit their specific needs. It will be used to hold the project team and the contractor accountable and to inform upward accountability to senior management. Each beneficiary will decide on the priority need of rehabilitation by drafting individual rehabilitation plan. The daily feedback through interaction with site engineers will be valued and used to develop trust and improve the rehabilitation works on an ongoing basis. A formal feedback will be through focus group discussions or interviews with women and endline survey by MEAL manager. SIF has an effective complaints and response system that involves direct communication flow among beneficiaries. Two complaint and feedback boxes exist in SIF office as management team meets bi-weekly to sort out the complaints and refer them to the responsible manager to respond within the response duration. A complaint and feedback template is enclosed with the beneficiary agreement. Due to COVID 19 situation SIF will encourage the beneficiaries to convey their complaint and feedback though phone call. A phone number has been allocated for receiving beneficiaries’ feedback and managed by the person in charge of receiving complaint and feedback. The beneficiaries will be oriented to the complaint and feedback mechanism from the beginning at the time of singing the agreement in order to increase their sensitization of the process of giving feedback in, build confidence ,and overcome fears about giving feedback

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The project is realized based on a needs assessment that has been carried out with participation of the beneficiaries where beneficiaries identify the gaps of shelter that negatively impacted their safety and raising protection concerns. Despite the methodology of the project implementation -either through contracting approach or shelf help , and cash modality , the beneficiaries will be given full freedom to decide on their specific and priority shelter needs in a way that maintain their dignity. Also it will foster build back better approach to provide long standing rehabilitation works.The beneficiaries will participate in the implementation of the upgrading works to ensure that upgrading works meet theirneeds and they will be able to input how the rehabilitation works can further address their protection needs. In the case of contracting modality, SIF will oversee the contractor’s works to ensure there is no discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect, exploitation or any other harm done to beneficiaries specifically any potential harm related to COVID 19 , and it holds the contractor accountable. A specific emphasis on applying protective measures and behaviors related to COVID 19 to ensure the protection and safety of all people involved in the project (project team , beneficiaries and laborer). SIF will ensure compliance by all parties to its protection Code of Conduct and protective measures related to COVID 19 SIF will ensure compliance by all parties to its protection Code of Conduct. SIF mainstreams the Abusive Behavioral Procedure among all project stakeholders including the beneficiaries as anyone can submit a real, clear, and accurate description of the facts of abuse or misconduct incident by writing directly to stop@secours-islamigue.org. The Abuse Behavioral Procedure documents will be an essential part of the contract document with the beneficiaries. At the time of signing the agreement with the contractor, SIF will conduct an orientation session for the contractor and workforce on the code of conduct andwill emphasis on respecting the culture sensitivity of working in the households headed by widowed women. SIF’s protection unit’s team (Protection focal point and Counselor and case manager) will carefully monitor the protection concern by building the capacity of the shelter project’s team, targeted beneficiaries, and the contractor team by enhancing their understanding of protection aspects, and provide an avenue to address any protection concerns such as GBV, child abuse and or child labor through monitoring visits and mentorship. In case any GBV case arises during the project implementation, SIF counselor will refer any case(s) through relevant referral services. The rehabilitation works will take into account any special needs according to Gender -Age and any potential disability. The project will address their needs of PwDs such as handrails and ramps. SIF applies the Core Humanitarian Standard from the beginning of the project design. At the end of the project SIF completes CHS self-assessment tool and the final score of the assessmentis used to further improve the accountability and protection mainstreaming. SIF will disseminate all lessons learned and good practices in protection mainstreaming withinthe project among other programs in SIF and with shelter cluster. A complaints mechanism is in place that is used in previous SIF shelter projects. In realization with the cultural and social challenges specific to the targeted beneficiaries with theemphasis of doing no harm approach, field staff will consist of men and women to ensure that women beneficiary are comfortable communicating with SIF womenemployees SIF complies with REGULATION (EU) 2016/679 OF THE EUROPEANPARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL of April 27th 2016 on the protection of individuals’ data. SIF uses a dignifying visibility and reporting system.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

SIF is already working with the women-headed households as part of its sponsorship program and hence has a good relationship with and access to the beneficiaries, facilitating acceptance of the project. The fact that SIF works with a pool of socially vulnerable HHs ensures that the basic shelter intervention are targeting one of the most disadvantaged groups in society. SIF will apply the Shelter cluster standardized criteria for vulnerability of the different thresholds including proportion of children under 5, type of housing unit,, availability and condition of household assets, household dependency ratio (adjusting for disability and chronic illness), and designated hardship (employment) as well as number of children /youth in school andUniversity, number of people with disability and elderlies. This will enable all the women headed households identified by SIF annual assessment in substandard housing condition anequal opportunity to participate in the project application. SIF will ensure that PWDs, elderlies and others at risk of COVID 19 have access to the project services and enjoy hygienic living conditions. The principles of impartiality, neutrality, independence and humanity are realized in beneficiary selection as a scoring system is used to prioritize the beneficiaries solely based on their humanitarian state and the prioritization criteria adopted by shelter cluster and the socioeconomic and technical considerations and scoring matrix. SIF will apply mix contracting and self -help modality approach to enable any of the targeted women headed household do not have technical capacity to manage the shelter works by self- help. Likelihood of acceptance is further increased by the close involvement of beneficiaries throughout the project cycle. Physical access to the communities where rehabilitation and distribution works take place are good, with main roads paved and all targeted beneficiaries are in accessible areas in the four governorates in Gaza Strip.Given the situation of COVID 19, SIF will apply the safe and control measures against COVID-19 and will coordinate the Critical Staff Movement in Gaza according to Standard Operating Procedures (SOP) developed in September 2020 by the Access Coordination Unit, UNOPS.
