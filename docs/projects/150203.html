Project Summary

This proposal is in-line with the objectives and framework of the OPTI country HRP strategy under strategic Objective 2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL. Further, this project is also aligned with the WASH Sector HRP objectives as follows:

• Ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable access to safe and sufficient WASH services in accordance with dignity of the unserved, underserved and most vulnerable Palestinians under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank • Strengthen WASH preparedness and response capacity to the unserved, underserved and most vulnerable Palestinians under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank to reduce threats and to cope with emergencies and shocks

The specific objectives include: -To provide an appropriate, integrated WASH response for vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in fragile and critically under-served communities by improving sanitation and sewage networks at the community and HHs levels -To Improve access to safe drinking water and enhance safe water handling at the HH level in targeted communities.
The objectives are in line with the needs identified in the HNO and Oxfam’s recent assessments

Needs

The needs assessment is premised on the WASH Cluster Ad Hoc Situation Report (July 2018), community vulnerability mapping study conducted by Islamic Relief (2017-2018) and the gender sensitive vulnerability risk assessment (VRA) conducted by Oxfam in October 2018 to address accessibility to safe water and adequate sanitation services and their impact on women and girls. The electricity crisis is seriously affecting access to safe drinking water in Gaza, with dire consequences on its population that has obliged them to purchase water from private suppliers. This resulted in increasing the burden on most vulnerable households. The quality of the water is questionable, due to increased contamination risks as a result of extraction and sewage contamination. 96% of Gaza's coastal aquifer is below WHO standards for drinking water. According to the 2018 HRP, 24,300 housing units are considered as substandard houses, in need for improvement or upgrades to overcome overcrowding and privacy constraints. People living in overcrowded houses suffer from the consequences of harsh weather conditions. Generally, people live in high temperature HH conditions due to inappropriate thermal insulation or good ventilation. The substandard housing units affect living conditions of people with disabilities and persons with chronic diseases. In April 2018, Oxfam’s assessment indicated that women are mostly affected by the lack of access to safe water and sanitation services, reporting high concerns about their children’s hygiene, health and safety. Women carry the responsibility of dumping/emptying cesspits during sewage flooding which overburden women’s household chores. Based on the vulnerability mapping study conducted by IR, and CMWU reports, Amer and Aslan communities in Bait Lahia were prioritized to assess WASH needs in coordination with the WASH Cluster members including area focal points. WASH needs were prioritized to improve adequate water and sanitation services including proper sewage water disposal through installing sewage water connections. Oxfam and MAAN verified these needs in October 2018 through a rapid assessment which revealed that 124 HHs in Amer and Aslan are not connected sewage networks, therefore rely on unsafe sewage disposal systems including cesspits and septic tanks. The assessment highlighted the need to improve access to adequate water and sanitation services due to shortages in drinkable water supply chain. The assessment also found that the majority of population at Aslan area have poor hygiene practices especially in terms of water handling which increased the risk of contamination in the water chain from source to consumers. However, 27% of the surveyed people reported that they have at least one diarrheal case during the last two weeks noting that 55% of the cases are among children and 72% of in north area assume that diarrheal diseases caused by contaminated water while 20% considered swimming in polluted sea water as the main cause. Based on the article carried out by Oxfam and WASH cluster and published in OCHA bulletin July 2018, More than 110,000 m3 of raw sewage and poorly treated effluent are discharged on a daily basis through 9 sewage outlets spread in Gaza’s coastal line. The pollution levels of the wastewater discharged into the sea during the first half of 2018 declined by some 20 per cent compared with the April-December 2017 period, attributable primarily to increased emergency fuel support to the relevant facilities. However, current pollution levels remain nearly four times higher than the international environmental health standard which can lead to disease. To date, there is a lack of internationally standard comprehensive seawater quality tests. Accurate mapping and monitoring of the coastal zone is vital, knowing that the poor environmental status of the coastal areas requires sustainable planning and management.

Activities

This project will respond to needs of targeted male & female beneficiaries including children, elderly & people with disabilities. The rehabilitation & upgrading of sanitation facilities will contribute to alleviating the burden on women & children. The complementary hygiene promotion, seawater pollution study & public awareness campaigns will enhance gender & age responsiveness by enhancing women’s knowledge & awareness.. MA'AN will implement the project in coordination with Oxfam with joint responsibility for M&E activities & stakeholder engagement. MA’AN was fully engaged in the project’s design, needs assessment, & community consultations. MA'AN works closely with vulnerable communities in diverse locations including farmers, women, children & youth & has extensive experience in WASH & access to sustainable & safe water, sanitation services in Gaza. Output 1.1: Rehabilitated & maintained sanitation facilities for underserved targeted communities at the HH & community levels Activity 1.1.1: Install 1,770 meters of sewer lines & house sewage connections in Amer & Aslan. Target: 124 households (806 individuals, 399 males, 407 females) Activity 1.1.2 : Rehabilitate/maintain HH sanitation facilities for vulnerable & under-served families in Amer & Aslan. Target: 124 HHs (806 individuals, 399 males, 407 females)Output 2.1: Increased public awareness on public health risks. Activity 2.1.1: Carry out awareness raising sessions on safe water h&ling & hygiene promotion at the community levels in Amer & Aslan. Target: 900 HHs (5220 individuals, 2584 males, 2636 females) Sub activities: 2.1.1.1 Carry out awareness raising activities on hygiene promotion at HH level using the total behavioural change approach. 2.1.1.2 Raise hygiene awareness on safe water h&ling through community sessions, events to minimize water contamination & the incidence of diarrheal diseases identified through the need assessment. 2.1.1.3 Distribute hygiene kits including essential personal hygiene items & household cleaning materials to 450 HHs. 2.1.1.4 Carry out a public health awareness campaigns in Amer & Aslan. Activity 2.1.2: Carry out a study on seawater pollution at the north beach including awareness campaign on its health hazards. (This study will enrich WASH sector information about the health impact of sea water pollution on Gaza population especially in the north && its result will support hygiene promotion actions to reduce the diarrheal diseases identified through needs assessment) Target: 200,000 individuals through mass media campaign Sub activities: 2.1.2.1 Carry out a mapping of coastal zone including classifying risks && safety zones. 2.1.2.2 Carry out a comprehensive assessment using WHO st&ards about the long-term impact of sewage on the sea water quality & outline measures to ensure the safety & attractiveness of the coastal zone. 2.1.2.3 Carry out a study about public & governmental awareness about health risk hazards of swimming, fishing, or living in close proximity to high risk & polluted zones. 2.1.2.4 Carry out awareness raising sessions targeting local authorities & NGOs about health risks & safe areas for swimming. 2.1.2.5 Develop technical guidelines for sea water pollution monitoring & control procedures. . 2.1.2.6 Carry out a mass media campaign on seawater pollution & public health risks. Output 3.1: Improved drinking & domestic water essential services & facilities for vulnerable & under-served targeted population Activity 3.1.1: Establish a Water Resources Management Committee to oversee the water-related activities in Aslan. Target: 850 HHs (4930 individuals, 2440 males, 2490 females) including 10 community committees members. Activity 3.1.2: Provide drinking & domestic water tanks in Aslan. Target: 150 HHs in Aslan Activity 3.1.3: Develop & implement a water safety plan in Aslan. Target: 850 HHs (4930 individuals, 2440 males, 2490 females).

Indicators

Based on Oxfam’s Common global Approach to MEAL and Social Accountability (CAMSA) the project will integrate a comprehensive Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) framework. The MEAL framework will promote gender sensitive collaborative and effective, results-focused and accountable MEL and social accountability practices throughout project implementation. Learning and feedback will also be key practices that will be an integral part of the MEAL framework and will be documented by Oxfam in close coordination with implementing partners. In case of emergency, Oxfam’s minimum standards for MEAL and gender in emergencies are applied. Gender-relevant needs, actions, and issues will be monitored during the project implementation ensuring gender sensitive project reviews and evaluations. Monitoring tools, data gathering analysis, and reporting will encompass qualitative and quantitative gender and age disaggregation and analysis. This will help in tracking performance against indicators, implementation plan. Monitoring tools such as baselines and end-lines. key Informative Interviews (KII), Stories of change, Focus group discussions (FGDs) and others will be included as relevant. In OPT, Oxfam developed a comprehensive Feedback, Complaint, and Response Mechanism (FCRM) under its Saving Lives Program (including under this project) and will be piloting a mobile case management tool to capture real-time data from the field through use of Information Communication Technologies (ICT). The FCRM ensures access of women and men to submit their feedback with safety and privacy. Feedback may be received either online, verbally or through hotlines and feedback boxes. The system automatically refers feedback to relevant program staff and is monitored by Oxfam’s MEAL Officer. However, complaints related to safeguarding, GBV, misconduct, or fraud, will be referred to Oxfam’s Country Director and safeguarding team and will be treated with confidentiality to ensure privacy.
