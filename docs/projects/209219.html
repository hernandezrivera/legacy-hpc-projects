Project Summary

The ongoing conflict has led of widespread displacement, economic crisis and disruption of food production. Approximately 17.7 million people are facing high level of acute food insecurity across Sudan. Of those, about 4.9 million are in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency), and almost 12.8 million people are in IPC Phase 3 (Crisis).
WFP’s primary focus is to reach the most food insecure populations (equivalent to IPC phase 4), due to funding constraints. The vast majority of the people in IPC phase 3 will not be assisted, despite being in need of life-saving humanitarian assistance. This will increase the risk of this demographic slipping into IPC phase 4, particularly with the continuation of the current conflict.
WFP’s emergency general food assistance will target 3.1 million out of 5.6 million1) of the acutely food insecure population (Residents, IDPs, and Refugees) in Sudan. This concern is particularly acute as WFP’s unconditional food assistance has comprised almost the full caseload of the cluster response post-conflict. Given the mounting need amid budget constraints, WFP will focus on geographical and household targeting to ensure available resources reach the most food insecure.
WFP will undertake cross-border operations for access constrained locations especially for West and Central Darfurs through the Chad-Frachana Corridor, as well as through South Sudan (Reink and Aweil) to reach people in East Darfur, South Darfur and Kordofans as well as other population group who have been double displaced in Al Jazeera and Kosti.
Activities/Outputs:
For the project's duration, WFP will support 3.1 million vulnerable people, excluding refugees with lifesaving general food assistance. 20% of the assistance will be in the form of cash-based transfers (CBT) while 80% will be in the form of in-kind food. WFP is scaling up unconditional CBT in its response, as this modality enables the most vulnerable people to address their multisectoral needs. The assistancewill be provided in close collaboration with other WFP’s Cooperating Partners, UN agencies, NGOs, and local partners, by building synergies.
WFP’s transfer value for CBT assistance has not been adjusted since August 2022 due to funding constraints. Monthly price monitoring indicates a significant food price increase (above the 15 percent threshold) that warrants a revision of transfer value. Therefore, the transfer value for half ration was adjusted from the SDG 5,000 to SDG 7,000/person/month (USD 11), for all categories of beneficiaries, i.e, IDP, resident population and refugees.
WFP Sudan’s targeting approach entails a geographic component, where localities and locations are identified based on a combination of assessed needs, with the IPC being the main reference point; the impact of conflict; level of displacement; and access and security as informed by the Humanitarian Access and Civil Military Coordination mechanism.. Household targeting, in which households are selected for assistance, is executed via three different targeting methodologies adapted to the operating environment on-ground. Blanket targeting is done in access-constrained areas, such as in Khartoum and Geneina, where, in line with WFP’s “no regrets” approach, all households in conflict-affected areas are eligible for assistance. In more accessible areas, status-based targeting is done among the newly internally displaced people living in state-arranged shelters and settlements as well as newly displaced camp-based refugees (e.g. refugee camps in White Nile). Community-based targeting is done in accessible areas to identify newly displaced households living in host communities and the households hosting these IDPs based on their level of vulnerability and food insecurity. Resident households in IPC Phase 4 are also targeted using community-based targeting.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

n alignment with the WFP Humanitarian Protection Policy, WFP remains committed to ensure that the assistance does no harm to the safety, dignity and integrity of the people it serves. The key objective is to improve the quality of food assistance programming by strengthening gender mainstreaming, protection, and accountability to affected population (APP) including Community Feedback Mechanism (CFM) as well as strengthen conflict sensitivity and conflict mainstreaming in programming. WFP has revamped its CFM to promote and ensure that we continue to hear the voices of beneficiaries through toll-free help lines. Furthermore, WFP has established robust digitalized help desks at over 75 percent of the ongoing food distribution sites. These infrastructure support community engagement activities to ensure two-way communication. Through dedicated senior conflict sensitivity advisor together with WFP protection and gender officers, WFP engages with communities, including through deployment of staff deep in the field during food distributions to understand the dynamics and challenges faced by our beneficiaries, particularly women, children and the marginalized.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

WFP deploys multiple approaches to share information and collect community feedback. This includes: Face to face with individuals (in homes) and at distribution sites (helpdesks) through regular interactions before, during and after food distributions. The feedback received is processed and are resolved either on the spot or through referrals to specific functional units withi WFP or to other mandated organizations. Through a functioning WFP toll-free hotline where beneficiaries can contact WFP directly WFP uses flyers and leaflets to raise awareness of the toll-free line (1460). Logged cases are handled in a secure and confidential. The received cases are safely managed in a corporate case management system, SugarCRM to ensuring data protection. PSEA WFP uses a variety of approaches to enforce its zero-tolerance policy on SEA: Developing the capacity of WFP and partner staff to receive, respond to, and escalate SEA cases. Sudan-specific PSEA training manual developed to train WFP, CP, services providers, and community workers. Ensure adequate capacity at the level of the CO and the area offices in support of PSEA efforts. Enhance community awareness of rights, prohibited practices, and channels for reporting violations, which may include face-to-face dialogue and PSEA posters. During partner selection, due diligence was performed to ensure PSEA policies are in place for each partner

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

WFP strives to promote inter-agency collaboration in Community Feedback Mechanism. WFP and UNHCR have an MOU to coordinate efforts and facilitate referral systems. WFP will advocate the expansion of this approach with other UN agencies. In addition, WFP works closely with the PSEA advisor and actively contributes to inter-agency fora such as PSEA network at national and state levels. Regular participation in these forums enhanced collaboration and coordination to strengthen WFP’s approach to PSEA throughout the project.
