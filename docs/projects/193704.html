Project Summary

Ensuring access to improved, safe, equitable, and sustainable WASH services, as well as enhancing preparedness capacity to mitigate and prevent floods, are considered urgent priorities to guarantee the protection, dignity, and resilience of the most vulnerable communities of the West Bank. The proposed intervention is fully in line with the Strategic Objectives 1, 2, 3 of the humanitarian community in the occupied Palestinian Territories (oPT) (see Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2022) and it is complementing WASH Cluster Specific Objectives 1, 2, and 3. The proposed activities were built upon the WASH Cluster eligible interventions for HRP 2023, targeting the most vulnerable communities according to the WASH 2022 vulnerability mapping and to the Protection Response Plan (PRP) an output of WeWorld-GVC Community Protection Approach (CPA). The intervention is also in line with and contributing towards the SDG 6 “Ensure access to water and sanitation for all” and complements other initiatives submitted by the West Bank Protection Consortium (WBPC) for the purpose of the HRP 2023. Finally, the intervention has been coordinated and endorsed by the Palestinian Water Authority (PWA) and is in line with the PWA Strategic Plan (2017-22) and complies with the Water Master Plan (2017-35) for Area C.
WeWorld-GVC in partnership with MA’AN Development Center (hereinafter MA’AN) aims to target the core vulnerabilities of people in need by: (1) increasing access, availability, quantity, and quality of water at household (HH) and community level, by connecting 13 communities in Area C (8,865 beneficiaries) through installation/rehabilitation of water networks, transmission pipelines, and HHs connection; increasing water storage capacity for 14 communities through construction/rehabilitation of cisterns or providing PE tanks at HH level (426 beneficiaries) and at community level (1,100 beneficiaries) (2) guarantying a safe and adequate level of sanitation and hygiene services at the household level, through the installation of mobile latrines and construction of HHs sanitation facilities and provision of hygiene kits and hygiene promotion campaigns in 3 communities in Area C (336 beneficiaries) (3) promoting floods mitigation and prevention, by the construction/rehabilitation of stormwater networks and drainage systems in 2 communities in WB (3,580 beneficiaries) (4) developing awareness products on access restrictions to WASH, advocating for the right of the most vulnerable Palestinians to receive adequate access to WASH services (10 awareness products). It is estimated that the awareness products can reach about 2,000,000 viewers/recipients in a year. In total, the intervention will support 13,447 direct beneficiaries (3,274 boys, 3,130 girls, 3,352 adult men, 3,216 adult women, 216 elders male, 259 elders women and 237 are PWDs) in 29 communities in the Governorates of Bethlehem, Hebron, Jenin, Nablus, Qalqilya, Ramallah, Tubas and Tulkarem, mainly in Area C (59%) and Area A/B (41%). The intervention will be delivered through different response modalities, encompassing primarily service modality through contracting, some activities by in-kind delivery and when feasible, through cash modality through WeWorld-GVC’s Community Implementation methodology. WW-GVC and MA’AN ensure that all the proposed interventions will trigger a positive effect on the reduction of the WASH vulnerability and increase the resilience of the communities, especially those under threat of forcible transfer in Area C.

1 - Needs

WASH vulnerabilities continue to be an urgent priority in the Westbank (WB) with more than 760,000 people having WASH needs according to the HNO 2022, in particularly more than 660,000 Palestinians having limited access to water and regularly facing water shortages. The most vulnerable Palestinian communities with inadequate WASH services, cannot adhere to the WHO recommendations of frequent handwashing and environmental disinfection and end up being at a greater risk of COVID-19 and future outbreaks. More than 480,000 Palestinians consume less than 50l/c/d, which is well below the 100 liters recommended by the WHO. In Area C particularly, consumption rates do not even reach 30 l/c/d in 149 communities and 125 communities are mainly dependent on unreliable, unprotected, and unimproved water sources (e.g. rainwater harvesting) or are forced to purchase trucked water as a coping mechanism (Vulnerability Mapping 2022). Additionally, 50 communities in WB are challenged by limited access to adequate household sanitation facilities leading to negative practices as open defecation, sharing latrines with other families or using shelter spaces for their personal sanitation needs, eventually triggering health and sanitary risks and protection concerns including gender based violence (GBV). Climate change will not only exacerbate the impacts of water scarcity in the most vulnerable communities but also increase the frequency and impacts of flooding events in WB according to climate change numerical projections. Currently more than 100,000 families in WB are affected by floods (Vulnerability Mapping 2022) and this number will only increase, impacting the livelihoods of Palestinians, their health and safety including triggering waterborne diseases and complicating access to and isolating communities. This intervention is grounded on the identification of access to water and sanitation and flooding mitigation as priority human needs by the humanitarian community (see HNO). Also, thorough evidence-based assessments were performed identifying the most vulnerable communities lacking these WASH services and those with a high risk of displacement and therefore lacking proper coping mechanisms to withstand the protracted threats and the recent COVID-19 pandemic impacts. These assessments are: - Community Based Protection Approach (CPA), initiated in 2015, updated on yearly basis, in the framework of the ECHO WB Protection Consortium, where WW-GVC collected quantitative and qualitative data, including a specific WASH sector-analysis for 172 communities (in 2022) located in Area C, H2 and EJ for the development of Protection Community profiles and Protection response plans; - WASH cluster vulnerability mapping of 2022- Water Master Plan for Area C targeting 247 communities, developed by WW-GVC and AAH in 2017 in coordination with PWA. Moreover, WW-GVC and MA'AN conducted an in-depth field assessment in September 2021 in the most vulnerable communities of the WB, identifying at institutions, community and HH level the gaps and constraints in providing adequate WASH services, highlighting the communities in need of urgent interventions. Lack of WASH facilities especially affects women, girls, elderly and PWDs. The responsibility of access to WASH often falls on women and girls for the households in Palestine and inadequate WASH extends the time that they spend bearing WASH responsibilities and jeopardizes women’s and girls’ sense of privacy and personal safety especially during menstruation. Lack of adapted infrastructure complicates the access to WASH services to elderly and PWDs. Increased access to WASH facilities stands as the humanitarian response to improve communities’ challenging living conditions and will contribute towards gender equality and increasing the safety and dignity of the most vulnerable community members. Based on the various assessments and in close coordination and with full support of PWA, WW-GVC and MA'AN identified following priority needs. Firstly, 3 communities were identified as highly unserved regarding access to sanitation and hygiene, not having functioning latrines for all the inhabitants, leading to open defecation and posing health and safety hazards affecting particularly the 80 women and 78 girls of these communities. Secondly, 26 communities were identified with having inadequate access to water especially impacting the lives of the 2,256 girls, 2,318 adult women, 343 elderly and 171 PWDs. 12 communities are in need of increased water storage capacity at HH level with 7 communities consuming less than 30l/c/d and 5 communities between 30 and 50 c/l/d and 10 of these communities being mainly dependent on water trucking. 2 communities, namely Samaria Village (Nablus) and Fahma al Jadida (Jenin) are in need of increasing storage capacity at community level. Thirdly, 13 communities were identified as in need of either construction or rehabilitation of their water networks and household connections to increase their access to water. Finally, 2 communities (Khirbet al Mkahhal and Khirbet al Hamaam in Jenin governorate) were identified as under high risk of flooding impacts due to the increasingly frequent extreme weather events in winter, in combination the poor maintenance of stormwater networks and drainage systems. Vulnerabilities to flooding includes risks of displacement and destruction of their properties, and severe health and environmental consequences if the stormwater mixes with the overflow wastewater and solid waste. Hence, a flood mitigation and prevention intervention is highly needed in these 2 communities especially for the 127 elderly and 63 PWDs. All considered, WW-GVC and MA'AN developed an integrated WASH response dealing with the source of WASH vulnerabilities at community and HH level, including water availability, adequate sanitation and hygiene facilities, and flood mitigation and prevention adapted to the needs of the most vulnerable community members.

2 - Activities

Proper coordination was performed with PWA, Local Authorities, and major stakeholders active in the WASH sector on the target communities to avoid overlapping. Governorates, Municipalities and Village Councils (VCs) will be engaged and involved in the collection of data related to the baseline and the end line. Concerning the construction and rehabilitation of household water cisterns, the intervention will encompass, when feasible, the adoption of WeWorld-GVC Community Implementation methodology (CI), which is mixed service - cash modality entailing the creation of a Community Project Committee (CPC) composed of representatives of the communities/CBOs (gender-balanced), which are the local implementing partners. CPCs are responsible for the implementation and maintenance of the infrastructures. The supply of materials for the implementation would be under the full responsibility of WW-GVC and MA’AN and it is considered an in-kind cost. The beneficiaries will receive direct costs coverage for their work through conditional cash transfer modality, and it will be paid upon receipt of a payment request and according to a progress report (verified by the Technical Coordinator and Project Manager) that certify the work accomplishment along with the payments amount eligibility. If not feasible full service delivery modality will be adopted. Regarding the provision of PE water tanks for the communities, the development of awareness products on access restrictions to WASH, and the provision of trucked water for the communities and schools, it shall be considered as a full service modality of implementation. While the construction/ rehabilitation of stormwater network/rainwater drainage systems and the provision of household latrines are also considered under the services delivery modality. Capacity building and monitoring will be done by WW-GVC along with the intervention. Before any activity, a risk analysis, including legal consultation with the lawyer of the community will be conducted. Specific Standard Operating Procedures are followed during implementation to minimize/mitigate risks and WW-GVC CI will allow reducing the risk of confiscation and other potential obstacles related to the coercive environment of Area C, enhancing the commitment and ownership of the beneficiaries, to guarantee safety, dignity and avoid harming (“do-no-harm”) the affected population. Therefore, the intervention will be committed to ensuring gender sensitiveness and inclusiveness on the facilities design for all, including girls and boys with special needs and/or disabilities. In accordance, the design of the sanitation facilities will ensure privacy for women and children with the provision of dignified space with good physical and sound insulation and will be adapted to special needs for PWDs. Proposed Key activities: Activity 1 – Community Implementation setting-up:The creation of a Community Project Committee (CPC) and, before any activity, a risk analysis, including legal consultation with the lawyer of the community and the Project committee. Activity 2 – Need assessments: At the beginning of the intervention, together with the CPC and stakeholders, a need assessment will be carried out to verify any change of needs and/or objectives, as well as to assess the security situation to enable the interventions to be conducted according to plan. 5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

SERVICE DELIVERY: this will be the main modality due to the technical complexity and operational difficulties: a procurement process will be initiated to select a contractor that will implement the works and deliver the finished product to the beneficiaries. Usually, the construction materials form about 50%, while the skilled labor costs form 50% of the total cost for each intervention. Capacity building and monitoring will be done along with the intervention. IN-KIND: This modality was chosen for every other activity in order to provide inputs and tools to beneficiaries to improve their access to WASH services. The beneficiaries will be asked and enabled to be committed to contribute and preserve the functioning of the provided inputs or performed improvement activities.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Well-designed and well-managed mechanisms for handling beneficiary’s complaints, feedbacks and allegations are of utmost importance for an inclusive, reliable and properly informed decision process. All feedback and complaints coming from beneficiaries will be given high consideration. WW-GVC and MA’AN are committed to guarantee transparency, fairness and equity towards all our beneficiaries and will jointly scrutinize the content of the complaints and/or feedback and-if necessary, will further investigate the matter to assess whether an adjustment to the programming or disciplinary actions are needed. Also, the two partners commit to addressing the complaints and feedback within a reasonable timeframe according to the severity of complaint / allegation.

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :

WW-GVC and MA’AN will arrange a dedicated hotline for complaints coming from beneficiaries and their communities throughout the whole duration and up to 6 months after the end of the project (as such mechanism is being implemented by both organizations as part of the responsibilities of their M&E departments). This will complement WW-GVC’s Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM), accountability and commitment to guarantee transparency, fairness and equity towards all our beneficiaries. It aims at establishing a system that gives individuals and groups the possibility to report complaints, suggestions and inquiries in a secured way, and receives a response in a timely manner according to the severity of complaint / allegation. All complaints/feedback are reported to the WW-GVC’s and MA’AN Monitoring and Coordination manager by focal points who refer the case to relevant staff according to it sensitivity, guaranteeing confidentiality, anonymity and DO NO HARM principle. This will guarantee separation between field staff and the person responsible for managing feedbacks, complaints and allegations. Additionally, through the Safeguarding focal point and relative procedures, the system in place to collect feedback and complaints also incorporate elements for a safe disclosure and proper Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). Upon registration of the complaint on the database, the Admin officer analyses the complaints and classifies each of them according to the internal classification system. If the complaint requires to follow up and consent was provided, the complainant will receive an answer within 15 calendar days. Once the case is solved, the complainant will receive a phone call, if consent provided, from WW-GVC’s and MA’AN’s Monitoring and Coordination manager. Then the complaint should be addressed and resolved within 15 days by WW-GVC and MA’AN team and/or Complaint Response Mechanism team.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

The entire project cycle including - need assessments, designed strategies, proposed interventions, set indicators, outcome measuring procedures – takes into account the need of the most affected groups such as women, children, PWDs and elderly, in order to achieve gender and age-sensitive relief through responsive assistance that meets the material and protection needs of specific vulnerable groups, ensuring adequate and equitable participation and access to humanitarian goods and services, and eventually preventing/mitigating potential negative effects of the intervention on different gender/age groups. In all interviews, surveys, focus groups and field visits, beneficiaries of all population groups will be informed about the complaint mechanism in place and modalities of accessing it. Accessibility to all population groups is a priority and is maintained through the whole project life-cycle. Field staff is obliged to inform and explain to all direct and indirect beneficiaries of the presence of a complaint-response mechanism (CRM), including the referral pathway for complaints and allegations and how to safely access them. Also, the beneficiaries’ agreements will contain special parts about the complaint mechanisms including the dedicated number for this subject. The complaint mechanism will be both in-person, at any time during the field visits of the staff of the two organizations, or remotely through the dedicated hotline. The latter will ensure anonymous and discrete means to file in a complaint, ensuring that all population groups may access the mechanism.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Build upon WW-GVC CPA, the proposed project ensured community participation -promoting the equal participation of the different groups in the community such as people with disabilities, women or children – in a way which endeavors to minimize exposure to additional hazards and mitigate as many risks as possible. Meaningful community involvement takes place since the identification of the needs and of the most suitable and equal solutions, resulting in increased empowerment at the individual and community level. WW GVC and MA’AN want to assure the compliance with the Do No Harm principle and that the programming reflects preferences, needs and resources expressed by the members of the HHs in a participative way. This approach is built from a multisectoral perspective, which supports the communities in assessing their vulnerability and in mapping appropriate protection responses, aiming to increase the capacity of the community to make informed decisions about their safety. Response and activities is carefully designed to ensure that interventions will not impair beneficiaries’ safety or expose them to further risk, at the same time upholding their dignity throughout the process. Activities are based on the preferences and needs expressed by the members of the HHs, considering the different threats that affect girls, boys, women and men of all ages including PWDs. Particular attention is given to input/feedback provided by women, whose participation is strongly encouraged in any decision-making process; further, the intervention takes also into account the indications provided by WW-GVC “Gender and WASH toolkit for Palestine”, which provides applicable and feasible suggestions on how to mainstream gender in WASH projects in the Palestinian context.. WW-GVC adopts a well-designed and established complaint response mechanism system (WW-GVC CRM) allowing beneficiaries and the affected population to provide feedback and complaints, which will in turn influence management decisions. Whenever possible the implementation will be led by community members through a participatory approach, nevertheless, the project will also instruct subcontractors, suppliers, and service providers to implement all needed measure to meet “Do No Harm” requirements, especially in light of COVID- 19 ongoing outbreak, by using all the preventive measures and equipment to minimize the risk of contagion. Finally, all staff involved in the projects is aware of, and in adherence to the Do No Harm principle as outlined in WW-GVC Code of Conduct and related policies including Child-Protection; Anti-Fraud, Bribery & Corruption; Anti- Terrorism; and Policy Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. COVID-19 protection measures (face masks, gloves, hand sanitizers, physical distancing) will be followed by WW-GVC and MA’AN teams during all project activities to prevent the disease spread.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project, in line with the sustainable humanitarian approach, aims to provide immediate relief, ensuring equity, sustainability, and impartial access by conflict affected communities of WB to decent WASH services. The project aims to increase access to water, sanitation, and hygiene, as well as the efficiency, predictability, and equity of the WASH service and has been designed considering the direct and indirect impact on women, girls, boys, men with or without disabilities. The planned interventions will improve the access of the most vulnerable communities including children and PWDs to safe water and sanitation, through the access to safe and equitable water, increasing the HH storage capacity, access to adequate and adapted sanitation facilities, rehabilitation of WASH facilities in schools and intervention towards flooding mitigation and prevention. The strategy, activities, and materials used for implementing the project were developed and selected to tackle the assessed needs, together with and in the best interests of the beneficiaries, building upon lessons learned from similar projects implemented in the past. As a consequence, the project is expected to positively impact all community members, but in particular, women, as there will be a noticeable decrease in time and effort spent in fetching water at the household-level, especially for girls and women who are usually in charge of this task; therefore, fewer tasks will be imposed on girls and women who will not have to face long walks to collect the water, reducing exposure to the protection risks related to their trips to the water source (i.e. attacks and harassment from Israeli Forces and settlers). In the case of families with PWDs members, access to water will help to alleviate the work of the caregivers and help to overcome difficulties. Finally, a specific design of WASH facilities (i.e latrines) will guarantee their ease of access during use and maintenance to all members of the community and institutions, including girls, women, boys, and men, with or without disabilities of all ages.
