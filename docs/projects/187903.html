Project Summary

The project goal is to enable children and vulnerable populations affected by conflict and displacement in Rural Damascus and Dara’a governorates to meet their basic needs and benefit from community-focused initiatives, through a multi-sectoral approach.
Terre des hommes - Lausanne, Switzerland (Tdh) will contribute to this objective through the direct provision of an integrated package of public services oriented assistance in order to build the resilience of individuals and communities, especially conflict-affected population (vulnerable returnees, host, displaced and non-displaced households living out of camps) in four communities in Rural Damascus (Markaz Daraya and Duma sub- districts) as well as in Dara’a governorate.
In 2022, the proposed intervention will target 17,500 beneficiaries (50% male and 50% female; 2,625 <5y; 8,750 5-17y; 5,68818-59y; 437 >59y) by adopting an area- based and integrated approach addressing WASH, Education and Child Protection immediate needs through increased access to and availability of water, educational and child protection services provided to communities. The planned WASH interventions will be implemented with careful consideration to short and long-term service improvement for host communities, IDPs and returnee populations alike. The focus will be on supporting resilient systems, capable of continuing to provide a predictable minimum service to users under extremely fluid circumstances. As a result, the following criteria will be considered in order to enhance the planned WASH interventions: security, technical sustainability, economic efficiency, flexibility and overall sustainability. The latter implies performing the comparative cost-benefit analysis of the environmental conditions that would encourage supporting large, centralized systems instead of small-scale initiatives dependent on private sector initiatives. Finally, it is worth mentioning that WASH-in-school activities are intended to provide children (grade 1-6) with access to functional and safe educational facilities by performing rehabilitation works.
In 2023, the proposed intervention will be scaled up and replicated in Rural Damascus and in Dara’a - one additional governorate.

Implementing Partners

The project will be implemented directly by Tdh – Lausanne. Nevertheless, Syria Trust for Development (STD) as well as national and local authorities, will be key partners in relation to access-related issues. Emergency construction and rehabilitation-related activities will be managed and supervised by local contractors and monitored by Tdh. Local private contractors will not carry responsibility for programmatic decision-making.
In order to guarantee sustainability, ownership and quality control-, community-based organisations will be involved in every step of the project - whenever possible and applicable.

5 - Implementation of activities

The activities will be implemented directly by Tdh. WASH NFIs and school material will be distriubuted by Tdh staff while the infrastructure works will be completed by private sector contractors. The following is a step-by-step summary of the process for the hardware (latrines, hand-washing facilities, water points and school general repairs) and software components: - Exisiting Tdh's preliminary assessments and coordination indicate school and community needs in each site - Preliminary designs are developed in collaboration with representatives from the authorities and local communities - Consultative process is completed - Distribution lists and detailed technical designs are finalised in consultation with local authorities and communities - Submission of distribution lists, technical drawings, BoQs, Scopes of Work, Operation and Maintenance Plans - Local Private sector contractors are appointed based on TdH's agreed procurement processes - The works are implemented by local private sector contractors; non-skilled labor are drawn from local populations and in accordance with Tdh's CoC, and accompanied by robust monitoring - The distributions are performed by Tdh staff - Tdh construction monitoring visits to check progress against work plans and certify completion against the designs and BoQs - Tdh performs after- distribution/completion/installation monitoring. 10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The main risk identified is that population, including victims, do not report allegations of SEA, which results in the organisation being unable to refer the allegation to investigation, hold personnel to account and assist victims. Therefore, TdH will regularly train staff on PSEA policy and will sensitise community and relevant stakeholders about PSEA and how to respond to misconduct. Additionally –and in also in accordance to do-ho-harm principles-, TdH will ensure that monitoring and coordination mechanisms with beneficiaries, civil society members and local authorities are in place and abide by the AAP framework. A specific complaint and feedback response mechanism will be implemented by also identifying a dedicated PSEA Focal point. In accordance with the facilities and centers where the project will be implemented, information and awareness material will be provided and displayed. The project will ensure that no individual can use the equipment and material as a mean of pressure or exploitation against beneficiaries, especially children, girls and women. Therefore, every recipient will be identified and final destination of the equipment will be clearly stated in the donation document and presented to the different stakeholders.
