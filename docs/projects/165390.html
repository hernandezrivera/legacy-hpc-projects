Project Summary


INSAN CHARITY started constructing and equipping PHC center in Burdaqly community which will be launched in May 2020. The project aims to increase access to basic curative and preventive health care services for IDPs and conflict-affected populations in targeted areas in the Idlib governorate Dana sub-district ( Burdaqly community) .
activities focus on meeting the health cluster’s response strategy and EHSP through the provision of primary health care that includes reproductive, maternal, Antenatal & postnatal care, and Child Health.
general practitioners provide treatment of non-communicable diseases & communicable diseases. The project provides pharmacy and nutrition services as well as supportive services with simple laboratory performing CBC, urine analysis, blood group, and stool analysis.
100% of all provided services including drugs and lab will be given free of cost.
The project will be part of the referral system, 1 ambulance provide referral services to specialized centers and hospitals.
The project will be registered in the health cluster while the 4w report will be submitted monthly . in addition to that, the information of the project will be filled regularly in HERMS on the prime system.
The project will serve a target of about 20,000 of the host community and IDPs, especially in the surrounding camps.
(( Target Population 20000 out of them 8800 IDPS / 10,275 Female -9,725 Male - 3215 children under 5 years))

4 - Needs

Eight years of crisis have heavily disrupted the health system in Syria, including 65 attacks on health care reported between January and September 2019. Up to 70 percent, of health workers, have left Syria and many others have been injured or killed. 64 percent (13.2 M) of the population lives below minimum standards for healthcare workers. Health personnel shortages are particularly acute in areas affected by recent hostilities and displacement. Distance, overcrowding and long waiting times are top barriers reported by patients to access health services. Accessibility and cost of transport also impede access to services for persons with disabilities. . The availability of medicines and treatment costs are also leading determinants of child mortality in Syria.Health facilities are entry points: humanitarian life-saving and life-sustaining essential health services at primary level – are vital to addressing population health needs and preventing further harm. Availability of humanitarian life-saving reproductive, maternal, newborn and child health services is critical in humanitarian settings. Recent assessments indicate the need for improved supply of medicines and services within PHCs, – particularly for noncommunicable diseases (NCDs), which are estimated to account for 45 percent of all deaths.The health sector recognizes population groups as the most vulnerable: children under 5 years of age, women of reproductive age (15-49 years), older persons (59 years and above), IDPs a. Additionally, areas of active hostilities, IDP camps, last resort sites, overburdened hosting communities, and locations without reliable water sanitatio and shelter remain highly vulnerable to epidemic borne diseases.According to KII conducted by INSAN CHARITY , bardaaqly is one of the neglected communities which doesn't have medical center provides medical assisstance . where there are increasing number of IDPs who settled in the camps arounds the area.

5 - Implementation of activities

INSAN CHARITY ( IC ) will be responsible for implementing the program in coordination with the health cluster and stakeholders. INSAN will apply the Essential health package to effectively and efficiently deliver comprehensive quality health services that are equitable, accessible and sustainable, ensuring the timely delivery of cost-effective, integrated and standardized health services tailored to meet the priority health issues faced by the majority of the population. furthermore, IC will be responsible for all program and relevant financial reports to the donor and to the cluster ( 4Ws ) and other related reports. Capacity building through training on different topics to strengthen the capacity of health care providers and community health care workers to provide essential health services. IC will ensure that suggestion boxes and phone numbers and email for raising concerns are available. Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) officers will have access to emails, complaint telephone phone calls, and the only key for the complaint boxes. It will be the responsibility of these officer to handle all complaints, analyze feedback, and report directly to M&E Manager, working at IC office in Turkey. A report of the complaint will be shared with the Project Manager. The quality and accountability will be monitoring by IC to ensure meet the standards. IC supply chain department in coordination with our team in Syria and turkey will ensure all needed medical equipment and operational supplies are delivered on time. medicine and medical supplies will be provided to beneficiaries for free. The project will Strengthen the capacity to investigate and detect disease outbreaks through lab services. Awareness sessions will be conducted by CHWs and Nutrition services will be provided for CU5 and PLW. The project will support referral through a referral system and ambulances network.
