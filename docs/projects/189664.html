Project Summary

The projects seek to ensure that the most vulnerable people in need and Persons with Specific Needs (PSNs) including IDPs, retunes and the host community will benefit from scaled up protection services with a focus on critical lifesaving and resilience targeted assistance through comprehensive structured protection monitoring, allowing to improve efficient targeting for overall protection, , as well as GBV, CP and HLP. The activities will be implemented in Juba, Wau, Bentiu, Bor, Malakal, Yei, Yambio and Torit and prioritized respective counties
The project will facilitate the use of the Protection Monitoring tool piloted in 2021 and used at household H/H level. This will offer standardized methodology of collecting data, which will be used by protection actors across the country for informed planning in support to vulnerable men, women, boys and girls of all ages and diversity components. Protection concerns identified will be dealt both on individual protection services/case management basis and through community-based approaches (by ensuring referrals to the respective actor or through provision of various types of direct assistance by UNHCR. Community based protection will involve quick impact projects to address infrastructure and livelihood challenges faced by communities.
Protection challenges will be addressed through awareness raising protection risk, targeting communities and their leaders. Support will be provided through child friendly spaces, outreach to reduce incidence of teenage pregnancy and child labor; support for psychosocial support affecting children.
SGBV issues will be addressed through outreach to ensure communities understand the benefits of reporting of incidence, addressing taboos linked to SGBV enhancing psychosocial support, case management (including cash-based interventions), legal assistance, renovation and construction of safe houses.
The project will address House Land and Policy issues by ensuring displaced populations benefit from outreach, legal aid/representation, counselling, support to replacement of ownership documentation through working with the Nationality, Civil Registry, Passports and Immigration.
Overall the project will seek to empower communities affected by displacement through supporting gaps, outreach and awareness, material and cash assistance to support their resilience and ensure sustainability of assistance provided.

1 - Needs

IDPs, host community and returnees continue to be affected by multiple multidimension challenges leading to a complex protection environment and heightened needs. The most critical issues affecting them include floods, inter-communal and armed conflict, inflation, lack of rule of law and access to justice pathways, which generate further GBV crimes and incidents, freedom of movement, HLP rights. leading to population movements influenced by various of pull and push factors and increased vulnerabilities among displaced populations as they seek safety, humanitarian assistance and attempt to rebuild their lives. UNHCR through the use of countrywide protection assessments and monitoring using Key Informant Interviews (KII) and Focus Groups Discussion (FDG) involving IDPs, community leaders, IDP returnees and host communities; which comprise of women, men, boys and girls, across different age groups and diversity components such as the elderly, disabled, female headed households and other groups gathers data to identify the most vulnerable among displaced population groups and addressed the following critical needs:  Protection Monitoring: Improved targeted assistance to assist the most vulnerable through direct assistance, case management/cash assistance for the most vulnerable or referrals to facilitate general protection.  GBV: Outreach on the impact of harmful traditional practices that constitute GBV cases and empowering communities with information on the nature of assistance available  GBV: Provision of critical support to GBV survivors not only women and girls but men and boys and other population group such as the youth or disabled. This includes but is not limited to dignity kits, construction and renovation of safe houses, case management and psychosocial support. GBV in South Sudan continues to affect the population having a significant impact on women and girls. Resources to address GBV continuously dwindle as services linked to case management such as psychosocial support require significant resources to provide. Safe houses are also few in number, while medial support to attend to GBV cases may not always be available. These elements create significant gaps in GBV response. Harmful cultural practices among communities continue to pose a risk to women and girls. Forces marriages, teenage pregnancies, rape, wife inheritance, intimate partner violence, often emanate or a linked to cultural practices. Reporting of GBV incidents is considers taboo leading survivors to suffer in silence before they speak up. This context compounded by the limited voice women have in Society creates further challenges. The project will seek to address these risks and gaps through: Community awareness/outreach to address these issues using vehicles such a SASA Together, FDGs among women, analysis of information from GBV IMS to support response address gaps, livelihood opportunities to empower women to address food insecurity.  CP: Infrastructure support through reconstruction or renovation of amenities such as child friendly spaces and provision of psychosocial services to enhance Child Protection and deter risks to children, UNHCRs value addition to CP corresponds to the additional benefits CP offers to women who constitute majority of UNHCRs person of concern. UNHCR participatory assessments indicate that there are growing concerns among women in displaced populations on the lack of support/resources available to meet the needs of children and in addressing issues impacting women.  HLP: Legal aid, counselling and outreach/awareness to support HLP cases for women, men, orphans, disabled person, widows and other marginalized groups seeking restitution, reclamation, of their properties  Support for IDPs to return and support to returnees and return areas

2 - Project activities

The project will be implemented through the following activities: 1. General protection: Protection Monitoring tool piloted in 2021. will support the systematic data collection and analysis of protection information based on aggregated data sets guided by AGDM with inclusion of particular groups – PWDs, PSNs. Protection response aims to be evidence-based , reflecting the voices percepts of people in need. Men, women, girls and boys will be targeted. Protection concerns will be identified and dealt in one of two ways, through referrals for or through direct assistance by UNHCR. To achieve this static and mobile protection monitoring will be used. Additionally, the projection monitoring tool will support safe dignified access to services through mainstreaming protection throughout the humanitarian response. 2. Child protection needs are significant in South Sudan, activities will include the creation of child friendly spaces, outreach to reduce incidence of teenage pregnancy and child labor; support for psychosocial support affecting children. 3. GBV component: Activities to address SGBV cases will include outreach to ensure communities understand the benefits of reporting of incidence, capacity building to law enforcement on how to support GBV survivors, addressing taboos linked to SGBV which encourage stigma, negative coping mechanism, forced and early marriage; enhancing psychosocial support, cash based assistance, repair and reconstruction of fences to reduce GBV incidence, case management including medical assistance and legal assistance, renovation and construction of safehouses. 4. House Land and Policy: Support will seek to empower those affected to by illegal occupation, land grabbing or loss of documentation. Activities will include: outreach on HLP issues, provision legal aid, counselling and representation and assistance in the re-issuance of ownership documents, replacement of lost documents through working with the Nationality, Civil Registry, Passports and Immigration; In cases where vulnerable men, women, boys and girls across all age groups and diversity components are identified further assistance through plastic sheeting and shelter support in the form of renovations or reconstruction will be provide including elements such as boreholes, dykes for flood mitigation and latrines to ensure the environment enables IDPs to live in dignity. The activities will be implemented in Juba, Wau, Bentiu, Bor, Malakal, Yei, Yambio and Torit

5 - Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)
5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The use of Cash is based on preference noted by IDPs during the UNHCR 2021 Participatory Assessment. Cash assistance is also favorable among IDPs due to its flexibility, allowing the IDPs freedom of choice on assistance they receive. For instance, UNHCR has used cash-based assistance to support the most vulnerable IDPs access medical care that was only available at referrals hospitals, support GBV survivors with further support or to support transportation costs to access services or even buy formula milk for orphaned infants in instances where lactating mothers are too ill to feed or have died.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Despite the challenges in South Sudan markets functions and are able to supply goods and some limited services to persons of concern at a fee. Regional infrastructure through roads which bring supplies into the county from Kenya, Uganda and Sudan mostly ensure goods are available. In addition, some items IDPs may require items that are not available in NFIs can be easily purchased if IDPs have access to funds.

UNHCR has the information materials on PSEA in protection desks, community centers with contact information on PSEA reporting mechanism. UNHCR and its partners are also actively implementing SASA Together and there are dedicated focal points appointed for PSEA. Partners are also peer assessed on PSEA elements ahead of any partnership agreement renewal

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Through the use of a combination of protection monitoring - participatory assessments, key informant interviews, focus group discussions and the community-based approach, UNHCR will maintain regular contact with persons of concern to collect data and analyze the needs of the population. This will inform programming and implementation of activities. Feedback will also be sought through existing feedback mechanisms such as the toll free line, suggestion box and community structures to facilitate corrective actions. The opinion of the community on how best to implement activities will be considered where operationally feasible. Feedback will also be received through individual interviews, focus group discussions and post distribution monitoring.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The project has safeguards to ensure the risks do not occur. Humanitarian assistance will be well coordinated with community leaders engaged ahead of distributions to limit possibilities of conflict linked to humanitarian resources. The most vulnerable among host communities will also benefit to ensure inclusion. SEA risks will also be minimized as community leaders and partners receive SEA related training and are aware of the zero-tolerance policy. Beneficiaries too will be empowered through training on their rights and how to report SEA. Banners and visual material in local language will also be hang at distribution locations and at protection desks and partners premises. Feedback mechanisms including suggestions boxes and a toll-free number will be availed. Protection services and referral services will be wide spread due to static and roving protection monitoring, persons with specific needs if identified will be assisted accordingly. UNCHR has an existing methodology for identifying and assisting persons with specific needs including disabled persons and the elderly (please see below). As such persons with PSN codes are deliberately included in assessments, consultations and decision making. UNHCR identified Persons with Specific Needs through fields mission with implementing partners, using mobile teams to conduct house hold surveys, to distinguish persons with specific needs from the general population. Once identified, PSNs are classified into codes to establish the nature of assistance they may require: Some PSN codes include: Single Parent (SP) without support, Woman at risk (WR), SGBV (SV), Child at risk (CR), Unaccompanied or separated child (SC), Elderly at risk (ER), Person with Disabilities (DS), Serious medical condition (SM), Person with specific legal and protection issues (LP), Family Unity (FU) and Torture (TR).
