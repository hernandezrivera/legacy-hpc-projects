Project Summary

The project aims to establishment and operation of two permanent veterinary centers, each center will have a mobile veterinary clinic that serves remote locations and villages in Aazaz, and countryside of Azaz , Mare’ and countryside of Mare’. the duration of the project is 1 year.
The project aims to support the veterinary services for cattle in the areas of Mara and Aazaz and their villages. The number of livestock in Azaz is estimated among 22,000 head of cattle, 1500 cow and 30,000 head of cattle in Mara including 2000 cows (according to the local council in Azaz and Mare') The project aims at supporting these clinics with the necessary veterinary services
The project will provide three vaccines for livestock:
septicemia vaccine: targets 40,000 head of cattle.
 smallpox vaccine: targeting 40,000 sheep.
the aphthous fever vaccine targets 40,000 head of cattle.
about 1500 breeders will benefit from the vet mobile clinic services ( Consulting, vaccinations, Treatment, prescription) in Azaz.
2000 breeders will benefit from the vet mobile clinic services ( Consulting, vaccinations, Treatment, prescription) in Mare'.
The project aims to support these clinics with essential veterinary services. Treatment of endemic diseases and epidemics, particularly epidemics, in targeted area, livestock development, increasing production capacity and ensuring the health of their products, as well as enhancing the income level of livestock breeders and the development of rural women in all villages and regions.
leading to reduce the migration of residents citizens, rural to other areas, and to maintain the overall health of the livestock and the environment of those areas and to protect citizens from the risk of common diseases transmitted from animals to humans, and increased commercial traffic related to livestock as reflected in the increase of people's income level.
Objective: To contribute to the support and protection of animal assets, through veterinary services in the areas of Aazaz, and countryside of Azaz , Mare’ and countryside of Mare’.
output: Establishment of two permanent veterinary centers and two mobile clinics in the districts of Azzaz and Mare’ providing veterinary services and vaccination campaigns in the project areas.
Clinics will be equipped with all the possibilities of diagnosis, treatment, and immunization of all diseases. It provides pharmaceutical services for livestock, in order to provide various veterinary services and livestock development in targeted communities.
The project has been designed to be a sustainable program. The past experience helped implement projects similar to the project to a broad understanding of the needs of the community and educators, which enabled the design of integrated projects and maximizing the resources available.
Specific hours will be allocated for livestock breeders (women) for consultation and treatment of their livestock within the veterinary center.
The dates of vaccination campaigns will be announced to suit all cattle breeders, whether men or women.
Safety considerations will be taken during the implementation of all project activities.

Needs

As the Syrian crisis approaches its eighths year, civilians continue to suffer the burdens of the conflict marked by destruction and disregard for human life. an estimated 6.5 million Syrians are food insecure and an additional 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity.Many people have been displaced several times. Children and youth, millions of whom are known only as conflict, account for more than half of the displaced, as well as half of those in need of humanitarian assistance. The parties to the conflict act with impunity and commit violations of international humanitarian law and human rights law.The area of Azzaz, its countryside, Mara and its villages are relatively stable within the Euphrates, so the establishment of projects that support livelihoods in war-affected areas is of great importance. The response should not only save lives but protect and strengthen livelihoods This improves the economy of the region, increases long-term resilience and reduces the vulnerability to long-term war and its consequences.The population of Mare' in the Euphrates Shield is 45,000 person . The displaced constitute 23%, ( 12,500 people) , 76.6% of whom are from the city of Aleppo and its neighboring villages. 8.4% are from the eastern regions of Syria and 5.5% from eastern Ghouta, 3.1 from Homs and 6.4% from other areas.The population of the city of Azzaz 120 thousand people, the displaced 50%, Rural Azaz has 100,000 residents, and camps for the displaced in rural Azaz 350 thousand displaced people.The number of livestock in Azaz is estimated among 22,000 head of cattle, 1500 cow and 30,000 head of cattle in Mara including 2000 cows (according to the local council in Azaz and Mare')The idea of stable and mobile veterinary clinics has been very Needed by beneficiaries of animal breeders to reach distant places that do not reach the services of clinics and large veterinary centers, especially in far distances and remote villages on the one hand, and on the other hand the high costs of veterinary treatment and medicine for livestock breeders as well as spread Epidemics in cattle herds which threaten livestock in these areas in light of the lack of veterinary services provided to livestock breeders.The project will contribute to the support and protection of livestock in the region and has a clear impact as it is one of the few veterinary centers in the region that will serve the area of Azzaz and the surrounding areas.The project targets all sheep and cattle breeders (women or men) who own livestock in the project implementation areas.The provision of veterinary services is an important strategy for strengthening communities and helping beneficiaries to protect their livestock. The provision of veterinary care will help reduce the effects of chronic diseases and epidemics that can affect the production of milk and meat, which is the most important resource for livestock breeders. Which will be adversely affected by expensive prices of meat, cheese and dairy sources in the region.

Implementation of activities

ONSUR will establish two vet centers in Azaz and Mare’ and two mobile veterinary clinics to serve remote areas where beneficiaries can not reach the centers in the city.Implementation will be in accordance with:1 - Coordination with the local council in Azaz, Mari and rural areas.2-cooddinatin with the local agricultural office in the region.3 - Recruit a technical team to oversee the work and commitment to the quality of implementation. Implementation activities:Activity .1 • rapid need assessment, Conduct a baseline assessment• Establishment of fixed and mobile clinics in the areas of Mara and Izzaz• Announce and promote project activities and explain all services to the community• Registration of beneficiaries in each implementation area• Conduct vaccination campaigns, each vaccine type according to a specific schedule.• Providing veterinary and therapeutic services to livestock through fixed clinics.• Providing veterinary and therapeutic services to livestock through mobile clinics.• Awareness lectures, provided by the veterinarian during field mobile clinic tour.• M&E activities. • Final narrative, financial reports.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk:1- the risk of shelling by the Government of Syrian forces and its allies in the targeted locations or and infighting between different armedgroups continues causing loss of access to the targeted locations inaccessible:Mitigation:ONSUR will assess several options like the temporary suspension of activities until the areas become more secure again.2- Communities and key stakeholders reject our project/plan;Mitigation:Include the local and IDP community in the design and the implementation of the activities of this project, conduct community mobilization ahead of the project promoting to its proposed activities and ensure the community acceptance. In addition to the close coordination of relevant stakeholder.3- A change of government policy/attitude in Turkey could lead to a closure of the organization and negatively impact on humanitarian access to Syria.Mitigation:As a registered NGO in Turkey, ONSUR is expecting that this legal framework will, at best, give the leeway to operate. If the general attitude towards cross-border humanitarian assistance should change, ONSUR will adapt their modus operandi to new requirements that may arise to ensure a continued assistance, although ONSUR has its operational offices inside Syria that can continue following up all the needed activities with the remote management from ONSUR headquarters in the neighboring countries.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption:- access to the target locations.- the availability of the needed materials and equipment.- the good security situation,- access to Syria remains possible and safe through the neighboring countries.• Risk: high prices and transport due to the crisis.• To mitigate the risk: assess the market prior to the start of the project to see the updated prices. • Risk: The reluctance of breeders to vaccinate because of fear to make harm to their livestock.• To mitigate the risk:the poject team build the trust of the educator in the project activities, through a dedicated staff, explain the activities and objectives of the project to the stackholdrs, and community participation, before the implementation of the project. • Risk: frequent visits to veterinary centers due to the gap in the implementation areas and the lack of veterinary centers in the region.• To reduce the risk: specific days will be specified to visit each region, and inform the beneficiaries in coordination with the local authorities in the region, and determine the number of fixed hours for the fixed veterinary center.
