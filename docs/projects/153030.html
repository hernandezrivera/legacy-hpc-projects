Project Summary

This project will seek to meet the emergency WASH needs of IDPs, Returnees and Host Communities in Koch,Leer,Mayendit and Panyijiar. The total targeted beneficiaries for this project is 11,000 , approximately 1833 HH . The project beneficiaries is further broken down as follows: Women: 4,000 Men: 1500 Girls: 3,000 and boys: 2500. Coalition for Humanity (CH) plans to support in provision of access to safe water, sanitation and Hygiene services in the transitional setting within the target locations. To do this, Coalition for Humanity (CH) will conduct rapid community consultations to specify WASH needs and reconstitute Community Water Committees in targeted payams within the four counties. Aside from this, Coalition for Humanity (CH) will support rehabilitation of protected community wells in at least 9 payams and sensitize the communities on the need to develop latrines and home waste disposal pits.
To mitigate the widespread GBV risks in the target areas, Coalition for Humanity (CH) will conduct a gender WASH risk assessment in the communities in order to secure access to water and sanitation points that reduce the GBV risk to women and girls. Coalition for Humanity (CH) will also distribute MHM kits for women and girls of reproductive ages.
As internally displaced persons return , there is a likelihood of pressure piling on the limited water and sanitation facilities, creating a risk of water borne diseases. To minimize the potential for this, Coalition for Humanity (CH) will conduct community hygiene promotion by training community hygiene promoters (CHPs), continuously monitor and report on the condition of water points, provide communities with water storage training, treatment tablets and equipment to ensure water available is safe for domestic consumption.
Coalition for Humanity (CH) will also intervene in the nearby schools (schools within TPA in Leer Town where there is a population of about 4000 IDPS) as well as in 8 primary schools it is supporting in Guat Payam north of leer town and help to establish clubs that champion good hygiene practices. Activities shall include but not limited to supporting parents, teachers and pupils with messaging about washing hands with soap after visiting the toilet and before meals as well as ensuring that the schools are supported to dig latrines, separate for boys and girls. The latrines will have a hand washing point with Water and soap available.

Needs

The conflict in South Sudan has had negative impacts on the entire population with particular adverse impact experienced by women and children. Since the beginning of the current conflict in 2013, millions have been displaced internally with hundreds of thousands (majority of them women and children) crossing the borders as refugees. The total targeted beneficiaries for this project is 11,000 . The project beneficiaries figures are further broken down as follows: Women: 4,000 Men: 1,500 Girls: 3,000 and boys: 2500. Leer, Mayendit and Koch counties in central Unity have been the epicentres of the conflict within former Unity State. Panyijiar County considered relatively safe received over 25,000 IDPS that overstretched the host communities coping mechanism. On several occasions, different locations in the targeted counties have changed control between government and opposition forces. The rich oil deposits in the counties make it an automatic region of interest for both sides of the conflict. In the recently released IPC in September 2018, it is expected that in the period from October - December 2018 and another stretch from January to March 2019 have projected pockets of population under the catastrophe (IPC phase 5) and large number in emergency phase 4 in Leer and Mayendit.The congestion in displacement settings in the different islands and swamps where population have sought refuge during the conflict creates special waster and sanitation challenges. According to Rapid Assessment Report conducted in Leer Town by DRC (DRC, 2018), host community, returnees and IDPS in Leer particularly in Pading, Payak, Koam had minimal access to WASH facilities. About 90% of the population in this areas practice open defection and do not practice hand washing. Drinking water was mainly consumed from the nearby rivers and streams without any treatment raising the needs to urgently distribute water tablets and storage facilities. There was no proper domestic waste disposal method visible and waste was strewn across the fence. With the on-going rainfall, this could be a time bomb for water borne diseases. The county has suffered cholera epidemics in 2017 as a result of lack of proper sanitation. These findings were echoed in similar assessments internally carried out by Coalition for Humanity in Leer islands south of Leer town and in the northern part of Leer and Mayendit County.Sparse populations and large distances towards water sources create new risks for women, who shoulder the burden of fetching water for their households. For this reason, WASH intervention is closely linked to our GBV project to mitigate any risks associated with water by women and girls. These women risk being attacked en-enroute to the water points while the long distances create an extra burden as women struggle to fetch water for their families. The lack of sanitation facilities, create the risk of disease, as human excreta is not safely disposed. Furthermore, there still exists insufficient awareness and lax cultural attitudes towards proper hygiene and safe sanitation practices creating significant risk of exposure to communicable diseases. This situation is expected to worsen with some expected return of IDPS to mainland as because of relative peace. Coalition for Humanity (CH) ES/NFIs team works closely with other partners to give alert for efficient and effective intervention to avert any emergency or catastrophe. Given the return of relative peace after the recent signing of the revitalized agreement on resolution of conflict in South Sudan (R-ARCISS) in Addis Ababa and the celebration of the revitalized peace deal in Juba, a number of displaced persons are likely to return to their homes where they lack basic WASH facilities, food or medical care. Most of the WASH facilities were destroyed during the conflict or have been dilapidated due to lack of maintenance for prolonged periods.

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

There are will be a record of all beneficiaries reached, the date and time, type of intervention served and a response from the beneficiaries. Distribution centres will be decided upon by all stakeholders and shall be done at convenient locations that are easily accessed by all beneficiaries. Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

The targeted intervention areas have a record of high cases of SGBV and as such community dialogues shall be done separately for men and women to address priority based on the different genders groups to increase openness and freedom of expression. Men shall be involved in planning and execution to mitigate the risk of domestic violence. Distribution of various WASH pipelines will be done during the day in good time for the beneficiaries to return home before dark. There will be different queues for males and females to ensure order and access for all during WASH core pipeline distribution.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Distribution of WASH pipelines and other related materials will only be done upon mutual agreement between Coalition for Humanity & the beneficiaries. Consensus will be sought through community dialogues. The involvement of the beneficiary at all stages before, during and after intervention will ensure that the dignity of the beneficiary is upheld at all times
