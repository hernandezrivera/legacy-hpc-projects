Project Summary

Plan International Bangladesh (PIB)’s education in emergencies (EiE) programme started in 2018 and, based on the learnings of two years into the response, will continue in 2020 to support across the 3 objectives of the Cox's Bazar (CXB) Edu Sector's JRP, corroborating equitable access to inclusive, quality learning opportunities and community engagement for girls, boys and youth in both camp and crisis-affected host communities in Ukhiya and Teknaf. In 2020, PIB will continue to support equitable access to culturally and age appropriate care, learning and development in a safe, protective and inclusive environment, spanning early childhood care and development (ECCD) to functional literacy, numeracy and life skills for older children, adolescents and youth. In camps, PIB will sustain its community-based learning facilities (CBLF) modality which has successfully engaged over 20,000 G & B as part of the 2019 JRP in different-sized facilities across different age groups, while ambitiously transitioning a portion of the learners into 10 LCs and piloting 2 two-storey LCs, conscious of the considerations of community acceptance and GoB approvals. Meanwhile, PIB will continue to support education in the crisis-affected host community (HC) building on its long experience with GoB across the country, and collaboration in CXB over 2019, particularly in ECCD.
Structured trainings for learning facilitators in camps and teachers in HC will aim to strengthen the quality of teaching and learning process for G&B across the age spectrum targeted by CXB Edu Sector, with regular monitoring of quantifiable and qualitative progress to feed into regular mentoring and on-the-job coaching. PIB adopts a pairing modality of learning facilitators from camps with HC Mentors, bridging the distance between both communities through capacity building initiatives. HC Mentors and education officials will be trained to provide better mentoring and supervisory support to learning facilitators and teachers for inclusive, quality education.
Strategically pivoting towards nexus programming, PIB will focus in 2020 on community-focused programmes to lobby community and authorities for support of education and mitigate sociocultural barriers to equitable access to quality learning opportunities. PIB will continue to support community ownership and engagement in G’s&B’s inclusive education by building capacities of Learning Facility Committees in camps to support equitable access to education, safeguarding and protection, including DRM and feedback, reporting and referral mechanisms. In addition, PIB contextualised and further developed a package for Parenting Education in 2019, with ECW/UNESCO funds and consultation of Edu Sector partners and GoB at national and local levels; this will be rolled out for awareness raising of caregivers of 0-8 G & B on child rights and protection, supporting G’s & B’s education, and tolerance and social cohesion. For caregivers of older G & B and/or youth, other resources will primarily draw on the Plan International global Adolescents in Crisis toolkits. Adoles. and youth will be supported to lead social cohesion initiatives to promote values of equality, tolerance and inclusion and foster a culture of peace within the camps and across with the HC.
PIB EiE team will continue to actively participate in the Edu Sector coordination platforms where the technical specialists are members of SAG and TWiG to ensure field-relevant inputs into strategic and technical discussions, as well as inter-sectoral linkages whether for certain vulnerable age groups as youth, supported through YWG, or crosscutting concerns such as CPSS and gender; PIB in fact volunteers for the role of GiHA focal point, and purposefully aims to incorporate gender and inclusion considerations for inclusive quality education as an organisational priority. Across all interventions, PIB’s programming will continue to link with GoB relevant stakeholders and Edu Sector guidance.

Objectives

Objective 1: Access - Expand and strengthen immediate access to equitable learning opportunities, in a safe, inclusive and protective environment, for crisis-affected refugee and host community girls and boys aged 3-24 years oldGender, Inclusion and CP: PIB will continue to support equitable access to all G&B to safe and protective facilities, monitored through continued SADDD collection. CBLFs offer a solution to limitations of space in camps, and aim to increase access of G&B through proximity. PIB will capitalize on opportunities for durable structures (e.g. 2-storey LCs) sought by Edu Sector, with due consideration of distance from traffic risks, crowding and natural hazards. Separate facilities, and same-sex facilitators will be dedicated for 15-24 G&B to encourage registration and attendance regularity as appropriate. PIB will seek to ensure learning facilities for 3-24 G&B in camps have accessible, functional, hygienic and child/gender-friendly WASH services ensuring provision of MHM and H&H kits. PIB will identify access barriers for CWD and take measures to address where feasible through community support and awareness raising, physical adjustments to the learning facilities and/or provision of assistive devices; while in HC schools, rehabilitation works will similarly foreground inclusion and CP, e.g. broken windows, exposed electrical wiring, dysfunctional toilets or water points, retrofitting for MHM and CWD, etc. Contingency Measures will also be taken to support learning facilities and schools reduce risks of natural hazards through awareness raising and provisions for DRM. Objective 2: Quality - Provide quality inclusive education to refugee and host community girls and boys aged 3-24 years old, aligned with Education Sector standards, and increase teaching-related professional development opportunitiesGender, Inclusion and CP: PIB will continue to purposefully incorporate gender, inclusion and CP in basic trainings of learning facilitators and teachers in camps and HC. PIB uses Edu Sector GIEP materials, as well as organisational resources such as SBK guidelines which are designed with respect to considerations of gender, inclusion, cultural sensitivity and CP, integrating DRM, PSS and H&H messages, as well as MHM for adolescents and youth. As with refurbishment, provision of education materials and equipment will be child-friendly and consider safety, protection and age-appropriateness, in alignment with Edu Sector relevant standards.Contingency Measures: streamlining DRM messaging in PD opportunities for targeted education personnel in camps and HC, especially around high-risk seasons of natural hazard (e.g. monsoon season) and health concerns. Objective 3: Community Engagement - Ensure refugee and host community ownership, as well as active and meaningful participation and engagement in the education of crisis-affected girls and boys aged 3-24 years oldGender, Inclusion and CP: Structures to ensure community participation such as Learning Facility Committees will be channels for accountability that represent a learning facility-community connection, while action will also target bolstering female leadership in those committees. Moreover, refugee and HC G&B will be mobilised for social cohesion initiatives addressing challenges they face to fully access their rights, with targeted action for CWD inclusion and using interactive methodologies such as drama and interactive reading festivals. Similar topics of inclusion and protection will be addressed in structured awareness raising sessions for caregivers through standardised Parenting Education package, addressing child rights, positive discipline and issues of child marriage, trafficking and labour.Contingency Measures: capitalise on opportunities of community engagement for awareness raising on relevant risks, and ensuring effective channels to disseminate and sensitise community members to messaging developed in consultation with relevant sectors.

Needs

PIB’s JRP project considered field knowledge and observations from ongoing projects, organisational strategy and strengths, and alignment with CXB Edu Sector JRP 2020 Strategy to inform prioritisation of proposed actions to address the multi-faceted complexities of needs in the camps and HC. Evidence on the needs consulted below resources by CXB Edu Sector:• Edu Sector PIN & Targets for Rohingya Community• Edu Sector Gap Analysis per online Gap Dashboard revised 06 Nov’19, per age group and camp – based on population update from 10 Oct and outreach from 29 Oct• Data on HC: DEO / DPEO PIN and Targets; details of primary and secondary schools in need; and numbers of out-of-school (OOS) and dropped out children• ISCG/UNHCR population data for Rohingya & crisis-affected HC• REACH ENA – Mar’19• REACH J-MSNA – Oct’19 Significant progress has been made through two years into the CXB influx response to provide safe and systematic access to learning opportunities. However, towards the end of 2019, 30% of the households across camps and crisis-affect HC still reported at least one child aged 5-17 is not attending any learning opportunities (JMSNA, Oct’19). A) In camp settings:Main factors preventing full access of children and youth to education are pre-dominated socio-cultural barriers (23%), marriage (36%) and domestic support (20%). To the barriers of gender and inclusion, lack of menstrual-friendly, segregated WASH facilities still factor, while CWD have additional accessibility barriers (e.g. ramps and improved geographical terrain), lack inclusive teaching-learning materials (TLM) and are accordingly less likely to attend learning facilities (10 to 40% less than children without disability) (JMSNA, Oct’19).An even more alarming 95% (56% females) of adolescents and youth aged 15-24 don’t have access to any educational activities (Edu Sector Gap Dashboard, Nov’19). For Gs, non-attendance after age 11 is closely linked to cultural norms limiting mixing with boys after puberty, with “cultural reasons” as the most common driver cited by caregivers and only 22% of learning facilities assessed splitting by gender. For boys, preference for attending madrassas was the most commonly-cited reason, reported for 44% of OOS boys across the age range above 11 years old. For older boys (and to a lesser extent girls), children and their caregivers also cited frustrations with limitations of the current TLM for older or more advanced learners as a key reason why older children don’t attend education. In this respect, 22% of OOS boys and 16% of OOS girls aged 6-14 were reportedly not attending because “what is taught is not relevant or age-appropriate.” Although 71% of the community consider the edu situation has improved in the camps, G, B and their caregivers are reluctant to pursue education, especially as levels 3 and 4 of the GoB-endorsed learning competency framework are yet to be approved, and around 30% of the G & B currently enrolled in level 2, will enroll at level 3 in 2020 (ENA, Mar’19). B) In the crisis-affect host community:The refugee influx in Aug’17 has significantly stretched the HC resources, whereby enrolment and retention in schools of Ukhiya and Teknaf are among the lowest in the country.Barriers to education for children and adolesc span deteriorating access to schools due to increased sentiment of insecurity, including criminality and road traffic concerns in addition to the engagement in household chores or economic activities due to high inflation in the area (32% - JMSNA’19). Moreover, the quality of education in schools has also significantly decreased as community para-teachers seek higher-paid occupations in the camps with the humanitarian community.The strains on HC resources also exacerbated tensions in the communities and towards the refugees, underlining a critical need for interventions towards tolerance and social cohesion and strategic considerations for long term solutions alongside immediate needs

Activities or outputs

Output 1: Immediate access to equitable learning opportunities, including life skills and resilience programs, in a safe, inclusive and protective environment is provided for refugee and crisis-affected host community girls and boys aged 3-24 years1.1. Learning facilities for refugee 3-24 G&B will be established (LCs), identified (new CBLFs), or sustained (existing CBLFs) in line with Edu Sector standards and guidelines.1.2. ASER+ assessment will be continued to register new refugee learners into level-appropriate sessions according to Edu Sector standards laid out in the Guideline on Informal Education Programme (GIEP).1.3. ECCD learning facilities for 4-5 G&B in HC will be identified and equipped.1.4. Government and non-formal schools in HC will be rehabilitated, including accessibility works for CWD.1.5. ECCD programmes will be delivered for 3-5 G&B in camps and HC, adopting PIB’s Shishu Bikash Kendra (SBK) approaches for play-based learning, basic PSS and education readiness.1.6. Learning programmes will be delivered for refugee 6-24 G&B according to Edu Sector GIEP.1.7. Learning facilities (3-24 G&B), and schools (4-14 G&B) in camps and HC respectively will be equipped with relevant and adequate materials for quality learning in a safe and protective environment, incl. T-L, DRM, H&H and/or recreational materials, aligned with relevant Edu Sector standards, and coordinated with GoB where and as relevant (particularly for HC).1.8. HC 4-14 G&B will be supported for equitable learning opportunities through remedial classes; life skills and resilience programmes; or awareness raising activities on inclusion and equitable education and protection.1.9. All facilitators and other education support personnel, e.g. HC Mentors, Education FPs and Aunties (volunteers to support girls’ participation and retention) will receive incentives, aligned with Edu Sector set rates. Output 2: Improved quality of inclusive teaching and learning for girls and boys aged 3-24 in both camp and crisis-affected host communities, and increased teaching-related professional development opportunities, aligned with Education Sector standards2.1. Learning facilitators/teachers and other education personnel in camps and HC will be trained in pedagogy and thematic areas of EiE, incl. DRM, PSS, H&H, CP, etc.2.2. To conduct training workshops for learning facilitators in camps, ToTs will be delivered for PIB Technical Officers and Coordinators, and HC Mentors respectively.2.3. Learning facilitators’ KAPs will be monitored in camps, in alignment with Edu Sector TPD programme framework.2.4. Learning facilitators and other edu personnel in camps will receive periodic, relevant and structured training, professional development and support according to needs and circumstances.2.5. G&B (3-24) in camps and (4-14) HC will receive adequate education materials, supplies and equipment aligned with Edu Sector standards.2.6. Refugee girls' and boys’ learning competencies improvement will be monitored and assessed in alignment with Edu Sector’s GIEP. Output 3: Communities actively and meaningfully participate in the provision and promotion of inclusive and quality learning opportunities in a safe and protective environment for girls and boys aged 3-24 in both camp and crisis-affected host communities3.1. Members of Learning Facility Committees in camps will be trained on learning facility management, DRM and participatory community engagement.3.2. Caregivers in camps and HC will be sensitized to caregiving/parenting education, esp. regarding child/youth rights, protection and tolerance and social cohesion.3.3. Girls and boys aged 10-24 in camps and HC will be oriented on leadership and life skills to take initiatives for social cohesion, promoting and supporting girls’ and boys’ equitable and continuing access to quality education, as well as safeguarding, protection and inclusion. The initiatives will address challenges they face to access their rights.
