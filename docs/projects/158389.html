Project Summary

The proposed project aims to provide free and quality healthcare for IDPs who have fled their villages in the Macina district of the Segou region of Mali due to intercommunal violence and security issues and settled on the outskirts of villages located in Niono district. Their primary needs (food, shelter, water and health) are not fully covered. The sites where they have settled are generally located far from the health centers.
Two mobile clinics composed of 4 people (1sss nurse, 1 auxiliary nurse 1 obstetrician nurse and 1 driver) and 1 community health worker in each IDPs site will provide free and quality healthcare to the IDPs. Healthcare will be adapted to the needs identified in each site, including those of pregnant women and children under 5, who are most vulnerable to the living conditions in the sites. ALIMA/AMCP’s curative activities will be associated with preventive activities aiming at reducing this vulnerability, such as antenatal consultations for pregnant women, vaccination, malnutrition screening for children under 5 and training of women on malnutrition screening techniques, so that they are able to detect malnutrition earlier and avoid complications for their children. Complicated cases, including deliveries will be referred to relevant health structures in order not to destabilize the cost-recovery system for healthcare applicable in the district.

Needs

The needs assessment carried out by AMCP/ALIMA on the outskirts of villages in Niono district where the IDPs have settled indicated that their primary needs were food, shelter, water and health. Their living conditions expose them to many kind of diseases such as respiratory diseases, water-borne diseases or diseases related to malnutrition. The prevalence of SAM in the IDPs sites is 3.5% according to a report by MSF France. But due to some factors we mentioned above, access to health care for the IDPs is particularly difficult. They cannot easily go to the health centers, the health centers cannot come to them. In such a context, the proposed project aims to respond to the urgent needs of the IDPs in the Niono district, providing free and quality healthcare through mobile clinics. There will be two mobiles clinics, each with a team of 4 people (1 nurse, 1 auxiliary nurse, 1 obstetrician nurse and 1 driver) and 19 community health workers covering all the IDPs sites who will be trained by ALIMA/AMCP. The number of CHWs per municipality is proportional to the number of displaced persons hosted. Municipalities hosting more IDPs will have more CHWs. In this way, 1 CHW will cover several villages not far from each other. The mobile clinics will allow the team to remain flexible and adapt its intervention if new sites open or if a situation needs investigating. The mobile clinic setup chosen by ALIMA/AMCP will also enable to monitor the IDPs situation in the entire district, as well as the epidemiologic situation. In case of emergency, ALIMA/AMCP will be able to quickly evaluate the needs and to respond in a short period of time. The choice to operate by mobile clinics is based on ALIMA's experience with health centers in southern and northern parts of Mali for 7 years. This experience tells us that if we interfere in the functioning of the health center with human resources dedicated to this project, this can lead to conflicts with the staff of the health center. Because people working for the project will receive fringe benefits and this can provoke envy in others. To avoid discontent and maintain good relations with health center staff, we may consider compelling measures that may impact the project budget. In addition, this problem could occur in many health centers and should be addressed everywhere. Generally, Alima avoids providing financial compensation to Ministry of Health staff. But intervening in health centers would expose us to this problem. The specificity of this project, which requires a lot of attention, does not leave enough time to manage aspects of this kind. The collaboration with health centers will be limited to the referral of complicated cases and advocacy activities to integrate IDPs sites into the advanced vaccination strategy and take them into account when ordering medical inputs. Given the free medical care offered by the project, the village population that is normally covered by the health centers on the basis of a cost recovery system could be tempted to mingle with the IDPs in order to benefit free care and destabilize the system in place. It is to avoid this that ALIMA plans to work with community health workers (CHW) based in the IDPs sites. The leaders of IDPs sites will also be closely involved in the implementation of the activities. In addition, the fact that mobile clinics will travel directly to IDP cites will mitigate the disruption of the health system.

Activities

The goal of the intervention is to provide free and quality medical care through mobile clinics for the IDPs who fled their villages in the Macina district due to intercommunal violence and insecurity and settled on the outskirts of villages located in Niono district. There will be two mobiles clinics, each with a team of 4 people (1 nurse, 1 auxiliary nurse, 1 obstetrician nurse and 1 driver) and 1 community health worker per site who will be trained by ALIMA/AMCP. They will be headed by a project coordination team composed of 1 project manager, 1 logistician/administrator and 1 medical activity manager. The project base will be Niono where the team will have offices, parking for vehicles and a dedicated space to store medical commodities as well as other inputs to be used in the framework of the project.The operating mode will be as follows: from Monday to Friday, the mobile teams will go to the sites already identified for the care of the IDPs on a rotating basis. For instance, in the municipality of Diabaly, the mobile clinic will have the capacity to visit 4 sites on Monday and the remaining sites of the same municipality on Tuesday. On Wednesday, they will continue other municipalities and so on. When all the sites of every municipality have been visited, the cycle will start again. The mobile clinics will remain dynamic and will adapt to the security context as well as the realities of the field namely the movement of displaced to other localization in the district, taking into account possible new sites and/or arrivals.On the basis of the planning, the teams will therefore visit the sites and carry out curative consultations, referrals, health promotion and preventive activities, namely:The activity will start with the triage of the patients. Unstable patients (emergencies) will be prioritized for care.The consultation and delivery of medication to patients. Each team will go to a mobile clinic with its drug supply. When pharmaceuticals are prescribed, medical staff will explain to patients how the medicine should be taken,Antenatal, Postnatal Consultations and follow-up of newborn growth,Vaccination of pregnant women and children from 0 to 11 months,Information, education and communication activities on themes like Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) and hygiene,MUAC by mother which is a training of caretakers of children under 5 years on malnutrition screening and detection of nutritional edema.The project will also have an advocacy component as the CSCOMs and the CSREF and District officials will be encouraged to integrate IDPs sites into their operational strategies (advanced vaccination strategy, growth follow-up, antenatal and postnatal care) and take them into account when ordering medical inputs.In order to specifically target IDPs, ALIMA’s mobile clinics will travel to the IDP sites, which will mitigate the possibility of local population assisting the mobile clinics and not destabilize the local health system.
