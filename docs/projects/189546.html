Project Summary

In 2020, the warring parties in South Sudan, signed the Revitalized Peace Agreement currently being implemented.
The cumulative effects of flooding and associated population displacements, localized insecurity, the economic crisis such as high market prices and currency depreciation have also consistently reduced the purchasing power of vulnerable households who are reliant on market purchases for their food and other basic needs. Prolonged years of asset depletion continue to drive the high levels of acute food insecurity in the country. Low crop production and COVID 19 outbreak, isolated insecurity incidents, disrupted livelihoods, and impeded households’ access to other food sources, such as wild foods, fish, and livestock products are some of the contributing factors to the above drivers too. Seasonal scarcity of food coupled with a general reduction in humanitarian food assistance, when compared to the recent past, will likely result in an increase of acute food insecurity in the country in 2022.
According to the humanitarian need Overview (HNO, 2021), 31 counties are described as being of extreme or severe concern and 36 counties are described as of high concern and 11 counties are moderated in the country. These mostly severely affected counties are in extreme need of immediate emergency food assistance through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs.
Among the counties most affected includes but not limited to Cueibet, Rumbek center and Aweil east in lakes and northern Bahr El Ghazal states. The number of people in need in Cueibet is estimated to be 45.8%, Rumbek center is 43,6% and Aweil east is 34.5% out of the total 8.3 million people (HNO, 2021 and IRNA, 2021 report).
PURPOSE OF THE PROJECT:
Based on the result of HNO and IRNA, 2021 report, out of 8.3 million people in need of immediate assistance, 45.8% are in Cueibet 43.6% in Rumbek center and 34.5% in Aweil east counties.
CEF in this regard intents to provide FSL assistant to the three counties aiming at.
 Improved food production and productivity through transfer of sustainable agricultural practices and technologies.
 Increased and diversified income for youth, women, and men.
TARGET POPULATION

The total target population will be 4,000 households (24,000 individuals) and will be apportioned as follows:
1- Aweil east County 1,000 households ( 6,000 individuals).
2- Rumbek Center County, 1500 households ( 9,000 individuals).
3- Cueibet County, 1500 households ( 9,000 individuals).

1 - Needs

According to IRNA cluster report of January 2022, in Aweil east County a total of 11580 People were displaced by the conflict which took place between the mesirya and the Dinka Malual communities in Yith-Pabol of Ajiep Payam. The situation has caused increase in prices of food and non-food items in the local markets within the areas. Food shortage was also observed at the household level as daily meals have drastically reduced to one meal a day. It was also observed that people are moving from one village to another in search of foods. There has also been loss of farm tools by households from all the villages, most of these tools were either burnt down inside the households or looted during the attacks.The attacked has claimed 24 lives of people lost, 11 children went missing and many others wounded. Meanwhile nearly 2620 households (HHs) were displaced to the nearest villages of Warnyiel, Majok yinthiou, War-agany, Riang-awei, Angoot, and many others ran to difference locations as far as Warawar, Malualkon Wanyjok Madhol, Malualbaai and other places seeking for safety as their homes including food items in the granaries, example grain, groundnuts were burned down to ashes.In Rumbek center, total population 4,334 households are identified and in need of food, seeds and farming tools (IRNA, June,2021).According to IRNA report of September 24-25, 2021, a total of 8,651 HHs were assessed and found to have been devastated by floods in 9 payams of Cueibet County. Crops failure is imminent because of the unprecedented levels of floods which devastated peoples’ farms. This has led to lack of food in most of the affected locations. Some of the displaced households are surviving by gathering Pumpkin’s green leaf from the gardens. Livelihood sources have been devastated too. Chickens and goats have died because of wall collapsing and there are stories of sickness of some of the domesticated animals such as foot & mouth disease.For few who were given humanitarian assistance depend on that food for 3-4 days which is not enough for everybody in those locations since the food distribution done was a targeted one. Adults and young boys and girls may spend days without food.In these three counties, the people most affected and need of immediate and long-term food assistant are the women, children, elderly, and people with disabilities.

2 - Project activities

 Training of community animal health workers (CAHWs) on animal health and livestock production (disaggregated by sex). Procurement and distribution of complementary wares (bicycles and protective gears) and veterinary kits to CAHWs to enable provision of animal health services through a cost recovery mechanism. Procurements and distribution of small livestock (sheep/goats) to households. Training of individuals households on improved animal production and health practices using Pastoralist Field School Approaches (PFSA) disaggregated by sex. Training of community-based extension workers on best agronomic practices and climate SMART agriculture (disaggregated by sex). Training of members of women groups on improved horticultural production and farming as a business. Procurement and distribution of vegetable kits to households (members of women horticulturally based groups). Training of youth, women and men on income generating activities and provision of in-kind support. Formation of Village Saving and Loan association (VSLAs- 10-20 members each). Identification and provision of support to Women Fishing Groups (WFGs) to developed business plans through provisions of grants through revolving funds. Formation and support of group-based post-harvest management facilities (10 members per group- disaggregated by sex). Provision of appropriate labor-saving technologies on post-harvesting and processing to women. Training of Community Animal Health Workers (CAHW) on inputs support, cold chain establishment and maintenance, prevention & treatment especially zoonoses e.g., RVF. Provision of timely food security information.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The information so collected before, during and after the project implementation will be use used to modify and or improve the kind, nature and quality of the services so provided to the beneficiaries. In a situation where the feedback reveals negative aspects of the services provided, the management will be able to make the necessary corrections needed to either improve, disqualify and or modify the services provided. In case it gives the positive aspects of the project, the achievement will be replicated elsewhere, Again, it will act as a lesson learn for future references for any undertaking of similar nature.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Feedback from most vulnerable people in the community are always important in making timely and effective decisions and changes to the project implementation. It is also important because it will support future project design, planning, implementation and monitoring and evaluation narratives. Lessons learn and successive stories provided about vulnerability of the most affected beneficiaries paved a way for short, midterm and long-term solutions to issues surrounding the most vulnerable communities in the project location. For example, issues affecting people with disability varies from different perspectives and location and required different approach in project design, planning, implementation and monitoring and evaluation. Therefore, to ensure that this most vulnerable people provide feedback to the project, the project will ensure they are participating in the project at various level. They will be provided with the necessary tools and means to present their issues and are part of the decision-making processes throughout the lifecycle of the project.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Issues related to protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse remains a taboo in most communities in the world and South Sudan in particular. Yet is an issue which is so harmful to the society and those inflicted or are victims of such scenarios. In this regard, through the approved PSEA documents and policies that are existing in the organization and other related document, the organization will ensure that these documents and policies are part and partial of the project implementation. This will be cascaded from the top to the lower levels of the project management and becomes a key component of the project implementation strategy. All staffs in the project will be required to agree, as pre-condition for employment, to abide by these documents and policies. These documents include but not limited to child Safeguarding policies, anti- sexual Harassment Policies, Gender-based Violence policies, Child Early Marriages policies, Child protection policies, Sexual Exploitation policies and other international and in-County related policies punishable by law. Cases of PSEA that will be committed during the project implementation will be reported, investigated and those found guilty will be subjected to the court of law and prosecuted if found guilty.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will ensure that all the necessary reporting tools such as weekly, monthly, and annual reporting templates are in place and in use to collect reports and provide information to relevant stakeholders and the beneficiaries. In addition, the MEAL department will use its current data based to ensure that all project and other related documents are kept safe for current and future use.The existing and or created feedback and complain mechanism documents use to collect information about the beneficiaries will be kept safe in the organization. These documents will act as an institutional memory for the organization to be referred to in the future or replicated in other areas and research undertaking.The lessons learnt will help future project design, planning and implementation. Successive stories will be an added value to the unique strategies used during project implementation and how it has support effect changes in the behaviors and lives of the communities in the project location
