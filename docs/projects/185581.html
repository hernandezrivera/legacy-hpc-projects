Project Summary

IOM will adapt and strengthen its GBV programme, established in 2017, targeting Rohingya refugees and host communities centered on three pillars: 1.) survivor-centered support, 2.) risk mitigation and 3.) addressing root causes that perpetuate GBV in line with IOM´s GBV in Crises Operational Framework.
IOM directly implements case management providing a multisectoral package of support via nine Women and Girls Safe Space (WGSS) across eight sites and through its emergency safe shelter. To mitigate risks, prevent GBV and empower women and girls, the WGSS provide information, age-appropriate and structured psychosocial support (PSS), awareness and educational activities, life-skills and skill building opportunities, forums for two-way information-sharing and learning, including peer-led curriculum on GBV, sexual and reproductive health, menstrual hygiene, and disaster preparedness.
Ensuring sustainability and localization, IOM and PULSE will scale up its capacity building and support with community groups, advocates, imams, majhis, volunteers, and women´s committee members, to transition key WGSS and outreach activities through regular meeting, capacity building sessions, regular supervision as appropriate. This will enhance community ownership and accountability by working holistically with communities to address the root causes of gender inequality. IOM will work closely with the Protection Research Unit to develop evidence-based curriculum working with men, boys, women, girls and persons with diverse Sexual Orientations, Gender Identities and Expressions, and Sex Characteristics’ (SOGIESC), to ensure inclusive approaches. Such strategies will ensure safe and non-stigmatising entry points to GBV services for male survivors and survivors with diverse SOGIESC.
IOM´s programme will feature a greater focus on GBV risk mitigation in the WASH sector through enhancing the participation of women and girls in the design and maintenance of WASH infrastructure. IOM´s primary focus will be on menstrual health education through peer-based models building on the findings of IOM´s summary report Ma Boinor Rosom or “Mother and Sister´s Ways” and its related curriculum, piloted as a model of best practice. The curriculum among other complementary outreach tools focused on life skills and male engagement will be further developed using participatory approaches for both Rohingya and host communities.
Empowerment and skills development activities for women and girls will become more structured and diversified. IOM will scale up the production of re-usable sanitary pads working with volunteers. Participants who have graduated from skills-building classes and groups will be engaged as peer trainers and supported with coaching and capacity building. IOM will identify opportunities for women’s participation and engagement through multi-stakeholder approaches and plans to scale up and replicate its Women´s Participation Project, piloted in 2020 and 2019 as a best practice model for fostering social cohesion and ensuring disability inclusion.
IOM is an active member of the GBV Subsector Working Group, GBV Case Management and Dignity Kit Task Teams and Co-chair of the PSEA Network. IOM GBV staff serve as interagency GBV Camp Level Focal Points and as part of interagency emergency and response structures such as the Mobile Medical Teams and the Protection Emergency Response Units (PERUs). IOM GBV Focal Points closely collaborate with IOM´s PSEA Champions who are frontline staff who support in PSEA awareness raising and safe reporting working with communities and service providers across the camp. IOM collect feedback from the community through its WGSS tools and Complaint and Feedback Mechanism Desks IOM will continue work with the DC, UNO, RRRC Office and CiC Offices at the camp level by coordinating regularly and providing capacity building in GBV.

1 - Identified Needs

The reduction of humanitarian actors and restrictions in activities due to COVID-19 in the last year exacerbated pre-existing GBV trends for Rohingya refugees and host communities. The 2021 JMSNA findings, ACAPS and interagency assessments have echoed such findings highlighting that the COVID-19 lockdown measures have contributed to new protection concerns for individuals at heightened risk who face additional barriers to accessing services such as single female-headed households, adolescent girls, persons with diverse gender and sexual orientation and persons with disabilities. WGSS facilities were closed due to fluctuating lockdown measures throughout 2021 which impacted on the protection environment at site level and the needs of women and girls. In Mar. 2021 a massive fire broke out across camps 9, 8W and 8E leaving two IOM supported facilities destroyed. Re-establishing services has been challenging against a backdrop of evolving GBV related risks at camp level. IRC´s 2021 analysis building from its 2020 “In the Shadows of the Pandemic” report, GBVIMS reports, and interagency gender analyses, there is an increase in incidents of intimate partner and domestic violence, since the onset of COVID-19. The reduction of in-person service providers and a shrinking humanitarian space that impacted on referrals and continuity of case management. According to GBVIMS monthly and quarterly reports, most GBV incidents occur at the survivor's or perpetrator’s residence, street/pathway, water point, public toilets/latrines and bathing facilities, highlighting an urgent need for GBV risk mitigation in WASH programming. Related to this, menstrual health is an issue intricately tied to GBV, given restrictive cultural norms, menstrual stigma and taboo, and lack of information. In-depth research using participatory approaches with Rohingya women and girls highlighted that in this context it is extremely different for women and girls to manage their periods. Social taboos impact how women and girls learn about their periods, their access to and awareness of necessary menstrual products, and how they manage their periods in the camps. Building on the IOM and UN Women joint report “Honour in Transition” and recent interagency assessments and reports, gendered norms are shifting in Rohingya communities and impact on the mobility of women and girls making control over resources, information, and access to services difficult. Gender norms amongst the Rohingya are changing in displacement intersecting with new family compositions and demographic changes. A focus on safe and meaningful women´s participation and empowerment are crucial to wider GBV prevention efforts and safe access to services. Women and adolescent girls are at heightened risk due to greater mobility restrictions in displacement among other contributing factors including poor living conditions in the camps, limited basic resources, lack of income generating activities, insufficient lighting and gender segregated toilets and bathing facilities, distance of water points, and insecurity. Many of these risks have shown to lead to harmful coping mechanisms such as child marriage, survival sex, and human trafficking. FGDs and reports have indicated that pressure and fear of parents and community leaders as well as the low participation of women and girls in making decisions at household level constitute reporting barriers for survivors and impact on safe access to services. Risks of forced marriage with the perpetrator and not being protected against reprisals are commonly cited concerns. Social stigma and perceptions about survivors, the community leaders’ interference in handling rape cases, lack of protection of survivors, insufficient awareness of consequences of rape, benefits of services and service accessibility could considerably delay survivors’ decision making in reporting and accessing services on a timely manner. 

2 - Activities or outputs

1- Case Management: IOM will ensure technical expertise and quality survivor-centred and multisectoral services through integrated capacity building and coordination with a focus on supervision skills. IOM will provide regular training required for systems strengthening with local organisations and national authorities on GBV. Technical training for caseworkers and health workers on key groups such as child and male survivors, individuals with diverse SOGIESC and persons with disability will be emphasised and cascade beyond IOM teams into field training in line with interagency guidelines and assessment tools and service audits. 2- Psychosocial Support: PSS activities in the WGSS will become more evidence-based and structured working with IOM´s Protection Research Unit to develop age-appropriate curriculum. To ensure sustainability and localization, IOM GBV will scale up work with its community groups and local partner to transition day-to-day management of key WGSS activities including group psychosocial support activities. Child friendly corners will be further developed in the WGSS to improve women’s access, participation, and leadership including persons with disabilities. 3- Skill development and empowerment activities: Participants who have graduated from skills-building classes and groups will be engaged as peer trainers and supported with coaching and capacity building. IOM will scale up several self-reliance pilot projects including the Women´s Participation Project and will identify opportunities for women’s participation and engagement to foster social cohesion through a do-no-harm approach including persons with disabilities and individuals with diverse SOGIESC.4- Community mobilization and outreach awareness sessions: IOM and PULSE will play a supportive role in mobilizing resources to support activities and capacity building to community volunteers, advocates and groups to improve community-led facilitation of awareness raising. IOM GBV and the Protection Research Unit will scale up its work to ensure awareness on GBV, safe referrals and dialogue on GBV prevention in line with appropriate social change curriculum. IOM will actively collect feedback fromcommunities including person with disabilities and individuals with diverse SOGIESC through surveys and service monitoring questionnaires to improve meaningful access in service delivery. 5- Distribution of dignity and Menstural Hygiene Management (MHM) kits: IOM will provide MHM information and materials through the distribution of kits. As a member of the Dignity Kit Task Team and in close coordination with the WASH and Health Working Groups, IOM will continue to work in close coordination with other service providers and sectors to ensure harmonized approaches. 6- Capacity building of community members: IOM will work with its community community-based structures including religious leaders, majhis, adult male and adolescent groups across eight camps and one host community area to improve their capacity in GBV core concepts and GBV prevention using evidence-based curriculum. This will allow IOM to create a support system in IOM´s areas of operation.7- Capacity building of service providers: IOM will conduct training on GBV and safe referral for Site Management, Health, WASH and such sectors working in IOM´s area of responsibility. IOM will also continue to support and facilitate the interagency GBV mainstreaming trainings for other sectors in coordination with the GBV Subsector8- Operation of WGSS and emergency safe shelter with trained GBV Caseworker, Manager and PSS Officers: Two trained caseworkers, one case manager and two PSS Officers will provide services in the WGSS and emergency safe shelter. 9- Support community advocates to facilitate male engagement curriculum- to be developed in collaboration with IOM GBV and the Protection Research Unit. IOM will support 40 groups of 400 community advocates across eight camps and one host community area.
