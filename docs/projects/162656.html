Project Summary

This project will ensure that the most vulnerable populations affected by conﬂict or natural disasters with speciﬁc needs have timely access to Emergency Shelter and Non-Food Items (NFI) assistance. This project contributes to the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) Strategic Outcome 1: Populations in areas affected by natural or man-made disaster receive timely assistance during and in the aftermath of a shock; and, Strategic Outcome 2: Displaced populations, refugees, returnees and host communities meet their basic needs and/or access basic services while increasing their self-reliance. IOM will conduct shelter surveys and needs assessments to identify and prioritize the most vulnerable individuals and households. Beneﬁciaries and committees will receive NFI and emergency shelters including proper training on construction techniques.

1 - Needs

Although it has been almost two years since the peak of the conﬂicts in Darfur and Kordofan, there remains a need for basic ES/NFIs kits and items for IDPs and returnees. According to IOM’s Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) registration exercises, as of October 2019, there were 21,587 individuals who were newly displaced IDPs in Central, South and West Darfur states and South Kordofan. The distribution of ES/NFIs for these new arrivals is considered a top priority. The provision of ES/NFIs will ensure targeted IDPs have some basic privacy and contribute to the restoration of a minimum sense of dignity for the newly displaced. It will also protect them against exposure from harsh weather conditions, which will mitigate potential health and safety concerns. In addition to this, since mid-2019, the level of armed hostility in Darfur has continued to decrease with the exception of the Jebel Marra region and, as a result, the number of returnees in Darfur has increased. According to information collected through DTM registration exercises in 2019, the number of returnees in need of ES/NFI Support in West Darfur is 19,911 individuals and the number of returnees in need in Central Darfur is 188,556 individuals. Although many of the returnees returned several months ago, they have encountered difficulties to secure adequate accommodation and they require additional assistance to complete shelter renovation or to receive basic ES/NFI support before the onset of the rainy season in 2020. Against this complex backdrop of continued displacement in some areas and a high number of returns in others, floods are expected to be a major natural hazard in 2020. Floods are anticipated in several locations where IDPs, returnees and other vulnerable populations have chosen to settle, severely affecting communities. The impact of floods was seen in 2019 with 45,000 homes destroyed and over 364,000 people affected in 16 out of 18 states. Therefore, it is expected ES/NF kits will be required to meet the immediate needs of those impacted by floods and communities will receive disaster-risk reduction support to ensure that flood-related risks are reduced.

2 - Activities / Outputs

1. Conduct needs assessments to identify and prioritize the most vulnerable households within each community including people with specific needs (PSN), and include gender considerations related to the participation of women in the distribution process and the roles played by males in promoting women’s safety.2. Provide training on construction techniques, ensuring that constructed structures will be sustainable in target camps and communities with high rates of displacement or return. 3. Train beneﬁciaries on Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) to support community early recovery response and raise awareness on environmental impacts, mitigation and use of environmentally friendly alternative materials.4. Purchase, preposition, and provide improved ES for 15,000 of the most vulnerable protracted IDPs and returnees based on assessed needs and PSN criteria utilizing environmental-friendly material and locally acceptable shelters. 5. Purchase and provide NFI assistance to beneﬁt 45,000 crisis and disaster affected IDPs and vulnerable persons, prioritizing women, children and the elderly. 6. Conduct post distribution and monitoring (PDM) in areas where ES/NFI and improved emergency shelters have been distributed. The PDM will include household to household interviews on the usage of distributed materials, beneﬁciary satisfaction, and complaints on the quality and quantity of received materials

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

IIOM will integrate environmental considerations into all programmes, and it will be in compliance with applicable international requirements. IOM will ensure that the proposed project will have a positive environmental impact by encouraging the beneﬁciaries to be involved in DDR activities such as awareness sessions, gender balance, environmental impacts, and safe construction of shelter. The training sessions will also include promoting alternatives to ﬁrewood such as fuel-eﬃcient cooking and livelihood options to preserve environment.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

This project will target prioritizing female, child and elderly headed households to receive ES/NFI assistance. Another group that will be prioritized is households assessed to be vulnerable and in dire need of ES/NFI assistance. All members of the community including unaccompanied minors, people with special needs, women and girls will be separately consulted and encouraged to be fully involved in the consultation, distribution and post-distribution processes.

7.1 - How will the project share information and collect community feedback and use it to improve your activities? Please select as many as relevant.

Face to face with individuals (in homes) through aid workers

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

IOM has developed a Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) format that will track the implementation of project activities and solicit feedback from ES/NFI beneficiaries face to face through visiting random households. PDM activities will involve on interviews with beneficiaries to understand their views on ES/NFIs quality, appropriateness, and utilization. The system has also been developed to assess the short-term effects of ES/NFIs in improving living conditions of IDPs/returnees. Information generated will be shared with the ES/NFI working group at the state level while policy or programmatic issues will be taken up in Khartoum ES/NFI Advisory Panel Meetings To achieve this objective, the PDM plan has been developed and outlines data collection methods (Household interviews, Focus Group Discussion and observation), data sources, time frame, responsibilities, and dissemination procedure. Methodologies used will include a desk review prior to the field exercise being conducting.
