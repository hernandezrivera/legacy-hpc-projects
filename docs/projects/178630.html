Project Summary

Under the purpose of reducing the burden of leishmaniasis, strengthening community resilience, and responding to Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) movements in north Syria, MENTOR implements the following activities:
MENTOR provides and equips 136 Health Facilities (HFs) in North Syria with leishmaniasis diagnostic and medical commodities, strengthens the capacity of health care workers (HCWs) by providing trainings on leishmaniasis case management and supervision, enhances disease surveillance by coordinating data collection across HFs and mobile clinics (MCs) to ensure robust epidemiological data reporting, operates MC in areas that lack access to HFs or areas that host a large number of IDPs, and improves the communities’ awareness about leishmaniasis detection, control and prevention through Information, Education and Communication/Behavioural Change Campaign (IEC/BCC) activities. The proposed activities to detect and control leishmaniasis will be complemented by a preventative WASH sector project aiming to reduce the disease burden through Indoor Residual Spraying (IRS).
MENTOR supports each HF with leishmaniasis medications, medical supplies and consumables, and diagnostic tools for both Cutaneous Leishmaniasis (CL) and Visceral Leishmaniasis (VL). To improve leishmaniasis control in the HCWs, MENTOR conducts 2-day trainings that include all the required protocols and guidance for the HCWs to detect and treat CL and VL based on the updated World Health Organization (WHO) guidance. MENTOR follows up with the trained HCWs to discuss complicated cases and ensure the correct implementation of the guidance. MENTOR’s MCs aim to increase the beneficiaries’ accessibility to leishmaniasis services, in particular that of vulnerable groups, such as persons with disabilities and those who cannot afford transportation costs to the nearest HF that provides leishmaniasis service.
In addition, MENTOR works to strengthen leishmaniasis surveillance, and regularly shares the data with WHO, EWARN, the Health & WASH clusters, the Health Working Group (HWG) and other stakeholders to improve coordination and synergize the humanitarian efforts against leishmaniasis.
MENTOR also runs combined IEC/BCC campaigns to ensure targeted communities have enough knowledge about leishmaniasis and disseminate key messages about leishmaniasis detection, control and prevention. MENTOR applies a comprehensive IEC/BCC approach through social media and various printed materials, such as leaflets and brochures, that are displayed at supported HFs, MCs and main areas of targeted communities. MENTOR also approaches key community people to deliver leishmaniasis messages in order to ensure a sustainable project and to improve the dissemination of the key messages with a long-lasting effect. Both IEC/BCC staff and trained key community people are asked to refer suspected leishmaniasis cases to supported HFs and/or MCs.
In 2021, MENTOR plans to support 136 HFs and 14 MCs and train 325 HWCs. MENTOR expects to conduct 580,000 leishmaniasis consultations and treatments, serving 65,325 beneficiaries across northern Syria.

4 - Needs

MENTOR has recorded dramatic increases in the incidence of CL and VL in both NWS and NES in 2020, and 2021 seems to continue on the same trajectory. In 2019 MENTOR diagnosed and treated 17,935 cases in NWS and 16,418 cases in NES, while in 2020 MENTOR recorded 35,577 and 33,456 cases respectively. At the same time, MENTOR conducted 564,463 treatment consultations to treat all CL cases. Of the overall cases 49.77% were female, 50.23% were male, 66.36% were children and 21.77% were below five years. In the first two months of 2021 MENTOR has already diagnosed and treated 8,679 cases in NWS and 8,400 cases in NES, surpassing even the numbers of 2020. MENTOR also recorded 54 cases of VL in NWS in 2020, and 18 cases in the first three months of 2021. This is particularly concerning, as such an increase in a vulnerable population with little immunity against the disease can quickly lead to outbreaks of the potentially fatal disease. In the past year, the number of IDPs has increased from 6.1 million to 6.7 million between December 2019 and early March 2020. Today, 1.93 million IDPs live in last-resort sites: 1.65 million people reside in 1,371 last-resort sites in NWS, in addition to around 271,443 IDPs living in five formal camps, five informal camps, 192 informal settlements and 154 collective shelters across NES.MENTOR bases its needs assessment on a network of MENTOR-supported HFs and MCs across NWS and NES, which collect and send comprehensive data to the MENTOR M&E department every week. All activity locations and programs are based on the most recent incidence and population movement numbers and after taking into consideration the needs laid out in the 2020 Syria HRP and the presence of partner organizations.

5 - Implementation of activities

MENTOR’s support to HFs is guided by CL incidence and the articulated needs of affected communities. Employing regular coordination with the health authorities and beneficiaries, MENTOR starts the HFs support by providing a 2-day leishmaniasis management training to the medical staff to strengthen the local capacity. MENTOR supplies HFs (70 NWS:66 NES) with medications, medical supplies and commodities, and equips most HFs with leishmaniasis laboratory diagnosis tools. MENTOR ensures robust technical support to the HFs via regular visits to each HFs to monitor stock, treatment outcomes and service delivery quality, enhancing the quality of existing health services and laying the essential foundations for long term recovery. MENTOR runs (8 NWS:6 NES) MCs that provide leishmaniosis diagnosis, treatment and case recording in areas that lack HFs. Each MC visits up to 10 treatment points twice weekly. The pathways of the MCs are chosen based on the lack of HFs and high CL incidence, prioritizing IDP camps, IDP movement and to reach people with disability. The IEC activities focus on improving the community resilience, disseminating leishmaniasis key messages through meeting with communities including people with disabilities by using brochures and social media. MENTOR targets key community people to support the circulation of messages to the communities. MENTOR monitors the activities by checking stock, availability of leishmaniasis services, IEC materials and staff behavior. Through implementation, MENTOR prioritizes the community’s feedback received by CRM channels. Through this approach, the program strengthens local capacity to cope with external shocks and reduce the leishmaniasis burden in the targeted area.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Child protection: Likely critical impact. All newly recruited staff sign the Child Protection Policy. MENTOR provides its representatives with guidance about ways to minimize risk to children and the standards of behavior and practice required when they are in contact with children. The monitoring method is Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) and ongoing monitoring activities to an acceptable risk level.COVID-19: Likely critical impact. MENTOR regularly supplies staff with personal protective equipment (PPE), follows strict preventive measures such as social distancing and temperature check for patients before consultations. MENTOR enrolled staff in infection-prevention control training and replaced all large gathering activities with one to one/small group activities. MENTOR applies the measures through photos, reports and monitoring. PPE are the required resources to an acceptable risk level.Gender-based Violence (GBV): Likely-critical. MENTOR analyzes and addresses any GBV-related risks, report to partners working in GBV and protection. All out training for health staff has a component on GBV and how to deal with GBV cases. Referral pathways are regularly updated. Staff are obliged to follow up with the referred case. Acceptable risk level.Provision of healthcare for vulnerable groups: Likely-critical impact. MENTOR assures that its supported HFs and MCs provide free service. MENTOR mitigates the risk of exclusion of vulnerable groups such as persons with disabilities by operating MCs which are flexible and can provide services to remote locations and IDP camps. MENTOR’s monitoring and staff are responsible to monitor this risk through spot check visits, KAP surveys and through CRM to an acceptable risk level.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

The possible risks for sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) are;1.SEA as an exchange for treatment.2.SEA as an exchange for promises of hiring to work at the NGO. 3.SEA as an exchange for money. All MENTOR staff sign a Code of Conduct (CoC) and Safeguarding, Sexual Harassment and Bullying, Whistleblowing, Child Protection policies. These policies outline the principles under which MENTOR operates, and incorporates the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) principles on Prevention of Sexual Abuse and Exploitation (PSEA). MENTOR enrols all staff on PSEA training and emphasizes refreshing the PSEA training regularly. Through CRM, MENTOR makes available channels to facilitate the reporting via WhatsApp hotline that is advertised on IEC/BCC materials, complaint boxes, and a feedback tool to collect feedback regularly during the monitoring activities. As part of our accountability to beneficiaries, MENTOR notifies all communities in which we work that our staff operate under a strict CoC, the standard of behavior that should be expected from our staff, and the confidential reporting mechanisms available to communities and beneficiaries. MENTOR staff are trained to identify sexual violence and report these immediately through their line management to the Country Director for (CD) for further action. In case of any PSEA reporting, MENTOR applies a strict follow-up method to address and solve the issue.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Security: Before HF/MC support, MENTOR conducts a security risk assessment (SRA) to avoid any security risks to beneficiaries or staff. MENTOR regularly updates the SRA in the operational area and take the required actions. If the security situation interferes with MENTOR direct access, MENTOR switches, where possible, to a remote management strategy. Coordination and duplication: MENTOR coordinates with the Health cluster, Health Working Groups, COVID-19 taskforce and WHO to ensure there is no duplication in programming. Necessary approvals for services are not secured: MENTOR maintains high coordination levels with the authorities to obtain/renew the permission of implementing the activities. MENTOR signed various Memorandum of Understandings (MoUs) with local authorities across northern Syria. Accountability to affected populations: MENTOR involves the needs of the beneficiaries at the preparation stage by conducting several assessments in the area that target community members, local authorities. During the implementation phase, MENTOR prioritizes the beneficiaries’ feedback received via CRM (WhatsApp hotline, Facebook page disseminated on complained boxes at HFs/MCs, printed IEC/BCC materials, social media), and the interviews with the community during monitoring activities. At the end of each grant, and via the final evaluation, MENTOR comes up with lessons learned to be implemented in other grants. Fraud: MENTOR askes all staff sign policies and refer to manuals such as Logistics Manual, HR Manual, Financial Manual and the Field Operational Manual where fraud and corruption are explained in detail. MENTOR regularly checks the financial, HR and logistic files from the field to headquarter level.
