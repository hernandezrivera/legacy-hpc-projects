Project Summary

The objective of the presented project plan is to Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3) in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19.The project also aims to Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support. This is in line with cluster objective for HRP Plan 2021 .
In line with FSL Cluster objectives and priorities, ARUDA will distribute food and livelihood protection inputs targeting three counties in Lakes states. The project will particularity target Yirol East, Yirol West and Awerial County whose total population under phase 3+ is 285,567. In Yirol East , the county is classified in IPC 4 (emergency) with a total of 31292 persons facing severe famine. In Yirol west , a total population of 17022 is under emergency while in Awerial , 20435 people are in need of life saving support . This project will therefore target a total of 68,749 people in need within the 3 counties in Lakes state.
The project will therefore target 68,749 most vulnerable IDP, returnee and host populations in lakes state within the 3 counties with distributions of crop seeds and vegetable kits or fishing kits.The project will take into consideration all Covid 19 SOP and guidelines through out the implementation period.

1 - Needs

The 2021 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) aims to meet the most critical needs of 8.3 million most-at-risk people. As such, this project aims to provide key resources/ assistance to population most affected by the prolonged South Sudan Crisis, flooding and inter-communal conflicts as well as the effects of Covid19 in order to save lives and reduce recourse to negative and retrogressive coping strategies in Lakes state and specifically targeting 12000 HHS (approximately 72000 people) in Awerial ,Yirol East and Yirol West Counties. The combination of high levels of flooding, economic shocks and the peak of the lean season have resulted in unprecedented humanitarian needs in Lakes State. These needs have manifested predominantly through displacement, forced changes to livelihoods, high reported levels of malaria and gendered protection concerns across the state. With limited humanitarian access in the region, information gaps exist on the severity of these needs and the locations of vulnerable populations Reports from REACH assessment conducted in late September 2020 indicate inadequate access to food in Lakes was reportedly driven by the lack of harvested food stocks during the lean season,severe flooding, and economic shocks linked to the depreciation of the South Sudanese Pound (SSP) . Within the assessed settlements KIs reported that most people did not have adequate access to food in (85%); 19% reported that this was due to the previous harvest being exhausted, 89% reported that this was because crops had been destroyed or did not attain ideal growth because of flooding, while 76% reported that this was because food prices were too high. An IRNA conducted by 18 agencies among them ARUDA operating in Lakes to asses the extent and effect of flooding in Awerial in the month of September presented huge glaring gaps in the needs among the community. During assessment, teams held interviews with key informants such as the chiefs, Payam and Boma administrators. The assessment found out that more than 50% of of the sites visited had experienced displacement from their homes and were in open places, local churches and / or in school buildings while some sought refuge from friends, relatives that have not been affected. 100% of the sites visited had no latrines and open defecation was witnessed in their surroundings. 100% of the households visited with FGD and KI conducted agreed that floods indeed affected their sources of food leading to change in their meals pattern and income. 100% of the respondents agreed that livestock were greatly affected by the floods with a high number of families reporting death of their livestock due to disease outbreak or flooding. In line with HRP FSL Cluster Objective 2,ARUDA proposes to support most at risk populations in selected counties in Lakes state with immediately implementable distribution of crop seeds, vegetable kits and fishing kits. This will be done in accordance with the seasonal calendar and with the intent of obtaining short and medium terms results for improved access to food. Immediate result will be obtained through distribution of fishing kits, short term through distribution of vegetable seeds and medium through distribution of crop seeds.The operating Covid19 Sop and protocols will be observed through out the project taking into account cross cutting issues (PSN, GBV, AAP) to ensure a safe and dignified service provision aiming for a good coordination and synergy with other sectors (protection, health, WASH). The project is will thus aim to to ensure the provision of safe , dignified emergency livelihood support that will have components of peace building by ensuring that peace committees as well as peace messaging and advocacy is well incorporated. Its also envisioned that the project will create referral pathways for GBV victims by working closely with the other humanitarian actors in the region including CUAMM(health) and WHO.

2 - Project activities

To achieve the project objective and contribute meaningfully to the overall cluster objectives, ARUDA will conduct a number of activities geared towards this end in respect to each objective. (A). Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3Activities: 1. Carry out rapid assessment in the identified locations using cluster tools such as Focus group discussions,Key informant interviews and direct observations to further identify gaps and inform the response targeting atleast 20 KI's & 12 FGD. 2. Carry out community mobilization, sensitization and registration of HHs in Lakes states targeting the most vulnerable in Yirol East , Yirol West and Awerial 3. Distribute quality agricultural tools ,fishing kits , crop and vegetable seeds to 12000 HHS(72, 000people) 4. Post distribution monitoring targeting 30% of the HHs beneficiaries 5. Cash & voucher-based transfers with food security/ nutrition objectives6. Provision of in kind food assistance to the most vulnerable population (B)Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19Activities to be carried out under this objective will have a sustainability and resilience component and will involve the following activities 1. Establishment of peace committee involving the local administration , women and youth leaders as well as faith based leaders. 2. 450 farmers identified and trained on modern farming techniques to increase food production. 150 from each project location who will share best with the rest of the community 3. Community Animal Health Workers Training and inputs support, maintenance, prevention & treatment especially zoonoses e.g. RVF 4. Support value chain addition e.g. through agriculture, fishery and livestock products/ by-products preservation & marketing through training of women groups and organising them into VSLA

3 - Project indicators

The Project Manager as well as the project officers directly in the field will manage this project. Programme Director based in the head office in Juba with technical support by the executive Director will support the project team. The project will be implemented with the support of the respective County Authorities, RRC Department and local structures like the women groups, fishing groups, youth’s groups and farmers groups, who will be actively involved in mobilization, selection and verification of the beneficiaries as well as to participate in the monitoring and evaluation of the project. Each project activity will be assessed individually against the set target and corrective measure taken should the activity be off track. ARUDA will put in place an implementation plan derived from the Log frame and program oversight structure to help successfully drive the project. The M& E Officer will help to provide progressive M&E tools supplementing the project log frame, track and document useful lessons emanating from the implementation process and will render support to the Cluster monitoring team when required. The Finance Department will progressively resource the project and ensures accountability through reports while the head of Programs will provide technical support as well as create synergies with other ARUDA projects and other humanitarian actors, the Executive director will have the administrative responsibility and overall oversight to coordinate and attend cluster meetings and inter-agency meetings at the County Level, State level and National level to ensure a well-coordinated intervention and to share information with other humanitarian actors to ensure maximum complementarity. At the field level, the Project Manager with support from the project officers will lead the monitoring and reporting of outputs and share reports with the Programme team in the head office in Juba for review and submission to donor as per reporting timelines. As part of our commitment on the AAP and our resolve to increase accountability to beneficiaries in line with the core humanitarian standards, the project team will set up a beneficiary feedback mechanisms in the form of help desks and suggestion boxes in distribution centers within counties, ARUDA will put into consideration the persons with special needs by selecting the convenient distribution site to avoid possible protection/SGBV risks. ARUDA will report to the food security and livelihood cluster on the progress and setbacks of the project at the State level and at National level as expected.Any challenges will be shared openly with the relevant authorities to ensure the smooth delivery of the project.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

ARUDA will conduct separate FGD for the various groups so that women, men , boys and girls concerns are taken into account. ARUDA will form gender committee to take care of complaints and educate the community on PSEA.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The PWSN groups will be involved at both the planning and implementation stages of the project. The different groups disagregated by age and gender will have separate FGD to inform project decision .

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The community in which the project will be carried out will be involved through community mobilization sessions conducted involving women and girls , men and boys as well as persons with special needs. This will be conducted through mass awareness creation , KIIs and FGD to inform project decision. A suggestion box as well as a complaints number will be displayed and communicated to the beneficiary at all times during the project engagement.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

ARUDA will have a feedback mechanism involving suggestion boxes ,as well as conducting PDM. Feedback from this sources will be used to inform decision making and shall be incorporated into the project implementation plan.
