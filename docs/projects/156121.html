Project Summary

This project proposes providing the protection for new IDPs in emergencies in reception center of Maaret Elekhwan, which has been established and managed by Saed Charity and receives new IDPs coming from crisis areas from the governorates of Idleb, Aleppo, Damascus, Hama, Raqqa, Deir Al-Zour, and Lattakia where the RC is located in Idleb governorate , Idleb district , Maaret Tamsrin sub-district in Maaret Elekhwan community.
This project also aims for Assisting and improving IDPs lives in squatter and forgotten camps who have not received any services before. This project will target random camps in Badama Subdistrict , Jisr al-Shughour district ,Idleb governorate, on improving the shelter circumstances of the IDPs by transporting the IDPs who lives in vulnerable conditions in this random camps to live in a formal camp settlement consist of caravans purchesed with a good manufacturer properties , located on a land with public ownership in Kherbet Eljoz community Badama Subdistrict , Jisr al-Shughour district ,Idleb governorate, closed to the turkish - syrian borders and has a graveled road , this camp will provide protection and safety and lifeseving assistance for the most vulnerable groups IDPs, and qualified in terms of infrastructure (lighting, drinking water, latrines, graveled corridors, fence, observation towers)
In addition, increasing the protection level in the northern Syrian camps, especially for vulnerable groups of IDPs like people with special needs, elderly, women and children , by upgrading and rehabilitation of public facilities, latrines and bathrooms through lighting these spots and the main roads in the camps, installation and construction of auxiliary means for security access to these facilities (concrete or gravel corridors , stairs, ramps, Barriers, handles, toilets for disabled people, coarse flooring)

Needs

According to the CCCM SECTOR INPUTS 2019 HNO "The absence of accountable camp management and maintenance of facilities in IDP sites, particularly for informal/self-established IDP sites, are deeply concerning and contribute to alarming reports of uncovered needs in these sites"Of the estimated 6.2 million internally displaced persons in Syria, an estimated 871,150, or 14 per cent of all IDPs, are living in IDP sites where they receive humanitarian assistance and services. Of these, almost half (420,103 IDPs) are in the northwest of the country, the majority of whom are located in Idleb Governorat.The reception and transit center of Maart Elekhwan has received more than 12446 IDPs between the period of January 2018 to December 2018 of them 24% men, 22 % women, 28% boys, 26 % girls, 10 person of special needs, and 223 IDPs in need of specialized medical aids due to having medical problems during refuge way. Most of IDPs who were hosted by Maart Elekhwan RC came collectively from Eastern Ghouta, and Rural Damascus. Other came from Oqairbat and rural Hama, Dar'a and Quneitra. The RC of Maart Elekhwan aims to continue providing humanitarian and live-saving aids in multi-sectors including temporary shelter for most vulnerable labels of newly IDPs.In case of displacement waves stop and there was no need for the RC anymore. Saed Charity, in coordination with CCCM Cluster, has suggested to make use from RC infrastructure and its assets optimally, turning RC into vocational Training Center In the Idlib Governorate, Jisr al-Shughour district, Badama subdistrict there are approximately 50,000 IDPs living in the camps, including 4% of people with special needs and 17% of families headed by women. Some of those displaced came from neighboring areas because of the conflict, unstable living conditions and shelling, where they had already lost their livelihoods, majority of them came because they could no longer reach their homes.The IDPs in a scattered camp built their camps in a personal or rented land in small communities, sometimes 5 or 10 tents, or in camps close to the Syrian-Turkish border, making the implementation of humanitarian responses difficult.IDPs in these camps suffer from difficult conditions and are unable to reach their tents easily because of the muddy land especially in the winter.The needs assessment conducted shows that 70% of the random and registered camps suffer from floods in the winter,where their tents are damaged by storms and floods, In the region, where they do not meet the minimum humanitarian standards for responding to infrastructural conditions that are weak or nonexistent at all,and this is often reflected on the vulnerable groups, women, children, and child-headed households, people with special needs and the elderly, tents are torn over time, making them with no shelter and given the high cost of repairing or replacing them for protection in summer or winter. All of the above has pushed IDPs into negative adjustment strategies such as shelters that do not fit people with their families in poor humanitarian conditions.The Needs Assessment Shows the urgent need to improve the conditions of IDPs there in terms of protection the affected population by lighting and improving main roads,lighting public facilities and improving their access conditions so that persons with special needs can easily access the facilities.The needs assessment also indicated to gap in safety and security measures, where firefighting points should be in the event of any potential fires, Also,to provide child-friendly spaces to mitigate the consequences of the negative impact of war on children.IDPs suffer from multiple health problems due to the lack of drinking water and sanitation systems that are appropriate to their needs, leading to many negative effects and diseases, especially vulnerable groups; because such health systems Cannot be created in private land or random places where it is impossible to do such things.

Implementation of activities

SCA team will carry out all project activities which are divided into three main categories and will continue for the duration of the project: 1- The activities of maintaining and developing the humanitarian infrastructure in the reception center through the repair and rehabilitation of the damaged infrastructure and periodic repairs of the faults resulting from the use or tampering or the result of weather conditions, etc., and also adding a limited number of assets to fill some gaps It includes routine or emergency repairs of the available assets and services as repair and maintenance of lighting poles in order to obtain good visibility throughout the camp, especially on the roads leading to bathrooms and toilets, in order to provide the necessary protection for children, women, the elderly, pregnant women, the disabled and people with special needs, Daily management activities and provision of daily life-saving humanitarian assistance to beneficiaries of the RC, including community participation, accountability, monitoring and evaluation activities. This part includes the activities of receiving, registering the IDPs and distributing them on the tents, providing information on available services, complaints and feedback mechanisms, 2- Improving shelter conditions for displaced people in squatter camps in Badama Subdistrict ,according to the CCCM SECTOR INPUTS 2019 HNO a large proportion of IDP sites in Syria are informal settlements, also known as “spontaneous” or “self-established” camps. These usually consist of groups of tents or other shelters that were set up by IDPs themselves or other actors. Crucially, these sites are not managed by anyone and often have no legal claim to the land on which they were established. In these sites, the delivery of dignified protection and multi-sectoral assistance cannot be guaranteed by humanitarian actors, a critical issue for vulnerable groups, and particularly for women- and child-headed households.According to need assessment conducted in the area there is 35 informal camp of households between 10 to 25 tents are going to be targeted , these camps will be evaluated by Saed charity team ,the beneficiary selection will based on the most vulnerable groups , community mobilization and advocacy ,preparing the land of the camp with taking into consideration of the HLP , purchasing 500 caravans which fits the standard of the humanitarian response ,moving the beneficiaries to the new formal caravan camp , taking into account the needs of women,children, elderly, disabled, and those with special needs according to the standards of human response. And Providing the camp with services related to the safety and protection of women and children like (25 latrine and shower blocks,50 lighting pols, 2 sets of children’s playgrounds with games, 2 child friendly spaces,100 fire points, fence and 20 guard towers)3-Improving access of disabled, women and elderly to services in camps in northern Syria by providing Lightning latrines blocks from the inside and Installation solar poles on the roads leading to the public facilities, Equipping The latrines designed for disabled persons by toilets, handles and ramps and improve the movement of people with special needs, disabled and the elderly by graveling the roads to the public facilitiesAll previous activities will be implemented with the active participation of beneficiaries, affected populations and local communities. Community-based community committees (male and female) will be formed to contribute to the planning, implementation, monitoring and impact assessment of activities. SCA team will use all mechanisms to receive feedback from beneficiaries and respond to their suggestions and complaints. SCA either through special funds or partners' contributions in coordination with CCCM Cluster and other sectors will cover the gaps that are not covered by this project proposal (food provision, protection activities, specialized medical care, etc.)

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: there is a risk of gathering men or women from multiple families in one tent and the absence of barriers that give privacy to people within the tent. The lack of doors and partition for sleeping and changing clothes can lead to increased exposure to sexual harassment and abuseLikelihood: Medium. Impact: HighMitigating measures: 1-The appropriate assistance will be provided to the people in the reception center based on the study of all needs and requirements and provide the assistance permanently and fairly. 2- building a privet rooms inside each rub hall in order to change clothes or special situations that need privacy. 3-insure that the tents are not overcrowded beyond the maximum capacity and the presence of a sufficient space for each resident within the tent.4- Awareness sessions will be held for men, women and children on a regular basis, as well as the distribution of awareness leaflets,5-The GPV cases will be monitored and privacy maintainedProtection risk: There is a risk of unfair distribution of aid and the sexual exploitation of IDPs by the IDPs themselves or the administration personnel and sexual exploitation in return of the assistance providedLikelihood: Medium. Impact: HighMitigating measures: 1-Identification a distribution mechanism of the assistance provided to ensures that assistance safely provided to all targeted persons, 2-Define eligibility criteria and circulate it to all beneficiaries3- Create an effective complaint system and encourage IDPs to make a feedback4- Follow-up of distributions and the preparation of satisfaction and post-distribution reportsProtection risk:Men enter the women's section or vice versa, causing tensions among residents of the centerLikelihood: low. Impact: HighMitigating measures: 1-Asian a female employee as a supervisor for the female section, in addition to female employees for washing room and cleaning the bathrooms for women2-Prevent entry of administrative staff and male maintenance staff to the women's section outside the official working hours and accompany the female supervisor of the women section when entering the women section during working hoursProtection risk:Exploitation of employees to their positions and obtaining part of the assistance provided to the IDPs , this leads to resentment of IDPs and tension between them and employees.Likelihood: Medium. Impact: MediumMitigating measures: 1-Establish an effective monitoring mechanism to prevent the exploitation of staff to their positions and obtain part of the assistance provided to the IDPs2-establishment of multiple complaints mechanismsProtection risk:Some men or women attempt to impose their control over others and exploit them materially or morallyLikelihood: Medium. Impact: MediumMitigating measures:1-Activate the role of community committees 2-held a regular and periodic interviews with beneficiariesProtection risk:The community participation in public facilities can lead to violation of the privacy of male or female individuals and can lead to gender-based violence.Likelihood: Medium. Impact: highMitigating measures:1-Provide the areas of latrines with good lighting 2- keep the locks for doors and windows 3-separate facilities of men from women's

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: threatening the safety and security of the IDPs as the area of implementation for this project (Ma’aret Al- Ekhwan) is considered low tension relatively speaking comparing to other areas in northern part of Syria. Mitigating measures:To insure the safety and security of beneficiaries and workers, and the assets for the project, coordination with the local communities, and sharing the location of the project with the Coalition and Russian forces through the office of OCHA, will be done indicating that these activities for humanitarian purpose.Risk: As the tents and the fuel tanks are a flammable material and there is a fuel tanks and electricity cables and generators there is a risk of a spark case a fire Mitigating measures:A fire points will be distributed over the place, 40 extinguishers will be distributed to large and small tents, shower blocks, and close to the fuel storage tank generators and kitchen.Risk: The unwillingness of targeted IDPs to be moved from the tents and live in caravans,Mitigating measures:A Project support session will be conducted and the beneficiaries will be fully informed about the benefits of moving from the tents and living in a caravan. In addition, SCA will always have more beneficiaries in the needs assessment so that they will be used in the event of a lack of targeting planRisk:An issue of not to find a spot of land of the appropriate size to be placed caravans on them, and the problems of the land ownership, Mitigating measures:The cooperation with local authorities in the areas and sign an agreement with the local council allows SCA to use the land without obstacles. If there is not enough land to build the camp, more than one sector will be divided, Risk: Beneficiary abuse of services provided to them, Mitigating measures:There will be training sessions and guidance for all the beneficiaries when they are transferred in these caravans are full explanation of all services provided in theory and in practice with an explanation of safety measurements.Risk: The sensitivity among the people of the camp when providing services for people with special needs and elderly in particular,Mitigating measures:The community mobilization team will visit the targeted camps and advocate the people and involve them in the development of the plans and stages of the project, beside the clearance of the selection criteria and sharing this criterion with the beneficiaries Risk: Lack of knowledge of people with special needs on how to use the services provided, Mitigating measures:there will be introductory sessions to the IDPs and staff on how to use all services with brochures containing details and drawings.Risk: Damage to any of the services provided (stairs, floors, poles, etc.), Mitigating measures:There will be regular maintenance visits to most services
