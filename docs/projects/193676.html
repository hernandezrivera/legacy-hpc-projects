Project Summary

The proposed project will seek to ensure civilians living in high risk conflict-affected communities in the Gaza Strip are better prepared and protected in case of armed conflict and are empowered with the knowledge they need to mitigate risks associated with explosive weapons.
NPA’s CPP approach for 2023 will focus improving civil protection through the following core activities: providing CPP and EORE training for adult beneficiaries living in Gaza via community-based training sessions, implementation of child-friendly CPP and EORE training for children in schools through in-class sessions and school events, public awareness raising of CPP and EORE principles via social media campaigns, psychosocial support to beneficiaries who have experienced trauma resulting from armed conflict, and the development of early emergency warning systems for target schools.

1 - Needs

Palestine has suffered from protracted armed conflict and the use of explosive weapons in populated areas (EWIPA) for decades with severe consequences for its civilian population. The last August 2022 military conflict which occurred in the Gaza Strip was only the latest in a long line of violent escalations which has underscored the critical need for Conflict Preparedness and Protection (CPP) activities in Palestine. The Gaza Strip remains in a state of protracted low intensity conflict in which Israeli military incursions and airstrikes are commonplace and high-intensity periods of conflict can materialize without warning. Armed conflict in Gaza is characterized by the widespread use of explosive weapons in populated areas, which has had a devastating impact on Gaza's civilian population. From 2010 through to the end of 2020, Action On Armed Violence (AOAV) recorded a total of 782 confirmed attacks using explosive weapons in Gaza which resulted in at least 1317 civilian deaths and over 3800 of civilian injuries. In 2021, Gaza was the third worst-affected country for civilian casualties of explosive weapons (after Afghanistan and Syria) as a result of the outbreak of violence between Israel and Gaza in May of that year, which led to the highest number of civilian casualties (256 civilians killed and approximately 2000 wounded according to the UN) in Gaza since 2014. ACLED has recorded 76 incidents of explosive weapons use (airstrikes, shelling, missile strikes, etc.) in the Gaza Strip so far in 2022, the majority of which occurred during the August military escalation. Facing ongoing instability and the continuous possibility of military threats, residents and communities across the Gaza Strip must be prepared for this eventuality and pre-emptively implement safety measures to mitigate the risks associated with armed conflict. Explosive weapons such as aerial bombs, missiles, projectiles, etc., have wide-area blast and fragmentation effects which can kill and injure people within broad proximity to the detonation. Safety and preparedness measures exist which can help civilians mitigate the risks associated with explosive threats and help to improve civilian survivability during and/or after an attack occurs.Knowledge of safety measures, risk mitigation techniques, proper evacuation procedures, basic fire safety, and basic First Aid can help civilians prevent loss of life either during or in the aftermath of an attack. Public awareness regarding Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) also plays a critical role in protecting civilians against ERW which can litter populated areas during a conflict and pose a threat long after an attack occurs. So far in 2022, there have been several accidents involving ERW in Gaza resulting in 15 casualties (4 women, 2 men, 1 girls, 8 boys). The presence of ERW contamination in Gaza and the ever-present possibility of renewed high­ intensity conflict underscores the need to educate civilians about the threats posed by explosive weapons and ERW prior to any future bouts of armed conflict. Given the clear need for improved civil protection in Gaza, NPA began conducting CPP and EORE trainings for civilians in 2016. Each year, NPA conducts a baseline CPP assessment in the communities where it is implementing CPP/EORE training and awareness raising activities to better understand existing civilian knowledge, attitudes, and practices regarding conflict preparedness and protection measures. NPA uses the same questionnaire to survey a separate (but demographically similar) sample of project participants after the project activities have been implemented to ensure the validity and effectiveness of its CPP/EORE project activities. As part of the 2022 baseline CPP assessment, NPA’s two implementing partners surveyed 732 individuals in North Gaza, Gaza City, and Khan Younis governorates, while 695 beneficiaries from those same areas participated in the endline assessment. The 2022 baseline CPP assessment results data (provided in supplementary email to this application) provides an eye-opening overview of the KAP of civilians living in Gaza which has highlighted that untrained civilians generally hold many misconceptions about what constitutes safe behavior during a conflict and lack basic information that could help improve the safety of themselves and their loved ones in the event of an attack. Endline post-training assessment surveying of adult and child CPP training beneficiaries conducted in 2022 demonstrated both groups retain the information taught in NPA's CPP/EORE sessions and are far more knowledgeable than the baseline (ie. untrained) on the training content. Testing shows that trained beneficiaries are significantly more aware of the safest actions and positions to take during an attack, how to protect one's head and body, breathe control, how to safely evacuate a building, and what they should do if they encounter ERW.The implementation of NPA's Conflict Preparedness and Protection (CPP) risk education training sessions in communities in 2016-22 has proven effective at improving public awareness about conflict-related risks and informing people about the safety measures they can implement to improve their safety during an emergency. By emphasizing that there are indeed actions which civilians can take to better protect themselves during conflict, emergency preparedness training is helping to empower civilians, motivate them to seek adequate protection, and combat feelings of indifference or helplessness.

2 - Activities

This project represents the fourth year of a four-year intervention in the Gaza Strip seeking to provide CPP training and support to Gaza's vulnerable civilian population, funded by the Norwegian Ministry of Foreign Affairs (NMFA). As a leading global advocate against the use of EWIPA, NPA aims to remedy the existing gap regarding emergency preparedness risk education in Palestine as one of the world's countries most affected by explosive weapons through CPP programming. NPA's CPP initiative empowers civilians to mitigate risks associated with armed conflict and explosive weapons. This project will utilize NPA's existing CPP framework, methodologies, and educational materials which have been effectively employed in Gaza since 2016 to expand the provision of CPP risk education training to adult heads of household and school aged children. To date, NPA has provided CPP risk education training to over 186,000 direct beneficiaries across the Gaza Strip in cooperation with the school system and NPA's network of local partners. Participants of NPA's CPP risk education trainings receive instruction on the following topics: home preparation and blast protection safety measures, conflict risk mitigation measures, safe evacuation procedures, basic fire safety, ERW awareness, and improvised First Aid solutions.NPA's CPP framework utilizes separate curriculums for adults and children, designed to convey safety messaging in a manner which is appropriate and effective for each age group. All training sessions incorporate high quality training aids, educational materials, and media to enhance the learning experience. This project aims to achieve its objective via the following activities conducted in partnership with Palestinian Save the Children Foundation (PSCF); Activity 1: Capacity development of CBO partners and delivery of CPP training sessions for adult direct beneficiaries in prioritized areas of the Gaza Strip. Indicator/Target 1.1: 20 partner CBO staff and/or volunteers (10 women, 10 men) are trained as CPP and first aid responder, equipped, and deployed with support from NPA to conduct community and schools-based CPP training across Gaza. Indicator/Target 1.2: 20,000 adult heads of household (10,000 women and 10,000 men) receive training on conflict preparedness and protection risk education (including EORE safety messaging); Activity 2: Delivery of CPP training sessions (including EORE safety messaging) for child beneficiaries via in-class school sessions and community-based outreach. Indicator/Target 2.1: 10,000 children (5,000 girls and 5,000 boys) receive training on conflict preparedness and protection risk education; Activity 3: Delivery of CPP training sessions for Persons with Disabilities (PwD) via household visit training and schools-based training sessions. Indicator/Target 3.1: 500 PwD including 400 children (200 girls, 200 boys) and 100 adults (50 women, 50 men) Activity 4: Provision of psychosocial support for beneficiaries suffering from trauma related to past conflict and EWIPA use. Indicator/Target 4.1: At least 100 individuals identified during training outreach are provided with psychosocial support and post-traumatic stress disorder support. NPA's CPP partner PSCF is an active member of Child Protection & PSS AOR and follows the general methodology of the PSS AOR, including the referral system and case management. NPA and PSCF piloted this activity over the course of the 2022 CPP project with very positive results and determined that there is a need for continued implementation of this project component in 2023. Activity 5: Civilians are made aware of CPP and EORE safety messaging through social media outreach. Indicator/Target 5.1: Development and dissemination of CPP/EORE safety messaging via social media; Indicator/Target 5.2: Initiation of a targeted CPP and EORE social media campaign in the event of an outbreak of armed conflict with the aim to reach at least 300,000 indirect beneficiaries. Activity 6: Increase awareness of project staff, partners and beneficiaries on PSEA. Indicator/Target 6.1: NPA will carry out a workshop with the implementing partner to raise awareness of at least seven project staff (4 women, 3 men) about prevention of and protection from sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment and IASC Six Core Principles to ensure the understanding of, and adherence to, the PSEA mechanisms. Additionally, NPA will facilitate a series of orientation sessions with beneficiaries to raise awareness about PSEAH and explain NPA’s whistleblowing reporting portal and complaints mechanism.

3 - Indicators

MEAL: While CPP training operations are carried out in the field by NPA’s cooperating partner, PSCF, training sessions are continually monitored by the NPA Palestine CPP team which consists of two national staff based in Gaza: 1) NPA's CPP Area Coordinator, and 2) the CPP Project Officer. During the start-up phase of each project, the CPP Area Coordinator and Project Officer are responsible for training NPA's local implementing partner as CPP trainers and practitioners - usually conducted via 2-3 days training-of-trainers workshops - to ensure that the partners have sufficient capacity to deliver the project. Partner procurement processes during the start-up phase are closely supported and monitored by the CPP team to ensure that the quality and quantities of the materials, training aids, and equipment procured are adequate for the proper implementation of the project.During the training phase of the project, the CPP programme team deploys on a daily basis to conduct monitoring visits at partner training sites across Gaza. These monitoring visits allow NPA to directly QA the delivery of the training in the field, provide guidance and assistance to the partner training team, understand and help address the challenges faced by the partner in the implementation of the activity, gather beneficiary feedback, and collect photography and video records. Partner reporting is conducted directly from the field via NPA's Survey123/ArcGIS-based electronic reporting system which enables the individual training teams to submit standardized training reports in-situ via mobile device. Electronic reports submitted in this manner capture all key training session data such as location, date, start time, end time, number and types of beneficiaries (disaggregated by gender, age, and disability status), materials used, issues/challenges, etc, and include photos for purposes of remote verification and QA. Teams complete and submit their reports in the field immediately following the completion of a training session, which are then automatically logged and collated on NPA's online Information Management {IM) dashboard system in real time. The system allows for close coordination between NPA's project monitors and the partner project team, enabling increased situational awareness and the ability to make rapid adjustments in the targeting of project activities. NPA will report on the project's indicators to UNMAS and the Mine Action Working Group of the Protection Cluster on a regular basis. NPA will also report project indicators through the HRP periodic reporting framework and update the Financial Tracking System as funding for project activities is secured. Results will be measured via pre- and post-training tests for individuals to determine changes in knowledge, attitudes, and practices of beneficiaries as a result of the intervention. Evaluation is a key aspect of NPA Palestine's CPP intervention approach in Gaza which is conducted with the aim of measuring the impact and effectiveness of a CPP project's activities. As a Protection activity focused on improving the safety and preparedness of civilians living in Gaza through the provision of training, CPP training activities are evaluated by how they impact the knowledge, attitudes, and practices of civilians living in at-risk, conflict-affected communities. Pre-/post-testing of beneficiaries is conducted throughout the training phase of the project to gauge baseline knowledge and attitudes regarding the training content. NPA evaluates the baseline (pre-training test) data and compares it with the endline (post-training test) data to determine changes knowledge and attitudes as a result of the training. Beneficiary feedback is collected continuously throughout the project's training phase to help the CPP programme team understand how well the beneficiaries are receiving the information, which content they find most/least useful, and which aspects of the training can be improved. While changes in knowledge and attitudes can be tracked relatively easily through periodic evaluation, determining changes in practices has proven far more difficult. NPA will implement a project evaluation over the final months of 2022 and again in 2023 to assess the impact of its CPP interventions in the previously targeted communities, especially in the wake of the May 21-August 22 military conflicts. This data will help NPA further refine its CPP framework to better address existing protection needs and gaps. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Preventing harm to civilians is the core of NPA's CPP programme and, as such, ensuring the safety and dignity of targeted communities is critical to our project plan. NPA is committed to the humanitarian principle of 'do no harm' and has designed this project's activities to empower beneficiaries and mitigate threats against them without jeopardizing their wellbeing. Special considerations regarding age, gender, disability, and displacement, among others, will be considered at all stages of the project. CPP training provides beneficiaries with the knowledge they need to provide for their physical protection and for the protection of others during periods of armed conflict. CPP safety messaging is designed to help prevent people from taking risks during conflict scenarios and to feel empowered to take active decisions for one's own protection before, during, and after conflict. Training content for children is specifically designed to be child-friendly and promote conflict safety principles in a way that is accessible and sensitive to their wellbeing. NPA employs a CPP curriculum for children entitled “Stay Safe”, which seeks to teach children basic conflict preparedness and protection principles and safety measures via the following child-friendly and entertaining approaches:• Use of cartoon character role models, each representing a different aspect of CPP.• Interactive in-class school CPP education sessions which include group activities and demonstrations.• “Hakawati” storytelling featuring CPP and EORE safety messaging• High-quality risk education materials (kids’ magazine, posters, banners, stickers, badges, etc.)• Child-friendly videos and music featuring CPP/EORE safety messaging.• School events featuring activators, entertainers, and clowns who engage the audience and affirm retention of CPP/EORE safety messaging passed on in classroom-based sessions.Classroom-based training sessions for children follow a “Before, During, After” structure with a focus on basic safety messaging which covers safe behavior during a crisis, preparation of the home, safe evacuation, useful improvised survival tips, and Explosive Ordnance Risk Education. The training package was initially deployed and continuously developed in cooperation with the Palestinian Ministry of Education and Higher Learning from 2016 to 2020 and is now being utilized by NPA’s CPP implementing partners.NPA and PSCF have worked together to integrate a psychosocial support mechanism into the project to help provide assistance to training beneficiaries who report having experienced trauma from past incidents of armed conflict. This support is provided by a dedicated psychologist staff member on the project who is responsible for following up cases and conducting sessions with those suffering from trauma, helping to mitigate the reverberating psychological effects of conflict. NPA's Code of Conduct and Safeguarding Policies help prevent and mitigate risks for project beneficiaries and staff. Complaint mechanisms will be put in place to help NPA and its partners respond to issues and complaints. Routine QA and monitoring in the field to ensure that activities are being conducted in line with the humanitarian principle of Do No Harm, while beneficiary feedback will help inform the CPP programme team of any potential risks observed by the beneficiaries themselves.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

NPA’s vision is solidarity in action and NPA stands for equal rights for all, irrespective of sex, ethnicity, religion, sexual orientation, disability, and social status. NPA’s CPP framework has been designed to meet the needs of those most vulnerable to the direct and indirect effects of armed conflict. NPA will prioritize targeting accessible at-risk areas and communities most severely affected by past conflict, as well as vulnerable populations such as women, children, people with disabilities (PwD) and caregivers, and refugees where possible. NPA will continue to work with its network of local partners with specialization in reaching specific disability groups and adapt its CPP messaging and methodologies to better meet the needs of PwD under our 2023 programme activities. CPP risk education activities will be disability mainstreamed in order to better meet the needs of PwD and guarantee their participation in the project. NPA and PSCF will work to better reach people with mobility-related disabilities by conducting home-visit CPP trainings specifically targeted at individuals who may not otherwise be able to attend community-based training sessions. NPA will coordinate and work with other organizations operating in Gaza which specialize in assistance to PwD in order to reach a minimum of 500 PwD, with an estimated 400 direct PwD child beneficiaries via school-based trainings and 100 adult PwD via home visit trainings. Accessibility will be a key consideration in the implementation of these training sessions and, where necessary, NPA and PSCF may provide transportation assistance to enable persons with mobility disabilities to attend community-based training sessions. NPA will seek to target groups of people with hearing disabilities through dedicated trainings which utilize a sign language interpreter for the delivery of the training. As the primary implementing partner for this project, PSCF will utilize its robust referral system to link PwD with essential services regarding health and psychosocial assistance. NPA has begun involving PwD in the production of its risk education media and materials and has developed two risk education videos in 2022 which utilize subtitles and sign language to better reach and meet the needs of people with hearing disabilities. NPA will continue to work to ensure future video content posted to social media will be designed and disseminated with the needs of PwD in mind. Over the course of the project, NPA will actively consult and seek out feedback from beneficiaries with disabilities to better inform the project design, lesson plans, materials design, and make continuous improvements with regard to accessibility. To this end, NPA and PSCF will conduct focus group discussions with PwD in January 2023 regarding the project’s planned approach and content, focusing on how to improve accessibility and effectiveness. At the global level, NPA has taken a leadership role in advocacy on Explosive Weapons in Populated Areas (EWIPA) and is heavily engaged with international actors leading up to the planned political declaration on EWIPA in Dublin in 2023. NPA will continue to leverage its advocacy efforts to highlight the disproportionate impact of explosive weapons on PwD and push for greater recognition of their needs.

NPA will ensure that the project will be implemented on the basis of the IASC six Core principles. NPA Palestine is in the process of getting full membership at PSEA Network. NPA’s Safeguarding Policy outlines how the organization will work to prevent and respond to harm caused by sexual exploitation, abuse or harassment in and outside the workplace. Special attention is given to exploitation and abuse of children. All NPA staff must sign and abide by NPA’s Code of Conduct in which outlines individual responsibilities and accountability regarding PSEA. NPA’s Safeguarding Policy aims to protect people from any harm caused by contact with NPA, including but not limited to the conduct of staff, partners, service providers, and personnel associated with NPA, and the design and implementation of NPA’s programmes and activities. The policy outlines the commitments made by NPA and informs staff and associated personnel of their safeguarding responsibilities, as well as guides NPA’s cooperation and dialogue with its partners. In line with its safeguarding policies, NPA Palestine has a trained focal point for safeguarding and PSEA based in Gaza, as well as dedicated personnel at NPA Head Office in Oslo, Norway, responsible for implementing and following up the organization’s safeguarding policy. As a partner-focused project, NPA will work with PSCF to ensure that PSEA policies are in place by linking our partner staff to with PSEA focal point training and organizing a PSEA orientation session for partner staff (4 women, 3 men) to be delivered by NPA Palestine’s safeguarding focal point at the beginning of 2023. Both NPA and PSCF will maintain robust complaint mechanisms (whistleblower website, email, complaint box, and phone) to enable project rightsholders/participants to report any infringement of the dignity of an individual by a project-associated individual’s comments or behaviour. This includes sexual discrimination or harassment of members of the local community, partners, employees, vendors or individuals visiting or conducting business. Internally, any staff reporting concerns or complaints regarding the project or staff participating in it will be protected by NPA’s Whistle-blower Guidelines. NPA will follow up safeguarding reports and concerns in accordance with its policies and procedures, legal and statutory obligations, and NPA’s Routines for Notifying of Censurable Conditions. NPA will apply appropriate disciplinary measures to staff found in breach of its Safeguarding Policy. If found to be a serious abuse, the matter will be reported to the police or other statutory authorities for criminal investigation unless to do so would cause the survivor further harm. Support will be offered to survivors of abuse and the person accused of perpetrating it. Support can include specialist psychosocial counselling or access to other specialist or appropriate support. Survivors of abuse can choose if and when they would like to take up the support options available to them, and this support must be tailored to local conditions.
