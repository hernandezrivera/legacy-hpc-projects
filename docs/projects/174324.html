Project Summary

The project aim at ensuring that children affected by the humanitarian situation in NW and SW receive integrated support and care through and efficient child protection case management system; and that Community-based mechanisms for child protection are strengthened for increased community capacity to prevent, identify and respond to child protection concerns. The project activities include:
• Providing mental health and psychosocial support to children and their caregivers
• Conducting targeted assessments of reported spikes in family separation to understand the reasons for the separation to inform appropriate response
• Identifying and implement measures to prevent family separations, and Identify Unaccompanied and Separated children, and ensure adequate alternative care arrangements and reunification for UASC
• Providing support to families to better protect their children, and build the capacity of Community-Based Child Protection Mechanisms
• Providing support to issue birth certificates to conflict-affected children
• Provide interventions for the prevention and mitigation of risks and response to GBV affecting children
• Providing support to identified at-risk families to protect children from child labour, including worst forms of child labour, through prevention support
• Providing children already engaged in child labour, including worst forms of child labour, with, at a minimum tailored child protection case management; multisectoral services (such as psychosocial support, education and family economic empowerment); and pathways to decent work for children of legal working age.
The project is going to mainstream gender and protection in it design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. In order to ensure accountability to affected population, the project is going to create community complaint and feedback mechanisms committees in each targeted community. The project is going to be implemented in the NW (Bui, Ngo ketunjia, Menchum, Boyo, Momo, Ndonga mantung, and Mezam division) in the SW (Ndian, Manyu and Meme division)

1 - Besoins / Needs

The on-going crises in the South-West and North-West regions of Cameroon and the multiple displacements caused by it have affected children the most. It is estimated that more than 270.000 children are in need of psychosocial support services in the SW and NW regions, in addition to 120.000 children in the adjacent regions, namely West and Littoral. Children are also being reportedly recruited into armed forces and armed groups by both parties to the conflict to perform different tasks. As the conflict persists, more cases of sexual violence, harassment and forced and early marriages accompanied by unwanted pregnancies, particularly targeting adolescent girls, are being reported. Majority of those girls are pregnant and/or HIV positive, while they do not have access to medical care, including pre-natal care, due to the lack/loss of civil documentation (i.e. ID card or birth certificate) to go through checkpoints to access health facilities/services. Family separation is one of the major concerns identified by Child Protection actors in this crisis, with at least 2500 children who are either unaccompanied or separated from their parent(s); most of which are reported to be living with other members of their communities or left alone in child-headed households in deplorable conditions in urban and peri-urban areas, including in neighboring regions of Littoral, West, and Adamou with very limited services available.
