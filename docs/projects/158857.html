Project Summary

The project contributes to the cluster objectives and activities. The project Is a humanitarian intervention that improves access to high-quality, cost-effective basic health services and to strengthen the resilience of vulnerable communities. The interventions include; Ensuring access to primary health care (PHC) services of the most vulnerable population in at least 47 locations in the district of Salfit, Tulkarem, Qalqilia, Nablus, Hebron, Bethlehem, Jericho, and vulnerable groups in East-Jerusalem, by the provision of equitable access to preventive and curative health services. Providing Social hardship cases with necessary medication in Gaza Strip and the West Bank. Ensuring access to Physiotherapeutic rehabilitation of people with disabilities as well as stroke, accident and postoperative patients in Gaza Strip. Improving community based emergency preparedness by training and establishing emergency response teams in vulnerable communities (West Bank and East Jerusalem).

1 - Needs

The needs of 2020 in terms of targeted locations and beneficiaries remain the same. According to the narrative report of the health cluster (2019), Palestinian continue to face challenges in accessing health services across the oPt, particularly in Gaza, where needs have substantially increased in the past years. But also, vulnerable locations and communities in the West Bank. An estimated 1.3 million people are in need of humanitarian health interventions across oPt, of whom two out of every three are in Gaza. In the West Bank, the total people in need is approx. 377,803 people are in need of humanitarian interventions. Out of which vast majority, over 162,633 people are located in Area C, Hebron H2 and the ‘Seam Zone”. They are suffering from inadequate or missing access to primary health care, medical emergency services, and psychological care. Since 30 March 2018, the Gaza Strip has witnessed an enormous increase in Palestinian casualties in the context of mass demonstrations taking place along Israel’s perimeter fence with Gaza. The demonstrations have occurred as part of the ‘Great March of Return’. The large number of casualties among unarmed Palestinian demonstrators, including a high percentage of demonstrators hit by live ammunition, has raised concerns about excessive use of force by Israeli troops. Gaza's health sector is struggling to cope with the mass influx of casualties, due to years of blockade, internal divide and a chronic energy crisis, which have left essential services in Gaza barely able to function (OCHA, 2018). Repeatedly the HNO identified the need for rehabilitation of patients with disabilities or traumatic injuries and the provision of walking aids, wheelchairs, hospital beds, etc. for those groups (partly, they are victims of Israeli attacks). The rehabilitation of persons with traumatic injuries that led to physical impairments remains especially in Gaza highly problematic. In addition, access to healthcare services for women across the oPt, including maternal and reproductive healthcare, is also negatively impacted by specific contextual challenge. The planned activities in this project aim to improve access to essential primary health care services and provision of medication to social hardship cases in WB and Gaza. Further, provision of physiotherapeutic rehabilitation of people with disabilities. Where providing access to services comes in proportion to need and without barriers, paying special attention to individuals and disabled people who may be particularly vulnerable or having difficulty accessing assistance. Concerning protection at least 1.9 million Palestinians experience or are at risk of conflict and violence, force displacement, and denial of access to livelihoods, and are in need of protection assistance. These needs have not been fully responded by other actors, as House demolitions, expropriations, eviction, expansion of settlements and military operations are still part of daily live under occupation and access to primary healthcare services continue to face major challenges. Repeatedly The humanitarian situation in the H2 area of the Hebron city reported that the need for emergency acute care remains critical with approximately 33,000 people affected in areas across the West Bank, including H2 in Hebron. As a response, this project supports the development of communities’ and individual capacities and assist people to claim their rights, through improving community-based emergency preparedness on the West Bank including East Jerusalem, and by this, the project the needs have been re-validated and the project contributes to the cluster objectives.

2 - Activities

Sub goal 1: Marginalized Palestinian communities in the districts of Bethlehem, Hebron, Tulkarem, Qalqilya, Nablus, Salfit, Jericho (Jordan Valley) and East Jerusalem have access to curative and preventive primary health care services and health education measures, through 5 mobile clinics. All mobile clinics are equipped with medical equipment, laboratory supplies, medical disposables and drugs. The (MC) perform preventive (screening) and health education sessions on reproductive health, healthy lifestyle, nutrition, NCD, infectious disease etc. MC operate in accessible places, and ensure high standard privacy. The needs of women, girls, and disabled people are prioritized through having a Women health care doctor, conducting on site inquiries to identify people with disabilities, and targeting women with their new born (Postnatal) to perform a health checkup and identify risk factors, if any. Provision of all kinds of essential medications needed for the treatment of women related diseases. Provision of health awareness sessions and training sessions to mothers, women, female students, and families of disabled people about most common health problems and first aid skills. Sub goal 2: Supporting the further treatment of patients by referral to health centers in the West Bank. 4 health centers in Qalqilya, Tulkarem, Jiftlik in (JV) and Hebron will receive the transferred patients for further treatment/diagnosis. A fifth center in Ramallah, the Non-Communicable Diseases center, will serve the referred NCD patients from all MCs. Sub goal 3: Providing social hardship cases with the necessary medication in the Gaza Strip and the West Bank. The procurement of medicines will cover immediate and acute medical needs of people who would not have access to these drugs and could not afford the treatment, especially in the current economic and supply crisis. The support is provided inter alia for patients with chronic or rare diseases in the Gaza Strip and the West Bank or persons with diseases that require expensive medications. The beneficiaries receive drugs upon submission of documentary evidence that confirm the needs on socio economic grounds. The criteria for selection; patients from families with no or low income, elderly people without financial support, people with disabilities and single mothers, families without head of household, and injured people. The whole mechanism is fully coordinated with MoH, UNRWA, and other health providers. Sub goal 4: Improved community-based emergency preparedness on the West Bank including East Jerusalem. Community based crisis preparedness will be improved in 33 localities. For this purpose, new crisis volunteers will be trained in 15 communities in order to build up 15 new crisis preparedness teams. Adding to that a follow up training with 18 existing teams will be conducted on a yearly basis. The basic training takes place in intensive courses on several days. The course was created in cooperation with the Palestinian Civil Defence Authority, An Najah University Nablus and PMRS. Activities prepare communities to emergency that may occur as a result of natural events or a deterioration of the security situation.it will improve the initial reaction on the ground. Female membership ensures a suitable response for both genders. Sub goal 5: Physiotherapeutic rehabilitation of people with disabilities as well as stroke, accident and postoperative patients in Gaza Strip. Receive access to qualified physiotherapy in the two PMRS rehabilitation centers, which might include home visits if necessary. Beneficiaries are selected according to medical and socioeconomic criteria (e.g. female head of household, families without or insufficient income, children, no access to other services etc.).obile clinics will be targeting the following locations that weren’t found on the locations' list: -Bethlehem, Rabaia -Hebron, Jabal Johar (H2 area).

3 - Indicators

Sub goal 1: •22,000 patient contact benefited from curative medical measures •21,600 patient contact benefited from preventive services (screening and awareness sessions. Sub goal 2: •Approx. 1,750 patients received further treatment after referral to the four PMRS health centers and the Non-Communicable Diseases Center in Ramallah. Sub goal 3: •Medications for approx. 11,700 patient contacts (classified as social hardship cases) were procured, delivered to the central pharmacies of PMRS in Ramallah and Gaza and distributed to persons in need. Sub goal 4: •15 new crisis response teams (107 community members living in 15 vulnerable communities in the West Bank and Gaza Strip) were formed, are ready to act and assure first lifesaving interventions if necessary. 80% completed the courses successfully (test pre and post course) •18 existing crisis response teams (243 community members living in 18 vulnerable communities) completed refresh training courses successfully and continue to assure lifesaving intervention when needed. 80% of the new formed teams finished the course successfully (final test). •3,000 persons received immediate assistance by 350 volunteers of the 33 crisis response teams through the first year of the project. Sub goal 5: •90 persons (people with disabilities, patients after strokes, accidents and surgeries) were newly registered for physio therapeutic care. •75% of these patients improved their condition based on the rehabilitation scale used by PMRS, depending on the medical situation of each case (e.g. by two levels for stroke patients, by 60 % for accident patients and for postoperative rehabilitation).

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The safety and dignity of beneficiaries is ensured through ensuring that the areas where the mobile clinic teams provide services are safe, easy to access within the community and with other surroundings communities, and separated from public spaces. In addition, 65% of the mobile clinic teams are female with skills and experience in order in order to work with women and make them feel comfortable. All the mobile clinic team signed the code of conduct that monitors and reports instances of abuse.
