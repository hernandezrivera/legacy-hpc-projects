Project Summary

The project will be implemented in three locations in northwest Syria within sub-districts of severity 4 and 5. The first location is in Idleb city, the second location is in Dana city, the third location is in Azaz city. After signing memorandums of understanding with the local councils and the Education Directorates in those locations, 3 Temporary Leaning Spaces (TLSs) will be established to accommodate 9300 out-of school (OOSC) and dropped out children (girls and boys aged between 8 and 17 years old – 10% children with disability). These children will be targeted with Self Learning Programme (SLP), Accelerated Learning Program (ALP), Remedial Classes and Catch-up Classes. 60 education personnel (females, males, IDPs and Host Community) will be hired and trained to run the 3 Temporary Leaning Spaces. The overall Objective is to scale up safe and equitable access to non-formal education for crisis-affected children (girls and boys aged between 8 and 17 years old – 10% children with disability) which is in line with the education sector objectives.
9300 students (10% children with disability) will be provided with school supplies in formal and non-formal settings (Stationary, notebooks, school bags, school supply kits). The project also aims at enhancing the quality of non-formal education through training 1200 teachers and education personnel (600 males, 600 females) on SLP, ALP, Remedial Classes, Catch-up Classes, Education in Emergency (EiE), active learning, classroom management ...etc.

4 - Needs

Based on the 2022 Syria HNO, the protracted nature of the conflict, economic duress and the Covid-19 pandemic, continue to impact overstretched education services.Attendance rates between boys and girls were similar but older children (12-17 years) were less likely to attend than younger children (5-11 years). Rates of non attendance were highest in Idleb (28per cent), Aleppo (26per cent). Based on Education Sector Findings 2022 HNO, over a quarter of households with school aged children indicated that their children are not in school because there is no school to send their child to. Based on CCCM ISIMM Education dashboard, in 1,017 assessed camp sites in northwest, there are only 196 schools. The most dominant form of learning (95 per centper cent) is in-person and formal with less than two percent of children attending non-formal education services that bring children back to learning and address learning loss (2021 MSNA).The consequence of underinvestment in teachers is reflected by the 60per cent of households with school age children, that perceive a need to enhance teacher capacity to improve education services. Idleb has a critical confluence of negative factors: low attendance rates, the highest classroom to school aged population ratio, the only governorate with triple and quadruple shifts, nearly half the schools are non-operational and the largest proportion of children traveling +45 minutes to school and the highest rates of attacks on education.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Threat 1: Learning facilities could be attacked. Attacks may include: airstrikes, bombs/explosions/shelling, fighting/gunshots, arson/fire, looting/theft; kidnapping/arrest of a teacher or students, direct attack on teacher or student, intimidation of school staff / students or military use of the school.Mitigating Measures: Threats and associated risks may be multiple and varied. They may be influenced by sex, age, geographic area, disability, time of day, time of year, events and other factors. These should be identified and to the extent possible measures taken to reduce the risks for each population group. Depending on the threats and risks it may be relevant to:• Consider sunrise and sunset times when setting learning hours. Risk/Threat 2: Teachers or other education personnel participate in physical punishment, act violently, bully and neglect students. Students participate in bullying or act violently towards other students and/or to teachers. Mitigating Measures: Learning facilities should have an enforced code of conduct against all forms of violence and unacceptable conduct, including bullying, corporal punishment, and physical, verbal and sexual abuse. Teachers and school personnel should be trained on prevention and reporting of, and issues related to, violence, physical punishment and bullying and the use of positive discipline The application of these skills by all school personnel should be monitored as part of ongoing professional development and support.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk:Aid workers or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they should be serving. Mitigating Measures:Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policies should be in place, practiced and monitored. Polices should cover standards of behavior for the organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and include accountability measures. Polices should address risks at the organizational level and the program level.The targeted communities and learning facilities should be aware of the organization’s policies and procedures.The organization’s staff and targeted communities should have access to safe and effective reporting systems including knowing when they will get a response to their report and having the right to report anonymously.Organizations should ensure a survivor centered approach that ensures the safety and confidentiality of the affected person. The survivor should have a say in the processes.A poor handling of allegations of sexual explanation and abuse can put the accuser/survivor, alleged perpetrator and others at risk. If you need support in dealing with PSEA issues, please contact OCHA who has technical PSEA experts.Afaq is now a member of the PSEA Network, and it commits all the network’s recommendations since the beginning of 2020. It has also an investigation committee and a PSEA focal point in place.All Afaq staff sign the HLG Code of Conduct.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Threat1:Children are over-exposed to the risk of, or experience, harm by teachers, school personnel, organization staff or related entities. Mitigation Measures:Learning facilities should have Child Safeguarding polices and systems in place by the organizationshould be regularly monitored and corrective action taken when needed.The goal of child safeguarding is to create and maintain a safe learning environment through preventive actions that protect children from actions, or inactions that may lead to harm.Child Safeguarding should cover all people who may interact with children.These could be organization staff, teachers, other school personnel, school bus drivers, contactors, vendors.-Organizations should ensure a survivor centered approach that ensures the safety and confidentiality of the affected person.The survivor should have a say in the processes. Threat 2-Learning facilities or their surroundings are structurally unsafe or do not provide sufficient protection from the elements (rain, snow, cold, dust, etc.) or from public health risks including Covid-19 and other communicable diseases.Mitigation measures:-Learning facilities should be improved to ensure that buildings and grounds are safe and protected.This may include stairs, walls, roofs, flooring, electric, water, etc. School grounds should have fencing.All works should be supervised and monitored by technical personnel.The organization should ensure Child Safeguarding measures are in place before work start.
