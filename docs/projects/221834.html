Project Summary

In line with the Nutrition Cluster and HNRP strategic objectives for 2025, this project will provide emergency nutrition interventions targeting IDPs, returnees, and Host communities affected by conflict, flood, disease outbreak and displacement in Pariang, Mayom, Abiemnhom and Pibor. The project will target 21,522 children with SAM (male – 10,546 & female – 10,976), 35,908 children with MAM (Male – 17595 & Female – 18, 313), 26,968 PLW with MAM, 19,178 children with BSFP, 15,981PLW with BSFP, 20,532 PLW & 41,064 children (0-23 months) with MIYCN, 87,261 children (6-59 months) with VASD, and 71,862 children (12-24 months) with deworming in Abiemnhom, Mayom, Pariang, and Pibor. During the emergency nutrition intervention, 2,441 and 3,857 children with SAM will be Returnees and IDPs respectively.
Within the 4 counties, CARE will work with communities to prevent malnutrition and build resilience among children, adolescents, and women. This will be done through awareness raising on good nutrition practices with a particular focus on MIYCN. Nutrition workers, CNVs and health workers will be trained on social, and behavior change communication and Maternal, Infant, and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) sensitization. CNVs will conduct outreach campaigns and will disseminate key nutrition messages. Mothers support groups will be established, sensitized on good nutrition practices through CMAM and MIYCN approaches using the same messages as those conveyed by CNVs
CARE will conduct routine screening (active case finding), referral and follow up at community level. This approach will be complemented with bi-annual MUAC mass screening in order to maximize identification of children under five and PLW/G with MAM and SAM as early as possible. Identified cases will be referred to the nearest nutrition facility to facilitate timely treatment. In order to support availability and quality of service delivery, nutrition facilities will be appropriately equipped and provided with nutrition supplies and tools such as anthropometric and reporting tools, in accordance with the national CMAM guidelines. CARE will also support 3 Stabilization Centers in Pariang, Mayom and Abiemnhom.
CARE will enhance nutrition program monitoring, analysis, and utilization of nutrition information through our MEAL department. The collected information will be shared with relevant stakeholders and also used to improve the quality and impact of the project.
CARE will ensure that Nutrition facilities are providing IMNCI services based on the Ministry of Health Guidelines. CARE will also carry out safety audits at all the nutrition facilities every 6 months and ensure that the gaps identified are addressed. Staff will be provided with basic skills on GBV prevention and response. CARE will also establish kitchen gardens at all the nutrition facilities, as well as providing WASH kits to children admitted with Severe Wasting. CARE will also ensure the integration of early childhood development programmes at nutrition facilities.
In implementing this project, CARE will ensure that the affected population is engaged and their voices heard throughout the implementation of the project. This will be done though monthly project monitoring activities as well as integrating feedback, complaints and response mechanisms throughout project areas. CARE will also ensure that data from this project is disaggregated based on gender and age to ensure that specific challenges affecting men, women, children, youth and the elderly are identified and addressed. In addition, CARE will ensure that the facilities are disability friendly and can be accessed with minimal challenges.
CARE will also work with communities to identify locally available nutritious food stuffs which can be used to reduce the prevalence of wasting through scaling up their production and proper preparation. The use of locally available food stuffs will encourage the level of acceptance from the community.

1. Needs

The recently released IPC results estimated that 6.1 million people will experience high levels of food insecurity between December 2024 and March 2025, with 45% of the population experiencing Phase 3 or above acute food insecurity. This number is projected to increase to 7.3 million people between April and July 2025. This will include 2.53 million people likely to be in IPC Phase 4 and 63,000 people likely to be in IPC Phase 5. This elevated food insecurity will have a negative impact on the malnutrition situation in the country, with up to 2.1 million children between 6 and 59 months expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025. The IPC estimates that during the same period, approximately 1.11 million pregnant or breastfeeding women (PBW) will also experience elevated levels of acute malnutrition.The four counties of Mayom, Abiemnhom, Pariang, and Pibor, with a combined population of 590,988 (NBS), have one of the highest malnutrition burdens in the country, compounded with flood impact, which has displaced population-affected nutrition facilities. Mayom County is among the severely affected counties, with an estimated 7,240 households comprising 42,844 individuals displaced by floods as homes are submerged and destroyed (IRNA, 2024), leading to exacerbated malnutrition risk due to destroyed wash facilities and crop and farming land submergence. Between December 2024 and March 2025, the estimated population in IPC 3 and above across the 4 counties was 71,000 in Pibor, 7,000 in Pariang, 8,000 in Mayom, 8,000 in 161,708, and 3,000 in Abiemnhom. Between April and July, Pibor will have a total population of 82,000 across IPC 3 and 4, and 12,000 under IPC 5. During the same period, Abiemnhom, Mayom, and Pariang will have 6,000, 16,000, and 7,000 people in IPC 3 and 4, respectively.The ongoing flooding has significantly impacted the communities we serve, especially in the counties of Pariang and Mayom, where floods have cut off up to 15 out of 36 nutrition facilities (IRNA, 2024). The flooding has destroyed crops, cut off communities from accessing health and nutrition facilities, markets, and other vital commodities, and led to the deaths of livestock, which are the lifeline of the population in the affected counties. The flooding is also likely to lead to an increase in mortality and morbidity, especially due to malaria and watery diarrhea, which have been key drivers of malnutrition. OCHA projected that by the 15th of November 2024, over 43,000 people had been displaced by floods in Mayom, as well as the IRNA assessment conducted by partners on September 20-27, 2024, in Mayom County, which demonstrated that an estimated 7,240 households comprising 42,844 individuals were displaced by floods as homes are submerged and destroyed (IRNA, 2024). Pariang is also affected, though on a lower scale.Pariang and Abiemnhom have also continued to receive returnees and refugees from Sudan through the Jau border point. Though the numbers have been fluctuating, CARE, through its programs in the two counties, supported 15,250 refugees and returnees up to October 2024.Frequent population displacement, driven by inter- and intracommunal conflict and cattle raiding, also contributes significantly to the high levels of malnutrition. During such conflicts, livelihoods are destroyed, particularly in Pibor, where 37,600 people are estimated to be displaced, heightening the dire nutrition situation in Pibor.The ongoing economic crisis, currency devaluation, and reduced market access and functionality are also expected to play a detrimental role in the deterioration of the food security situation in 2025.It is likely that severe food insecurity and the malnutrition and acute hunger crisis that will follow, including the deaths of 12 people in Lekuangole in July 2024 (OCHA, 2024), will continue to be caused by acute food insecurity, flood, and violence between communities and cattle raiding.

2. Project activities

To respond to the above needs and in line with the 2025 Nutrition Cluster Strategy and Objectives, CARE will support at total of 37 Static Nutrition Facilities and 4 Mobile Nutrition facilities distributed as follows (Pariang 16 Static sites; Mayom 16 static sites, Pibor 4 Mobile Sites, and Abiemnom 5 Static Sites). CARE will employ the mobile strategy when the needs arise to ensure access to populations displaced by either floods or conflict, so the number of mobile Nutrition facilities may increase based on the needs and wasting trends through 2025. CARE will carry out the following activities: 1. Sectoral Objective 1: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services to prevent malnutrition and build resilience among children, adolescents, and women Pariang, Mayom, and Abiemnom and Pibor by the end of 2025. i. Support Blanket Supplementary Feeding Programme (BSFP) for girls and boys between 6-23 months. ii. Provide MIYCN counselling services for primary caregivers of children 0-23 months. iii. Support Vitamin A and deworming campaigns in the 4 counties. Iii. Support Social Protection through cash assistance. 2. Sectoral Objective 2: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under-five-year of age and PLW affected by acute malnutrition Pibor, Mayom, Abiemnom, and Pariang by the end of 2024. i. Screen children 6-59 months for Acute Malnutrition. ii. Admit and treat children between 6-59 months diagnosed with SAM. iii. Admit and treat children between 6-59 months diagnosed with MAM. iv. Screen Pregnant and Lactating women for Acute Malnutrition. v. Admit and treat PLW with MAM. vi. Conduct programmatic visits to nutrition sites (nutrition manager/coordinator from Country Office) to improve programme quality. vii. Provide on job training to health and nutrition staff. viii. Provide CNVs with training and tools to enable them report on a regular basis. ix. Support Kitchen gardens at the Nutrition Sites in the 4 counties. x. Support Kitchen gardens at the household level in the 4 counties. xi. Support Family MUAC data reporting in the 5 counties. xii. Support the establishment of a feedback mechanism in the 4 counties. Xii. Advocate for the integration of standalone nutrition sites into health facilities. Sectoral Objective 3: To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis, and utilization of nutrition information in Pibor, Mayom, Pariang and Abiemnhom by the end of 2025. i. Conduct Nutrition Surveys in at least 3 of the 4 counties. ii. Improve Nutrition Information Management Systems in the 4 counties. Sectoral Objective 4: To increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and integrated programming in Pibor, Mayom, Pariang and Abiemnhom i. Provide Prevention, screening, and treatment for comorbidities for PBWs and children under five years with acute malnutrition in nutrition sites ii. Integrate Nutrition with WASH and provide WASH kits to children with SAM in nutrition sites iii. Establish or maintain demonstration kitchen garden at nutrition sites or in the vicinity iv. Integrate child stimulation and ECD in the provision of care for children with SAM and MAM v. Mainstream GBV risk mitigation at nutrition sites vi. Collaborate with health in the management of diarrhea.
