Project Summary

In Jabal Smaan District 387,561 persons are risking to lose a vital service if “Al-Huda Hospita” stops its services. Due to the collapse of the health system in Northern Syria jeopardizes the continuity of care by lack of human resources, pharmaceuticals, vaccinations, medical supplies and medical equipment to serve most vulnerable people.
Availability of health services in Northern Syria is ensured mostly by NGOs. Despite this, health continues to have acute problems including lack of health systems structures, weak emergency and referral systems. On another hand, isolation of primary health care from secondary Health Care risk to create a rupture in health service continuity and in a nutshell risking to loose quality of health services and by that risking to loose lives.
According to OCHA , since late 2017 North-West of Syria received several influx of internally displaced people (IDPs) from different parts of the country, exacerbating already existing high levels of needs. These new displacements had a noticeable effect on the number of people in need of humanitarian assistance in North-West Syria, which increased from over 2.3 million in August 2017 to almost 2.7 million people over a year from which 6 % are in the area of intervention considered in this proposal. According to CCCM report and OCHA, in September 2018 over 84,000 people were newly displaced in Syria. Most recently, an estimated 53,000 people were newly displaced in Idleb resulting from renewed conflict in the first half of September 2018. As of October 2018, out of the total number of people in need 1.9 million are displaced people (IDPs). The impact of the winter season increase humanitarian needs especially for displaced people, affecting water and sanitation in camps, health, food, and winterization services as life-saving humanitarian services.
On another, shortage of funds supporting such a vital and urgent service is also major dominator for SKT to apply to this reserve allocation to saves lives.
The Proposed project aims to respond to immediate emergency requirements for most vulnerable communities as well as cover the vital gaps in the health system. The proposed project aims to improve the access of IDPs and host communities in Western rural side of Aleppo to emergency health care services by running one secondary health care hospital located in western Aleppo. Al-Huda Hospital plays a vital role providing vital and life-saving health services to the vulnerable population from Jabal Smaan, Daret Azza, Haritan and Atareb sub-districts. The proposed hospital is out funds since June 2018 and is risking stopping to provide its services if no urgent fund allocated to continue operating.
This proposal aims to cover the running cost of primary and secondary health services provided by Al-Huda Hospital as well the emergency referral services by supporting the running cost of three ambulances.
In close coordination with Health Cluster partners, Aleppo Health Directorate and local councils the hospital was found critical and an urgent support is needed to save lives.
The proposed project will follow the logic of 2018’s SKT baseline to ensure a high level of gender equality and maintain General Assembly resolution 57/306 (PSEA) related to Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). On another hand, this equality could be jeopardized by any unpredictable change in the field and/or flow of patients due trauma and illness cases and medical priorities/triage.

Implementation of activities

SKT with its core management staff is responsible for the overall planning, supervision and M&E of the project. The existing SKT technical teams will build the capacity of field staff for the respective project components. SKT’s field teams will implement the project based on OCHA SOPs. SKT has integrated the project into its existing and functional programme structure and health data collection and analysis system. Health information data from hospital are fed into the overall humanitarian coordination structure, including EWARN, through the respective clusters. In-kind donations for medicines and equipment will be sought from other sources (WHO, UNICEF, crowd funding, etc.). Equipment purchasing and high-level service contracts will be implemented by SKT turkey logistic team. Financial and reporting management will be done by the senior staff of SKT in Turkey. Close coordination with hospital staff will be applied to improve their capacity in management and health sides. The project will be supervised, monitored and managed by a Programme Manager and a Programme Coordinator with assistance from project teams including Project Manager. The Project Manager will be responsible for direct management of hospital. The Project Manager would assure that all necessary materials, equipment, medications, etc. are available in the field. The technical management and financial management of the programme will be under the Project Manager. The Project Manager will be responsible to coordinate with WHO and Health Cluster Coordinator for the provision of technical training to hospital staff.

Protection Risk Analysis

1- Violence against Health Facilities and Healthcare Providers: Mitigating measures: -Applying the designated de-confliction mechanism for health facilities, and increasing advocacy efforts by the health sector with all parties to the conflict to engage them on health protection topics and respect of international humanitarian law (IHL). - Continued reporting on attacks against healthcare, and enlisting support of the Strategic Steering Group (SSG) and humanitarian leadership in their efforts to highlight protections challenges facing health workers across Syria. 2- Gender Based Violence GBV:Mitigating measures:Prevention and management of sexual violence and Intimate Partner Violence is considered to be a life-saving intervention. Accordingly, affected population should have access to the reproductive health services of the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) as part of the essential health package.- Specialized health staff should be trained on providing first-line support, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals.- Identification of safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases connecting health services and clinical management with social services, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), in a manner guarantees safety, confidentiality, and privacy of the survivor.- Presence of female staff in health facilities increase access of women and girls to health services. 3- Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA: SKT strive and commit to preventing sexual exploitation and abuse both on organizational and programmatic levels. Mitigating measures: - A PSEA policy describes standard of behaviour for organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and inclusive to accountability measures. - Effective community based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance. - Effective and comprehensive mechanisms should be established to ensure awareness-raising on SEA amongst personnel, with continuous training on the topic. - Effective recruitment and performance management of all humanitarian staff. - Provide information to communities and people affected by crisis about the organization, the principles it adheres to, how it expects its staff to behave, the programmes it is implementing and what they intend to deliver. - Design and implement appropriate programmes based on an impartial assessment of needs and risks, and an understanding of the vulnerabilities and capacities of different groups, clear reporting and feedback mechanisms. 4- Provision of Healthcare for Vulnerable Groups and Equitable Access to Health Services:Mitigating measures: - Inclusion of vulnerable and minority groups needs throughout the program cycle (analysis, design, implementation, and monitoring) through gender, age, and disability disaggregated analysis of beneficiaries with identification of risk factors impacting service provision. - Implementation of standard essential services package across the sector interventions, including improved quality reporting and robust monitoring through standardized SOPs. - Regular revision and updating of regular, contingency, and emergency plans with emphasis on Do No Harm principles, specifically in scenarios involving sudden mass movements of IDPs and significant returns activities. - Addressing the specific needs of vulnerable groups through supporting dedicated health subgroups groups (trauma, physical ….ETC - Ensuring that health project design encompasses accessibility to services by vulnerable groups (physical accessibility, disaggregated areas for women and girls, translation, etc.) - Continuous advocacy effort to facilitate safe evacuation of injured people cross conflict lines to access the required lifesaving assistance.
