Project Summary

Access to water in Somalia remains a big challenge, as the country continues to experience regular droughts and floods, resulting from the effects of climate change. Recent assessments estimate that about 4.6 million Somalis are in need of sufficient portable water and sanitation services across the country; with the situation likely to worsen as the October to December 2020 Horn of Africa Climate Outlook indicates depressed rainfall season. This will result in water scarcity, putting a lot of pressure on strategic water sources as the majority of other sources dry up. During such a crisis, humanitarian interventions are scaled up to prevent the situation from deteriorating and leading to loss of lives. The interventions carried out depend on the availability of reliable data for informed decisions.
FAO, through the Somalia Water and Land Information Management (SWALIM) project keeps an inventory of water sources across the country. The inventory helps to quickly identify the operational status of the water sources, and the quantity and quality of water available for planning and timely intervention especially, during drought periods. In 2019, SWALIM with the support of UNICEF updated the database, bringing the total number of water sources to over 6,400. The limited resources available for the activity could however not cover the entire country. Gaps still exists in some regions, especially in the southern parts of the country where the survey teams did not go. There is a need to keep the data up-to-date for it to be useful in drought response as well as development projects. Through the proposed project, SWALIM intends to carry out field surveys, map and update data on at least 1,500 strategic water sources, including areas not covered in the 2019 survey. Out of the surveyed sources, 250 representative samples will be taken and analysed in the laboratory for common compounds and heavy metals to determine the suitability of the water for domestic consumption.
The WASH Cluster recently began discussions on criteria for the identification of strategic water sources. The exercise would lead to the prioritization of the key water sources in resource allocation to ensure constant water supply to vulnerable communities. SWALIM was tasked with the development of criteria for the identification of the strategic water sources. The criteria was developed and shared for review and adoption. Through this proposed project, SWALIM will screen the 6,430 water sources currently in the database to identify the strategic ones, and develop a mechanism of tagging them in the online database/Water Sources Live Map for easy identification.
The overreliance on groundwater in Somalia without adequate monitoring is likely to lead to over exploitation. The risk is higher in aquifers supplying water to urban areas where a rising population increases demand for this precious commodity. In the past, SWALIM has installed groundwater monitoring stations in six locations in northern parts of the country. In the proposed project, SWALIM intends to expand the monitoring network to 10 more locations, spread across the country. The monitoring equipment records water levels and basic water quality parameters at least once a day.
The above activities will be implemented in collaboration with the Ministry of Water Resources staff, as a way of building their capacity for eventual take over. The project will provide crucial data needed to support Cluster Objective 1 dealing with emergency water supply and Objective 2 on water supply. The data will be made available for review and download by partners through the Water Sources Live Map https://swims.faoswalim.org/dashboard/view.

Project Activities

Selection of strategic water sources from the existing database based on developed criteria (February – March 2021). Field surveys in selected drought-affected districts in southern and central regions of Somalia to update the strategic water sources database (April – August 2021). Detailed laboratory analysis of water samples from strategic water sources across the country (April – August 2021) for domestic use. Expand groundwater monitoring stations at key groundwater aquifers across the country, and continuous monitoring of water levels and basic water quality parameters (January – December 2021). Analysis of water sources data and production of monthly groundwater status updates (January – December 2021). Strengthening the capacity of Ministry of Water staff at Federal level, Federal Member State level and Somaliland in the monitoring and mapping of water sources, data analysis and reporting.

Project Indicators

- Number of strategic water sources selected out of more than 6,400 water sources in SWALIM’s database. - At least 1,500 strategic water sources visited in selected districts of Somalia and data collected on their functional status.- At least 250 representative samples from the strategic water sources analysed in the laboratory for water portability.- At least 10 groundwater monitoring stations procured, installed and made operational at different aquifers across Somalia. - Monthly groundwater status bulletins produced and disseminated. - At least 15 Ministry of Water staff trained in groundwater monitoring.

Mitigation Measures

1. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. In times of droughts women and children are most affected in the community. Information generated during this project will target the most vulnerable members of the community who are women and children. The participation of women in strategic water sources data collection will be encouraged. Data collected will be beneficial to the marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women boys and girls in identification of nearest water source for their water demands. 2. Risk of unsafe and unethical management of data, especially disclosure of sensitive personal data on beneficiaries. Some of the data collected during the water sources is sensitive, as it involves operator’s names, phone numbers, etc. FAO has developed strong IT and IM systems, which are secure from external access and interferences, which will be used to manage the data. Information shared to the public will also exclude this sensitive information, to safeguard it from misuse. 3. Risk of explosive hazard contamination in project target area.Access to some areas within Somalia is limited to conflict/security threats including presence of landmines or explosive remnants of war. The survey teams are expected to move across the country mapping and collecting information on water sources, hence exposing them to this type of risk. Coordination with the local security teams, and involvement of local staff who know the security issues/risks of their respective areas, will be adopted. FAO will monitor closely the security of local staff and partner organizations with guidance from the United Nations Department of Safety and Security (UNDSS) in Somalia.

Please provide further explanation

The proposed project aims to provide up-to-date information on the strategic water sources that communities depend, on especially during drought periods. The livelihood of Somali communities depends to a large extent on water resources, and information on this scarce resource in terms of availability (quantity and quality) is crucial for communities’ long term planning of economic activities and interventions. For the pastoralists, provision of water for livestock is as important as domestic water supply. For such communities lack of sufficient water could trigger water borne diseases, as people share with their animals the little amounts available. It is therefore important to maintain an updated water sources database, which can be used for informed decisions on water supply for domestic and other purposes, in line with communities demands.
