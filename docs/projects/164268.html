Project Summary

HNO 2019 estimated 2.1 million out-of-school and 1.3 million children at risk of dropping out. Over 5.8 million school-aged children and over 120,000 teachers need education assistance inside Syria. The HNO found that hostilities have resulted in over one in three schools being damaged, destroyed, no longer accessible or occupied for shelter and other purposes. Furthermore, the crisis and escalation of violence has led to increased war related psychological trauma for children, their families and education personnel, negatively affecting the overall learning outcomes.
This project aims to increase safe and quality access to education to crisis affected children aged 5-17 years by rehabilitating damaged schools in a gender and disability sensitive modes and providing teachers and other education personnel with a child- centered -psychosocial support programme training. UNHCR’s will use needs based approach to programming to implement the rehabilitation of schools in areas with high education severity scales. School rehabilitation will be undertaken in most affected locations in 54 sub- districts in Governorates of Rural Damascus, Aleppo, Dara’a, Hama, Homs, Der- er- Zor, Latakia and Al – Raka including in locations with increased trend and / or potential areas for refugee returnees. Of the total target for intervention, 83 % will be carried out in areas of severity scales of 4, 5 and 6 across 45 sub- districts while the remaining 17% will be undertaken in nine (9) subdistricts falling under severity scale of 2 (4%) and 3 (13%) respectively. Interventions in sub- districts below severity scale 3 will accommodate education needs of refugee and IDPs children returning to areas of places of origin where relative security and stability have been attained.
The rehabilitation activities will target light and moderately damaged classrooms and WASH facilities with gender sensitive and disability friendly approach to safeguard the protection of boys and girls and ensure adequate access to the school premises and WASH facilities. This project will build capacity of 3,000 teaching staff and education personnel on child – centered social counselling, non- violent communication, behavior modification, active learning and other life skills to redress psychosocial needs and / or other special needs of children in schools. UNHCR pays specific attention to the future adolescents and youth populations that are able to contribute to resilience and social cohesion in their communities as the result of provisions of education opportunities through access to schooling and thus empowering adolescent girls and boys to inspire and lead changes in their communities. In line to AGD, AAP and rights-based approach, UNHCR will reach out to children and their parents during project monitoring activities and through focus group discussion seeking feedback including receipt of complaints regarding the project. Through the project 112, 200 children (52% girls and 48% boys) of whom an estimated 2,587 (2.3% of project beneficiaries) are children with disability and 3000 teachers will directly benefit.
UNHCR financial requirement to undertake the education project is USD 8,105,727. Of the total cost USD 5,352,500 is needed to rehabilitate 65 schools and USD 825,000 for training of 3,000 teachers and other education personnel. Based on direct activity budget, the cost per beneficiary is rated at USD 55. At the writing of the project proposal USD 56,955 have been secured. In 2019, UNHCR rehabilitated 33 damaged schools and provided 2,314 teachers, social counselors, administrative and other education support staff a training on school-based child – centered psycho-social support programmes. 73,920 children are expected to have benefited from 33 rehabilitated schools and other 106,444 indirectly benefited from the training that teachers and other education personnel has received on psycho-social support.

4 - Needs

As per the HRP 2019 , 2.1 million children are reported to be out-of-school and 1.3 million children at risk of dropping out. Most of out of school children are at primary school age. Children who are out of school face increased protection risks including child marriage and the worst forms of child labour. The escalation of violence has led to increasing war related psychological stress for children, their families and education personnel. Exposure to violence decreased school attendance and led to school dropout affecting the children’s right to education. In some areas, over half of children with a disability have an unmet need for education. The proportion of conflict affected children and adolescents is of high concern. These children and adolescents remain to be in heightened protection risks and if their education needs are not addressed now, they will be negatively impacted in building up a prosperous future. During the eight years of crises over one in three schools were damaged, destroyed, no longer accessible or occupied for shelter and other purposes. Consequently, many children were forced to stay out of school or if managed to access schools, they were often exposed to unsafe and overcrowded classrooms. The destruction and damages of schools have led to an acute need for additional learning spaces for both host community children and internally displaced children residing in these areas. Teaching capacity remains overstretched with more than 150,000 teachers and education staff who have left their positions since the start of the crisis been absent, the capacity and capability of available teachers are severely over-stretched. These shortages have limited the quality and provision of education. Despite considerable investment by the Ministry of Education and Education Sector members to rebuilt destroyed and repair damaged school infrastructures and other efforts to restore the education system, access to safe education remains a challenge.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project aims to enhance safe, equitable and quality access to education to crisis affected and returnee children, in line with Education Sector Response Framework. UNHCR will rehabilitate 65 damaged schools in 45 most affected locations and other nine (9) locations with increased trend and / or potential areas for refugee returnees. The rehabilitation activities will include repairing of damaged classrooms and WASH facilities in a gender sensitive and disability friendly method to ensure protection needs of boys and girls as well as needs of children with disabilities and allow adequate access to the school premises and WASH facilities. Partners (PUI, ADRA, GOPA and SIF ) will implement the activities in close consultations with UNHCR engineer staff members, and will seek approval from the MoE prior to start of school rehabilitation project to ensure that prioritized areas of interventions are jointly identified, and needs assessed. UNHCR and partner engineering staff will play a key role in undertaking monitoring of the project activities in liaison with the MOE.Partners and communities will be sensitized on PSEA, to ensure that project implementation is conducted in dignified manners and in case of any concern, communities will be encouraged to report of any misconduct through the FCM channels that exist in community centers.AAP will be ensured through engaging communities through participatory assessment / FGD to understand education needs and interventions required. Following assessments and in consultation with other stakeholders including the MoE and DoE, partners will commence implementation of rehabilitation of schools.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Inaccessibility to education project sites due to lack of safety and insecurity including contamination with explosives and mines. Mitigation measure: Ensure flexible and context informed approach mechanisms to deliver project activities of school rehabilitation so that it could be implemented in a changing context and / or explosive cleared project sites. Ensure that relevant authorities are aware of and are engaged with the project and are committed to ensure basic safety/ access for project implementation and ensure that staff members, teachers and education personnel are trained in safety and security measures including in mine risk education. Risk: Insufficient safety and health standards at schools Mitigation measure:Planning and on going communication with local education authorities, community leaders and school management resulting in clear agreements on every actors' responsibility to create and maintain a safe learning environment; regular monitoring to verify adherence to these agreements.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk:Threat of military offensives and disturbance in safety and security of some project area or changes of security situation restricting implementation. Mitigation measure;Ensure that projects on school rehabilitation in insecure locations have security plans for their staff and the project beneficiaries have sufficient resources to consent to those plans.
