Project Summary

Adapting to major changes in Sudan socio-economic and security context, in 2024, ACF will focus on responding to the humanitarian need of conflict affected people, particularly IDPs and refugees, through multi-sector approach while scaling up its operation area in additional localities and cities within the four states to reach IDPs and refugee where they are largely resided. Adopting balanced approaches of prioritization and feasibility, with consideration of the current realities of access and operational capacity, ACF intend to scale-up Lifesaving and protection assistance through critical basic services and livelihood opportunities.
The overall project objective for 2024 is “To provide safe, dignified and gender-responsive life-saving humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable groups’’. The objective aims to tackle life-threatening conditions, address protection needs and provide livelihood opportunities. ACF will endeavor to ensure a more equitable, equal and participatory approach throughout the project cycle.
ACF will continue to operate in and expand to hard-to-reach areas as well as scale up to IDP and Refugee camps in Central Darfur, South Kordofan, White Nile and Blue Nile states. Expansion to Khartoum and Port Sudan is under review. The project will cover 19 Localities/cities, 75 Health facilities and their catchment area and Seven Hospitals (stabilization Center). The geographical targeting is based on the inter-sectorial highest severity of needs (especially nutrition and food insecurity) and ACF's access, operational capacity, multi-sector needs assessments, as well as limited to no presence of other major INGOs in those areas.
ACF’s 2024 programme will focus on four core sectors, by promoting synergies and complementarities, to ensure a holistic approach, Nutrition & Health Water Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH), Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL), and GBV (Protection). In Nutrition and Health, in line with HRP 2024 and both sectors’ objectives, ACF will scale up treatment and preventive nutrition interventions integrated with Primary Health Care (PHC). Main interventions include CMAM, Emergency IYCF, Micronutrient powder distribution, food-based prevention of malnutrition, prevention and treatment of communicable diseases (cholera, dengue. Etc.), Mental Health and Psycho-Social Support (MHPSS), outpatient consultation, reproductive health, vaccination, and health education. In WASH, aligned to cluster objective and SDG 6, ACF will maintain WASH assistance for vulnerable populations to ensure their access to safe water, dignified sanitation services and increased good hygiene practices through hygiene promotion sessions and Baby WASH. ACF will increase access to safe water and sanitation through rehabilitation and construction of water points and Latrines, water trucking, water treatment, distribution of WASH kits and waste disposal. In FSL, in line with HRP 2024 and sector’s objectives, ACF will focus on responding to the immediate food and livelihood needs of internally displaced people and protracted crisis victims through multi-purpose cash assistance, Agri-input distribution and income generating activities. ACF will mainstream gender and protection throughout all activities prioritizing safety and dignity of vulnerable groups, Do No Harm for all for an effective and impactful programming. Considering the added value of local partners in terms of access and acceptability, especially during active conflict, ACF will collaborate with grass root level Community Based Organizations (CBOs) and women led organization to implement activities.
The programme will give priority to the immediate need of conflict affected IDPs, refugees, returnees, nomadic and vulnerable host community members. Children, Adolescent Girls, and PLW will remain the main target group. Total estimated number of beneficiaries is Nutrition and Health: 392,765, NUT:58,314,WASH: 959,611, FSL: 46,923, and Protection-GBV: 39,485

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Throughout implementation of all activities outlined across outcomes 1-4, ACF will focus on bridging the access gap of girls and women to essential services and ensuring their participation through the programme cycle. ACF will sensitize people on GBV and protection risks at HH, community, health facility and school levels, as well as engage in partnership with relevant actors at federal and local levels. ACF will prioritize reinforcement, activation and / or establishment of referral pathways for enhancing support for GBV survivors, as well as supporting free psychosocial counselling (to be done by phone for reasons of safety and security of beneficiaries). ACF will support community leaders and other ‘influencers’ as champions to encourage community behavioural change to mitigating GBV. All ACF and partner staff signed policies on Safeguarding, Gender, Harassment, Child Protection, Code of Conduct, Charter of Principles. ACF will arrange for bi-annual review meeting with partners and field staff to evaluate performance of gender and protection mainstreaming activities, in addition to joint field visits from ACF Gender expert

The project collect and use community feedback to adapt or improve its activities. Feedbacks are collected through different channels and analyzed by MEAL team. Findings from feedbacks will be used to respond to community, donor and program team. Latrine and water points may be altered, service delivery area can be changed, packaging of kits can be changed, health worker behaviors can be modified.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Special consideration is taken an account to consider vulnerabilities and capacities of different people, groups and recognize gender and diversity. ACF uses gender-balanced field teams and culturally appropriate communication in all field work. Field teams work with local stakeholders to identify those populations that may be more invisible to traditional assessments to directly engage with them in assessing vulnerabilities and Programme relevance to these specific populations. ACF field staff engages with affected populations to clearly introduce them to Programme activities, expected impacts, and selection criteria. This engagement supports stakeholder trust and acceptance of Programme activities and provides a venue for stakeholder feedback on the relevance of Programme activities. ACF is accountable to its beneficiaries, meaning it will acknowledge and act on all feedback and complaints from communities, local authorities, beneficiaries, contractors, service providers, and all other stakeholders throughout Programme implementation, especially through amplifying its Feedback and Complaint Response Mechanism (FCRM). ACF’s Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) has established different context appropriate channels (hotline, email, comment boxes, direct interview with MEAL team) and incorporating some of the key questions in assessments, through which the community members, beneficiaries and stakeholders raise their concern, feedback, or complaint regarding the ACF services directly to the organizations. The hotline is managed during weekdays with specifically defined roles and responsibilities for all staff working within the organization. These channels provide a two-way dialogue with stakeholders, offer timely information, and provide a means for communities to have an input into the work of ACF. Stakeholders are also able to contact ACF directly through field officers on the ground and through a FCRM email account and hotline.
