Project Summary

The intervention aims to provide a wide-ranging support from emergency to regular food assistance as well as household and community based agricultural, livestock and livelihoods assistance. The project aims to target populations who are both foods insecure as well as at risk of food insecurity through multiple activities but mutually reinforcing activities to ensure that all four pillars of food security related to access, availability, utilization and stability of food are adequately addressed through lifesaving, life-sustaining, appropriate resilience, and early recovery interventions.
The project will improve the food security status of assessed food insecure people through distribution of food baskets, RTEs, cocked meals, and bread directly to the HHs, support self-reliance of affected households by protecting and building productive assets. It will also be restoring or creating income-generating opportunities through distributing agriculture inputs, support to small-scale food production, support to livestock asset building and protection, support livestock herders by feed distribution, animal treatment service. Moreover, the project will support income generating activities including vocational training and improve communities' capacity to sustain households’ livelihoods through the rehabilitation/ building of productive infrastructure and supporting services through rehabilitation of relevant economic/productive infrastructures. The total beneficiaries to be reached by the project is 253,802 over 12 months.

4 - Needs

Humanitarian needs in Syria are expected to remain high in 2020, based on HNO 2020, about 7.9 M people in Syria are food insecure and 1.9 M people are at risk of food insecurity. 70% of the assessed HH reported that 65% of their expenditure on food. More than 37% of food-insecure HH have outstanding debt and 57% of food-insecure people had adopted. Almost a decade into the crisis, the main drivers to food insecurity are conflict and displacement, loss or lack of livelihoods and reduced production capacity. These factors limited physical and financial access to food, soaring prices and inflation, contributing to reduced purchasing power and continuous livelihood depletion for the most vulnerable. The recent market trends indicate that the national average price of a standard reference food basket is USD 77 based on formal exchange rates is more than 8 times higher than pre-crisis levels. In December 2019, the reference food basket had increased by 17 per cent month on month and 43 per cent year on year while the Syrian pound had depreciated by 43 per cent on the informal market in December 2019 compared to December 2018. including floods, fires, hailstorms, hailstorms, high temperatures and plant diseases and pests specifically yellow rust.

5 - Implementation of activities

All the project design and activities will be finalized after close consultation with the beneficiaries' families with all age segregation male and female and local councils. The aim of this intervention is to improve the food security status of families and their youth girls and boys and engage them to effectively attempt to be self-reliant in the best possible way. The intervention plan anticipates that this will lead the families and their youth to think constructively and positively to work hard and be self-reliant in medium to long term. The intervention will target all population of IDPs, returnees and host communities and taking into consideration the most vulnerable people such as: the women headed families, elderly headed family and child-headed family. Moreover, the project will meet the food needs of lactating women, elderly people, girls and boys, people with disabilities and people with special health conditions. Following are the activities which will be carried out by the project: 1. Improve the food security status of assessedfood insecure people through life-saving and life-sustainingfood assistance by distribution food basket, RTEs, cocked meal, and bread directly to the HH. 2. Support self-reliance of affected households by protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income-generating opportunities to save and sustain lives through distributing agriculture inputs, support to small-scale food production, support to livestock asset building and protection, support livestock herders by feed distribution, animal treatment services and support income generating activities including vocational training. 3. Improve communities' capacity to sustain households’ livelihoods by improving linkages with value chain through the rehabilitation/ building of productive infrastructure as well as supporting services through Support rehabilitation of relevant economic/productive infrastructures through appropriate modalities.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

During the project implementation, the following protection risks may arise and actions to mitigate the risks are also outlined. 1. Risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment) of women and girls. Risk Mitigation: Establish a confidential complaint mechanism, train staff on a code of conduct and code of conduct in all staff contracts. Address any SEA complaints. Recruit femalestaff and train them. The field MEAL staff with monitor the situation by conducting Postmonitoring feedback. 2. Risk of violence from beneficiaries among themselves/withhumanitarian workers when huge onset occurring (crowd control). Risk Mitigation: Identification of suitable sites/ time. Set up several distribution spots on sites to reduce crowds. make joint distribution with another partner 3. Risk of Diversion of aid. Risk Mitigation: Coordination meetings with community leaders/gate keepers/local councils on targeting criteria. Sign MOUs with local authorities 4. Risk of Cash/voucher over spent on non-food items negatively impact food security outcomes. Risk Mitigation: Provide vouchers or e-card not cash, varythe payment days. MEAL staff will continue vendor monitoring systems (i.e. the selected shops). 5.Risk of targeting for IDPs versus Host communities . Risk Mitigation: From local beneficiary selection committees with equitable representation of both IDPs and host communities. Set clear guidanceto the Community Focal Points (CFPs) on the beneficiary selection process and criteria. 6. Risk of Access to land/assets to secondary rights holders e.g. female headed households. Risk Mitigation: Engage with local authorities to verify the status review appropriateness of the activities versus the dynamic pertaining land rights. 7. Risk of Diversion of assets for communities’ infrastructures. Risk Mitigation: Monitoring of Assets by CFPs. Keep close coordination and cooperation with local councils. Establish governance committee to mange the community’s assets

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The current security situation that tends to be stable in most areas inside Syria willfacilitate the intervention implementation to achieve the planned impacts. in addition tothe good understanding and cooperation from most of the local stakeholders, some risksbeyond QC control may affect the intervention implementation: 1. Risk of huge number ofIDPs may reach the targeted areas if the East Euphrates operation started, so the demandon the food will be increased suddenly. Mitigation measures: Keep close coordination with the local councils and active organizations in the targeted area in order to harmonize the intervention and advocate through FSL cluster to the donors to address the gap and big demand for the food. 2. Risk of Delayed of the deliveries due to closure of borders or close internal roads between targeted areas for unforeseen reasons. Mitigation measures: QC will keep close coordination with Turkish government since the humanitarian aids have exception, otherwise the procurement will be shifted to Syria. Close coordination with the local councils and main actors on the ground in order to facilitate the aids movements between the different areas and arrange warehouses in the different targeted areas. 3. Risk of New conflict may start in Idleb areas between Syrian regime and other army groups or between different army groups itself. Mitigation measures: QC safety and security team in Gaziantep and the fieldinside Syria will be continuously follow up and monitor the security situation in thearea of implementation. Safety and security plan will be developed and updated on daily basis according to the real situation on the ground in order to keep staff and beneficiaries safe. 4. Risk of Community tension arising from the beneficiary selection. Mitigation measures: Community-based beneficiary selection where all stockholders will be engaged in the developing selection criteria. The criteria will be published and disseminated to all beneficiaries
