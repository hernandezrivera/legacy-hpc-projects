Project Summary

Following the failure of the past two consecutive rainy seasons, in Somali Region, over 3.5 million people are facing water shortage, 131 out of 448 boreholes are non-functional (29%) & an estimated 3.675 million people are facing food shortage. A total of 1,123,111 livestock died in 77 woredas of the region due to the current drought. According to the zone government report, 47,725 HHz are internally displaced in 37 woredas due to drought and others also displaced triggered by internal conflict.
In March, the drought-affected schools are 432, out of this 137 are primary, & 226 ABE schools whereas 34 ABE and 35 primary Schools are partially closed in March 2022. A total of 130,830 students were affected by the drought. In March, 403 and 413 schools have no safe water and separate latrines respectively in 6 zones of the drought-affected areas. According to the regional health bureau report, a total of 23,467 SAM cases were reported between Jan to feb-2022 with monthly average of 11,736. On Feb-2022 total new SAM cases reported were 12,665 with the RR% of 97.9%. When compared both the previous month and last year of the same month the SAM admission trends increased 14.71% & 26.48% respectively.
Due to poor Livestock body condition the availability of livestock product (milk) is very low and the price of milk were very high. There is no livestock outbreak disease reported in Dawa and Libe zones. However, in Moyale woreda 15 cattle death due to black leg, sudden death of camels with unknown case and due to CCPP, CBPP & Diarrhea death of shoat and cattle are reported by the zone emergency task force on March 12, 2022. The shortage of water & pasture may further aggravate drought related livestock diseases such as Goat and Sheep diarrhea, CCPP, CBPP, pasteirolsis, Trypano and MCF.
The project is planning to implement in Dawa and Liben zones of Moyale and Filtu Woredas, which are among the zones highly affected by the drought. CISP is currently working emergency interventions in the woredas and this project is design to complement the existing and try to bring sustainable durable solutions to the affected communities.
To improve learning through equitable access to resilient & quality education, access to WASH & food security for emergency-affected children in Libe and Dawa zones the project is targeted 49,976 people (25,485 women & 24,491 men or 4463 IDPs & 45,111 Host communities) living in the targeted areas. The project is focused on three sectors, which constitute education 30% of the total budget, agriculture (34%) & WASH (36%) as per the regional priority.
The education activities emphasize reopening of closed schools/back to school campaigns and promoting equitable, safe, MHPSS, CSG and inclusive education to all in the targeted areas. The project reinstitutes the damage school classrooms, water facilities & latrines segregated by sex & CWD friendly. It also supports students through provision of scholastic materials, school feeding to needy children, adolescent girls’ sanitation materials and comprehensive intervention to protect students and teachers from COVID19 pandemic. Totally, the project will support 14731 students and 48 teachers.
In the agriculture sector, the project is planning to support the vaccination campaigns of the government against the prevailing livestock diseases in the area. The project also targets communities that lost their basic livelihood means, livestock by providing small ruminants & protecting their animal from the prevalence of diseases by supporting the local government to improve vet treatment services & vaccination campaigns. In the sector the project will address 35675 people directly.
In the WASH sector the project targets 53256 people affected by the drought through rehabilitation of water supply systems, provision of water treatment chemicals, conduct water trucking, provision of water tankers hygiene & sanitation promotion, local capacity building activities & durable solutions.

1. - Projects activities

WASH: To address the above mentioned situation, the project will rehabilitate total of 7 non-functional water sources (5 water source:1 borehole, 2 ponds, 2 underground water tanks/birka) in Mesajid, Biyoley, Dahyelo, Helebon & Kulay Kebeles & Filtu town, & 2 nonfunctional water sources (in Dhedhertu & Dhokisu Kebeles), specifically motorized and Solar power boreholes that can serve large number of people & use a source of water trucking. The main problems of the boreholes are damage of the electromechanical equipment (pumps & generators), damaged on the pipelines, distribution points & water reservoirs. The Birkas will be used for water trucking dropping points & will harvest appropriate volume of water during rain as durable solution. The ponds will serve to lessen the livestock water shortages after rehabilitation. • Provision of 16 water tankers to serve water collection/drop off point for water trucking, • Conduct water trucking to serve 31,375 people with 6 trucks for 60 days until the next rainy season starts & the damaged water supply systems maintained, • A total distribution of 2,050,000 sachets of water treatment chemical to 12,804HHs to serve for 120 days in both intervention areas • Conduct community based hygiene & sanitation promotion, including safe disposal of animal Carcass that died due to the drought. •Community and local government capacity building activities and Provision of sanitary materials like soap to schools, health institutions and poor families. Constructing of 28 blocks (224 seats) of semi-permanent communal latrines for 4480 IDPs & the host communities that has no access to latrines & defecate in the bushes, Provision of sanitary materials like soap to schools, health institutions & poor families in Filtu IDPs, Kualy site, • Community & local government capacity building activities.Education:The project will address at least 14,731 students including 1031 students with disabilities, and will train 48 teachers on MHPSS and SCG issues, passing lifesaving messages to all targeted schools. Equitable emergency response learning space services will be provided both the IDPs & the Host communities, • Community-based gender sensitive & inclusive enrolment campaigns involving PTSAs members, kebeles & religious elders, • Conduct emergency school feeding program, • Undertake back to school campaign involving model women, PTSAs, community representatives & school leaders by house to house visits & discuss with drop out children’s families & the students, • Provision of scholastic materials to 10133 children (at least half of them will be girls) of very vulnerable to students to continue their education, • Conduct school performance review meeting with stakeholders including teachers, PTSAs & community representatives participated, • Provide reusable sanitary pads & underwear to 1,334 adolescent girls • Provision of school furniture & rehabilitate school buildings that have been damaged by the previous conflicts & used as shelter by the IDPs during their arrivals. Construct semi-permanent/temporary learning space 3 IDPs settlements that will provide improved learning space for 672 students, • Cleaning & Sanitation Materials items were to be distributed to 56 schools of ECW intervention woredas. Food security/Livestock • Livestock restocking for those lost their livestock assets due to drought (8 shoats per HH) to 1,000 poor HHs, • Provision of concentrated Livestock feed for1800HHs, • Support the government animal Vaccination campaign through logistical & perdiem support to host communities & IDPs in vouchers system, • Supply of veterinary treatment drugs & equipment to the government clinics, • Training to the government health workers on disease surveillance & reporting, • Capacity building & refreshment trainings to community animal health workers including provision of of vet kits to CAHWs. IGA to 1200 poor HHs highly affected by the drought.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

The project will be implemented directly by CISP and Pastoralists Concern (PC) in close collaboration with community and Woreda and Zone Education, Livestock and Water, Energy and Mineral offices. The regional bureau will provide continuous coordination and monitoring throughout the project implementation period. In addition, Liben and Dawa Zones Water, Energy and Mineral Offices will mobilize its experts and service rig for the installation of the pumps and jointly facilitate training of community operators. The education and Livestock development bureaus will be appraised the project and also responsible to monitor and coordinate interventions implementing in the region. Similarly, the Women and (Children) Social Affairs will monitor the participation of women, children and people living with disabilities. It will also make sure that the systems are children, women, and disabilities friendly. CISP will be also responsible for direct implementation of the project, supply project inputs, provision of logistic and overall coordination. The organization will provide all staff necessary for the implementation of envisaged activities. CISP works in the area in partnership with local and International Humanitarian Organizations implementing emergency response projects and during the implementation will be applied the usual principles of the of coordination, complementarily, synergy and harmonization with other development actors operating in the area. Intervention kebeles of this project have been systematically selected in such a way that there will not be duplication of resources/efforts rather complementing with their interventions, for example, the rehabilitation of the water systems will shorten the distance of water source not only for this project but also other partners (Save the Children and the government) working water trucking. CISP and the government are working on nutrition project in the operational areas for host communities and IDPs. CISP will also complement with its own similar projects implementing in Dawa and Liben zones funded by EHF and Italian Cooperation, respectively for complementarity and liking the emergency with development. Hence, our intervention complements the already existing interventions, and no activities will overlap activities of other partners. Particularly, the project will integrate with nutrition projects supplying water to health centres. Basically, the major coordination mechanism in Ethiopia is the cluster system at different levels (Federal, Zone and Woreda). All the emergency interventions in the zone are coordinated by the zonal emergency task force led by zone administration while sector based clusters are coordinated by the zone respective sector offices (Water, education and livestock offices). CISP is an active member of the zone emergency taskforce as well as of the federal and regional WASH taskforces.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

The project will consider the Feedback and Compliance Mechanism (FCM) as accountability for the affected populations (AAP) and the donor to deliver the project objectives to the best satisfaction of end beneficiaries/clients through client response programming approach (CRP). The CRP will systematically listen to the perspectives of our clients and use their feedback to make decisions and communicate and explain to our clients how their feedback has informed our actions. The mechanism involves people at every stage and has been developed in a participatory way with representatives from all stakeholder groups including the most vulnerable and excluded. This was done through involving the community in the process of assessments, program design, monitoring and evaluation and then set up complaints and response mechanisms including listening exercises; perceptions studies; community score cards; story-telling, mobile phone calls or text messaging (SMS), SOP and others. In case that a project receives complaints or feedback that fall outside our remit, we will have a system in place to refer the feedback onwards to someone who is in a position to deal with the issue (known as referral pathways). The mechanism will handle sensitive and non-sensitive complaints with the proper procedures to handle both types. The mechanism for handling complaints will also take into account specific contexts and safety to recognize potential dangers and risks to all stakeholders involved in each process, including staff, complainants, witnesses and others. The mechanisms will include ways to prevent harassment, injury or harm to those wishing to raise an issue or complaint. The system will ensure the confidentiality of program participants when managing their complaints. This will assure that any information given is restricted to a limited number of people on a need-to-know basis and is not disseminated more widely. Feedback and complaints will be handled objectively and with fairness. All users will be made aware of the procedures; so that they understand its purpose, have sufficient information on how to access it and understand how it works. It will also be accessible for all groups within the community, including the most vulnerable and those with disabilities; this will be done through multiple channels to ensure everyone can be reached. Staff will be trained to ensure that standards are met in receiving, processing, reviewing, ensure everyone can be reached. Staff will be trained to ensure that standards are met in receiving, processing, reviewing, responding and resolving complaints. The system will be guided by policy and procedures that set out the key characteristics of the mechanism, such as who is responsible for receiving and handling complaints, how feedback will be recorded, reviewed and actions communicated, who has access to what information. Response time. The policy or guidelines will also clearly articulate, or signpost the process for managing sensitive complaints. The FCM will also be complemented by post-distribution monitoring (PDM) to measure the clients' feedback and satisfaction on items distributed by the project
