Project Summary

The primary education enrolment in Cameroon has seen a steady increase since 2018 -2023 from 4,197,486 to 5,155,547, a growth rate of 3.5%, (except in the North-West and South-West -NWSW regions which decreased during the same period). In 2024 access and completion rates continued to increase in Cameroon. More children continued to transition from primary to secondary schools, including in NW and SW regions with 42.0% and 67.9% respectively; more girls attended secondary schools, and more girls completed the first cycle of secondary education.
However, as Cameroon continues to be impacted by three humanitarian protracted crises and climate change, 1.3 million school-aged children will need assistance in education in 2025. These needs are exacerbated by structural challenges of the Education Sector: the insufficiency of trained teachers, insufficient classrooms, families who are unable to support their children’s education due to extreme poverty and WASH infrastructure in schools; including violence in schools and attacks on education (threats, kidnappings, maiming, killing etc).
The conflict in the Northwest and Southwest (NWSW) regions of Cameroon, ongoing since 2016, remains tense. Continued violence and targeted attacks on civilians have resulted in education being significantly impacted, with high figures of teachers and student kidnappings reported between 2020-2021 . In 2022, an estimated 462,000 school-aged children and youth in the NWSW regions needed humanitarian education assistance ., and a total of 1,033,000 school-aged children were impacted by this crisis . Conflict disproportionately impacts children, making them vulnerable to attacks while accessing essential services and education. In the NW region, 24% of children identified are at risk of exposure to school-targeted attacks. This not only increases exposure to protection risks but also results in damaged and abandoned school infrastructures and school closures due to fear of attending school. Furthermore, teachers are equally affected with an estimated 52% (of 22,230) reported absent during the current academic year . Out of school children are faced with protection risks such as child marriage, juvenile delinquency, and drug use. These out of school children are forced to do domestic chores or get absorbed in the informal sector. Adolescent girls become ‘cheap’ domestic servants or are sexually exploited.
The Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) findings round 10 conducted at the household level in August and September 2024 in the North-West and South-West Regions classify education as the second top priority needs of the affected population in 2024.
As a result of this challenging context, access to safe, quality, and inclusive education in the NWSW regions has been highlighted as an overarching priority by the Education Cluster Strategy 2023-20253. To succeed, both traditional education access and psychosocial support, protection from exploitation and abuse, and prioritization of the well-being of school-aged children is vital. This includes focusing not only on formal education but also identifying informal and non-formal education and training, as highlighted in the Continental Education Strategy for Africa . In 2018, Cameroon also became the 81st country in the world to join the Safe School Declaration , demonstrating its commitment to addressing the impact the existing context has on education access.
The proposed action aims to address education and protection needs in response to NWSW conflict displacement. It focuses on providing safe, inclusive, protective informal education for school-aged children and ensuring protection from violence, exploitation, abuse, neglect, and harmful practices.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

This 12 months response aims to ensure access to inclusive, quality education and learning for affected children and adolescents in a safe and protective learning environment, and to ensure that children and adolescents are protected from violence, exploitation, abuse, neglect, and harmful practices. For the eighth consecutive years after the onset of the crisis, children in the NWSW regions experienced school closures at the start of the school year due to lockdown calls by some NSAGs. From January to August 2024, 96 incidents against education have been reported (over 15 a year before on the same period) . Accessing education remains a major challenge in the two regions despite an increase in enrolment data. An estimated 865,029 school-aged children need humanitarian assistance, including 195,648 IDPs. The non-formal education opportunities fail to facilitate integration of children into formal education (in 2024, Cluster partners only reached 49,520 out-of-school crisis affected children). Data from the NWSW regional delegations of basic and secondary education for 2022/2023, revealed that a total of 4,195 schools are operational, out of 6,557 schools. An increase in the number of formal schools open confirms the gradual improvement in the security situation and the interest of communities in sending their children to school. At least one inclusive primary school has been opened in each division, (still only 2% of children living with a disability served). There is a lack of material, financial and technical resources available to school communities and education actors.This response aligns with the Cameroon Education Cluster (CEC) Strategy 2023-2025 for the NWSW regions and Plan International Cameroon’s country-specific priorities. The CEC seeks to enhance safe, protective, and inclusive learning environments by rehabilitating and constructing inclusive spaces for children aged 4 to 17. Trained facilitators, versed in psychosocial support, conflict/disaster risk reduction, and pedagogy, shall be used to assist in and out-of-school children in acquiring comprehensive education and life skills within a protective and safe setting. The learning approach will be inclusive and individualized, evidenced by providing personalized learning materials. Accessibility for persons with disabilities (PwDs) will be ensured during the construction/rehabilitation phase. All of these in alignment with the recently validated National Inclusive Education policy. Additionally, child-friendly feedback mechanisms will enhance the monitoring and evaluation of the learning environments.The proposed interventions will utilize a gender-transformative approach by enabling an environment for gender equality by addressing gender-specific needs through the provision of dignity kits to ensure menstruation does not hinder access to education, the provision of gender-based violence (GBV) risk mitigation, prevention, and response interventions, and the provision of safe and accessible channels to report sexual exploitation and abuse.
