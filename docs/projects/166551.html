Project Summary

The outbreak of COVID-19 in Zimbabwe came at a time when the nation is facing multiple calamities including food insecurity due to drought and hyper-inflation, collapse of health service in both rural and urban areas, displacements due flooding and evictions. The heavily informalized economy has resulted in many families surviving from hand to mouth, a situation which has exposed young women and men to adopt negative coping strategies such as selling drugs, exploiting children for forced begging, engaging children in transactional sex and irregular migration to neighboring countries in search for better prospects. The initial 21-days national lockdown with subsequent extensions announced by the President saw migration patterns shifting from the traditional rural to urban to urban to rural migration.
Trafficking and smuggling occurs before, during, and after crises. Traffickers and smugglers capitalize on the widespread human, material, social and economic losses and consequent vulnerabilities caused by emergencies. In Zimbabwe, a number of factors cause people in such circumstances to be even more vulnerable to trafficking and smuggling including the hampered ability of governments, families and communities to provide for their basic needs; limited options to seek domestic or international protection safely and regularly; negative coping mechanisms adopted by those affected by the crisis. For this reason, IOM proposes multi-sectoral COVID response, including remote support modalities, for children Victims of Trafficking and Smuggling in Zimbabwe.

Plan fields

1 - Activities

Key activities include:1. Support national coordination on advocacy for prevention of stigma towards Children Victims of Trafficking and Smuggling with suspicious symptoms or already infected by COVID;2. Build the capacity of health workers on psychological first aid adapted for pandemics, using previous models developed for outbreaks such as Ebola, to assist Children VoTs;3. Increase remote MHPS support through the existent anti-Trafficking in Persons (TIP) hotlines;4. Train Community volunteers on COVID19 infection prevention control and impact on Children VoTs;5. Adapt, Print and distribute COVID19 IPC and TIP IEC materials (SMS campaigns/Radio sensitization, including disability friendly materials)6. Train immigration officers and border management officials in COVID19 prevention/response and Smuggling Trafficking in Children (including remote training)
