Project Summary

The project will be focus on emergency IDP needs including cholera response and floods to ensure that IDPs, host community and most vulnerable people have access to basic WASH services: at least 15 litters/person/day of safe water; access to latrines, showers, and hand-washing facilities according to WASH cluster standards and that they are adapted to the needs of all, easily accessible and safe. NCA will raise awareness of key moments for hand washing, home water management and storage, and latrine maintenance and use. The WASH approach to address the key gaps in basic WASH services in Pulka and Dikwa is based on real needs, covering gaps as identified in the needs analysis - in relation to both accesses to water appropriate sanitation and hygiene. Working with the 3 components has a strong synergy effect, significantly increasing impact on community health and mortality rates from waterborne diseases. NCA will support the CTC with safe water supply and cleaning items as while as disinfection of the facilities in all locations of intervention training of washcom members in camps will help in addressing the issues surrounding environmental sanitation and proper refuse waste disposal.
Finally, NCA will contribute towards the HDP Nexus by combining voices with other humanitarian and development actors

1 - Needs

IDPs living in Dikwa remained inaccessible from humanitarian aid for almost 10 months in 2021 due to the insecurity in the area. Dikwa hosts 79,770 IDPs including 16,048 children under 5 years, mainly settled across 17 informal or planned sites (IOM DTM 41). The MSNA August-October 2021 report highlights the situation in terms of drinking water access, sanitation, and hygiene promotion in Dikwa. 54% of people are living with less than 3 liters/person/day under humanitarian sphere standard, it represents around 34,663 people. The problem, however, is not related to the amount of water available, but rather to the capacity of the existing water distribution, storage infrastructure and the strength of local water governance involving RUWASSA, SEMA and the communities themselves to provide permanent and continuous flow of water. For the sanitation, 57% of people don’t have access to safe functioning latrine. The problem in sanitation is related to latrine security access. In hygiene, 28% of the households do not have access to soap, and 97% are using a kettle as handwashing facility.
