Project Summary

Street Child will address the most pressing concerns and the critical barriers to accessing quality learning for 15,000 children affected by the conflict in NWSW Cameroon, focusing on out-of-school children who face increased risk of child recruitment, gender-based violence (GBV), child abuse, and child labour in NWSW, IDP children in the West and Littoral. Working with community-based agents, school authorities and communities, Street Child will also seek to improve the capacity of education stakeholders to ensure schools are safe, protective environments for all children.
In the NWSW, the focus will be on improving access to quality non-formal education by working with existing community-based learning spaces and play groups, supporting community volunteer teachers to meet the foundational education and social emotional learning needs of out-of-school children; identifying and training community-based agents to provide MHPSS within the learning spaces. In addition, in areas where schools are open, Street Child will seek to improve access to quality, protective education which meets the differing needs of conflict-affected and displaced children – in addition to pedagogical methods to meet differing learning levels and provide catch-up classes, teachers will be trained on TARL, the last mile learning approach developed by Street Child, PSS and child protection and school disaster risk reduction plans will be put in place to ensure schools are protective spaces. In the West and Littoral, Street Child will seek to improve access to education for displaced children, including unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), through a similar approach focusing on teacher training and the provision of catch-up classes where needed.
Given the scale of the education needs and identified limited technical capacity of local partners on EiE minimum standards and best practices, Street Child aims to build the capacity of local partners on innovative education in emergencies approaches – low-resource, scalable approaches which can meet the foundational learning needs of out-of-school and conflict-affected children in a protective manner. Street Child will work closely with local partners as well as with community stakeholders to build the capacity of those working in education in the region, contributing to longer-term systems strengthening even with the current access barriers and challenges facing education programming in NWSW.

1 - Besoins / Needs

As a result of the ongoing conflict (and particularly the targeting of education) and displacement, the education needs in the NW and SW regions are acute: only 34% of schools across the region are functional, and in 72% of communities children have no access to alternative learning options when schools are closed (MSNA 2021). Many children have had little to no access to education for over four years, leaving them with low learning levels and a need for support to be able to catch up and mainstream back into formal school. High numbers of children and families have been displaced to the West and Littoral regions because of the conflict, and according to a recent survey by Street Child 69% of the school aged IDP population does not attend school regularly. Across both the NWSW and West and Littoral, the experiences of children through conflict and displacement have led to psychosocial stress and increased child protection risks, but teachers and school management often do not have the capacity to identify or respond to these concerns. In most areas of the NWSW regions, formal schools are not open, and children have limited access to learning through community-run schools or ‘bush schools – a recent assessment by Street Child found that an estimated 45% of children across the NWSW are enrolled in some form of community learning. Here, the focus will be on improving access to quality non-formal education by working with existing community-based learning centres. Community teachers will be trained to provide basic literacy and numeracy through a low-cost, low-resource and play-based approaches (including teaching at the right level, and last mile learning approaches), targeting children who have been out of school and lack foundational learning competencies. Locally available teaching and learning materials will be distributed to support this. Teachers and community education committees (CEC) will also be trained on psychosocial support, child protection and positive pedagogies to ensure that the learning environment is safe and protective for all children. In urban areas where schools are open, many schools struggle to meet the differing learning needs of children who have been out of school, classrooms tend to be overcrowded and lacking appropriate learning materials, and teachers lack the capacity to meet the psychosocial needs of children who have been affected by the conflict. Street Child will work with teachers to improve their capacity to meet the different needs of children with different learning levels in overcrowded classrooms, and to be able to identify and refer children at-risk of child protection concerns. Teachers will also be trained to provide catch-up classes to children who have been out of school, to allow them to access the curriculum for their age/grade level. Teaching and learning materials will be distributed based on need, as well as WASH materials. Street Child will work with PTAs and school-based management committees to develop and implement school safety/disaster risk reduction plans, to make the school environments safer. In the West and Littoral, Street Child will target IDP children, including unaccompanied and separated children, and provide support to enable them to access quality education that meets their social emotional learning needs. This will include the provision of catch-up classes for children who have been out of school to allow them to mainstream properly, the training of teachers to be able to meet their needs in the classroom (including psychosocial support needs), and the provision of teaching and learning and WASH materials. In addition, Street Child will identify vulnerable caregivers of children who have dropped out or are at-risk of dropping out, and provide livelihoods support (using Street Child’s award-winning Family Business Scheme model) to improve their capacity to meet the costs associated with education.
