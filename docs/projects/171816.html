Project Summary

Despite legal protection, sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) –including domestic violence, rape, and sexual abuse –remains widespread throughout Somalia as a result of the continuing insecurity, weak rule of law, gender inequality and large-scale displacement, although reliable data on numbers and frequency are not available and abuses are likely to be under-reported. SGBV is particularly prevalent in camps for internally displaced persons (IDP) and in conflict zones. Women and girls who are displaced, from marginalized groups, or who live in areas under Al-Shabaab control are particularly vulnerable SGBV owing to a lack of or limited access to protection mechanisms and weak clan protection.
Even as refugees escape from the immediate dangers of armed conflict, the risk of SGBV remains. Refugees—and women and girls in particular—face increasing risks of violence, abuse and exploitation as their displacement becomes more protracted. Among other reasons, this is due to prolonged family separation, breakdown of community structures existing prior to the conflict, loss of financial and social assets, or overcrowded housing lacking privacy
Refugee men and boys are also at risk of SGBV, and persons with disabilities remain highly vulnerable and in need of specialized services. In addition, refugees who flee their countries after surviving SGBV incidents continue to require protection and services in the country of asylum, given the long-term consequences and risk of repeated violence
As a serious human rights problem further intensified by conflict and its ensuing displacement, SGBV requires all sectors to work in concert to strengthen prevention and response programmes. Mercy-USA will closely work through the protection cluster in making sure the needs of this people are meet.
The Gender Based Violence and Child Protection project is targeting 2500 vulnerable IDPs and host communities in Hiraan; to reduce suffering of women, girls, men and boys. Training of health care providers on Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) will be conducted to respond to reported cases of rape, construction of women center to enable women to get together and discuss issues affecting them and use the center as a place to conduct their IGA activities and get informal education on numerals, dignity kits will be distributed to 10% IDP population of the vulnerable women and girls of reproductive age, solar lamps will be bought and distributed to the identified women and young mothers where their security is deemed threatened at night, an Income Generating Activities (IGA) will be conducted to women, men, boys and girls on basic business skills to help enhance the coping mechanism.
The trained community on psychosocial support and rights of children will be there to support SGBV survivors and issues affecting children in the community through Community based approaches, therefore the trained frontline service providers will be able to respond and offer psychosocial support to Sexual Gender Based Violence (SGBV) survivors, unaccompanied and separated children will be gathered for by the community through identification of caregivers within the community in line with survivor centered approaches and using the existing referral pathways. Identified unaccompanied and separated children will be registered and tracing, follow up and reunification will be arranged through the support of the cluster and other actors. SGBV survivor will be supported through service accessibility and livelihood project. Gender Based Violence Information Management System will be used to inform actors on the different types of GBV occurring in the community and it will help in GBV program.

Project Activities

Improve timely access, availability and utilization of multi-sectoral services to vulnerable and conflict affected women, girls, men and boys to strengthen coping mechanisms and live a dignified life in Beledweine. The main activities include:• Distribution of dignity kits to be prepositioned and distributed to vulnerable women and girls in reproductive age (15 - 49 years) in Beledweine to uphold dignity in the community and to facilitate their access to social services. • Train medical staff on clinical management of rape (CMR) and survivor centered approach• Identify, rehabilitate and maintain 1 existing women centers to up scale the weekly group psychosocial activities (beads work and bed sheets flower decorations and coffee making) among GBV survivors and other vulnerable women and girls• Support to 1 GBV one stop centre• Procure, transport and distribute solar lamps to the most vulnerable women and girls with special needs to mitigate protection risks• Recruitment of staff in the field and training them on psychosocial support using the Minimum Standards of Child Protection and Gender Based Violence, signing of code of conduct and use of Do No Harm principle• Train frontline service providers (Case managers, police, community leaders, community support teams) on GBV, referral pathways, survivor centered approaches and psychosocial first aid. Frontline service providers will be training for effective response and support to survivors of GBV and enhance proper coordination in support to the survivor needs• Training of Case Managers who will respond to the needs of women and children Women with disabilities have been described as being doubly marginalized due to their disability and gender. They may be more vulnerable to poverty and social exclusion and often have limited social, political, and economic opportunities and lack of access to basic services. Women with disabilities may also be at greater risk of sexual and physical violence and abuse. Girls with disabilities often experience discrimination in access to education and family life.Mercy-USA will ensure GBV survivors with disabilities have equal access to services ranging from mental, psychosocial, counselling, and case management.Mercy-USA will ensure that people with disabilities participate in the implementation of project activities by ensuring that they are represented in all community meetings. The project will consult with PWDs about location, design, safety, and facilities' appropriateness. During project implementation, segregated focus group discussions will be held with the community to identify gender inequalities, discuss all the genders' needs, and understand the risks faced by PWDs in accessing nutrition services. Activities will target to address these needs during program implementation. Mercy-USA will also provide home visits as more appropriate for disabled female care-givers to access information about GBV and services available and consider the use of mobile phones as an important method of communicating about available services, but also for seeking assistance when a person with disabilities has experienced violence (i.e., through a hotline). During routine and regular program monitoring and evaluation, Mercy-USA staff will collect disaggregated data by disability, age and gender to monitor the impact of programs on vulnerable groups. Mercy-USA, during both monthly and quarterly reviews and analysis of data, will seek to monitor the inclusiveness of programming by comparing achievements versus available population data.

Project Indicators

The success of this project will be dependent on the reduction of the risks of SGBV faced by persons affected by conflict, number of cases effectively concluded, number of communities/individuals reached with information and knowledge. The main objective is to improve the dignity and wellbeing of persons at risk of SGBV including SGBV survivors through provision of Non-food items, capacity building of various stakeholders, case management, psychosocial support and strengthened community structures to be able to respond to SGBV cases.# of women, girls, men and boys reached with information: 30,000# of health workers trained on survivor centered approach: 50# of health workers mobilized to provide services: 50# of social workers trained on PSS: 60# of counseling sessions conducted: 100# of GBV one stop centers supported: 1# of GBV survivors provided with competent, companionate and confidential services: 100# of women receiving dignity kits: 2,500

Mitigation Measures

• Mercy-USA, guided by internal PSEA and Child Safeguarding Policy and international best practices, will safeguard gender justice and equity and promote gender equality.• Mercy-USA will carry out mapping exercises on a semi-annual basis to understand marginalized communities in the areas of operation and their needs. • Data collected for program implementation will be segregated in gender and disability, ensuring that the program reaches marginalized communities. • Mercy-USA will ensure that formal confidential feedback and complaints mechanisms are accessible to all community members, especially marginalized communities. • The project will engage women, girls, boys and men in all consultations to identify safety and security risks regarding the location and distance of health facilities. Emergency clinical treatment and referral services will be available to survivors of GBV. Awareness-raising for GBV prevention will be done as part of the sexual and reproductive health related information provision, involving women, girls, boys and men. The project will ensure accessibility to services for populations vulnerable to GBV, such as women, children and persons with disabilities. • Mercy-USA will have three forms of confidential feedback mechanisms to ensure that all community members are reached, including illiterate people, those who cannot physically travel, and finally, those who do not have resources to feedback. The follow mechanisms will be in place:• Suggestion boxes • Hotline numbers• Focal persons at the community level. • Community mobilization will be intensively carried out to ensure the uptake of the formal feedback mechanisms established.• Mercy-USA will continuously monitor and evaluate the effectiveness of the mechanisms established and make improvements continually.
