Project Summary

The overall objective of this project is Contributing to the clusters, objectives (CO2) of :Enhancing and sustaining emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19 (compulsory use of new SOP & guidelines);The overall objective of this project is Contributing to the FSL cluster, objective of integrated service (CO2) of :Enhancing and sustaining emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19 (compulsory use of new SOP & guidelines to HHs ); households at risk of food insecurity , WASH needs, and nutrition amongst 2000 HHs who are in Priority 1and (IPC severity 4) in Rumbek East county. As per cluster integrated service and the Humanitarian response plan of the allocation strategy. HAGI proposes to sustain an intergraded service of FSL, intervention. The intervention will provide trainings, distribute emergency livelihood kits (vegetable seeds, crops seeds, and fishing kits) to 2000 HHs that will receive both crops seeds , vegetable seeds through direct distribution and cash voucher schemes through conducting seed fair or cash for seeds, 2000 HHs will received fishing kits to enable fish catch increased and improved nutrition on among children's and women under the Highlighted IPC targeted areas).The training of 10% targeted beneficiaries will be conducted along site distribution. The project will save lives among the target group of Rumbek East county. The county is currently experiencing IPC Level 4 food insecurity, and the fishing kits will provide almost immediate yields in increased food production, and vegetable kits will support food production during the main season, which is a typical period for farmers to practice in this area. Both kits will greatly enhance food consumption and dietary diversity as well as boosting livelihoods for targeted population, and contribute significantly towards efforts to prevent malnutrition among children and breathe feeding mothers from the region. This project is designed to target areas which were missed out on the dry season from FAO distributions in Rumbek East , Cueicok , Malekagok payam, pacong, duel, Akot and Atiaba, there is currently a high number of IDPs in this county. we will target 50% IDPs and 50% host community households as beneficiaries. There is also a small number of IDPs living in Rumbek East county that were displaced from Amongpiny due to flooding and insecurity, who will also be targeted in this project. Please refer to beneficiary disaggregation showing the target beneficiary break down. In addition to distributing emergency food production kits, HAGI staff will provide a series of tailored and integrated trainings in vegetable cultivation, fish harvesting, covid-19 awareness and prevention , trainings as well as preservation methods, targeting 10% ( 200 HHs) beneficiary households. 200 people will participate in vegetable farming training, and 200 people will receive training in fishing, Community garden plots will be established in each of the 15 target bomas, in locations that are safe and accessible for affected populations, near established water points. Trainings will be delivered to both men and women across different age groups, and also integrated messages on nutrition and cooking. This project will be integrated in with HAGI' existing programs in the area including our current synergy of the dry season period response support through FAO project.

1 - Needs

As per the IPC release, HAGI is try to response to implement integrated FSL service inR.East county out of 72 counties which are reported to be in an extreme need (IPC level 4) and 5 of the recent release in 2020 who are in severe need (level 3+). Related to specific intersect oral indicators, such as access to a sufficient quantity and quality of water is one of the essentials humanitarian needs.Food insecurity: In South Sudan and the targeted counties in Lakes State, it is estimated that 7.7 million people are expected to experience acute food insecurity and worse (IPC 3, 4 and 5) in 2021 according to the latest IPC analysis published in December 2020. This includes 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers who are in need of FSL, WASH and Nutrition support in 2021, This call HAGI to target 2000 HHs with livelihoods, WASH and nutrition package including distribution of IEC materials for Covid-19. The number of people in need increased by 15% compared to the 6.7 million people who were acutely food insecure in 2020. The number of counties in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) is projected by the peak of the lean season (May to July) to increase to 45116 as compared to 28 million in 2019. The same comparison cannot be made for the 2020 lean season due to the delayed data collection. However, the projection for October to November 2020, usually a time when people harvest, 34 counties are in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) The states with the highest number of counties in Emergency (IPC Phase 4), projected at the peak of the 2021 lean season are five counties in Lakes, According to the IPC report , the counties of greatest food insecurity /severity are found in locations where the compounded shocks are exceptionally amplified due to intensified sub-national violence, two consecutive years of widespread flooding, indirect effects of COVID-19, and a protracted macro-economic crisis resulting in record high food price spikes. These multiple shocks have severely impacted people’s livelihoods and access to food from markets, livestock and farming. WASH: Living standard and wellbeing of an estimated 5.6 million people will be negatively impacted by lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices in 2021. In addition, 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers lack access to sufficient and quality WASH services. As access to water sources is predominately communal in South Sudan, The limited access to WASH services impact all population groups. Those in need are not restricted to one specific group. IDPs, non-displaced and host community members, returnees from internal displacement and refugee returnees are vulnerable with limited access to WASH services. The main drivers of WASH needs include limited physical infrastructure, sub-national violence and recurring flooding in many parts of the country. These shocks have led to the continued displacement of people and restricted their access to already limited and functional WASH infrastructure. Increased displacement also places an extra burden on existing WASH infrastructure in displacement sites and creates barriers for WASH partners when implementing activities. High WASH severity can be attributed to a reliance on surface water, a lack of sanitation facilities, and areas witnessing high insecurity. The quantity of water available per day is also below standards in many locations, with 17 % of people unable to collect enough water for drinking, and 39% of people reporting insufficient access to water for hand washing. Access to sanitation remained low, with only 17 % of households reporting owning a latrine in their compound, 4% have access to a shared latrine, and 2 % have access to a communal/institutional latrine. This means that 77 % of people do not have access to a latrine. In 34 counties, zero to 10 % of households reported using latrines. At the country level, open defecation is practiced by every rural HHs, Therefore HAGI planned to use (CLTs ) for community.

In the seed system, it was discovered that diversification of crops/new varieties of local crops seeds are integrated with the local farming community crops fields. Seed fairs will target people with disabilities who do not have access field crops or affected by flooding in 2020.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Delivering kits to beneficiaries in a timely manner assumes smooth logistical support and minimal disruptions from weather and insecurity/conflict along the roads to Rumbek East, Rumbek Centre -Cueibet roads and Wulu distributions sites. Distributions are scheduled for May , the beginning of the Main agricultural season, so seasonal access is assumed to be fine. There is some current insecurity along some roads in Rumbek Centre, but the roads are the ones leading to neighbouring Counties, not the roads we will need to distribute the kits. Additionally, HAGI is well respected across Lakes State and generally are not targets for car jackings, lootings, or compound intrusions. Furthermore, peacebuilding efforts by government, UNMISS and peace sector partners are ramping up in Rumbek Centre and neighbouring counties, with anti-violence campaigns to encourage people to hand in their weapons. Community leaders, government and potential peace actors in Rumbek will actively disengage youths from violence and engage them to participate in productive activities. We assume that when we give people the kits, they will use them appropriately. There is a risk that people could eat the seeds rather than plant them. However, with effective community mobilisation and sensitisation this risk is not high. Furthermore, HAGI has been doing seed distributions in Rumbek Centre and greator Yirol counties for several years so general community are aware of the purpose of the seed kits and have opened their minds to Main season farming culturally.Participation in training is at risk of disruption due to violence/conflict and environmental factors. We assume all invited participants will attend and complete the training and that the communities will remain peaceful and stable. In our experience delivering participatory and practical trainings, attendance rates are usually quite high, if conducted in accessible areas, and mobilization is done well. We assume that people will implement the skills they learnt at the training. In our experience, this is quite likely (ie an insignificant risk), it can be reduced with adequate sensitization. Additionally since people have been receiving this kind of assistance from us over the years, and we have done community awareness raising and sensitization, many are aware and have changed their mindsets. Another health risk is the Covid-19 pandemic; we assume that Covid-19 will not restrict the free movement of the target population during delivery of input to the farming communities.
