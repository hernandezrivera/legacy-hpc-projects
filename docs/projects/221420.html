Project Summary

The initiative seeks to provide assistance to vulnerable displacement-affected populations in the Far North region of Cameroon, through an integrated response, focussed on creating overall individual and communal wellbeing where people can exercise their rights, including support to existing Rapid Response Mechanisms (RRM) and Post-RRM. NRC will work towards durable solutions, where the co-existing contexts in Cameroon allow, and strengthen its local partnership programming and roll out its Safe and Inclusive Programming plan to improve NRC access and service provision in hard-to-reach areas. This will be achieved through the following intervention priorities;
Contribute to increase access to quality education for children affected by displacements in the Far North region of Cameroon
Improve access to adequate and reliable supply of portable water sanitation and hygiene facilities for affected populations in the Far North region.
Improve access to safer shelters and learning spaces for displacement-affected populations.
Contribute to meet the immediate food and longer-term livelihood needs of affected populations
Reduce Protection challenges related to the lack legal and civil documentation and HLP among affected population through information counselling and legal assistance.
Foster awareness and commitment to Cameroons Humanitarian response structured advocacy initiatives .

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

In the Far North region, after 10 years of protracted conflict, some 573,263 people remain displaced by violence and natural disasters. Insecurity caused by non-state armed groups and seasonal natural disasters continue to impact civilian lives, undermining protection and limiting access to basic social services. The main humanitarian needs include protection, education, food, shelter and non-food items and access to clean water, sanitation and hygiene.Relocation and conflict have exacerbated GBV trends in the region. Not only do women and girls have lower socioeconomical status, but they are also more susceptible to violence, especially gender-based violence (GBV), as women are often the ones who undertake household chores like fetching firewood when men and boys are not around due to restricted movement. This causes barriers in access to education and exposes the population to higher risks of restrictions of movement, limited ability to maintain livelihoods and income generating activities. Access to Housing, Land and Property (HLP) is another major challenge for most refugees and IDPs in the Far North region, with only 39% of IDPs and 40% of refugees having access to land. Of those, only 2% have documentation to prove ownership, leaving the majority exposed to good faith negotiations. As a result, most IDPs and refugees are living in IDP sites of which some spontaneous sites where they do not have authorization to stay. The above-mentioned coupled with recurrent inter-communal violence, have a negative impact on education, causing the closure of 26 primary schools (HNO 2023) in the Logone Birni subdivision, depriving 38,800 children, including 15,800 girls of access to education. The main barriers to education remain the lack of resources (69%) and the lack of schools (25%) causing overcrowded classes of 149 students according to the Ministry of Education. WASH and shelter services also remain critical in the Far North region. According to the NRC MSNA (June 2024) 41% of the respondents reported to not have access to proper latrines making open defecation a common practice due to barriers to latrine construction linked to a lack of resources, insecurity and access to land. Hand hygiene practices remain a major concern, as 63% of respondents could not identify three of the five critical handwashing moments and 69% did not have soap. Menstrual hygiene management remains a significant issue with many women (51%) reporting to use unsanitary materials, such as old cloths and 68% of women reporting difficulty in accessing facilities to change or dispose their menstrual items. Furthermore. 41% of the respondents are currently living in emergency shelters, while 25% are hosted or renting. Most respondents also reported issues related to inadequate space and lack of privacy and 80% of IDPs and refugees are living in houses needing critical repairs. 92% of the households NRC surveyed in the Far North also have indicated negative coping strategies to address their basic needs (CSI of 29.2) as 76% of the households report to be unable to meet their basic needs. Many of the households rely on farming and petty trading or temporary work for their livelihoods, but this is often insufficient to meet their needs, with 64% of the households indicating that they are in debt. Food insecurity remains a major concern as well in the Far North, with 96% of the population reporting difficulties accessing food due to constant price increases and financial constraints. NRC thus seeks to provide a holistic response to the identified needs, by delivering an integrated response that covers, the WASH, Shelter, protection, education, food security and livelihood needs of the most affected population. This will be assured with Cash, in-kind and service delivery assistance modalities, in strong coordination with affected populations.
