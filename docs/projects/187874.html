Project Summary

The project aims to improve the food security situation for the most vulnerable families in northwest Syria by providing live-saving humanitarian food assistance for 7000 families (42000individuals: 9009men, 9471women, 11540Boys, 11980girls) That will be achieved through two inventions
1-Distribution of cash for food for 1000 vulnerable families (6000 individuals: 1287 men, 1353 women, 1649 Boys, 1711 girls) in Idleb and Aleppo governorates while each family will be supported with monthly restricted e-vouchers (60 USD balance) to address their food needs for continuous 8 months.
The e-voucher modality provides beneficiaries with the freedom to choose from a variety of healthy food items.
BINAA will prepare a vendor map and provide the selected vendors with devices and training on how to use these devices. in addition to conducting orientation sessions on using e-voucher cards for the beneficiaries.
2-Free bread distribution (The essential food of the Syrian families) for 6000 vulnerable families (36000 individuals: 7722men, 8118 women, 9891Boys, 10269 girls) on a daily basis for continues 6 months in Idleb and Aleppo governorates while each person will receive 330 g of bread per day based on the FSL cluster recommendations BINAA will contract with the closest bakeries to the targeted areas and supporting them by the sufficient flour and the operational cost in order to produce the needed amounts of bread, then distribute it to the beneficiaries on a daily basis. BINAA will take the COVID-19 precautions in all project phases starting from beneficiary selection till the bread distribution according to BNAA COVID-19 Guidance
We are targeting both males and females in this project including all ages. We will also take the elderly and the people with disabilities through the project implementation while we will deliver their portions to their houses
Regarding 2023 we are planning to conduct the same activities according to the following details: 1-Distribution of cash for food for 500 vulnerable families (3002 individuals: 644men, 677women, 825Boys, 856girls) in Idleb and Aleppo governorates. 2-Free bread distribution for 3000 vulnerable families (18000 individuals: 3860men, 4059 women, 4946 Boys, 5135 girls) in Idleb and Aleppo governorates
Those activities are not secured yet.

4 - Needs

Based on HNO2022, Syria continues to suffer from multiple and complex socioeconomic difficulties, the protracted crisis, now heading into its 11th year, is exacerbating food security and livelihood needs for many families across the country, further eroding the resilience and recovery prospects. The overall estimated sector PiN for 2022 - of food insecurity plus those at risk of food insecurity - has increased from 14.2 million to 14.6 million, with 12.8 million facing acute food insecurity and 1.8 million at risk of sliding into food insecurity. Furthermore, 1.8 million people living in camps are deemed to be 100 percent food insecure, with an increase of 470,000 people facing acute food insecurity in 2022 compared to 2021.BINAA conducted a rapid need assessment. The assessment covered 51 camps in 6 sub-districts (Aghtarin, Ghandurah, AlBab, Suran, Azzaz, Jarablus). The main objectives of the assessment were to Identify needs, affected groups, and geographical areas and interventions requiring immediate assistance and develop an evidence-based strategy and implementation plan for improving the situation for the vulnerable people in these communities. A semi-structured questionnaire with Key Informants was designed to address the above objectives. The assessment results indicate an urgent need for food security interventions, especially support by bread and food baskets to families who cannot secure their daily need of food.

Activity. 3- Conducting awareness campaigns to prevent the spread of COVID-19. that will target the same beneficiaries of activities 1 and 2. Activity 4- monitoring and evaluation of the project activities.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

**The risks of GBV can happen because of overcrowding in distribution points, or may happen after distributing while returning home, which may lead to harm to some women and may cause domestic violence. - Likelihood: M. Impact: H. - Mitigation: -We will not distribute at a late time of the day. -We will not choose distribution points far from the residential complex, also we will distribute in each village separately. -During distribution, we will divide the location into two sides, one side for women and the other for men. -Ensure that complaint mechanisms for reporting sexual exploitation and abuse/GBV are accessible (WhatsApp, email, etc.) and that focal points for receiving complaints understand how to discharge their duties. **The risks of exploitation of beneficiaries by the field workers. -Likelihood: w. -Impact: H.- Mitigation: -Choose the field team carefully, who have high behavioral qualities. -Ensure that complaint mechanisms for reporting sexual exploitation and abuse/GBV are accessible (WhatsApp, email, etc.) and that focal points for receiving complaints understand how to discharge their duties. -In case any exploitation happens, BINAA will take appropriate action to protect persons from retaliation where allegations of SEA/GBV are reported involving our personnel (suspending staff till the investigation is conducted, referrin the victim to PSS, and even witness protection program if needed) and investigate allegations in a timely and professional manner.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: The risks of SEA can be compounded during project implementation Likelihood: L. Impact: H. Mitigation:1-Ensure effective systems are in place for preventing and responding to acts of sexual exploitation and abuse by integrating PSEA in BINAA policies and making sure that all personnel read and signed code of conduct, and that all contracts with partners/suppliers/contractors include clauses on PSEA or GBV.2-BINAA has a PSEA officer who is the PSEA focal point, and he will make appropriate recommendations to management on enhancing prevention strategies. This could include collecting and analyzing information on actual/potential risk factors for vulnerability to sexual exploitation and abuse and elaborating measures to address them. Make sure that PSEA materials and posters are visible and in place in all targeted locations. Receive complaints, reports, and questions about alleged acts of sexual exploitation and abuse committed by the organization’s personnel. And track all PSEA-related activities for the Organization and ensure that all information on PSEA-related activities is shared with relevant persons in the Organization


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1-Changes in the security situation or authorities/groups in control of project areas-Mitigation: -Regular monitoring for INSO security and incident reports. -Assess the access security records for each targeted location. -If it’s during the implementation, will look for other accessible locations with beneficiaries meeting the criteria. -BINAA has regular access to the proposed locations. Exploring different distribution modalities and plans for flexible deliveries in areas that are inaccessible2-Attacks may affect the vendor shops-Mitigation: -Implemented works will be reported daily by BINAA filed team -Temporary suspension in the targeted location and reassess the security situation. -Change the targeted communities/locations in coordination with clusters.3-Fraud in redeeming vouchers-Mitigation: -Using the E-voucher system will limit this risk, -Conduct daily monitoring through the dashboard and the control panel. -monitor the markets through the field team on a daily basis. -publishing the complaint posters and the complaint. -The MEAL team acts as a focal point.4-Bakery malfunctions:-Mitigation: The contract between BINA and the supplier will include a topic that the supplier will be committed to secure the daily planned amounts.5-COVID19-Mitigation: organizing the distribution to avoid gatherings consider social distance. Put awareness posters against COVID-19 and conduct awareness sessions.
