Project Summary

The project entitled “Supporting the livelihoods of affected families by the Syrian crisis in Idleb's countryside”. This intervention will be to support agricultural production which will help alleviate the scarcity of basic food commodities and reduce dependency from imports and food aid. winter vegetable and cumin seeds and other agricultural inputs will be given to the people crisis affected including internally displaced persons (IDPs) to cultivate them lands. Host communities will help in protecting and restoring the food security, employment, economic growth, natural resources and social cohesion based on the fact that recovery of agricultural production, diversification of income-generating activities and employment creation can begin to rebuild food security and livelihoods and contribute to stabilization. This intervention will strengthen the production of cumin crop and winter vegetable widely used in the food system of the Syrian people to address food insecurity while protecting and gradually restoring agricultural-based livelihoods and strengthening the capacity of people to manage and recover from shocks. The proposed action will contribute to strengthening of the resilience of the targeted crisis-affected people and their host communities. MASRRAT will target 250 beneficiaries each beneficiary will receive through the voucher system the agricultural inputs to cultivate 10 Donums of cumin. In addition, to 250 beneficiaries each beneficiary will receive through voucher system the agricultural inputs to cultivate 4 Donums winter vegetables.

Needs

The scale, severity and complexity of needs across Syria remain overwhelming. Conflict continues to be the main causative of humanitarian needs, with the civilian population in many parts of the country exposed to significant risks which threaten life, dignity and wellbeing on daily basis. Before the crisis in Syria, the famers situations were good in general, livelihoods were in average and the inputs were accessible. low crop production combined with import constraints, insecurity, and high transportation costs reduced the availability of food and pushed prices up. The affected crisis families are increasingly adopting negative coping strategies in the absence of sufficient means to survive. The food consumption patterns and frequency of meals have been reduced in addition to the quality of the food system has also significantly decreased. As more families adopt negative coping strategies and dependency on food aid, the risk of malnutrition increases as their ability to supplement the food aid with fresh food decreases. The most vulnerable groups are young children, pregnant and lactating women, women of child bearing age, elderly and chronically/acutely ill. MASRRAT team conducted many visits to some villages which located in Idleb's countryside (Idleb, Mhambal and Saraqab Sub-districts) and conducted needs assessment through many meetings with specialists in the agricultural sector and many stakeholders in addition to local councils’ members through focus group discussions. The visits results show the need to support the agricultural sector where these Sub-districts depend heavily on crops and vegetable cultivation. The people of these villages rely on the agricultural activities especially cash crop and vegetable cultivation as a basic source of income.

Implementation of activities

MASRRAT field team will Sign the agreements with the local councils in the targeted villages. The project will be advertised in the public places in the targeted villages, all potential beneficiaries in the village they will have details on the support and the conditions to apply for receiving assistance under the project. This process will be conducted in local council’s headquarters. The field staff will start registering the beneficiaries’ names. The registration process will be conducted in the local council’s headquarters which also included the Monitoring and Evaluation team in order to monitor the registration process. At the same time that the required agricultural needs are purchased, the designer at Gaziantep office will design and print the vouchers according to the required number of beneficiaries (250 beneficiaries for vegetables and 250 beneficiaries for Cumin) and send them to MASRRAT office in Idleb to be filled out with the beneficiary data and their personal identification numbers. Each voucher is used for the same beneficiary and for all distribution’s stages at the same time as empty boxes are left for the rest of materials to be delivered later. For each item there will be a box for the signature of the beneficiary, box for supplier stamp and also empty box for MASRRAT employee during distribution. The distribution process will be carried out twice for vegetables and twice for Cumin. During the distribution, MASRRAT team visits the beneficiaries in their homes, giving them the vouchers and informing each beneficiary about the distribution date, time and place. The supplier will secure a warehouse for the goods in each of the targeted villages. During the distribution, a representative of the supplier, one from the Local Council and another one for MASRRAT agricultural team will be present at the warehouse to supervise the distribution process. Public transportation vehicles will be renting (freight) and workers (loading and unloading) will be hired to transport and distribute agricultural inputs from the vendor to all beneficiaries in the targeted villages. MASRRAT team will carry out visits to each beneficiary’s field to verify the process of agriculture and to see the status of the land, crop and provide the necessary guidance. If the security situation is suitable, the distribution process will be in the specific warehouses in each of the targeted villages. Whereas if the security situation is bad, MASRRAT will deliver the inputs to the beneficiaries’ houses. Each beneficiary will sign a good receive note for the items and receive a complaint sheet include contact information numbers and emails to submit a complaint regarding the distribution and quality of the material and the team's attitude (if any). However, if the beneficiaries were not able to contact the management of the project the M&E Officer will send the complaint letter to the M&E Manager to communicate with the project manager to solve the problem as soon as possible. The distribution methodology will follow distributing the allocated support to beneficiaries on more than one distribution several times in order to avoid the threats of beneficiaries selling or not planting their rations.

Protection Risk Analysis

MASRRAT team will take into consideration all precautions, starting with obtaining approvals to move freely from the local councils on the ground, strictly observing security and safety regulations and not going out to work if the airstrikes are in the air. The following consideration will be taken into account with regard to protection- :Prioritization for safety, dignity, and ‘do no harm’ principles will be our main concerns in this program; for this project has been designed to prevent and mitigate any negative unintentional effects of intervention that might physically jeopardize people (like violence, offence, coercion, and environmental risks), or psychologically threat them (like disrespect, lack of privacy, and disengagement). This will be done through adopting voucher system instead of direct distribution -. Equal and unbiased access to assistance and services: Assistance and services will be provided basing on the needs with no discrimination. Enough quantities and types of agricultural inputs will be available and provided equally to beneficiaries with a consideration of the geography of the targeted locations. Priority will be given to the vulnerable segments. Type of assistance will be announced about in the advertisements at the local councils. Consequently, local councils will communicate with potential beneficiaries, and physically and financially facilitate their access to have the assistance easily and safely. MASRRAT will try to strike balance between the implementation of the program and safety and security of the employees who are our paramount priority in safety and security plan. MASRRAT team has been trained on safety and security procedures. Procedures and guidance have been adopted for implementing all other programs.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: - Money transfer while Sending the money to Syria - Procurement problems/challenges (trader’s correlations, procurement inputs availability, quality of materials…)- Price changes/ Mitigating- Sending the money to Syria using PTT centers only and using the Turkish Lira which will led to lose money due to the instable of exchange rate.- Sanction list check, market survey, proper and suitable technical specification, neutral evaluation and organized process for procurement- Procurement all the raw materials in the proper time
