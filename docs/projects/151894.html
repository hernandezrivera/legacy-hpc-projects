Project Summary

WFP will start the implementation of a complementary school meals programme as means to ensure children’s access to food in times of crisis and enhance diets through increased micronutrient intake. Assistance will take into account protection and safety, and where possible, contribute to their broader protection. During the initial stage, the school rations including nutritionally-fortified date bars, which are culturally-acceptable, programmatically-feasible and can contribute to enhanced micronutrient intake among children, will be provided to 20,000 school-aged children.
Introducing emergency school meals into a platform for nutrition-sensitive and gender-responsive programming that can support local food systems. This programme could also provide food incentives to promote and encourage the enrolment of the children of the migrant population who are living in urban areas.
The programme would review implementation arrangements to strengthen synergies with education and livelihood sectors and increase nutrition-sensitivity, gender-sensitivity, local procurement and community ownership. This would promote community infrastructure, rehabilitation, and reconstruction (including schools and health centres) as well as promote the reintegration of displaced populations and returnees, with special attention paid to the different needs of women and men.

Needs

According to the HNO 2019, 92,981 school-aged children are in need of education ﴾40,299 Libyans and 681 non‐Libyans﴿. Access to education remains one of the key priorities for returnees (the majority which are found in Benghazi (51%), Sirt (41%), and Ubari (8%) in order to establish a resumption of normalcy in children’s lives. Migrants and refugee children particularly from Sub-Saharan African face the most difficulty in accessing education. Latest data available regarding the out-of-school children is from 2014 and it says that 16% of children are out of school. It is not representing the reality of the whole country; but only accessible areas and it is not considering migrants and refugees.The Libyan Ministry of Education has highlighted the problem of the triple burden of malnutrition which school-aged children suffer from, i.e. undernutrition, micronutrients deficiency and overweight/obesity. During the implementation phase, quick baseline surveys and needs assessments will be conducted prior to the roll-out of the programmes.
