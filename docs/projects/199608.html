Project Summary

Northeast Nigeria is facing a landmine crisis as a result of the ongoing conflict between non-state armed groups and the Nigerian Army. From 1 January 2016 to August 2022, 1646 people were either injured or killed by landmines and other explosive remnants of war (ERW). Nigeria is among the countries with the highest casualty rates in the world. Most of the incidents are of mines of an improvised nature and ERWs resulting from numerous clashes between government forces and non-state armed groups. The threat posed by such explosive ordnance (EO) drives communities from their home and restricts the productive use of land. Further, expansive contamination of EO in vast areas of land has exacerbates the humanitarian crisis by denying or restricting access to assistance for the most vulnerable at-risk population.
This project aims to address EO contamination in Gowza and Monguno LGAs by tailoring an integrated approach to humanitarian mine action, by delivering safety messages and life-saving Risk Education to IDPs, returnees, and host communities, identifying EO victims, and collecting information on contamination through Non-Technical Survey (NTS) and Remote Contamination Baseline assessments (RCBA). MAG will build on its partnership with INGOs and a national NGO and explores other collaboration opportunities such as liaison with all relevant stakeholders within the area of operation in order to expand the impact, reach and sustainability of the project.

1 - Needs

Currently, the scale of the contamination and dangers posed by explosive ordnance (EO) in northeast Nigeria is unclear, as no official records are available. To comprehend the extent and nature of the contamination as well as its impact on the livelihood of local communities, MAG keeps record of the reported incidents and casualties of affected communities. From January 2016 up to 16 April 2022, a total of 1646 casualties were registered. This is nearly 6 casualties every week for the past 6 years. As such, MAG’s findings demonstrate an urgent need for raising awareness about the risks of EO, for increasing knowledge among communities on how to recognize EO, how to report these and how to share the information on the locations of the identified EO. Concurrently, MAG also shares the locations of identified EO with relevant stakeholders including Mine Action sub-sector, protection sector, UN agencies, other INGOs, and INSO upon request to have consolidated data for a comprehensive understanding of contamination. However, these figures do not represent the actual magnitude of EO incidents and casualties in the BAY states due to under-reporting, the lack of accessibility and accuracy in data collection. In fact, instability and high security risks have precluded mine action intervention and limited humanitarian assistance as well as data collection and validation efforts. The number of casualties from EO is expected to increase significantly as armed clashes continue, and former conflict-affected areas become accessible for IDPs to return home. In line with the Mine Action subsector priorities, MAG project is addressing the needs of IDPs, host communities and returnees, exposed to the threats of land contaminated with Explosive Ordnance (EO), which limits access to basic services and socio-economic activities, as well as movements of returnees. For 2022 the Sub-sector is targeting 407,150 individuals. MAG project will contribute to reach the target, through direct, as well as indirect engagement with the population at risk. Humanitarian workers are also highly exposed to the risks of EO unknown to them since most of contaminated areas are neither marked nor registered, and they have not had access to information to help them recognize and report EO when found. In fact, 99.94% of MAG’s beneficiaries have never received Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) before MAG’s sessions. This is particularly pronounced in areas recently brought back under government control, where there is expected to be a higher rate of defensive landmines and EO as a result of the fighting. MAG continues to receive requests for help via phone calls and text messages directly from local communities experiencing EO incidents, as well as requests from humanitarian organizations operating in different sectors to provide EORE to their staff, beneficiaries, as well as assessing the EO contamination in their project areas. Without a clear understanding of the risks posed by EO, the humanitarian intervention will be delayed or will pose unmitigated risks to staff delivering aid. To counter this, MAG will continue to deliver under this project EORE to UN agencies, INGOs, government officials, and stakeholders to help the humanitarian community ensure the safety of respective operations. Furthermore, the project aims to build the capacity of local responders (NGOs, CSOs, CBSs), in order to create a sustainable platform and mechanism for safety messages dissemination.

2 - Activities

The proposed project activities are based on three pillars: 1) Awareness raising, through Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) for IDPs, host communities, humanitarian workers and service providers; 2) contamination assessment, through non-technical surveys in accessible areas, and remote contamination baseline assessments (RCBA) in inaccessible areas, and 3) victims assistance, through landmine victims data collection and referral to the sector.EORE sessions will empower at-risk communities to protect themselves through adopting safe behavioural practices and to report any signs of EO contamination. NTS and RCBA will identify contaminated areas that have not been reported or registered. Newly identified contaminated areas will be shared with various stakeholders including NHF and will help the humanitarian community make informed decisions for planning and operation. In addition, the project will continue to collect data from primary, secondary and tertiary sources of data with respect to victims of EO to inform the protection sector in developing its operational priorities, and to contribute to the sub-sector efforts to establish a referral mechanism. In line with the Humanitarian Response Strategy (HRS), the target groups for this intervention will be IDPs, returnees, host community members, government officials, service providers and humanitarian workers who are exposed to the threats of EO. Under this project MAG aims to assist at-risk populations in the hard to reach areas of Gwoza and Monguno in Borno State. MAG believes in the added value of working with local organizations to deliver EORE, especially in a context such as Northeast Nigeria where access constraint remains to date one of the biggest challenge for INGOs. Since 2017 MAG had partnered with national NGOs to implement its project activities, and will continue to do it in the current project. Through these partnerships MAG has strengthened local responders’ capacity to deliver EORE, as well as their institutional management capacity. Further, MAG will continue to collaborate with humanitarian actors to expand and strengthen capacity of staff and organizations to better assist the vulnerable communities in the most appropriate way. MAG plans to continue delivering EORE in child and youth friendly spaces and to train volunteers for their further capacity enhancement and to ensure long-term retention of safety messaging to children. This will increase the access to at-risk groups and the number of child recipients of EORE.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

MAG’s gender policy provides clear directions ensuring that MAG’s activities are delivered based on need, recognizing the full range of interests and perspectives held by women, girls, boys, and men without discrimination. MAG acknowledges that contamination does not affect the different groups of population in the same way, as they have different responsibilities and roles in their communities and therefore different areas of activity as well as different ranges of movement outside of their home. MAG's gender mainstreaming includes ensuring gender-balanced recruitment, in particular of RE teams so that all beneficiaries can be reached. MAG disaggregates data by sex and age, which informs operational planning and prioritization. MAG's equal opportunities employment policy ensures appropriate working environments for women and men; MAG communicates with its stakeholders about MAG’s organizational understanding of gender in the context of its work. The principles of the “do no harm” approach mainstreamed in MAG policies, including on conflicts of interest, equal opportunities, data protection, personal conduct, gifts and hospitality, anti-bribery, gender, HIV AIDS, environment protection of children and vulnerable adults, and modern slavery. Specifically, for this project, MAG ensures that the community members receiving RE are brought together in safe locations. Based on previous experiences, in the target locations MAG will continue to collaborate with other protection organizations to expand and strengthen the capacity of staff and organizations to better assist the vulnerable in the most appropriate way. MAG will continue delivering EORE in child and youth-friendly spaces and train humanitarian staff to build their capacity and ensure long-term effect and retention of messaging to children. In exchange, MAG staff will be trained on Psychological Support First Aid to equip staff with knowledge and skills to engage with vulnerable children. Moreover, MAG staff will receive protection mainstreaming training to identify at-risk populations, such as widows, unaccompanied children, minor, GBV victims, and people with disabilities who need particular assistance among IDP communities when MAG engages with beneficiaries, and other activities and make referrals whenever necessary. The training will also allow MAG teams to conduct activities with a more sensitive approach when encountering EO and GBV victims.MAG ensures that teams only travel to accessible LGAs, as approved by the national military. MAG applies an inclusive approach towards operation, ensuring that target groups have equal access. Various sites will be targeted, such as schools, IDP camps, and host communities. MAG’s teams approach community focal points in order to build relationships as well as plan MAG’s RE sessions together. Community focal points include, among others: LGA Chairmen, Wards Heads, Civilian Joint Task Force members, Bulamas, and other local leaders, and civil society organizations. MAG will inform the community focal points about the upcoming EORE sessions a few days in advance of the sessions. This gives community focal points enough time to inform community members to be present on the day of MAG’s sessions. This way of working generates trust between communities and MAG and ensures equal and impartial accessMAG recruits staff from local communities as much as possible to ensure acceptance. By recruiting from all ethnic groups, MAG offers equal opportunities of employment and encourages people to work together. MAG also hires, as far as possible, equal numbers of women and men, as well as people from different religious groups. This not only ensures MAG’s commitment to equal opportunities of employment, but also allows MAG to effectively implement programmes in areas where religion and gender issues can be sensitive and to ensure the safety of beneficiaries.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

MAG has a well-established feedback and complaints mechanism, by which community members and beneficiaries can raise any concerns directly to CLTs through phone calls or texts, or camp managers. Feedback and complaints from beneficiaries are encouraged by MAG, which are recorded and analyzed by the team to ensure the effectiveness of activities and the protection of beneficiaries with sensitive complaints dealt with immediately.
