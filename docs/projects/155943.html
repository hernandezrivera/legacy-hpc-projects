Project Summary

This project aims to target a variety of direct and indirect beneficiaries across all North East Syria.
First of all, refugees and IDPs will be mainly targeted in Al Hol camp. Displaced people are to be considered as the most vulnerable of all, and ensuring continued access to health care is crucial for their wellbeing, with special regard to children, women and elderly.
Women and girls will be specifically targeted through the reproductive health programs that UPP is already implementing: all the health facilities included in this project proposal (PHCs in Al Hol and Shaddadi), are equipped with delivery rooms, while specialized staff such as gynecologists and midwives are available to provide assistance to patients in need 24/7.
Children will be assisted too, as pediatricians will be also available in the clinics for consultations.
Emergency and trauma cases, including people wounded by mines, Improvised Explosive Device (IEDs) and other remnants of war, will also receive medical assistance: emergency rooms for trauma stabilization are attached to each PHC, while ambulances are available 24/7 to refer patients to better equipped health facilities in Hassakeh.
People with disabilities (PWDs) will be targeted too, mainly through the distribution of assistive devices both project locations, besides the regular consultations provided by the doctors and the medications dispensed at the pharmacies. Their families should be considered as indirect beneficiaries, as they will also be relieved by this kind of specific care.
Where possible, people with chronic diseases will also be assisted. In Al Hol camp, a broad spectrum program for the distribution of medications for such diseases has been already put in place, with the support of the Committee of Health in Hassakeh. A similar assistance will be provided in other project locations as well, at least for the most common illnesses, such as diabetes, hypertension, etc..
People with complex medical conditions will also be taken care of, and referred to better equipped facilities for secondary and tertiary care, in order to receive more specialized services, such as c-sections, surgery, x-ray, etc.; if required, they will be evacuated to Damascus or Iraq.
Women, men, children, and elderly will also be targeted by health and hygiene promotion campaigns in both camps and urban settings, as well by other awareness raising-related activities: teams made up of community health workers and outreach volunteers will be available in each project location in order to sensitize beneficiaries on a range of important topics, including mine risk, fire hazards, insect bites, etc., mainly through face-to-face interaction with patients, outreach visits and thematic sessions. Camp residents and local communities will benefit from this crucial activity directly and indirectly, especially in terms of public health.
Although mental health and Sexual Gender Based Violence (SGBV) are not specifically part of this project, patients and survivors will also benefit from a basic kind of assistance, as UPP and are already coordinating with other NGOs and service providers in order to refer such cases to psychiatrists and case management actors.
Finally, the whole population in NES will benefit from the activities proposed in this project, as they are intended, more widely, to strengthen the local health care system, both through the continuous support to facilities (i.e. medical equipment, drugs, renovations, etc.), as well as through capacity building and transfer of expertise (i.e. application of standardized clinical protocols, supply chain management, etc.).

Needs

Al Hol CampAl Hol is currently the biggest camps in Hassakeh Governorate with a population of over 30,000 individuals. Around 1/3 of residents in Al Hol are Iraqis who fled Deir Ez Zor during the military operation that started in August 2017. The camp population in Al Hol has increased significantly starting from December 2018, mainly due to the arrival of many IDPs fleeing from military operation in Hajin area. These may return quite soon to newly retaken areas. According to the Camp Administration most of the Iraqis residing in the camp are intending to stay because the areas where they come from are not stabilized yet and because living conditions back home are worse than the camp. On the other hand, displaced families are still arriving to Al Hol on a regular basis, both from Iraq and DEZ area. Need for basic health services, including 24/7 emergency health care, pediatric and maternal service, is very high and it has increased with the cold weather and the new arrivals. UPP and its sub-implementing partner are currently covering over 80% of Al Hol health, as well as leading the case management working group, which was established in order to deal with special and urgent medical cases. ShaddadiAl-Shaddadi is a town located in southern Hassakeh governorate, in North East Syria. The whole district currently has a population of approximately 100.000 individuals, including residents living in the rural villages (about 40 to 50), and is hosting also displaced families from Deir Ez Zor and Raqqa. In July 2018, with the support of the French Ministry of Foreign Affairs – AHF and in agreement with the Health Committee in Hassakeh, UPP and its partner started to support a pre-existing clinic by providing additional staff, medical supplies and equipment, activity monitoring and capacity building.As of the end of October 2018, the Primary Health Care centre is providing an average of 600 consultations per week across the three specialties (internal, gynecology and pediatrics). An Emergency Room is operating 24/7 and two ambulances are available for referrals to other secondary health facilities in Hassakeh. A laboratory for clinical tests has also been set up in late October 2018

Implementation of activities

PHCCs in Al Hol camp (Phase 1 and 4); Implementation period: 1st Jan – 31st DecemberPHCC in Shaddadi; Implementation period: 1st Jan – 31st December The following services will be provided in the 2 PHCCs in Al Hol: - (2) PHCCs with doctors (gynecologist, pediatrician, internal), nurses and midwifes - (2) emergency and nursing care rooms (24/7);- (4) ambulances for emergency referrals to secondary health facilities in Hassakeh (24/7); - (1) laboratory for clinical tests;- (1) maternity department for reproductive health programs and deliveries;- (2) pharmacies to provide medications upon medical prescription;- (13) Community Health Workers (CHWs) for Health and Hygiene Promotion activities (face to face and orientation group), with particular attention to women and children;- (10) Health Outreach Volunteers recruited in the camp and embedded in the Health Promotion team, to support in organizing awareness raising campaigns. Most of the staff in the camp were already trained between 2016 and 2018 by UPP, WHO and other partners to also provide the following services:- identification of PWDs and distribution of assistive devices/referrals to center for physical rehabilitation in Hassakeh;- Psychological First Aid (PFA) assistance in case of incidents and/or traumas;- community mobilization;- identification and referral of SGBV survivor cases and Child Protection cases;- contact tracing in case of epidemics such as TBC or poliomyelitis;- breastfeeding counselling; - identification of malnutrition cases and referrals. The following services will be provided in the 1 PHCC in Shaddadi: - (1) PHCC with doctors (gynecologist, pediatrician, internal), nurses and midwifes - (1) emergency and nursing care room (24/7);- (1) ambulance for emergency referrals to secondary health facilities in Hassakeh (24/7); - (1) laboratory for clinical tests;- (1) maternity department for reproductive health programs and deliveries;- (1) pharmacy to provide medications upon medical prescription;- (4) Community Health Workers (CHWs) for Health and Hygiene Promotion activities (face to face and orientation group), with particular attention to women and children.In terms of coordination with other partners in Al Hol, UPP and its partner will continue to attend all the general and sectorial meetings organized on a regular basis in the camp, in order to promptly address issues, harmonize actions and avoid duplication. UPP and its partner are already coordinating with WHO on referrals to secondary and tertiary healthcare facilities in Hassakeh: 100 surgery operations were successfully performed from October to December 2018. UPP and the partner also started to cooperate with UNICEF for the referral of malnutrition cases to Al Hikma Hospital: as of the 31st of December, 10 cases were referred for treatment. UPP will also continue to organize Case Management meetings on a weekly basis: UPP’s Programme Assistant usually chairs such meetings, during which medical cases in need of special treatment or evacuation (to Damascus or elsewhere) are discussed among the relevant health actors in order to possibly resolve them.It is worth noting that UPP and its partner are also coordinating with the other relevant actors (including UN agencies) for the Deir-Ez-Zor response, and providing medical assistance to the new arrivals in the reception area of the camp. Occasionally, WHO and UPP’s partner are conducting mass campaigns in the camp (i.e. on hygiene, typhoid, etc.), and field staff has also been trained on EWARN reporting.As for Shaddadi, no coordination mechanism was in place when UPP took over the clinic, given that a very few other INGOs were working in the area, in non-health related sectors. Regardless, UPP and its partner tried their best to harmonize actions with other partners and a coordination mechanism was established with Humanity and Inclusion for the referrals of patients in need of physiotherapy.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The assumption behind the Action is that a stable situation allows the clinics to be operative 24h/day. The emergency system has been built based on this primary assumption and proved to be effective in ensuring health services in NES. However, operational capacity of health facilities and safe spaces depend not only on the NES Forum sufficient level of information sharing / coordination capacity, but also on the general geopolitical situation. The main risks are the following:- Escalation of violence between SDF and GoT and new displacement waves (high risk);- Start of violence between GoS and SDF (medium risk);- Withdrawal of US/Coalition Forces may lead to destabilization in NES (high risk);- IS is not defeated and takes back some areas or infiltrates carrying out attacks (medium risk);- Use of chemical weapons (medium risk);- Lack of access and difficulties to provide services and supplies; difficulty to monitor the activities in case of remote management (medium risk);- Cross-border INGOs relocate staff further west and therefore are more limited in their capacity to respond while active hostilities are ongoing (medium risk);- Hospitals and project staff become a target in the conflict (medium risk);- Demining operations are stopped in Raqqa and DEZ (medium risk).Mitigation strategy consists in setting up or keeping extremely flexible the response and activating mobile units in order to move them according to emerging war scenarios. Equipment and staff may be deployed in other locations within few days (Raqqa city, Raqqa Governorate, Aleppo Governorate, Hassakeh Governorate, Deir-ez-Zor Governorate, other IDPs and refugees camps in NES) according to needs and if the scenario changes. Particular attention will be given to the commodity disposal, especially the ones included in the cold chain.Local partner is able to shift its support to facilities where is needed or use mobile response to follow population needs. In case of a serious deterioration of the situation in NES and of complete lack of accessibility for international staff, depending on the context, UPP will choose between a mixed strategy of direct and remote management, maintaining its field office with national/local staff under the supervision of senior management responsible for the Action from Dohuk office in KRI. In case UPP is obliged to close its Syria mission, the Action will be remotely managed by UPP from Dohuk office in KRI, and the activities will be carried out by the implementing partner. UPP local staff in Amuda currently is 60 people; all of them are included in ongoing capacity building. Among them, the most experienced will be assigned to different leading roles, to ensure that project activities will continue to be implemented in case that remote management is necessary. As for the partner, the capacity building action has been initiated two years ago and most staff on the field is currently able of implementing activities autonomously.
