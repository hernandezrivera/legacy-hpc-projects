Project Summary

Lebanon is facing a multi-layered crisis due to economic mismanagement, structural vulnerabilities, poor infrastructure, a weak public sector and deteriorating social services, as well as the COVID-19 pandemic and the 2020 Beirut blast. This has led to civil unrest, high poverty rates and limited functionality of public services, and has driven household (HH) vulnerability. Humanitarian actors need up to date information to guide their programming. Unemployment rates have skyrocketed and living conditions have deteriorated. The Kafala System in Lebanon further exacerbates vulnerabilities and opens the door to employer abuse. This suggested project will take a holistic approach to meet the needs of critically vulnerable female migrants, often survivors of abuse and trafficking.
Under this project, Tabitha will support a total of 4,410 women with case management, but also with food, non-food items, psychosocial support, referrals to services such as legal assistance and voluntary repatriation, and most importantly the feeling of security and dignity in a situation where so many women are left without any social safety net. The legal support and the voluntary repatriation will be done with the partner MSD. Tabitha also runs a safe shelter, migrants' women survivors of GBV or at risk can also benefit from this service.
Tabitha will prioritize cases of trafficking and/or GBV in a shelter with 15 beds. Women will be able to reside in the shelter for up to 6 months before finding a durable solution, including the possibility of voluntary repatriation to their home country. Women will be referred to the shelter from partner organizations, consulates and embassies, or through Tabitha's outreach network in Aley, Batroun, and Achrafieh through its community centers; Tabitha will utilize its reputation as a trusted protection-based organization with community outreach workers to ensure that women are aware of the service and able to get support. While this project is focused on the locations where Tabitha has its community centers, Tabitha is open to referrals from across the totality of Lebanon and can also reach women through its mobile protection units in the Bekaa Valley to accept more referrals.
Moreover, Tabitha's staff is well known with its protection expertise and will be able to deliver case management and related services such as food and NFI so that the women are able to secure their basic needs.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

This project falls in line with the migrant sector objective, "Migrants have improved living and working conditions through an improved protection environment". The project will achieve this objective by providing multi-layered services, including protection services, for women seeking support at the 3 different community centers run by Tabitha in Aley, Achrafieh, and Batroun but also the women staying at the Tabitha's shelter. The services provided will include the following :- case management: trained social workers will be in charge to identify cases in need of follow-up whether they fall under child protection, GBV or persons with specific needs category. Identified cases will be provided with case management, after the needs assessment conducted with the person of concern, an action plan will be designed jointly with him/her. - Individual counseling: will be provided on a daily basis for migrants cases who approach Tabitha's centers and did not need case management. The social counseling will allow them to get the needed information and get referred to other services providers if needed. - psychosocial support: individual or through group- provision with food, NFI and multi-purpose cash assistance.- awareness: GBV, child protection, PSEA For the legal component, Tabitha will collaborate with its partner MSD who will be able to offer this service, in addition to repatriation to their home country through insuring a return ticket.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Tabitha mainstreams protection in all its activities which allows maximization of the protective impact of the project through ensuring that the activities target the most vulnerable and enhance safety, dignity, and promote and protect the human rights of beneficiaries without contributing to or perpetuating discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect and exploitation. Social cohesion activities are designed to bring together individuals of different backgrounds. Activities create a space for discussion and peer mentoring which allows different groups to reflect on their similarities rather than differences. Project activities are designed to include any critically vulnerable individual within the population, regardless of their nationality, gender identity, disability, race, religion, or socioeconomic background. Staff will be sensitized to gender & diversity and should recognize that vulnerabilities differ from one person to another.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Tabitha creates opportunities for beneficiary involvement throughout the program cycle. This project’s activities have been designed based upon the feedback given by beneficiaries and stakeholder engagement, where possible. Tabitha has a robust two-way feedback mechanism with multiple channels like complaint boxes, email address, hotline, and SMS, and engages with stakeholders such as consulates and embassies of concerned vulnerable migrants. Within this project, Tabitha will have a complaint & feedback mechanism located inside each community center but also inside the shelter with weekly meetings to ensure issues are addressed as quickly as possible. For those who do not speak Arabic, French, or English, Tabitha will locate community volunteers who can communicate with them to ensure the opinions of all residing in the shelter are heard.
