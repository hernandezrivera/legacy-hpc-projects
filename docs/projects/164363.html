Project Summary

The purpose of the proposed intervention is to respond to continuing health needs in al-Hasakah governorate, challenged with protection concerns. Viyan Organization will provide an integrated package of services spanning primary health including reproductive & mental health and psychosocial support, community health and incorporating appropriate GBV prevention and response activities. Services provided under the proposed intervention will address specific needs of women, men, girls, and boys, including older persons and persons with disabilities. All phases of the intervention will rely on close coordination and consultation with affected populations, as well as key stakeholders, including national and sub-national clusters, Directorate of Health, and relevant line ministries. The target beneficiaries will mainly be IDPs, returnees and host populations in areas of priority that continue to experience inadequate basic services, overwhelming existing resources and are yet to experience relatively stable security.

4 - Needs

Against the background of the continuing humanitarian crisis since 2011, an estimated of 11.7 million people are in need of various forms of humanitarian assistance (2019_HNO). Health Actors will therefore continue to focus on immediate life-saving emergency measures through providing quality essential health care services especially for IDPs Camps and area of returnees, this includes full package of primary health care services with more focus on mental health and psychosocial support services in line with WHO global initiatives to integrate MHPSS into the level of PHC package. The mental health sector is currently struggling to keep up with the needs of IDPs, returnees and host communities, where after conducting health need assessment in al-Hasakah governorate, the MHPSS services are severely inadequate with lack of well-trained clinical psychologists and non-specialized mental health (MH) care providers and ineffective referral system & pathways for MH cases (Health_Sector_Bulleiten_Jan_2020). Health Infrastructure in al-Hasakah city is under considerable strain where many health facilities are being exhausted and running out of the required resources (Attacks_on_Health_Care_2019) with increased morbidities, mortalities and disabilities among affected and vulnerable population, likelihood of a rise in the communicable diseases. Which strengthening the capacity of public PHCCs to respond to the current overwhelmed emergency is in top priorities (Severity degree 4, 2020 HNO Key Message 1)

5 - Implementation of activities

A1: Provision of quality primary healthcare including RH & MHPSS services in al-Hasakah governorate and through supporting two public PHCCs and one MMU. Assistance will be through the supply of essential medicines, equipment and medical supplies, incentives to PHC staff, minor rehabilitation works, purchasing of essential furniture and ensuring a stable power and water supply. The MMU will be equipped to serve as rapid response team to provide emergency and timely health services to locations where the populations seek immediate assistance. VOMRD will ensure that PHCCs & MMU are staffed (8 Doctors, 6 M-Nurses, 6 F-Nurses, 4 Midwives, 6 APHs, 2 PHs, 4 laboratory technicians, 4 Psychologists, 24 CHWs, 2 Data Entries, 4 Service Staff and 2 Guards) A2: Contribute to the improvement of health services through capacity building and supportive supervision of health personnel and outreach workers (at the MMU and PHCCs) through a two-folded approach. Health personnel will be trained on BLS, management of CDs & NCDs, EWARS, SRH topics, MHPSS topics, hygiene promotion and CMR. The trainings and related service package will be focused and tailored to the priority conditions identified in the areas of intervention. The proposed activities will be delivered through well-established coordination and reporting mechanisms included but not limited to, regular attendance of NES HWG meetings, MHPSS Sub-working group & health authorities’ meetings, reporting on 4Ws, AI, HeRAMS and EWARN. The project will be monitored and evaluated by MEAL Manager / Erbil and MEAL Officer NES whom will ensure data collection forms are designed and completed through internal reporting system consisted of monthly log-frame reports, periodic feedback reports of program status using proper quantitative & qualitative tools.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Ineffective external referral system to secondary facilities (Contract a Focal Point, approved by the LHA, in order to establish a legal operational framework for external referrals; Constantly inform the LHA on admissions to / rejections from the specialized facilities, in order to provide the LHA with the evidence from the field) 2. Shortage of staff to be contracted (involve LHA and HWG in promoting the vacancies) 3. Insecurity in most target areas potentially poses a risk to women and girls and thus may not be able to access and utilize services (undertake proper mechanism of feedback of beneficiaries through focus-group discussions with women and girls as well as men and boys - initiated community based feedback mechanism which works through (suggestion boxes & hotlines) of which beneficiaries can access - participation within the protection network and GBV sub-WG, HWG will helps Viyan Organization to identified undeserved areas) 4. Other vulnerable groups such as PWD’s and non- ambulatory older beneficiaries may not be accessed (Interventions offered are provided through two different modalities – static as well as mobile. While static services provided a wider scope of services, the mobile modality brings basic health care and facilitate referral - Community outreach network which is responsible to provide house-to-house and community awareness activities allows for beneficiaries unable to reach static and mobile services to be identified and proper referral be facilitated) 5. Deterioration of security conditions in the areas where the project operates (Arrange provision of larger quantities of medical supply in order to overcome protracted interruption of services – Constantly monitor the security situation through our security focal point as well as relative local networks and UN sources - All project locations are equipped with first aid kits) 6. PSEA: Viyan Org will enhance awareness of PSEA policy including accountability measures to the staff

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

RISKS 1. Authority shifting impacting service delivery 2. Duplication or mis-coordination in activities 3. Limited availability of medicines, delayed deliveries at boarder points 4. Fraud and aid deviation 5. Accountability to affected populations. ASSUMPTIONS: 1. The current authorities remain stable and supportive 2. Coordination network among partners is good 3. Local market is stable 4. Antifraud policy in place and being implemented 5. Local community members are well involved in the project design and implementation. MITIGATION: 1. Improve coordination with WoS HC and NES HWG to identified safer locations where the high needs found 2. improve networking with WoS HC, NES HWG and LHA to avoid duplications 3. conduct medical quality assessment both in KRI and NES 4. Actively participate in the UN & Health Cluster coordination meetings and strengthen policies on antifraud and misappropriation of humanitarian aid-staff are sanitized and sign code of conduct 5. conduct key informant interviews with IDP, host community populations, project staff and LHA officials about the project (all gender & age) in addition to conduct patient satisfaction surveys in the target locations, Viyan Org will hire local staff providers for service delivery, which allows for a better understanding of the local operational context.
