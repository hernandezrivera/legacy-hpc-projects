Project Summary

This project aims to ensure that vulnerable Lebanese and migrant children can access quality education in the 2021/22 school year. SCI will build on its extensive expertise in the education sector and presence in local communities to reach the most vulnerable families and support them with crucial information and support, both through awareness sessions and referrals, as well as by financially supporting them (cash transfer) to address barriers to education such as transportation and other educational costs. SCI will aim to reach the most vulnerable children, including those with disabilities, at risk of child labour or other CP concerns such as GBV. There will also be a specific focus on supporting remote modalities and providing guidance to parents, where SCI will use its previous experience in remote learning to provide the necessary support in creating a conducive and safe learning environment at home. Under objective 1 of the ERP, SCI will aim to reach 14,000 individuals. In addition, as part of objective 2 to help address the increase of children moving from private to public schools, SCI aims to directly support 10 schools, 200 teachers and 8,000 students, through material support, as well as a contribution to schools’ operational costs and teachers’ salaries. SCI will follow the sector set criteria for selection of schools/students

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

S01-Activity 1: Assessment of Out of School Lebanese and migrant children-those who have not been in any form of learning for more than a year and those who have recently dropped out. This will aim to determine where these children are, their soci-demographic characteristics and what the key barriers for learning are. S01 - Activity 2: Outreach activities shall disseminate messaging on how crucial education is for children’s learning and well-being and critical information that can support parents and caregivers in enrolling their children to either formal or non-formal education. S01 - Activity 4: Cash transfer will be given to a total of 1,500 vulnerable households with OOSC, children who are at risk of dropping out and children who are prone to risky coping mechanisms.S01 - Activity 5: In the event that schools will not be able to reopen due to the surge of Covid 19 or shortage of electricity and fuel supply, remote or blended learning will be deployed and approaches will be a combination of online and paper-based solutions. S02 - Activity 1: learners in identified schools will be supported with necessary learning materials for classroom teaching and learningS02 - Activity 3: This activity will support approximately 10 schools in hosting additional numbers of children by covering part of their operational costs, and thus ensuring that a total of 5,000 students can learn in safe and inclusive learning environments.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

At the start of all projects SC undertakes a risk assessment, including a focus on ensuring the protection and safeguarding of children and adults. All staff are trained on PSEA and safeguarding and sign up to SC’s code of conduct & safeguarding policies, with regular refreshers on reporting procedures. SC is committed to mainstreaming diversity and inclusion across all its projects and programs. Gender Equality Markers are completed for all project and use of GBV Minimum Actions at the design stage with a close monitoring and on-the-job coaching at the field level. SC works towards creating gender-sensitive and inclusive programming with strong focus on women and girls, including through an outreach approach to look for the most vulnerable groups, providing constant coaching/training and technical support on gender and disability inclusion to SC staff, etc. In addition to that, SC has a mandate to promote diversity and inclusion at its own work place prior to doing so on the ground.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Accountability to affected populations is central to SC’s work and ensured through feedback and reporting mechanisms, regular engagement and communication with stakeholders including children. SC has a well-defined set of procedures and reporting protocols from the community/learning centers level up to the response and feedback levels. Focus group discussions are held with adults and children to ensure full understanding of the project, as well as to hear any feedback. In particular, for this project, communications to households will ensure clarity in the purpose and application of criteria for material support and cash assistance. Field teams will always distribute hotline number cards during any distributions. In addition, and as relevant, SC will help the sector in developing transparent criteria for the selection of schools in need of support to ensure that the most vulnerable learners are reached.
