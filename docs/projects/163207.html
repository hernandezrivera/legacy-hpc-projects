Project Summary

Since the crisis escalation in late 2017 the North-West and South-West regions have experienced a drastic deterioration of living conditions, putting children and women at high risk of various forms of abuses, exploitation and violence. Access to essential services has been severely curtailed due to insecurity, large-scale displacement, destruction or closure of health facilities and schools, withdrawal of implementing partners, and relocation of health, education, and social workers.
Girls and boys are the primary victims of the current anglophone crisis. This translates into serious violations of their fundamental rights and constant insecurity, causing both visible and invisible trauma in the short and long-term, separation from their families and limited access to basic social services such as education, for the past three years. Being direct or indirect witnesses of violence linked to the conflict, their wellbeing, both physical and psychological, is fundamentally at stake.
An estimated 279,545 children are currently displaced in the North-West and South-West region (Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA), September 2019). This is in addition to the estimated 120,113 children from the NWSW displaced in the neighbouring Littoral and West regions (October 2019 MultiSector Rapid Assessment (MIRA), OCHA).
In order to protect children from violence, abuse and exploitation, as a priority and main intervention, UNICEF will work on the offer of services and provide psychosocial support to 79,431 children including 40,510 girls affected by the conflict through Psychosocial Support Units, youth clubs and Psychosocial First Aid in urban, peri-urban and hard to reach areas. Psychosocial support will be provided to caregivers as well to the extent possible and when necessary so that they are better equipped to protect their children.
As a direct corollary, an estimated 1,630 unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) are foreseen to be identified during the implementation of the project with an estimated number of 831 girls. All the 1,630 children will benefit from alternative care arrangements, support and individual follow-up while due to the difficult access and insecurity UNICEF plans to reunify a minimum of 163 unaccompanied or separated children with their family.
UNICEF will complement its support to children activities, work on the demand side with awareness raising and prevention activities at community-level. Concretely, UNICEF with its implementing partners will run sensitization campaigns with the aim to reach out to 158,863 persons with specific attention to parents to pass on messages on child protection in emergencies, trauma early warning signs for children, effects of family separation, GBV and other types of violence children are at risk of at the community level so that parents and community members can play an active role in child protection in emergencies.
UNICEF and its partners will also provide support to an estimated 2,418 conflict-children who do not have a birth certificate, as they are particularly vulnerable to various forms of discrimination and abuse because of the lack of birth certificates.
UNICEF as lead of the Child Protection Area of Responsibility will continue to lead the child protection in emergency sub-cluster in Buea with an antenna in Bamenda, in order to consolidate the coordination and ensure alignment and complementarity of interventions in the field of child protection.

Besoins / Needs

Children are the primary victims of the current crisis. This translates into serious violations of their fundamental rights and constant insecurity, causing both visible and invisible trauma in the short and long-term, separation from their families and limited access to basic social services such as education, for the past three years. Young boys, and girls to some extent, are reportedly joining the ranks of NSAG. Adolescent boys are also more likely to face arbitrary arrests, forced recruitment and extrajudicial executions and are more at risk of injury or death directly related to the conflict. Lastly, many boys and girls do not have birth certificates, impacting directly any administrative procedure and making them more at risk of exploitation and arbitrary arrests and detention, especially for boys aged 14 years and older, as well as exposing them to human trafficking. Many children are separated from their parent(s) and are being taken care of by other members of their community or left alone in child-headed households, especially in urban/peri-urban areas, including in Littoral, West and Adamaoua. Family Tracing and Reunification for unaccompanied and separated children has been hampered by the prevailing unstable environment. Many families, being less stable, are adopting negative coping mechanisms such as survival sex, early marriage and sending children to do menial jobs. Population displacements directly affects the regions of the SW and the NW, but also affects the regions of West, Littoral and to a lesser extent, Adamaoua, where people are looking for security, economic opportunities and access to education. The needs of these children are similar to those who remained in NW and SW, with particular emphasis on family separation, early pregnancies and sexual exploitation of children, as well as child labour.
