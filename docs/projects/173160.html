Project Summary

In coordination with the CPSS and relevant sectors, the Project will prevent and respond to abuse, exploitation, violence (including GBV) and neglect of children in targeted communities, adopting a socio-ecological approach that recognizes the roles of children, families and communities in the protective environment and builds on their inherent strengths. The Project will contribute to systems-strengthening that ensures children have access to a continuum of care that bridges the humanitarian-development nexus.
The project will reach 160,864 boys (48%) and girls (52%) including CwD (10%), adolescents, and unaccompanied and separated children with high quality services. The project will leverage significant programming experience in Cox’s Bazar to provide PSS, life skills-based learning, pre-vocational training (such as ‘greening’ initiatives), social cohesion and resilience, and recreation activities. The Covid19 pandemic will see community outreach mobilizers, volunteers and community groups will playing a crucial role. The support to adolescents will recognize their capacities as agents of positive change. 108,873 men (48%) and women (52%) will benefit from positive parenting support, protection awareness and social mobilization through CBCPCs. Overall, 75% of beneficiaries will be from camps and 25% from host communities. Interventions will follow a survivor centered, Do No Harm approach that minimizes stigma. AAP, complaints and feedback mechanisms will be embedded in all activities.
Services will be delivered primarily through Multipurpose Centers and Social Hubs. Through an integrated approach, the Project will prioritize case management with trained personnel linking and facilitating children’s referral to specialized services, the quantitative and qualitative scale-up of CBCPCs, and bolstering protection at the household level will be bolstered through guidance to parents and caregivers. PSEA will be afforded the highest consideration.
The Project will build the capacities of government; strengthening the social workforce, improving information management through roll-out of CPIMS+, and accelerating birth registration in through upgraded systems and trained personnel.

1 - Identified Needs

Analysis of the Population in Need (PIN) in Cox’s Bazar shows that 799,184 vulnerable children and adults in Rohingya refugee camps (79.2%) and in the host communities (20.8%) will require humanitarian assistance in 2021. The protection of girls and boys under the age of 18 in both the camps and host communities remains of serious concern, with disproportionate risks to women and girls, further exacerbated by the Covid-19 pandemic. Violence against children is a significant issue with findings from the Child Protection Sub-Sector Assessment Report (May 2020) showing that many parents and caregiver are especially concerned about the safety of their children. Primary concerns include physical violence (53%), child marriage (47%), kidnapping and/or trafficking (77%), exploitative work and child labour (49%), with girls and adolescents being most at risks. Caregivers are also worried about the health of children (91%), sexual harassment (46%), the effects of natural hazards (46%) and wildlife attacks (21%). Findings show that adolescent boys and girls have similar concerns. Moreover, records from Department of Social Services (DSS) shows high numbers of unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), with over 4,500 requiring alternative care arrangements. The mental health and psychosocial wellbeing of children and their families remains a major area of concern. The refugee community continues to live in profound stress in very cramped living conditions, limited access to basic services, and a significant dependence on humanitarian aid. Parents and caregivers require guidance on positive parenting and coping skills both at the household level and through community-based mechanisms that may have been weakened during the COVID-19 pandemic. Adolescent boys and girls face unique challenges requiring special protection measures especially with the limited access to formal education and livelihood opportunities exacerbated by Covid-19. Adolescents also report being anxious about redundancy, lack of engagement and participation in decision-making, and the ongoing threats of child marriage, drugs and the possibility of further refugee movements in search of better opportunities. Child Protection systems in Cox’s Bazar are fragile. The influx of refugees has put a strain on social services especially for the host communities. DSS requires additional human resources with the professional competencies to ensure quality social work coverage that, through case management, links and facilitates the access of individuals to specialised services. The Birth and Death Registration Information System (BRIS), that was suspended immediately following the influx of refugees, is now updated and requires concerted input to ensure its expansion across all upazila in the district. Non-custodial systems, including family and community-based care for children in the justice system requires significant improvement to bring it in line with the Children’s Act. Strengthening coordination, improving the capacities of police, probation officers and social workers to meet the needs of children is critical. This is especially important considering that Cox’s Bazar district is also vulnerable to natural hazards (monsoon, cyclones and landslides) which regularly impact safety, livelihoods and infrastructure.

2 - Activities or outputs

OUTPUT 1- Community-level child protection mechanisms (CLCPMs) including the CPCs, volunteers, parents’ groups and local authorities are engaged, have enhanced capacities and take lead in the protection of children including identification and referrals of children at risks in a coordinated and multi-sectorial approach. OUTPUT 2 - Sub-national child protection systems- formal and informal including the police, prison, judiciaries, probation and social welfare are coordinated, technically and logistically supported and have adequate human resources and technical capacities to prevent and deliver quality child protection services including but not limited to birth registrations, justice for children and social welfare services in a social cohesive way OUTPUT 3- Improved psychosocial wellbeing of boys and girls in refugee camps and in host communities including strengthened resilience, positive coping, emotional wellbeing, parental attachment and conducive environment for positive development. OUTPUT 4- Enhanced parents, caregivers and community capacities to apply positive parenting competencies at homes and in the communities in a gender sensitive and violence free supportive nurturing environment that enhance child wellbeing. OUTPUT 5- Adolescent boys and girls are empowered, act as agents of social change and have access to appropriate social services including but not limited to life skills, peacebuilding, technical skills development; and resilience activities in an integrated and multi-sectoral approach. OUTPUT 6 - Enhanced Coordination, Protection Mainstreaming and Accountability to Affected Population UNICEF will provide leadership and maintain strong coordination with the various coordination forums including with the Child Protection Sub-Sector (CPSS), Case Management Technical Working Group (CMTWG), Strategic Advisory Board of CPSS (SAG), Anti-Trafficking Task Force (ATTF), Protection Working Group (PWG) and with other ad-hoc forums such as the Age and Diversity Task Teams. UNICEF will also actively participate at the bi-weekly Camp in Charge meetings and the Child Protection Camp Focal Persons meeting hosted by the CPSS. COVID-19 & Disaster Risks Reduction (DRR): As Cox Bazar is regularly impacted negatively by weather vagaries including winter coldness, UNICEF will support winterization targeting the most vulnerable children, encourage children to support afforestation by planting trees around schools, MPCs and Children will be sensitized about climate change. COVID-19 measures will be ensured in all facilities including ensuring social distancing, provision of hand-sanitisers, COVID-19 awareness Participation & Accountability to Affected Population (APP): UNICEF will ensure complaint and feedback mechanisms are established in all Social Hubs and at all Multi-purpose Centers (MPCs) to ensure the voice of children and families are heard and that their feedback utilised to improve programming. Children with disabilities (CWD), adolescent and parents will be consulted during assessments and their voices will be used to improved programming ensuring no-one is left behind. All children and adolescents will also be involved in planning for activities at MPCs and at Social Hubs. Exit and sustainability Plans: in 2021, UNICEF will rationalised partnership with greater reliance on national Civil Society Organisations (CSOs) while limiting partnership with international agencies. The partnership with local organizations and with DSS will ensure local ownership. The involvement of local refugee structures, CPCs, Adolescent Clubs, Youth Clubs also ensures local ownership and promotes sustainability
