Project Summary

This program aims at increasing the resilience and livelihood opportunities of the most vulnerable olive farmers and thus revitalization of olive value chain in Idleb, Harim, Salqin and Afrin sub-districts, where various olive farming are the major pillar of providing employment and livelihood opportunities to majority of the populations. 2000 small and mid-sized olive farmers who already own 3-10 dunum planted with olive trees at fruiting stage. Olive farmers will be prioritized based on specific eligibility and vulnerability criteria to be supported with agricultural inputs, agricultural services, harvesting, packaging, and marketing services sufficient to 3 dunums of their olive orchards. To increase the technical capacity and knowledge on the best agricultural practices and integrated olive management, the target olive farmers will receive practical training (Farmer Field School training).
Furthermore, this program aims at creating short-term employment opportunities of most vulnerable women households (1000 women, “500 women of the host community and 500 women from IDPs”) through CfW intervention for the harvesting of olive orchards owned by the target olive farmers. To increase the technical capacity for strategic and sustainable planning for the olive sector in the target location, Ihsan RD will support each local council (one member of agricultural department of each LC in each targeted community) through the provision of ToT on strategic planning for olive sector, sustainable and friendly environmental agricultural interventions and respecting environmental principles during all production cycle of olive value chain.
Note: The budget for 2022 is secured, while the budget for 2023 is planned (unsecured)

4 - Needs

Generally, agriculture sector in the targeted locations had severely impacted by the ongoing crisis in Syria. High cost and limited availability of agriculture inputs, agricultural services, degradation of livelihoods and income-earning opportunities, COVID-19 and other climate-induced shocks such as floods and erratic rainfall have all contributed to the worsening situation in targeted locations (HNO report 2021, the draft HNO report 2022 ). The Key Informant Interview (KIIs) conducted by Ihsan RD field team with olive farmers, local traders in olive sector, community agricultural experts of both Males and Females in the proposed location indicated that approximately 80-85 % of the population was dependent on olive cultivation and olive value chain as main source for income generation and job opportunities (including cultivation, harvesting, agricultural services, milling, marketing, packaging …etc.).Olive farming and olive value chain had been negatively affected by the current crisis. As olive activities have not been conducted thus far, thousands of hectares of land in the targeted areas are not being used, thereby affected the agricultural environment, soil as well as health of the trees. Several factors had accumulated together to cause the negative impacts on olive value chain such as high prices of agricultural inputs and agricultural services (fertilizers, pesticides, tillage, pruning, harvesting, labor, marketing, etc.).

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risks that may be occurred during implementation of this program: Protection risk (1): Child labor (involving children in CFW or farming activities). Mitigating measures: Ihsan RD will be sure that the staff working in this project are trained and fully aware of child safeguarding and child protection Protection risk (2): Lack/difficulty of accessibility for PWDs for the proposed activities. Mitigating measures: Availability of wheelchair for PWD at the reception point, trained staff to deal with PWDs, priority for the PWDs, included in the criteria, house to house distributions for PWDsProtection Risk (3): Sometimes, crowding during distribution of support, may not provide the services at the desire quality, which might affect the BNFs dignity, especially women. Mitigating measures: Set up separate waiting and entry lines at registrations, Establishment of multiple distribution sites to avoid creating crowds, Ensure that female staff are assigned to work during distributions and other activities that will need supervision such as CFW. Protection risk (4): field staff attitude lead to Undignified distribution and harm beneficiaries’ dignity and rights during assistance distribution. Mitigating measures: Ensuring that all project staff had signed and adhered to code of conduct , ensuring that the field team gained a full knowledge on the highest ethical standards and respect for humanitarian principles


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Based on Ihsan RD PSEA policy and before starting of project interventions, by starting of the project Ihsan RD will manage complaints mechanism and ensure accountability to beneficiaries after advanced coordination with Ihsan RD protection canters and PSEA focal points in the targeted location Ihsan RD will conduct SEA risk assessment of to identify all potential SEA risks such as :1- Risk of SEA act by non-PSEA trained staff while provide the support to BNFs,2- BNFs do not report SEA cases for Ihsan RD staff, which results in the Ihsan RD being unable to investigate these cases and provide the needed assistance to them.Mitigation: After in-depth assessment for these risks, Ihsan RD through collaboration with PSEA focal point will designed the mitigation plan all these risks as follows Mitigation measures:1- Ihsan RD will be ensure that all its staff had2- signed code of conduct to prevent acts of SEA by its staff and all recruited staff should attend PSEA training before any contact with BNFs. 3- Beneficiary awareness-raising (Beneficiaries must be aware on what SEA is, what their rights, what they can do if SEA occur4- Establish safe and accessible community reporting channels to report SEA complaints tool will be also available (e.g., complaints box / telephone hotline to deal with these cases)5- Choosing appropriate time for distributions of support (during daylight hours to allow women to return home safely, ensuring regular security and incidence reporting,
