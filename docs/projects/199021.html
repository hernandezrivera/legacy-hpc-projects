Project Summary

The purpose of the Strengthening Humanitarian Response and Sustainability in Nigeria (SURESN) project is to contribute to reduced morbidity, mortality, and improved well-being of conflict affected and displaced Nigerians through strengthening integrated multi-sector humanitarian response and sustainability in Borno State, Northeast Nigeria.

1 - Needs

With breastfeeding and complementary feeding practices dangerously low with only less 3% consuming a minimum acceptable diet (MAD), FHI 360 will implement an integrated package of services, including MIYCN-E to improve maternal, infant and young child feeding practices. An estimated 13% of under-five child deaths could be averted if 90% of mothers exclusively breastfed their infants for the first six months of life. If the same proportion of mothers provided adequate and timely complementary feeding for their infants from 6 to 23 months, 6% of under-five child deaths could be prevented. Highlighting the urgency of optimal MIYCN interventions.One in every five children with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and one in every 15 children with moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) risk dying if not treated in time. Between February and July 2022, FHI 360 admitted 3,652 SAM children with and without complications, which is 215% more than those admitted during the same period in 2021. Due to increased food insecurity, lack of livelihood and growing inflation, this trend is projected to increase. FHI 360, through the project, will implement nutrition activities that contribute to the reduction of under-five mortality rates, including active case finding through MUAC to identify children with MAM and SAM to promote ownership and sustainability and treatment of acute malnutrition among children under 59 months. FHI 360 will strengthen MIYCN-E activities across Bama (Bama town and Banki), Mobbar-Damasak and Ngala LGAs. FHI 360 will support community-based initiatives, including setting up community support groups approach in delivery of MIYCN-E services and will provide facility-based MIYCN-E at the PHCs, inpatient (stabilization centers) and OTP services. MIYCN-E counselling and referral services will also be available at other contact points such as safe spaces and ANCs. Building on the success of IHANN, FHI 360 proposed interventions will be inclusive, deliberately targeting stakeholders on positive shift in MIYCN-E. The intervention will build the capacity of pregnant and lactating women, males, caregivers of children under two (U2) years, traditional and religious leaders, health and nutrition workers and volunteers and other stakeholders in strengthening IYCF practices.

2 - Activities

Behavior Change Intervention: FHI 360 under the new project will utilize community support group structure of both mother to mother (MTM) and father to father (FTF) support groups approach in delivery of MIYCN-E using National SBCC Strategy. These interventions will target caregivers of children under two years, adolescent girls, and PLW. FHI 360 will focus on addressing adolescent malnutrition issues by setting up Adolescent support groups to target girls aged 15-19 years old being the most at-risk group for malnutrition. The program will establish 1,100 community support groups (CSGs) across Bama, Banki, Damasak, and Ngala to implement MIYCN-E activities to complement FGDs, including religious and community leaders.Lactation Management and Skilled IYCF Counseling: Across Bama, Banki, Damasak, and Ngala, FHI 360 will implement specialized lactation management in E-corners, ANC/PNC and stabilization centers focusing on improving breastfeeding and resolving breastfeeding difficulties that may impeded breastfeeding increasing breastmilk, special conditions regarding breastfeeding such as breastfeeding and HIV, relactation, and use of BMS as last resort for children with no prospect of breastfeeding in line with the IYCF-E Operational guidance and national protocols. FHI 360 will provide specialized one on one counseling services at Breastfeeding spaces in health facilities and Community MIYCN-E to promote, protect and support breastfeeding in all locations.Improving Micronutrient Status through Supplementation to Children aged 6-59 months and PLW: Across the four locations, FHI 360 will utilize all her delivery health facilities, OTP Sheds, and CSGs, GMP to facilitate Vitamin A supplementation for children 6 -59 months and distribute Micronutrient Powder (MNP) to children aged 6-23 months as part of the home fortification strategy in line with the HRP objectives. This will boost children’s immunity and increase the quality of the diet being consumed. Supplementation will improve the protection against diseases, particularly the major causes of childhood morbidity and mortality. Growth Monitoring and Promotion (GMP): FHI 360 will provide GMP services at the health facilities and will scale up this service to the community MIYCN centers and breastfeeding spaces to children 0-2 years. Active Case Findings: In line with the principle of timeliness, FHI 360 will carry out active case findings through MUAC and edema checks. The program will employ both house-tohouse (through trained volunteers) and family-led MUAC screening approaches to ensure high coverage. The family led MUAC approach will be scaled up as the primary intervention for identifying children with MAM and SAM.Outpatient Therapeutic Program: SAM children (6-59 months) without medical complications identified by CNMs, CMAM Assistants and family members will be admitted and managed at the OTP. FHI 360 will maintain and strengthen the 10 OTPs (established through IHANN project) in Bama, Banki, Damasak, and Ngala to provide services. FHI 360 plans to enroll 4680 children into the program.Management of Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) through Target Supplementary feeding: In line with the HRP 2022 target, FHI 360 will provide Ready to Use Supplementary Food (RUSF) to children who are diagnosed with MAM. This activity will be carried out in all OTP sites across Bama, Banki, Damasak, and Ngala targeting 6000 children.Inpatient Care: SAM children 0-59 months with medical complications referred from the community and OTPs will be admitted in the stabilization center for specialized medical and nutritional management. The stabilization centers in Banki and Ngala will be maintained to ensure continuous provision of lifesaving service, FHI 360 plans to provide this service to 764 children.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

FHI 360 will sensitize nutrition beneficiaries on identifying GBV and where to report such cases for clinical management and psychosocial support. To prevent caregivers from being a target of such, nutrition interventions will be carried out during the day and in safe locations. Caregivers of children with malnutrition will be linked to other project components such as food assistance and livelihoods to prevent the risk of sexual exploitation, abuse, and harassment (SEAH). We will advocate and involve men in maternal and child feeding/care, which also include supporting women in household chores so that mothers can have enough time to take care of infants and young children. FHI 360 will integrate positive parenting skills in BCC, and key messages on GBV mitigation with nutrition messaging.FHI 360 will ensure that all services are provided in a gender-sensitive and safe environment. OTP and FHI 360 sites will have child friendly equipment, a safe waiting area, gender segregated toilets that are safe and are disability friendly. CSG meetings will be held in a safe and conducive environment, and children will be kept and looked after in safe corners during nutrition activities. All services will be provided during the day, except those requiring 24-hour stabilization care. FHI 360 will ensure staff and volunteers continue receiving PSEA sensitization, ensuring referral of beneficiaries who may require protection services. FHI 360 will include both female and male members of target groups and stakeholders during the program implementation following ‘Do no harm’ principles. Both male and female will be involved in identifying the most pressing needs, developing messaging and even communications strategies. Women (duty bearers and caregivers) will be targeted as part of vulnerable groups for nutrition intervention. Most of the members of the Community Support groups would be women formed into Mother-to-Mother support groups. Rather than targeting women only, household would be targeted to include men as well to help promote redistribution of household tasks between men and women, support breastfeeding promotion, counseling and other MIYCN activities, improve intra-household food distribution in favor of children and women and incorporate women and children opinion in food decisions and choices at home.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

To enhance accountability to affected populations, the project will incorporate a Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) system to ensure two-way communication with communities and beneficiaries for the duration of the program. The system allows for sharing program information on the quality, timeliness, and relevance of the aid being provided to communities while also providing anonymous, confidential ways for beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries to share programmatic feedback (such as related to minor or major dissatisfaction with project aspects) or sensitive complaints (such as related to PSEA, staff conduct, or fraud). FHI 360’s CFM channels are safe and accessible to everyone, including the physically disabled, vulnerable, elderly, and marginalized groups. In the year 2022, FHI 360 received 779 Feedback and complaints across its field implementation sites in Borno and closed 99%. FHI 360 CFM utilizes various channels for feedback and complaints collection. These include verbal feedback post-service delivery, complaint/information help desks, suggestion boxes, and community feedback sessions. Based on beneficiaries’ feedback and recommendations, FHI 360 is piloting a toll-free line as another feedback channel. CFM community awareness and information sharing sessions shall help enhance knowledge of existing reporting channels and how to access these services. Utilizing different methods, including the broadcast of CFM jingles in the local language, FHI 360 staff, community volunteers, and community focal persons, shall help raise community awareness of existing CFM channels. Additionally, FHI 360 solicits the participation of the community in its CFM approaches. Through CFM committee meetings at the community level, FHI 360 meets with community members, leaders, and stakeholders to address feedback from CFM.
