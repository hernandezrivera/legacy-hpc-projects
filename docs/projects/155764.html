Project Summary

According to the Bureau of Statistics, FATA, the overall literacy rate of FATA stands at 33.30%, while for male literacy rate is 49.70% and for female is 12.70%, much lower than the rest of Pakistan while girls literacy rate in FATA is alarmingly low at 12.70%. Similarly, the dropout rate for girls students at primary level is 91% and boys 81% students are able to transition from Grade 5.Over all enrollment in the government institutions(including unadmitted students) 677543 with 589936(85.48%) in primary, 21279in middle, 57995(7.53%) in high, 7947(1.03%) in higher secondary. Over all Drop-out rate from Kachi to Class-5 in Government primary level Schools over cycle of 6 year(2013 to 2017) is 73% in which 69% is of boys and 79% is of girls. While the overall retention Rate from Kachi to Class-5 in Government Primary Schools over a cycle of 6 years(2013 to2017) is 27% in 31 is of boy and 21 is of girls.
The percentage of public institutes with basic facilities i.e., Boundary wall is 70%(60% boys and 83% girls), latrine is 45%(35% boys & 59.3 girls), drinking water is 45.2%(40%boys and 52.7% girls) and electricity is 43%(38.2%boys and 49.8% girls).
Considering the complexities and cultural context of FATA, one of the major factors for such a high drop-out, especially for girls, is represented by the “missing facilities”: boundary walls, appropriate gender segregated latrines, and water facilities are quoted as some of the most important missing infrastructure in schools, and the most likely to lead to increased drop out of girls students especially in the passage from primary to middle school. UNDP will identify these missing facilities in schools for interventions. Due to non-availability of residential facilities for women teachers most of girls schools are non-functional as women staff are out of districts cannot attend schools on regular basis. These missing facilities are discourage parent to sending their girls children to schools.

Needs

The future of erstwhile FATA depends on investing in the capacity building and development of its children and youth through formal education, Technical Vocational Education and Training (TVET) and life skills amongst others. Children and youth comprise the largest bulge of FATA’s population (almost 59%) yet far from achieving their full potential due to financial constraints and lack of quality education. The challenges related to access, quality and governance of education and lack of support system is depriving development of human resource and preventing them playing an effective role in the stability and development of FATA. Since the main source of education available to the population of FATA is through public institutions which currently total to 5,957 , there is a need to invest and support these institutions and build their capacities so that they may be able to function in a sustainable manner. The project proposes to provide a comprehensive response to FATA’s complex education related challenges, complementing the FATA Education Sector Plan 2015/16 to 2019/20, Recommendations of the FATA Reforms Committee 2017 and the Sustainable Return and Rehabilitation Strategy (SRRS) 2015. This project is based on the lessons learnt through UNDP and UNICEF’s previous and ongoing multi-donor support to FATA. Under the FTRP, UNDP has rehabilitated 770 schools, trained 2,000 teachers, provided over 350 schools with required furniture and strengthened the capacity of 750 Taleemi Islahi Jirgas (TIJs), etc. Despite the continuous efforts by the Government of Pakistan, military, international and national non-governmental organisations, donors and other stakeholders to bring FATA at par with other areas of Pakistan, a lot of work still needs to be done. Approximately 2,200 public schools require rehabilitation and over 2,000 schools lack essential facilities .
