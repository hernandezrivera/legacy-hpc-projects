Project Summary

The humanitarian crisis has been growing as Sudan is facing high inflation rates that raised the number of food insecure people and deepening poverty level. The number of people falling before poverty line has grown rapidly, which needs swift response. It has also reduced the capacity of the state in availing basic services to its residents. The COVID-19 pandemic has added more burden to the exiting challenge, by contributing to slowing down of the economic growth, loss of jobs and reduction in remittances flowing in to the country. Sudan has been doing an economic reform which includes to remove subsides on fuel, food and medicine. As result of this, the prices of the goods are going up and more people are finding it hard to access basic food and medical services. The increment on fuel prices has contributed to higher production cost for irrigated farms, directly contributing to increased food costs.
This project will include range of activities from filling food gaps using cash and voucher, to supporting for improvement of production (crop and animal) by providing or strengthening the purchasing power of the affected people through cash and voucher assistances. The quality of seeds will be checked for germination and purity tests, and inputs that fit to the existing climatic conditions will be introduced. The livestock sector will focus on restocking and providing veterinary services. Community members will be trained and supplied with essential medical equipment/drug for wider coverage of vaccinations and controlling of potential disease outbreaks. Trainings on post-harvest management, to reduce the loss that is currently happening and demonstrations on improved farming systems will be included. Income generation activities will benefit youth and some small scale business people, to re-activate the local economy and assisted with up-to-date market information sharing. The cash and voucher assistance (CVA), will support vulnerable groups to fill the monthly food gaps, to cover 50% of the MEB. And the early recovery activities will provide improved seeds and tools, in collaboration with local universities and research centers. This will pave the way for adaptable and sustainable food production in the near future. Affected families will be supported on enhance production skills and reduce the post harvest loses. Swift and timely support for the most affected families, will play critical role in stopping from worsening food insecurity and begins with recovery to grown their own foods.

1 - Needs

Sudan is experiencing severe food insecurity, the number of people under acute food insecurity (IPC 3+) has increased compared to previous years, by around 10 million people, caused by the economic challenges, flooding, internal conflicts and displacement of people. In addition, over 1.4 million of people are facing IPC “Emergency” Phase 4 compared to the same season in 2020. The current year people in need for food security and livelihood sector is 9.8 Million people. Around 8.4 million will benefit from the sector response; of which 5.7 million on food related support and another 3 million on livelihoods/rehabilitation programs. The FSL sector will prioritize areas with high level of IPC load. The project will be implemented in areas which are frequently exposed to natural disasters like drought, flood and other man-made disasters; most needy families, families with children under five, IDPs, communities with high level of malnutrition; farmers who suffers from poor production, areas with high post harvest loses and benefit from sharing of up-to-date market information. The proposed project will directly support families to improve their crop and livestock production by assisting them with agricultural inputs (using cash and voucher modalities). As per the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022 (HNO), 14.6 million people will be in need of humanitarian assistance across Sudan in 2022. Needs are driven by an extended economic crisis characterized by sustained high inflation, weak public service provision and a volatile political context; with repeated shocks including intercommunal conflicts, mass displacement, flooding, and disease outbreaks including the COVID-19 pandemic.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Improved agricultural (crop and livestock) production: the project will assist families with agricultural inputs like seeds and tools; by providing cash/voucher to increase their purchasing power, restocking (small ruminants) and treatment of animals, providing trainings on improved crop production techniques and community animal health workers. Increase food consumption by using cash and voucher support to increase household diet diversity; the project will assist families to reduce the monthly food gaps by supporting them with cash that will increase their purchasing power.Enhanced early recovery; farmers will be assisted with improved seeds that have better tolerance to drought and flood or climatic changes, so that the productions become more predictable and sustainable. Families will be also supported to have more access to financial services, community resources management activities that includes cash for work to work on public goods, tree plantations, soil conservation, road rehabilitation and pasture land development/enrichment.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Climate change has been causing severe damages in the environment, contributing to loss of production and forcing many people to face extreme weather (drought, flooding). As a result the number of people facing severe food insecurity is increasing. Save the Children has an Environmental Sustainability and Climate Change (ESSCC) Policy, and is committed to minimizing the negative environmental impact of our programs and operations, contributing positively to environmental sustainability and climate change action, and implementing interventions which reduce vulnerability to climatic shocks.The project will evaluate each activity on any potential unwanted impacts, all the responses will be environmental friendly, agricultural inputs using organic sources of maintaining the soil nutrient status. Training the community members on improved farming systems that reduce soil erosion, mobilize the community to invest on soil and water conservation, plantation of trees, grazing land enrichment and pasture land management. The project will contribute to resilience and a sustainable use of natural resources through involving and encouraging children and their communities to implement better practices which support and better environmental protection and a safe local environment. SC Sudan will also communicate publicly about the impacts of climate change and environmental degradation in Sudan. The project also considers the traditional farming knowledge and uses the life time skills of the community, to come up with improvement or adaptation ideas, giving high priority to positive contribution towards environmental management.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Through all programming, Save the Children aims to increase safety, uphold dignity, and ensure equitable access to services, including through addressing the concerns of individuals and groups with specific needs. Save the Children will ensure that girls and boys, women and men (of all ages, abilities and backgrounds) are consulted in a meaningful way in programming at all stages of the project lifecycle, and have regular opportunities to provide feedback and concerns. Gender, age and disability sensitive measures will be adopted, including through adapting meeting/service schedules and meeting venues to ensure equitable participation, using single sex discussion groups, considering ways to support the dignity and privacy of beneficiaries, and ensuring facilities are physically accessible to all, and adopting child-friendly methodologies. Save the Children will provide timely and accessible information (in local languages and culturally appropriate mediums) to affected communities on proposed interventions and deliverables, expectations of staff behavior especially with respect to our Child Safeguarding policy and Code of Conduct, and how to raise concerns or provide feedback. SC Sudan has a well-established Complaints & Feedback Response Mechanism, including a telephone hotline, and multiple further modalities accessible to those with varying levels of literacy and for those that prefer anonymity; and a clear system for handling feedback of all natures.Programme design and implementation is informed by context analysis including local power and conflict dynamics. Save the Children will work closely with multiple stakeholders (community members, government partners, local NGOs) to avoid unintended negative effects on individuals or exacerbation of community tensions, and will seek programming which safeguards, protects, and capacitates vulnerable and marginalized individuals, and reduces tensions. SC Sudan Protection staff support the effective mainstreaming of protection across all programming including though training on referral pathways, conducting protection audits, and providing technical advice. A Gender Equality Specialist supports with improving the gender sensitivity of programmes, and all projects are assessed through gender equality marker. Through community engagement activities, training of community-based structures, and training of service providers, information will be shared on protection risks and appropriate prevention and reporting actions.Prioritize safety & dignity, and avoid doing harm; The project will ensure all the beneficiaries safety and security get's highest attention, beneficiaries served with dignity and make sure they didn't face any harassment. Accountability, Participation & Empowerment; The project will establish and strengthen community based accountability mechanisms, that contributes to higher level of community participation and create an empowered community.
