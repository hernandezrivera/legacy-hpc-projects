Project Summary

The purpose of this project is to enhance GBV mitigation and prevention through improved coordination.
Specific Objectives include –
a. Establish 15 new coordination mechanism/and enhance 12 existing ones in rural locations in Puntland, Somaliland and FGS
b. Build capacities of 90 GBV sub cluster coordinators for gender responsive data generation, utilization and utilization.
c. Support the mainstreaming initiatives on IASC Guidelines for cluster programming on GBV for 300 cluster-coordinators at national and sub national levels

Needs

The protracted conflict in Somalia and the cyclical drivers of displacements including the drought, floods and other natural disasters have destroyed political, economical and social infrastructure in the country. The protective environment remains weak and IDP women and girls are often survivors of GBV. The capacities of the local authorities is weak and requires strengthening. There is need to strengthen the coordination of the activiteis in order to maximize the use of the limited resources, identify gaps and avoid overlaps. Effective coordination is essential to the delivery of quality, timely and confidential services. UNFPA is the lead agency of GBV sub cluster in Somalia with the overall technical coordination role. Some of the sub clusters are newly established or weak and require support for improved coordination of GBV activities. UNFPA provides technical support and guidance to sub clusters and task forces at the national and field levels. Each zone has a dedicated GBV coordinator who provides the overall leadership in support of the government’s coordination of partners/ actors working on GBV prevention and response. The Task Forces include the FGM/C, GBVIMS and the CMR Task Forces at the national and field levels. Regional and national GBVIMS coordinators continue to support the GBVIMS Task Forces, which has led to improved GBVIMS reporting and coordination The humanitarian response in Somalia has functioning GBV sub clusters at zonal levels – South Central Zone, Somaliland and Puntland with over 25 coordination hubs with chair and co-chairs from UNFPA, UNHCR, government and international and national NGOs. There still exist gaps in coordination in regions of Somaliland and Puntland with some sparse GBV service providers in need of establishing a platform for coordination for effective service provision. Out of the existing 25 coordination hubs only 50% is activated (Hargeisa, Bosaso, Galkayo, Garowe, Baidoa, Belet Weyne, Dhobley, Dhusam

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

This project will enhance the capacity for coordination for improved service delivery by improving the capacity of coordinators, updating and expanding referral pathways for access to services; and enhance capacities of actors to undertake safety audits to provide valid primary data on GBV risks and exposure within clusters of the humanitarian response in Somalia.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

This project will enhance the capacity for coordination for improved service delivery by improving the capacity of coordinators, updating and expanding referral pathways for access to services; and enhance capacities of actors to undertake safety audits to provide valid primary data on GBV risks and exposure within clusters of the humanitarian response in Somalia.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Evidence based GBV programming informed by understanding of trends and emerging issues on the different needs of women, girls, boys and men is needed to inform programming for GBV. The dearth of routine assessments for GBV risks and exposures for women, girls, men and boys is an impediment for programming to respond to immediate urgent needs in Somalia humanitarian response. Also, the lack of capacity and understanding of the GBV sub cluster coordinators to conduct rapid assessments/safety audits to inform and direct programming in areas affected by conflict, droughts and floods complicates the situation for GBV programming. In addition, the lack of capacity by sub cluster coordinators and other cluster coordinators to apply the IASC GBV mainstreaming guidelines 2015 continues to be opportunities lost to improve GBV programming in sectoral response.
