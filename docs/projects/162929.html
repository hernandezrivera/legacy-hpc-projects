Project Summary

The Food Security and Livelihoods Sector aims at improving the food and nutrition security situation of the most vulnerable refugees, returnees, internally displaced persons (IDPs), and host communities in Sudan. The sector’s overall objectives are to respond in a timely manner to the immediate lifesaving humanitarian needs; improve and restore the livelihoods and self-reliance and promote nutrition sensitive interventions of the affected population. This FSL Sector Coordination project will 1) support timely, reliable and continuous data flow on food security and livelihoods related issues, where assessment findings are produced and disseminated; 2) provide overall strategic guidance to the sector members and all humanitarian actors; 3) support sector partners in capacity building initiatives and training opportunities on food and nutrition security related topics (example: food security concepts and indicators, Integrated Phase Classifications – IPC, monitoring and evaluation of FSL activities, agricultural and livestock technical standards, emergency preparedness, disaster risk reduction, etc.) to insure adoption of international standards and guidelines; 4) promote sector activities and support resource mobilization; 5) monitor partners’ interventions to minimize potential overlaps, ensure complementarity and address gaps; 6) ensure inter-sectoral coordination for a multi-dimensional response and mainstream protection, gender, and environment. This project will target about 20 agencies and 250 people who are members in the FSL sector with interventions that aim at building their capacity on food and nutrition security related topics including assessments, monitoring and evaluation, disaster risk reduction and emergency preparedness.
Food insecurity is caused by multidimensional challenges that affect the availability, accessibility, utilization and sustainability of the country’s food production system. This complex environment requires multifaceted immediate, and medium- and long-term response approaches. Over the three-year period of the Multi-Year Humanitarian Strategy, the Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) sector response contributed and will contribute to addressing immediate food security needs while restoring livelihoods and strengthening the resilience of beneficiary population in the affected areas. In particular, FSL response interventions aim to: (i) reduce the acute food insecurity of recently displaced people affected by natural disasters; (ii) restore and sustain the food security and livelihoods of vulnerable populations affected by protracted displacements and crisis; (iii) build the self-reliance of food-insecure populations by reducing their vulnerability to crises and disasters; and (iv) strengthen the capacity of national partners to address food insecurity and improve coordination in FSL core functions at national and state levels.
The FSL Sector co-led by the FAO and the WFP has been facilitating coordination mechanism at the national and subnational levels. The support provided by different donors particularly OFDA has allowed for strengthening its operational capacities; the Sector has also provided platforms for partners to discuss sector needs and plans to ensure effective and sustained humanitarian response. Strategic decisions were based on needs assessment and food security analysis. Application and adherence to sector humanitarian standards and guidelines were ensured. Advocacy on behalf of affected population and sector partners was undertaken. Progress and achievements on the implementation of sector strategic work plan were monitored and reported. Serious efforts were exerted to build the capacities of national partners.

1 - Needs

The 2020 HNO identifies 6.2 million individuals in Sudan that are in need of timely and adequate food and livelihoods security assistance, an increase from 5.7 million individuals in 2019. Out of the 6.2 million people in need, 4.7 million will be targeted under the FSL sector, an increase from 4.4 million compared to 2019The overall objective of the Food Security and Livelihoods sector is to support improved food and livelihood security of conflict and disaster affected, vulnerable, and small-scale farming households.Addressing the driving factors and responding to the acute and protracted food insecurity requires multidimensional coordinated interventions, which demand concerted efforts of all actors and partners in the country, including the United Nations system, government institutions and National and International Organizations. Currently, there are over 250 partners in Khartoum and the states that are part of the existing FSL sector coordination structure, including National and International Non-Governmental Organizations (NNGOs and INGOs), the United Nations (UN), government institutions, Donors, Civil Society Organizations (CSOs) and Community Based Organizations (CBOs). To ensure a better understanding of the real scale of the response, the sector requires field level FSL sector coordination structures. The sub-sectors will provide better understanding on the needs and gaps, and ensures a proper information flow. This will support designing proper and relevant FSL strategies and responses. Similarly, the need for Information Management support is crucial to ensure accurate and timely information and dissemination. Currently, the sector produces quarterly dashboards on the food security response across Sudan based on a 4W matrix of who does what where and when, monthly bulletins and newsletters, supports the monthly sectoral meetings as well as the information management working group sand other technical working groups including the IPC. To ensure continuation and scaling up of the food security and livelihoods coordination, the FSL sector requires qualified and technical human capacities to provide support to the partners involved in the FSL response. The situation in different areas requires continuous assessment and analysis to provide the right support to the right people, taking into consideration the distinct needs of women, men, boys and girls. Food security assessments, reviews and gaps analysis are important sources of evidence for accurate planning regarding the sector strategic program. FSL assessments will be supported to improve sector programs effectively by taking into account the needs of different categories of affected population. Key constraints and challenges identified through various sector visits, reviews, discussions and assessments include noticeable gaps in state level FSL coordination in terms of staffing and improved backup support. There is limited capacity of national partners in Project Cycle Management. Shortage of funding resources against needs remain wide. Cross coordination with other sectors, particularly Nutrition Sector needs special improvement. There is limitation of FSL sector information management staff at central and state levels.

2 - Activities / Outputs

 Maintain and strengthen one national and nine state level FSL sector coordination mechanisms and platforms by ensuring full involvement of all sector partners with proportionate representation of male and female participants;  Inform strategic decision making of FSL sector response through assessment and analysis of needs, gaps, and priorities; application of tools & guidance; & mapping sector interventions and capacity of sector partners with appropriate consideration of differing needs of girls, women, boys and men;  Support in conducting FSL technical and specific assessments based on the need of information identified by the FSL sector partners. This includes topics such as social protection and child labour in agriculture, animal transboundary disease outbreak, farm level labour market assessment, etc.  Develop FSL sector strategic / humanitarian work plan by aligning to countrywide HC/HCT strategic objectives / priorities and milestones, including review and approval of sector partners project sheets;  Facilitate FSL sector eligibility and partners’ project preparation / selection for in country pooled funding [e.g. SHF] through appropriate sector coordination mechanisms, and advocate for adequate funding of FSL sector work plan;  Develop, review, and update preparedness plans for recurrent disasters;  Train and develop capacities of 250 staff of national and international partners’ staff [NNGOs, INGOs & GoS line ministries] in FSL needs assessment tools, standards, and guidelines; project cycle management; IPC; and reporting at national and state levels with proportionate involvement of male and female participants;  Network, liaise and build partnerships within FSL sector/cluster [with Global Food Security Cluster] and across sectors [ Inter Sector Coordination Group -ISCG/, OCHA, HC/HCT, other relevant sectors];  Prepare and disseminate information, education and communication [IEC] materials in promoting sector progress and achievement.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Although this FSL Sector coordination project has no direct impact on the environment, it places high emphasis on the importance of the FSL partners and members to be fully proactive in mainstreaming climate change adaptation measures in their strategies, programmes and projects. The FSL Sector encourages all the FSL Sector members and members of the other sectors to be fully active in implementing environmentally sound interventions. This project will deserve no efforts to advocate for addressing gabs in the knowledge , skills, attitude and practices of the FSL sector and other sectors to ensure environment-friendly interventions.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The FSL members will consider the protection of people depending on their needs and requirements duirng project design, implementation and monitoring. The FSL members will provide suitable interventions that improve women’s capacity to produce all or part of good-quality food requirements and generate cash income for their families, contributing to empowerment and social protection of women, especially women headed households. The Sector partners will give equal opportunities to all categories of the population to access the provided livelihood support, addressing the rights of all population categories to decent work to restore their food security and livelihood situation. The targeted communities have been involved in dialogue through meetings, interviews and group discussions throughout all steps of this project design and preparation and will be directly involved in the implementation processing including beneficiaries selection, registration and monitoring of the implementation at the field and beneficiaries level.

7 - Accountability to Affected People and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse
7.1 - How will the project share information and collect community feedback and use it to improve your activities? Please select as many as relevant.

Face to face with group consultations with aid workers

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

FSL accountability towards affected people starts from selection of types, varieties and quantities of livestock and agricultural inputs. The identified inputs are selected in a participatory manner during the consultation phase. FSL members encourage the local communities not only to participate in the implementation of the activities but also to ensure ownership of the project. As a result. FSL members provide assistance to the beneficiaries for the formation of local committees and training of its members so that they can play an active role in the implementation and monitoring phases. Any beneficiary complaints about types, varieties and quantities of inputs will be captured during the monitoring missions. FSL members show continues interest to hear the voices of the beneficiaries about its standard packages of inputs which have been subject to change every year taking into account the views of the targeted people. FSL members urge all its implementing partners to open hot lines with the beneficiaries to allow them to express their opinions about the intervention and give them the chance to bring out any grievances they may have about the interventions. The project will promote understanding between the different livelihood groups (pastoralists and farmers) through group meetings, discussions and other community platforms to reduce conflict and encourage mutual support. Special focus will be on enhancing the knowledge and skills of men, women, adolescent girls and other at risk-groups on natural resources management and leadership to ensure sustainable livelihood interventions; this will help in avoiding competition over natural resources and/or unsustainability of natural resources..
