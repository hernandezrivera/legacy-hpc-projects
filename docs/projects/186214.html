Project Summary

The fast-deteriorating situation in the North West and South West (NWSW) regions and subsequent displacement has led to growing levels of food insecurity and a higher prevalence of negative coping mechanisms adopted by affected populations (Cadre Harmonisé, October 2021). EPDA’s intervention will aim at restoring and safeguarding the livelihoods of crisis-affected and vulnerable populations (IDPs, host communities, PWD and returnees) by providing agricultural inputs to kick-start production and to provide a source of food and income within 6 months time frame as well as refresher sessions to maximize their use and value. This project will have four priority components: i) support to nutrition sensitive egg production/poultry farming; ii) support to nutrition sensitive vegetable garden production for both home consumption and market sale; iii) support to nutrition sensitive organic oyster mushroom production and iv) support to nutrition sensitive fish production. These will provide access and availability of highly nutritious products such as eggs rich in protein, mushrooms high in both medicinal and nutritional value, tomatoes full of vitamin A and and fish rich in alternative protein values . Hens of 4-5 months old will be distributed to provide households with eggs in less than 2 weeks; the hens will complete the laying cycle in a year, after which the beneficiaries can sell the meat at the local market and/or consume it and able to re-start the production cycle on their own. The activities will be implemented with the objective of rehabilitating beneficiaries’ productive capacity for their own consumption and for income, consequently improving their livelihoods through the sales of harvested eggs, vegetable, tomatoes and catfish. EPDA in collaboration with partners will organize a refresher short sessions and sector leads (MINEPIA, MINADER and local NGOs) to strengthen capacities and ensure quality assistance towards sustainable production and sales. Participants of these refresher sessions will subsequently empower local producers / beneficiaries in the areas of intervention. Members of the community will also be involved. Beneficiaries will benefit from the refresher sessions and technical support on poultry farming, mushroom, vegetable production and handling and fish production, as well as in nutrition. The project will target 4,800 beneficiaries (equivalent to 800 households). The targeted beneficiaries will be drawn from three divisions of the South West region of Cameroon. In total we will have 1,500 beneficiaries involved in egg production; 2,500 beneficiaries involved in vegetables production, 300 beneficiaries involved in organic oyster mushroom production and 500 beneficiaries in catfish fish production. At least sixty percent of the beneficiaries will be women and girls and ten percent person with disabilities (PWDs). Survivors of gender-based violence (GBV), youths, and including out of school aged youths. The project will have the following outputs: - 1,500 beneficiaries benefiting from 250 nutrition sensitive egg production/poultry farming units - 2,500 beneficiaries benefiting from vegetables production with 105 hectares cultivated, 300 beneficiaries benefiting from 50 organic oyster mushroom production units, and 500 beneficiaries benefiting from 100 fish ponds - 800 heads of households refreshed on mushroom, vegetable, fish and egg production techniques.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The fast-deteriorating situation in the North West and South West (NWSW) regions and subsequent displacement has led to growing levels of food insecurity and a higher prevalence of negative coping mechanisms adopted by affected populations (Cadre Harmonisé, October 2021). According to the Cadre Harmonisé report of March 2021, the situation is rather precarious in the North-West and South-West regions, which are plagued by socio-political conflicts, and in the Far-North, Littoral and West regions, where internally displaced persons are adding pressure to household food stocks. Between March and May 2021, the food situation deteriorated in 14 divisions that are in the crisis phase, in the North-West (06), South-West (06), Littoral (01) and Centre (01) Regions, that is, 4 additional Divisions compared to March 2020. Between March and May 2021, the food situation deteriorated in 14 divisions that are in the crisis phase, in the North-West (06), and South-West (06). In the divisions especially those in the South West, populations are particularly affected either by socio-political crises or poverty. Even though no division was in an emergency or famine phase at that time, the situation requires immediate response which is exacerbated by the global health crisis. In some divisions of the South West region, basic foodstuff prices are stable compared to last year. This is the case for, beans, potato, solanum potato, yam and banana in the, North-West and South-West and East Regions.There is a heavy presence of internally displaced persons (IDPs), disruption of cross-border trade, pressure on food resources and above all the deterioration of livelihoods in some divisions in crisis or under pressure in the South-West among others. This situation is the result of the socio-political crisis in the North-West and South-West Regions. The deterioration of livelihoods remains a cause for concern in the South-West (Meme, Manyu, Kupe-Manenguba, Lebialem, Ndian, Fako) where people have abandoned or even lost their assets in their migration to safe areas.
