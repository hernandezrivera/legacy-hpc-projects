Project Summary

The occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) lives in a state of protracted crisis that significantly hinders development. Médecins du Monde-France's (MdM-F) recently concluded an assessment done between May to August 2022, findings revealed that health facilities (HFs) and medical staff in West Bank (WB) and Gaza are not adequately prepared to respond to emergencies. Community structures are also not sufficient to protect the affected populations from the shocks of protracted crisis.
This proposed action, therefore, aims to mainstream resilience at all structures involved in health crisis management in WB and Gaza, from community, Primary Health Care (PHC), and hospital settings, targeting vulnerable communities, such as women, Persons with Disabilities (PWDs), GBV survivors, among others, and providing the means to reduce risks and improve resilience through capacity building of well-equipped Community First Responders (CFR) and mobilizing them into a self-sustaining community-based emergency response group; training HFs staff on common medical and mental health (MH) emergencies with integrated topics on gender-based violence (GBV), disability, and Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) to provide inclusive health services for vulnerable populations; supporting HFs and Village Councils (VCs) in the development, updating, or operationalization of Contingency Plans (CPs); rehabilitation of HFs to be adapted to the needs of PWDs and GBV survivors as well as raising awareness of communities on common medical emergencies and early warning signs and on MH and breaking the stigma surrounding it. The intervention will target 6 PHCCs in Gaza (North Gaza and Khan Younis governorates) and 8 in WB, in addition to 8 communities and 2 hospitals (Khalil Suleiman and Qalqilya Hospitals) in Jenin and Qalqilya Governorates in north WB and will benefit a total of 12,102 direct beneficiaries, of which 3527 are men, 3405 are women, 2646 are boys, 2524 are girls, out of them 1210 are PWDs. The total catchment population of target PHCCs and communities (413,298) will be the indirect beneficiaries of the proposed action (North Gaza, Gaza city, Khan Younis, Jenin and Qalqilya governorates). The proposed intervention is aligned with Cluster objective 1 related to Strategic Objective 2 (SO2).

1 - Needs

MdM-F conducted two assessments on emergency preparedness in both the WB and the Gaza Strip and community perspectives on healthcare services in the WB between May and August 2022. The results of the assessment in the Gaza Strip clearly showed the need for training Primary Care Providers (PCPs) at level-four PHCCs in emergency preparedness and emergency topics (for example, Basic Life Support (BLS), Advanced Cardiac Life Support (ACLS), and Primary Trauma Care (PTC)) given the high number of the PCPs who were unaware of the CP, untrained, or had their training since more than two years ago. The PCPs also expressed their need to be trained in emergency medicine. The assessment also showed the lack of preparedness in the emergency rooms at level-four PHCCs in the Gaza Strip regarding the availability of emergency drugs, disposables, and equipment. Accordingly, the assessment concluded that level-four PHCCs in the Gaza Strip are not adequately prepared to respond to emergencies and disclosed the need to strengthen the PHC’s capacity for disaster and emergency preparedness, including human resources, facilities preparedness, and CPs for future emergencies. Similarly, the assessment of the WB also showed comparable results in terms of the unpreparedness of the PCPs working at different PHCC levels, the improper infrastructure of the PHCCs (inadequate space or design), and the lack of essential emergency drugs and equipment. These hinder the provision of proper emergency services at the existing HFs and necessitate the need to rehabilitate them to improve the quality of provided services. The assessment also revealed the need to rehabilitate the HFs when it comes to providing services tailored to vulnerable populations, such as persons with physical disabilities. In the MdM-F assessment, six out of 20 PHCCs in the North of the WB are not accessible to persons with physical disabilities. Ministry of Health (MoH) -PHC Directors and participants from the community also clearly expressed the need for services tailored to PWDs. Moreover, the need for medical services for GBV survivors is indispensable as none of the assessed PHCCs in the WB has ever provided Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) or medical care for GBV survivors and none of the hospitals have the CMR guideline. This necessitates the need to rehabilitate the HFs to ensure the availability of special rooms for GBV survivors to ensure the proper provision of health services while maintaining privacy and confidentiality. The assessment also showed the lack of MHPSS services in PHCCs and hospitals in north WB. The MHPSS staff at the assessed hospitals consisted mainly of social workers (only one or two), while none had a psychiatrist or psychologist. Moreover, the WB assessment disclosed the need to train community members in emergency management First Aid (FA) and Basic Life Support (BLS) owing to the unavailability of adequate emergency services. The participants in the assessment also expressed the need to train existing MH providers from the community, like social workers, on MH and disability. Such training would help the communities in the WB prepare for any emergency, especially when hospital access could be prohibited due to road closures. This proposed intervention targets hotspot areas in north WB (Jenin and Qalqilya governorates) as the needs are higher in terms of emergency preparedness and response as these areas are more affected by clashes and violence. On the other hand, the PHCCs in the Gaza Strip were chosen based on the assessment recommendation in strengthening the PHC’s capacity for disaster and emergency preparedness, including human resources, facilities preparedness, CP, the catchment area, and functionality of those PHCCs during an aggression, and to be in line with the MoH’s strategy to consider Al-Rimal and Muscat Al Qarara as PHC models.

2 - Activities

The proposed intervention will be made up of five main thematic activities, categorized into capacity-building, HFs rehabilitation, community awareness, development of CPs, and quality medical and MH provision, which are aligned with the Health Cluster priorities. Detailed activities are shown below: 1) Mobilization of well-equipped Community First Responders (CFR), trained in Medical and Psychological First Aid (PFA) with integrated topics on disability, GBV, PSEA, and Community Based Complaint Mechanism (CBCM). Considering the humanitarian-development nexus, the proposed intervention will aim to mobilize trained first responders into an organized body that can be self-sustainable with initial support from MdM-F, and later from the VCs which will continue maintaining the link between CFRs and PHCCs. Emergency kits will be provided to each trained CFR and to each Village Council. This activity, which will encourage the utilization of community-based resources and capacities in responding to common, basic emergencies, especially at times when movement restrictions and security context are not feasible for timely referrals, will be implemented in WB as MdM-F has already previously trained communities in Gaza through previous projects. 2) Community awareness on MHPSS, including de-stigmatization, common medical emergencies and early warning signs, PSEA, and CBCM. This activity aims to build individual and community resilience to gain healthy coping mechanisms from the shocks of a crisis and to mobilize individual and community resources during any emergency. This activity will be implemented by local partners in Gaza and WB. In Gaza, CFTA, a long-time partner of MdM-F in community-based activities will implement this intervention. In WB, MdM-F is currently mapping local NGOs operational in the target areas, and a local partner will be identified before the launching of the project. 3) Rehabilitation of HFs to improve emergency services and PWDs accessibility as well as to enhance access to quality health care services, using the build-back-better approach and ensuring confidentiality and privacy for GBV survivors, including provision of drugs, supplies, and equipment. This activity aims to contribute to inclusive access to health services by vulnerable populations, such as persons with physical disabilities, MHPSS clients, and GBV survivors. Ensuring that PHCCs and hospitals have the structural capacity to manage emergency cases and receive referrals from hotspot communities is vital for proper emergency and trauma care. In WB, rehabilitation will target the following PHCCs: Kufardan, Al Fanduqumiya, Al Tayba and Silat Ad Dhadhr in Jenin Governorate and Jayyous, Kafr Qaddum, Azzun, and Habla in Qalqilya Governorate. In Gaza, the following PHCCs will be targeted: Abu Shebak, Shuhada Beit Lahia in north of Gaza, Shuhada Al Remal, Sabha in Gaza city, Muscat Al Qarara, and Bani Suhaila PHCCs in Khan Younis. Two hospitals in north WB, Qalqilya and Khalil Suleiman hospitals, will also benefit from this action. 4) Capacity building of HFs staff on MH, trauma and emergency care, e.g. BLS, Primary Trauma Care (PTC), Minimum Initial Service Package for SRH (MISP), Psychological First Aid (PFA), Trauma Recovery Model, with integrated topics on disability, GBV PSEA, and CBCM for the HF staff to be capable of providing inclusive medical and MH services. The MoH has already been consulted regarding the number of staff to be trained in Jenin and Qalqilya governorates in WB as well as in North Gaza and Khan Younis governorates on trauma and emergency topics where MH, GBV, PSEA and CBCM will be integrated in the training curriculum. Increasing the knowledge and skills of HFs staff will enhance their confidence to provide appropriate emergency care in a timely and effective manner, referring only to higher level facilities when necessary, and supporting decongestion of hospital emergency departments and community mental health centers. 5) Provision of emergency medical and MHPSS services at HFs, including strengthening the referral mechanisms. The capacity-building of staff and rehabilitation of HFs are essential components for quality provision of services. Strengthening the referral mechanism between CFRs at community level, PHCCs and hospitals is also vital for a well-functioning emergency and trauma care system that promotes efficient utilization of resources and saves more lives in a timely manner. Furthermore, MdM and MoH teams will support HFs in ensuring quality provision of services through continuous supportive supervision, conducted throughout the project duration. MoH will continue the supervision following the end of the project. 6) Support civil society through the VCs, HFs and districts offices in the development, updating, and operationalization of crisis management/CP. This document will guide and coordinate actions effectively during times of emergency. Simulations or drills will be carried out to operationalize the plan. The completion of these activities aims to achieve the following expected results: A) Communities are empowered to self-help and develop positive coping mechanisms and resilience to crisis; B) HFs are prepared and equipped to manage emergencies and deliver quality and inclusive medical and MHPSS services; C) Crisis management and resilience building are institutionalized at community, PHC, hospital and district levels.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

Majority of the proposed activities will be related to service delivery while a small percentage will be in-kind (rehabilitation, provision of equipment and supplies, etc). MdM team will directly support the MoH and local partners in improving the provision of medical and MHPSS emergency response. Based on long experience in the field of emergency preparedness and response, service delivery has been proven to be the most effective modality to ensure a quality provision of medical and MHPSS emergency services, taking into consideration the different needs of vulnerable populations. Through the mentioned modality of assistance, the impact and sustainability of services are ensured and better emergency response is provided.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

MdM-F accountability system is based on sharing information in a timely manner with the different actors involved in the project as well as regular reporting to the Health Cluster. For this proposed project, the presence of MdM teams in the field makes this flow of information continuous and bidirectional, establishing a system that allows the participation of the health staff at MoH and targeted community members in the definition and adaptation of MdM activities. In the same way, regular communication is kept with the International Cooperation and Projects Directorate (ICD), General Administration of Hospitals Directorate, PHC and Mental Health Directorates of the MoH to ensure full information sharing, participation, and feedback on MdM interventions. When feedback or complaints are received through any of the mechanisms in place, an immediate reply will be provided to the user with a reference number so the user can follow-up and track the process. The complaint will be processed first by the focal point who will categorize the feedback into 2 main categories: regular or sensitive feedback/complaint. Regular complaint is a non-sensitive complaint that relates to programmatic issues. A sensitive complaint includes issues related to abuse/inappropriate behavior with beneficiaries involving verbal, sexual abuse (PSEA), physical assault, or discrimination. Based on the details of the complaint, MdM will take up the feedback internally or with the partner, depending on the circumstances of the complaints. The committee will gather information and conduct hearings and visits. Users who provide feedback will be continuously updated about the process through email or phone, and final decisions and actions will be officially communicated through official mechanisms. All complaints will be registered anonymously by the focal point in a specific database and analysis will be done regularly. Feedback will be used to improve service delivery.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

MdM has functional Feedback and Complaint Mechanism (FCM) available for beneficiaries, partners, and suppliers through FCM cards which highlight that positive or negative feedback will all be taken into consideration and addressed independently from project team and will be dealt with, respecting the principles of confidentiality and privacy, without affecting the possibility to receive assistance in the future. The cards, written in Arabic language, include a unique mobile and WhatsApp (accessible for persons with physical disabilities), number to receive the feedback calls or text messages, the working days and hours to receive the feedback, and a unique email address to receive electronic feedback. In-person complaints are also available for people who do not have access to electronic means. All these modalities are in place to allow safe access to the FCM by different groups regardless of sex, age, disability status, literacy, etc. MdM teams distribute and explain the FCM cards to the beneficiaries during their presence in the field while MdM logistics department also distributes and explains the card to all suppliers involved in the project. For this specific project, FGDs) among groups of men, women, boys, and girls were conducted in a safe, accessible venue within the villages to understand their needs and tailor activities accordingly, and same mechanisms will be used to elicit feedback within the course of the project. A satisfaction survey will also be administered to collect feedback from the beneficiaries of services (trainings, awareness sessions, including on PSEA, HF rehabilitation, medical and mental health service provision) to identify the degree of satisfaction and elicit suggestions for improvement by beneficiaries. The survey, FCM, and FGDs will be key elements in the project to take the necessary actions in enhancing the service quality and accessibility via timely analysis of feedback from the target groups who have utilized MdM services.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The form of humanitarian assistance that MdM is providing ensures that it does not further expose people to physical hazards, violence, or abuse. One of the components of the present proposal aims to increase access to quality services taking into consideration age, gender, and safety for healthcare providers, CFRs and beneficiaries. MdM takes into consideration the specificity of the current health situation with the presence of COVID-19 and the mental well-being of the patients. Within its intervention, MdM is following specific rules for the data collection, gathering, and publishing based on MdM Guidelines, “Ethics in the Field”. As a rule, all MdM teams signed the MdM confidentiality charter. MdM is also keeping safe storage of the beneficiaries’ data and information. When providing feedback to communities and stakeholders, maintaining confidentiality is critical for MdM and its team. The interest of beneficiaries is always protected, and individual names are not revealed in public or group meetings. HR involved in the project, including that of the MoH and the partners, will be trained, supervised, and gender-balanced and it will be ensured that they are updated about the cultural and religious contexts. The project will also ensure that the communities are aware of the protection and complaints mechanisms in place. MdM interventions at the community level are provided by experienced teams through outreach programs which reduce the exposure of communities to harm and ensure equal access, including easier access for PWDs. For this specific project, FGDs and KIIs were conducted among specific target groups (groups of men, women, boys, and girls) where needs of vulnerable communities (women, children, adolescents, PWDs, and elderly) were identified. Based on MdM assessments (2022), some of the barriers identified are related to protection issues, such as transport and access-related and social stigma. Thus, to mitigate these risks, community-based approaches were devised (training CFRs and equipping them with emergency kits, developing CPs) to improve accessibility to services (rehabilitating HFs to make it more accessible for PWDs, such as installation of ramps and handrails) as well as promoting dignified access (awareness sessions to fight stigma and promote normalization to access MHPSS and GBV services). HF assessments were also recently conducted where the privacy, confidentiality, and safety of PHCC’s users were evaluated. Some PHCCs were found not having gender-separated toilets, not having locks on the door, and not having enough lighting in the surroundings. To ensure safety and protection of PHCC’s users, these elements will be addressed during the PHCC’s rehabilitation. Trainings will also be conducted among target groups (PHCC’s staff and CFRs) on inclusive service delivery without discrimination and the principle of Do No Harm approach, ensuring that both genders are represented among training participants. The selection of CFRs will be ensured that men, women, boys, and girls are included to promote inclusive access to emergency services regardless of gender, age, and disability status.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Impartiality, non-discrimination, gender, and inclusion are adopted and put into practice by MdM within its intervention in oPt. During MdM’s existing interventions, the capacity and supervision of MdM field staff and partners on gender, GBV, PSEA, and disability were reinforced to promote inclusive access for vulnerable populations, and refresher trainings on these topics will be done regularly. MdM is addressing the needs of the local community based on gender mainstreaming approach (gender-specific health), for instance, developing different IECs for women, men, and children, developing different protocols based on gendered various manifestations, and addressing the barriers preventing PWDs to access services. Through promoting community-based emergency responses, various specific groups will be represented (women, men, young people) while communities are empowered to take actions for their own health and to increase their resilience. Through HFs rehabilitation, the principles that will improve access for PWDs (infrastructure and service provision) will be adopted. MdM MEAL process takes into consideration the monitoring and documentation of needs per age, gender, vulnerabilities including that of PWDs and GBV survivors. The established gender policy of MdM guides gender mainstreaming in its intervention. Through MdM assessments, done in consultation with community leaders and different gender and age groups (2022), the needs of vulnerable groups, such as women, elderly, children, and PWDs have been identified and interventions within the proposed action are tailored to target the specific needs: having a gender-balanced team, trained staff on inclusive service delivery, rehabilitation of HFs for inclusion of PWDs, and ensuring privacy and confidentiality for sensitive consultations (e.g. GBV, MH and Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH), and adoption of community-based emergency responses to promote local empowerment and more efficient access to basic emergency services.
