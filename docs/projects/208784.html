Project Summary

Sirri Care Foundation is proposing to target a total of 3,200 people; out of which 2000 (1300 girls and 700 boys) will be children including 300 adolescent girls and 200 adolescent boys and those living with disabilities in project communities. The adult target will be 200 (120 women and 80 men) including caregivers, community-based case workers, community volunteers, and members of the community-based child protection committees (CBCPCs). A minimum of 5% of the overall target will be persons living with a disability. The children will be reached with value-added, structured recreation activities, life-skills education, and case management services especially for children formally associated with armed groups (CAAGs). Through the project, Sirri Care will also provide positive parenting sessions to parents and caregivers in project LGAs. The project will also facilitate birth registration for girls and boys under the age of five. Sirri Care will also strengthen the child protection mechanisms in both schools and the communities in the target LGAs through awareness creation sessions, on GBV prevention, PSEA, positive parenting, and child protection concern, risk communication using tools developed by UNICEF/IRC.
Sirri Care will also concentrate on the economic reintegration of CAAGs through tailored skill acquisition training and support. 60 CAAGs will be targeted with this activity. Sirri Care will work in collaboration with the Ministry of Women's Affairs and Social Development and community-based caseworkers to identify and provide case management to 150 UASC children including former CAAG (100 girls and 50 boys). Through this project, Sirri Care will facilitate the capacity strengthening of the case and social workers at the LGA levels, CBCPC members, and community volunteers on Child protection, primary prevention, and positive parenting.

2 - Activities

SIRRI Care will create/reactivate three child-friendly spaces in the three project locations. The creation of a community-based child-friendly space for conflict-affected boys and girls is crucial to providing a safe and supportive environment for children especially those formerly CAAG. This concept involves establishing a dedicated physical center within the community where children can be engaged in various activities that promote their well-being, psychosocial development, and resilience. The project proposes to provide psychosocial support (PSS) to 3000 boys and girls across the project locations through recreational activities including age and gender-specific child clubs, 150 children affected by protection risks (55%girls) including UASC, CAAG, and other at-risk children will be targeted for tailored case management (including referral for specialized services as required). 30 children under the age of 5 will be provided with birth certificates to enable their full reintegration in the community. Enable children to acquire national documentation. The project will target 60 CBCPC members and social welfare workers (20 from each project LGA) on their role and key child protection issues within the communities. This will help reduce the rate of human rights violations faced by boys and girls within the community and also increase community participation in the response to child protection concerns. Sirri Care will provide 30 children (55% girls) with socio-economic activities across the project location to enable their seamless reintegration into the community. This will include conducting art and expressive therapy sessions tailored for girls and boys who have experienced grave human rights violations, through innovative artistic mediums such as self-expression, storytelling, and peer-to-peer emotional support. This will be done via child advocacy podcasts. (Develop podcasts aimed at children, featuring stories, discussions, and interviews related to child protection issues. Also, skill training in areas such as cap making, knitting etc will be conducted. This medium can empower children with knowledge while fostering a sense of community, self-reliance and belonging) Sirri Care is proposing to reach

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Sirri Care will work with community members including women, men, girls, and boys, particularly female-headed households, and people with disabilities to establish gender-sensitive and appropriate compliant mechanisms in project target communities. The established complaint channels will provide for women and girls, particularly female-headed households and people with disabilities that are directly affected by the project a means to raise complaints about the quality of program and staff behavior. Sirri Care will also make provision for a complaint booth where issues of GBV and SEA can be reported. Also, there will be complaint and feedback desks and boxes at strategic areas in the project communities to ensure that community members are able to provide feedback and complaints about the project.
