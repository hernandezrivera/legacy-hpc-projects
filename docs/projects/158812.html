Project Summary

The project objective is “Improved protection and respect of rights of acutely vulnerable women 18-59 and >59 years and girls <18 years survivors of GBV and increased access to essential services based on IHL and IHRL.” WAC partners with RCS4GS and WEFAQ to make incremental changes in situation of women and girls, community and duty bearers as reflected in project outcomes:1. Vulnerable women>18-59 and >59 years and girls <18 years manifested well-being, knowledge and confidence, good health status, economic self-reliance, legal enablement and protection; 2. Targeted communities actively engaged in GBV prevention and protection; and 3. Duty bearers are accountable to vulnerable women >18-59 and >59 years and girls <18 years and CBOs and increasingly act to protect them and reduce incidence and impact of human rights violations against them. The project targets 6693 direct beneficiaries (3775 women and 1335 men>18-59 and>59 years, 1343 girls and 240 boys<18 years) identified based on defining vulnerability and acutely vulnerable groups according to their location, status, gender, protection risks and needs severity. Beneficiaries are selected from most affected locations in North (Arab Maslakh Beit Lahia, Siafa, Jabalia east in Ezbet Abed Rabo and Jabal Rayyes, Ezbet Beit Hanoun, Beit Hanoun ARA) handled by RCS4GS; Gaza (Gaza City: Shijaia and Zaitoon, Juhor adDik, Sabra, Daraj) and Deir ALBalah (Bureij camp, Nuseirat camp, Mussadar, Deir Al Balah) managed by WAC; and Khan Younis (Bani Suhaila, Fukhari, Qarara, Abasan AlKabira) and Rafah (Shuket Elsoofi, Rafah camp, Rafah, Mawasi) handled by WEFAQ. The project links to HRP2020 Strategic Objective 1 and Protection Cluster Objective 2, and standard activities/indicators. Partners introduce an effective partnership model that consolidate their human capital to implement responsive, remedial and environment-building activities to address urgent needs of women/girls reflected in HNO/ HRP 2020 and achieve project objective/outcomes. Activities are: group psychological support and recreational days for 1056 women and 528 girls; individual psychological counseling to 150 women and 75 girls; referral of traumatized 63 women and 18 girls; psycho-therapy for 254 couples; group legal counselling to 1056 women and 528 girls; individual legal counseling to 120 women and 30 girls; legal representation to 180 women and 45 girls; emergency hotline for (420) women and (170) early married girls; emergency health care for 540 women and 240 girls; cancer diagnosis, treatment, rehabilitation and reintegration for 90 women survivors of cancer; case management to 30 women; dignity kits for 300 women and 90 girls; training and grants to 48 women; awareness for 1056 women, 1056 men, 528 girls and 528 boys; training of 25 women and 25 men of local women CBOs; periodical monitoring and coordination with GBV humanitarian key actors; and 3 advocacy initiatives. This project is in line with HRP multiyear strategy and “do no harm” principle. It is designed based on documenting lessons learned from community engagement (CwC,AAP) like 9 community meetings, 18 FGDs, complaints mechanism, 3 radio ads, 9900 brochures, 3 fact sheets, 9 press releases and pre post evaluation. Partners follow 4W activity tracking matrix to coordinate with organizations executing activities in project locations in a way that ensures humanitarian needs are met, complementarity with ongoing projects, resilience of beneficiaries, participatory monitoring GENDER/AGE differences, environmentally neutral, protection mainstreaming, innovation process and principles, inclusion of WWDs/GWDs, security and full access to project services

1 - Needs

Partners (WAC, RCS4GS and WEFAQ) conducted 9 FGDs with beneficiaries under the current OCHA-funded project and concluded needs to be addressed for women/ men 18-59 and elderly>59 years and girls/ boys<14-17 years as they have differentiated needs. Women>18-59 and >59 years and girls<14-17 years survivors of GBV, particularly those with disabilities, refugees, cancer survivors, impacted by the mass demonstrations, and female headed households explained their main problem as “limited access of marginalized women 18-59 and >59 years and girls14-17 years to well-designed multi-sectoral and gender responsive services, protection and economic support”. They highlighted the need for case management services to address sophisticated women cases with multiple problems, and increasing the numbers grants for small businesses and dignity kits. They requested WAC, RS4GS and WEFAQ Association to continue meeting their following priority needs: 1) MHPSS services like group psychological support sessions, recreational open days, individual psychological counseling, mental health treatment services, and psycho-therapy sessions to couples; 2) group and individual legal counselling and court representation; 3) emergency health care services including diagnosis, lab testing and medication; 4) cancer diagnostic and treatment services and home visits; 5) dignity kits that include women’s basic needs; 6) emergency hotline to provide legal aid and referral services; 7) training and grants to establish small businesses; 8) awareness raising workshops on early marriage, GBV types and GBV protection mechanisms; 9) case management; and 10) advocacy initiative to raise protection concerns. WWDs and GWDs have additional needs including mobility and hearing aids. Women with cancer have additional needs including diagnosis using mammography and ultrasound, pathological lab testing, medication, home visits, rehabilitation and reintegration. Men>18 years and boys< 18 years need awareness raising about women’s rights, early marriage and GBV risks. Staff from local women CBOs expressed their training needs in detection of women and girls GBV cases and their referral pathways.

2 - Activities

- Provide MHPSS services to women>18-59 and >59 years and girls 14-17 years survivors of GBV including: structured group psychosocial support for (99) groups (6 sessions/ group) in accordance with UNFPA supported practical manual for PSS for survivors of GBV as well as recreational days for 1056 women>18-59 and >59 years and 528 girls 14-17 years; individual psychological counseling to 150 women and 75 girls; referral of traumatized 63 women and 18 girls; psycho-therapy for 254 couples (254 women and 254 men). - Provide legal aid including group legal counselling to 1056 women>18-59 and >59 years and 528 girls 14-17 years; individual legal counseling to 120 women and 30 girls; legal representation to 180 women and 45 girls; emergency hotline for (420) women and (170) early married girls. Partners will use child protection referral networks for meeting needs of girls in need for child interventions not provided by them.- Provide emergency health care for 540 women>18-59 and >59 years and 240 girls 14-17 years.- Provide cancer diagnosis, treatment, rehabilitation and reintegration for 90 women>18-59 and >59 years survivors of cancer- Provide case management to 30 women>18-59 and >59 years (individual psychological support, mental health, individual legal support, social services, cervical cancer, mobility/ hearing aids). - Provide dignity kits for 300 women>18-59 and >59 years and 90 girls 14-17 years. Selection criteria include: women who attended 6 group psychosocial sessions, mothers of high number of daughters, poorest women, and women with cancer. - Organize small business training, grants and follow up visits to 48 women>18-59 (30 women start new businesses, 18 women upgrade existing businesses) to access self-employment. - Conduct awareness raising sessions for 1056 women>18-59 and >59 years and 528 girls 14-17 years about early marriage, GBV types and GBV protection mechanisms. - Conduct awareness raising sessions for 1056 men>18-59 and >59 years and 528 boys 14-17 years about women’s rights, early marriage and GBV risks. - Conduct training of 25 women and 25 men>18-59 and >59 years of local women CBOs on detection of women and girls GBV cases and their referral pathways. - Communication with Communities (CwC) and (AAP) including: (3) radio ads, (9) press releases, (9) community meetings involving (90) girls and (90) boys 14-17 years and (90) women and (90) men>18-59 and >59 years to explain project’s objective and outcomes; publishing and disseminating (9900) copies of information brochures; elaboration of 3 fact sheets including safely and ethically compiled dis-aggregated data to document GBV related violations to be shared with affected communities and other relevant stakeholders; organizing (18) focus group discussions (FGDs) with affected 70 women, 70 girls, 10 men and 10 boys to discuss possible deficiencies in the quality of GBV services to improve current and future services; undertaking a built-in feedback and complaints mechanism handling; and conducting pre- and post- activity evaluation. - Organize 3 advocacy initiatives to raise concerns about protecting women>18-59 and >59 years and girls 14-17 years from GBV. Each initiative involves (20) representatives INGOs, (200) women and (200) men18-59 and >59 years representatives of related formal institutions (Judges, police, Ministry of Women Affairs, PLC), CBOs/NGOs, community leaders, religious leaders, political parties, (50) women and (50) men households, (100) girls (5 GWDs and (100) boys (5 boys with disabilities) 14-17 years. - Periodically monitoring/coordination by three partners with GBV humanitarian key actors at national level, but also with institutions and organizations working in GBV response in Gaza. - Document and systematize good practices/lessons learned from community engagement mechanisms.- Establish a follow up/tracking system of referrals and cases received and put in place feedback mechanism to referring organizations

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

- The locations of service provision facilities and the routes to them are in the dense urban areas (RCS4GS Health Center in Jabalia Camp, WAC safe space in Al Nasser- Khalil Al Wazir Street, and WEFAQ office in Rafah down town) distant from threats of GBV and attacks from armed groups. - The service provision facilities of the three partners are equipped with INFRASTRUCTURE adaptations such as fitting ramps and railings as well sign language so that all beneficiaries including with physical and hearing disabilities can access and use the facilities in safety and with dignity. - Partners respect confidentiality and privacy during consultation and counseling through separate rooms - Partners follow information sharing protocol reflected in the SOPs. - Partners do not collect information that is not required to promote the well-being of beneficiaries.- Partners do not share identified names and addresses of beneficiaries unless consent has been given by the beneficiary.- Partners have female and male competent/ qualified staff with gender and cultural sensitivities to provide psychosocial, legal, health and economic services to women/girls’ survivors of GBV.- Partners will distribute dignity kits to women and girls and the health care kits to women with breast cancer without having risk for them (pushing, shoving and theft).

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

- Partners provide services at an adequate time (flexible staff time schedules and calendar) when beneficiaries can realistically access them.- Partners use mobile technology (like SMS) to inform beneficiaries to access services.- Partners ensure that women and girls can access the services through having female project staff, engaging men from the families of beneficiary women and local CBOs as safe and accessible places.- Partners ensure that WWDs/GWDs, elderly and bed-ridden beneficiaries can access the services through smooth and fit ramps for wheelchair access, mobile counseling units, and having home visits. - Partners ensure that women and girls, men and boys with vision, hearing and intellectual disabilities can access services and served by well trained and competent staff.- Partners ensure that lack of documentation does not exclude women and girls from accessing services. - Partners raise the awareness of women and girls’ beneficiaries about their rights and how to access services. The design of awareness materials considers the difference in education levels of participants. - Partners monitor access of beneficiaries and discrimination via using project indicators disaggregated by age, gender, and location or specific group. - Partners ensure that services reach the most vulnerable.
