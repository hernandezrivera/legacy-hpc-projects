Project Summary

Crisis affected populations who are underserved are at great risk of Child violation, sexual exploitation and abuse, and forced/early marriages are usually the consequences in this insecure and unstable setting with weak rule of law, scarce of opportunities to sustain the daily lives and limited access to the quality multi-sectoral services. The project will be targeting boy and girls in hard to reach areas including children with disabilities as they are especially vulnerable and report a very high level of unmet needs. They are the most vulnerable, marginalized and at-risk group within Somalian society as a result of the numerous attitudinal, environmental, and institutional barriers they face. The proposed project will establish child friendly space with age appropriate and culturally sensitive orientated activities for boys and girls - Training for project staffs, animators, facilitators and volunteers on HEART(Healing and Education through the Arts) - Conduct HEART (Healing and Education through the Arts) activities for boys and girls at the Child friendly spaces. - Establishment of community based protection mechanism (child protection committees) - Formation of child rights clubs (CRCs) - Capacity building for government and other community based structures including community stakeholders - Identification and provision of case management service to UASC and other children at risk including child headed household, unaccompanied and separated children, survivors of SGBV, children associated with arm force and arm groups (CAAFAG) etc. - Identification and training of standby foster parents - Sensitization and awareness rising session to populates on child protection key messages includes gender base violence. In addition, the project will enhance protective environment for children and adolescents through support to Community-Based Child Protection Mechanisms (CBCPMs). The community-based approach will target 140 community members with capacity building on child rights violations, strengthen referral pathways, and coordination of services at the IDP camps and host communities. The community members will facilitate identification of child protection risks, referral of cases to specialized services through case management. Optimizing Child Protection outcomes through meaningful integration with other sectors and providing various forms of support to caregivers and families of children facing protection risks in order to enhance their capacity to care for and protect their children. Awareness raising activities will target 14,000 community members with a wide range of prevention messages and awareness sessions including on recruitment/CAAFAG, SGBV, harmful practice, family separation, and Children’s rights. The project will be implemented in the hard to reach areas of Bari (Bandarbeyla and Iskushuban) Mudug (Galdogob and Jariiban) Nugaal (Burtinle and Eyl )Sanaag (Laasqoray) hence the proposed activities will contribute to two strategic objectives of the child protection sub-cluster: 1. Boys and girls including adolescents, children with disabilities, facing life-threatening risks of abuse, neglect, violence, exploitation, and severe distress have access to well-coordinated and disability, gender-sensitive quality child protection services by 2021. 2. By 2021 vulnerable and at-risk girls and boys in IDP camps and in host communities have access to quality child protection prevention and response services

Mitigation Measures

in this project TASS programs will seek to improve access to beneficiaries of the proposed interventions by scaling up services to be within a walking distance. in Far and hard to reach areas in rural settings TASS will ensure mobile teams reach in that area so as to reduce risks and hazards faced by these groups when they are accessing services. Additionally There will be a deliberate effort by TASS to ensure accountability to the target population through open, transparent and inclusive project implementation and management processes. The target beneficiaries will be highly involved in the planning, implementation and monitoring of the project activities. In addition, proper feedback mechanisms will be set in place to continuously improve on the quality of programming and ensure that TASS is responsive to the community needs. Additionally a Complaints Response and Feedback mechanism with a hotline is already available for use. The use of this system will be enhanced by making beneficiaries more aware of its existence in the proposed project locations.The protection from sexual exploitation and abuse of children is dealt with under our Child Safeguarding policy and PSEA Policy. This includes direct or indirect beneficiaries of our programming and adults in the wider communities in which we work. For anyone under 18, refer to the Child Safeguarding Policy. The PSEA policy contains essential principles and rules that reflect the behaviors and standards expected from employees, trustees, volunteers, interns, trainees, consultants, partners, and other representatives working for, or contracted on behalf of TASS in any capacity and covers our international development and humanitarian work. TASS is committed to reducing the opportunities for abuse and exploitation to happen through effective prevention, ensuring that survivors are not exposed to further harm through good reporting and responses, and ensuring that survivors receive appropriate care. Effective prevention and providing safe responses require all staff, volunteers, and intern to be clear about their individual accountabilities and responsibilities. It also requires all senior staff and managers to ensure that their working environments minimize the opportunities for sexual exploitation and abuse, and to ensure that reporting and response do not expose survivors or reporters to any further harm. TASS takes the position that early reporting of any concern supports both prevention of further abuse and safe response through the hotline available 24/7. There must be increased awareness about reporting, including the importance of reporting of any and all concerns, however apparently minor. TASS’s commitment to Zero Tolerance of sexual exploitation and abuse means all programme design and planning must include a gender power analysis, which also looks at the roles and structure of TASS staff, volunteers, interns and partners and a safeguarding risk assessment TASS has a PSEA and child safeguarding Focal Points, who is responsible for driving awareness of the policy and on how to report, inviting, receiving and collating all causes for concern. When these collected pieces of information generate a picture of a site, or a programme, or a particular person, the PSEA Focal Point must escalate the matter to trigger further inquiry and/ or investigation.
