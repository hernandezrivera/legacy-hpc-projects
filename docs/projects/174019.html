Project Summary

This proposed HOPE360 project aimed at scaling up equitable access to quality food assistance through the most appropriate modalities (in kind, voucher, cash or multi-modalities, ready to eat food kits, wet rations, dry rations) and also move forward to build livelihoods that will enable the targeted households to build resilience. The proposed project is a bridge from relief to future development interventions. This aims to move Households beyond relying mostly on aid and build capacity and resilience. through agricultural and non-agricultural livelihoods to support household food security. The resilience will further be achieved through the start-up and development of Village Savings and Loans (VSLA) model adapted to the local context and capacities,
As such, the over-all goal is To improve the most vulnerable crisis-affected people’s access to timely and appropriate food assistance, including fuel and energy related support, to meet their immediate food needs, other objectives include
• Reduced hunger of the 1,800 vulnerable crisis-affected people living in Camps and Host communities within MMC and Jere LGA who do not have access to food assistance and cannot afford for themselves.
• Improved nutrition of the 1,800 vulnerable crisis-affected people living in Camps and Host communities within MMC and Jere LGA.
• To increased food security for hunger-affected individuals and households for 1,800 most vulnerable households through both increased agricultural food production and non-agricultural income generation.

1 - Needs

The protracted crisis in North-East Nigeria due to conflict resulted in widespread displacement of more than 2 million people. Due to increased insecurity, people do not expect to return to their homes and have to remain within their temporary settlements. On the average, IDPs in the target area are displaced for almost 3 years. In Jere, the Multisectoral needs assessment conducted in September 2019, showed that expensive food is a barrier for 79% of the respondents. Jere continues to be classified as “Severe in living standards and wellbeing” measures and in over-all severity in the Humanitarian Needs Overview.The target of this intervention are the crises affected Internally displaced populations (IDPs) and host communities in MMC and Jere.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Beneficiary targeting, selection and registration exercises – HOPE360 utilizes a criteria that allows of targeting and selection of the most in need or vulnerable, The vulnerability criteria includes variables such as female and child headed households, vulnerable members such as pregnant and lactating women, elderly, PLW and chronically ill, large household members and dependency and in cases such as food security, access to land and productive assets. A socio-economic score is determined and a cut-off to determine the number. In the case of livelihood, the ability to engage in livelihood will be part of the selection criteria. This is followed by biometric registration using tablet phones. Activity 2: Community entry meetings and monthly feedback meetings. These meetings explain to the community the project and develops champions from within the community. Activity 3: Agriculture food Procurement (rice, beans, maize etc) and distribution- distribution is conducted with beneficiaries separated in smaller groups. HOPE360 distribution system through the use of color-coded cards and different distribution dates by batch of beneficiaries to avoid drawing crowds and reduce security risks as part of HOPE360 commitment in protection mainstreaming by ensuring the safety of beneficiaries at distribution sites. The integrity lay-out of distribution sites also ensures appropriate sections and priorities for vulnerable groups such as elderly and pregnant and lactating women and PLWD. Activity 4: Sensitization on Protection issues and gender based GBV. These are sessions where women and girls join focused groups discussions that identify risks, threats, vulnerabilities and align these with community capacities to mitigate or external sources for reporting. Activity 5: Post distribution monitoring, community feedback sessions and operationalizing CRM- The CRM system is operationalized on field through help desks during distribution, face to face conversations, suggestion boxes and hot lines Activity 6 End line and project evaluation
