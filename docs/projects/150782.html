Needs

For decades, Somalia has suffered numerous calamities which have resulted to persistence humanitarian crisis affected the most vulnerable groups like children, youth and women. Drought, other climatic shocks, insecurity, armed conflict, recurrent human rights violations, political instability and lack of basic services continue to drive humanitarian needs and create high levels of vulnerability among millions of Somalis. Despite the humanitarian response efforts across Somalia, numerous settlements remain cut off from any health services with women and children dying from preventable diseases.INTERSOS HMIS data analysis in Middle Shabelle, covering Jowhar, Balcad, Mahaday and Hawadley (June- September) 2018 showed, Malaria, AWD and ARI with proportional morbidity of 1.4%, 8.3% and 10.1% respectively as most prevalent diseases. A coverage of 31% calculated from the middle Shabelle target group of <1yr was realised which shows the need to expand in other districts.Bay region, Baidoa district has highest number of IDPs, with over 357 IDP camps scattered all over the town. The incoming population merge into the existing IDPs and host communities which result sharing and overstretching the existing limited health care services. Baidoa has been ranked one of the critical priority location in health and nutrition, mainly due to high number of IDPs accessing limited health services. In 2018 (June-Sept) INTERSOS HMIS in Baidoa showed Malaria, ARI, AWD with proportional morbidity of 27.3%, 9.5% and below 1% respectively.In Bakool region, Waajid districts, INTERSOS supports mobile clinic services in four camps serving a population of 98,897 of which 10.2% are IDPs. INTERSOS HMIS data (July-Sept)2018 in Waajid showed morbidity trend of Malaria, ARI, bloody diarrhoea in proportional of 28.8% ,8.8% and 2.8% respectively. INTERSOS plans to have a foothold in Banadir mainly in Daynile and Kahda district responding to IDPs needs in health integrated with other relevant sectors.

Activities

1. Support 14 Health facilities. 6 fixed PHC and 1 Hospital in Middle Shabelle (2 in Jowhar, 2 in Balad,1 in Warsheik,1 in Hawadley, 2 in Mahaday) 3 Fixed PHC in Bay (2 in Baidoa and 1 in Dinsoor), 2 Fixed PHC in Bakool (Waajid) and 2 fixed PHC in Banadir (1 in Daynile and 1 in Kahda) as per EPHS through PHC approach with a strong referral component. 2. A network of 13 Mobile health teams (each linked with fixed PHC) are deployed for outreach points, targeting U5 and PLWs. 3. Nutrition services such as OTP/SFP and IYCF/ Nutrition education are integrated in the PHCs (MCHs) and mobile clinics units to enhance therapeutic efficacy of health and nutrition services. 4. Strengthen Health information system through training staff on utilizing existing MOH/Health cluster data tools and databases, disaggregating data by age and sex to improve quality of data and enhance services for women, men and children of various age groups in line with MoH Essential package of Health Services. 5. Train and supervise medical staff on sexual and reproductive health, maternal child health, child spacing and Basic Emergency Maternal Obstetric and Neonatal Care in fixed facilities and Mobile team units.6. Referral services are strengthened through provision of ambulance services in all areas for prompt emergency treatment of critically ill prioritizing PLWs and U5. 7. Integrated surveillance, referral and epidemic response mechanisms to strengthen and promote early detection and response of outbreaks.8. 120 CHW and 24 nurses of MCHs are trained on techniques to deliver health/nutrition/protection related awareness messages and support the referral to the nearest PHCs, Mobile clinic or secondary health facility. 9. Strengthen community ownership by training community Health committees as the link between health facility and community and vice versa.10. GBV survivors referred and accessing INTERSOS health facility receive comprehensive Medical treatments in line with set standards.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

INTERSOS consults with people of diverse age, gender, ethnic, religious, and socio-economic groups, including displaced and host communities, and ensure their representation on any program committees. INTERSOS will ensure that protection is mainstreamed throughout all phases of the implementation in each sector of intervention, ensuring that the affected population has safe and dignified access to services provided in an impartial way and provided based on the population real needs through strictly following confidentiality, respect, do no harm and accountability principles. INTERSOS work to minimize any negative effect that the intervention might have on the affected population, ensuring respect for their dignity and safety with particular attention to hidden vulnerabilities (such as age and physical/mental disability) to provide services to all categories of people equitably. INTERSOS work to build the skills and knowledge of local social constructs, such as the community health committees and community health volunteers (CHV) as well as the local health staff, with a desire to instil capacity for prevention and preparedness for future emergencies and build resilience. INTERSOS create sustainability within its intervention and therefore pushes its programs to be participative and inclusive of community and existing local structures. Engagement of the communities is also ensured by training and mentoring different community groups, to increase their knowledge and capacity to continue developing their skills throughout the interventions with the aim of sustaining quality service delivery when the security situation (among others) will allow the population to return to normality. In addition, to strengthen sustainability, INTERSOS engages the ministries representatives (especially the State Ministry of Health and the State Ministry of Women Affairs and Social Development) and will continue to do so, not only for them to endorse its activities, but also to receive and grant them support by strengthening existing health system. INTERSOS integrates protection in health and nutrition in its programming, this approach aims not only to address protection needs, but also to focus on awareness raising and rights-based information sharing. In this way, the response builds resilience and reduces future risks by enhancing positive copying mechanisms for long term societal changes. A strong outreach network of community volunteers as mentioned above are trained on cross-cutting issues, including health, nutrition, WASH and protection principles and best practices enable sensitization among the targeted population while developing capacities of members of the community. In the creation of community committees, INTERSOS prioritizes and will continue to prioritize gender parity, as well as a representative inclusion of older people, disabled people and youth.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

INTERSOS strongly believe in the importance of gender equality in humanitarian assistance, and recognize that men, boys, girls and women have specific needs and constraints. In Somalia, Displaced Women and Girls have been particularly exposed to violence. The needs of women and young girls remains unmet and INTERSOS will continue involving the vulnerable groups at all stages of the project cycle. INTERSOS put into consideration recruitment, training and deployment both male and female staff with varying age group, to create a free interaction between a health care provider and the clients. Participatory sessions will be used to access and incorporate gender equality and age perspectives in all aspects of the project cycle, with a specific focus on gender during the implementation phases, with community discussion and assessments mandatorily conducted with groups of men and groups of women. As well, protection mainstreaming and referral pathways among the partners will be ensured throughout the intervention. The implementation of this project is aligned with cluster objectives of saving lives and the desire to help mitigate and respond to public health shocks through provision of quality health services in emergency. INTERSOS using the mobile teams will reach out to the IDPs and the marginalised communities to provide basic health care and refer cases whenever necessary.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Limited access to safe, effective reproductive health services has adversely impacted the well-being of women in reproductive health group. Cultural practices of marrying off girls at a young age is one area worth noting in Somalia context. The presence of sexual and gender-based violence is also a problem, despite the low reporting due to stigma and lack of information about the existence of services. Targeting beneficiaries most in need but with no access to a functional health facility, INTERSOS, work with local leaders to develop specific service sites considering the safety and security of both staff and the most vulnerable group like women, children and the elderly. INTERSOS is providing outreach/mobile centres to ensure that caregivers do not need to walk more than 5 km seeking for services. In fixed centres, INTERSOS ensure construction of ramps and other disabled-friendly physical facilitations during renovations for easy access of a wider population to the facilities. First is making sure that vulnerable people have access to health services by making structural modifications during design and building. Building a ramp makes it easier for disabled people to enter health facility, toilets and other building. Female health workers are recruited, trained and deployed appropriately to allow for culturally appropriate and ethnically representative members for women, girls, men and boys. INTERSOS will ensure that during the outreach activities, CHW/CHVs targets older persons and persons with disabilities by enrolling representatives from the vulnerable categories as part of the community volunteer network to ensure higher representation and include all other identified vulnerable groups like single mothers, young mothers, people with disabilities. INTERSOS will consult with people of diverse age, gender, ethnic, religious, and socio-economic groups, including displaced and host communities, and ensure their representation on any program committees.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The project will Involve and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to protection needs specifically by consulting women, men, youth, persons with disabilities, older persons and marginalised groups to understand their needs, coping strategies and preferences for protection services.Engage the community and committee representative to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes that affect the targeted population, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response.Promote and support self-protection capacities and avoid persons resorting to negative coping strategies to mitigate risks. Reinforce the capacity of the community and family to act as a protective layer for children against violations.INTERSOS will set up accessible, confidential and locally understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improving programming, understanding community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct eg. Feedback box in centres. A strong feedback mechanism will enhance the capacity of INTERSOS Staff to prevent challenges and address areas of the project weakness thus improving risks management in relation of the delivery of services.INTERSOS will provide reproductive health services and treatment to victims of violence (CMR and other GBV related injuries), beside the increased access to services avoiding risks due to the distance to services through use of mobile team in locations far from fixed health facilities. INTERSOS will offering information about availability of protection services or even, a referral system to those services.
