Project Summary

(i) Provide effective coordination by strengthening area based coordination and leadership, reinforcing sub-national effectiveness and linkages between the various coordination and working group levels from ICCG in the capital and intersectoral working groups in the regions to HCT level engagements with the Government; (ii) Promote humanitarian access in a complex operating environment, where the security situation remains volatile; (iii) Strengthen civil-military coordination at the regions and capital levels to improve response preparedness, rapid and safe delivery of humanitarian assistance, and foster enhanced predictability of civil-military coordination processes and platforms; (iv) foster greater accountability of the humanitarian response, raising awareness of the crisis, promoting best practices and international law, and bringing the voices of affected people to the forefront; (v) support locally-led identification of strategic priorities through the Flagship project, promote HDP collaboration which will inform the multi-year Humanitarian Response Strategy framework (2023-2025) (vi) advocate for principled humanitarian action by relevant authorities and sharing its coordination expertise based on global best practices. This will include an enhanced interface and support to the Government of Niger, especially the Ministry of Humanitarian Affairs; (vii) Enhance partnerships with existing and new humanitarian donors through improved bilateral and multilateral engagement; (viii) Increased accountability of the humanitarian system to meet humanitarian needs.

1 - Besoins

Niger is in the heart of the Sahel, he has a poorly diversified economy, dependent on agriculture for 40% of its GDP. The level of extreme poverty is expected to reach 44.1% in 2023 due to negative per capita growth and rising inflation . Increased activities of non-state armed groups in the country and neighboring areas in Mali, Burkina Faso, and Nigeria has led to severe protection concerns. More than 3,030 protection incidents were recorded from January to October 2023, resulting in more than 4,800 casualties. While the majority concerns physical violence, kidnappings and extorsion, violence against children remains highly problematic . 2023 was also marked by the deterioration of the security situation in the regions of Tillabéri, Tahoua and Diffa. Due to insecurity, 989 schools are closed nation-wide affecting 84 500 pupils. The insecurity has resulted in huge population movements across the country as 733 000 people are forced to move. 291 000 refugees are fleeing from violence in neighboring countries, and 442 000 people are internally displaced. Food insecurity is a long-standing issue affecting every year millions of people. The “Cadre Harmonisé” of November 2023 has estimated 3,2 million people will be in food insecure (phases 3 and 4) during the lean season (June-August 2024) or almost 12,36 percent of the entire population. Furthermore, there are 258, 401 severely malnourished children out of 2.3 million people affected by acute malnutrition. The global acute malnutrition rate among children under five reached 12.2 percent in 2022. Natural chocs, such as desertification, unpredictable rainfall (causing droughts and floodings), and systematic deforestation, often aggravated by the effects of climate change, further increase the pressure on households. The 2023 floodings in Niger affected almost 169,500 people while 52 humans lost their lives. As a result of the volatile security situation, the already precarious socio-economic and political situation is further aggravated. Access to essential social services, health, education, and markets become more limited. In addition, increased costs of imported products due to security concerns, border closures, and financial sanctions implemented by the central bank of west African states (BCEAO) and geopolitical events such as the war in Ukraine, lead to soaring food prices on the local market. After years of systematic poverty, the resilience of households to deal with such price fluctuations is limited. Therefore, loss of purchasing power is one of the biggest humanitarian risks in Niger. The consequences of escalating violence, climate change, and persistent socio-economic challenges lead to a broad range of complex humanitarian needs in Niger. Despite the strong presence of humanitarian actors, the population in need increased from 1.9 million people in 2017 to 4.6 million in 2024. With no immediate improvement foreseen soon, humanitarian needs will likely continue to increase.The humanitarian community continues to rely heavily on OCHA's coordination functions, primarily strategic coordination at the capital Niamey and operational coordination at region levels, information management, access, and civil-military coordination. The challenging and volatile security situation limited this work in certain areas of Tillabery, Diffa, Maradi, and Tahoua regions. There is no indication of a trend reversal due to the complexity and interconnectivity of the root causes, the prolonged political crisis, the sanctions that beyond 2023. The efforts to address the humanitarian crisis–though somehow significant can be improved.
