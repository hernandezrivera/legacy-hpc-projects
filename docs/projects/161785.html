Project Summary

FHI 360’s Integrated Humanitarian Assistance to Northeast Nigeria project aims to contribute to reducing morbidity and mortality and improve the wellbeing of conflict-affected and displaced Nigerians through the provision of multi-sector humanitarian response in Borno State, Nigeria. The program has the following core objectives:
1. To improve access to critical Primary Healthcare (PHC), reproductive health, and health outreach services in conflict-affected and displaced communities.
2. To improve the wellbeing of vulnerable individuals through increased access to psychosocial support services and integrated gender-based violence (GBV) and child protection support.
3. To improve the wellbeing of IDPs and host communities through access to clean and safe water, improved sanitation facilities, and improved hygiene practices.
4. To improve the nutritional status of the most vulnerable groups, including pregnant and lactating women (PLW) and children under five, in both the IDP and the host community populations.
The project targets 150,000 individuals affected in the target areas – Banki (Bama), Damasak (Mobbar), Dikwa, and Ngala.
With support from OFDA, FHI 360 currently implements the following activities, delivering targeted interventions that are designed to meet the most pressing needs of IDPs in Dikwa, Ngala, Banki and Damasak. Key activities will include:
• continued support to five clinics in the form of medical commodities and staff training to facilitate primary health care (PHC) and sexual and reproductive health (SRH) services, among others;
• training, provision of outreach materials/supplies, and supportive supervision to Community Health Workers (CHEWs) to enable them to resume community outreach;
• establishment of health outposts in each location to reduce the caseload on static health facilities
• support to safe spaces with case management and psychosocial support (PSS) services for survivors of gender-based violence;
• support to safe healing and learning spaces with case management and PSS services for children aged 3-17 and child protection community-based mechanisms to identify and respond to child protection concerns
• rehabilitation/installation of essential WASH infrastructure, including latrines/bathing facilities, and solar-powered boreholes;
• distribution of hygiene kits coupled with hygiene promotion;
• sanitation interventions, such as installation of waste points, drainage interventions in flood-prone areas and clean-up campaigns, to reduce the environmental health risks;
• support for optimal infant and young child feeding (IYCF) with integrated multi-sectoral interventions
• training of CHWs and health workers and support for them to provide appropriate services for the management of SAM
FHI 360’s nutrition, health, protection, and WASH activities will continue to comprise a fully integrated package to promote comprehensive support to IDPs, thus reducing risks of morbidity and mortality and contributing to improved wellbeing of displaced and conflict-affected individuals in Borno State.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation remains critical in northeast Nigeria, with an estimated 7 million people in need of humanitarian assistance, and more than 1,926,748 internally displaced persons (IDPs) from 364,635 households. This represents an increase of more than 8,000 people since June 2018. Borno State alone hosts more than 1,441,635 IDPs – approximately 75% of the total number of IDPs in the northeast – 655,728 returnees. Numbers of IDPs, new arrivals and returnees are increasing. In Borno State, the highest increase was observed in Bama LGA where the number went up by 16 per cent, from 52,911 to 61,473 persons. The increase was due to arrivals from inaccessible locations in the LGA including Soye, Gulumba and Goniri wards, as well as people returning from Cameroon into situations of secondary displacement in Bama. Similarly, Gwoza and Ngala recorded increases of 3,468 and 3,462 persons respectively, because of the arrival of people from inaccessible LGAs, military operations and returning refugees. Displacement of populations means they cannot access services in their home areas. It can also mean that those previously providing services, such as health workers, have also been displaced and are unable to continue working. Coupled with limited access and increased attacks by armed opposition groups (AOGs), the result has been low levels of essential, lifesaving services. Humanitarian assistance remains critical as more areas are coming under GoN control, and thus be-coming accessible to relief actors. Life expectancy and key health indices are poor in the northeast region, and the situation is constantly exacerbated and complicated by the insurgency, due to disruptions in the health system and other social inequities. Many skilled health care workers have been displaced through migration. Health commodities/medical supplies are in short supply, due to weak logistics systems or increasing security threats along supply routes outside of Maiduguri. Large populations in the northeast remain dependent on assistance to meet their food needs as they face crisis (IPC Phase 3), while populations in hard to reach areas with severely limited access to livelihood activities and poorly functioning markets are facing emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes. Significant areas in the northeast remain inaccessible to humanitarian actors, and it is likely that the situation is similar or worse than in adjoining accessible areas. FHI 360’s IHANN II project focusing on mitigating the risk of heightened vulnerability, spread of disease and increased mortality brought on by poor WASH conditions, lack of available health services acute malnutrition and protection concerns.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

FHI 360 has set up a Complaint and Feedback Mechanism system linked to all sectors to received feedback from the community members. The various channel set up include help desk, FGD, suggestion boxes and community session which are incorporated into project activities. These feedbacks are received during activities such as Hygiene kit distribution, medical visit to the health facility and during community meeting and outreaches. Also, suggestion boxes are placed at startegic locations within the communities to receive feedback from members of the community.
