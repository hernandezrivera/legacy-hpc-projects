Project Summary

IRC will continue to provide lifesaving quality and timely services to GBV survivors through its Women Protection and Empowerment Program in integration with other sectors in Juba, Koch, Aweil East, Rumbek, Rubkona, Rotriak, Panijiyar and Renk. The project will target a total of 20,860 beneficiaries including 6800 Refugees/Returnees, 7750 Host community and 6310 IDPs at the target locations. IRC has a qualified and trained GBV response and prevention team that will continue to provide services at the grassroot level. For GBV response, the team will improve the quality of the existing and new women and girls’ safe spaces at all target locations. IRC will provide group psychosocial support to at risk women and girls through various recreational and skill building activities to facilitate their healing process. IRC will also engage the women and girls who have completed the sessions to lead the activities at the safe space. All interventions will be guided by the interests and needs of women and girls. Additionally, the team will provide comprehensive GBV case management services to survivors guided by interagency case management guidelines. Along with regular individual psychosocial support sessions, the IRC response team will lead the process of case management to ensure the survivor will receive all the services as per the need assessment. The case management information will be stored, managed and analyzed through GBVIMS+. The trend analysis will be shared with relevant agencies on regular basis to observe GBV trends, available services and gaps in services. IRC will establish and update the multisectoral integrated referral pathways to strengthen coordination with GBV service Providers at national and state level. IRC will also focus on economic empowerment of women and girls to build resilience and enhance decision making at home and in community.
For GBV Prevention, IRC will closely work with the communities including women, girls, men and boys. WPE team will work through local staff for awareness raising in the community through sessions, seminars, door to door campaigns and IEC material in local and easy to understand language. IRC will also engage men and boys through its behavior change interventions. Through its GBV risk mitigation interventions, IRC will identify and mitigate the GBV risk through conducting safety audits by engaging women, girls and other relevant stakeholders. IRC will also distribute material support and dignity kits as per the needs of women and girls. IRC will engage persons will disabilities through accessible GBV services. The staff will provide information in accessible and local language through sessions and door-to-door visits. The safe spaces will be made accessible by taking any special measures if required. IRC will provide training to front line workers on sign language by engaging in country experts or local partners.
IRC will focus on localization guided by its Strategic Action Plan and will engage local partners including CSOs, relevant ministries and community leaders. IRC will build the capacities of its 4 local partners including Active Youth Agency (AYA), Women for Change, Voice of Change and Humane-Aid for Community Organization (HACO) along with new partners under WPE program for provision of quality services to at risk women and girls. IRC will also mainstream gender and GBV information in other sectors including Health, Education, WASH, Nutrition, Child Protection and Protection Rule of Law and will coordinate with them to provide integrated and coordinated services to at risk population.

1. Needs

Gender-based violence (GBV) is the most critical threat to the wellbeing of women and girls in South Sudan. It is driven by harmful social norms and systemic gender inequality, severely impacting their safety and rights. Emergencies exacerbate these existing gender inequalities and discrimination within communities. Currently, South Sudan faces multiple crises, including climate change, disease outbreaks, and an influx of refugees from Sudan. Refugees and returnees continue to enter South Sudan from various points, increasing pressure on the already limited resources, thereby worsening the situation and heightening protection risks for women and girls.According to the HNRP guide, the Protection Risk Monitoring System reported GBV as the main protection risk in 38% of the 2,497 communities surveyed in 2024. The ISNA revealed that 36% of women and girls avoid certain areas due to safety concerns, while 53% of households face barriers in accessing vital services. Additionally, a survey of GBV experts found a limited availability of core GBV services in 35% of counties. Multilayer data analysis estimates that 2.75 million people will need GBV interventions in 2025 due to increased risk.The safety audit conducted by IRC in various counties, including Panijiyar, Bentiu, Renk, Koch, Juba, and Ruweng administrative areas, indicates an increased risk of physical and emotional violence against women and girls. The reports show that intimate partner violence and domestic violence are rooted in the lack of basic needs and psychological abuse, leading to misunderstandings and conflicts within families. Women and adolescent girls highlighted sexual harassment and violence directed towards them by men and boys while fetching firewood and at tap stands during evening hours. They also reported risks of early marriage and exploitation due to a lack of basic services, poor living conditions, lack of family support, and lack of education opportunities in the TCs.GBV services are often inaccessible, unsafe, non-confidential, or inadequate due to barriers such as lack of information and services. Mobility barriers, including limited access to assistive devices like wheelchairs, white canes, and crutches, restrict their ability to receive cash assistance and safely access services. Institutional barriers include limited access to information, delays in receiving services, and limited opportunities for participation in decision-making. Safety and security concerns relate to physical safety, lack of disability-friendly wash facilities, lack of dignified shelters and private spaces, and limited mobility to surrounding facilities like markets and hospitals.

2. Project activities

IRC will continue to provide quality GBV response services, risk mitigation and prevention intervention targeting the crisis affected populations among communities including IDPs, Returnees and host communities through static and mobile facilities.IRC will continue to provide lifesaving quality services to at risk women and girls including GBV survivors. With support of the technical team at national and regional level, the frontline workers will provide case management services to the GBV survivors following survivor centered approach. IRC will ensure the service provision will be in coordination with other service providers in line with the multisector approach comprises of medical, legal, safety and psychosocial support. IRC will also provide self-care training to the frontline for stress management. All incidents reported will be captured in the GBVIMS upon the consent of each survivor and the team will ensure satisfaction of GBV survivors. Baseline and follow up assessments will be conducted based on outcome scales to measure psychosocial wellbeing of survivors. This will contribute in improving the services for the survivors. Additionally, IRC will provide quarterly reviews and updating of the referral pathways as well as continuous feedback sessions to ensure the pathways are functional across the proposed locations. The women and girls’ safe spaces will serve as avenues of empowerment for women and girls with a major focus on provision of contextualized psychosocial support interventions which seek to support in the healing, recovery and building resilience of women and girls. The Women and Girls Safe Space toolkit will remain instrumental in providing guidance on the operationalization of the safe spaces especially in ensuring accountability as well as encouraging the women-girls led processes and ownership. Structured sessions will also be conducted following the Updated Girl Shine and Women Rise Manual. Girl Shine curriculum will focus on provision of life skill education to adolescent girls while Women Rise focuses on provision of psychosocial support to at risk women including GBV survivors. IRC will also focus on skill building activities, Village Saving Loan Associations and income generation activities to build resilience among at risk women and girls and to enhance their participation at family and at community level. Through its prevention interventions, IRC will continuously engage women and girls and the community at large to identify barriers in accessing the GBV services and collaboratively work together to address. Community leaders will be engaged through training and dialogues to equip them with knowledge and skills for safe referrals. GBV prevention IRC will engage communities through SASA! Together and EMAP+ as the primary GBV prevention approaches. The two approaches seek to provoke discussions on Power Imbalance as the root cause of violence while analyzing negative social norms, beliefs, behavior, and practices that contribute to violence against women and girls. Through EMAP+, the trained community EMAP+ facilitators (male and female) will lead the community engagement activities as well as facilitation of the EMAP sessions. SASA! Together activities will be collaboratively implemented through the community SASA activist who will be trained at the project inception to ensure community ownership and sustainability of the engagement even after the project phases out. IRC will work closely with women and community led structures to carry out GBV safety audits where action points to address the identified risks and concerns will be tasked to the relevant actors. Findings from the safety audits will inform other interventions such as training on Fuel Efficient Stoves for the women as well as production of reusable sanitary materials with the locally available raw materials. In coordination with the GBV AoR IRC will distribute dignity kits to the women and girls of reproductive age with MHM sessions.
