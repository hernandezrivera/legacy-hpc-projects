Project Summary

The objective of this project is to enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock support for households classified in Crisis (IPC 3) or worse food insecurity in Panyijiar(Unity), Aweil East (NBeG), and Rumbek Center(Lakes) Counties. IRC will provide immediate, short term to recovery livelihood protection support to the food insecure and vulnerable households beginning 2020 lean season. To augment household recovery and resilience, the program will mainstream conflict sensitivity and disaster risk reduction support to at-risk households focused on improving crop, livestock and fisheries production and bolstering resilience towards recurrent shocks in the community. Cash-based activities will be used to boost vulnerable households’ income and purchasing power while engaging in rehabilitation and building household and community productive and disaster risk mitigation assets. In pursuit of program objectives, the following activities will be implemented: (a) Engagement community and local leaders to confirm needs and agree on selection criteria and register beneficiaries. (b)Distribute emergency (short term maturing) agricultural inputs/kits (seeds and tools) through input trade fairs (ITF) to farmers including in-kind pipelines from FAO. ITFs will be utilized to promote restocking (sheep and goats assets) and backyard poultry targeting households with malnourished mothers and children and most vulnerable households including flood and conflict-affected female-headed households and the elderly. (c) Market informed Cash-Based Activities targeting rehabilitation of community prioritize economically viable assets supported through conditional cash transfer activities providing income opportunities and boosting the purchasing power of food insecurity households and the use vouchers to access needed inputs- seeds and tools and livestock during ITFs or food. (d) Capacity building to county-level extension agents (CAHWs, AEA), crop/pastoralists and fisherfolks on sustainable production skills, post-harvest handling technologies and climate-smart agronomy demonstration gardens set up within the community. Value addition and marketing shall be stimulated through improved post-harvest handling, proper storage at household and community level – including granaries, fish preservation skills and local cereal market structures established through cash-based interventions. In collaboration with FAO and local government, mass livestock vaccinations are envisaged for households restocked and in suspected disease outbreaks. The IRC will mainstream gender equity and promote gender inclusiveness, allowing for the most vulnerable women, girls, boys, and men to participate and realize benefits for their wellbeing. Participatory monitoring and evaluation will be boosted through routine consultation of community, local leaders and authority from start. Beneficiary identification and selection (BIS) include agreeing on affirmative action to register at least 70% of female beneficiaries under the project. An inclusive community-led project feedback and complaint mechanism consisting of a committee’ set up to foster equal chances for female and male beneficiaries to voice their complaints and feedback. Complementarity will be promoted through integration (nutrition, protection, and health) including registration of referred vulnerable persons facing intersectional needs e.g- chronic illness and diseases, acute food insecurity, malnutrition, and flooding. Overall the IRC will reach 33,825 (17,759 Women, 57,610 Men, 5,919 Girls, 2,537 Boys; 10% of the most vulnerable households’ classified in IPC Phase 3 or worse food insecurity in Panyijiar (7,188), Rumbek Canter (9,237) and Aweil East (17,400). By program type: 33,825 through ITFs, 6,765 through cash vouchers, 16,913 through production skills training (50%), 5,500 through restocking and livestock vaccination and 18,000 through fisheries support.

1 - Needs

By September 2019, over 6.35 million people (54% of the population) faced a crisis or worse acute food insecurity in South Sudan with an estimated 10, 000 people in catastrophe. While the situation is expected to slightly improve, acute food insecurity is expected to prevail into 2020 lean season with over 5.5 million (47% of the total population) people in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity by April 2020, all in the presence of planned humanitarian food assistance (September 2019 IPC Report). Across the country, an estimated 234,800 people are reported to be affected by the floods in the past months with 132,800 people in Jonglei, 52,000 people in NBeG, nearly 37,000 people in Unity, some 8,000 people in Eastern Equatoria and 5,000 people in Upper Nile (OCHA- September 2019), with associated assets destruction projected harvest reduction in main productive counties. Additionally, Fall Army Worm (FAW) destroyed leafy cereal crops and is expected to negatively impact the 2019 agricultural season harvest which further worsened the anticipated drop in food harvest benefits of the season in targeted locations. Livestock assets have reduced in size due to livestock diseases (Including Foot and Mouth Disease) and raids, further exacerbating the food insecurity of dependent livelihoods of the households. Across the three targeted counties, fishing prevails as a viable livelihood and food source however, the limited appropriate fishing gear and poor preservation techniques have impacted benefits. Moreover, the low purchasing power of household have resulted in poor coping capacities and negatively impacted the livelihoods of vulnerable households and led to a deteriorating community resilience index. Overall protracted conflicts have affected ethnic groups across the country causing displacements and limiting access to farmland and with deteriorating harvest as productive assets are depleted-e.g livestock assets and other livelihoods. Although counties like Aweil East, Panyijiar remain stable, they are extremely vulnerable to the worsening economic crisis and disruption of normal trade including livestock and influx of IDPs. Hyper-inflation (now estimated above 75%) drives high food prices and cost of living - a situation that is contributing to erratic movements within and out of the country in search of income and food. Most poor and vulnerable displaced persons without kinship, elderly, disabled, children under five years of age, widows, and female-headed households are worst affected. Across the three counties, youth remain a concern as they are lured into crime and inter/intra conflicts (Rumbek) fighting, lack incentives to cultivate or run IGAs, face limited employment opportunities and are most cases excluded from a stake in the future of the country. Furthermore, access improvements following the peace process have increased numbers of vulnerable persons in hard to reach areas with climatic shocks (floods) exacerbating situations. As such affected communities of Aweil East, Rumbek Center and Panyijiar are in dire need of emergency food security and livelihood support. Cognizant of the cautious hopes for peace, inter-communal conflict and displacement within the country, coupled with returnees from bordering countries such as Sudan has created more demand for assistance and livelihoods. Also, factions not a signatory to the agreement may further conflict and disrupt livelihoods and propel poor economic conditions and food insecurity. The currently volatile economy will worsen given the population now 82% ‘officially poor’ continues to receive significant returnees leading to increased numbers of vulnerable persons facing multiple shocks.

2 - Activities

Below are a series of activities to be implemented during the seasonal calendar of 2020 in Aweil East, Panyijiar and Rumbek Center. The IRC will exercise program integration with Nutrition and WASH projects including and during beneficiary selection through referrals, data triangulation and sharing, reporting and follow up of beneficiaries receiving integrated support- e.g households with children and mothers under treatment for malnutrition -referred from the Nutrition projects or protection cluster within the counties. a)Engagement and consultation of community and local leaders to sensitize them (Commissioners, Senior Agricultural Inspectors, Paramount Chiefs, Executive Directors and Payam Administrators), Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC) and community with stakeholders at the ministry and state level about the project. The beneficiary selection committee will also be formed through these meetings. b)Identification of vulnerable households and registration of beneficiaries for ITFs. These will follow the standard stages of registration and verification processes. A client complaint and feedback structure will also be formed and led by the community themselves, capacity building training on how to handle complaints and ensure participatory monitoring of the program will be given to the committees formed. c) Conduct Input Trade Fairs and Distribute inputs (including pipelines from FAO) to vulnerable households. The IRC will organize and conduct agricultural input trade fairs (ITFs) to ensure beneficiaries access seeds, tools, fishing gear and restocking support. The Input Trade Fairs will include identification and registration of beneficiaries and local seed dealers, holding the Input Trade Fairs, payment to seed dealers and conduct of post-distribution monitoring exercises. IRC may also distribute other pipeline inputs provided by FAO directly to vulnerable households. d) Conduct cash-based interventions: IRC will work with community leaders and representatives to prioritize key/economically viable productive assets for cash-based interventions. Targeted households in IPC phases 3 and 4, will be engaged in the rehabilitation of the community to prioritized assets through conditional cash voucher activities. Agricultural and livelihood assets such as local grain storage facilities for seeds, fish preservation innovations, livestock rearing pens (goat and sheep pens/shades), community market footpaths and market places, water sources and vegetable demonstrations will be established at both community, group and household level. Selected beneficiaries capable of providing daily labor during public works will be engaged in communal assets development and upon completion of tasks will be provided with cash vouchers to access seeds, food and other prioritized needs of the beneficiaries. e) In order to build local adaptive and absorptive capacity of target households and the community as well as enhance production and marketing of agricultural products, IRC will identify and train community based extension agents (Community Animal Health Workers and Agricultural Extension Agents) who will in turn train target beneficiaries and continue to provide extension support to them. Livestock mass vaccinations will also be provided to households in collaboration with country and state leadership and FAO programs. f) IRC will deploy a team of technical staff to directly implement and monitor the project activities in the field. Through the engagement of beneficiaries and local authorities, participatory monitoring and evaluation of the project will be exercised using Gender Inclusive Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms (GICFM). Payam level project monitoring committees and routine joint field monitoring exercises will be organized by the project manager. M&E systems will use data collection and tracking through standard tools for the livelihoods sector and conduct PDMs and project baselines and end-line evaluations.

6 - Can the project be linked to durable solutions?

Yes

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes, within the frameworks of humanitarian principles and laws for which IRC is a member and often providing services to those in need. This will further include child rights with zero tolerance to child labor and exploitation.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes, at least 10% of conflict and disaster (flood) affected IDPs will be targeted by the program.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, the project targets engagement of county-level extension agents and CAHWs through an incentive approach. Tailored capacity building will be given to selected agents and supported during the project process. These agents will remain to provide more extension services through a cost recovery system (in-kind barter or cash) from progressive farmers.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, the program is aligned to both the National Refugee Return Framework and the South Sudan Humanitarian Response Plan 2020 SO3. The project will enable vulnerable people to recover from crisis, seek solutions to displacement, and build resilience to acute shocks and chronic stresses through targeted programming to support coping capacities and livelihoods in prioritized areas. The project also takes into account protection principles for the vulnerable refugees, IDPs and host communities targeted in Panyijiar, Rumbek Center and Aweil East.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Through a case-based approach, the project will engage the community to develop community prioritized productive assets such as footpaths, market places, storage granaries and including disaster risk-related structures such as water channels, dykes where applicable. Disaster risk mitigation structures will ensure the community can mitigate the effects of recurrent floods, hence minimizing impact and reducing production loss, this directly contributes to long term solutions and resilience of the community and reducing on displacements related to flooding.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, the project entails both launch at start and closeout meetings at the end of the project period with community members and leaders. It also targets building the capacity of county extension agents who in turn will continue to support households within the community. Furthermore, community structures rehabilitated through cash for work will continue to serve them in support of agricultural production, marketing, and risk mitigation.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

The IRC through community leaders will work with UNMISS and other actors tasked with demining to assess cash for worksites and ensure explosives are not present. IRC will also sensitize local authorities and leaders about the project and level expectations including advocacy on no taxation of aid. UNOCHA and other partners may also be brought in the loop if such actions are realized and collective action is taken to avert the situation with concerned local governments. Meanwhile, gender-sensitive beneficiaries' complaint mechanisms will be set to provide feedback and channel complaints from clients. In planning for Cash for Work activities, IRC will consult both women and men and ensure participatory planning and scheduling of works at a time when women are free and can comfortably participate in the public works. protection centers and shelters will also be set for lactating women and clients to refresh, breastfeed their children and where nannies can sit with babies. shelters will be provided for places of convenience during the cash for work activities.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

IRC has developed a gender-sensitive feedback and complaint mechanism for projects which ensures both male and female committee are selected by beneficiaries into the feedback mechanism led by the community. this approach ensures both genders are free to share critical information with those who they feel comfortable with- e.g Women may prefer talking to women about their problems as some men are intensive. The model will be adapted to include other special groups such as youths, disabled, etc and capacity building given to the committees so that they handle complaints and feedback with utmost confidentiality and professionalism without causing harm. The program will manage a complaint and feedback log sheet from which program teams follow up cases. other multiple complaint desks manned by both male and female field monitors will also be used. Referral of cases to the GBV and protection program will be exercised. process monitoring of field activities by staff and jointly with local leaders will be done and ensure targeted feedback is sought from special groups.
