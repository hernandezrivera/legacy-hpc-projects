Project Summary

An integrated protection, HLP, GBV, CP and MHPSS approach to support vulnerable displaced, returnee and affected host communities to provide critical protection response to meet immediate as well as longer term durable solutions. The protection environment will be improved through collaboration with government, other humanitarian actors, community leaders and affected communities.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

This project aims to provide critical protection services and assistance to forcibly displaced persons, affected members of the host community and returnees in Northern Mozambique. Women, adolescent girls, elder persons, persons with disabilities and other persons specific needs will be specifically targeted through interventions in the following areas: Protection monitoring: Protection Monitoring teams will bolster the collection of sex and age disaggregated data and provide evidence-based analysis to inform essential and lifesaving inter-agency protection and multisectoral interventions. UNHCR-led protection monitoring reports will serve to inform the wider humanitarian response, including the GBV and child protection response, ensuring that protection remains central to all interventions. Through protection monitoring, protection cases and vulnerable people will be identified, including women, girls, and people with specific needs (older people, people with disabilities, etc.) and referrals will be made to relevant service providers.Access to documentation: UNHCR and partners will facilitate and support access to civil documentation in prioritized districts to improve access to essential life-saving services and humanitarian assistance, and to reduce barriers in exercising the basic right to freedom of movement, including for women and adolescent girls. Having documentation serves to reduce protection risks such as extortion, physical abuse and harassment, unlawful and/ or arbitrary detention, sexual abuse, etc. at check points; it reduces risks of forced labour, human trafficking and facilitates access to employment, education and other available essential services; it supports reintegration and land tenure; and ensures equity before the law and to seek legal remedy.Provision of support to people with specific needs: Support will be provided to people with specific needs, including single/female headed households, separated and unaccompanied children, people in need of rehabilitation, people with disabilities, and older people at risk women and men (e.g., through the provision of assistive devices such as wheelchairs, crutches, etc. and community-based rehabilitation services, referral of services including mental health and psycho-social (MHPSS) targeted support).2. GBV prevention, risk mitigation and response: Awareness raising, integrated case management (CM), MHPSS and legal support will be provided to survivors and persons at risk of GBV and SEA.MHPSS support will include individual counselling, family counselling, psychological first aid, screening for MH disorders, referral to MH health services, case follow up (Case Management - CM) with MH health services in hospitals, support with travel, medication and other related costs to access MH care. Legal aid is comprised of legal awareness raising and signposting to government services as well as follow up of cases with government institution and the prosecutor, and provision of legal personal identification documents through referral to UNHCR’s partner, UCM. GBV and SEA survivors are also referred using a survivor-centred approach to health, safety and secondary assistance options according to availability (food assistance, shelter, NFIs, Livelihoods, etc.) to make sure the most at risk are provided with comprehensive support in in a safe way.Disaster Risk Reduction

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

UNHCR works with government institutions, implementing partners, wider protection partners including local civil society, and the displaced community to provide enhanced protection according to the following areas: - Protection monitoring (PM): Collect protection data from 50,000 +32,496 = 82, 496 HHs (IDP and returnees) in all accessible districts, to (i) produce external PM reports that inform protection programming and advocacy efforts, and (ii) facilitate the identification and referral for services of PSNs.- Access to documentation and legal aid: Support access to documentation (birth certificates and IDs) to the displaced and host community (i) safeguard rights and facilitate access services/assistance, and to (ii) mitigate protection risks associated with lack of documentation. Provision of legal aid (legal counselling, individual legal assistance/representation).- Support to people with specific needs (PSNs) and community-based protection: Provision of life-saving support, including MHPSS and PSS, to PSNs, especially people with disabilities, and older people at risk. Strengthening of community protection structures including Protection Focal Points and community spaces. - CP: safely identify child protection needs and link with quality care, supporting monitoring of child protection environment, supporting MRM. - GBV: response through integrated GBV services (case management, MHPSS, legal support), community led risk reduction across the humanitarian response, and gender transformative prevention models.- Disaster Risk Reduction: Build resilience in DRRM by mainstreaming to affected communities through capacity building to (i) District technical Committees on DRRM and (ii) Local Committees on DRRM as well as through the Women Safe Spaces and Child Friendly Spaces.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

UNHCR as the protection mandated agency ensures the centrality of protection throughout all its work. Equally, UNHCR supports protection mainstreaming across the wider humanitarian response through providing a variety of data and evidence, collected from affected communities in a safe and ethical manner, to better inform protection risk reduction and response across all humanitarian actors. UNHCR Protection Monitoring, participatory assessment, GBV Safety Audits, community consultations, Protection assessment missions, complaints and feedback mechanisms (CFM), and regular project monitoring in the field all allow for the identification of potential risks which are shared internally within UNHCR sector programs and with the relevant Clusters. To ensure protection integration UNHCR provides tailored training and regular specialist Protection technical support, by expert Protection staff, to UNHCR program staff and partners, as well as other humanitarian actors including local government institutions and civil society, on protection mainstreaming across their programs. UNHCR also conducts advocacy to promote the centrality of Protection across the response including through regular coordination with key stakeholders and advocacy notes.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Across all interventions, UNHCR's Policy on Age, Gender and Diversity (AGD Policy) (2018) aims to ensure that all displaced and affected populations have equitable and non-discriminatory access to protection and assistance programmes, and a say in decisions that affect their lives through employing participatory methodologies, guided by a number of tools and documents developed at the global level to support field offices that are used in the planning, design, implementation and monitoring and evaluation stages. The GBV component of this particular project specifically targets women and girls who are survivors or at risk of GBV to raise awareness about their rights and where to seek services, to provide survivor-centred response assistance and to make safe referrals to other services, including livelihoods programming that specifically targets women to empower them and make them more resilient and self-reliant. The project will also build the capacity of national (state and civil society) service providers in the interest of sustainability and to advocate for inclusion of the affected population into existing systems. This project also targets inclusion of persons with disabilities and elder persons (who often live with mobility challenges), and provides assistive devises. The civil documentation component of this project facilitates freedom of movement and access to services and humanitarian assistance. UNHCR will also work closely with the partners to disseminate information on already existing safe, accessible and confidential feedback and complaints mechanisms (CFM). These will include anonymous feedback surveys on services, complaints/suggestion boxes at distribution sites, phone lines and designation of community-based focal points to receive and respond to complaints. UNHCR will involve women and men of different ages and abilities from the affected communities in design and implementation of these mechanisms and will organize awareness raising sessions on their functioning and consider a jointly designed feedback mechanism with other sectors to streamline referral of cases.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will work with affected communities to actively engage persons with disabilities in the program design and implementation. UNHCR is the co-chair of the Persons with Disabilities (PWD) Working Group and thus is coordinating integration across the response. PWD will be consulted throughout the program, especially during design to ensure inclusion and accessibility. Programs will be designed to be accessible and protection referral pathways, as well as direct services provided by UNHCR, will include persons with disabilities specialized services to improve access to support. UNHCR trains staff and community volunteers on supporting persons with disabilities to actively engage them and enhance safe community led support. UNHCR is currently providing services to PWD and has provided tailored support to improve their access and respond to needs (e.g., distribution of mobility devices, PSS, case management and engagement of a sign language interpreter for service provision). The proposed project will include training for humanitarian staff on disability inclusion. Protection Focal Points (PFPs) or also know as Community Outreach Volunteers will be reinforced with phased approach and deliverables of IEC materials and incorporation in the prevention and information sharing of protection messages on civil documentation, MHPSS services, People with Disabilities and Elderly Sensitization. Technical capacity building to Government entities on disability and inclusion as well as to community leaders and members will also be done.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

UNHCR ensures environmental considerations across the project. The project is not material assistance or infrastructure based so risks are less significant. However, materials used for safe spaces are local, natural, and sustainable, the work on the spaces is conducted directly within the community. Also, the project tries to ensure sustainability and localization of all inputs including furnishings for safe spaces and re-usable material masks and other items for project participants. The integrated protection approach helps to reduce environmental impact through sharing resources such as transportation of different protection teams together, the use of a single space for all protection activities, and sharing of project materials.
