Project Summary

Niger is hosting 57,274 Malian refugees. While maintaining the current assistance to malian urban refugees in Niamey, UNHCR and the Government of Niger are accelerating the out-of-camp policy. The key orientation defined by UNHCR and the Niger authorities is to work on the logic of ‘responsible disengagement’, transforming the camps of Tillabery into sustainable settlements and transforming the ZAR of Intikane in administrative village. The objective is the closure of the Tillabery camps by 2020, through the urbanization program, mixing refugees and host population, and providing legal access to land and social housing for the most vulnerable refugees and host communities. One-stop-shop will be built in each area hosting refugees. In the context of prolonged displacement, the assistance and protection strategy for Malian refugees focuses on medium and long term solutions that will promote autonomy, self-reliance, and integration into national service systems. Assistance will continue to be based on vulnerability rather that status. In this out-of-camp approach. UNHCR is also engaged with development donors such as eth EU, GIZ and the World Bank whom joint efforts is allowing to strongly structure the socio-economic integration of Malian refugees. Existing protection intervention, including child protection and SGBV, will continue to be strengthen. An emergency response capacity will also be maintained.

Besoins

Niger is hosting Malian refugees since the outbreak of the crisis in Northern Mali in 2012. The refugees are living in 3 refugee camps in the Tillaberi region, in a refugee hosting area (ZAR) for nomadic refugees in Intikane, in the Tahoua region, and in urban areas such as Niamey and Ayorou. Malian refugees are recognized on a prima facie basis and enjoy a favorable protection environment with the right to settle where they wish, to access basic social services and the formal and informal job market. Due to increasing instability in the regions bordering Mali, a State of Emergency was declared by the Government in several departments in March 2017. This has been extended and prolonged on several occasion. The security situation is impacting dramatically the socio-economic fabric of the areas hosting refugees. A tripartite agreement between UNHCR and the Governments of Niger and Mali signed in 2014, provides a legal framework for voluntary returns in respect of international standards. However, the parties consider that the conditions in parts of Northern Mali are not yet conducive to returns in safety and dignity and therefore returns are not currently promoted. Persons that wish nevertheless to return to Mali are counseled on the prevailing situation, sign a Voluntary Repatriation Form, and receive one-time cash assistance to pay for their transport. As no signs of improvement are awaited in north Mali, the acceleration of the socio-economic integration is a priority for both UNHCR and Niger Government. Beside the needs in terms of access to land for housing, permanent shelter for the most vulnerable, family latrines, it remains central to strengthen the national mechanisms – water, health, education - to absorb the refugees. In parallel, nutrition and food security activity need to be maintained for the most vulnerable, as well as cash transfer. Support the needs in terms of economic opportunities is core in the strategy focusing in self-reliance. Community based approach will continue to be at the center of the intervention including for activities related to child protection, SGBV, pacific coexistence…
