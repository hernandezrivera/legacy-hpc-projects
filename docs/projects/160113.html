Project Summary

CMC aims at provision of equitable access to safe and adequate water, dignified Sanitation, and safe hygiene practices to mitigate WASH related malnutrition and GBV through integrated WASH intervention reinforcing protection, enhancing resilience, communities coping mechanisms and sustainability to achieve cluster strategic objectives 1, 2 & 3 (SO 1:1, 1:2, 2:1, 3:1) , and cluster objectives (CO1 and CO3) tackling 70% WASH-nut and 30% WASH-GBV in the areas of Pibor and Kapeota South targeting crises affected population, IPC3 to IPC4 population, mainly IDPs, returnees and most vulnerable host communities in Likuangole, Gumuruk, verteth, Pibor town in Jonglei and Kapeota South in Eastern Equatoria, to reduce morbidity and mortality resulting from WASH-related GBV and malnutrition within the affected population. CMC focuses on lifesaving WASH activities mainstreaming protection, accountability to affected population (AAP) and strengthening the beneficiaries copping mechanism and building resilience through training of different groups of people in the community including; pump mechanics, Water management committee, women groups, community leaders, the AAP focal persons, teachers, pupils and nutrition workers. The project targets 25,000 (16,000 Direct, and 7,000 indirect) beneficiaries facing IPC3&4 and GAM ranging at 20.8%, the IDPs, most vulnerable host communities affected with conflict and natural disasters, and malnutrition patients with limited access to WASH services and food insecurity, through supported coordination and complementation of WASH needs in the targeted areas, achieving Sphere standards, the core humanitarian standards and the WASH cluster objectives CO1 and CO3, and contributing to eradication of the IPC.
CMC aims at structured, effective preparedness and response programme through improving access to safe water, enhancing community awareness on safe hygiene and sanitation practices within population with limited access to WASH services and food insecurity, supporting institutional WASH Services through coordinations with nutrition, protection and FSL partners to intergrate WASH in their interventions by enhancing the knowledge of mothers and children with equitable WASH practices to mitigate WASH related GBV and malnutrition within the population. Enhancing sustainability and resilience by building capacities of the communities to cope with current shocks through sustaining livelihoods linkages with development action to promote ownership, perticipations, and accountability to affected people through benficiaries centered aproaches.
CMC will ensure that services delivered are gender-sensitive, considering people with special needs and disables by establishing strong feedback and complain mechanisms at all stages of the implementation, training of the AAP focal point dominated by the beneficiaries, and the CMC M&E team to ensure feedbacks and complains are addressed. Initial needs assessments will be conducted in consultation with the WASH cluster. the activities includes; safe water supply through water point repair and rehabilitation, provision of sanitation facilities at institution levels, hygiene promotion focusing on key messages that encourages the used of locally available materials and CAT-method of excreta disposal at households and institutional hygiene promotion. Capacity-building of community through trainings (pump mechanics, water user committees, community hygiene promoters, school's hygiene clubs, the AAP focal points, nutrition workers), distribution of WASH NFIs and MHM kits to the most vulnerable population.
Ensures strong M&E mechanisms, active citizenship, AAP score cards are used to evaluate activities being implemented. CMC protection team will be involved at all stages of designing, planning, implementation and evaluation of every proposed activities to ensure maximum protection mainstreaming and Mitigate GBV threats. Recruiting staffs at field to avoid Logistical constraints.

1 - Needs

CMC activities will target populations facing crisis with limited access to adequate safe water, unsafe hygiene and sanitation practices, high malnutrition rates resulting from conflicts and other natural disasters. Highly prioritizing locations with influx of newly displaced populations and most vulnerable IDPs who are displaced lately due to conflict and natural disasters leaving in remote hard to reach areas, protracted IDPs in collective centers, returnees and vulnerable communities hosting IDPs, malnutrition patients with limited WASH facilities. CMC project seeks to implement integrated WASH interventions to vulnerable community, Malnutrition Patients, GBV prone and IPC 3, 4 locations. CMC recognizes the need for increased WASH interventions at household and institutional levels for displaced population, host community in the areas with high level of food insecurity coupled with malnutrition (GAM rate 25-30%) (IPC4), areas at high risk of protections issues especially GBV that are WASH related. Protracted conflict in South Sudan has caused erosion of physical and social infrastructure, death and displacement of hundreds of thousands of people in Jonglei and Eastern Equatoria state, South Sudan. The conflicts echoed to tribal clashes, cattle raiding among others. Pibor, Likuangole, Gumuruk and Verteth counties in Jonglei State and Kapeota South are among the most affected with recurrent attacks, floodings and IPC4, often causing more displacements, loss of lives and destruction of infrastructure. Coupling, the national financial crises have worsened the situation. According to the World Bank report 2018-2019, over half (51%) of the 12.3 million South Sudanese live below the national consumption poverty line. This implies that majority of the population in the country cannot easily afford basic needs including water, soap, among others. This situation continued to worsen where more people are displaced, livelihoods destroyed and more fighting being reported. The Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) ‘Key IPC findings of January-July 2019 and September-October 2019, report highlighted Pibor and its neighboring counties in Jonglei state as one of the most food insecure counties in South Sudan. Additionally, the October 2018 Standardized Monitoring and Assessment of Relief and Transitions (SMART) survey indicated the prevalence of global acute malnutrition (GAM) was 26.8% and Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) at 10.5%.2 3 Cattle raids, and flooding which previous REACH studies have linked to food insecurity, have become progressively more frequent, far ranging, and violent letting to displacement and subsequent WASH gaps. Different needs assessments shows that, in most parts of the country, people are faced with immense lack of access to safe adequate water, poor hygiene practices and sanitation conditions, food insecurity exposing people to the risk of WASH related diseases and malnutrition especially among children under-fives. This is attributed to several reasons the main one being displacement due to conflict. In recent reports, Several civilians were reported to have been displaced from Pochalla, Manybol and Kapeota, of which, about 15,000 IDPs have been registered by partners in Pibor town between 12 and 15 January 2019, and several were still waiting to be registered. (Humanitarian key context updates 22 September 2019). Inter-agencies assessment report (IRNA) conducted by the partners in Pibor including WFP, Plan international (for OCHA), UNMISS and UNICEF, CMC and the multi-sectorial partners in Pibor lately August 2019 indicates higher influx of IDPs and higher rate of mortalities and morbidities due to WASH related and communicable diseases as most of the villages have limited access to WASH activities. Fact finding mission report of CRS, Oxfam And Plan International together with CMC, show that IDPs are suffering from lack of safe adequate water, basic hygiene and sanitation facilities.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Conducting needs assessments in areas of crisis prior to any response to determine the exact needs of the community and establish relevant recommendations. CMC will participate in IRNA assessment and conduct its own assessment and share reports with the WASH cluster and other partners. Activity 2: Providing timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected populations through repair and rehabilitations of water points, provision of water purifiers (PUR and aqua tabs), building capacities of local pump mechanics and water management committee to ensure sustainability and resilience, water quality analysis at water points, Households and public facilities (Nutrition centers, Schools etc.). Consideration will be made concerning the location of the water point through involving women in deciding for the area where the water point can be constructed to ensure secure access to women and girls. Activity 3: Increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices among vulnerable and affected populations, to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases. Hygiene promotion activities like HH visits by trained Community Hygiene Promoters targeting key messages like hand washing at critical times, safe water chain, food hygiene and safe excreta disposal. CMC will also target schools and nutrition/health centers focusing on mothers who are taking care of children with malnutrition. Menstrual Hygiene Management will also be an area of focus considering the fact that MHM is still misunderstood in most communities. Activity 4: Enable vulnerable and affected populations to practice safe, sanitary and Hygienic living through secure, dignified and gender appropriate sanitation services. At institution level like nutrition centers and schools, latrines will be constructed to cater for boys and girls, men and women separately. For the girls and latrines will be equipped with water disposal bins for disposing used sanitary pads and adequate space for changing and washing. Additional HH latrines will also be constructed as samples using local available materials for community to learn from. Activity 5: Mainstreaming cross-cutting issues across all activities with particular focus on gender mainstreaming, GBV, protection, putting additional effort to ensure accountability to affected population and committing to improve effectiveness of cooperation with communities. CMC will complement other sectoral responses through integration and mainstreaming methodologies.

CMC protection staff will work closely with well-established protection partners.
