Project Summary

DRC works alongside affected communities and local authorities to address protection issues and meet basic needs in food security and livelihoods (FSL), shelter and non-food items (SNFI), and humanitarian mine action (HMA) in Sudan. DRC has also started implementing activities within water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), site management support (SMS), and multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) sectors to address the growing emergency needs and widening gaps in support to affected communities impacted by the war.
DRC will continue to target Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), host communities and returnees in Central Darfur, Khartoum, and South Kordofan as well as refugees in Gedaref along with other vulnerable groups in the state. Since the war broke out, DRC has also added two more operational bases: White Nile State and Al Jeziera, and will be expanding programmatic operations to Port Sudan within the coming months. In South Kordofan, DRC operates a non-technical Mine Action programme through two activities: (1) providing EORE to vulnerable persons and (2) conducting non-technical surveys
DRC has grown its emergency response through several interventions such as intersectoral mobile response teams and cross border programming. For the former, these teams will be based in Port Sudan and Gedaref but able to be deployed throughout Red Sea State, Northern State, Gedaref, River Nile, Sennar and Kassala. Regarding the latter, DRC will work with its counterparts in South Sudan and Chad to support cross border populations. From Sudan, this programming will particularly focus on those located in Darfur and White Nile State with possible support in South Kordofan. Furthermore, DRC has been providing technical and financial support to a local partner in Khartoum. The partner has been providing emergency support since the start of the war in Khartoum to support life-saving self-relocations of highly vulnerable individuals.
In addition to direct and immediate assistance and despite the challenges due to the outbreak of the conflict in April 2023, DRC continues to engage in and implement programming to build resilience and support for vulnerable populations, particularly with displaced and returnee communities; to address their priority needs, reducing aid dependency, supporting early recovery and paving pathways to durable solutions. One key dimension to this work is through ensuring that all stakeholder groups’ needs and vulnerabilities are considered and accounted for in the project’s implementation; community engagement, mobilization, and feedback are also key to the approach, ensuring participation of all relevant stakeholder groups. DRC also focuses on partnership building to ensure complimentary and integrated response and programming.
Throughout its interventions, DRC works to secure government buy-in and ensure that coordination with relevant federal, state, and local authorities is continuous throughout all projects such as HAC and line ministries. DRC will also collaborate with local NGOs and representative community structures that already have an operational presence in the areas of intervention. This approach aims to increase the efficiency of community engagement while improving the ownership of the local community and actors in the response. The engagement of local actors will improve local acceptance and understanding of the activities and serve to ease the delivery of activities vis-à-vis communities and authorities, given that these stakeholders are already part of the community and are familiar with the social context and its internal dynamics.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

DRC upholds the centrality of protection and protection mainstreaming principles, including meaningful access, participation, and empowerment, throughout the entire project cycle. As such, DRC ensures consideration of age, gender, and other relevant diversity characteristics in its context and needs assessments, protection risk analyses, selection criteria, and interventions. DRC supports staff from all sectors with capacity building training on protection mainstreaming; to ensure that protection is understood in relation to each sector and that its integration throughout DRC programming is done at a high-quality level.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

DRC’s community-driven, participatory approach and long-standing involvement in community structures has been pivotal to maintaining its service delivery. Beneficiaries are consulted in-person and through community leaders before and during implementation of specific activities. Beneficiary selection criteria and distribution methods are communicated at village and community levels through meetings and awareness sessions facilitated by community networks, ensuring transparency and agency in the beneficiary selection process, while reducing community tensions. DRC strives to ensure meaningful participation of beneficiaries at every stage of the project cycle. Community mobilisation and awareness raising on assistance modalities and targeting criteria will be conducted prior to assistance. Households will be consulted through regular monitoring visits and PDMs, in which individuals will be consulted on the quality and type of assistance received prior to, and during the distribution process. Male and female enumerators will be hired to ensure female participation and feedback in a context-appropriate manner, allowing beneficiaries to provide feedback appropriately. Continuous beneficiary feedback will be provided regularly, helping to shape DRC's current and future programming. The active participation and decision-making of populations of concern, accounting for the different experience’s mechanisms ensures that beneficiaries are informed about various feedback channels.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

DRC’s Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) provides beneficiaries with a channel to share comments, feedback, and suggestions, which affect the delivery and quality of services received including PSEA complaints. To facilitate beneficiary feedback and help address issues across all its programs, DRC operates a feedback hotline to receive complaints or inquiry calls, followed-up by the independent Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) Department. In addition, beneficiaries are consulted on needs, assistance modalities and impact during RNAs, market assessments and PDM.
