Project Summary

IOM will continue to strengthenits Mental Health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) programme in a more systematic and comprehensive approach with engagement of community volunteers and health promoters. Access to quality reproductive health care services remains a challenge, including the gaps in sexual and reproductive health services including family planning, HIV, clinical care for GBV survivors and linkages with MHPSS. IOM intends to enhance quality access to comprehensive services through the integration of MHPSS with Primary Health Care (PHC). IOM will also continue working with the health partners in outbreak prevention and response efforts, capacity building of health and mental health professionals, health information management, and emergency referral and support system. As part of the health promotional and preventive activities, IOM will partner with local NGOs to implement health outreach through Health Promoters and community volunteers. Under this action, IOM will also strengthen its collaboration with the State Primary Health Care Development Agency (SPHCDA) and will continue to support the State Ministries of Health.
IOM partnered with the University of Maiduguri Teaching Hospital (UMTH) in setting-up and operating a 24-bed and 2- bed ICU (intensive car eunit) Health center which includes an isolation center for humanitarian aid workers in north-east Nigeria and a COVID-19 rt- PCR laboratory that addresses the PCR (polymerise chain reaction) testing of humanitarian workers and the hospital community both for case management, surveillance and travel purposes. IOM aims to continue the partnership with UMTH in sustaining essential emergency health services, operating the isolation center and COVID- 19 laboratory for humanitarian aid workers and COVID-19 testing to at ‘risk community’ in north-east Nigeria.
In coordination with the Nigeria Centre for Disease Control (NCDC), Federal Ministry of Health and related departments, World Health Organization and the UN Medical Services; IOM will continue to leverage its health clinic infrastructure and capacity to contribute to the government’s effort to respond to COVID-19. IOM will continue to support the provision of COVID-19 testing services in Borno, FCT (Abuja) and Lagos. Activities include sample collection and testing for the UN staff, staff of International Non-Governmental Organizations (INGOs), diplomatic community and migrants using existing UMTH facilities and the communities at risk

1 - Needs

Across three states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) in north-east Nigeria, around 8.7 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance including life-saving essential health care services (OCHA HNO February 2021). In addition to the ongoing humanitarian emergency, the COVID-19 pandemic poses a significant social and economic threat to Nigeria, with a potentially devastating effect for the most vulnerable population who have endured a decade of conflict. The protracted crisis in north-east Nigeria has led to a meltdown of socioeconomic and development structures, which has contributed to the destruction of health facilities and an incapacitated healthcare sector. Additionally, quality of care is unstable due to the volatile security situation and lack of standardized health care packages. An increase in quality service provision, enhanced technical capacity of the healthcare providers and the establishment of a robust monitoring mechanism are thus noted as critical issues by the agencies responding to the crisis.Furthermore, gaps in referral services have been strongly raised by Health Sector partners currently providing the responses at PHC in camps and host communities, as a key need specifically the inability of patients to receive critically needed health care nor timely access emergency care in hospitals. In hard-to-reach areas, communities face serious challenges in accessing health services due to various reasons, including lack of medical staff, unavailability of drugs and supplies, security barriers, transportation issues and damaged/destroyed health facilities. Critical gaps also remain in functional primary and secondary health services. Other common challenges in accessing health services are related to high direct and indirect costs associated with healthcare, including the price of quality medicines. The disruption in supply chain of medicines and medical supplies has compounded the problem. The dire living conditions of these IDPs together with the often- poor water and sanitation conditions contribute to increased health risks and higher vulnerability to the spread of outbreak prone communicable diseases, such as cholera.IDP returnees in newly accessible areas or IDPs living in “Satellite camps” have reportedly limited or even no access to health care services. In Borno State, the vast majority of public servants have not yet returned to conflict-affected areas, in part because they remain inhospitable. There is an urgent need to expand the provision of integrated basic PHC services, to prevent further deterioration of the health status of affected populations. Health services also need to be made available and accessible at the different levels of care, supported by a well-functioning referral mechanism, at and between the community, primary and secondary/higher care levels. While the Ministry of Health has made efforts in integrating the mental health through PHC, there is a need to further this approach to ensure increased access to mental health services. Gender-based violence (GBV), including sexual violence, is widespread in the region due to the ongoing conflict, insecurity, living conditions in the IDP camps and informal settlements, also inadequate WASH facilities, lack of safe access to firewood, and restrictions on freedom of movement in and out of camps. Furthermore, it is equally important to strengthen the referral system to address gaps in sexual and reproductive health including medical care of GBV survivors and MHPSS interventions through appropriate referral support.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Establish three Mobile Health Teams providing an integrated standard PHC service package, including referrals to secondary care. Activity 2: Identify and train local community volunteers on health promotion and community-based health and mental health interventions. Activity 3: Support the national disease surveillance system and contribute to control of disease outbreaks and other public health events. Activity 4: Refurbishment and equipment of 10 selected public health facilities to resume their function to provide integrated primary health services. Activity 5: Conduct training for PHC workers with the use of the WHO Mental Health Gap Action Programme Intervention Guide (mhGAP-IG), Psychological First Aid (PFA), and staff self-care using already trained trainers. Activity 6: Strengthen referral and provision of specialized Mental Health services at tertiary and secondary health facilities (e.g. Federal Neuro-Psychiatric Hospital, State Specialist Hospital). Activity 7: Conduct an assessment on access of Mental Health services in PHC. Activity 8: Conduct awareness campaigns to increase concern, informing the targeted groups and attempts to change their behaviours promoting mental health and psychosocial well-being. Activity 9: Support the coordination of the Mental Health and Psychosocial Support Sub Working Group (MHPSS SWG) through capacity building activities and provisions of technical support in close coordination with the State Ministry of Health Activity 10: COVID Isolation facilities providing psychosocial support and training health personal on the isolation to prevent mental health problems due to isolation and tackle stigma/discrimination at community level. Activity 11: Continue the partnership with UMTH in operating the isolation center and COVID-19 laboratory for humanitarian workers in north-east Nigeria Activity 12: Provision of COVID-19 testing services for humanitarian aid workers and communities at risk, including sample collection and testing for the UN staff, staff of International Non-Governmental Organizations (INGOs), diplomatic community and migrants using existing UMTH facilities Protection while sustaining essential emergency health services, gender and MHPSS approach will be mainstreamed throughout all health interventions.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Protection principles will be mainstreamed throughout the project interventions. Activities will aim to contribute towards addressing the needs of the affected population, including vulnerable groups. IOM will ensure to encourage community participation by engaging beneficiaries in face mask production and awareness, which will contribute to a better COVID-19 response in IDP camps and beneficiaries’ empowerment. IOM team will priorities community members’ access to information through awareness-raising and provide support to newly arrived households to increase their knowledge on available services and how they can be accessed. Additionally, IOM will support in reinforcing two main aspects of a prevention model: through dissemination of harmonize GBV and PSEA harmonized messages to the community and ensuring the inclusion of vulnerable groups; support in strengthening existing pathways for any referral activities to ensure appropriate access, observing do not harm principles and appropriateness of the response.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Participatory approach will be encouraged to ensure women, men, girls, and boys and community members' involvement in the decision-making processes throughout the regular day-to-day activities. Furthermore, IOM teams will ensure to coordinated effectively with other actors, including the referral pathways to ensure beneficiaries' needs are addressed. IOM will aim to support and led the GBV field coordination in locations to ensure a well-coordinated GBV response by identifying and sharing concerns and gaps to improve service provision. Sensitisation sessions will be conducted for community members on countertrafficking, GBV, health and hygiene promotion messages, services available at the resource center, and information on public services in line with the referrals and access to available services. Continuous engagement with the affected population and stakeholders through FGDs, Assessments, and surveys will be considered as an integral part of the IOMs approach in ensuring accountability throughout the implementation of this project. Safeguarding:PSEA and child safeguarding will be integrated into training packages, which will provide team members with an opportunity to understand their role in ensuring accountability to the affected population and how they can provide assistance in a safe and dignified manner.
