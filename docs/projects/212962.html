Project Summary

Leading the fight against malnutrition, ACF implements gender-sensitive and life-saving WASH interventions and provides communities with access to safe water and sanitation as sustainable mitigation for the causes of hunger. After 13 years of hostilities and persistent economic crisis, people in Syria saw their living conditions worsen when a series of earthquakes hit northern Syria and Türkiye. The erosion of basic service capacity has continued steadily, with water and sanitation systems, and public health services under immense strain, in a context of little development investment.
ACF operates through four offices in Damascus, Aleppo, Hama, and Al-Hassakeh. ACF has MoUs with the Ministries of Water Resources, Local Administration and Environment, Education, and Health. ACF actively engages with the WASH, Health and Nutrition, Food Security and Agriculture, and Early Recovery and Livelihoods, and Protection Sectors, as well as other sub-sectors and technical working groups such as Cash and AAP. ACF maintains close coordination with SARC, as well as with Governors’ offices and local administrations, and Line Ministries with which it has MoUs in place, including MoH, MoE, MoWR, MAAR, and MoSAL. In NES, ACF coordinates with UNICEF and the NES WWG for its interventions in Al-Hassakeh.
ACF conducts integrated assessments to understand the needs and interlinkages of WASH, FSL, and Health and Nutrition. After conducting assessments in Aleppo, Hama, Latakia, and Al-Hassakeh, it was identified that community members have access to less than 30 l/p/d of water. Al-Hassakeh city relies on humanitarian agencies for water trucking, with no sustainable and complete exit strategy in sight (not least due to the condition of water resources in the governorate, and the lacking political solutions to ensure the permanent functioning of Allouk pumping station), and all targeted governorates need rehabilitation of the water systems, and the water pipelines as well as the sewage systems.
Under Sector Objectives 1 and 4, emergency WASH services provided by ACF include life-saving water trucking, distribution of water tanks, water quality testing and light maintenance of boreholes. ACF also undertakes the rehabilitation of damaged and malfunctioning water systems, which includes repairing boreholes, upgrading to solar energy, connecting water stations to the electric grid, repairing elevated tower tanks, repairing water networks at the community level, and providing equipment for water testing facilities. By providing water through public networks, ACF ensures greater control over quality and quantity, making it an affordable service. ACF also repairs sanitation systems at different levels, including pipelines, manholes and septic tanks, and provides support for solid waste management. This includes providing garbage bins for safe disposal in coordination with municipalities, as well as technical capacity building for technicians, PPE, tools and spare parts provision for sustained service provision after handing over to the local authorities. To improve public health, ACF engages in risk communication and community engagement activities that are tailored according to age and gender.
Gender sensitive approaches and safeguarding of beneficiaries are integral to ACF programming, with dedicated staff upholding gender minimum standards and mainstreaming safeguarding processes. ACF recognizes the right of everyone to be treated with dignity and respect and protected from sexual exploitation and abuse. ACF’s policy on PSEA complies with the IASC six principles relating to sexual exploitation and abuse. ACF provides compulsory training to each new staff member and refresher sessions to existing staff on organizational ethical policies. Internal policies relating to fraud, anti-corruption and abuse of power and whistleblowing/reporting mechanisms are followed with employees receiving instruction on these internal policies.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

ACF employees and any third parties contracted by ACF sign a Code of Conduct and are inducted on its contents. There is various gender-balanced focal points against harassment at both capital and base levels in Syria, as well as a Safeguarding lead for ACF Syria based in Damascus.ACF staff are trained in Gender and Protection mainstreaming. Protection standards are followed to ensure safe access to WASH services, with particular attention for the needs of PwD, elderly people, women, and girls. The quality of water is closely monitored to comply with WHO standards. Child Protection charters are included in contracts and HR procedures, with zero tolerance for child labour and abuse. ACF upholds the IASC principles on PSEA and has a zero-tolerance policy on SEA. ACF protect people’s dignity and safety by implementing protection measures in all implementation modalities, whether in-kind or cash, and maintaining Protection and Gender minimum standards for all activities, including at on-site in rehabilitation projects of basic service facilities, interventions in collective shelters, or any other contact points with beneficiaries throughout ACF programming such as during assessment, consultation, or feedback mechanisms. All employees are trained in safeguarding and have access to reporting systems. ACF also upholds a multi-modal and accessible Feedback and Complaints Response Mechanism, through which people can freely provide feedback to ACF, which is handled according to a stringent internal protocol. Assumptions are related to securing timely approval for proposed projects, including by the concerned Line Ministries. An unstable security situation, restricted access to targeted locations, and lengthy administrative procedures may cause a high impact. Mitigation strategies include advocacy, ongoing context analysis and monitoring, and coordination and access negotiation with local authorities. Additionally, there is a risk of currency devaluation, fraud, lack of data access, and ethical concerns. Mitigation strategies for these risks include close and direct monitoring, training staff members on organisational policies, transparency, and trust-building.
