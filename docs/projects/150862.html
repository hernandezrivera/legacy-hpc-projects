Project Summary

American Refugee Committee (ARC) proposes a nutrition project to be implemented in Banadir and Jubaland of South-Central Somalia, and Sool, Sanaga and Togdeer in Somaliland, where the humanitarian need is critical due to ongoing impacts of conflict, drought and displacement. During armed conflict there is risk of loss of livelihood and properties making especially women and children vulnerable and requiring nutritional support.
This emergency nutrition project will provide quality life-saving nutrition services to the vulnerable crisis and drought affected population including IDPs and host communities by improving equitable access to quality nutrition services with the goal of reduce mortality and morbidity. The project is targeting women, boys, girls and men, with special focus on pregnant and lactating women and malnourished children under-five. The project is planned to directly benefit 286,494 beneficiaries (Women 172,860 Men 113,634).
The linkage to WASH and health components will ensure a comprehensive approach to reduce morbidity and mortality associated with the compound effect of diseases resulting from poor sanitation conditions, lack of access to safe water, and malnutrition. To increase the impact, the nutrition project will be coordinated also with ARC Protection and Livelihood projects.
ARC will closely coordinate with MoH and respond in locations where gaps in nutrition services have been identified. ARC will ensure the participation of the local communities, with special attention to participation of women, in decision making throughout the project cycle to strengthen community acceptance and ownership. Throughout the lifetime of the project, ARC will work with the MoH to pave the way for a proper phasing out at the end of the project.

Needs

According to Humanitarian Bulletin Somalia, 1 August - 5 September 2018, the total number of people in need still exceeds levels prior to the onset of the drought crisis in 2016. This is driven by the increase in internal displacement that occurred during the drought as people in inaccessible, rural areas sought assistance in populated areas humanitarians could reach. The total number of internally displaced Somalis is estimated to be 2.6 million.An estimated 4.6 million people, including 2.5 million children, continue to require humanitarian assistance. Of these, 1.5 million are in crisis or emergency (FSNAU &FEWSNET). Communities that lost most of their livestock during the 2016-2017 drought and those affected by conflict, as well as this year’s devastating floods and cyclone, will take longer to recover. The internally displaced are the most vulnerable and in dire need of immediate and long-term assistance. According to FAO-FSNAU, an estimated 294,000 children are acutely malnourished, of which nearly 55,000 are severely malnourished. The surveys show that the overall level of acute malnutrition is serious (median GAM of 14 per cent), which is an improvement from critical compared to the 2017 post-Gu assessment (median GAM of 17.4 per cent). Severe acute malnutrition (SAM) prevalence is critical (more than 4 per cent) only among Mogadishu IDPs and in Guban pastoral livelihood area in western Somaliland.The project will work in coordination with the local health authorities to ensure that services provided are of high quality as the the Somali government guidelines and strategy.

Activities

• Screening of under-five children, pregnant and lactating women for malnutrition• Treatment of moderate and severe acute malnutrition by providing treatment using IMAM guidelines, children with MAM will be given RUSF and those with SAM will be provided with RUTF and pregnant/lactating women-fortified CSB and oil• Formation of mother to mother support groups and training them on mother-MUAC strategy• Provide nutrition education at the community level integrated with timed and targeted counselling• Training of CHWs/CNWs on Integrated management of acute malnutrition• Training of community nutrition workers on timed and targeted counselling• Information systems in all ARC health facilities will be fully be collected, analyzed and shared with the nutrition cluster• Nurses, auxiliary nurse and CNWs will be trained on the identification, treatment and prevention of severe acute malnutrition• Ensure there is consistent supply of nutrition commodities-procurement of nutrition commodities and drugs• Training staff on proper quantification and forecasting of supplies • Quarterly supportive supervision and community feedback sessions is done for all MCHs to provide support• Monthly on job training and mentorship to MCH staff to enhance their capacity to provide quality life-saving nutrition services.• Organize quarterly data review meeting with MCHs and mobile clinics to draw action points and recommendations for improvement

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The project enables achievement of durable solutions by enhancing community engagement in provision of services and ensuring that beneficiary feedback is ingrained into the delivery of the programme. Working in close collaboration and coordination with MoH, and seeking the guidance and ownership of the government department of health will create a favorable conditions for the project implementation. The project will support the department of health to conduct quality control measures by providing supervision and oversight. The project will also empower the community through training of community nutrition workers, mother to mother support groups, local technical staff with the aim of developing their capacity to deliver quality nutrition services as this will enhance improving community systems and structures. As part of the sustainability plan, the project will endeavor to enhance the capacity of local health workers as well as community structures such as community nutrition workers, mother to mother support groups who will continue to provide services to the community beyond project life. ARC will ensure that MoH will taking the lead in the implementation of the project with all activities fully delivered within the MoH systems and structures and without setting up parallel systems for implementation. This will mean developing a detailed implementation plan with MOH and ensuring that MOH is aware of what ARC is implementing. This creates confidence within the MOH and enhances cordial working relationship. Before the end of the project, ARC will share an exit strategy plan by clearly outlining the handover mechanism of the facilities to the MOH and community so that all are clear about end of the project so that MOH will be prepared to advocate for continuity of services after the end of the project. This will ensure uninterrupted services provision to the community and continuity of service. ARC will also supplement the project with other funding for the same regions.The entitlement of beneficiaries to services/utilities provided will be legally recognized as ARC is fully conscious about beneficiary accountability and right to access quality services by the beneficiaries. A mechanism to collect beneficiary feedback will be put in place for purposes of improving service delivery.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

ARC will prevent risks of exclusion based on displacement by ensuring that internally displaced persons benefit from the interventions just like the host communities. The project will target women, men, girls and boys in both IDP camps and host communities so that equity and equality is maintained at all levels. In actualizing this, there are risks that ARC will need to mitigate so that service provision to the most deserving communities is not hindered. All distributions will be done in safe, convenient places considering all aspects of protection and security of the beneficiaries as per the principle of "Do No Harm" ARC will also put in place community consultation and feedback mechanism Kuja Kuja to collect real time community satisfaction feedback (www.kujakuja.com).

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The risks that will be anticipated has to do with social exclusion of women and girls in accessing nutrition services because women and children especially girls are treated as socially inferior to their male counterparts thus will have an effect on health seeking behaviour. Inequities arising from gender and age are often associated with poor health and reduced well being. Health and nutrition problems often faced by women and children and vulnerabilities are poorly recognized by the community. ARC will ensure that women and children being the most marginalized and vulnerable have access to quality health and nutrition services in the target locations and basically integrating with other sectors such as WASH and protection. This will minimize the risks associated with exclusion based on gender and age because of the provision of an equitable service to all. ARC will continuously engage women and children in it's programmes, gather their priority needs and inputs when designing programmes so as to have a people-centered programming. The data generated from the field by ARC will have gender disaggregation so that numbers reached can be determined and analyzed regularly to inform decision making. The project will also collect feedback from beneficiaries on a quarterly basis including women and children to get their perceptions/views and recommendations about ARC program which will form a basis for improvement in future programming.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Through the adoption of "Do No Harm" approaches, efforts are made to ensure relationships with the project targeted population and local leaders are enhanced and programs are implemented to improvement relationships, reduce tension, and promote peaceful pathways out of Somalia conflict situation. ARC believes that understanding the situation and the context in which we operate is paramount to ensuring that interventions are successful and to avoid negative consequences in the operation area. As a part of the Protection project, ARC will carry out community capacity building to promote peaceful co-existence and conflict resolution.Insecurity arising from extremist groups (Al Shabaab) and inter-clan clashes through contextual understanding and working with the community to acquire information about threats, ARC will maintain a high level of acceptance at community level and with key stakeholders and decision makers. ARC will continue monitoring the security situation and trends in the region, analyse risks (their likelihood and impact), review mitigation measures, and integrate the findings in to ARC’s Safety and Security Management Plan, and ensure that all staff are trained on and adhere strictly to our security guidelines
