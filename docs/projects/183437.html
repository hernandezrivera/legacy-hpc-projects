Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims at ensuring continuity of learning and safe and equitable access to inclusive and quality education for 20,991 students (9,919 boys and 11,081 girls) and 951 school staff (319 men and 632 women) in a context of increased violence as a result of protracted crisis and continued military occupation, as well as increased vulnerability due to the COVID-19 pandemic. The project targets 26 schools that are affected by frequent military incursions, clashes and settler violence and are located at risk areas namely East Jerusalem, Hebron H2, Area C, and inside refugee camps.
The first aim of the project is to ensure that students affected by education-related violations or the COVID-19 pandemic benefit from a protective environment and that school-aged children are able to complete their basic education. This is achieved through the provision of safe transportation to vulnerable children while commuting to and from school, and protective presence in two schools particularly affected by ISF incursions, use of live ammunition and tear gas and settlers' violence. Emphasis is also given in supporting the psychosocial well-being and enhancing resilience of students and teachers. This is achieved through the deployment of ten school counsellors at high-risk schools; provision of recreational activities through school-based summer camps, enhancement of recreational spaces, and stress management workshops aiming to strengthen teachers’ resilience.
The second objective of the project is to ensure inclusivity of all vulnerable students. Learning support through catch-up classes will be provided to help children with learning difficulties. In addition, 1,852 children with disabilities (767 boys and 1,085 girls) and special needs will be supported through assistive devices and referrals.
In the context of COVID-19, the third aim of the project is to guarantee that schools prevent the spread of COVID-19 and protect the health of the students and teacher. UNRWA will provide hygiene kits and Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) to all targeted students and teachers.
Finally, the intervention also aims to improve the confidentiality, safety and accessibility of complaints mechanisms, including complaints of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA). The project intervention provides multiple child-friendly channels to receive and respond to complaints which are adapted to the target population. UNRWA has developed ad hoc SoPs on the management of cases of SEA which abide to CBCM SoPs. Complaints of SEA will be handled by PSEA focal points who will ensure a survivor centered approach in how the complaint is handled and support is offered to the survivor. UNRWA West Bank Field Office works closely with the PSEA Network and will undertake compulsory reporting and external referrals.
This action contributes to the achievement of two of the strategic objectives under the HRP:
STRATEGIC OBJECTIVE 2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL.
STRATEGIC OBJECTIVE 3: Safe, accessible mechanisms are in place for reporting complaints and feedback, including complaints of SEA, particularly in high-risk areas.

Implementing Partners

The United Nations Relief and Work Agency for Palestine Refugees in the Near East (UNRWA) has one of the most significant humanitarian operational presences in the West Bank, both geographically and in terms of sectors of intervention. Next to the Palestinian Authority, UNRWA is the biggest service provider in the West Bank. In total, the Agency serves 1,078,772 Palestine refugees and other registered persons (UNRWA, Registered Population Dashboard, January 2021), which amounts to one-third of the population in the West Bank, of whom more than half a million are women and girls. UNRWA was established by the General Assembly in 1949 and is mandated to assist and protect some 5 million registered Palestine refugees in the region. It has five fields of operation - Lebanon, Syria, Jordan, the Gaza Strip and the West Bank, including East Jerusalem. As the main service provider for Palestine refugees in the region, UNRWA delivers primary education, health care, relief and social services, infrastructure and camp improvement, microfinance, and emergency assistance. The Agency has extensive experience providing emergency assistance to Palestine refugees during humanitarian crises to save lives, preserve human dignity, and prevent and respond to the impacts of such crises. In the West Bank, the Agency's programmes seek to reduce the impact of the protection crisis stemming from over 50 years of the Israeli occupation by addressing the critical needs of the most affected individuals. UNRWA's protection work is fundamental to further the ability of Palestine refugees to enjoy their rights fully; including the provision of services to address violence, coercion, deliberate deprivation, and abuse, both by working actively to prevent these and mitigate their consequences. UNRWA is working on prevention through protection advocacy efforts; by actively promoting changes and shifts in policies and practices amongst duty bearers and working at the community level to enhance protective behaviours amongst refugees, and by improving the protective nature of its service delivery. In terms of response, UNRWA provides critical services to those exposed to protection threats, including psycho-social counselling, medical assistance, cash-based interventions, and the provision of dedicated programming to specific vulnerable groups. UNRWA is the best-placed UN agency to engage with both Israeli and Palestinian duty-bearers in the West Bank - it has wide-reaching access to all the relevant actors and decision-makers on the ground. UNRWA itself has developed a broad operational presence alongside its protection mandate. UNRWA utilizes and carefully cultivates its unique access to the duty-bearers to further the protection of Palestine refugees. As such, UNRWA is uniquely well-placed to take on a wide-reaching role in protection advocacy.

1 - Needs

Children (boys and girls) attending UNRWA schools across the West Bank, including East Jerusalem are exposed to several education-related vulnerabilities including protection concerns, COVID-19 restrictions, lack of MHPSS support and outdated school infrastructure. These factors undermine the quality of education, generating and contributing to higher school dropout rates. 20,991 UNRWA students in Area C, H2, East Jerusalem and those living in refugees' camps continue to face challenges in accessing schools. The fulfilling of their right to education is severely impacted by Israeli Security Forces (ISF) violence and armed search operations in schools; harassment, intimidation, and settlers' violence while commuting to schools. According to the 2021 OCHA Multi-Sectorial Needs Assessment, 26.9% of refugee households reported safety concerns in relation to their children's education. Traveling to or studying in education facilities is considered unsafe or very unsafe due to the proximity of the schools to Israeli settlements, frequent clashes with ISF use of tear gas on schools and students (16.5%), military presence around schools (12.1%), delays on checkpoints (5.8%) and detention of students from school (11.3%). Also, according to UNRWA data, ISF is more likely to use live ammunition during operations in refugee camps; 48% of injuries occurring in refugee camps are caused by ISF use of live ammunition, compared with 14% of injuries in non-camp locations. Exposure to violence during such incidents is a key source of stress and anxiety among children, parents, and teachers, who are increasingly in need of mental health and psycho-social support. However, major capacity gaps in school-based psychosocial support (PSS), particularly in the most exposed areas, have prevented the educational system from properly addressing this need. This has resulted in an extra burden on UNRWA teachers and has increased the risk of long-term mental health disorders among affected children (boys and girls). The situation is further aggravated for the 1,852 students (1,085 girls and 767 boys) with disabilities enrolled in UNRWA schools who face a higher risk of dropout, due to gaps in adapted facilities and assistive devices at schools, inadequate transportation means, and limited staff capacity to accommodate for their needs. According to the 2021 OCHA Multi-Sectorial Needs Assessment, 45.8% of households with children with a disability reported challenges to accessing education services. Boys and girls face a specific set of challenges. According UNFPA data, the prevalence of child marriage within the oPt is at 24%. Although, in general CEFM seems to be declining, an increase has been perceived in certain areas, particularly isolated parts of Area C and East Jerusalem. 28.5% of refugees’ households reported safety or security concerns for girls and 30.5% for boys. The COVID-19 emergency has had a significant impact on the education system in the West Bank. According to the MSNA, 4% of HHs with school-aged children in the West Bank reported that at least one child dropped out of school during the current school year while 72% of HHs with school-aged children reported a need for their children to participate in catch-up learning programmes because of missed learning due to COVID-19 related school closures. UNRWA is working within this challenging context to continue providing quality, inclusive, and equitable education to Palestine refugees, in addition to the context of military occupation and the challenges it provides. Some remote UNRWA refugee communities, especially in Area C, have struggled with access to education, and especially with remote learning. Since the beginning of COVID-19 pandemic, UNRWA has put in place various distance learning programmes to be used in the new academic year, which are not fully accessible to many children (boys and girls) living in houses without internet connectivity, or who could not collect the printed self-learning materials distributed by schools. According to a survey conducted by UNRWA during May 2020, between 20-25% of students have no internet access in their homes or efficient IT equipment such as personal computers and smart tablets to access these electronic resources. These students (boys and girls) are mainly living in remote Bedouin communities in Area C with limited or no internet connection and are highly vulnerable socially and economically. Consultations have been carried out with students from the targeted schools (girls and boys) through the school parliament in order to identify needs and preferences for complaints. School counselors and school principals, who are considered main stakeholders, have also been consulted. During the above-mentioned consultations, UNRWA staff has ensured that safe complaints and feedback channels based on consultations with beneficiaries are accessible and known to the target population, including feedback and complaints on services and more serious complaints related to misconduct and sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). Consultations undertaken by the PSEA Network for the oPt confirm the need for all organizations to strengthen SEA policies and processes, in addition to MSNA 2021 data which found that 23.8% of refugees (vs 11.1% of non-refugees) knew how to access complaint mechanisms. Information gaps remain on the needs, preferences, safety and confidentiality concerns of refugee women and adolescent girls, especially women with disabilities, single headed households, pregnant/lactating women and new mothers and elderly women. This information will be obtained through surveys, interviews and focus group discussions that UNRWA plans to implement across the West Bank in 2022 to inform and strengthen UNRWA’s SEA channels and processes based on the consultation and participation of affected groups.

2 - Activities

Activity 1.1) In order to guarantee access to education and tackle the protection threats while commuting to school, UNRWA will provide safe transportation to 634 students to and from six UNRWA schools; three schools in East Jerusalem, two schools serving remote Bedouin communities around Jericho, and Ghor Al Faraa. Activity 1.2) To prevent drop out and ensure protective presence in Hebron 2 and Ghor Al Faraa, and Schools, which are particularly exposed to military incursions and use of live ammunition and tear gas, UNRWA will hire two security guards who will survey the schools during school hours and report incidents to school principals. Furthermore, 62 school staff (39 men and 23 women) will be provided with UN uniforms to facilitate children entering and existing the schools providing further protection. Activity 1.3) UNRWA is strengthening the provision of school-based psychosocial support to boys and girls through additional school counselors' in ten schools. School counselors provide both individual and group counselling activities and identify, support and refer children-at-risk. They conduct and support psycho-social activities to enable children to learn positive coping skills and ways to express their anxieties and fears. Activity 1.4) Furthermore, the project will organize school-based summer camps that will offer an opportunity to 4,200 vulnerable students to strengthen their ability to cope with the current environment and to develop physical, social and emotional skills. Selection criteria will be determined based on consultations with school staff directly involved with the educational process. School counselors will prioritize children with special needs, disabilities and social and economic vulnerabilities. Activity 1.5) UNRWA teaching staff continuously faces the challenges of providing a safe learning environment for children in very volatile security settings. As PSS for teachers has been identified as a need, a resilience building day will be organized for 951 education staff working in high-risk schools. Activity 2.1) To ensure continuity of learning in the context of COVID-19, part of the school curriculum has been converted into Self-Learning Programme (SLP). For all student to be able to follow SLP, UNRWA will equip 7,331 vulnerable students from 7th to 9th grade living in remote communities with tablets containing electronic versions of self-learning material and electronic copies of the textbooks. Activity 2.2) In order to ensure that school environments are safe and child friendly for students and teachers, UNRWA will procure furniture and equipment for six multipurpose rooms, accessible for children with disabilities. Activity 2.3) In order to facilitate the inclusion and access of 1,852 students with disabilities in the 42 targeted schools, UNRWA will provide capacity development training for 951 frontline education staff on disability inclusion and mainstreaming. The training will be provided by UNRWA protection staff and tailored towards education programming. Furthermore, UNRWA will provide referrals to external specialist/specialised centres in the WB for children who need short and long-term rehabilitation. Schools will also provide assistive devices including wheelchairs, prosthetic, hearing aids, glasses, etc. The initial identification of the needs will be done by school-based education staff while school counsellors will be responsible for follow-up with students' post-distribution. Activity 2.4)The project will provide remedial education services and learning support for vulnerable students with learning difficulties and cognitive disabilities in the 42 targeted schools. 168 teachers will provide extra classes for students from grade 1 to 6 on Saturdays for Arabic and mathematics. Activity 3.1) To guarantee that students learn in a healthy and hygienic environment, PPE will be distributed to 20,991 students. Hygiene material and additional schools attendants will support the 26 targeted schools. Activity 3.2) In addition, as lack of materials can be a key barrier to accessing education, 2,000 students with social and economic difficulties from 1st to 3rd grade will receive school bags and school stationary kits. Activity 4.1) Consultations are held with boys, girls, children with disabilities, education staff to inform on complaint channels for SEA. This includes a survey, interviews and focus group discussions identifying reporting barriers and mitigation measures. Existing complaints channels, SOPs and PSEA strategy will be adapted to reflect feedback from consultations with beneficiaries. Activity 4.2) This project has built-in clear process for receipt and referral of complaints of PSEA in accordance with CBCM SOPs and internal SOPs on Case Management which support education staff to safely identify and refer protection cases within the agency. Complaints of SEA will be handled by PSEA focal points who will ensure a survivor centered approach in how the complaint is handled and support is offered to the survivor. UNRWA works closely with the PSEA Network , including compulsory reporting and will also strengthen existing processes and raise issues requiring technical support and/or external referral through this forum. Activity 4.3) Age and gender-sensitive PSEA awareness raising materials are disseminated to all schools containing information on rights, safe complaint channels and how to access survivor-centered assistance. All project staff will complete at least one PSEA training during the project period, to strengthen SEA safeguards for reporting SEA and referrals. Activity 4.4) Project staff will indirectly engage in the HCT oPt PSEA Network, through the active participation of UNRWA PSEA focal points and senior management, including contribution to the development of interagency workplans and SOPs.

Accountability to Affected Populations
9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

UNRWA has child-sensitive reporting mechanisms which will enable children to engage and communicate their complaints. This will be done through a participatory process with key stakeholders such as children, students and parents. A child-friendly reporting mechanisms will be designed to be in line with the Agency’s regulatory framework, the ongoing work on Accountability to Affected Populations and minimum protection standards as well as aligned with and situated within the existing Agency system for reporting misconduct. Online options will also be explored, although feasibility may be limited due to the limited access to devices or data amongst UNRWA students, thus risking exacerbating equity and access issues. It will aim to ensure disability-accessible complaints mechanisms and provide age-appropriate guidance in ensuring children understand what to expect from the process once they have submitted a report. It will also aim to engage students and parents in tackling the stigma of reporting. This will begin with a to be agreed number of schools in each area, to monitor their progress, with a view to scaling up over time.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

The project adopts a variety of mechanisms to facilitate the meaningful participation, feedback and complaints from all population groups. This includes a dedicated free phone hotline, in-person satisfaction surveys, email and phone hotlines for SEA and serious misconduct complaints. Recognizing the limitations of these mechanisms to persons with disabilities, children and the elderly, and Bedouin/herder communities, project staff also reach out to these populations through dedicated focus group discussions and interviews conducted by Crisis Intervention social workers. Technical protection staff working thematically on SEA, child protection, gender and disability inclusion will also advise where additional mechanisms should be developed and existing mechanisms strengthened to improve access to these populations, including through training of project staff, awareness raising of feedback and complaints channels with community based organisations representing these groups, linking with community-based complaint mechanisms and improving cross-programmatic referrals within UNRWA departments (health, education, infrastructure and camp improvement and relief and social services). The UNRWA Protection Department has also taken the lead in enhancing the accountability to affected persons (AAP) in UNRWA's service delivery in the context of COVID-19, establishing online platforms for information sharing and feedback, hotlines, and conducted dedicated outreach. Finally, the COVID-19 emergency has increased demands on UNRWA and its services and presented challenges to establishing appropriate communication with communities, as well as to accountability mechanisms in the context of COVID19 restrictions. The Protection Department has led the establishment of dedicated AAP lens to the COVID19 response, including drafting key communications to the refugee community.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The project adopts a do no harm and conflict sensitivity approach, including specific measures to ensure that identified risks are addressed within the action and unintended negative consequences are minimised. UNRWA has developed an internal guideline for ethical practices in the context of briefings, field visits, communications and others. UNRWA’s Case Management SOPs ensure that protection cases are identified and referred to specialised support services. SOPs cover compulsory reporting on SEA and support to survivors. A dedicated psychological consultation hotline is available to all refugees. UNRWA has existing guidelines for disability inclusion, gender equality, PSEA and child protection which are all fully applied in this project. In UNRWA's Protection Strategy (2012), the prioritisation of safety and dignity is explicitly address as a key protection standard where UNRWA programmes deliver services in a manner that ensures the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and staff. UNRWA ensures that its actions do not cause unintended harm or result in reduced protection of beneficiaries, communities or staff, including risk mitigation for SEA. On a biannual basis all of UNRWA's activities are subject to a protection mainstreaming audit, which assesses the integration of protection principles in UNRWA's work. This audit included extensive interviews and focus group discussions with beneficiaries, and sought to assess UNRWA's application of the four main protection principles, including the principle of Do No Harm. In 2019 UNRWA developed a new information management system for the collection of sensitive protection monitoring and documentation data; specifically the system now separates out biographical data from incident related data, with individual cases being only referred to through generated case code. In 2019/8 UNRWA developed a free feedback and complaints hotline for the use of all beneficiaries under this project. For the purposes of ensuring that briefings involving affected persons, are carried out in a way that prioritises the safety and dignity of these persons, UNRWA has developed an internal guideline for ethical practices in the context of briefings, field visits, communications and others. This includes a checklist to guide all staff working in this capacity. UNRWA has developed, in coordination with the West Bank Protection Consortium, a dedicated vulnerability assessment tool in order to prioritise assistance to the most vulnerable refugees. This tool was pioneered by UNRWA and has since been adopted by other members of the Consortium and is to be applied throughout the West Bank. Under Outcome 2 of this project, within the Case Management response UNRWA ensures that vulnerable individuals can access assistance by external referrals, UNRWA has developed dedicated case management SOPs and internal/external referral pathways, to ensure that the comprehensive needs are met. In the planning of its advocacy approach and strategy UNRWA engages with a range of community representatives, including protection committees, village councils, camp resident committees – as well as holding focus group discussions including for women, children, youth and the elderly in target communities. In the context of the COVID19 response, additional steps taken to ensure UNRWA's activities prioritise safety and dignity. UNRWA has adapted its case management in response to difficulty of physical access to concerned individuals/households. UNRWA has established a dedicated psychological consultation hotline available to all refugees. UNRWA has existing guidelines for disability inclusion, gender equality, PSEA and child protection which are all fully applied in this project. Finally, UNRWA developed guidelines for remote documentation and monitoring, including a do-no-harm assessment, and has also established guidelines to prioritise field work in the context of COVID-19 risks.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNRWA welcomes all Palestine refugee children from grade 1 to 9 in its 96 schools across the West Bank. Educational staff, who are regularly targeted by capacity building activities including on protection mainstreaming and gender, are able to refer specific protection cases to MHPSS counselors for follow up. Towards greater student inclusion, UNRWA continues to strengthen support for students with additional needs or those who risk losing education opportunities due to heightened conflict. Thepercentage of students with disabilities provided with support according to their needs increased from 37.71 per cent in 2018 to 71.74 per cent in 2019. The Education in Emergency programme continued to enable increased psycho-social support, parental engagement and safety and security efforts to facilitate access to education in times of crisis. In relation to gender equity, the majority of gains in key education indicators are reflected among both male and female students. The work of the Educationprogramme to enhance systems for gathering, monitoring and engaging actively with relevant data to improve quality, equity and inclusion at multiple levels continues. The Agency also continued to strengthen the implementation of its School Quality Assurance (SQA) process. The SQA process is one of rigorous review of a school with resultant clear linked recommendations and subsequent tailored support.
