Project Summary

Under the leadership of the Education Cluster, the project aims at providing access to protective learning routines for a minimum of 30,000 out of school crises affected children. In 2016-17 UNICEF designed and piloted the first-in-its-kind emergency Radio Education in Emergency Prototype: 144 episodes on literacy and numeracy for all contexts where crisis affected children cannot physically access schools. A prototype for illiterate school aged children was also tested in DRC, CAR, and Burkina Faso, NWSW crisis affected communities, and is under recording in Douala (ready to be narrowcast). This model is unique in targeting children for individual listening or in groups – without the help of an adult or a teacher – from within their communities or from their new location. This is different from other radio education programs, usually designed to help teachers teach (i.e. radio assisted instruction). In the current phase, the emergency program prototype does not substitute classroom education but constitutes a powerful educational and protective platform for hard-to-reach children in emergency and conflict contexts, until a safer learning environment is provided, and children can be integrated into the normalcy of the national education system.
Preliminary evidence confirms the complementing protective nature of the Radio Education Program prototype for children living in high insecurity: besides life-saving messages, it offers psychosocial support. Children listening are brought back in time to when they were in school before displacement. It gives them hope.
Evidence from Niger and Cameroon (2017/18) suggests that children who follow the program in listening groups improve their competences in literacy, numeracy and life-skills. Pilot studies on learning (based on pre-tests and post-tests) are being planned in all countries, targeting children who listen in group, as well as children who listen individually from home.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The target population children who cannot access formal education, due to school closure. The NW and SW crisis has left more than 1.2 million school-age children (51% girls) in need of humanitarian assistance in NW and SW, Adamawa (Mayo Banyo), Littoral, West, and Centre (Yaounde). Approximately 900,000 of them need urgently access to education service. The main needs for children of the NW and SW are linked to the risks associated with attending formal education, the unavailability of formal education offer in areas of insecurity, the general lack of protective learning environment. The project targets a minimum of 10,000 children (listeners) who will be reached initially with narrowcasting, and - depending on the situation - with broadcasting.
