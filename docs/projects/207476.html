Project Summary

Through the intervention, UN Women will support more Rohingya and host community women to have access to diversified livelihood opportunities and are empowered with increased social and economic security. UN Women, with mandate of gender equality and women’s empowerment, responds not only to immediate protection needs including GBV services but to address the needs of sustainable livelihoods and resilience-building for vulnerable women. In order to assess the specific livelihood challenges and opportunities that women in refugee and host communities experience, UN Women plans to conduct a gender analysis of livelihoods in Cox’s Bazar. Upon assessment, UN Women tailor skills development and vocational training based on the needs and priorities of the targeted women and provide the livelihood services through the Multi-Purpose Women’s Centers (MPWCs).
Since 2018, UN Women had established the MPWCs and the Women’s Market in camps 3, 4, 4ext, 5 and 18 and host communities in Ukhia, and Teknaf. The MPWCs provide women, girls and gender diverse persons with access to tailored multisectoral services that consider their unique needs and difficulties, with an emphasis on empowering women to build resilience and enhancing their leadership and participation in their communities. The MPWCs, already providing livelihood services, will strengthen its efforts on women’s economic empowerment and offer a diversified livelihood opportunities to both refugee and host community women, ensuring the inclusion of the most marginalized women, girls, and gender diverse persons. UN Women plans to relocate one MPWC in the host community. Furthermore, UN Women will further standardize the operations of the MPWCs, and train local women-led organizations with the standards and technical tools so that they can take over the Centres by themselves independently in the future. Beyond the camps with the MPWCs, furthermore, 6 UN Women partners through WPHF provide women with accelerated learning and non-formal vocational training to strengthen socio-economic stability in Ukhiya, and Teknaf and camp 1E, 1W, 2W, 5, 6, 8E, 10, 14, 15, 16, 18, 19, 22, 24, and 26. UN Women continue to enhance their capacities in service delivery, and monitor their activities and results in the field.
In 2024, UN Women plans to provide three kinds of vocational training courses, sewing machine operation, bakery and pastry, and solar (PV) system installation and maintenance to the vulnerable Rohingya and host community women through its existing MPWCs. In the camps, a total of 2,600 women will benefit from the skills development opportunities (500 with vocational training, and 2,100 with non-formal technical training. Out of the 2,600 women, 1,000 will receive the accelerated learning before training to enhance feminist literacy skills. Upon training, 2,100 women will be provided with in-kind grants for self-employment. In the host community, 1,350 women (150 with vocational and 1,200 with non-formal technical training) in total will benefit from training opportunities and in-kind grants that support them to start business. In addition, 25 women will be provided with computers and access computer literacy training. Through the project, UN Women plans to mobilize 93 unskilled volunteers (55 Rohingya and 38 host community members) to support the training sites within the MPWCs within the camps. In addition, 24 women will receive monthly grants to run the shops in the Women’s Market in the camp 5. Overall, the project plans to refer 100 trainees as semi-skilled and skilled volunteers once they graduate from the vocational training.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"The Rohingya refugee and host community face a limited variety of livelihood opportunities. 2023 Joint-Multi Sector Needs Assessment (J-MSNA) Bangladesh shows that 53% of refugee households and 29% of the host community households reported no income/livelihood opportunities nearby and take coping mechanisms such as reducing essential non-food expenditures (education, health, clothes), including withdrawing children from school. The situation is deteriorating in 2023, as 91% refugee households and 62% of host community households reported they afford fewer goods and services compared to the year prior. This reflects the economic insecurity that both communities experience, especially for the refugee community which are dependent on humanitarian services and experience ration cut in the beginning of 2023. The decreasing income in households impacts women disproportionally, especially in the context where women have limited decision-making power and self-reliance. The livelihood opportunities for women to gain literacy skills and vocational skills are thus important, for them to increase their own self-reliance and resilience.According to the Assessment of Economic Opportunities for Young Rohingyas in Bangladesh report (Population Council, November 2022), in Bangladesh, two-thirds of male Rohingyas are economically active inside the camps, while 6 percent for females. Small business, tutoring, masonry, hawking, and mechanical work are the major economic activities for male Rohingyas. Tailoring and handicrafts are the principal economic activities of women. Additionally, some males are engaged as paid employees, while wage employment is much more limited among females. MSNA 2023 confirmed that high unemployment rate are observed in the host communities as well, particularly among youth and women. Not only job opportunities, but vocational training opportunities are also very limited inside the camps. The Assessment found that the percentage of Rohingyas who received training in Bangladesh (male 5%, female 9%). Though more women received training than men, the study also highlighted that there was less of a variety in trainings for women as opposed to men. Overall, females received training mostly on home-based work such as tailoring and handicrafts, whereas skills training for men ranged from agricultural to technical skills such as mechanical work, masonry, paramedical work, and computer operation. Women also face difficulty in enrolling vocational training opportunities as a low level of literacy affects involvement in training. The variety of training, including accelerated learning on literacy skills is crucial for women to access diversified livelihood opportunities, beyond home-based work, and enhance economic resilience against current economic challenges and future shocks, such as frequent natural disasters. In terms of access to business, the assessment found that less than 10 percent of young Rohingyas have been able to conduct small businesses inside the camps, for which they mostly use their own money while a few borrowed from others. The most common business for males was operating a grocery shop, followed by tea stalls and food shops. Females were involved in home-based businesses and earned substantially less than their male counterparts. Average monthly income from business for males was Bangladeshi Taka (BDT) 7,180 (USD 71) 1 and for females it was BDT 1,785 (USD 18). Increasing access to business is therefore important for women to secure a sufficient amount of income, and make it sustainable. "

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

"UN Women has been providing its livelihoods and skills development activities through Multi-Purpose Women’s Centers (MPWCs) since 2017, with the locations of the MPWCs agreed with RRRC and CiCs upon consultations, and building on inputs from stakeholders. The 8 MPWCs are run by UN Women’s implementing partners, ActionAid Bangladesh, BRAC, and OXFAM with PSEA and Child Safeguarding Focal Points in place. The 5 MPWCs are in the Rohingya camp 3, 4 , 4ext and 18 and 3 MPWCs is in the host communities Cox’s sadar, Ukhiya and Tenknaf. UN Women is reassessing the location of the MPWCs and is exploring the relocation of 2 of the MPWCs with the aim of ensuring they are serving underserved host community women.All UN Women implementing partners closely coordinate their livelihoods activities with relevant stakeholders, ensuring approval from the RRRC and CiCs, with the latter periodically visiting MPWCs to observe activities. UN Women also consults with Department of Women’s Affairs to work on the livelihood services and awareness-raising on gender equality in the host community. MPWCs provide a one-stop shop of access to multisectoral empowerment and protection services and support through an integrated service model, with agencies such as FAO, UNFPA and UNICEF providing key services for women and girls, inclusive of off farming activities, SRHR services, and secondary education for adolescent girls respectively. UN Women is a member of the LSDS, protection, and GBV sectors."

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The programme will ensure the safety, dignity, and rights of the targeted women, girls and gender-diverse population. The programme will implement do-no-harm principles. In addition to the livelihood services articulated above, the MPWCs provide protection services, such as psychosocial counselling and referral pathways, awareness-raising sessions on reducing GBV, child marriage, polygamy, and women's economic empowerment and leadership activities at one-stop. These services will reduce the impact of physical and psychological harm that arises from violence, coercion, deliberate deprivation, and other threats. Thus the operation of the MPWCs mainstream protection. Furthermore, the programme will assist training participants in claiming their rights through awareness-raising on women’s rights and gender equality, to facilitate access to appropriate services in a safe manner. The MPWC ensures providing a safe and open space for women, and thus the access by men is restricted.

3.3 - Age & Disability

Building on the principle of “leaving no one behind”, UN Women has been working with Organisations of Persons with Disabilities (OPDs) for promoting human rights, gender equality and non-discrimination against persons with disabilities. All UN Women staff, partners, Gender Field Officers, and gender volunteers receive training from OPDs on disability and inclusion. Contributing to the localization agenda, UN Women closely works with local women-led and women’s right organizations with the aim of enhancing their role in the response, strengthening their capacity to identify the needs and priorities of the most vulnerable women and girls from an intersectional lens, including disability, and address their specific challenges. WLOs/WROs are supported to prioritize support the most vulnerable groups, setting targets for persons with disabilities and providing tailored support to access interventions. UN Women ensures the safety and accessibility of persons with disability to the protection and livelihood services provided at the MPWCs. The MPWC is called a "peace centre" by the rohingya women and girls, considering privacy, safety, fire, and weather resistance in terms of overall protection. The livelihood services set a specific target to reach at least 10% refugee and host community women with disability, or women with family members who have disabilities. Staff engaging with the services are trained on the disability and inclusion before the service provision. Furthermore, UN Women ensures that a feedback and complaint mechanism system in place at the MPWCs is accessible for persons with disability, regardless of types of the disability by providing various forms of complaint available. UN Women continue to support the WLOs/WROs that target persons with disability to advocate for persons with disabilities at the inter-agency levels, and establish a dedicated disability helper function within the MPWCs to support women and girls with disabilities.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to women and girls is at the core of UN Women's humanitarian response. UN Women collaborates with communities to identify and address their specific challenges, emphasizing the meaningful involvement of crisis-affected women and girls in decision-making processes across the humanitarian program cycle. It aligns with the IASC Revised Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations and Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse by taking account of, giving understanding to, and being held to account by the people UN Women supports. UN Women emphasizes incorporating a gender analysis that considers intersecting identities throughout all phases of humanitarian programming. This approach aims to address those affected by the crisis, such as women and girls who may have diverse sexual orientations, gender identities, gender expressions and sex characteristics (SOGIESC), disabilities, and/ or migratory status. By preventing consequences that put women and girls at risk and addressing common gender biases in conflict analysis, UN Women provides a more accurate, comprehensive understanding of conflict's root causes, triggers, and drivers. UN Women's Gender Field Officers and gender volunteers across the camps are a conduite between communities, in particular women, girls, and gender diverse individuals, and the CiCs, camp management and wider response coordination. The GFOs and gender voluneers act as gender equality advocates; collecting feedback and complaints from their communities and addressing the issues through, understanding that the communities will feel comfortable and confident in sharing with them. This feedback is shared upwards as well into the response coordination through the GIHA WG.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

"UN Women prioritizes protecting individuals in all their diversity from sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) through robust governance frameworks and operations. With a survivor-centred approach aligned with the IASC’s core principles on SEA, it promotes compliance with humanitarian standards and advances for gender equality, women’s empowerment, and the protection of women’s rights in humanitarian action. UN Women is an active member of the PSEA network, Protection Sector, GBV sub-sectors and GiHA working group and ensures that it is abreast of sector discussions and strategy development.All UN Women staff, partners, Gender Field Officers (GFOs) and 65 gender volunteers working in the camps and host community are trained on PSEA including reporting mechanism, and signed inter-agency code of conduct. UN Women PSEA focal point and reference points are linked to the inter-agency PSEA network, and the focal point information is displayed in the office as well as the MPWCs. During the field work, partners, GFOs, and volunteers closely assess the gender situation in the camps and host community, including PSEA, and report and escalate issues following the reporting lines. UN Women also established the PSEA feedback and complaint mechanisms at the MPWCs, available in written and verbal forms through the box or the hotline number, and ensures the client confidentiality and safety while processing the cases.Furthermore, UN Women strengthens the capacities of local women-led organizations and women’s rights organizations (WLOs/WROs) in humanitarian principles including PSEA, and promote their participation into humanitarian coordination spaces through the localization agenda. UN Women will continue to collaborate with the PSEA network coordinator in the provision of related trainings, and facilitate the active engagement of WLOs/WROs in the network. "

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

"UN Women strives for a resilient and empowered community, placing emphasis on vulnerable groups of women and girls in disaster-prone areas, conducting capacity-building at the community level to strengthen disaster preparedness through community volunteers and local women’s organizations. UN Women plans to renovate the existing MPWCs and Women’s Market, which function as cyclone shelters for women, children and the elderly, to be more fire and weather resistant, enhancing durability and accessibility to enhance access among persons with disability and the elderly to shelters in times of need. In concurrence of scaling up its livelihood services, the MPWCs will provide 250 Rohingya refugee women and girls (50% women; 50% adolescent girls) and 300 Bangladeshi women and adolescent girls (50% women; 50% adolescent girls) capacity building training on disaster risk management. In the Women’s Market, furthermore, solar panels and their installation, electricity connectivity disaster preparedness equipment and material relief items will be procured to enhance availability of much needed items for emergency relief and preparedness. UN Women also works on capacity building at community-level to strengthen disaster preparedness through community volunteers and local women’s organizations. Existing community volunteers (gender volunteers and GBV response volunteers) will be supported to develop skills in disaster and emergency preparedness and response. "
