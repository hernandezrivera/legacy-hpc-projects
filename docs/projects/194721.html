Project Summary

This project aims to support sustainable peace and development and protect populations affected by the Explosive Ordnance (EO) (including landmines and Explosive Remnants of War (ERW)) problem through the mitigation of the threat posed by explosive hazards.
Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) will be promoted through different interventions including direct EORE, community-based approach, radio broadcasting, public information campaigns and distribution of awareness materials. At least 19,000 people from the people affected with the EO problem will receive EORE messages. Community Liaison Officers (CLOs) that accompanying the land release teams will provide EORE interventions to the affected communities in addition to their community liaison work. Gender issues will be considered through the implementation of EORE activities where women will be addressed through specific methodologies taking into account women roles in the communities and their specific responsibilities to assure the safety of their children and families. Most of the CLOs are normally ladies however EORE teams are gender balanced.
The contaminated lands will be released for humanitarian purposes and productive use. Land Release (LR) teams will be deployed to the targeted states and the village-by-village survey/assessment approach will be followed especially in Darfur states. LR activities including survey, nontechnical survey and clearance will be implemented through UNMAS contractors from national and international NGOs and companies. UNMAS is aiming to release about 1.5 million square meters of land and open at least 100 km of roads. Through this project UNMAS will ensure that the female deminers will be given equal competitive opportunity to be recruited with the LR teams.
The EO victims will be supported through strengthening and expanding the existing local capacities to coordinate physical, psycho-social, and economic rehabilitation and reintegration of mine/ERW survivors. Victim assistance will be enhanced in other MA activities such as land release and EORE activities. A swift referral pathway will be promoted and equal access to government institutions including medical care, rehabilitation, psychosocial support, education, social protection, and livelihood support.
Building the capacity of the relevant national institutes and national implementation will be considered to ensure quality delivery of MA interventions through direct training to the MA workers, supporting the quality assurance efforts, and providing training and the technical expertise to the National Mine Action Centre (NMAC) in different areas.

1 - Needs

Several long-lasting conflicts has led to the contamination with EO including Anti-Personnel (AP) mines, Anti-Tank (AT) mines as well as ERWs. South Kordofan maintains the highest contamination records of EO, followed by Blue Nile and West Kordofan, while Darfur states are affected by ERWs only. The presence of landmines and ERWs not only poses a direct threat of killing and injuring the local population, but it also limits their access to the basic life support facilities, to the livelihood activities and limits the access of the humanitarian aid reaching the vulnerable communities, explosive hazards contamination further hampers early recovery and development efforts. IDPs, returnees and refugees are at most risk due to the limited information/awareness about local landmine or ERW contamination within their settlement areas. Furthermore, and following the Juba Peace Agreement and peace negotiations which took place in 2021 with Al Hilu, the movement of civilian people and goods becomes high crossing lines heading to peace markets which increases the risk of interaction to landmine and ERW and hinders the humanitarian assistance delivery. Sudan has committed to comply with the Anti Personnel Mine Ban Convention (APMBC) known as Ottawa Treaty, under Article 5 to clear all known AP landmines by April 2023 however the government of Sudan is seeking extension of the deadline to meet its obligations under the APMBC or Ottawa Treaty. EO contamination remains one of the key concerns with an estimated 2.1 million men, women and children affected in Sudan (according to 2023 HNO analysis). To Date, IMSMA has registered 2,539 casualties as a result of EO accidents, including 1,901 people who have been injured and 638 fatalities. In 2022 boys and girls accounted for 38.4% of the known victims of landmines and ERW. In 2022, so far 80% of the victims were registered in Darfur states. EO contamination also limits access to food, livelihoods and markets, as people cannot fully engage in agricultural and animal grazing activities. It also puts people at risk when fetching water or collecting firewood. Furthermore, explosive ordnance effects extended to hinder early recovery and the development activities of communities. EORE remains insufficient to the scale of needs, building resilience and adapting safe behaviour is essential for communities’ safety and wellbeing. As of Q3 2022, Sudan Mine Action Programme had registered 5,015 hazardous areas with 221 newly registered in 2022. A total of 4,624 areas - covering more than 137.6 square kilometres of land - have been released and handed over to communities for productive use including 870,942 sqm of land release in 2022. Additionally, more than 38,529 km of roads have been opened since 2002. Even though there are about 34.8 square kilometres of land in 391 locations across Sudan confirmed to be contaminated by EOs and hundreds of kilometres of roads suspected to be contaminated with landmines/ERW blocking access to services, socioeconomic activities, or delivery of humanitarian services are waiting for clearance activities. However, still the actual level of contamination is not yet fully known due to inaccessibility and movement restrictions. In 2022, UNMAS caried out several need assessments in South Kordofan and Blue Nile states either independently or jointly with other UN sister agencies identifying the needs for MA interventions. These assessments resulted in identifying roads that are safe to be used by people and humanitarian workers or determining alternative roads. Also, through the assessment missions’ activities prioritization were discussed with the stakeholders including affected communities with consideration given to the gender and age categories views.According to the HNO severity analysis, 38 localities were reported to be impacted by EO, out of which 19 are ranked as being very highly or highly impacted.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The activities of this project include provision of EORE interventions to ensure people at risk know how to mitigate the threat of EO and light weapons promoting safety among them. At least 19 thousand people from the at-risk population including host communities IDPs, and returnees as well as humanitarian workers will benefit from the EORE activities in recognizing, avoiding, and reporting hazards to minimize the threat of EO accidents. The EORE activities are including disseminating EORE messages through direct and indirect sessions (indirect sessions could be through radio broadcasting, TV, social media and etc). EO affected communities will receive community-based (CB) EORE through training of community focal points, key persons and volunteers including women and children. The CB EORE activities will also be given to the social workers, teachers, health workers and other active people in the communities including women to deliver EORE to their respected communities’ especial communities in remote or inaccessible areas. Humanitarian workers including UN personnel, UNITAMS, NGOs personnel will receive EO safety briefings for their own or colleagues safety. Information Education Communication (IEC) materials will be distributed through the EORE activities. To ensure the UN, NGOs and the local populations safely use areas previously contaminated with explosive ordnance LR activities including survey and clearance will be caried out. Safe accesses will be opened to residential land, agriculture land, infrastructure, roads, access to basic services, restoring livelihood capacities as well as safe access for the humanitarian actors to enable the delivery of life-saving aid. The activities will include village by village assessment approach to identify the contaminated areas and set the priorities by considering the views of the affected communities including gender and age categories. Women views will be highly considered considering their roles in the communities. About 1.5 million square meters of land will be released for productive use through survey, nontechnical survey and/or clearance operations. At least 100 kilometres of roads will be opened through assessment, verification and/or clearance activities. To improve the management of the mine action programme, the national authorities including the National Mine Action Center (NMAC) will be supported. The support to the NMAC will include supporting the accreditation of MA NGOs and teams, and the monitoring and quality assurance efforts. Internal and external trainings to NMAC and national operators will be provided to strengthen their technical capacity. Also, the project activities will include advocacy for stronger recruitment policies for women in mine action partners. In 2021, UNMAS trained 21 female deminers in its efforts to empower women in mine action. UNMAS will monitor the recruitment process of the demining teams to ensure the female deminers have given fare competitive opportunities. The life condition of the explosive accident’s survivors will be improved through promoting their social reintegration and identifying referral pathways to services. Victim assistance interventions will be enhanced and integrated in other mine actions activities including EORE and LR activities. Survey will be undertaken to enhance the data of EO victims. Information about service facilities for victim’s referral will be collected and provided to the victims. The VA activities also will include promoting EO victims’ rights and access to services, community engagement, mainstreaming victim assistance in the relevant services (medical care, rehabilitation, psychosocial support, education, social protection, and livelihood support), provision of medical care, prosthetics and assistive devices, psychological counselling, and income generation kits.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

UNMAS will ensure that mine action activities are carried out in compliance with theSudan National Mine Action Standards (SNMAS) and International Mine ActionStandards (IMAS) for what concerns environmental preservation. UNMAS and NMACwill identify and assess relevant environmental aspects and determine appropriate andeffective measures to mitigate adverse impacts of explosive detonations on soil, airand immediate surrounding environment. 6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Mine action is basically a protection intervention aiming to protect people lives fromthe threat of landmines and explosive remnants of war, MA sub sector ensures equalparticipation, consultation and inclusion of affected populations in the planning andimplementation of mine action programs as well as ensuring that the differentexposure to and knowledge of risks related to explosive ordnance among women, girls,boys and men from diverse groups is incorporated into risk reduction efforts, includingclearance and risk education. It will also facilitate and support multi-sectoralassistance to victims of explosive ordnance that is responsive to the needs of women,girls, men and boys from diverse groups, and advocate for the mainstreaming ofgender and diversity considerations in national mine action policies, institutions andprograms. Additionally, mine action implementation partners are encouraged to ensurethey “do no harm” to the environment or livelihoods, and avoid increasing the longterm vulnerability of affected communities. Therefore, this project considers protectionmainstreaming through all phases of the project starting from the project conceptthroughout project design, implementation and closure of the project.
