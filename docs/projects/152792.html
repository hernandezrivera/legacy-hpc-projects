Project Summary

This project is expected to mobilize 984,129 United States dollars to provide emergency lifesaving health services for 16,393 IDPs, Host communities & returnees in Kajo-Keji, Yei, Morobo and Magwi counties over a period of 1 year. The locations targeted are mapped as Ebola risk counties due to the outbreak of Ebola in Neighboring Congo. These areas are still experiencing active fighting despite the peace agreement. As a result of the conflict, a total of 85, 621 people are displaced; 66,326 in Yei, 6925 in Morobo, 5855 in Kajo-Keji and 7145 in Magwi (Health cluster, PIN 2019). While environmental conditions are ripe for vector growth and disease transmission, there are inadequate health services due to lack of trained health personnel, functional health facilities and increased attacks on health care increasing the potential for disease outbreaks.
The proposed intervention is expected to provide lifesaving health care to the IDPS and host community by implementing essential primary healthcare emergency clinical packages targeting the most vulnerable displaced populations (PHCU, PHCC& Mobile teams), immunizing the most vulnerable displaced groups (Children U5,PLW), Implementing outbreak investigation and response (IDSR) and Mainstreaming Accountability to Affected People. Five thousand four hundred and thirty four (5434) IDPS & Host community in Yei, 3,809 in Morobo, 3220 in Kajo-Keji and 3930 IDPs, Host communities & Returnees in Magwi will be targeted.

Needs

Recent fighting (ongoing) in Kajo-Keji, Yei and Morobo has displaced 66,326 in Yei, 6925 in Morobo, 5855 in Kajo-Keji while previous fighting in Magwi displaced another 7145 people (IRNA Kajo-Keji, 2018, RRC Yei & Morobo and Health Cluster PIN, 2019) A September 2018 IPC update indicated that Kajo-Keji is at an emergency phase 4, Magwi at crises phase 3 while Yei & Morobo had no sufficient data to reach a classification however recent indicators suggest crises/emergency. The key drivers to vulnerability are armed conflict (Kajo-Keji, Morobo & Yei), limited agricultural activities and markets; disease outbreaks, low EPI coverage and inadequate health facilities. According to a REACH Assessment report for central & Eastern Equatoria (September, 2018), 35% of the community reported lack of access to health facilities within a 30 minutes walking distance. The report indicated a high prevalence of malaria at 46% followed by Typhoid fever at 10% and Hepatitis at 7%. Based on this deteriorating health situation and in line with the health cluster response plan 2019, TRI SS plans to reach 16,393 people with health interventions using Essential Health care clinical packages (PHCU, PHCC and Mobile teams). The proposed project will support 2 PHCCs, 5 PHCUs and 4 Mobile teams in Kajo-Keji, Yei, Morobo and Magwi and targets 5434 IDPS & Host community in Yei, 3,809 in Morobo, 3220 in Kajo-Keji and 3930 IDPs, Host communities & Returnees in Magwi.

Activities

• Implementing essential primary healthcare emergency clinical packages (2 PHCCs, 5 PHCUs & 4 mobile teams) targeting the most vulnerable displaced populations • Immunizing 5,000 most vulnerable displaced groups (Children U5,PLW) • Implementing outbreak investigation and response • Mainstreaming Accountability to Affected People

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

In line with IASC guidelines (2012, 3) and TR-SS’s draft policy on accountability to affected population, we will continue to streamline and implement appropriate and robust mechanisms to ensure beneficiaries stay informed about project activities at every stage of the project lifecycle. These activities include;i. Project inception meetings,ii. Project quarterly review meetings,iii. Project close out meetings

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The messages are designed in consultation with the community and relate to the role of the community in supporting the project activities, planning and monitoring. It openly addresses concerns of the community in regards to the project activities and how these impact their health and living.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

• As a standard practice, all project staff and volunteers will sign a code of conduct as an annex to their contracts which explicitly mentions prohibition of sexual exploitation and abuse of affected communities• TRI-SS has a trained focal person on protection from sexual exploitation and abuse and we have clearly defined channels through which to report abuse to the focal person.• We have standard operating procedures for handling of complaints ranging from every day programme issues, to allegations of sexual exploitation, abuse and corruption

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

• TRI-SS conducts consultative meetings on quarterly basis with stakeholders to identify emerging needs and draw a community oriented plan of action. Such consultative meetings are embedded into the project proposal with clear indicators to monitor the number of consultations conducted, the type of community consultations conducted and the number of people who participated in the consultations

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Beneficiaries are briefed on how to raise their complaints regarding project implementation and other related issues to the organization. While we conduct focus group discussions with community and stakeholders to listen to their complaints, there are mechanisms in place to report urgent issues like sexual exploitation to the field coordinator and to the focal person for immediate action. We try to make sure concerns raised by stakeholders and the community are addressed and feedback provided. We make sure sensitive issues are handled with due respect and confidentially and that the community is aware of the available approaches and channels of complaints and feedback.

In line with IASC principles (2013), this project will ensure,• Project activities are implemented in such a way that it does not cause further harm to the affected population• Equal and impartial access to services in proportion to need and without discrimination,• Affected communities are protected from physical and psychological harm arising from violence and coercion.• Affected people are assisted to claim their rights, access available remedies, and recover from the effects of abuse

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

• Training of health workers, County health directors and community leaders on the need to ensure protection sensitive approaches in project implementation including mitigation measures,• Monitoring and reporting of abuse to relevant persons through established channels,• Establishing referral mechanisms for survivors of abuse as a result of the project activities,• Sensitization of health workers and community to accept and not stigmatize survivors of sexual, gender based violence,• Providing psychosocial support as a psychological first aid to survivors of violence and referring survivors to appropriate care facilities
