Project Summary

The aim of the project is to provide emergency food support on a temporary basis to the detention centers and provision of nutritious food packages to most vulnerable detained migrants and refugees including pregnant and lactating women, children, elderly and people with medical needs.

Needs

There are between 6,000 and 10,000 migrants and refugees detained in the government held detention centers in Libya at any given time. Throughout 2018, on many occasions, these migrants and refugees were faced with lack or insufficient amount of nutritious food, leading to significant health and nutrition concerns. Pregnant and lactating women, children, elderly as well as people suffering from medical conditions are particularly affected by this situation. In situations where the authorities are unable to address these ongoing needs (due to access or capacity dealing with the increased number of detainees for example during the hostilities in Tripoli in September 2018), IOM provided emergency food for several thousand detained refugees and migrants as a life-saving measure. With the unpredictable nature of the situation and the ongoing detention regime for refugees and migrants, there is a need to continue providing nutrition and food assistance for vulnerable migrants and refugees in detention.

Activities

- Provision of emergency food rations (up to max 5 days)- Provision of nutritious food packages for pregnant and lactating women, children, elderly as well as people suffering from medical conditions

Indicate any study/assessment that has been used, if any

Considering the dire food situation in the detention centers, provision of food to detained migrants and refugees should only be undertaken when an emergency situation arises or to meet the needs of extremely vulnerable individuals.The approach of humanitarian actors in Libya (formerly members of the Mixed Migration Working Group), has thus far opposed the systematic provision of food inside detention centres to avoid further sustaining and institutionalizing a system of arbitrary detention. This project will focus on food only on an emergency basis. In order to avoid the creation of expectations amongst the migrant/refugee population and detention centre managers, IOM will undertake sensitization about the temporary nature of the food support. Measures to prevent, monitor and respond to gender based violence or sexual exploitation associated with distributions will be considered and relevant considerations may include implementing distribution for women and men separately to minimize exposing women and girls to sexual harassment in the distribution areas. Physical security risk due to conflict and access barriers for certain groups including those with medical conditions will be taken into account. Gender balance of distribution teams for will be considered to ensure that there are enough women available undertaking distributions especially for pregnant and lactating women.
