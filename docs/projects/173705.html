Project Summary

Street Child will enhance the protection of 150,000 (55% girls) conflict-affected children through the provision of targeted case management, PSS services, and Covid 19 messaging, as well as strengthening of community-based protection mechanisms. Key child protection needs and gaps across high-priority LGAs in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa states will be targeted, through a holistic and integrated approach. Specific focus will be on the most at-risk children, including unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), children associated with armed groups (CAAG) and children at-risk of gender-based violence (GBV), and Street Child will seek to address immediate needs as well as work with parents, caregivers, communities and stakeholders to address deeper root causes of child protection risks and strengthen the child protection system. To prevent and mitigate the impact of COVID19 spread, children and communities will be equipped with key age appropriate life-saving knowledge.
In Yobe, LGAs which will be targeted are Gujba and Gulani. In Adamawa, the targeted LGAs will be Mubi, Michika and Madagali. MMC, Jere, Gwoza, Damboa, Monguno, Biu, Michika and Gujba. In Borno the targeted LGAs will be MMC, Jere, Gwoza, Damboa, Monguno, Biu, Mobbar, Dikwa and Konduga.

1 - Needs

An estimated 1.8 million children and adolescents in North East Nigeria are in need of child protection services. At least 6,000 children (over 50% girls) have been recruited, abducted or victims of violent attacks since the start of the conflict, and at least 15,250 children (over 50% girls) have been affected by risks including abuse, exploitation, family separation, neglect and violence. Girls and boys may be exposed to different types of risks, with girls more likely to face SGBV and boys more likely to be at risk of recruitment. The risks faced are also age-specific; particular risks are heightened for adolescents, such as underage recruitment into armed groups, sexual exploitation, child labour, early pregnancy, early marriage and other reproductive health issues. There is therefore a need to design more gender-specific initiatives which meet to the different risks and capacities of girls, boys, women and men. Case management of acutely at-risk children, including UASC and CAFAAG, has been identified as a significant gap in the current response in the region. There is a need to improve FTR services, inter-sectoral referrals and follow-up, and the provision of alternative care. Coordination mechanisms are weak, further hampering the execution and timely follow-up of referrals made. Analysis of cases management cases at the sector level has indicated that children living with vulnerable caregivers are amongst the most likely to face protection risks. An estimated 110,700 parents and caregivers are in need of support to be able to care appropriately for their children and avoid negative coping mechanisms. Certain groups face heightened risks and are more in need of support than others: female-headed households, for example, are amongst the most vulnerable. Street Child's integrated approach of working holistically with families on child protection, education and livelihoods and seeking to build capacity of caregivers to provide for their children is therefore relevant and timely. The severity of needs is highest in Borno state, with the needs in 12 LGAs rated as 'severe'. This includes the LGAs of MMC, Jere, Gwoza, Damboa, Biu and Bama. In Yobe state, 2 LGAs are rated as having 'severe' needs, including Gujba. In Adamawa state, the needs in Michika LGA are rated as 'severe'. A final key need is the limited institutional and technical capacity of national child protection partners, particularly in providing quality case management services and timely referrals. Linked to this, there is a need to strengthen field-level coordination and build LGA-level capacity.As of 3rd December 2020 Nigeria covid 19 confirmed cases have increased to 68,303 confirmed cases with 1,179 fatalities across the country. Cases have been registered in 36 states and the Federal Capital Territory (FCT), with Lagos and the FCT reporting the highest number of cases. While the cases in the north east has significantly dropped with 25 new cases in the NE being reported on 3rd December, there is need for continued community sensitizations and provision of PPEs to minimize covid 19 infections. Children with covid 19 in NE Nigeria are reported as losing or being separated from their primary caregivers due to quarantine or confinement measures, thus are at increased risk of neglect, abandonment, violence and exploitation. They may also lack access to health treatment, and suffer mental health and psychosocial impacts, as well as malnutrition. An infectious disease outbreaks as COVID19 is going exacerbates the existing risks and needs in NE Nigeria.

2 - Activities

Street Child will provide psychosocial support (PSS) to 88,465 children through after-school recreational activities and peer clubs facilitated by trained PSS facilitators. The activities will be tailored to the different gendered and age-specific needs of different children and will address topics such as health and hygiene, Covid 19, GBV prevention and child rights. Caregivers will also be engaged in PSS activities to promote positive parenting skills. Child Help Desks will run alongside PSS activities, providing more tailored support for children identified as at-risk (including referral for specialised services as needed) and supported by trained clinical psychologists. PSS facilitators will be trained community members to build community capacity and improve sustainability. 1,535 children affected by protection risks (including GBV survivors, CAAG and UASC) will be targeted for tailored case management, including a tailored care plan and regular follow up from dedicated case workers. Street Child's services include mental health and psychosocial support, family tracing and reunification (FTR) for UASC, socio-economic reintegration of CAAG, the provision of protection kits and GBV case management. In addition, referrals will be made for specialised services including medical support and legal support and follow-up conducted for all referrals made. Capacity building to local partners and actors will include the strengthening of field-level referral pathways and developing LGA-capacity to monitor and respond to needs. In addition to traditional case management services, CAAG will receive tailored socio-economic reintegration support, including vocational skills training and resettlement grants or education and life skills training, as appropriate. Street Child will also work with communities through adolescent and community activities to advocate for reintegration and acceptance of CAAG, and to reduce stigmatisation. As well as providing FTR services to UASC, a focus will be on improving care providing by alternative or interim caregivers, including foster parents. Positive parenting skills training will target these caregivers (both male and female), and the situation of children in alternative care arrangements will be monitored at least once a month, with the aim of identifying any challenges and providing foster parents with support as needed. Key actors and stakeholders (such as MoWASD) will be engaged to screen and monitor foster parents as well as supported to develop sector-wide guidelines on alternative care, building capacity and strengthening the system as a whole. Parents and caregivers of the most at-risk children will also be supported with livelihood activities, which aim to build sustainable income-generating opportunities and ensure that increased household income improves the wellbeing of their children (including through enrolment in school). In all target communities, Street Child will build community-based protection and response mechanisms, establishing or strengthening community-based child protection committees (CBCPCs) and mapping referral pathways to empower community members to be able to identify child protection concerns and respond. CBCPCs will also be the entry point for working with communities for disaster risk reduction (DRR), identifying and mitigating major risks and dangers to children's safety and security. Street Child is committed to localisation and will work through local partners to implement project activities and provide tailored mentoring and capacity building to strengthen technical and organisational capacity. Furthermore, Street Child will provide technical support to the Ministry of Women Affairs and Social Development (MoWASD) to develop a guideline on alternative care for Borno State. The guidance will improve the quality of services and implementation of minimum standards for working with children who require alternative care.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Street Child is committed to ensuring that protection is central to all of its activities and projects, acknowledging that prioritising protection, safety, dignity, accountability and minimising any unintended negative effects of our activities are crucial to meaningful, dignified access to support in the communities where we work. To achieve this in this proposed project, specific risks at all project stages have been identified and mitigation strategies integrated into the project design, clear protection reporting and response mechanisms have been included, and protection mainstreaming indicators have been included in the monitoring and evaluation framework. At the beneficiary selection stage, Street Child will use tested vulnerability assessment tools and clear selection process to ensure that beneficiaries are selected based on need and through predetermined and clearly communicated criteria of vulnerability. In order to ensure participation and minimise inter-group tensions, targets have been set to include different communities (IDP, host community and returnee) as well as both men and women. Community leaders and members will be informed of the selection process and criteria during the inception stages, and continuous sensitisation of the selection criteria will be used to ensure that possible intra- and inter-group tensions are minimised. Sensitisation on these topics through regular community visits and feedback sessions has proven successful during past projects, where dissatisfaction about not being included in the project has been reduced by clearly explaining the aims of the project and how beneficiaries were selected. As part of our commitment to accountability and protection, Street Child commits to work towards the elimination of exploitation and abuse in the communities where we works and in our engagement with vulnerable populations. Street Child has a clear safeguarding policy in place which applies to all personnel and affiliates, including implementing partners and community members who are affiliated to our work. All Street Child staff is trained on the safeguarding policy and is aware of their responsibilities. Similarly, all partner staff receive a training on Street Child’s policy and how to report incidents. This training is also given to all community members who are affiliated to the project – community leaders, PMC members, community committee members and community volunteers, for example. Sensitisation during activities through community forums and through the use of visuals such as posters also seeks to empower community members to better understand their rights, and to know how to report any concerns they may have. Finally, Street Child is an active participant of the PSEA working group in Borno, as an extra level of accountability, and follows the best practices and lessons learned shared through this working group.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

SC will utilise proven mechanisms for engaging stakeholders and beneficiaries throughout all stages of project planning, implementation and evaluation. A needs assessment will be conducted before implementation, and will include focus group discussions (FGDs) will different community groups - boys, girls, men and women. FGDs will also be held at baseline and endline to provide community members with the opportunity to give feedback and to understand community satisfaction with the project. A dedicated complaints mechanism (CRM) officer will be identified for all targeted communities. This person will be responsible for collecting feedback and complaints, and liaising between community members, partners and Street Child to share this feedback. The CRM officer will hold weekly help desk sessions in each community, where beneficiaries and community members can share feedback. A register of all complaints made, and responses to these complaints, will be kept. In addition, weekly meetings of community coalitions such as CBCPCs will be attended by either local partner or Street Child staff to collect feedback and share progress with regards to specific child protection concerns. Men and women will be equally represented in the CBCPCs. Anonymous feedback mechanisms will also be established in each community. Anonymous complaints boxes will be placed in each community, and will be checked weekly. Street Child will provide a toll-free line which can be called at any time by community members to provide feedback or report urgent concerns (such as safeguarding concerns). All feedback received through these diverse mechanisms will be fed into review sessions and management decisions, allowing activities to be adapted to respond to feedback where appropriate in line with Street Child's commitment to accountability and evidence-based learning.
