Project Summary

The main objective of this project is to strengthen the interface between humanitarian action and sustainable livelihoods and economic recovery while building the resilience and self-reliance of vulnerable communities in Syria and delivering sustainable and integrated interventions. The proposed four main areas of interventions will be as follows:
a) Rural Economic Recovery through Rural-based Sustainable Livelihoods; b) Inclusive and sustainable Micro-Small and Medium Enterprises recovery and development; c) Market-driven vocational and skills training and employment services; and finally d) Sustainable job creation for Persons with Disabilities (PwDs) provided with physical support.
Under the first component, UNDP aims to support sustainable and climate smart agricultural livelihoods through supporting community-based sustainable agricultural activities, developing value chains on key commodities, supporting new innovative business ideas for rural areas, and the establishment and management of cooperatives, farmers’ markets and rural development centres targeting a total of 150 men and women. Under the second component, UNDP aims to support both start-up, recovery and acceleration of new and existing MSMEs, targeting 600 men and women. Support will include entrepreneurship training and start-up support, recovery support, and acceleration support with advance business training, access to inclusive finance and access to market. Under the third component, UNDP aims to result in job creation beyond provision of vocational and skills training through providing employment services such as job matching, targeting 100 men and women through OJT and 70 men and women will be supported to be specialized trainers, coaches and mentors. In addition to provision of soft skill training, career counseling and internship opportunities to 100 youth at higher education and graduates, and provision of support to 35 graduation projects to be start-up microbusinesses.
Finally, under the forth component, UNDP aims to continue supporting PWDs with both physical rehabilitation and socioeconomic inclusion of PWDs; moreover, prosthetic services will be provided through already established prosthetic workshops for physical rehabilitation of which support will enable PWDs to be ready to access to job opportunities. Activities will include support of existing three workshops for continued and expanded services, in addition to selecting 234 PWDs with lower limb amputation and support the to provide lower limb prosthetic devices and other rehabilitation treatment to selected beneficiaries, and providing them with sustainable livelihoods opportunities. Types of livelihoods support will be identified based on the feasibilities of PWDs and also market and livelihoods conditions in targeted areas.

4 - Needs

The SEIA report, conducted by UNCT in 2021 highlights rapidly deteriorating socio-economic condition of the vulnerable populations in Syria due to multiplicity of crisis, including the following: Unemployment rates reached unprecedented levels of 44% for those over 15 years and exceeded 60% for youth. The overall poverty rate reached to 86% at the end of 2019. Moreover, nearly 40% of respondents experienced a loss of employment for at least one male member of the HH, while 14% experienced such loss for at least one female member of the household due to COVID-19. The overall food security situation has deteriorated in many parts of Syria over the past year. Food security monitoring shows an overall worsening trend in indicators during 2020, reaching unprecedented levels in June 2020. Finally, COVID-19 impacts have further exacerbated challenges that persons with disabilities face. Prevalence of injuries, impairment and disability during the crisis is extremely high with 30,000 monthly traumatic injuries resulting in 9,000 monthly permanent disability cases, including: 450 monthly spinal cord injuries and 1,350 monthly amputation cases. Without jobs, the living conditions of Syrians and their livelihoods prospects have significantly eroded, severely increasing their vulnerability and undermining their ability to cope with the consequences of the conflict and additional layers of crisis including Lebanon and COVID.

5 - Implementation of activities

UNDP will provide technical support and oversight on the quality implementation of the project and will take the lead in the overall implementation of the project in close collaboration with the different stakeholders. The project team will consist of both programme management and technical teams on the ground that will develop a detailed plan based on the implementation strategies. Moreover, the UNDP field staff will share their expertise in community mobilization, beneficiary selection, data collection and data analysis, awareness raising activities. In addition, UNDP will follow its procurement protocols during project implementation. UNDP will setup a solid and transparent selection criterion of beneficiaries, although there may be variances in the vulnerability criteria in the different communities, UNDP will work with the local communities to prevent discrimination and ensure inclusion of the most vulnerable groups. To further ensure the participation of the most vulnerable groups, UNDP will follow-up with the selected beneficiaries who did not benefit from the project activities in order to ensure that the selection criteria has been implemented correctly. The project team will provide regular, impartial monitoring through direct field monitoring visits, occasional, remote telephone monitoring, and indicator data tracking mechanisms. This is intended to provide a regular assessment of the project for appropriate course corrective actions.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk : Resources and response capacities being limited and far below know needs, there will always be perception of biased targeting of beneficiaries or of biased selection of response activities, thereby creating or exacerbating tension; Mitigation measures: 1. Perform conflict analysis before planning the response; 2. Ensure that a comprehensive needs analysis informs the response formulation, and that this is openly documented and made transparent; 3. Make sure those in similar situation of need, at least within the geographical areas of response, area reached equally; 4. Obtain validation of the prioritization and of the formulated response from a representative community group, or through well-selected key informants and/or participation mechanisms; 5. Communicate the planned response, and the rationale behind, to the communities concerned; 6. Efficiently deliver on the obligation of ‘Accountability to Affected Populations’, by keeping the communities concerned regularly informed of the project and its implementation; 7. Make sure some process of receiving complaints and of responses to complaints is put in place; 8. Have in reserve a strategy in case the planned response does unexpectedly create tension (e.g. add-on of other activities or change in activities, in consultation with concerned communities.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:1- Levels of support to targeted households are large enough to meet needs on the ground2- Activities supported by the UNDP are coherent and lessons can be consolidated to have an impact on policy and implementation.3- Approvals are granted by local government to start project implementation4- The exchange rate of local currency is stable. Risk: 1- Needs on the ground are higher than available resources.2- Factors of implementation change making a change in the policy or implementation plan a necessity 3- Delay in implementing project activities due to delay in receiving consent from related government bodies.4- Rapid fluctuation of local currency will affect negatively the procurement process where some supplier might withdraw their offers in case of currency depreciation.Mitigation measures:1- UNDP will apply solid selection criteria methods of beneficiaries. 2- UNDP will plan for different implementation methods to be able to adapt with abrupt changes.3- UNDP will advocate through different means to chase up the approval process. 4- UNDP will speed up the bidding process to reduce the risk accompanied to currency fluctuation.
