Project Summary

The proposed project aims to allow the most vulnerable and food insecure households to meet their basic food and non-food needs as well as supporting livelihood protection and recovery. This will be achieved through emergency cash-based assistance combined with livelihood input provision and training as well as critical livelihood asset rehabilitation.

1 - Activities

SO 1: the partners propose multi-purpose cash transfers (MPCT) to address basic food and non-food needs through a combination of modalities including unconditional cash (UCT), cash-for-work (CfW) and ‘Cash+’ a combination of cash and livelihood (agricultural or livestock-related) inputs. Approximately 34,050 people over six months in urban, peri urban and rural areas will be targeted, with modality choices based on market monitoring and community preference. Vouchers may be prioritised in rural areas if essential commodities are scarce. Careful attention will be given to complementing other ongoing and planned lean season assistance and urban livelihood programming through the Cluster and CWG.SO 2: approximately 6,850 farming households in rural areas will receive nutrition and season-sensitive emergency crop and livestock input assistance (and related training); a combination of cash-for-work and technical support will be provided to rehabilitate or construct critical community assets, including improved water points (complementing ACF’s WASH programme); FAO’s ‘Cash +’ approach will be adapted to support livelihood recovery and protection providing households with MPCT as well as agricultural and livestock production inputs; given the highly inflationary environment, partners will promote the formation of asset-based VSLA groups amongst motivated cash beneficiaries as a means of preserving the value of any savings, with a focus on women, through the provision of livelihood and financial literacy training to support female economic empowerment and household nutrition (small livestock as a good source of protein). This will complement partners’ Community Based Model for Stunting reduction through Care Groups promoting good health and nutrition seeking behaviours.Target areas are in Masvingo and Manicaland Provinces:Chiredzi urban; Chiredzi rural; Mwenezi; Chivi; Chipinge Urban; Mutare Urban; Mutare Rural with a possibility of expansion to other districts where ACF has a presence if gaps are observed. Cash Transfer Value and modality choice will be guided by rural and urban MEB as defined by CWG as well as local market monitoring. According to current estimates, the transfer value for rural and urban areas would be approx. USD12 per person per month. Urban areas are expected to recover more quickly from COVID-related shocks with the lifting of lockdown so the duration of transfers could be reduced if this is verified. Transfer duration is currently foreseen at 6months, to be finally determined based on seasonality and need also depending on project start date and funding timeline. In urban areas cash transfers are expected to last approximately from March to August in line with current project projections. In rural areas, efforts will be made to ensure transfers cover the lean season during the second half of the year and ideally into early 2022. Type of ag and livestock inputs will vary according to seasonality, beneficiary preference, market availability and agro-ecological zone but indicative provisions are in attached excel. Overlap is estimated at 20% between beneficiary groups in rural areas. Total beneficiary number is estimated at 61,450 persons or 12,290 HHs. The project proposes a series of measures to ensure approaches which give equal opportunities to men, women and boys and girls to access different interventions. This will include:- Male engagement to let them appreciate the value of women participation in leadership; Promoting Gender sensitive work norms; project meetings at convenient places and times for security of both men and women; Use of labour and energy saving technologies/tools; Promote the targeting of women and child headed households and the elderly during registration; Promote the registration of women for small livestock; Collect Sex and Age disaggregated Data (SADD) and monitor impact of the project on gender dynamics; Flexibility so beneficiaries can access services at convenient times.

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

ACF and NAZ have a series of AAP guidelines and measures in place. The project values ethical considerations in the design and implementation of activities. This has been done through:- Training staff and service providers who interact with our beneficiaries on AAP with the aim of putting the needs of affected persons at the heart of the emergency response.- Field assessments and consultations with target population for prioritization of their needs and delivery mechanisms. ACF’s Cash Feasibility Study, Market monitoring, PDMs and lessons learned from ongoing programming will be used to inform modality selection. - The project will utilise existing beneficiary feedback mechanisms (toll free line, suggestion boxes and interactive-voice recording remote monitoring tools). This enables project staff to listen, collect, analyse raised complaints and concerns and giving prompt feedback for continual improvement. - Increased engagement with beneficiaries in vernacular and through their preferred communication channels promotes responsive monitoring as a continuous process to track humanitarian assistance.- Active engagement and participation and building on people’s own knowledge, capacities and experiences so that they are at the centre of decision-making.- Post distribution monitoring surveys will provide information related to safety, security and safety of the beneficiaries are in line with set guidelines and the expectations of the beneficiaries.Additionally, the project will ensure disability inclusion through: - working with community leadership structures and department of social welfare for the identification and vetting of the most in need. - consultation with men, women, boys, girls, the elderly, and persons with disabilities to understand their needs and preferences for location, design, and methodology of assistance. - Direct observation and discussion with groups and representatives of the community to identify the adaptions that are needed for the most vulnerable.- Ensuring project vulnerability criteria considers people living with disabilities and that such disabilities do not in any way exclude them from participating, particularly in CVA programmes.- The project is explicit about the selection criteria of the beneficiaries, including ensuring the inclusion of special vulnerable groups such as those living with disabilities. Our approach is to ensure that key stakeholders are consulted on the criteria for selection of the beneficiaries. Considerations such as the distance travelled by the beneficiaries to access services, access to transport system, the number of household members etc will inform how the services will be provided to the beneficiaries.ACF and NAZ will ensure programming addresses protection, GBV, SRH and PSEA issues ensuring referrals to adequate structures and contributions to specialised hotlines. The design of the project will prioritize the safety and dignity of the affected population through:- Comprehensive context-specific analysis including of cost efficiency, secondary market impacts, the flexibility of the transfer, targeting and risks of insecurity and corruption should inform the choice of programme. - Gender and age-specific saftey and security is incorporated into project design. E.g. Establish communication channels to report on sexual assault and robbery while travelling to and from a distribution points, theft, intimidation and extortion. This is backed by the organisations’ relevant policy frameworks.- Continuous assessment through post distribution monitoring and feedback mechanisms to assess whether the impact of cash or vouchers on household dynamics and gender relations. - The principle of Do No Harm to the beneficiaries is integrated throughout the project cycle. This process is solidified through key stakeholder engagements, selection of the beneficiaries without discrimination and provision of equitable access to services provided by the project.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project promotes care and awareness of environmental issues. Awareness campaigns will be given on environmental conservation, use of fuel-efficient stoves, solar power for drawing water and food preservation and use of improved farming practices which include Pfumvudza (Zero tillage) which is being promoted by the government.Farmers will also be encouraged to use organic fertilizers thus reducing on reduce use of harmful chemicals which may negatively impact on the environment.
