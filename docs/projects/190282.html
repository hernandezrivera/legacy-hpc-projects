Project Summary

The situation in northern Ethiopia continues to be tense and volatile. The hostilities continue to affect civilian lives, their safety and restricting their access to livelihoods, increasing the humanitarian needs notably with a continuing ad growing number of displaced people and hindering humanitarian access and delivery of aid to affected areas.
The humanitarian situation in the Afar region remains very severe and the service provision across all sectors remains suboptimal. The target woredas have been selected based on the actual needs on the ground. Furthermore, due to the recent dynamics of displacement and the districts being inaccessible for several months before June, providing any assistance in these areas was not possible earlier, leaving them with severe unaddressed needs.
According to different needs assessments conducted in the regions have indicated lack of access to basic services and, needs and gaps in service provision which exacerbates protection risks of women and girls in the IDP sites. IDPs in the project sites are residing in sub-standard shelters, sharing sleeping space and in an unlockable shelter exposing them to various protection and Sexual Gender Based Violence (SGVB) risks. Settlements are unsafe for women and girls because of mixed sex sleeping arrangements and overcrowding. The shelters are further lacking windows, doors and fencing. These factors contribute to the increasing rate of SGBV in the IDP sites. Provision of shelter and ensuring a safe living environment is the prioritized need of IDPs, especially of women and girls.
Gender-based violence is widespread in Gawane Woreda at all socio-economic levels and age groups. Violence against women and girls is still regarded by many as a way of life. The humanitarian needs in Afar, Somali and Oromia Regions have impacted by COVID-19, inter-communal clashes, and the desert locust infestation, and floods. Afar region, in north-eastern Ethiopia is home to 1.9 million people, and has been affected by the pandemic, as well as flooding, conflict, and locust infestations. According to UNOCHA humanitarian bulletin updates from 29 March– 23 April 2021, hundreds of people have reportedly died due to fighting that broke out on 2nd April between Afar and Somali communities in various locations along their regional boundaries. Despite agreements reached between the two regional presidents to peacefully resolve the conflict, fighting was again reported in Hanruka, Gelalo, Gewani and Mile Woredas on 7th April and the situation has not normalized yet. Afar Region hosts 80,343 IDPs, which includes 45,343 displaced due to the Tigray conflict and 35,000 displaced due to the Afar-Somali/Issa conflict.
Women have to walk long distances to collect firewood in the hills (unavailability of charcoal in the markets and no electricity for cooking) which puts them at risk of sexual violence and abduction. Women and girls have been sexually harassed and sexually assaulted while on the way to collect firewood and/or water. Children are also at heightened risk of violence, exploitation, abuse, neglect, harmful practices, rights violations, adverse mental health and psychosocial (MHPSS) wellbeing and of resorting to negative coping strategies.
As part of the COVID-19 prevention measures schools were closed. Since the schools are already re-opened there is high gap on COVID-19 prevention and control measures among the targeted schools. The numbers of students in a class are more congested as availing of additional classrooms and teachers is not an easy decision for the schools and the government. There are limited response efforts to protect students from the pandemic. In most of the schools, toilets are dilapidated and classrooms need maintenance. In some schools there is a need to construct new toilets. There are students in the targeted woredas that need support with scholastic materials, with some drop out of school due to this problem. 1. - Projects activities

DPO has already in place an agreement with EDRMC; Provide case management (CM) services through trained caseworkers: GE intends to offer case management services in line with the survivor-centered perspective. GE caseworkers will regularly assess the survivors' needs, plan appropriate actions including safety planning, and facilitate referral to access services, and follow-up with service providers to ensure that the survivors' needs are addressed. DPO’s psychosocial and recreational activities will be conducted in Women-Friendly Spaces and are intended to bring women and girls together, share their experiences, learn from one another, and build social networks to promote emotional healing and social acceptance from the family to the community. DPO will provide initial and follow-up case management and CCSAS training for DPO caseworkers, program officers, and Government health service providers. The training content will be in line with the global guidelines on GBV case management which includes: Interagency Gender-Based Violence Case Management Guidelines and the IASC Guidelines for Integrating Gender-based Violence Interventions in Humanitarian Action. DPO will also train and mentor caseworkers on conducting emotional counseling, needs assessment, action planning, referral, and documentation. DPO will train and mentor staff from other local organizations and Government office (BOWCYA) implementing GBV in emergency and related activities. The partner’s assessments and mapping will be carried out at the onset of the project to distinguish capacity gaps and intervention modalities. To support vulnerable women in their communities, DPO through its case workers will identify voluntary women’s groups and associations and build their capacities to identify survivors of GBV and vulnerable women who needs emotional support and refer them for supportive services. The case workers will be the one to conduct the case management and follow ups of the cases referred to their attention by the Women groups. The GBV officer will make sure the CM process takes in to account the CM standards and principles , data protections in place, coach and mentor case workers to discharge their responsibilities properly. The provision of the dignity kits will help the project team as entry point to begin working with women to identify the GBV risks in the community, and to advance GBV prevention and response programs. During the distribution DPO GBV team will also sensitized and encourage the communities to engage in discussions on important topics such as preventing and responding to GBV, to share information on where women can access GBV services and to provide additional support to GBV survivors. Distribution of dignity kits can be focused on reaching women at risk; GBV survivors, pregnant and lactating women, women head of household, women and girls with disabilities, women and girls living in remote areas. Venerable women and girls will also identify from health and nutrition and education programs and will be beneficiated from kits provision and awareness sessions. Through regular tea-talk ceremonies, community-based outreach campaigns and other key community events including 16 Days of Activism, general food/NFI distribution, International Women’s Day etc., GE will engage IDPs in different dialogues to prevent GBV. DPO will also use experience from the “Start Awareness and Support Action” (SASA) approach to continue engaging community members in discussion. The root cause of this violence is the imbalance of power in relationships between women and men, girls and boys and awareness sessions to balance of power between women and men will organize across the community and encourage the communities to be active agents in their own protection. DPO as part of the sustainable capacity building initiatives will train the government health staffs in clinical management of rape (CMR) and guiding principles. 3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

DPO will ensure that information sharing and coordination for the project activities implementation is maintained at all levels. At national level, DPO will continue attending and sharing information in the Shelter and NFIs cluster's meetings as well as submitting timely 5Ws/updating for the cluster's coordinator. DPO will ensure proper engagement and participation in the protection clusters at subnational and zonal levels, in order to avoid duplication of efforts, maximizing use of resources and supporting an integrated emergency response. DPO will establish clear, easily accessible mechanisms that allow partner staff and community members to provide feedback on the project and how it is implemented. These mechanisms will be included in the MEAL plan and accountability framework developed for the project and include locally preferred and most effective means, such as surveys, direct feedback, face-to-face meetings, focal persons and suggestion boxes. DPO will also deploy Safeguarding Monitors to distribution/activity locations to observe distributions and converse with participants, providing another direct avenue for sharing feedback and any safeguarding concerns. Timely responses will be provided for programmatic issues reported by staff and community members.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

Affected populations will actively participate in all levels of the project cycle, including in the rapid needs assessments in the design and planning, implementation as well as in monitoring and evaluation. DPO will establish information sharing mechanisms to enhance participation and ensure accountability to beneficiaries.DPO will maintain a commitment to engaging with affected communities through focus group discussions with women, men, and youth on issues regarding their concerns and feedback about the project. Regular meetings with community leaders in the areas of operation will generate feedback for the calibration of activities.Using the different complaints and feedback mechanisms, beneficiaries can report compliances in person, to DPO staff or anonymously by using the reporting tool in the area.DPO will pursue a people-centered and rights-based approach that will promote empowerment and protection of sexual and gender minorities, women, and girls. Additionally, regular oversight will be provided, and monitoring visits conducted to observe service delivery. Feedback collection mechanisms will be instituted, and data will be collected and segregated by gender and age.The projects will work with all segments of society equally. It will ensure participation of men, women, boys, girls, people with disability, people with chronic illness and the like starting from the inception of the project. The project intends to actively engage and provide opportunities to communities in targeted areas to participate in all aspects of the project. The project will ensure that protection is mainstreamed in all activities of the project by using a protection mainstreaming checklist. In beneficiary identification, priority will be given for vulnerable groups such as FHH, PWDs, pregnant and lactating mothers. Distribution of dignity kits will be made in a time and places that is safe for women and girls and PWDs to avoid any safety risks. Trainings will also be provided in a time and place that is convenient for women and people with special needs to increase their participation. Training on gender-based violence and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse will be provided for selected beneficiaries, and representatives of government and other partner organizations to raise their awareness and to allow beneficiaries to report if cases of sexual exploitation and abuse occurs. At time of the training COVID-19 prevention and mitigation measures will be adapted.
