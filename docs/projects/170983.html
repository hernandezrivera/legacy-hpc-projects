Project Summary

Context:The occupation and its practices, manifest in : the excessive use of force, movement restrictions, prevention of PA services in Areas C , EJ, demolitions, forced eviction, settlement expansion, settler violence, attacks against schools, killings, injuries and arrests, specifically among minors. Combined with factors that pose increased protection threats for the Palestinian population: anticipated escalation resulting from enforcement of the “Deal of the Century” and West Bank annexation plan, restrictions and attacks on humanitarian partners, violations of IHL, economic hardship, Covid-19, ever growing shortfalls in humanitarian funding, the suspension of coordination between the PA and Israeli authorities, urgent responses are necessary to meet these emerging needs and facilitate access to basic services for vulnerable people. Moreover, due to the protracted humanitarian crisis and as the global pandemic of COVID-19 continues to spread all around the world, the above concerns are magnified, fact that ultimately results in additional protection threats leaving large numbers of vulnerable people living in sub-optimal conditions. The dramatic lifestyle changes and stressors associated with this pandemic posed an exacerbated threat to mental health of the people.The project design has been informed based on the results and feedback received by the communities during a Survey conducted in August targeting 203 households. Thus, the proposed activities number: 1, 2 and11 have been tailored to the surveyed population feedback. Additionally, the results highlighted high rates of anxiety and helplessness amongst women and PWD's,Based on the above results and on MdM-CH expertise in these locations the last 2 years, the project will target the following locations and groups:
-Targeted Locations: Vulnerable ,marginalized and PwDs into their Communities witnessing increased exposure to Israeli political violence in Area B in Eizariya ,Area C in
Bethlehem (Tqou’), EJ (Essawiyyeh, Jabal al-Mukabir, and Shu’fat Refugee Camp) and H2 area of Hebron.
-Targeted beneficiaries: Children: boys and girls, aged 6-18, Adults: vulnerable men and women (including families) exposed to grave violations, including child detention, demolitions, evictions and settler violence.
-Aim: The project will seek to address protection risks, enhance the psychosocial well-being and mental health of the targeted beneficiaries and communities and enable better access to basic services (education, health) and smooth reintegration into community life and structures. This shall be attained through the provision of a holistic package of services, based on a human rights approach as described below:
(Responsive): The project will seek to address the psychosocial impacts of conflict related violence, through the provision of urgent and timely responses, as well as structured PSS emergency interventions and MHPSS following the latest IASC standards and guide for COVID-19 Responders. The project activities will include:emergency visits within the first 48 hours of an emergency, screening resulting needs and providing PFA. Children of H2 area will also recive PFA sessions provided by their own community members (teachers, counselors). The proposed program is adapted and mainstreamed to COVID-19 pandemic and the response will address some of the pillars and areas covered in WHO's Strategic Preparedness and Response.(Remedial): The project foresees tailored case management, MH and PSS plans that will include psychosocial group and individual counseling sessions for children and adults (caretakers and the wider community), peer to peer groups and further screening, detection and treatment of children/adults facing psychosocial challenges, referrals and rehabilitative care when required; as well as remedial education after school hours.Recreational and ventilation days for children and families will also be organized and are aligned with the education cluster.

1 - Needs

MdM-CH and YMCA are currently working in 5 out of the 6 proposed locations, operating under OCHA HF of 2018, since November 2018. As a result of this presence and as part of MdM-CH’s standard project cycle management procedures, monitoring, evaluation and SWAP analyses were regularly conducted. An emergency needs assessment and risk analysis update was recently conducted, alongside with a lessons-learned, exercise following escalations in Essawiyeh village during May-June 2020. Moreover, the results of the recently conducted community survey has informed the design and strategy of this proposed response plan. The project targets the cluster’s geographic priority areas after 1. a thorough review of needs assessments conducted by different clusters and/or other partners 2. consulting academic papers, research and studies at national and international level(including the MdM documents listed above) in order to take informed decisions and propose responses based on MdM-CH’s and partners’ added value. Child detention issue remains of high concern given the fragility of children who undergo such traumatizing experience; Israel maintains its child detention measure all over the West Bank, as well as its house arrest policy in East Jerusalem. According to the Military Court Watch (MCW), as of 31 May 2020 there were 4,236 Palestinians (West Bank, East Jerusalem, Gaza) held as “security prisoners” in detention facilities including 142 children (12-17 years). According to the IPS, 77% of child detainees were forcibly transferred and/or unlawfully detained in Israel in May in violation of Article 76 of the Fourth Geneva Convention. Economically, the PA’s fragility remained unchanged and has even worsened with the C-19 outbreak. Under a protection angle, the outbreak of the pandemic in Palestine has posed additional protection risks other than those resulting from occupation violence. Several assessments and studies report increased domestic violence cases and suicide attempts as a result of the lockdown which has highly affected the psychosocial wellbeing of an already vulnerable groups mainly young girls and boys and women. Poverty and loss of income resources have also contributed to increased psychosocial risks and increased violence, let aside the deteriorated living conditions and lack of access to services. Furthermore, other social issues have resulted, especially the stigmatization issue against cases contracting the virus. Culturally, The Palestinian context still encounters gender injustice, domestic violence and even killing of women and girls over the so called “honor killing”. As a result of all the above we would like to emphasize the following key findings: EJ and specifically Essawiyyeh, Jabl al Mukabir and Shu’fat R.C, have been witnessing an alarming escalation in violence, fueled by Israeli practices over this year 2020. By the end of August 2020, some 89 structures had been demolished in East Jerusalem, displacing 419 people. In Essawiyeh, an unprecedented number of ISF operations has been recorded, with continuous violent clashes(110 clashes), search operations leading to 95 people arrested among them 22 children between Nov 2019 and Oct 2020, 130 people wounded and one young adult killed. As for Tqou’, in Bethlehem, this area has been witnessing worrisome escalations especially in settler attacks, confiscations for the construction of bypass roads, as well as demolitions following the absence of building permits since 74.1% of the land belongs to Area C, surrounded by 5 settlements. Regarding H2 area in Hebron – a governorate where MdM-CH has been working for more than ten years – the project takes into account OCHA’s recent assessment1: the humanitarian situation in Hebron H2 area - Findings of needs assessment, which documented grave violations against children (85% of the surveyed sample indicating harassment by ISF as one of the major concern affecting children’s access to education). YMCA and MdM-CH’s past experience in Hebron Governorate has enabled both organizations to identify efficiently the particularities of the area. As of area B ('Eizariya), The Israeli settlement bloc Ma'ale Adummim is considered one of the most threatening settlement blocs in the WB, as it lies within the borders of Jerusalem governorate and presents a geographical danger to links between northern and southern areas of the WB. The recently announced and developed by the ICA E1 Plan some as an additional threat to the protection and entails building a new settlement bloc in the WB to create a physical link between Ma'ale Adummim and Jerusalem. The plan includes the construction of 3,900 housing units to the west of Ma'ale Adummim on an area of 13,213 dunums, which was included in the settlement's Master Plan.The current activities and involvement of the partner organization YMCA with UNICEF and Save the children in H2 of Hebron have allowed us to design a needs-based response where complementarity with existing projects is guaranteed at all stages of implementation. Is worth mentioning that recent research evidence has estimated that prevalence of of post traumatic stress disorder in Palestinian children and adolescents is 23-70%. Moreover, MdM-CH as medical organization involved in the national C-19 country response plan through other projects will ensure that C-19 SOP’s and M&E of hygiene and safety measures is respected and promoted.Finally,The project seeks addressing MHPSS impacts of political violence and PwDs to promote psychosocial wellbeing and coping mechanisms, offer them with services facilitating their access to opportunities and rights as well as promoting an inducive and supportive environment through targeting their caregivers and concerned actors as well as the community as a whole to further facilitate their reintegration, protect their rights and timely address their needs.The back to school plan during C-19 and MICs survey were taken taken into account during the design phase.

2 - Activities

Regarding in East Jerusalem (Essawiyyeh, Jabl al Mukabir and Shu’fat R.C ), Tuqu' and E'izarya: 1- Group and community psychosocial activities facilitated by the psychosocial counselors in the targeted population of at risk detention of children, adults, youth and their families through the peer to peer groups. This will focus on mobilizing and training community level volunteers and promoting peer support. 2-MHPSS specialized team: The project will ensure that people in need will receive in-depth psychological intervention. Senior psychologists will screen relevant cases and help individuals and communities to ensure quality and in depth Individual counseling and therapy in a safe place in targeted locations. 3-Training (Capacity Building for CBOs): The project will also target CBOs (caregivers by profession and/or by education) in the targeted communities to enhance cooperation and eventually facilitate access and successful delivery of the desired outputs. 4-Cases identified having severe mental health disorder and in need of psychiatric care, will be referred to psychiatrist and child psychiatrist (TrC, BASIR, CHMC). Those cases based on MH standards and by experience represent 3%-5% of the total cases and includes cases in need of medication. 5-Dissemination of awareness messages through MdM-CH network in n East Jerusalem (Essawiyyeh, Jabl al Mukabir and Shu’fat R.C ), Tuqu' and E'izarya of inclouds CBOs’, emergency committees, social media platforms including the RCCE taskforce weekly and refresh messages. 6-Emergency Visits and PFA provision for 86 security incedents. 7-Sensitization Sessions and Psychosocail Support for 70 Caregivers/ Parents on Child Protection. Aims at sensitizing parents on impacts of conflict violence on their children, equipping them with skills to help adress the needs of thier children, raise thier awarensess on children rights, get azquainted with service providers in situations of emergencies, share experiences, support each other, and follow up thier childnren's academic, health and psychosocial needs. 8-Groups counseling for 32 ex-detainee children (each group engages 8 ex-detainee child and delivered over 10 sessions.those sessions aim at helping children overcome impacts of detention, prevent developing PTSD, increase their social participation towards their reintegration, share experiences and support peers. 9- Sensitization and Psychosocial Support for 32 parents of Ex-Detainee children .Parents will be provided with the opportunity of supporting each other, share experiences, help overcome the psyhcosocial impacts of the detention of their children, enhace thier awareness on thier children's needs and rights, better communicate with them, and encourage thier participation in the social and public spheres of life to help reintegrate them into thier communities and overcome impacts of the traumatizing expereince of detention. 10-Individual counseling for 10 Parents of Ex-Detainee children. to help them overcome the taumatizing experience of the detention of their children, become more aware on impacts of detention, become more understanding to their children's needs and more aware on their rights, ehnace their communicatin skills with their children and encourage their social and public participation. 11-Individual psychosocial counseling for 48 PwD's to help them overcome impacts of their traumatizing experience and facilitate their reintegration into their families and communities. 12-House Adaptation projects PwD's in the Targeted localities,24 PwD's will be provided with house adaptation projects. Adaptation projects include the installation of handrails, ramps, rehabilitation of bathrooms to enable facilitated mobility of PWDs and hence promote their independence and accessibility. 14-Stress release / Management sessions for 120 Parents of Ex-Detainee children, house arrest and PwD's shall receive 1 Stress release / Management session, 2 per locality an average of 10 participants per session. For H2 area; 15-Fun and recreational days at 4 schools in H2 area in Hebron following and promoting Covid-19 public health messages and measures at all stages. ‘’Children will need to be outdoors, active, playful and sociable, even more than ever’’ and as long as we follow social distancing rules and learning through play we can increase children awareness and protection towards C-19 and also built up strong healthy minds. 16-Remedial Education for children from the Targeted 3 schools in H2 3. 9 children will be targeted in remedial education classes to help them catch up with missing classes while in detention and thus facilitate their reitegration in the formal education system, enhnace their academic attainment and minimize drop out rates. 17-Awarenes Sessions for parents of children in the targeted schools (health promotion and videos following a child to child message communication approach) A total of 150 parents of pupils in the targeted H2 schools shall engage in a total of 5 awareness sessions, each engaging 30 parents.Sessions shall be delivered at shools, after schooling hours. 18-10 targeted Schools have received Hygiene Kits. 10 hygiene kits needed to ensure the protection of pupils and school cadre. 19-PFA training in the targeted 10 H2 schools with Providing 13 PFA Kits. The targeted schools shall receive the necessary means to enable them to address adverse MHPSS impacts of the Covid-19.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

MDM-CH and YMCA understand and fully adhere to the core principle of “Do No Harm” as a humanitarian imperative, a cornerstone in all programmes followed by the pyramid of IASC minimum standards in regards to MHPSS. Both organizations remain vigilant throughout project implementation to identify and prevent unintended negative consequences of their actions. In this regard, MDM-CH and YMCA are adopting a number of steps to ensure that its interventions maximize positive impacts for community stability, mainly by strengthening their capacities and resilience, while minimizing harm. AS a result of all the above the do not harm principle appears as a cross-cutting principle at all stages of project cycle management : diagnosis phase of the project, implementation phase, M&E phase and following MDM health project planning tool . (https://www.medecinsdumonde.org/en/actualites/publications/2017/04/19/healthproject- planning) Moreover, in order to further enhance protection mainstreaming the project will ensure that health staff will be capacitated and/or refresh trainings on the following 4 topics: - MHPSS IASC minimum standards: Guidelines on Mental Health and Psychosocial Support in Emergency Settings (WHO guidelines) and Covid-19 Guide - GBV: Minimum Standards for Prevention and Response to Gender-based Violence in Emergencies (following UNFPA standards) - Child Protection Minimum standards in Humanitarian action (following Unicef and Sphere guidelines) -Minimum Standards for Child Protection in Humanitarian Action (following Sphere standards and MdM guidelines) - Disability mainstreaming and health: CBR(Community based rehabilitation guidelines). Child safeguarding standards are integrated in all the stages of the project following MdM Project Cycle Management Tool (diagnosis, M&E, data collection and safeguarding policy etc). More precisely MdM takes the following steps into account for this project: 1. Ensure that confidentiality and privacy are respected during consultations (ex. child friendly rooms or separated rooms are available, information sharing protocol is in place etc) 2. Safe recruitment procedures following HR and CP focal point monitoring 3. Parental consent form whenever is required and following the mental health advisor and CPO recommendations. Child Medical history form when required for MH or other treatment following MDM medical coordinator and MH advisor recommendations. 4. Child safeguarding Policy principles trainings (1 every year) 5. Guide for occasional visitors and volunteers when facilitators and CBO’s form the communities are contributing to the project objectives. MdM’s Policy to address SEAH is underpinned into the project by six MdM principles of zero tolerance and follows six specific complaints procedures. More specifically for this project: 1.MdM Rules and code of conduct document is signed by all employees and staff working for this project. Online trainings and refresh trainings on PSEA amongst project staff and HQ focal point once every year (since 2018) for all MdM-CH missions. 2. Encouragement to report: the project will install a complaint mechanism, which allows and encourages staff to report any suspicions of sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment of others. 3.Alerts and feedback procedures with the following reporting email address visible to all in all project locations: abuse@medecinsdumonde.ch 4. MHPSS and or access to other specialist (medical, legal) support for survivors and Victims is provided if and when survivors and victims would like to take it up.. Gender and Age Mainstreaming; This project is designed with a special focus on children and adults exposed to conflict related violence, however, a special attention will be given to ex-detainee boys and to girls siblings of ex detainees so to ensure they have access to all activities and be able to address their very different needs (see detailed GBA analysis following project code marker)

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

DM bases its intervention in ethical and humanitarian principles. This project employed a principalist approach that is widely accepted in the humanitarian community through such vehicles as the SPHERE Humanitarian Charter. Ethical principles in health care include respect for persons to advance human dignity and for individuals’ autonomous choices; beneficence, the promotion of others’ wellbeing, commonly known as “do no harm,” and justice, encompassing both the fair distribution of resources and fair processes for decision-making. Humanitarian principles is the a second source of values and norms that animate all MDM-CH actions. HP include humanity, requiring that suffering must be addressed wherever and for whomever it is found; neutrality, the duty of humanitarian actors not to take sides in a conflict; impartiality, which stipulates that humanitarian actors must not discriminate or give preference to any nationality, race, religious belief, class, political opinion, or similar status; and independence, which demands that humanitarian actors retain their autonomy and remain independent of political or military objectives of other actors MDM-CH makes sure that representatives of all social, ethnic and religious groups in the area of intervention participate in needs. For example in the current project and following an analysis conducted following focus group discussions and MDM-CH teams observations and reports we are proposing minor rehabilitation works in Essawiyeh Community center in order to improve accessibility and communication of the center adapted for PwD’s. Thus, technical advice was already requested by Humanity Inclusion.
