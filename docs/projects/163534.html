Project Summary

Project aims to address the food security needs of crisis-affected populations in the SW of Cameroon( (including in Meme, Fako and Ndian Divisions)— of both IDPs and vulnerable host community members — through the distribution of food vouchers. The aim of this intervention is to prevent those populations from resorting to high-risk negative coping strategies, such as: reliance on child labor, skipping meals, transactional sex,selling livelihoods productive assets, and other potentially dangerous survival strategies in order to meet their basic food needs. This includes an integrated approach with the Protection Cluster in the South West.
Project has 3 components:
Protection Risks and Basic Needs for Conflict-Affected Communities in the Far North Region, Cameroon (
(Start Date 01/04/2020 - End Date 31/03/2021) - This aims at ensuring the that women, girls, boys and men are able to meet their basic needs; and are safe in their communities and receive support when they experience harm in the targeted locations in the Far North region. The project will provide multi-purpose cash assistance, gender-based violence (GBV) and protection programming to 8,619 beneficiaries to help them meet their basic needs, be safer in their communities, and receive support when they experience harm.
Humanitarian Framework Agreement - Program Based Approach in the South West, IRC Cameroon
(Start Date 01/01/2020 - End Date 31/12/2020) - This multi-year programme (started in 2018) will continue to focus on two main outcomes in the areas of Economic Wellbeing and Safety that support IRC Cameroon’s SAP updated in December 2020: Outcome 1- People meet basic needs and avoid negative coping strategies; Outcome 2- People are safe in their communities and receive support when they experience harm. Within the scope of those outcomes, should any new acute or severe needs emerge, new and alternative strategies of interventions might be considered.
Supporting the Most Vulnerable Crisis-Affected Households to Meet their Food Needs in the South West Region of Cameroon (Start Date 01/10/2019 - 31/03/2021) - This includes the following key activities will be carried out under this project:
Activity 1: Design and print food vouchers to allow targeted households to access essential food items
Activity 2: Identify, select, and contract vendors for a voucher redemption process
Activity 3: Identify, select and register 510 client HHs
Activity 4: Conduct voucher handling training for vendors and clients
Activity 5: Distribute vouchers and support voucher redemption
Activity 6:Conduct monitoring.

Besoins / Needs

The populations in these areas have been severely impacted by prolonged loss of livelihoods, income, and productive assets as a result of the crisis, and they are currently living in very difficult conditions with limited access to food.The IRC has been providing similar assistance in the targeted locations for about one year. With assistance from LDS Charities, the IRC will be able to strengthen its impact in these target areas and continue to provide urgent assistance to meet these communities’ food security needs. This intervention will also complement the IRC's ongoing multi-sectoral projects (in Environmental Health, Food Security and Protection) in the targeted area funded by other donors. Under the proposed project, the IRC will provide food assistance to 510households (HHs)(3,300 people)through three monthly transfers of restricted food vouchers worth $70 each, which will cover 60% of the minimum food expenditure basket of a household in the SW to cover the gap that households are unable to meet themselves. The vouchers will be distributed to selected households and can be redeemed for set food items at contracted vendors. A similar IRC project in the target area succeeded in reducing households’ negative coping strategies by 93%after three food voucher distributions.In addition,90% of clients who received the cash-valued food vouchers in this previous project self-reported that their access to food was enabled by the project intervention.Therefore, the IRC has strong evidence to suggest that similar vouchers, will equally contribute to reducing negative coping mechanisms and helping households to meet their food needs.
