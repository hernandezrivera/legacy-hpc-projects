Project Summary

The Rohingya community people from Myanmar fled their country of origin, sought as refuge. Some pre-existing social reasons (i.e. discrimination, violence etc.) put the Rohingya community in a vulnerable situation. However, children, adolescent, women, elderly person and person with disabilities from any community are always more vulnerable than others and are at risk of any kind of harm and violence. Caritas Bangladesh will implement this project Rohingya camp 20 (palongkhali, Ukhiya, Cox’s Bazar). First goal of this project will be to reduce the risk of gender-based violence, intimate partner violence as well and as other form of violence among the FDMNs. Community people will play the key activist to identify community protection risk, mental health needs through door to door visit. 6 Community activist group will be formed with 72 community people, where the participation of different group (Age, gender, person with disabilities) of people will be ensured. Among 8052 people, 5000 (>17 years= 1843, 18-59 years= 3060 and 60+ years= 100) individual including will 92 person with disabilities will have awareness raising session on needs based protection and mental health issues. Community volunteer will conduct the sessions. The next goal of this project will be to improve the quality of response services and to enable the access to the non-communicable community based mental health and psycho-social support services to. Multipurpose Women and girl center (MWGC) will be established in an inclusive and person friendly form where person with disabilities, adolescent girls and victim of any form of violence can get their required services. IASC guidelines of MHPSS pyramid for mental health service, PFA services will be maintained to community people segregated by age, gender and disability to build the resilience and well-being. Case worker will provide the case management service maintaining ISCG and protection guidelines. To provide prevention and response services this project will also focus on the capacity building of community protection activist, community volunteer and project staff. They will have training on facilitation skill, protection mainstreaming and process of community people engagement, advance MHPSS services, GBVIMS and referral. To identify the protection risk, concerns and to get immediate response, the community protection activist will trained up on developing action plan. To integrate with other sector, training on protection mainstreaming, safeguarding, PSEA will be conducted to other sector staff of CB. As part of reaching the vulnerable people proactive engagement in empowerment activities such as life skill or vocational training (i.e. solar repair, tailoring and jute product making) initiative will be taken, where the participants will be the survivors of GBV. To engage the children and adolescents, play therapy will be provided to build early childhood development. Assistive device (Wheel chair & hearing aid) will be provided to support the person with disabilities. Women and male led focus group discussion will be conducted to identify the barriers, needs and way of mitigation to any protection concerns. For addressing the needs of diverse groups (i.e. persons with disabilities, marginalized groups, members of LGBTQ etc.), CB will ensure the coordination among the government stakeholders, developmental organizations, multi-sectors through participating coordination meeting, submitting- 5W & sitRep, incident report to sectors, participating training, Day observations (i.e. World mental health day, 16 days of activism, International Women day etc.) and so on as part of gender mainstreaming and disability inclusion. Feedback and response will be collected through the CFRM mechanism and response safeguarding and committee to improve the quality of the project activity. These activities will have impact of community based protection and helps when the FDMNs will be repatriate to their country of origin.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Violence impacts women and men differently, even in conflict situations. Because of social constraints as well as the fear of being stigmatized women are usually reluctant to share and seek support after violent incidents (Benjamin and Fancy, 1998; El-Bushra, 2003).Rohingya communities living in refugee camps are in acute need of protection. Among the many protection issues, women and girls are particularly vulnerable to various forms of abuse. Rohingya women and girls are vulnerable because of their gender, refugee status and ethnic affiliation. In addition, they become even more vulnerable because family and community structures have broken down. These intersecting vulnerabilities make them exposed to gender-based violence from several different perpetrators. Sexual violence against Rohingya refugee women when they work outside the home is common, but it rarely gets reported because of the attached stigma.For sexual abuse, adolescent girls are at greater risk. But when the issue of seeking support comes, less want to talk about it. Because, in research, it has found that the community is afraid to talk about sexual harassment. (Mar-21, Blog). Rohingya women are exposed to gender-based violence both from the outside and the inside of the community. In 2018, IRC (international rescue committee) had a project to provide lights to women and girls. Because when the women and girls went to the toilet in the evening and at night, they were at greater risk for sexual assault and rape. In camps, there are minimal gender differences for adolescents suffering parental beatings (32% of girls report compared to 37% of boys). (Policy brief-April-2020). In 2022, intimate partner violence is the main GBV case typology, physical assault, sexual assault, forced marriage, denial of resources and risks of human trafficking (labour and sexual). The primary form of GBV reported by women and girls remained physical assault, with spikes in recorded in GBVIMS data found particularly during April to June-222 that 85% of incidents are perpetrated by intimate partners, 10% are by family friend/caregiver/neighbor. 6.2% of reported incidents are of sexual violence. Among them 92% of the survivors are Married; 4% are Single; 2% are Divorced and 1% are widowed. In addition, 42% of incidents were reported in the Evening/Night; 31% in the Morning and 27% in the afternoon. In camp 20, total 314 families required specific needs, where adolescent girls, boys and children, due to the lack of educational facilities, are at risk of psychological distress and negative coping mechanisms are facing problems. It is important to understand where social norms are interrelated to these various dynamics and what impact they have on different groups and their access to services. Such intersections are important spaces through which to transform norms, but also points of contention. Humanitarians need to be aware of social norms, attitudes and practices to ensure response efforts do not contribute to GBV. Important to address structural underlying causes and contributing factors of GBV including access to livelihoods and education, camp governance systems and GBV risk mitigation in site planning. Therefore, the need for specialized and culturally appropriate psychosocial support, community structures, multi-purpose community centers, and safe spaces for women and girls, is high to reduce Gender Based Violence in the Rohingya community.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

From the beginning of the Rohingya influx in Bangladesh since 2017, Caritas Bangladesh (CB) is one of the first responders and has been working in the Rohingya refugee camps. As part of humanitarian crisis response activities, Gender based violence (GBV) program has been operating since 2018. Same as before, CB is going to implement GBV activities in line with the JRP objectives as well as maintaining the IASG, IASC, Protection, GBVSS (11 guidelines from 16 of the GBV guidelines), child protection & MHPSS guideline. This project will be implement in camp 20. CB already implementing GBV services in camp 1E, 17, 19 and 20 ext. and already have integrated protection facilities in camp 4ext and 19. As per the needs of the community people, GBV services, multipurpose Women and adolescent (MWGC) centers might overlap with other actors, but in practical communication with actors of same sectors will help to continue the smooth process of services. The field office at Ukhia can support in effective implementation of all projects. CB has been working for sustainable capacity development for the community people. In this regard, this specific project is going to implement SASA! together approach. For data protection, GBVIMS is one of the key parts in this project, which is under processing between GBV sub-sector and CB. CB is the active member of PSEA Network and have PSEA Focal, GiHA WG and ADWG, GBVSS, MHPSS WG and at camp level CiC Office and multi-sectoral coordination. CB never found any objection so far from any Government authorities, refugee camps. Caritas Bangladesh (CB) implement activities with community feedback. The safeguarding committee is responsible to maintain the community feedback, response and complain mechanism. There are complain box, dedicated hotline no. and email id. Feedback box is established in all service centers of CB. After a regular frequency, feedback collected and response immediately as per Caritas policy and program needs.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Caritas concerns with the unique and immense risks of the safety and dignity of Rohingya persons, especially those who have elevated vulnerability like women, girls, boys, persons with disabilities, elderly persons, etc. Women and girl center are in a safe location, free from dark footpaths, risky slopes, and isolated areas. Separate latrines there for girls and boys in the facilities with labeled clearly. PSEA and Safeguarding is the major concern areas of humanitarian response. Consultation with stakeholders planned to ensure access to all to the services. Community centered approach is following to ensure participation, empowering, and strengthening communities. Community-based planning reduces risks and hazards and promotes resilience. AAP and responsiveness to the communities will maintained through MEAL structure and CFRM mechanism. Conducting real-time monitoring to inform project adaptation and build flexibility into this project considering the fluidity of the situation.

3.2 - Gender

CB follows comprehensive Gender Policy in operation of humanitarian responses. In the proposed project, it will continue services to ensure female participation in all committees and decision-making process, trained equally both female and male staff, gender segregated WASH facilities including MHM. Focusing on gender sensitive activity e.g: developing women forum, separate female and male leader group is part of this project. Deploy female volunteers for adolescent girls and adult women focus session and adapting services culturally responsive and gender responsive. All project staff will be trained and equipped on gender sensitive programming. The program will also be emphasized to enhance girls friendly and gender-equal environment in the learning facilities. Proper monitoring and early warning mechanism will establish and aligned with CFRM and MEAL structure. Organize community consultation to remove the cultural barriers and involve men to improve the gender discriminations.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

CB follows a comprehensive Child and Vulnerable Adult Safeguarding Policy to this humanitarian context. CB has Safeguarding Committee and one dedicated Safeguarding Focal in all offices, including Cox’s Bazar office. Staff, volunteers are trained and Safeguarding, Code of Conduct, PSEA orientation organize each year for all. Staff and volunteers must sign a safeguarding, Code of Conduct and self-declaration form during joining and reference checked before offering job. Beneficiaries are oriented on safeguarding policy through providing orientation before any service delivery, distribution. Agenda on safeguarding keeps in all meeting, training and workshops. Bengali version of Safeguarding policy and one pager distributed among the staff and host volunteers. All visitors, advisors and vendors read and sign the safeguarding form before visiting the camps. All above discussion and obligation are applicable for the staff, Facilitators and volunteers under the proposed project.

3.4 - Diversity

To make the project activities more inclusive, community center will be accesible for the person with disabilities, LGBTQ person along with the adolescent girls and women. Campaigning program will be conduct to screen the person with disability. And according to this assistive device will be provided. Though it is planned to provide hearing aid and wheel chair, but assistive device selection can be changed as per the screening. Diversified will be the part of all activities of this project. Also to engage the diversified group coordination with ADWG will continued.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Systemic MEAL mechanism ensures AAP principles, CFRM and stakeholder consultation, and periodic meetings with stakeholders are designed in the project towards improving the quality of protection. CB aims at capacitating staff and volunteers to facilitate cultural programming with a perspective of sustainability and potential replication and to promote empowerment of community. Involvement of community members in needs assessment through Participatory Action Research (PAR) methods. Moreover, in prevention of GBV and to promote the safeguarding issue, Complain and feedback mechanism will be monitored by the feedback register. This register will be mantained in a floating form. Sharing impact findings with beneficiaries, organizes monthly Community based protection committee meeting to take decision on the ways to ensure the quality protection to the community people. Children’s including adolescent and youths of diverse population group’s feedback will be considered/addressed importantly

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes. Emergency preparedness and response activities are in place in the proposed project. CB will continue to provide integrated MHPSS and protection services with DRR services so that each facilities has DRR contingency plan and continue following plan accordingly with community people. Capacity building of Facilitators, Community Volunteers, CMC members, CBCPC, community stakeholders, and their active engagement in PERU, DRR program management is the crucial part of the proposed project to prevent and mitigate the crisis. Their knowledge and skills contribute to the decision-making process and implementation, ultimately help the community to be sustainable and resilient. Facility-based EPR plan will be developed in 2023.
