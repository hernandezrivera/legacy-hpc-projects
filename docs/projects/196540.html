Project Summary

Bonyan Organization designed its SNFI intervention based on the analysis of humanitarian needs (MSNA), and need severity for SNFI assistance, as well as to be in line with SOs of HRP and SNFI Cluster.
The project will address SNFI needs for totally 27,250 INDVs (11990M/ 15,260F – 1700PwD) of the most vulnerable people of IDPs, HC and RTRNs in NSAG controlled areas in Aleppo and Idleb.
The project will contribute to provide lifesaving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support. BO will respond to the emergency or high severity situations; especially in hard-to-reach areas, last resorts, and informal settlements. BO will conduct assessment survey in order to collect SNFI needs data (with keeping in mind the specific needs of different age and gender groups), in addition to collect any other necessary data (i.e., market assessment or security assessment).
As for the response to NFI needs, BO will conduct the following:
• Provision of core NFIs for 7500 INDVs including mattresses, blankets, cooking utensils, solar light, plastic sheets, and kitchen kits
• Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs for 7500 INDVs including winter clothing, and shoes.
Likewise, BO will respond to shelter needs through:
• Repair of tents for 2000 INDVs
• Distribution/ installation of emergency shelter kits/materials for 2500 INDVs
• Implementation of site level improvements/infrastructure for 1500 INDVs
• Installation and/ or construction of temporary transitional shelters for 250 INDVs
• Supply and installation of HH level solar energy systems for 250 INDVs
Throughout the project, BO will select the target communities based on scoring and prioritizing system in coordination with SNFI Cluster, then will engage the selected community in all phases of the project. Also, BO will ensure that the support will be provided to the most vulnerable people through transparent process based on clear vulnerability criteria (HLP verification will be included). Principles of Do No Harm and AAP as well as humanitarian standards are incorporated in the project design and will be ensured during the project timeline. Also, MEAL team of BO will conduct ongoing monitoring of project activities to verify achievement of indicators and to correct any deviation.

3 - Needs

It is estimated that 5.5m people in Syria will require shelter support in 2023. In accordance with the MSNA 2022 findings, 34% of the overall population live in ‘sub-standard’ shelter, of which 19 % reside in finished residential buildings classified as damaged and/ or displaying “adequacy issues”. Lack of access to electricity/lighting, insulation from heat and cold, deterioration of shelter integrity (notable increase in damages to windows/doors), in addition to lack of privacy, are among the top shelter adequacy issues identified. In conjunction with this, 38 % of the overall population are renting or hold weak forms of occupancy agreement, which hampers eligibility for shelter assistance and/ or heightened risks of eviction.With the security environment remaining somewhat volatile, deterioration in economic conditions, compounded by a depreciating Syrian pound, has led to further intensification of the severity of need for NFI support. As such, the overall NFI PiN has increased by 5 % to 5.7 million. Winter clothing, mattresses and blankets have witnessed a significant increase in inaccessibility in 2022 when compared to the previous year. In particular, clothing expenditure ranks as the highest monthly SNFI expenditure. Such items are crucial to ensure the well-being of the most vulnerable, particularly during harsh winter seasons when access to basic NFIs is considered lifesaving.Aleppo PiNs Shelter630596 NFI443120Idleb PiNs Shelter1278364 NFI840338
