Project Summary

Mercy Corps with funding from Bureau of Humanitarian Assistance (BHA) is implementing a multisector humanitarian response to Address Diverse and Acute Primary Threats (ADAPT) to human security in Northeastern Nigeria. Phase 1 of this program started March 2021 – February 2022 and Phase II is expected to start March 2022 to February 2023. The program responds to the urgent humanitarian needs of 129,584 displaced, returnee and host households living in 5 LGAs i.e. Damboa, Dikwa, Ngala, Gwoza and Bama LGAs and covers essential humanitarian needs across a range of essential sectors, including Food-Assistance, Nutrition, Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance, WASH, Shelter and Settlements and Protection.
The nutrition response supports both nutrition specific and sensitive activities that contribute to prevention, detection, and management of malnutrition. It supports continuity and scale up of best practices for Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition in Emergency (MIYCN-E), Provision of supplemental nutrition assistance (SNA) and filling existing gaps in CMAM services including supporting SPHDA led OTP services. The program ensures an integrated holistic nutrition approach through mainstreamed agriculture, WASH and protection activities.
The ADAPT Phase II will continue to support a). MIYCN promotion, counselling, and support at community level using a mix of approaches to strengthen social behaviour change and communication. This will include provision of Integrated Health, Nutrition and WASH messaging (SBCC) through Community Health Influencers, Promoters and Services (CHIPS), capacity building of nutrition Service providers and CHIPS on MIYCN-E, counselling on breastfeeding difficulties in IYCF supportive spaces, strengthen Group awareness and counselling through Peer-to-peer support groups (MTMSG and FTFSG) and cooking demonstrations. b). Provision of supplemental nutrition assistance to facilitate regular consumption of diversified nutritious diets by Young Children, pregnant and lactating women. This includes distributing fresh food vouchers (FFV) to increase access to vegetables, fruits and animal source protein food items and also supporting kitchen/ backyard gardening and small-scale poultry production to foster long-term diet diversification. In addition, Mercy Corps will provide supplemental food assistance to children 24-59months, pregnant and lactating women with Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) to ensure that malnourished participants living in chronically food-insecure Households meet the required caloric and protein intake. c). Lastly, Mercy Corps in coordination with the Nutrition Sector Technical Working Group will support Outpatient Therapeutic Programs (OTPs) to fill existing gaps in Gwoza and Dikwa, strengthen community level nutrition surveillance through enhanced nutrition screening, referral and follow-up of acute malnourished children by CHIPs and also support surveys/ assessments to evaluate program contribution to reducing prevalence of malnutrition and coverage of CMAM programming.

1 - Needs

The Nutrition and food security surveillance assessment (Round 10) conducted in North East Nigeria in September/ October 2021 shows that the Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels are high (11.8%) in Borno State; and even worse in Eastern Borno domain (12.9%), that includes the targeted ADAPT Phase II LGAs (Bama, Dikwa, Ngala and Gwoza LGAs). Analysis of the nutrition screening data from the Nutrition TWG for July to September 2021, estimates prevalence of acute malnutrition to be very high (18.9%) above the emergency threshold, 48,203 children had SAM and 166,700 had MAM. In the LGAs currently targeted by ADAPT program (Damboa, Dikwa, Gwoza, Ngala and Bama), 414,777 children 6-59 months were screened, and the estimated prevalence of acute malnutrition is at medium severity (9.8%). The majority (96.2%) of the identified SAM cases were admitted in OTPs, however enrolment in TSFPs was low, and only 7.2% of identified MAM cases received care. Treatment of SAM with medical complication is available (490 cases treated in SCs), however with the exit of MSF in Gwoza, this disrupted care in this LGA. In Dikwa, delivery of CMAM services was interrupted due to escalated insecurity, and most nutrition actors including FHI that was providing the bulk of CMAM services withdrew/ halted activities. The nutrition situation among women of childbearing age (15-49) is also poor, acute malnutrition estimated at 14.8% in Borno (13.4 in Eastern Borno). Only 42.4% of women are able to consume diets that meet the minimum dietary diversity (38.9% in East Borno domain). Additionally, consumption of iron rich food by women is low ((44.5%), especially in East Borno. Exclusive Breastfeeding is also poor at 49.1% up from 45.6% (Eastern Borno from 57% to 67.2%). Consumption of solid, semi solid, or soft foods by infants aged 6-8 months, was estimated at 76% in Borno State, however it is lower in East Borno at only 61.2%. Consumption of meals that meet the recommended dietary diversity remains poor in East Borno when compared with the general Borno State situation i.eMinimum dietary diversity is estimated at 15.6(East Borno at 5.2), Minimum Meal Frequency was at 25.4 (East Borno at 18.6) and Minimum Acceptable diets at 3.6 (East Borno at 0.4). Generally, the nutrition situation and adoption of IYCF practice is improving when compared with the previous assessments, however the situation is still poor with numerous aggravating factors, and required continued effort to support and strengthen quality preventive and curative nutrition services.

2 - Activities

Promote and support MIYCN including counselling to ensure good nutrition status among women, infants and young children through various SBC strategies that foster nutrition-related behavior change, especially among women of childbearing age, adolescent girls, and children. This will include supporting CHIPs to provide key multi-sector SBC (integrated Health, Nutrition and WASH) i.e. support recruitment of men and women, their trainings, equip them with IEC materials, data collection and reporting tools and also provide monthly stipend. Mercy Corps will establish supportive Spaces for IYCF-E, Peer to Peer Support groups (MtMSG, FtFSGs and Adolescent girl groups) as enabling environments for MIYCN promotion, counselling, education and CMAM service promotion. Additionally, to ensure linkage of MIYCN activities with on-going SAM and MAM treatment services, following the “Family MUAC approach” the project will build the capacity of mothers and other caregivers on nutrition screening to enhance their knowledge of acute malnutrition, facilitate timely case identification, referral and improved coverage of treatment programs. Mercy Corps will also support immediate and long-term sustainable approaches that facilitate increased availability, access, and regular consumption of diversified nutritious diets by Young Children, pregnant and lactating women. For example, distribution of fresh food vouchers to households with children U2 and pregnant women and supporting participants to start up simple SMART agronomy and livelihood techniques i.e. equip households with tools and skill-building on agronomy to facilitate kitchen/ backyard gardening and small-scale poultry production. Mercy Corps will also provide supplemental food assistance to children 6-59months with Moderate Acute Malnutrition. The program will also support and strengthen nutrition surveillance systems and gap filling for CMAM services through supporting CHIPs to conduct nutrition screening, referral and follow-up of acute malnourished children, support transportation of Identified SAM & MAM Children to CMAM Centers in some LGAs to further improve coverage and access. Mercy Corps will also support Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) centers in Gwoza and Dikwa LGA to respond to the current/ expected CMAM service gaps. Lastly, to evaluate the effect of activities on the prevalence of malnutrition and coverage of CMAM, Mercy Corps in collaboration with the Nutrition Sector will support KAP surveys, SMART survey and SQUEAC assessments in targeted LGAs.
