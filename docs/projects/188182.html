Project Summary

Through the proposed interventions, SIF (Secours Islamique France) will directly contribute to the WASH Sector’s objective 1, 2 and 4 of Syria by supporting water, sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems; Deliver humanitarian WASH supplies and services and Improve WASH facilities and services in schools, in Rural Damascus, Dara’a, Hama, Homs, Sweida, Quneitra, and Aleppo Governorates.
WASH systems in Syria have suffered from damages owing to years of functioning at high capacity where large numbers of IDPs overburdened the system, limited or no maintenance, destructions, displacement of technical staff and poor water resources management. WASH infrastructures and service provision thus require significant investment, repairs and operational support.
The project interventions will improve access to basic WASH facilities through the identification of critical gaps and providing WASH assistance in particular in areas with a high number of IDPs and returnees and areas with anticipated significant inward displacement.
Proposed locations were identified according to the severity of the needs: 15% of targeted locations are in scale 2 as SIF considered to target Madiq Castle, Kafr Zeita, Muhradah and Talbiseh as it was reported to have a large population with high needs for WASH assistance.; 26% are in scale 3; 54% are in scale 4 and 5% are in scale 5), their level of accessibility, and the experience of SIF. The comprehensive, multisector and multiyear approach was the determining element in the selection of the locations for 2022 and 2023, with the aim of promoting the sustainability of the interventions, community self-reliance and resilience.
In line with the results of consultations with the local community and relevant authorities, and taking into account sector priorities, the proposed SIF intervention will contribute to building the resilience of 501,155 community members in 2022 and 403,500 community members in 2023 affected by improving housing conditions and related public infrastructure for vulnerable populations through:
(1) Repairing and rehabilitating water systems.
(2) Repairing, rehabilitating and operational support to sanitation/sewage and solid waste management system.
(3) Improving access to humanitarian life-saving/emergency WASH facilities and services, including strengthening Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) measures.
(4) Supporting WASH facilities and services in schools and child friendly spaces in close coordination with Education and Protection sectors.
Out of the 501,155 community members targeted in 2022, there are 253,358 males and 247,797 females, 51,765 children 0-5 years old, 162,762 children 6-17 years old, 262,537 adults 18-59 years old, 24,090 older people (+59 years old) and 50,116 People with Disabilities.
Safeguarding and protection principles, including the Do No Harm approach, will guide SIF team throughout all the phases of the project cycle. SIF is fully committed to the SPHERE standards and humanitarian principles based on which SIF’s internal policies and procedures have been created to guide project implementation.
Accountability to affected population will be realized through strengthening community-based complaint mechanisms, improved participatory assessments and enhanced outreach through SIF MEAL team.
SIF will implement this project in close coordination with various stakeholders on the ground for an effective response, including the Ministry of Local Administration and Environment, the Ministry of Water Resources, WASH sector, I-NGOs, contractor, SARC, Clusters, local committees, refugee, IDP/returnee and host community leaders, in order to bridge gaps in available services and ensure better access of People in Needs to services.
In 2021, SIF targeted 167,462 community members through its WASH interventions.
For 2022, SIF has already secured 35% of the proposed project budget.

4 - Needs

47% of the population in Syria rely on non-streamed and unsafe water supplies to meet water needs and 70% of sewage is discharged untreated (HNO 2022 – Draft). Such factors pose significant public health risks and have negative effects in terms of public health, inequality and poverty. In the last trimester of 2021, SIF contacted local authorities in the identified locations to discuss multisector needs and identify area priorities, aiming to define intervention based on WASH logframe. SIF assessed needs in Rural Damascus (Jisrein, Hammura, Saqba, Sbeineh); Daraa (Shaqra, Ghasm, Atman, Da'el); Homs (Deir Baalba, Jouret Ash-Shayyah, Ar-Rastan, Talbiseh, Ghanto, Al Zhouria camp); Hama (Madiq Castle, Kafr Nabutha, Halfaya, Latmana\Latmin, Hamra, Karnaz) and Sweida (As-Sweida, Mazra'a, Salkhad, Milh, Ariqa).The assessments showed a great need to improve access to drinking and domestic use water as the shortage of clean water is causing severe lack of personal and community hygiene and is a burden on residents who spend increased amount on buying water from private tankers. In many location, the main sewage network which reaches all houses needs to be rehabilitated as it floods in many streets in the area during winter.In particular, the following needs were identified:- 79 boreholes to be rehabilitated - 7,975 manhole covers to be installed- 37,045 LM of sewage network to be rehabilitated - 275 waste bins to be provided- 5, 800 LM of water network to be rehabilitated

5 - Implementation of activities

SIF intervention will include the following: Repairing 1,500 LM of water network in 2022 and 2,000 LM in 2023; Rehabilitating 2 boreholes in 2022 and 3 in 2023, with provision of solar power suppliesRehabilitating 2,600 LM of sewage system in 2022 and 3,600 LM in 2023; installing 900 manholes covers in 2022 and 1,000 in 2023; distributing 100 solid waste bins in 2022Installing 20 WASH points in one IDPs camp in Homs in 2022Repairing WASH points in 17 schools in 2022.SIF will submit assessment/implementation approvals to MoLAE. Selection of locations will be conducted in collaboration with donors, SARC and municipalities based on sector criteria. Results of technical assessments conducted by SIF will be endorsed by an appointed Committee composed of representatives from Municipality, Governorate and SIF, also responsible to monitor implementation of activities and issue handover documents.SIF will rely on a contractor to carry out the work selected based on SIF SoP, which include a transparent and objective tender process. SIF will take into account local capacities and encourage contractors to recruit workers from local communities. SIF grants a period to each Municipality in case repairs are needed from post-implementation to exit strategy.Activities will be monitored by SIF technical/MEAL teams. Participatory approach will be implemented through structured dialogues with groups of different ages and gender ensuring that protection concerns are detected and addressed.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Discrimination or perception of discrimination may occur if assistance is provided to a specific group/ individuals while others are excluded. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: (1) To ensure uniform and transparent beneficiary selections procedures which are agreed among the sector members and government authorities and are clearly communicated to the community. (2) Ensure that comprehensive service mapping has taken place that risk mitigation and communication with communities are proactive and informed. (3) Ensure standardize assistance package and guideline that are followed by the sector. Risk 2: Risk of gender based violence (GBV) and sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) while accessing the infrastructures. Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation: (1) conduct safety audit to ensure safe access of beneficiary to infrastructures sites. (2) ensure close coordination and consultation with local communities on the accessibility of infrastructures in order to receive their inputs and understand their perspectives in respect to safety of beneficiaries. (3) Consolation with women, girls, boys and men should be held. (4) sensitizing others sectors in gender sensitive measures in WASH Assistance (5) ensuring locks on each door to provide privacy to girls and women and reduce risk of sexual abuse.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Sexual exploitation of beneficiaries in return of promise for assistance. Mitigation measures include the following: (1) SIF ensures that specific PSEA indicators are included in the monitoring framework.(2) Vetting process is conducted during the recruitment of a candidate at application and reference stages. (3) The code of conduct, which includes the strict prohibition of SEA, is signed by everyone working for and with SIF, including staff, SIF volunteers and contractors. After the selection of the contractors, as the same time of the signature of the purchase order, the contractor signs the SIF Code of conduct which is then attached as an annex to the contract/agreement.(4) SIF employees are trained on PSEA and a PSEA focal point is assigned in all governorates targeted by SIF. Awareness sessions of the community members on PSEA are conducted every year. Sessions includes the definition of the what is SEA and channels of reporting/whistleblowing. (5) SIF community-based complaint mechanisms is accessible to affected populations in all response locations. (6) A process, steps, and timeframe for the investigation of allegations of SEA has been developed by SIF.
