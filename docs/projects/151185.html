Project Summary

The project seeks to rehabilitate water infrastructures and promote water treatment activities in order to increase access to emergency, sustainable, adequate and safe water to vulnerable households. The project also seeks to promote sanitation and hygiene practices through awareness campaigns and construction of sanitation facilities.

Needs

WASH conditions in Somalia slightly improved in 2018 compared to 2017 due to above average rainfall. The rainfall caused flooding affecting WASH conditions. Nonetheless, sustainable access to safe drinking water, hygiene and livestock remain an obstacle across Somalia especially in rural areas, and IDP/refugee camps and settlements. Many parts of Somalia are classified as high vulnerable to extreme vulnerable (2018 WASH Somalia WASH vulnerability map) Interventions by WASH sector players to improve the WASH conditions plus the oral cholera vaccination campaign across Somalia greatly aided in reduced number of new cholera cases in 2018 compared to 2017. Recorded AWD cases were 6394 with 42 associated deaths in 2017 compared to 60678 cases with 820 associated deaths 2018. About 30% of AWD reported cases were of children below five years. (The Ministry of Health, Somali: Outbreak update – Cholera in Somalia, 4 Oct, 2018). NAPAD needs assessment(Sept, 2018) in Jubbaland and Galmudug state revealed main challenge to water access as high cost averaging 1.24 USD/20-liter jerrycan. Women and girls are burdened with providing water exposing them to GBV as they walk long distances to fetch water. 97% of sampled households don’t treat drinking water reason for high cases of diarrhea incidences 36%. About 88% of the sampled household didn’t have latrine with 92% respondents opting for OD away from home increasing risk of GBV to women and children.It is against these identified WASH challenges that NAPAD proposes to initiate interventions targeting most vulnerable populations, hard to reach, IDPs and host communities with linkages to education and health sectors. CLTS activities will trigger construction of decent safe latrines minimizing risk of GBV to women and girls while overall improvement of WASH conditions reduces AWD associated morbidity and mortality amongst children, women and men. Baseline and monitoring will be reporting using agreed tools including 4W matrix.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The project proposes to increase access to sustainable and affordable water for both livestock and human. Livestock rearing is the main livelihood for the target communities thus its improvement improves the wellbeing of the people reducing their vulnerability. Provision of sustainable WASH services improves the health of the community which frees resources otherwise used for medication and increases their productivity.The proposed interventions go beyond humanitarian assistance to sustainable service provision through construction of durable WASH infrastructures; concrete water kiosks and elevated tanks. This programming approach is explicitly articulated in the 20017-2022 Somalia National Development Plan and supports 2011 African government declaration that recognized that the recurrent crisis in horn of African can only be addressed through reforms to emergency response program by incorporating development assistance systems that promotes long term solutions. Solar power is emphasized as alternative energy source. Solar is abundant in Somalia where the project will be based. Solar Powered Water Supply System (SPWSS) have been tried and proofed- a NAPAD and other aid organizations have successfully implemented similar projectsThis proposal has put capacity building of communities at the cornerstone of programming, and emphasis on reducing key gender gaps by ensuring that women and youth are given the tools, resources, and opportunities to lead and participate in the management of WASH projects.All the WASH infrastructures will be constructed as per the WASH SOF guidelines and supervised by a WASH specialist. They will be sited in areas with minimum risk of flooding. The shallow wells will be protected with raised apron walls to minimize risks of flooding. The institutional latrines will be appropriately sited and constructed to minimize flood risks. The WASH committee training will include topics on disaster management and response. To enhance accountability to the beneficiaries, village project implementation committees will be formed during community mobilization to oversee the project. NAPAD will also share its complaint and feedback mechanism with the community with phone numbers of officers to be reached.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The project targets both IDP and host communities as a strategic way of avoiding exclusion based on displacement. In areas with IDP camps/Settlements water kiosks will be constructed at both host community and IDP selected sites. The communities will be actively involved is site selections to minimize disagreements that may lead to conflicts and eventual displacement. Communities will also be fully mobilized at the project start to ensure community buy in thus the community will protect the WASH infrastructures to avoid take over by militant groups leading to displacements.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The proposed WASH interventions are designed to benefit both genders and persons of all ages. The proposed institutional toilets will be separate for males and females, will be clearly marked in pictorial form for illiterate users and will work with the management of the schools and health facilities to ensure that the users adhere to that. In addition the toilets will have doors lockable from the inside to provide privacy and keep off any intruder. Efforts will be made to ensure that the toilets are easily accessible by the elderly and disabled by avoiding steps at the entry to the toilets. For water access, water collection points will be sited in open central areas not more than 500KM from the household. The water collection points at the kiosks will have raised platforms and ramps to ensure safe manual lifting to all, especially children, pregnant women, and persons with disabilities.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Poor engagement of community and their leaders from the inception of the project is a major risk factor that can lead to conflict that can escalate to violence. Comprehensive community and leadership engagement and orientation to the objectives of the project will ensure that the community owns the project. Discrimination, inequalities of targeting among clans as well as inaccessibility and competitiveness over natural resources is a risk factor that can lead to conflict and violence greatly impacting access to WASH facilities production and other small holder livelihoods. This project seeks to engage the administration, local leadership and food security actors in target areas right from the inception of the project to the completion. This community leadership will ensure that there is will peace building and conflict management structures that will intervene in times of conflicts.
