Project Summary

This proposed action contributes to building household and community resilience to withstand economic and climatic shocks leading to food insecurity in Borno state. The project seek to engage existing community agricultural groups (including the youth and women) within the target population who will be nabled to increase productivity through realistic, conflict sensitive, sustainable, environmentally conscious and profitable livelihood opportunities. The project focuses on community owned and led resilience projects that supplement government effort to revive nutrition sensitive agricultural productivity, improve entrepreneurial engagement and provide agricultural input support required to rehabilitate livelihood assets of conflict-affected populations in Northeast Nigeria. NRC’s planned action is in line with government’s post insurgency plan for agriculture in rural areas, and promotes sustainable food production through rehabilitation of smallholder irrigation schemes to allow communities to rebuild their livelihoods. The project targets 7 LGAs in Borno and plan to reach an estimated 1,300 households with irrigation rehabilitation support and an additional 1,125 households with small scale business and vocational trainings as well as starter kits to enable them access to reliable livelihoods.

1 - Needs

In much of the northeast, conflict and displacement have disrupted the agriculture industry, which is the backbone of the majority of the rural population’s livelihoods. This disruption has affected several smallholder irrigation schemes initiated by the Government of Nigeria (GoN) to promote agricultural productivity and promote resilience in the drought-prone Sahel region. Insecurity and displacement has also destroyed the once vibrant agricultural community cooperatives that used to operate viable small-scale irrigation schemes. The conflict continues to disrupt farmers' livelihoods. As of August 2018, only 42% of targeted households had managed to receive agriculture and livelihood assistance. Farmers in Borno state have been unable to cultivate their lands due to the conflict and fear of improvised explosive devices as well as a lack of agricultural inputs and military restrictions. These restrictions include limits on the sale of fertiliser and bans and curbs on the trade of goods in the northeast, in an attempt to reduce supplies and revenue for the insurgency. Similary, youth who constitute a signifacant proportion of the population have no access to employment opportunities and the majority lack the requisite skills to engage in meaningful livelihoods activities. Lack of skills and capital to start viable projects has been identified as a major challenge within the LGAs in Borno.

2 - Activities

. Smallholder irrigation schemes rehabilitation and development: NRC will use community based targeting approaches to target, select and register 6000 households from 6 LGAs previously engaged in irrigation farming to be assisted with rehabilitation of their irrigation schemes. The project will target a maximum of 1 hectare per household. 2. Small scale business trainings and distribution of start up grants: NRC will support 2,000 households, mainly female headed households with trainings on selection, planning and management of small scale business. Upon completion of the trainings, each beneficiary will be supported with a business grant approximately EUR 145 to enable them to start an income generating activity of their choice. 3. Vocational and technical skills trainings and distribution of starter kits: In order to support the smallholder irrigation skills and ensure sustainability, NRC will target approximately 125 individuals from the within the irrigation schemes who will be capacitated on small irrigation scheme maintanence. They will also be supported with starter kits to enable them to provide for themselves through self employment. Throughout the implementation process, NRC will establish strong field teams that will coordinate and supervise the implementation. Activities will be coordinated with other partners in the Early Recovery Sectors and Livelihoods Sector. NRC will ensure beneficiary feedback mechanisms form part of the M&E framework to ensure protection concerns are avoided such as sexual abuse. Beneficiary feedback will be enhanced through complaints boxes and hotlines to allow communities to provide feedback on our implementation
