1 - Needs

In Eastern Sudan, more than 1.4 million people face poverty, drought, famine, and inadequate access to basic services, including health and nutrition. Surveillance reports show that the burden of infectious diseases is compounded by cross-border population movements from Ethiopia, further contributing to disease spread. A 2019 survey led by the Federal Ministry of Health (FMoH) using simple, spatial method (S3M) showed that in the previous two weeks in East El Galabat, 67.1% of respondents had sought treatment from a medical facility for their child in relation to diarrhea and 50.8% had sought treatment for their child’s suspected ARI. While local/traditional systems to facilitate the referral of cases that require higher-level of medical care do exist in the PHCCs of East El Galabat, there are no ambulance services at the primary healthcare centers. Once people finally arrive at the facility, staffing is poor due to poor salaries and incentives, which leads to delays in accessing appropriate care. Overall, in 18% of health facilities in East El Galabat, not a single healthcare provider was found to be present. Ninety percent of facilities had no medical assistant, 87% did not have a midwife and 38% did not even have a community health care worker. These findings were consistent with findings in other states of the country. Fortunately, around 90% of the community/catchment population can reach a health facility in less than 30 minutes by foot. Eighty-eight percent of children under two years of age had a vaccination card, however, only 29% of children less than 12 months old had full EPI coverage. In East El Galabat, almost 66% of women did not attend four (or more) antenatal care (ANC) visits during their last pregnancy. Of those that did receive ANC, only 8% received postnatal care (PNC) during their most recent birth, while 30% did not receive at least two doses of tetanus toxoid injections. More than 50% of pregnant women did not use iron-folic acid tablets for three or more months, while more than 80% did not receive vitamin A tablets. Most healthcare providers (90%) could identify two danger signs of pregnancy. The S3M survey indicated a high prevalence of child malnutrition in Sudan. In the East El Galabat locality, Global Acute Malnutrition in children under 5 was very close to emergency levels at 9.6 percent based on WHZ, with 1.6 percent SAM and 0.9 percent oedema. The prevalence of acute malnutrition among pregnant and lactating women was 11.6 percent, with 3.2 classed as severely malnourished with MUAC <185mm, the highest of any of the localities in Gedaref (average 7.7 percent). Approximately 13 percent of the caregivers were aware of a growth monitoring program in their area. In regards to messaging, most people (48 percent) preferred to receive health and nutrition messages from an out-reach worker, followed by social media (35 percent) and radio (12 percent). Thirty-four percent of caregivers of U5s reported having received counselling on IYCF.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Deliver minimum basic package of primary health care services (including maternal and child health) and support referral to secondary health care.In four PHCCs, Mercy Corps will carry-out minor repair and maintenance, hand-washing facilities, toilets/washrooms (for men and women), and fences. This will be based on the initial assessment at the beginning of the project. Mercy Corps will also provide solar lighting for each PHCC. In addition, Mercy Corps will support SMoH in managing the safe disposal of waste (for example, by providing basic waste burners) and help prevent infections within and outside health facilities. All construction/rehabilitation will be carried out by a contractor and monitored by our engineer.Mercy Corps will coordinate with SMoH, WHO, UNFPA and UNICEF and WHO to ensure availability of essential medicines, equipment and other supplies to the four PHCCs. The equipment and supplies will include medical and non-medical items such as hospital beds for short stays, disinfectants, hand washing facilities, water storages tanks, soap, solar-powered refrigerators, chairs, benches, couches, tables, cupboards, OTP cards, stationary, IEC materials, and appropriate signboards.Mercy Corps will support the SMoH’s master trainers and collaborate with UN partners to train 20 (12 male, 8 female) public-sector healthcare providers on IMCI/CCM (Community Case Management), CMAM and IYCF; and support the SMoH in conducting refresher training for 15 midwives on family planning, clean delivery and GBV. The support will include per-diems for the facilitators and participants (according to the agreed government rates), travel costs (where necessary), refreshments, and training materials. We will encourage the facilitators to create time and space for a two-way dialogue with participants to understand, and where possible address, barriers to delivery of quality healthcare provision. Because of the COVID-19 pandemic, we will ensure a limited number of participants at each training, maintain physical distancing, and use face masks and hand sanitizers.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Women and girls, especially refugees, in Gedaref State face numerous obstacles in accessing health and nutrition services. To ensure that gender and protection are mainstreamed in the program, Mercy Corps will abide by Do No Harm principles. In terms of staffing, we will strive for gender balance in the recruitment of staff at all levels. We will train officers, responsible for delivering services and inputs to participants, on gender issues and gender-sensitive approaches. These efforts will be supported by a Gender and Safeguarding Coordinator who will be partially funded through this project. Mercy Corps’ in-house gender expertise will provide a solid foundation for the program. We have carefully designed the proposed project to ensure maximum participation of women and girls, for example, availability of safe, separate washrooms and waiting areas will encourage women to seek quality care from the participating PHCCs.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Mercy Corps has strong global policies in place to prevent Fraud, Conflict of Interest, and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), along with a strong whistleblowing policy including an independent agency to receive serious complaints. Mercy Corps’ whistleblowing policy allows a person to raise a concern directly with senior leaders if they feel unable to use usual reporting processes. All staff, volunteers and partners that work with or on behalf of Mercy Corps sign the policy prior to beneficiary engagement. Regular training ensures employees/volunteers are provided with necessary information regarding child safeguarding issues. Mercy Corps will participate in various fora to share experineces on accountability with other INGOs and during Cluster meetings.
