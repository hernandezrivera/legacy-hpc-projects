Project Summary

The conflict in Sudan, which started mid of April 2023, has created a significant barrier to education, as well as suffering and psychological impact on children, teachers, and communities. According to UNICEF Humanitarian Report No.13 as of November 2023, there are over 3 million children displaced by the war and with 19 million children still unable to return to classrooms. The report elaborates that millions of vulnerable children have been denied access to learning as a result of the crisis, particularly in areas of active conflict, where 5 million school-aged children remain out-of-school. Furthermore, the report indicated 2 million school-age children, including 51% girls, have been internally displaced, and half a million school-age have crossed international border. The impact of conflict on education is putting the future of the entire generation of children at risks, as they will likely miss out on education. According to observations of WR field teams, dozens of schools are damaged whiles other school buildings are used as shelters for the displaced populations, while teachers have not received their salaries for months.
The project is designed to mitigate the impact of Sudan conflict on education sector and significantly contribute towards achieving education cluster objective to increase access of crisis-affected vulnerable children to education in safe and protective learning environment, improving equitable quality education, and strengthening capacities of education institutions and communities to deliver a timely and coordinated education response. At least 20317 vulnerable children, women, girls, children with disabilities, parents and teachers, will benefit from this project, where 54% are female and 46% are male, composed of 75% IDPs, 13% host community, and 12% from nondisplaced communities in conflict affected areas of Jazeera, Blue Nile, South Kordofan, Central and West Darfur states of Sudan. At least 16608 children, of which 731 children with disabilities from the IDPs, host and nondisplaced communities will receive timely, appropriate and quality support to access education.
WR aims to establish safe and child-friendly learning spaces, equipped with both learning and recreational supplies with safe drinking water and hand washing facilities, rehabilitate temporary learning spaces as well as some classrooms. WR will distribute dignity kits as well as MHM educations and emergency feeding to children at the schools and safe learning spaces. Furthermore, it also aims at providing psychosocial support with social emotional learning through counseling, recreational activities and other interventions essential to equip children, caregivers, PTA and teachers with the skills they need to cope with trauma and protect children from further harm. To ensure quality education and access to alternative education and learning opportunities, teachers will be provided with incentives and teaching materials, training on child protection, GBV prevention, PSEA, and professional development. To ensure ownership of the project, PTAs shall be trained on school management, safety, protection of children and PFA and MHPSS. WR will ensure collaboration among all sectors and partners to ensure coordinated response to protection of children and ensuring safe access to education.
WR's previous experience implementing education in emergency programs in Sudan provided valuable lessons and insights towards improving quality and safe access to education services in emergency contexts following EiE and inter-agency standards in humanitarian actions. WR will ensure participation and empowerment of the ministry of education, teachers, PTAs, children and communities as a whole for a safety learning environment and timely delivery of services and collaboration and coordination throughout the project cycle-from needs assessment, project design and implementation, monitoring and reporting, evaluations, to learning.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

WR will ensure adherence to minimum standards for education in emergencies and national and international guidelines to ensure gender inclusive and sustainable education programming. Gender mainstreaming will be strictly applied through staff recruitment, beneficiary’s selection, and project monitoring. To ensure protection is mainstreamed and conflict sensitivity, beneficiaries will be addressed with consideration of DO NO HARM principles, ensuring fair representation of different vulnerable groups regardless of their tribes or ethnicities and gender. In addition, the project will ensure project staff and community leaders are involved for raising their awareness on protection of children and the value of access to education, proposed activities will ensure that all activities are implemented to improve the well-being of children and contribute towards improving boys' and girls' access to education on equal basis. The proposed project will consider analyzing of the social constraints that affect access of children to education and developed strategies that ensure their active participation of communities. WR is ensuring educational materials provided at the safe learning spaces and child-friendly spaces in quality and equal measures.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

WR uses community-based approached to protection of children and ensuring meaningful access of children to learning and education, in which capacities of children, adolescents, PTAs, protection workings groups, authorities and the communities as a whole are strengthened, engaged meaningfully and substantially in all aspects of the project that affects them and ensuring their leading role as driving force to protection and education of children, which will significantly contribute towards affective and adaptive activities that address the needs of the affected population, particularly children.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

WR will ensure full induction and orientation on WR's zero tolerance SEA policy to the staff, volunteers, and education personnel on-ground (teachers, facilitators, PTAs, headmasters, etc.) and collaborate with inter-agency mechanism to improve approaches. WR will produce PSEA and child safeguarding materials to clearly reflect the polices in an easy-to-understand messages. These messages will also include reporting and complaint process as well as mechanisms to handle and record reported cases. These materials will be placed in schools and government offices. WR monitoring visit to the project sites will include the focal point of PSEA.
