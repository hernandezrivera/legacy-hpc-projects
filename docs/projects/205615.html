Project Summary

Through this project, Action Against Hunger is aiming to target a total of 125015 (60,008 males and
65,007 females) vulnerable IDPs, Returnees and Host community.
Our areas of intervention will primarily be Fangak, and Pibor Counties of Jonglei State,
Gogrial West, Gogrial East and Twic counties of Warrap state and Aweil Centre County of Northern
Bahr el Ghazal. Our response activities will be integrated with nutrition, WASH, and
protection mainstreaming. All the under 5 children, pregnant and lactating women will be screen for
malnutrition at the health facilities and enrolled to our nutrition programming. All our
health facilities will have minimum package of WASH services. We will consider
protection aspect and our medical team will be train on SGBV counselling and
management. We will take into consideration the protection area by providing the first aid
care to the SGBV victims and MHPSS as well as identifying and strengthening the referral
pathway for clinical management of rape and mental health cases. Our response will
focuses on returnees, IDPs and host communities. The response plan will be prioritized
based on epidemiological prole of disease outbreaks, such as cholera hot spot locations,
measles outbreaks and malaria seasonality. IDSR, EWARS, attacks on health care and
disability tracking tools will be used to determine the feasibility of the response. Our
operational plans will always involve the community members, women, girls, youths,
PLWs (people living with disability), County Health department (CHD) in planning, MOH and
Humanitarian partners in our operational areas. Meetings with communities and CHDs are some of the
indicators used to monitor the accountability for affected populations. We will facilitate
meetings with all stakeholders like partners, beneficiaries, women groups and local
communities for their involvement in planning, implementation, monitoring and
evaluation of the projects in all stages of the program cycle.
To improve access to essential life-saving health care services, and to address
inaccessibility and long distance to access health services, the organization will use
outreach clinics through community health volunteers BHI/ICCM to provide health services at
the community as well as static health facilities (PHCCs and PHCCs) to provide Basic
Package of health and nutrition services as per South Sudan guideline to achieve the
following objectives:

1. Needs

Currently, South Sudan is experiencing a steep escalation in humanitarian needs, with 9.4 million individuals, including 4.9 million children and 2.2 million women, requiring assistance as of 2023 attributed to factors such as flooding, food insecurity, inflation, and subnational conflict. There are a number of complex challenges that are faced in the proposed areas of intervention (Pibor/Kassinor, Fangak, Twic, Gogrial East, Gogrial West and Aweil Centre) including displacement of populations, losses of livelihoods, property, assets all of which contribute to people engaging in negative coping strategies especially in Pibor, Fangak and Twic Counties. The main health problems in the proposed counties are malnutrition, malaria, acute respiratory infections, diarrheal and measles among children particularly under-fives. The key health services provided in the mentioned counties are outpatient and inpatient consultations, maternal and child health care, immunization, integrated community case management, nutrition and minimum water, sanitation and hygiene however the service providers are straggling due to lack of funding. Gogrial West County is one of the largest counties in South Sudan, with 56 health facilities of which HPF now supports only 14 health facilities. It is one of the major influx points of returnees due to the conflict in Sudan from which they then disperse to other locations within South Sudan. Both Gogrial East and Twic have suffered from flare ups in inter-communal violence driven by border and land disputes as well as competition for natural resources and cattle raiding. According to the South Sudan Health Cluster Dashboard, Gogrial West, East and Twic are among the counties with the highest number of people in need of health services. Gogrial East has 19 health facilities (of which HPF supports only 13), and Action Against Hunger supports 1 PHCU and provides mobile health outreaches at Mobiortiit and Malual Kuel in Gogrial East. Twic has 25 health facilities (with 23 supported by HFP). Aweil County has a total of 22 health facilities but only 8 are functional including 5 PHCCs, 2 PHCUs and Aweil County Hospital. In 2022 UNOCHA identified Aweil Centre County among nine counties with the most severe health related conditions, with the need for healthcare services being at catastrophic levels. According to UNOCHA Humanitarian Needs Overview 2023, more than 56,000 people in the county require humanitarian assistance, up from 51,900 in 2021 and represents approximately 73% of the total population. The county suffers from high levels of inaccessibility, particularly during the rainy season with large areas prone to flooding. This has led to reduced availability of food stuffs in the market, increased prices and heightened levels of food insecurity. Generally, primary healthcare access in Warrap, Jonglei and Northern Bahr el-Ghazal is poor leading to huge disease burden and deaths particularly among infants. Limited financing, weakened healthcare infrastructure and governance, lack of medical supplies and equipment and some cultural beliefs are the key constraints undermining healthcare delivery and outcomes in the proposed locations.The targeted locations are among the counties with the highest number of people in need of health services (Health Cluster).

2. Project activities

This project will focus on the following Activities: 1- BHI/ICCM services: To address the issues of inaccessibility and health service coverage, the organization will use BHI/ICCM approach to diagnose and treat common childhood illnesses such as mild malaria, pneumonia, and diarrhoea at the community level. The BHT/CBDs will be train and equip with raped diagnostic test for malaria/mRDT, Respiratory timer, Thermometers, oral anti-malarial drugs, oral Amoxicillin, Zinc tablets and ORS for diagnose and treatment of Malaria, Pneumonia and Diarrhoea. The BHI/ICCM will be used as the first referral point, whereby the BHT/CBDs will be referring the complicated cases to the nearby health facility for further management. 2- EPI services: To increase the coverage and to respond to the outbreaks such as measles and Vitamin A supplement, the organization will use mobile vaccination in the community and static vaccination at the health facilities to ensure the wider coverage. We will be engaging UNICEF to provide Artech, refrigerator for hard to reach areas and referral of women of childbearing ages to receive routine immunization in static and outreach EPI services. 3- OPD consultations/services: The OPD will provide consultation and treatment for common illnesses such simple malaria, Acute water diarrhoea (AWD), acute respiratory infections (ARI), UTIs, skin and eye infections, minor wounds/injuries, active case finding for TB/HIV defaulters, Visceral Leishmaniosis (Kala-Azar) and timely referral of such cases to the nearest TB-HIV or Kala-Azar treatment centres. 4- Antenatal care and maternity services: Through ANC services, blood pressure and haemoglobin level will be monitor in each visits, and all the pregnant women will be provided with ferrous + folic acid, Albendazole for deworming, screening for Syphilis, HIV and other STIs, rapid test for malaria (RDTs), malaria prophylaxis, support of normal deliveries in health facilities and referral of complicated pregnancies and deliveries to the nearest CEMoNC facilities. 5- Detect, investigate, report and respond to outbreaks example of cholera, measles, COVID-19 in the areas of target through strengthening EWARS/IDSR system in training and coordination with stakeholders (Health Cluster-WHO, MoH and CHD). 6- Identification, provision of psychological support and first aid treatment to the SGBV victims and Clinical Management of Rape/CMR as well as referral of Sexual and Gender Based Violence (SGBV) through protection partners in the areas of our operation. We will use our existing projects across the proposed locations to boost up the 2024-HRP project activities. 7- Screening for acute malnutrition: To ensure the integration of the services, all the health workers (outreach teams and health workers in health facilities) will be train on identification of acute malnutrition in both children under-5 and pregnant and lactating women/PLWs using MUAC and link the malnourished one to our nutrition programming. 8- Identification of PLWDs (People Living with Disabilities) and mentally ill persons among the community and refer them to specialized centres after giving basic health care at our health facilities. Action Against Hunger is capable to provide humanitarian response to emerging crisis that are anticipated in 2024. We will use our presence in 3 states to contribute to the strategic plan of health cluster, WHO and MOH in the year 2024. These activities will tailor to achieve the health cluster strategic objectives. Reporting tools will be ERWARS weekly report, IDSR, monthly HMIS2 and 5Ws to Health Cluster, WHO, CHD and MoH. All these forms will be availed to the field staff in all locations and make sure all indicators are captured and any outbreak investigation will be recorded and reported during the implementation.
