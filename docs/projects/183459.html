1 - Needs

The need for stronger cluster coordination and information management in oPt remains to be emphasized by the persisting Covid-19 pandemic, sporadic violent clashes and escalation in hostilities, access barriers, along with the overstretched, and fragile healthcare system. Monitoring healthcare facilities and resources availability has become more difficult as the situation is fluid, demanding greater investment in tools such as the HeRAMS which currently only covered MoH facilities mainly in the Gaza Strip but need to be expanded to include NGO healthcare facilities and include facilities in West Bank. To ensure accessible, acceptable standard and quality of humanitarian health interventions there is need for greater monitoring and support to Health Cluster partners. Documenting and reporting on the challenges that patients and their companions across oPt encounter in accessing healthcare due to the delay and denial of permits required by Israel to move between areas of oPt, as well as to Israel and Jordan. In addition, 2021 witnessed an elevated level of attacks on healthcare which hindered access for patients and put the lives of health workers at risk. There is increased demand for capacity building and support on cross-cutting issues such as gender, age, disability, protection, PSEA and AAP for Health Cluster partners. With the large membership of the Health Cluster there is need for a robust cluster coordination structure providing leadership and technical guidance to maximize rational allocation or resources and benefit to the most vulnerable amongst the affected population. With 40% of membership being national NGOs, it is important that the Health Cluster fosters stronger partnerships, complementarity, and capacity building especially considering the operational challenges they experience. Platforms such as the technical working groups and the Strategic Advisory Group offer opportunities for the partners to benefit from technical expertise. In addition, there is massive health information generated from daily operations, assessments that need to be done to ensure that the ever-changing needs of the population are identified and met. Information needs to be properly collected (including disaggregated by gender, age, disability and any other relevant intersection and social determinant) using online platform and analyzed to highlight the health needs of the vulnerable groups and for a more evidence-based approach to cluster priority setting and identification of gaps within interventions. The need to enable Health Cluster Partners of accessing health information, health assessments, Cluster activities, HeRAMS dashboard through the sustainability of Health Cluster website, that also will enhance greater collaboration and information sharing between the Health Cluster and other clusters, which is needed to ensure the integration of inter-sectoral issues in all aspects of health interventions.

2 - Activities

The Health Cluster will aim to enhance coordination and the response of its membership. Key coordination activities such as the monthly meetings and providing platforms such as the Health Cluster website and social media for partners to engage and strengthen partnerships will continue to take place. Working together with partners in monitoring of health indicators and setting up of minimum standards for health and interventions with guidance from technical expertise from the WHO and latest scientific evidence. The Health Cluster will promote greater information sharing with other clusters and inter-sectorial coordination and linkages to ensure a comprehensive operational response. Across the oPt, the Health Cluster activities will be to: 1) Produce Health Cluster Bulletin quarterly and times of crisis SitReps or contribute to inter-cluster SitReps. 2) Develop Health Cluster Contingency Plans to ensure a timely response to emergencies, whether it is conflict-related or disease outbreak e.g. COVID-19.3) Expand the HeRAMS (an approach to monthly monitoring of health facilities and resources availability) to encompass health facilities in the West Bank.4) Promote monitoring of Health Cluster activities through online platform, that include the PARP, HRP periodic reporting and procurement tracker. 5) Promote monitoring for attacks against healthcare, including barriers to accessing healthcare. 6) Support partners in conducting health needs assessments.7) Mobilize resources through donor engagements via quarterly briefings, disseminating information the impact of funding shortages. 8) Enhance gender, age, disability, protection, PSEA and AAP mainstreaming through having a dedicated focal point to support the cluster and partners to improve these key areas mainstreaming in health interventions, coordinating and linking with key agencies and stakeholders working on the issues (UN Women, Protection Cluster, GBV sub-cluster, PSEA Network, Local women rights´ organisations) and reporting as well as providing specialised trainings to partners and visibility materials such as posters on reporting mechanisms and PSEA9) Support partners in enhancing community engagement in health through encouraging the active participation of beneficiaries.10) Continue the work of the technical working groups i.e. Trauma, Nutrition, Mobile Clinics and Advocacy11) Conduct monitoring and support field visits to partners
