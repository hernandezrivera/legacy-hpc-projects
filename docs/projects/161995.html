Project Summary

"CARE is the SMS agency of camps 13 and 16 where CARE has also been implementing site development and DRR works, protection, GBV and SRH activities in addition to WASH interventions only in camp 16. Our different teams’ collaboration has been directly contributing to a more comprehensive, inclusive and tailored approach in shelter assistance. In 2020, CARE aims to provide transitional shelter assistance (TSA) to households who previously received “upgrade shelter kits” (USK). This will include in-kind core items to strengthen shelters against adverse weather shocks and technical assistance in implementation to extremely vulnerable households. In consultations and coordination with other agencies in the camp, our proposed project for 2020 will target 4000 HHs through a community-led approach. We will ensure during implementation to implement slope protection measures surrounding the house where needed and to connect the small drainages around shelters with the nearest large drain. CARE will explore alternative approaches to in-kind NFI distribution along with the Shelter/NFI Sector such as solar light home systems that will continue to be provided in-kind as the market is not yet able to offer items with the required specifications and in needed quantity. We believe that the installation and maintenance of home base solar lights will contribute to ensure safety, especially for women, children and vulnerable groups.
CARE will provide an orientation on DRR to men, women, boys & girls and shelter construction and maintenance training with different techniques to men and women. Having analyzed the different type of works and expertise the female workers, CARE Bangladesh will take initiatives to engage more female and disadvantage workers in the construction works. CARE will directly procure all materials in the proposed project. Our project will ensure coordination with national authorities, sector partners and sector coordinators to identify and address changing priority gaps and needs in assistance and ensure collaboration with CiCs, site management agencies and affected communities through our existing CFM in person through outreach and in information hubs at block level and through the hotline.
CARE Bangladesh is committed to implementing shelter improvement interventions with community led approach, through community engagement with women and men for equitable participation. In household training, women will be reached in targeted groups on DRR, shelter inspection, use of materials and drainage maintenance. as well as building more resilient communities who will be better able to support themselves in the longer term with maintenance. Partially damage causing moderate to major incident during monsoon will be addressed through IOM common pipe line agreement and sever event like cyclone incident, immediately response from stockpiling in the container at camp level.
At project onset, stakeholder orientation meetings will be conducted, engaging community members, imams and majhi and engaging the voices of the most marginalized such as women and girls and people living with disability. During the participatory beneficiary selection process, information will be triangulated and verified by CARE to ensure that the most vulnerable are targeted, and the communities understand all aspects of the activity. Shelter participates in the triangulation with site management population data, WASH and site development CFW opportunities, findings of gender and protection teams. Families define the shelter design according to their specific needs. In temporary relocations during construction, CARE prioritizes the needs of the most vulnerable (e.g. single headed households, pregnant women/new mothers).

Needs

The Cox’s Bazar district, situated on the Bay of Bengal, has witnessed to influx of forcefully displaced Rohingya people from Myanmar over the past two years. Bamboo provided in upgrade shelter kits in the past is deteriorating, further reducing the overall strength of shelters. Improved shelters (TSA) to be continue to build will be main needs for 2020 for shelter response same way of 2019 needs in collaboration with the Government, taking safety, durability, cost and dignity into account as emergency Shelter/NFI preparedness and this response remains a high priority.Camps and sites remain congested seriously impacting physical and psychological well-being, especially of children, women, and persons with disabilities. Moreover, the lack of proper lighting in shelters and public spaces was reported as a top safety concern followed by danger or violence whenever the dwellers need to leave the house. Exacerbating this, nearly all structures have been constructed using low quality bamboo creating perfect conditions for pests and rot. The above factors mean that the vast majority of bamboo within the camps will need to be replaced within the next 12 months, a much shorter time frame than expected. Extensive pest damage can already be seen throughout the camps, particularly in culms harvested while still immature or during the monsoon season (which are particularly attractive to pests). Owing considering the above factors, it is anticipating that about 30% shelters need to be repair with small house connecting drainage and slope stabilization surrounding the house where needed within 2020. In the CARE-managed camps 13 and 16, shelter gaps persist despite previous assistance. In camp 13, there are 7,413 shelters (80% total) that require upgrading, while in camp 16 the shelter gap is 3,209 shelters (65% of total).The recent monsoon season had weakened the shelters. Bamboo has also deteriorated due to beetle infestation, resulting in a situation in which shelters can easily be broken into creating an urgent need for precautionary and response measures, to meet immediate needs and protecting those who are most vulnerable. CARE plans to focus this intervention in camps 13 and 16. Camp 13 has roughly double the population of camp 16, with higher levels of congestion, poor shelter conditions, steeper and more varied topography, and a greater number of roads, footpaths and slopes requiring significant site improvement and infrastructure assistance. Different vulnerabilities affect households, including household condition and family make-up. 80% of the camp population is women or children, and 29% of households are extremely vulnerable, with a member with a disability, a pregnant or nursing mother, or an elderly individual. In shelter construction, these households require targeted support including labor, temporary relocation, to ensure they can safely access shelter assistance.
