Project Summary

As a strategy for 2022, Shafak, in line with education cluster’s objectives and in coordination with involved authorities and stakeholders, intends to focus on achieving two main goals for a total of 43,712 5-17 yr. school children (20,545 boys; 23,167 girls, including 1088 CWDs) and 1,536 education personnel (736 males, 800 males):
1) Scaling up access to formal and non-formal education for Syrian crisis-affected children: Access strategies will include small-scale rehabilitation of damaged schools and establishment of other safe temporary/alternative learning spaces through a gender-sensitive and disability-friendly approach.
a)- Formal education activities in 56 schools targeting approximately 34,496 students (16,213 b; 18,283 girls; 5-17 yr. old) through classroom learning modality (with plan B to resort to distance learning modality, based on developments of COVID 19). This intervention will minimize the risk of students dropping out, where monthly incentives to teachers will be provided, as they don’t have any alternative fund in addition to provision of education materials for students and teachers and providing schools with necessary operational costs (including heating for winter and school safety equipment). As education is one of the very basic children’s rights Shafak is planning to provide access to education for children with disabilities by providing transportation services and improve school environment by provide targeted assistance within the schools in order to encourage these children to continue their education and integrate them with their peers.
b)- Non-formal education opportunities and pathways back to inclusive education at age appropriate levels are increasingly required to address the complex learning needs of children through classroom learning modality (with plan B to resort to distance learning modality, based on developments of COVID 19), making use of 4 BTL campaigns conducted for that end. Thus, non-formal education will target school girls and boys (aged 8-12 and gender disaggregated) to fill in existing needs, where 24 Non-Formal education centers will be supported targeting approximately a total of 9,216 children (4332 b; 4884 g; 5-17 yr. old) to provide SLP and catchup for 2416 children (1136 b, 1280 g) in addition to remedial classes for 6800 (3196 b, 3604 g) along with PSS sessions; the PSS activities will be selected from I-Deal curriculum; the curriculum used for non-formal Activities will be curriculum B that combines two academic years in one, SIG for remedial and Self Learning Material books.
2) Enhancing the quality of formal and non-formal education within a protective environment. Teachers will be equipped with the skills in non-formal education modalities, child-centered pedagogy, and positive classroom management to cope with overcrowded classrooms, address and accommodate host community, displaced and returnee children within their classes, in addition to regular monthly conducted PTA sessions.
Stakeholders in the project implementation site, including children, adolescents and older persons, including PwDs and their opinions are taken into account while developing the project design, implementation of activities so that they have better access to humanitarian services and information in accordance to their specific situation.
The intervention will make sure that children engaging in child labor or recruited for military purposes, or living with older or disabled caregivers or adolescent girls at risk of early or forced marriage are given special attention and have their own space to participate in the project activities and are properly referred as per child protection sub-cluster guidelines.
As for 2023, according to Shafak's strategy and fund availability, it is expected to implement the same activities with the same size and budget.

4 - Needs

The protracted nature of the conflict, economic duress and the Covid-19pandemic, continue to impact overstretched education services. Over 180,000 school-aged children are in need of education services. 2.1M Children in Syria are OOSC, and a further 1.3 million are at risk of dropping out. … In 1,017 assessed camp sites in northwest, there are only 196 schools. (HNO, 2022)Due to the mitigation measures, children remained out of school for most of the 2020 – 2021 academic year. COVID-19, along with the economic situation, resulted in high levels of drop out. More than 1 million out of 1.7 million school aged children are out of school in the north-west, compared to the 580,000 in 2019. Children who do not continue their education are more exposed to protection risks, including child labour and child marriage. (Situation Report No. 33 – November 2021)The number of school-age children in the NW of Syria (within the assessed districts) reached 1,712,468; and according to JENA report, 34% (582,239 children) of those school-age children are out of school.In a study conducted by Shafak in August 2021 within the targeted locations, 63% of the interviewed 414 OOSC children are out of schools due to the school unavailability in their residence areas especially primary education schools; 41% mentioned shortage of stationary and formal/non-formal curricula as the second factor; 35% of the total studied cases due to transportation.

5 - Implementation of activities

The intervention is designed to provide quality education services to Syrian conflict-affected children who most vulnerable inside Syria, by proposing activities within areas with high density of IDPs and HCs. To do so, Shafak will make sure to involve stakeholders, including children, including PwDs and their opinions in the implementation plan and that their voices are heard and taken into account while specific needs assessments are conducted and while implementing activities, so that they have better access to humanitarian services and information in accordance to their specific situation. These consultations include FGDs with participation of different target beneficiaries, including stakeholders such as teachers, school management, local authorities, and students themselves (with age and gender taken in consideration) in order to identify particular needs as highlighted by the various groups. Other crosscutting issues such as GBV protection, child protection from violence, exploitation and abuse, and rights of vulnerable groups will be mainstreamed in the project. Shafak will start with validating the needs in the target schools through field surveys to reassess the needs. By these surveys, Shafak will identify the exact schools’ needs, children (girls and boys) and teachers (females and males) numbers in the proposed schools. BTL campaigns are conducted to make referrals of OOSC to schooling again, and Plan B for COVID-19 outbreak has been detailed in PRA.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk1: Occurrence of COVID-19 cases in the targeted communities among children and education personnelMitigating Measure: daily communication and coordination with ED and other Edu and health organizations to know the latest updates about COVID-19. Suspend the schools immediately and prevent any kind of physical attendance at schools and shift into the adopted remote education modality. Risk2: Schools closure due to COVID-19MM: start the adopted distance education modality that Shafak has a good experience in, since the spread of COVID-19 disease all over the world, provide more training and feedback instruction by supervisors and S&S departments to teachers concerning the E-Learning skills to improve their performance with students.Risk3: Child safety incidents in school due to bullying and overburdened teachers MM: Training of partners and local teams on Child safety, development of schools’ specific child safety policies; establishment of complaint mechanisms in school; Security/safety training for organization, students and school staff.Risk4: Protection risks of mixing different age groups in the same centre/class MM: Create separate times of attendance for each age group.Risk 5: Some targeted teachers may receive salaries from a third party, or are forced to get part of their incentives deducted MM: Confirmations are obtained from all education directorates and education actors that no teacher is permitted to get paid gain from any other party.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), including harassment, against women and girls by distributors and male beneficiaries while queuing and before/after distributions Risk Management Strategy/Mitigating Measure: - recruit male and female volunteers who are respected in the community and independent of armed groups. No children to be employed (under 18 years old) - make sure staff are already trained on Do No Harm, protection from SEA (PSEA), and child safeguarding; and sign and adhere to code of conduct that respects and uphold the rights of beneficiaries and PSEA - set up communication and complaints mechanisms or desks that are clearly marked; face interviews with beneficiaries, including women and children.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Risk: borders are temporarily closed, or humanitarian access restricted, between Syria and its neighbouring countries because of national regulations, of advances by GoS towards neighbouring countries' borders. Mitigation: - ongoing advocacy with relevant authorities for humanitarian access to vulnerable populations in Syria. Dividing stock of commodities between Syria and neighbouring countries in anticipation of risk; prioritizing local procurements (inside Syria).2. Risk: Existing traditional practices and community structures conflict with child protection principles (e.g. corporal punishment, child rights messaging is perceived as a threat, etc.). Mitigation: - thorough contextual analysis to be undertaken and ensure safe access as part of the project design; - teachers are trained on positive discipline techniques as part of their basic training and sign a code of conduct in the school; - teachers and classrooms are regularly monitored; a child safe-guarding focal point is identified in the school; - Parent-Teacher Committees are consulted on any reports from their homes of children being mistreated.
