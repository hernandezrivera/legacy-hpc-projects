Project Summary

The proposed action will provide response to winter S/NFI needs and protect the well-being and thermal comfort of conflict affected highly vulnerable IDPs and host communities in North West and North East Syria. This will be achieved through a programme of assistance focused on providing cash-based winterization assistance to the most vulnerable host and IDP households (HHs) in areas with greatest severity of winter need. Whenever possible, complementary Food Security (FS) assistance will be provided to S/NFI beneficiaries.
During the first quarter of 2021, secured funding will provide cash-based winter assistance to a total of 6,275 acutely vulnerable HHs in Ariha, Atareb, Daret Azza, Afrin and Jandairis sub-districts. Community-level targeting will be informed by close coordination with the SNFI cluster and other members. One-off cash-based assistance will be provided to verified beneficiaries meeting set eligibility criteria with complementary FS assistance provided to the same HHs to protect the cash transfer for winter needs whenever possible. Funding is also sought for cash-based assistance - $150 p/HH - to meet the winter needs of total 11,750 HHs during the 2021 / 2022 winter season to maintain 2020 levels of assistance and therefore reduce the risk of gaps in the overall response. Geographic targeting will continue to be finalized in close coordination with the S/NFI cluster and other members to identify gaps in areas with high severity of winter need, while pre-and post-market assessments will be completed to ensure local markets can continue to support a cash-based approach. Winter 2021/22 assistance is planned in Ariha, Jebel Saman, Afrin and Raqqa districts and will target the most vulnerable HHs including, child- and female-headed HHs and PWDs.
Please note for activities implemented with a Lead Partner (INGO / UN), our submission shows only the caseload and budget to be implemented by our organisation. The Lead Partner will input their planned activities to the HPC separately, to ensure no duplication in targets reported.

4 - Needs

According to the 2021 Needs and Response Summary for Syria, an estimated 13.4 million people will require some form of humanitarian assistance during 2021. Two million Syrians are now living in acute poverty with 82% reporting a significant deterioration in their ability to meet basic needs since August 2019. Protracted displacement, COVID-19, currency depreciation and price increases mean already vulnerable conflict-affected people are increasingly unable to meet S/NFI, specifically increased needs to maintain thermal comfort during the coldest winter months. A recent MSNA, which we completed in Idleb and Aleppo, underlines the extreme socio-economic vulnerability of targeted groups. Interviews were conducted with 327 households, of which 87% IDP. Of these, 87% of HHs reported no source of income, rising to 90% of female respondents. All HHs interviewed stated that access to income generation opportunities had decreased since the start of the conflict. The majority (78%) of HH reporting being in debt, rising to 85% of female respondents. Given existing vulnerabilities, the majority of assessed HHs are already unable to meet basic needs, and will struggle to meet SNFI needs, specifically during the winter months, without resorting to harmful coping strategies. The assessment also found markets able to support a cash-based approach. The majority (92%) of vendors were observed to be fully stocked, with 81% found to be accessible to People With Disability (PWD). No protection concerns for men or women were observed with accessing surveyed vendors.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Project will follow cross-cutting protection mainstreaming guidelines as per protection cluster guidelines, including collection of sex and age disaggregation of information, participation, access, and an understanding of how different needs will be met. The project is designed with an emphasis on meaningful access, safety, dignity and non-discrimination so that all potential BNFs will benefit from inclusive service provision to the greatest extent possible. To mitigate protection risks, we work with communities & civilian stakeholders to ensure open & accountable feedback mechanisms. Informed by an understanding of the preferences of men & women & their access to various communication options, these include face-to-face interviews with BNFs and non-BNFs in our offices and a dedicated Whatsapp hotline. All assessments & monitoring activities will consider protection risks, monitor for concerns and assess relevance and implementation against the specific needs of men, women & PWD. Well-established procedures will ensure activities are equitable and gender sensitive, for ex. having separate lines for male & female BNFs at distribution points and ensuring female staff are always present. Accessibility measures will be built into assistance delivery, including the option for home delivery to BNFs unable to safely access distribution points due to mobility or safety reasons. The main protection risk of the loss of documents will be mitigated by using a community council registration and community verification mechanism. COVID-19 guidelines for all field activities have been successfully rolled out to mitigate the risk of transmission.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

We have strict protocols related to PSEA and Child Protection specifically, with refresher trainings completed regularly with all staff on the organizational PSEA and CP Policies and staff responsibilities as these relate to conduct, monitoring and reporting. All staff sign the organization’s code of conduct, which includes the obligation to report any concerns regarding SEA. Gender balance in our S/NFI and M&E teams will continue to ensure that activities are responsive to the particular needs of men, women, boys and girls, with female staff present at all distribution points and responsible for all M&E activities completed with female beneficiaries. Our Complaints Response Mechanism will continue to be widely promoted, both through information displayed prominently in public places, at all distribution points, distributed with winter cash assistance and through face-to-face interactions with project beneficiaries and key stakeholders. Distribution Monitoring and routine Post-Distribution Monitoring – completed by male and female staff with a representative sample of both male and female beneficiaries - will also continue to monitor for any SEA risks or concerns. Rapid Need and Market assessments will continue to inform the planning and design of all cash-based interventions. These will continue to ensure local markets support a cash approach, and that these are safely accessible by male and female beneficiaries.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The following assumptions have been applied to program design: •LCs and local authorities (including AFAD) remain supportive of the NGOs activities and continue to cooperate with established SOPs designed to ensure targeted & transparent delivery of assistance on the basis of need alone.•Local markets continue to support a cash-based approach to winter NFIs. Market functionality will be assessed thru rapid market tests completed to inform all winterization activities. •100% house-to-house BNF verification reduces the risk of inclusion or exclusion criteria while close coordination with clusters & other SNFI actors reduces the risk of duplication.•Established guidelines for COVID-19 prevention measures for all field operations continue to mitigate the risk of transmission.•Security context in targeted areas allows ongoing access by our staff.Mitigation measures can include temporary suspension of field activities in times of extreme conflict. In line with SOPs, distributions will be placed on temporary hold during heightened risk to staff and BNFs. We employ multiple measures to maintain delivery assistance which can include door to door distributions or phased distribution times to reduce risks of large numbers of people gathering. Guidelines are in place for all field activities to reduce the risk of COVID-19 transmission, incl. PPE usage, sanitization & social distancing during project activities. The use of cash seeks to mitigate possible supply chain breaks or border closures, while ensuring maximum dignity and flexibility for BNFs. For in kind NFIs, FAs are in place with NFI suppliers who have demonstrated reliability & ability to consistently deliver.
