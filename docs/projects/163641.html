Project Summary

In 2019, continued conflict, migration, displacement, and collapsed health system increased outbreaks of communicable diseases (CDs), and the limited capacity of surveillance and response system added to the burden of the CDs.
WHO supported Communicable Diseases (CDs) and Neglected Tropical Diseases (NTDs) control programmes in Libya over the past years by strengthen capacity, improve access to medicines and enhanced surveillance. The National Centre of Diseases Control (NCDC) works in a siloed manner with no coordination between different programmes. Cross-cutting interventions can be improved to work across programmes to deliver in a more inclusive manner. Combining expertise in disease-specific areas with cross-cutting issues such as surveillance, capacity building, advocacy and medical supply management can enhance NCDC control programmes. This project aims to streamline and integrate approach of NCDC programmes to:
• Support the development and updating of evidence-based norms, standards, policies, guidelines and strategies and research for prevention, control and elimination of CDs and NTDs in order to chart a course for reaching the related targets set in resolutions of the Health Assemblies.
• Provide prompt diagnostic testing of all suspected cases of CDs, NTDs and notifiable diseases.
• Enable improvement of supply chain management in particular through forecasting, timely procurement of quality-assured medicines, facilitating importation, customs clearance and improve stock-management systems.
• Strengthen epidemiological surveillance system of CDs and NTDs through the Integrated Data Platform to collect, validate and analyse data from different programmes.

1 - Needs

The ongoing conflict, migration, displacement and the collapsed health system requires urgent intervention, the year of 2020 will be the year of the shift of coordinated response to “severity scale” prioritization. The health sector has identified 3,970,842 people in need of health assistance, this includes 1,662,586 people in areas ranked 4 on the severity scale, and 122,485 people in areas ranked 5. In 2020 coordination will focus to identify solutions for access: to expand and carry out health services; to deliver health supplies; to carry out assessments and surveys; to monitor and evaluate needs and levels of assistance. Refugees and migrants in the west, east and south listed health as one of their top three priorities (source: MSNA). Severe shortages of health staff, medicines, supplies and equipment have been compounded by years of under-investment in the health system and a lack of transparency and accountability. Ineffective detection and response to communicable diseases through community health education, promotion, integration of vertical programming with other services led to Limited health care for patients with CDs. A potential for increase of vector-borne diseases across the country. Shortage of diagnosis and treatment. There are gaps in assessment, supervision, monitoring and evaluation-related activities across the country. Absence of comprehensive monitoring and evaluation plans to systematically evaluate trends, and weak capacity for data analysis and regular reporting. The nine-year long conflict in Libya has badly affected its health system. Most health care services and programmes have severe shortages of staff, medicines and medical supplies. Attacks on health care have damaged or destroyed facilities and the prevailing insecurity has further reduced access to services. According to the Early Warning Alert and Response Network (EWARN), lower and upper Acute Respiratory Infection (ARI), watery and bloody diarrhoea remain the leading causes of mortality in most of the districts of Libya. Similarly, spread of Tuberculosis (TB) among migrants and mobile populations with the identiﬁcation of Multidrug Resistance (MDR) continue to be documented in overcrowded detention centres and displaced settlements. The spread of TB among migrants who are originally from countries with high prevalence rates of Human Immunodeﬁciency Virus (HIV) raise another alarm for TB and HIV co-infections. TB infection with HIV is the most powerful known risk factor inclining for Mycobacterium tuberculosis infection and progression of HIV to active disease which increases the risk of latent TB reactivation 20-fold. Globally, TB is the most common cause of Acquired Immunodeﬁciency Syndromes (AIDS) related death; thus TB bacteria and HIV act in synergy accelerating the decline of immunological functions and leading to subsequent death if untreated.WHO targeting 8 NCDC Branches at Mantikas on severity scale 4-5 West: Zwara, AlJabal AlGharbi – 25%Middle: Sirte, Misrata– 25%East: Ejdabia, Alkufra– 25%South: Sabha and Murzuq – 25%

2 - Activities

The following activities will be implemented to enhance access to point of care testing and decentralize clinical management by activation of 8 NCDC branches (Ejdabia, Alkufra, Sirte, Misrata, Zwara, AlJabal AlGharbi, Sabha and Murzuq) for initial assessment of the patients, diagnosis and management of CDs and NTDs, to strengthen capacity for rapid detection of public health risks, prompt risk assessment, notification and response through enhanced early warning and rapid response mechanism and awareness on Anti-Microbial Resistance (AMR) and hospital acquired infections. In order to achieve the overall objective of the project: 1. Improving access to case-detection at point of care through:a. Procurement of rapid tests: Influenza rapid test, Measles Ig G rapid test, ToRCH Combo, Cryptococcal Antigen test (LFA), Strep kit rapid test, 4th generation HIV test, Rapid Antigen Detection Tests (TB LAM), Hep A Ig M rapid test, Combo HBV and rapid diagnostic kits for Cutaneous and Visceral Lieshmaniasis.b. Procurement of CBC machines.c. Procurement of Mobile X-Ray Units for screening (to detect TB cases through screening campaigns). d. Procurement of Pima™ Analyser (CD4 T-cell analysis).2.
