Project Summary

Cameroon is the second most affected country in the West and Central African Region by HIV/AIDS. Since the malnutrition is a major reason for discontinuing Anti-Retroviral Treatment (ART), nutrition and food support should be integrated into HIV programmes. Research shows that severely malnourished individuals are six times more likely to die in the first three months of infection than those with normal nutrition status. Malnourished people living with HIV and on ART need, accordingly, food supplements through food by prescription(FbP) to improve their nutrition status and adherence to treatment under the WFP/UNAIDS joint programme. WFP will provide food assistance to total 2,000 malnourished people living with HIV under ART treatment with nutrition support and counselling under the Food by Prescription (FbP) programme in the East and Adamaoua regions for 12 months in 2019. A nutritious ration composed of 25g vegetable oil and 250g of super cereal per person/day will be provided to these ART clients to maintain their food security and nutrition status.

Needs

The HIV/AIDS epidemic in Cameroon is assessed with an HIV prevalence of 4.5% among general population (15-49 years), women more affected (5.6%) than men (2.6%). HIV prevalence is considerably higher among population groups such as female sex workers (36.9%) and men who have sex with men (24% to 44.5%). Approximately 620,000 people are living with HIV (PLHIV) in Cameroon, including 341,000 women, 240,000 men and 39,000 children aged 0 to 14 (UNAIDS, Cameroon Annual report 2015). The vulnerability assessment of food insecurity in 2011 among PLHIV under antiretroviral therapy(ART) in Cameroon cited the prevalence of acute malnutrition among ART clients at 14.1% and the prevalence of food insecurity among ART households at 47.4% The vulnerability assessment conducted in 2017 on ART clients discharged from Food By Prescription (FBP) in the East and Adamawa regions revealed that 25 percent of household with ART clients were food insecure and in some areas such as Kadey division, more than 55 percent of ART client household were food insecure (WFP, Vulnerability of People Living with HIV Discharged from Malnutrition Treatment Programme, 2017). For these malnourished ART patients, the nutrition support is critical for the treatment.
