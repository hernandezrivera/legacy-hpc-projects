Project Summary

The security situation resulting from the insurgency and recent attacks in the states have led to an increase in new conflict related displacement including the influx of Internally Displaced Persons (IDP), refugees and returnees with thousands of new arrivals recorded in recent months into camps and settlements in several Local Government Areas (LGA) in Borno State (e.g. Pulka, Gowza, Monguno, Bama, Dikwa, Kukawa, Ngala). Most of the displaced population are in host communities, with about 20% in IDP camps.
This situation predisposes the affected population to increased risks of epidemic-prone diseases, like cholera, measles and viral hemorrhagic fevers. Children under 5, women of reproductive age (15-49 years) and the elderly (over 60 years) are the most vulnerable and left increasingly susceptible to such outbreaks. In order to address the increased risk of outbreaks of epidemic prone diseases within the communities affected by low/no access to health services, WHO will support the state governments to enhance epidemic preparedness and capacity building to effectively respond to emergencies in the targeted areas through:
• The deployment of mobile technologies at wards and LGAs level to improve disease surveillance and reporting by the deployment of mobile technology. Based on the lessons learned in previous initiatives and leveraging the deployment of health service delivery platforms in the affected communities, WHO will increase capacity of the MHT and CORPs to quickly notify potential outbreaks and alerts through the procurement and distribution of mobile phone equipped with specific alert notification application.
• Strengthening and expansion of the surveillance activities in new and partially accessible areas, through engagement of community volunteers to immediately notify alerts of epidemic prone diseases to enhance response to outbreaks in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states using the network of community volunteers to strengthen community based surveillance reporting system and encourage ownership of the program at community level to link all informants to health facilities and strengthen the existing system of zero reporting to further improve the ability of all levels of the health system to detect and respond to diseases and hazards that cause high levels of death, illness and disability at community level. The system will allow collecting weekly or monthly epidemiological data, validation and rejection of alerts, as well as improve timeliness and completeness of health facilities reports. This will develop a platform for efficient use of surveillance information for decision making and increase the health sector partner’s participation in disease surveillance. The community volunteers will also serve as first line responders during outbreaks. The activity will entail trainings of the community volunteers to ensure triggered epidemiological alerts were investigated and effective public health interventions are instituted.
• Capacity building of government and health sector partners on operational readiness to respond to the potential alerts and outbreaks of epidemic prone diseases through;
o Activation and training of Epidemic Preparedness Response (EPR) committee and State/LGAs Rapid Response Teams, which composed of 8 members per team to ensure the timely verification of all outbreak alerts and rumors, including appropriate investigation and risk assessment where needed
o Engagement and training of active case search teams during outbreak response.
o The procurement, deployment and prepositioning of medicines and supplies for the effective prevention and management of outbreaks of epidemic prone diseases such as cholera, meningitis, viral hemorrhagic fevers, etc.
o The organization of social mobilization and health promotion in the targeted areas to sensitize the communities on the existing health risks, the adequate precautions to take, mitigating risks of outbreaks of epidemic prone diseases.

1 - Needs

Data generated from the WHO Early Warning and Response System in November 2019 indicate that malaria compounded by malnutrition is the leading cause of morbidity and mortality for 45% of cases and 33% of reported deaths. This vulnerability to malaria is increased considering that 45% of the health facilities that would have provided malaria prevention or treatment are either completely or partially destroyed. In 2018, Borno (5,953), Adamawa (2,498) and Yobe (2,108) experienced outbreaks of cholera and in 2019 only Adamawa (805) is still experiencing Cholera as of 25th October. More generally, the combination of lack of adequate health care, overcrowding due to new conflict related displacement, including the influx of IDPs, refugees and returnees raise the risks of outbreaks of epidemic prone diseases such as cholera, hepatitis, meningitis and measles. WHO will support the state governments to enhance epidemic preparedness and capacity building to effectively respond to emergencies in the targeted areas
