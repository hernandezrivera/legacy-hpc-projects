Project Summary

UNHCR as protection lead agency will maintain both static and mobile field presence response in the greatest areas of concern in 2019 being Central and Southern Unity, Central and Eastern Jonglei, parts of Upper Nile, Western Bar-al-Ghazel (in particular Wau county) and in Yei as well as UNHCR priority locations in EES, WES, Northern Bar-al-Ghazel, Lakes and Warrap states. The project will cover IDPs, returnees and host communities. Interventions will be based on rapid needs assessments to prioritise interventions. Throughout the program consultations with affected populations will be carried out taking into account age, gender and diversity approach. In 2018, UNHCR focused on prioritised activities in general protection, child protection and GBV. Field missions were undertaken to hard-to-reach areas for life-saving activities and protection assessments. Protection Monitoring was strengthened as were activities aimed at promoting solutions including surveys, FGDs and population profiling. In 2019 UNHCR and partners will continue to build on the protection gains achieved to further strengthen a rights-based protection response for the populations, in particular those most vulnerable through: timely life-saving activities in remote or hard-to-reach areas, with a focus in expanding outreach to 190 identified priority locations both in POCs and outside POCs; developing the capacity of affected communities to cope, recover and contribute to durable solutions paying attention to specific needs of the youth; and mainstreaming protection across other clusters. To this effect, UNHCR will maintain its mobile outreach team to ensure a strong presence in integrated missions, rapid assessments and RRM intervention. There will also be need for continued coordination of activities at national and state levels to implement protection and assistance activities.
The recent revitalised peace process has created optimism for new opportunities for the protection and durable solutions for persons of concern in 2019. These developments, combined with noticeable slow reduction in related conflict as well as spontaneous returns of IDPs and refugees from neighbouring countries highlights the need to focus on solution-oriented programming in 2019. In order to improve quality of responses and support implementation of durable solutions objective of the Protection Cluster and HCT, UNHCR will engage in protection profiling through its protection teams and Joint IDP profiling Service (JIPS) for a collaborative profiling exercise.
The changing context in view of the peace agreement will require increased capacity for UNHCR and its partners to carry out protection activities if access for humanitarians improves. Protection activities in support for durable solutions such as HLP, community empowerment, income generating activities for protective outcomes, quick impact projects, peacebuilding and peaceful co-existence initiatives and youth related activities will be enhanced. Multi-purpose cash-based initiatives to support durable solutions will be implemented based on needs assessment and protection risk analysis. To maximise impact UNHCR will seek to explore possibilities to target the required assistance to the most vulnerable categories of the affected populations and to join efforts with other clusters ensuring centrality of protection in humanitarian intervention and seek linkages with development actors for complementarity with other frameworks to promote resilience and solutions. UNHCR will support accession to Kampala Convention and its operationalisation supporting the government in finalising of the national legislation on internal displacement and related legal reforms such as in HLP and land resource issues supporting access to resources by women and vulnerable populations and to promote national responsibility to prevent, protect and resolve internal displacement

Needs

By the end of 2018, an estimated 1.9 million persons are internally displaced in South Sudan. The population inside UNMISS POC sites remained at about 220,000 across the country with limited freedom of movement and fear/risk of being attacked by armed elements within and outside the PoCs, forcefully recruited or abducted or being subjected to sexual violence with limited psychosocial support. New displacement were reported with the Equatorias, Western Bahr el Ghazal, Jonglei, Upper Nile and Unity State maintaining the greatest levels of displacements. Throughout 2018 clashes in multiple locations, including Jonglei, Central and Sothern Unity, Western Bahr-el-Ghazal, Central Equatoria, Western Euquatoria and Greater Upper Nile region have caused additional population displacement, exacerbating intra-communal tensions, cattle raiding, overall criminality and other negative coping mechanisms, expanding the scale of needs. The priority for UNHCR intervention in 2019 will be addressing the needs of the vulnerable populations. Protections needs analysis indicated serious protection risks including threat to physical safety, serious human rights abuses such as sexual violence, killing of civilians, rape and gang rape and incidents of abduction of women and girls particularly in Southern Unity, perpetrated by different groups. Around 61% of IDPs are children while women and girls make the most vulnerable among the displaced. The prolonged conflict coupled with new displacements and lack of access resulted to severe food insecurity, according to IPC analysis released in September 2018. The number of IDPs living in hard-to-reach areas remained high, requiring mobile response, integrated missions and rapid assessments as priority. In 2019, synergies will be maintained both within UNHCR led clusters of Protection, CCCM and NFI/Shelter and with all other clusters such as FSL and WASH and with development actors to ensure an integrated and solutions-focused response to the needs of the populations.Despite the foregoing, there were spontaneous returns in 2018 indicating possibilities of IDP returns to some secure locations in 2019. The Solutions Working Group in Bor facilitated voluntary returns of 221 IDPs from Bor POC to Akobo. It is expected that with the revitalised peace agreement, an increasing number of IDPs, including those of POCs, may opt to voluntarily return to places of origin, relocate elsewhere in the country or seek to integrate in places of displacement. It was noted that majority of returnees to do return to their places of origin, while those returning to their places of origin do not have access to their properties. Temporary and spontaneous returns of refugees from neighbouring countries will expand the scale of the needs In 2019 UNHCR and its partner will enhance population profiling to identity solutions needs of the populations to support them to exercise their options. This will include support on HLP issues, family re-unification, identity and civil documents in particular for women and PSNs for access to services. Return packages both in cash and in kind will returning families to settle in their new communities. Full prioritization is required to implement strategies to decongest POCs in Wau, Bentiu, Malakal and Bor and comprehensive solutions pursued for IDPs in the two POCs and urban settlements in Juba in coordination with relevant actors. Operationalisation of area-based strategies will by key to implementing the solutions approach. Further operationalisation of the 2009 Kampala Convention and development of a national legislation on IDPs is key to breaching the current gap in law and policy. Major efforts will be required to ensure protection monitoring for IDPs in remote locations. To this effect, the engagement for profiling of IDP and host communities through JIPs in early 2019 is a priority to inform needs and enable targeted assistance to most vulnerable populations.

Consultations with affected people

Yes

Provide breakdown by women, men, boys and girls

IDPs were consulted through interagency participatory assessment (PA) exercises conducted by UNHCR, partners countrywide. The PA process aimed to gather information on specific protection risks faced by IDPs, analyse risks jointly with them, understand their capacities, and to hear their proposed solutions. The principles of age, gender and diversity, a rights-based, and community-based approach guided the process. PA ensured that IDPs are put at the centre of the decision-making concerning their protection, ensured accountability to affected populations. Qualitative data collection was done through FGDs and KIIs and feedback sessions were held with communities to present findings and recommendations. Key findings of PA for POCs population: (i) intentions to return, but inability to do so due to insecurity compounded by the lack of access to information on security situation in the areas of potential return and services; (ii) lack of freedom of movement; (iii) presence of armed elements that compromises civilian character of POCs. Key findings for out of POCs population included: (i) armed conflict and economic meltdown that affect security situation of IDPs; (ii) lack of information on security situation and services in potential areas of return; (iii) lack of access to basic services that contributes to displacement and prevents durable solutions; (iv) impunity, lack of rule of law and access to justice; (v) poor representation of women in community structures. Key recommendation made by consulted IDPs included: (i) international support to peace process in South Sudan; (ii) support voluntary return of IDPs to the places of origin; (iii) improve access to services in the areas of return; (iv) ensure meaningful participation of women in community structures. Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

The information will be provided to beneficiaries through the direct engagement with the community using age, gender and diversity mainstreaming approach. Through its protection desks, open all week as well as community workers; UNHCR ensures access by IDPs to information of different nature, either access to services, assistance as well as situational awareness. UNHCR also regularly promotes participatory assessments, regular consultations with communities, either through surveys, regular FGDs as information sessions on specific topics or identified gaps or KIIs in order to assist shaping our information sessions. Protection monitoring tools also include questions on access to information as well as preferred means of dissemination in order to inform our materials, conselling and information sessions.
