Project Summary

The proposed intervention is in line with Strategic Objective 3: the capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued. It also meets WASH cluster objective 3: to strengthen WASH preparedness and response capacity to the most vulnerable Palestinians to reduce threats and cope with emergencies and shocks.
The intervention, which has been coordinated with relevant stakeholders including the PWA and the Gaza Municipality, will help people of Gaza strengthen their WASH preparedness and response capacity to cope with emergencies and shocks through improving their access to flooding mitigation and prevention measures.
Through this proposed intervention, the Qatar Red Crescent Society is planning to construct a storm water/harvesting system through establishing a 1 kilometer long carrying line as the main line with all fittings including manholes in Al-Zaytoon neighborhood/ al hadaba area (Amalaha Bedouin village). A storm water collection network at the targeted area suffering from recurrent flooding will be established and will include all fittings (e.g., manholes, gullies, …etc.).
Rain water collected through the system will be dumped in an existing filtration basin to the north of street 10 which was constructed on a land of an area of 10 donums, injection boreholes were drilled reaching the water table and a sand filter layer was laid more than 10-12 years s ago. To ensure the effectiveness of the infiltration system, the basin will be scrubbed and a layer 70cm – 100 cm in depth will be removed/managed in order to increase the volume capacity of the basin as well as clean it and optimize its filtration capacity.
The project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation take into consideration the particular needs of all groups of the population particularly women, children and people with disabilities who are the most affected during floods. Benefits of the flood system that will be implemented will be maximized for these vulnerable groups.
The implementation of the proposed intervention will be supported by a comprehensive monitoring and evaluation system enabling QRCS to collect all needed data to steer the action, minimize risks, maximize the impact, measure efficiency and effectiveness as well as support project team in corrective measures when needed. 1 - Needs

For more than 15 years now, Gaza has been suffering from the direct effects of the blockade which has been described by the UN as an "illegal blockade" and the internal Palestinian division. This combination of suffering has left Gaza in a suffocating economic situation affecting several public services, including health, water, and sanitation. The unemployment rate has been alarmingly rising. The consequences of this have affected the lives of the vulnerable groups devastatingly, mainly in the last years after the emergence of COVID-19 in the community. Furthermore, and according to Gaza WASH sector damage assessment report (2021), more than 290 WASH infrastructures in the Gaza Strip were affected during the May 2021 escalation of the Gaza Strip. This for sure weakened the service providers who were on the verge of collapse before the escalation. The political situation after the May escalation has been fluctuating, the Israeli import restrictions on equipment and materials designated as ‘dual-use items’, the poor governance of the WASH sector due to the limited technical and financial capacities of the WASH service providers and also delaying reconstruction projects in light of the fact that winter is approaching will expose the Gaza Strip to great unforeseen risks. According to the Palestinian Water Authority (PWA), more than 97% of the coastal aquifer water is unfit for human consumption due to unsafe pumping that also cause the aquifer level to considerably decrease.According to 2022 WASH HNO Narrative Draft, approximately over 1.35 million Palestinians across the oPt are affected by public health risks associated with insufficient access to WASH services and will be in immediate need of humanitarian assistance in 2022. The WASH Cluster partners’ assessments and consultations identified risk of flooding as one of the four main key issues generating people’s need for WASH assistance as it is found according to WASH vulnerability Mapping 2021 that almost 94,000 Palestinians reside in low-lying areas at risk of flooding events during winter due to heavy rains and overflow of storm water facilities and sewage pumping stations. The report stated that recently and due to climate change, flooding became more frequent and have caused significant losses of assets, displacement, disrupted access to livelihoods and services, and exacerbated the risk of waterborne diseases. Al Zaytoun community, an overcrowded area with a population exceeding 87,000 citizens, where more than 400 households are under risk of flooding and assets loss is considered one of the most zones in need for urgent intervention. According to Gaza Municipality, this is an area that is under development and the number of households is expected to rise.Usually, women and girls are the biggest victims, they face discrimination and social stigma, since by the nature of society they are required to bear the brunt of hardship in flooded homes, where they are expected to continue to perform their domestic duties under these adverse conditions as well as clean the aftermath of the floods. In addition, people with disability (who are about 2.3% of the population) suffer the most in terms of mobility and accessibility and this suffering is increased during flooding. To ensure community engagement during the preliminary design phase of the intervention, on October 13, 2021, Municipality of Gaza conducted a meeting with representatives of al Hadaba area (Amalaha Bedouin village), discussed the current situations, solutions and the proposed interventions and positive feedbacks and also appreciations were found. This will ensure that proposed interventions are meeting the needs of the targeted beneficiaries and that they will support the success of the project.QRC at the early beginnings of the project will consult and communicate with varied groups of people in terms of Age, Gender and Diversity (AGD), to obtain greater and deep understanding on their needs, how the outlined issues affect them and their life, and the different ways they would like to be supported to prevent and mitigate those risks.

2 - Activities

Through this intervention, the following activities will be implemented:1) Constructing storm water/harvesting system through establishing:1.1) 1 kilometer carrying line as the main line with all fittings including manholes in Zaytoun neighborhood/ al hadaba area (Amalaha Bedouin village) which will deliver collected water to an existing infiltration basin. 1.2) A storm water collection network at the targeted area suffering from recurrent flooding is to be established and will include all fittings (e.g., manholes, gullies, …etc.). 2) To ensure the effectiveness of the infiltration system, the basin will be scrubbed and a layer 70cm – 100 cm in depth will be removed/managed in order to increase the volume capacity of the basin as well as clean it and optimize its filtration capacity. Rain water collected through the system will be dumped in the existing filtration basin to the north of street 10. The existing filtration basin was constructed on a land of an area of 10 donums, injection boreholes were drilled reaching the water table and a sand filter layer was laid since more than 10-12 years. Water collected and filtered through the basin and passed to the aquifer will routinely be monitored and tested through sampling water specimens from the existing recovery well. Data and tests results can also be used for Environmental Impact Assessments, quality control and research purposes. Preliminary designs for the proposed interventions are available and they will be reviewed by the Qatar Red Crescent technical staff and tender documents are to be developed by them. Activities of the project have been coordinated with the local authorities including PWA and are in line with national needs and Gaza Municipality developmental plans for the targeted area. QRC will consult and communicate with varied groups of people in terms of Age, Gender and Diversity (AGD), to obtain greater and deep understanding on how the outlined issues affect them and their life, and the different ways they would like to be supported to prevent and mitigate those risks. As such, planned activities will be tailored, to the extent that it is feasible to their points of view and suggestions. Ensuring this level of diverse community engagement is maintained, QRC will focus on ensuring adequate protection, age, gender, diversity and PSEA mainstreaming by mapping risks and opportunities for different groups of people, mitigating and/or strengthening them appropriately. To ensure community engagement during implementation phase of the proposed intervention, meetings are planned to take place with representatives of al Hadaba area (Amalaha Bedouin village) in the presence of the Municipality of Gaza and all related authorities (i.e., PWA and CMWU) in order to keep beneficiaries updated and to gather their feedbacks and recommendations that will be used for enhancing the intervention and thus its impacts. It is worth mentioning that the municipality of Gaza is ready to start pavement works at the targeted area pending completion of infrastructure works, which will be concluded once the storm system has been established. This ensures that the intervention will contribute to the development of the targeted area.

(disaggregation of reached people in terms of age, gender and disability will be available) - # of people protected from seasonal flooding (Activity outcome indicator)The number of people who will be protected from seasonal flooding as a result of the proposed intervention is estimated to exceed 2200 and the number will increase in the near future, as the area is developing rapidly. (disaggregation of reached people in terms of age, gender and disability will be available) - # storm water systems installed/ rehabilitated (Activity output indicator)The project includes the establishment of a complete storm water collection system, including a 1 km carrying main line, and a collection network with all needed fittings and components (e.g. manholes, gullies …) All relevant gathered data are to be used to steer the actions, maximize its impact, minimize risks, measure the effects and anticipate and mitigate any challenges. Data is planned to be gathered during the project's period continuously through meeting/filling questionnaires by a random representative samples of different gender and age groups. The results and findings will be reflected in progress and final project reports. Action plan is to be developed at the early beginnings of the project and will continuously be tracked to ensure that progress is on track with no deviations nor delays. Financial cash flow is to be initiated also to ensure well financial management of the project and smooth flow of payments to contractors/suppliers. Deviations will be reported to relevant staff /management and corrective measures will be taken. Lessons learned are to be recorded, studied and used for further enhancement in any similar interventions in the future. Success stories are to be reflected in the project’s reports. All indicators will be disaggregated by age, gender and disability.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The storm water system is designed to run on the outskirts of the condensed locality where it won’t interfere with people’s homes and therefore it will not be causing any inconvenience. The project will be in full coordination with all stakeholders and local partners to guarantee maximum benefit. Coordination will start at the community level in order to avoid any resistance from the population to the proposed activities. Meetings with community leaders, led by local stakeholders including the Gaza Municipality will ensure that the community is supportive of the intervention. These meetings have already started to discuss possible intervention in the near future and to gather people’s feedback. QRC’s Do No Harm policies are clear throughout all of its program designs based on its principles of Accountability to Affected Populations document. For this project, QRC will ensure that beneficiaries, staff, and partners are protected from harm; the safety and dignity of beneficiaries is ensured throughout the project from the design to monitoring phase through regular field visits and assessment to ensure that their dignity is preserved at all stages, this includes ensuring adequate representation of vulnerable groups in the project design and monitoring. QRC ensures that all its activities are guided by protection mainstreaming to protect the safety, dignity, and rights of the communities it serves, and monitor access and accountability from the project planning phase. The intervention comes at a time all communities are suffering because of the spread of COVID-19, an infectious disease that spreads at a higher rate when the infection risk is increase, similar to what happens during floods. The intervention proposed here will in fact help minimize the risk of floods, thus reducing the risk of transmitting diseases and in turn contributing to lower harm of the population.
