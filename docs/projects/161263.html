Project Summary

In this project, GREDO will provide critical child protection services and assistance to separated, unaccompanied and children associated with armed groups in Renk, Pibor and Maiwut Counties. The intervention is participatory, integrated and right based and will be implemented for a period of twelve months. To ensure critical provision of child protection services, GREDO will develop existing child protection referral pathway ensuring safe and secure identification, documentation, referral to service providers and case follow up of all cases of child protection. GREDO will train 24 Case Workers on minimum child protection standards and support them in managing CP cases. Two Child and Youth Friendly Spaces will be established in each location. Structured activities targeting at risk children will be conducted within the child friendly spaces and will include provision of psycho-social support, registration of unaccompanied and separated children followed by family tracing and reunification as well as a youth resilience program. Young girls who have reached reproductive age will be provided with Menstrual Hygiene Management training and hygiene kits. Caregivers of Unaccompanied and separated children and former CAAFAGS will be supported through restricted Cash Based Intervention Cash Voucher system for them to be able to obtain essential supplies and basic needs such as food. To mitigate Child Protection risks, GREDO will establish one Children Based Protection Mechanisms in each county and train them on Child Protection in Emergency, protection awareness and messaging, preparedness and Child Protection Risk Protection mitigation. To integrate the response, GREDO will work with Nutrition partners during Nutrition outreach activities and in stabilization centers on Child Protection Messaging targeting women. Children with protection concerns and malnourished will be referred to Nutrition Service providers for supplementation feeding and treatment. Education partners will be engaged in enrolment of at risk and out of school boys and girls. Injured children or those suffering from extreme trauma will be referred to Health Providers for specialized care. To ensure accountability to affected persons, a Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) will be activated on contact with target communities. Community communication and sensitization will provide sufficient information to affected persons on how to submit complaints and receive feedback. In this intervention, GREDO will reach 350 UASC who will be identified, documented, and reunited with their families. On the other hand, 100 UASC (50 boys and 50 girls ) will be supported through Cash Based individual protection assistance as part of case management and referrals. Similarly, 500 cases comprising of 245 girls and 255 boys will be supported through case management and referrals services and 12000 individuals reached through specialized PSS and related services such as recreational activities, livelihood and parenting skills. Finally, 63 persons (52 Females/11 Males) will be trained on CPiE while another 14618 persons will be reached through child protection awareness activities.

1 - Needs

South Sudan has remained a very dangerous place for Children. A report recently released by United Nations has documented 109 abductions, 453 recruitments, 72 cases of sexual violence, and 125 incidences of killing. In rural and urban parts of the country, protection concerns including child protection continue to ravage several states in the country. For example, according to Humanitarian overview report for 2019, individuals in great need of protection services is ranked as follows in target counties; Renk 43600; Pibor 30,000 individuals and Maiwut 63700 individuals. Of those in of protection services are separated, unaccompanied, orphaned, and children associated with armed groups. According to Save the Children, 8765 unaccompanied, separated and missing children were documented in South Sudan as at 2016. Of this number, Upper Nile recorded 966 cases UASCs while Jonglei State had 1042 cases. Following the fighting that erupted in 2016 and continued until late 2018, the number are expected to be more , with Global Initiative Report (2019) estimating the number of UASCs at 16,715. UNICEF on the other hand has found that 50% of girls in South Sudan are married off before they attain the age of 18 years. This is collaborated by IRNA Assessment findings for Maiwut (Maiwut IRNA, April 2019) which has noted that 14 out of 47 schools in the county remained closed as at April 2019 forcing an approximate 12500 boys and girls out of school. These worrying numbers are reinforced by International Rescue Committee findings that have noted that vulnerability of children as a result of war has extended beyond missing on schooling due to war to include rape, sexual violence and torture as acts of war which has affected 65 % of women and girls . Apart from exposure to Gender Based Violence, the Oxfam/EU report found that adolescent girls are facing extra challenges of early and forced marriages with families in rural areas that have exhausted their household assets marrying off girls in exchange for cows to replenish family wealth/assets. The report noted that 82% of girls in camp and non-camp setting were likely to miss on schooling as a result of early or forced marriage and by extension getting sucked into vicious cycle of poverty. The United Nation’s Children Fund (UNICEF) reported that between December 2013 and December 2016, one thousand one hundred and thirty (1,130) children were sexually assaulted. For boys, recruitment into local gangs and fighting forces has remained a key threat to their lives and constitute a grave violation of their rights. Girl face the risk of early or forced marriage. Other factors driving protection concerns among children include; Lack of alternative pathways for adolescent girls due to limited education particularly for girls who have dropped out of school, education related expenses making it difficult for IDPs and returnees to retain children in school, patriarchal gender norms and cultural practices that restrict girls options, the practice of bride price, restriction to girls mobility and participation in activities outside the home, weak community protection structures and poverty and limited options for conflict affected families leading to forced marriage in exchange for cattle to replenish family animal assets. Due to fighting, protection partners were affected by conflict, leading to relocations on multiple occasions leaving areas such as Renk and Maiwut with few FTR partners from December 2013 to September 2019. Many of the existing FTR partners do not have extensive experience in FTR, and coupled with high staff turnover, technical support is required to ensure that minimum standards are understood and inform programme design.

2 - Activities

ACTIVITY 1.1: Community Consultation and Sensitization: GREDO CP staffs will conduct sensitization sessions with Community Leaders, Community Members, Chiefs, Local Authorities, Parent Teachers Associations, Teachers and Religious leaders to provide information on the project, receive inputs from the affected community and seek their support. Information provided to affected communities to include; Project aim, project objectives, expected results, project activities, project lifespan, beneficiaries’ selection criteria, modality of intervention, role of the community in project implementation and role of project staffs. Also, information on Complaint and Feedback Mechanism will be provided with reporting and feedback mechanism being articulated. ACTIVITY 1.2: Safety Audits: GREDO Protection staffs will work closely with chiefs, community leaders, teachers, local authorities and parents to map child protection risks prior to commencing project activities. Report generated from the audit will be utilized by project staffs while deciding locations for Child Friendly Spaces, Sites for recreational sports and to create awareness among staffs and community members of risk mitigation measures. ACTIVITY 1.3: Establishment of Child Friendly Spaces (CFS): GREDO will establish six Child Friendly Spaces using locally available materials. CFS Managers will oversee activities within the safe spaces ensuring safety of children and supervision of PSS and structured activities. ACTIVITY 1.4: Comprehensive case management and referrals including CBI: The exercise will involve identification, documentation, family tracing and reunification of unaccompanied and separated children. UASC headed households, Caregivers of UASC will be supported with Cash Based Individual Protection assistance as part of case management and referrals. GREDO will facilitate training and support of 24 Case Workers conducting case management. ACTIVITY 1.5: ACTIVITY 1.6: Psychosocial Support Services: GREDO will provide psychosocial support services to UASC boys and girls and caregivers of UASC. Specialized PSS will be conducted by trained Case Workers and will be supplemented with Life Skills training, recreation sports, and training on beadwork, leatherwork, painting, and other livelihood skills. ACTIVITY1.7: Child Protection awareness and prevention messaging: GREDO will conduct awareness and prevention messaging through community mobilizers, case workers and Community Based Protection Networks targeting women, men, boys, girls, teachers, chiefs, community leaders, youth leaders, women leaders and religious leaders on the rights of children. ACTIVITY 1.8: CBP preparedness and protection risk mitigation: GREDO will facilitate in the formation of formation, training and support of three Community Based Protection Mechanisms (one in each County). CBPMs will be trained on Child Protection in Emergency, child protection risk mitigation, preparedness and early warning. CBPMs will remain active in their respective communities advocating for the rights of children, mapping, monitoring and reporting protection risks likely to impact on children. ACTIVITY 1.9: Distribution of Menstrual Hygiene Management: GREDO will conduct Menstrual Hygiene Management training targeting reproductive age girls. Girls will be provided with menstrual hygiene kits in order for them to manage menses.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes. The project is participatory project that need full involvement of right holders and work with duty bearers to achieve intended objective. Through the right based approach the accountability side way, down ward and upward will be fulfilled.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes. The displacement-affected communities will be the main beneﬁciaries of the project. Indeed, this project will provide support to the return of households and IDPs of the area. In addition, GREDO will focus on the host communities who have been impacted by the crisis and saw their livelihood reducing.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes. The proposed intervention will be in line with previous child protection projects implemented for several years by GREDO in South Sudan. More particularly, this project will involve existing community groups and networks, new or already trained and engage them in child protection work in their locality, to ensure their sustainability and the project’s durability. At the ﬁeld level GREDO will coordinate with the State Focal Point and other actors in presence such as IOM to avoid any duplication and overlapping of the distribution. The Mine Actions Organizations can be consulted to ensure the security of GREDO teams and HHs. Chiefs, local authorities and women and youth associations will be engaged in creating awareness and advocating for the rights of children.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes. This project will ensure the protection of the returnees, IDPs while helping the host communities to absorb this extra pressure on the limited resources. In addition, through this project, GREDO will contribute to the protection of vulnerable girls and boys though provision of specialized and integrated protection services. Furthermore, GREDO will ensure that communities are consulted throughout the project period through systematic consultations and set up of complaints and response mechanisms to ensure quality and integration of beneﬁciary views in the project allowing achievement of goals in line with Protection and Cluster HRP objectives.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The context in Renk, Maiwut and Pibor has been more and more stable for a few months, allowing the beginning of the return movements and improving its access while reducing the protection risks. Furthermore, GREDO’s solid experience of this context will be a strong asset to monitor the situation. This project is protection intervention , through process of implementation it will ensure safe environment that connect to long lasting /durable solution that will link with local systems such as customary institution , chiefs, traditional leaders, civil society , line departments and local administrative body.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes. As the project will be implemented in coordination with traditional institution, relevant government line departments, local administration, community members and leaders, the sustainability starts from beginning of the project. At the end the activities will be handed over to Community Based Child Protection Mechanisms. It will develop phase of guidelines/ strategy during implementation. Furthermore, a dynamic of empowerment and resilience will be given with the training of Community Members on Child Protection. The collaboration with local protection actors will allow the protraction of this speciﬁc assistance and support after ending this project. Furthermore, this project will allow Child Protection Intervention will advocate and encourage other sector actors to implement activities (WaSH, FSL, livelihood, health, education, etc.) in the same locations in order to build the resilience of the targeted population, to support them in setting up positive coping mechanisms.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

GREDO will strive to gain acceptance from local authorities and stakeholders; follow strict logistics and ﬁnancial procedures; streamline security risk assessments at all stages of program design; gather up-to-date security alerts from various formal and informal sources and adjust our actions. Community awareness of the principles and arrangement of the program, with Community Feedback and Complaint Mechanism. (CFCM) are in place. GREDO staff are equipped with the capacities to negotiate access with the authorities. Access negotiation is based on a strong commitment on the respect of humanitarian principles. Speciﬁc trainings of the key people involved in access negotiation are conducted. In addition to the risks detailed above, GREDO will mitigate the speciﬁc risks such as risks linked to the important variations of the population size in the areas of intervention. Mitigation measures will include advocating with other partners to complement GREDO interventions when necessary. Another risk to take into account is the exclusion of women in project activities. GREDO will ensure increased participation of women in project activities from the start to the end. Community members will be engaged in project planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

This can ensure the continuity of the project outcome. Accountability will be taken into consideration during the proposed action to ensure that the points of view of the affected population and returnees are considered. They will be involved all along the project, from the assessments, to the monitoring of activities, including beneﬁciaries’ selection and composition of kits and implementation of activities. GREDO staffs will be well trained to ensure the needs of the children are assessed. Before the intervention, GREDO will organize a minimum of 3 focus group discussions per location with girls, boys, women, men, elderlies and people with disabilities for the community consultations to ensure community inputs. This feedback will be used to adapt the activities, improve the mobilization and participation of the population, monitor and improve the quality of the work. This approach guarantees safety, dignity and protection of affected people. For each location, GREDO will also create an implementation committee and a complaint committee with members of the community. They will be responsible for the link between affected households and GREDO. They will be able to spread messages, explain the selection criteria, report needs from the community, etc. GREDO will set up a speciﬁc complaint/feedback mechanism adapted to the local context to collect the complaints and feedbacks from the beneﬁciaries and take appropriate action. Considering the context of RENK and Maiwut Counties, GREDO will organize regular feedback desk within each location. In this way, GREDO will be present to listen and register the complaints, to collect information and to answer the questions. GREDO already large experience of accountability to the communities and complaints responses mechanisms, the AAP for this project will be also aligned with the standardized community-led complaints and feedback.
