Project Summary

Caritas Bangladesh (CB) has been operation education program in Rohingya camps both for children, adolescents and youth. A total of 223 children of 3-14 years age group including girls, boys, Children with Disabilities have opprtunities of education in one Learning Centre and 8 Home-based Learning centres at camp 1E and camp 3, where they have a safe and secure space to enjoy, and opportunities to play, and socialized. The program is being implementing under the guidance of Education Sector, based on LCFA, GIEP and will continue in 2022.
Education for youth and adolescent is very crucial in camps and aiming to increase the community’s access to information and learning opportunities for them CB has been started activities through set up community library space with the technical support from Bibliothèques Sans Frontières (BSF)/Library Without Boarder. BSF and CB propose to equip a Community Center with an Ideas Box and reinforce 2 outreach teams with Ideas Cube kits and will be implemented in camp 4 extension. It is a multi-media hub that provides learning and cultural resources to vulnerable groups to meet and engage in activities together in a safe space. Through its modularity and the variety of contents and tools it offers, Ideas box increases the scope of existing activities and fosters local initiatives. The teams equipped with Ideas cube and will conduct centre session and outreach sessions to improve access to information and learning for extremely vulnerable or isolated people, particularly for adolescent girls, adult women, persone with disabilities and gender divers population, who are restricted in their movement due to social or other barriers. There will be specific sessions especially geared towards adolescents, young adults and women of 14-25 years of age. But the library will also have dedicated times for free use and provide open access to its content for all community members (men, women, boys and girls with diverse identities).
Caritas employs a robust protection and gender mainstreaming strategy, and apply age/gender/diversity lenses to all aspects of implementation at all phases of the project cycle, including paying special attention to particularly vulnerable groups in this context. It utilizes the Protection Mainstreaming Framework, and committed to follow the guidance and practical tip-sheets developed by the Protection Sector by incorporating the principles of Safety and Dignity, Do No Harm, Meaningful Access, Accountability, Participation and Empowerment throughout the response.
CB’s national policy for PSEA, is adapted and complaints will be collected through the several entry points and are directly processed by the dedicated PSEA focal point. Trainings on PSEA, Safeguarding and how to report is organize for volunteers and staff.
Better responding to improve the environment and activities related to DRR including monsoon preparedness CB has been working with sector and the PERU team. Capacity building of volunteers is a regular activities of CB aligned with the Protection Sector.
CB closely works with Government like- NGOAB, Deputy Commissioner, CiC and sectors for approvals and coordination. Regularly submits 5Ws and Sitrep to sectors and as active member it participats in Education Sector Coordination meeting at Cox’s Bazar level and camp level CiC meeting and education sector meetings. CB Intends to participate and work with Youth WG.
For AAP Effective complain and feedback mechanism is in place through setting Opinion & Complaint box in all offices, camp facilities, distribution points and majhi blocks, dedicated Hotline and e-mail account. CB conducts PDM and beneficiaries feedback survey regularly. These feedbacks are processed, and regularly analysed by the Safeguarding and M&E team and shared with higher authority to action & program team to re-design the programming.

1 - Identified Needs

Education, the major interventions for Rohingya children, adolescent and youth for reviving lost dreams with sports, life skills, music, and arts. Right to Education supports development, growth and ability to build their future. Lack of education leads to social problems like domestic violence and child labor, early marriage, drug abuse etc. By achieving education, they can use their talents, and can improve themselves as well as can work with dignity and rights. The Education Sector has identified over 500,000 Rohingya children, adolescents, and youth still in need of education services. Even more alarmingly, 83% of the adolescents and youth aged 15-24 years old do not have access to any educational activities. Access to sustained education services for girls, women, persons with disabilities and gender divers population is further compounded by sociocultural barriers, domestic support roles, movement restrictions, and significant safety and security risks within the camps. Women and girls are the most vulnerable during any crisis. The main reason for this is that women have always been denied equal opportunities for education, and this is the same in Rohingya camps as well. Abuse of power by block leaders and fear of repercussions to be a major obstacle for survivors’ access to specialized services, which has resulted in a culture of impunity that seriously affects women and girls’ health and well-being, security and dignity.” An assessment done by BSF and CB on implementation of youth education projects, have shown both the necessity to engage the community with innovative mediation techniques and the difficulties to program such an approach due to the methodological and cultural inadequacy of traditional approaches and the lack of training of the humanitarian workers. The Ideas box and the Ideas Cube constitute a key innovation for this project. Both tools have been developed to provide educational and cultural resources to vulnerable groups around the globe. The Ideas Box is a mobile “pop up” multimedia center and learning hub that can be adapted to camp context. It is equipped with tablets and laptops, paper and electronic books, a built-in cinema, games and furniture to welcome users. The internal server of the Ideas Box offers thousands of offline contents selected based on the objectives will be used as a tool to facilitate sessions with the beneficiaries, as well as to overcome limitations due to the high illiteracy rate and the language restrictions as well as creating attractive learning environment to the youth and adolescent. COVID 19 affected mostly the limited opportunity of education for Rohingya children, adolescent and youth. As per the J-MSNA 2021- “48% of households with school-aged children(girls/boys) reported at least one school-aged child that will not be sent back to learning facilities once they re-open.” Continuation of education has a key protection benefit - keeping children safe by attending regularly in safe spaces which can reduce the prospect of child labor or exploitative work as well as gender-based violence. The socializing aspect of education provides particular benefits to children and adolescents, where sites of learning can promote positive social networks. Social emotional learning can mitigate risks and enhance efficacy of educational programs, demonstrating significant positive effects on academic outcomes, school retention and attendance rates, lower stress and anxiety, reduce negative student behavior like bullying, violence, SEA and juvenile crime, and improve health outcomes such as reduced drug use. Realizing the situation and necessity CB intends to continue it education program for Rohingya children, adolescent and youth in 2022.

2 - Activities or outputs

1. Repair of Learning Centre and gender segregate WASH facilities: Need based repair of LC and WASH facilities will be done and ensure continuation of separate WASH facilities specially latrine for girls and boys. This gender segregate community latrine must be ensured near by the LCs.2. Life skills sessions for adolescent girls: Separate life skills session will be conducted for adolescent girls. The sessions and discussion might be determined by the context, what services are available, and what they are interested in, health & personal hygiene, participate in decision making, GBV, stress management and coping strategies.3. Distribution of Hygiene Kits: Hygiene and dignity kit will be distributed among the adolescent girls and youth women participated in the interventions.4. Learning and education services to the children: A total number of 223 children (girls, boys, CWD) have the opportunities of education in one learning centres and 8 Home-Based LCs at camp 1E and camp 3. Dedicated facilitators both from Rohingya and HC deployed for children education. Caregivers/volunteers from Rohingya community engaged to support children education.5. Facilitate community engagement and remote learning for Youth and Adolescent: A total of 1500 adolescent girls, boys, youth both female and male have the opportunities of getting information, education, life skills, portable occupational skills. A group of skilled facilitators both from Rohingya and host community deployed for facilitating the intervention.6. Distribution of learning materials: Learners are provided by the learning materials collected from education, unicef and other actors. Supporting aid like- notebook, pen, pencil, eraser, sharpeners distribute among the learners. Required and sufficient toy, poster, supplementary books, paper, colored paper, Board, marker and relevant logistics provides to all LCs. 7. Formation of Learning Centre Management Committee (LCMC) and organize regular/monthly meetings: LCMC forms with the membership of female, male, peoples with disabilities and gender diverse population.8. Organize regular/monthly parents meeting: Joint and separate monthly meeting of female and male parent/guardians organize and conduct. Ensure participation and additional support provides to Peoples with disabilities to join in the parents meeting.9. Meeting with religious, social, elders and other traditional leaders for sensitization to education: Meeting will be organized regularly/monthly with religious & social leaders, elders and other traditional leaders for sensitize them to children, adolescent and youth education.10. Capacity building of LCMC, Parents, community leaders, volunteers: Organize training for LCMC members, Parents, community leaders, volunteers on COVID 19, Gender awareness, protection, inclusive education, child safeguarding, reporting mechanism, gender responsive behavior etc.11. Capacity building of staff: Organize foundation and refreshers training for staff on the session/class facilitation, monitoring and reporting, subject specific foundation and refreshers, COVID 19, Child Psychology and issues related to protection, gender and inclusive education etc.12. Organize awareness session on monsoon preparedness and DRR in participation of learners, parents and community leaders, works with PERU
