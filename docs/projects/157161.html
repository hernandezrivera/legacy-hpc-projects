Project Summary

The programme addresses the basic needs of all non-South Sudanese refugees and asylum seekers, including refugees from Eritrea, Ethiopia, Syria, Yemen, Chad, Somalia, CAR and others. This includes ongoing voluntary repatriation of Chadian refugees in West Darfur.
Voluntary return is not a viable option for most refugees in Sudan, and resettlement options remain limited. For this reason, the refugee response integrates asset-building and livelihoods, to strengthen self-reliance and ensure refugees may lead dignified lives while in Sudan. Mainstreaming of protection assistance, including direct support to SGBV survivors, will be prioritized. In eastern Sudan, the response will continue to bolster reception and protection services for new arrivals, including prepared meals and health and nutrition screenings. It will maintain basic assistance for the most vulnerable refugees in camps across Kassala and Gedaref, including food assistance through cash based transfers (CBT) for both asylum seekers and newly recognized refugees. Protection assistance will emphasize UASC identification, family tracing and reunification and psychosocial support services, as well as improved referral pathways for direct GBV support to survivors. The response also prioritizes longer-term solutions, with a strong role for protection through self-reliance initiatives to address the unique needs of protracted caseloads. The response will include specific protection assistance for refugees transiting through Sudan, as well as for victims of trafficking. Targeted outreach to asylum seekers on onward movement risks will be pursued and integrated within livelihoods programming to support a more favourable protection environment.
The response also addresses the needs of refugees living in urban areas, especially in Khartoum, with a focus on livelihoods programming to support self-reliance, such as vocational training opportunities, CBT, micro-financing and livelihood asset protection, with efforts to create partnerships with the private sector and development actors. To safeguard basic needs support will also be expanded to ensure refugees can access national education and health systems, as well as to strengthen community-based protection networks and improve referral pathways for GBV prevention, treatment.

Needs

In eastern Sudan, there has been a slower rate of new arrivals from Eritrea, with 570 individuals per month in 2018, down from 2017’s monthly average of over 800. They are hosted in some of the poorest regions of Sudan and face high levels of poverty, and many remain reliant on humanitarian assistance. Over 70 per cent or newly-arrived asylum-seekers in eastern Sudan migrate onward. Along these migratory routes, refugees and asylum-seekers can be exposed to various forms of exploitation that can result in human rights violations. Socio-economic challenges and protection concerns are often cited by refugees as primary factors in their choice to move onward, including the lack of education and livelihood opportunities. There is an urgent need for more durable solutions that support greater self-reliance and enhanced protection. In Darfur, a new influx of refugees from Central African Republic (CAR) began in March 2018, with over 4,000 individuals fleeing violence and seeking asylum, who require protection and NFI support. Additionally, over 4,800 Chadian refugees returned to Chad in 2018, and it is expected that the remainder will return through an organized voluntary repatriation process in 2019. In the urban context, the majority of PoC continue to face obstacles in accessing employment and their survival remain precarious as they have to pay for their own housing, health and education. Khartoum is foreseen to remain a main hosting and transit point for refugees in Sudan in the upcoming years. Financial support for the establishment of businesses, technical and skills training and counselling are some of the important areas to be strengthened in a bid to boost self-employment for the refugee population in Khartoum. Promotion of employment opportunities as a means of attaining self-reliance is a key operational priority for the urban refugee programme in 2019. Despite the overall budget reductions, core education activities will be prioritized in 2019 to ensure refugee youth have increased access to primary education. Support for the most vulnerable children with school fees, school grants, and educational material have been fully prioritized and UNHCR will organize enrollment campaigns within the communities to boost enrollment rates.

Activities

In camps, individual and household-level assistance will continue to be provided to address ongoing needs in protection, ES/NFI, energy, food security, nutrition, health, WASH and livelihoods. This also includes greater participation by refugees in camp management, shelter and latrine construction, protection and security functions within their communities, community mobilization and service delivery. Continue facilitate return of Chadian and Ehtiopian refugees that opt for volrep, including registration, transport, and multi-purpose cash assistance with continuity in providing basic assistance and protection services to the residual caseload. Ongoing biometric registration and protection assistance will continue for new arrivals. Refugees based in urban and out of camp settlements, self-reliance and livelihood assistance will be priority interventions with continuation of CBI while working with partners to respond to the basic needs of refugees alongside host communities. UNHCR in East will work with GoS and initiate capacity building of community structures in refugee camps to pilot community self-management in education and health sectors. Prevention and response mechanisms for gender based violence, trafficking cases and extremely vulnerable people will be embarked. The office will continue capacity building of local institutions, law enforcement officers, courts authorities and PoCs to counter trafficking and smuggling. Khartoum is foreseen to remain a main hosting and transit point for refugees in Sudan in the upcoming years. The urban refugee protection strategy will prioritize enhancing protection mechanisms for extremely vulnerable individuals, including women at risk, VoT and UASC, the promotion of employment opportunities as a means of attaining self-reliance, and expanding access to services through local community based structures and enhanced community outreach.

Impact on environment/natural resources
