Project Summary

Based on the South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (2023), an estimated 9.4 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance. Among them, there are 2.2 million women and 4.9 million children, including 2.4 million girls and 2.5 million boys. Child protection needs have significantly increased since 2022. These needs are highest in severity in thirteen counties: Pibor, Akobo, Nasir, Fangak, Melut, Rubkona, Fashoda, Leer, Cuebet, Panyikang, Ayod, Aweil East and Canal/Pigi. This situation has been exacerbated by protracted displacement and limited access to basic services which continues to affect the physical, mental, and social well-being of children, adolescents and caregivers. While progress has been made in the provision of PSS to children and their caregivers, the experience of any humanitarian crisis increases the vulnerability of children affecting their mental health and psychosocial well-being. This situation has been worsened by the limited availability of mental health and psychosocial support services and the low level of core child protection services including: family tracing and reunification, case management, and reintegration services for children affected by armed forces and groups, leaving many children untreated and unable to access the needed services. GREDO is seeking to provide critical child protection services and assistance to separated, unaccompanied and children associated with armed groups in Pibor, Lafon, Akobo and Kapoeta East Counties. The intervention is participatory, integrated and right based and will be implemented for a period of twelve months. GREDO will strengthen existing child protection referral pathway ensuring safe and secure identification, documentation, referral to service providers and follow up of all cases of child protection. GREDO will train 40 Case Workers on minimum child protection standards and support them in managing CP cases. Two Youth and Child Friendly Spaces will be established in each location where structured activities targeting at risk children will be conducted including provision of PSS, registration of UASC followed by family tracing and reunification as well as youth resilience programs. Young girls of reproductive age will be provided with Menstrual Hygiene Management training and hygiene kits. Caregivers of UASC and former CAAFAGs will be supported through restricted Cash Based Intervention to enable them obtain essential supplies and other basic needs like food. GREDO will establish one Children Based Protection network in each county and train them on CPiE, protection awareness and messaging, preparedness and Child Protection Risk Protection mitigation. On integration, GREDO will work with nutrition partners on Child protection messaging targeting women. Children with protection concerns and malnourished will be referred to nutrition service providers for appropriate care. Education partners will be engaged in enrolment of at risk and out of school boys and girls. Injured children or those suffering from extreme trauma will be referred to health providers for specialized care. A complaint and feedback mechanism (CFM) will be activated on contact with target communities where GREDO will provide sufficient information to affected persons on how to submit complaints and receive feedback. GREDO will reach 600 UASC who will be identified, documented, and reunited with their families. At least 200 UASC (100 boys, 100 girls) will be supported through Cash Based individual protection assistance as part of case management and referrals. Similarly, 600 (300 girls, 300 boys) will be supported through case management and referrals services and 16,000 individuals reached through specialized PSS and related services such as recreational activities, livelihood and parenting skills. Finally, 80 persons (60 Females/20 Males) will be trained on CPiE while another 30,000 persons will be reached through child protection awareness activities.

1. Needs

The deterioration in the humanitarian situation has stripped people of their physical and mental well-being, of their living standards and coping mechanisms. According to HNO 2023, it is anticipated that protection needs are likely to increase, while protection risks will exacerbate, and vulnerabilities rise. Repeated shocks have weakened community-based structures and services. Inadequate access to services will continue to push families to adopt negative coping mechanisms, exposing the most vulnerable, particularly children and women, to protection risks, such as violence, exploitation, abuse, recruitment, trafficking, child labour, and early marriage. Protection concerns remain high as civilians continue to be displaced and killed, their houses and properties destroyed, and access to livelihoods and essential services disrupted due to violence. Gender Analysis and Safety Audit conducted by ACF in March 2022 shows that persistent gender inequality for women and girls in South Sudan hampers their ability to access social services. GBV remains a critical threat, particularly for women and girls who are exposed to risks when carrying out routine activities as simple as collecting water and firewood. South Sudan children and adolescents continue to face a multitude of risks, including abduction, GBV and other forms of violence, recruitment into armed groups and separation from families. An estimated 2.9 million children and adolescents, including those with disabilities, are at heightened risk of violence, abuse, and exploitation and need critical child protection services in 2024. Child protection needs are highest in severity in thirteen counties: Pibor, Akobo, Nasir, Fangak, Melut, Rubkona, Fashoda, Leer, Cuebet, Panyikang, Ayod, Aweil East and Canal/Pigi. Continued outbreaks of violence, food insecurity, and floods in some parts of the country, including, Jonglei, Upper Nile, Warrap, and Lakes states, continue to weaken the protective environment for children and significantly impairing the capacity of families to appropriately care for children, putting them at risk of violence, abuse, neglect, and exploitation. The 2022 FSNMS+ qualitative findings indicate the presence of mental health and psychosocial concerns within communities and identify an urgent need for MHPSS and life-skills education. Other factors driving protection concerns among children include: lack of alternative pathways for adolescent girls due to limited education particularly for girls who have dropped out of school, education related expenses making it difficult for IDPs and returnees to retain children in school, patriarchal gender norms and cultural practices that restrict girls options, the practice of bride price, restriction to girls mobility and participation in activities outside the home, weak community protection structures and poverty and limited options for conflict affected families leading to forced marriage in exchange for cattle to replenish family animal assets. GREDO therefore aims to aims to prevent and respond to immediate life saving child protection services to 47,200 vulnerable children and their families in Pibor, Lafon, Akobo and Kapoeta East counties.

2. Project activities

GREDO CP staffs will conduct sensitization sessions with Community Leaders, Community Members, Chiefs, Local Authorities, Parent Teachers Associations, Teachers and Religious leaders to provide information on the project, receive inputs from the affected community and seek their support. Information provided to affected communities to include; Project aim, project objectives, expected results, project activities, project lifespan, beneficiaries’ selection criteria, modality of intervention, role of the community in project implementation and role of project staffs. Also, information on Complaint and Feedback Mechanism will be provided with reporting and feedback mechanism being articulated. GREDO Protection staffs will work closely with chiefs, community leaders, teachers, local authorities and parents to map child protection risks prior to commencing project activities. Report generated from the audit will be utilized by project staffs while deciding locations for Child Friendly Spaces, Sites for recreational sports and to create awareness among staffs and community members of risk mitigation measures. GREDO will establish eight Child Friendly Spaces using locally available materials. CFS Managers will oversee activities within the safe spaces ensuring safety of children and supervision of PSS and structured activities. Through 40 caseworkers, GREDO will provide Comprehensive case management and referrals including CBI: The exercise will involve identification, documentation, family tracing and reunification of unaccompanied and separated children. UASC headed households, Caregivers of UASC will be supported with Cash Based Individual Protection assistance as part of case management and referrals. GREDO will facilitate training and support of 40 Case Workers conducting case management. GREDO will provide psychosocial support services to UASC boys and girls and caregivers of UASC. Specialized PSS will be conducted by trained Case Workers and will be supplemented with Life Skills training, recreation sports, and training on beadwork, embroidery, painting, and other livelihood skills. GREDO will conduct awareness and prevention messaging through community mobilizers, case workers and Community Based Child Protection Networks (CBCPN) targeting women, men, boys, girls, teachers, chiefs, community leaders, youth leaders, women leaders and religious leaders on the rights of children. GREDO will facilitate in the formation, training and support of five CBCPN (one in each County). CBCPNs will be trained on Child Protection in Emergency, child protection risk mitigation, preparedness and early warning. CBPNs will remain active in their respective communities advocating for the rights of children, mapping, monitoring and reporting protection risks likely to impact on children. GREDO will conduct Menstrual Hygiene Management training targeting reproductive age girls. Girls will be provided with menstrual hygiene kits in order for them to manage menses.
