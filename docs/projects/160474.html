1 - Needs

Lifesaving assistanceThe HNO 2018 highlighted seasonal lifesaving assistance (access to warm clothes, bedding, heaters/fuel, etc) to be a priority especially in contact line areas which continue to experience disruption to markets and facilities particularly for children, women and elderly. More women headed HHs in GCA Donetsk and Luhansk require winterization (22% of IDPs, and 23% of ND vs 17% of men headed HHs) particularly on winter clothing. According to REACH, significant issues with access to fuel for winter heating have been reported especially in rural areas.Availability and accessibility of hygiene items for women and infants also remains an issue for families in insecure areas and those renting accommodation who struggle to afford these items in addition to food, rent and utility payments. Addressing sub standard and over crowded accommodation is of particular concern for children, elderly, persons with disability or chronic illness, and for women at risk of sexual violence. The ongoing conflict has exhausted people’s financial savings and prevented many from recovering their livelihoods. As a result, almost 30,000 vulnerable households in both GCA and NGCA are struggling to adequately prepare for the coming winter and were identified as a priority for receiving winterization assistance by Shelter/NFI Cluster partners (Shelter and NFI analysis)Rent has been reported as a significant expenditure of HH in all assessments. The HNO 2018 reported more than 80% of IDPs living in private housing (3/4 are paying rents). As a last resort, people still live in collective centers mostly near the contact line or in large urban centers due to inability to pay high rents. SCI’s MSNA indicated protection concerns related to the lack of privacy for women and girls in these centers. Families with children and pensioners (in rented accommodation and collective centers) are at risk of eviction or having utilities cut off due limited finances. SCI’s IYCF assessment also showed infants living in collective centers were significantly less likely to achieve minimum dietary diversity. For the 2019-20 winter period, in GCA there is a gap of 5,531 of vulnerable households (or 13,385 people) in need of winterization assistance. In NGCA, this number reaches 23,182 households (or 56,100 people). The overall number of people in need for winterization assistance in GCA for 2020 is 50,200 HHs (or 121,485 people), while on NGCA this figure is 105,903 HHs (or 256,285 people). Many such households depend on coal, wood or other types of fuel for heating, which are often unavailable or costly, especially in isolated settlements in contrast to administrative centers where there are functioning and quality markets (Shelter and NFI analysis).Gender analysis showed that more men have self owned shelter than women while more women live in collective centers than men. Comparing the number of rooms of the shelters it is visible that female headed HHs have more often less rooms and rent less expensive shelter, and taking into account the conclusion that such HHs have more children and elderly, they have worse shelter conditions, because they have lower income.

2 - Activities & outcomes

Activities1) Conflict affected boys, girls, women, men and elderly receiving winter Non Food Items (NFIs) in NGCA2) Conflict affected boys, girls, women, men and elderly receiving coal in NGCA Post distribution monitoring will be conducted to assess the outcome of the activity and monitor proper utilization of assistance provided. In NGCA implementation monitoring will be done jointly with local partners Outcomes% Of beneficiaries (disaggregated by sex, age) reporting that humanitarian assistance is safe, accessible, child-friendly and age- and gender appropriate% of persons/target population in a given context reporting an improved feeling of safety and dignity by the end of the intervention compared to at the beginning% of surveyed households who report that the winter NFIs and coal received has supported their winterisation needs.Vulnerability criteria: HH with members with disabilities, HH with members with special needs, female headed households, HH with orphans, etc)To mitigate risk of beneficiaries duplication while implementing activities there will be cross-check verification with partners and other stakeholders applied.PartnershipSCI will implement through its existing local partner organizations in NGCA. These local partners have been already trained and have experience in community mobilization, distribution and implementation of shelter related activities. In addition, there is a strong coordination between SCI and the Donetsk NGCA local de-facto authorities who are informed beforehand about all program activities. Operations in NGCA definitely require advanced risk management and mitigation measures, as there are certain peculiarities of project implementation in this area. Firstly, for this project SCI selected only qualified and accredited partner organizations, to ensure that implementing the project is done by partners with sufficient capacity, knowledge and expertise. Secondly, all project components will be approved by the de-facto authorities, to demonstrate that all activities of SCI and relevant partner organizations are transparent and clear. Direct access to beneficiaries is carried out only through the relevant permission issued by local de-facto authorities. These measures will help to reduce risks for implementing partners.
