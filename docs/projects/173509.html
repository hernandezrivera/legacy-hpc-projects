Project Summary

The aim of COOPI is to reduce vulnerability, to build resilience to a reasonable level of preparation, and to reinforce the coping capacity of communities in order to decrease suffering and to prevent future loss of life. The proposal intends to achieve this, through a combination of Community-Driven approaches (to empower people in poverty by putting resources and responsibility for decision making) and of direct assistance interventions such as the provision of NFIs, cash/vouchers, trainings and awareness campaigns.
In line with the 2021 HRP Sectoral Clusters Objectives COOPI will target the most vulnerable group of newly/protracted IDPs and host communities, spontaneous returnees, and hazard-affected not-displaced communities, in 8 localities of North Darfur State and 1 locality in Khartoum State
This project intends:
- to provide timely life-saving assistance and to improve vulnerable people’s access to livelihoods and life-sustaining basic services carrying out vocational training to people with special needs and supporting their families with food assistance;
- to provide access to basic water services and to adequate, protected, and gender-sensitive sanitation services including hygiene promotion;
- to improve living conditions through the provision of timely ES/NFI assistance and supporting the transition to sustainable and appropriate shelter durable solutions;
- to mitigate the impact of future hazards through the prepositioning of life saving NFIs and supporting vulnerable households to build safer shelter, and community sensitization of the local risks and hazards explaining to vulnerable communities how they can manage these risks better to mitigate the impact of floods and fire.
In order to mitigate the protection risks linked with the implemented activities COOPI will monitor and respond to protection needs through cross-cutting humanitarian actions for all time of project implementation.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in Sudan remains severe and complex, characterized by several, concurring and interdependent negative phenomena such as poverty, underdevelopment, economic shocks, natural disasters, climate change and localised violent conflicts. The economic, social, political and environmental Sudanese dimensions are characterized by weaknesses and high vulnerability that put the population at risk. Moreover, tensions between IDPs, returnees and local communities exacerbate the already difficult living conditions and resilience of the population as a whole, completely in distress and characterized by a multi-factorial vulnerability that needs to be addressed with an integrated approach as a response to an increasing combined severity of needs. The lack of investment in public services and high inflation contribute to increase the vulnerabilities of the population not able to address basic needs and cope with food insecurity. Food insecurity remains alarmingly high in Sudan with increased and protracted displacement, economic decline and inflation, and high food price hikes exacerbated by the impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic and the high inflation rates. The lockdown measures to prevent the spread of COVID-19 significantly decreased commodity movement, market function and cross-border trade, and compromised livelihoods and daily labor opportunities, reducing household purchasing power and food access of the vulnerable population.Communicable disease outbreaks are proliferating at the same time as the economic situation hampers the Government's capacity to respond: the country is experiencing concurrent outbreaks of water and vector-borne diseases, including cholera, malaria, dengue, Rift Valley fever and chikungunya. IDPs, returnees, refugees and other vulnerable groups experience some of the most acute needs. Shelter continues to represent a major concern in affected community of Sudan. The recent rainy season left several communities affected by flood and it increased the number of households in need of shelter. In Kabkabiya (North Darfur) for example, a recent Inter-Agency Assessment reported an estimated of 9,145 houses have completely been destroyed and 8,513 partially damaged. An estimated 7013 HHs' latrines have been reported to have collapsed.In Khartoum State, an Inter-Agency rapid assessment, was conducted on 5th September 2020 in 5 Jebel Awlia villages along river Nile, and in MAYO-Mandela Area followed by repeated field visits and interviews with community leaders during the severe flood crisis of August-September 2020. The assessment confirmed a major need of rehabilitation and/or reconstruction of shelters and of sanitation facilities as well.The Clusters in HRP 2021 identified 7.1 million of people in need of FSL assistance, 8 million of people in need of WASH assistance and 1.9 million in need of Shelter assistance.This project aims to respond to identified needs shared by sectoral clusters and several assessments conducted in the areas where COOPI is currently operating implementing funded projects.This proposal will assist 15,000 affected population in FSL, 252,900 people living in communities targeted with WASH interventions and 19,000 people targeted with shelter assistance.

6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

COOPI adopts several Protection policies in adherece to core humanitarian standars such as "do not harm" (avoid causing harm), ensuring access to impartial assistance, GBV (protect people from violence) and assisting with rights claims, access to remedies and recovery from abuse – i.e. HLP issues et al/support to transition with regards to shelter. The roll-out of such policies is responsibility of COOPI Sudan's Protection Focal Person. All Project Management Units are expected to get trained and apply such policies in the day-to-day work. Local partners - which are mandatory for INGOs according to HAC regulations - will also be carefully selected based on existing policies. Past COOPI's programme experience has confirmed that selected local partners can contribute meaningfully to the promotion and mainstreaming of protection across multi-sectoral humanitarian interventions. A beneficiary complaint mechanism system is being established to ensure prompt and direct acquisition of feedback from the targeted communities as well as their representatives. Where COOPI will implement shelter assistance on provision of durable solutions, a minimum due diligence tools will be set in place in order to check land tenure or ownership and prevent farther risk of eviction or conflict.
