Project Summary

According to the 2024 HNRP, the protection environment in Sudan has grown increasingly dire,marked by a stark disregard of civilians’ safety and civilian infrastructure.This deterioration is linked to urban warfare in Khartoum,the spreading conflict in Darfur, complex fighting in South Kordofan, and ongoing strife in Blue Nile.Multiple joint assessments highlight pressing child protection needs, family separation, GBV, child exploitation and abuse and child recruitment;emotional trauma and psychological distress.Over 3,150 child rights violations and 165 cases of sexual violence against women and girls have been reported.The GBV AoR and service providers have received surging GBV cases including sexual violence against IDPs on transit and during looting of homes.The number of domestic violence cases is rising and sexual violence/exploitation as women and girls are displaced, in transit, in temporary shelters, or at border crossings waiting for visas and economic hardship due to increasing prices of basic goods including food, water, and fuel . RI will provide the following services to address identified needs:
General Protection:
RI will provide protection assistance, mental health and psychosocial support(MHPSS) including referrals and raise awareness on general protection issues targeting 11,094 individuals.294 project participants (100 girls,100 boys,50 women and 44 men) will be targeted with protection assistance; 400 adults (200 women and 200 men) with MHPSS through health facilities and 300(150 women and 150 men) referrals.Awareness raising will benefit 10,000 individuals (1500 girls,1500 boys,3500 men and 3500 women) while 100(50 men and 50 women) will be trained on protection and safe service delivery.1 community based network will be established to strengthen community linkages;protection monitoring through 5 focus groups and 5 protection needs assessments conducted.
Child Protection:
To address prevailing child protection issues,9660 children and caregivers will be targeted.Case management services including referrals will target 390(195 girls,195 boys) at-risk children,including unaccompanied and separated children,at-risk of or experiencing psychosocial distress,violence etc and those in need of alternative care.To address psychological needs of distressed children,structured and sustained MHPSS including mental health counseling will target 1945 children (973 girls and 972 boys).325 parents/caregivers (163 women,162 men) will access positive parenting skills.6900 individuals (1725 girls,1725 boys,1725 women and 1725 men) will receive Child Protection Awareness raising & Information Dissemination while 9 adults and children groups/committees supported to encourage active community participation.To strengthen and ensure a more predictable,accountable, and effective CP response,50 women and 50 men frontline staff will be trained on CPIE and case management.
Gender Based Violence:
This intervention will target 7230 individuals with GBV services.1 Women & Girls safe space/ center will provide psychosocial support, awareness raising and information sharing and adolescent girl’s sessions.10 women and 10 girls’ capacity will be strengthened through vocational skills and start-up capital;500 adolescent girls and 500 women reached with life skills and recreational activities.Specialized GBV response services will target 1000 women and girls (500 women and 500 girls) while 1800 dignity kits (900 adolescent girls,900 women) are distributed.To improve community knowledge on GBV,available services,referral pathways etc. awareness sessions will target 5,000 individuals (1000 girls,500 boys,3000 women and 500 men) including 4 public campaigns.To enhance community linkages and referrals,3 CBPNs will be supported.Capacity building will target 75 men and 75 women involved in GBV prevention;GBV mainstreaming (5 men and 5 women); and specialized GBV trainings for frontline staff (25 women and 25 men).1 GBV assessment conducted.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

As a protection based project, various aspects of protection are already embedded including key principles and approaches. The design is guided by the Humanitarian Response Plan developed after comprehensive needs assessments taking into account vulnerabilities, potential risks, and specific protection concerns for the targeted communities as a basis of identified needs. Against the identified needs project strategies, objectives and activities that directly address and mitigate these risks have been articulated. Targeted communities were engaged and their views incorporated in designing contextually and culturally appropriate protection interventions. The needs of various groups have been carefully integrated and observed during the design of the project including allocation of adequate budgets.Protection and non-protection staff capacity-building initiatives aimed at augmenting their understanding of various protection principles and basics have been factored in the project. Protection guidelines and standard operating procedures will inform activity implementation including identification, referral, and response to protection concerns. Assistance and services will be provided according to needs and not on the basis of age, sex, gender identity, nationality, race, or ethnic allegiance. Services and assistance will be provided in good quantity, within safe and easy to reach locations, known by the affected individuals and accessible by all various groups including persons with disabilities, GBV survivors, vulnerable children etc.By incorporating regular community complaint and feedback mechanisms in the project, protection concerns of affected people are voiced and incorporated into decision-making processes. Affected individuals and communities play an active role in the measurement of the quality of interventions that affect them and guide establishment of effective and easily accessible mechanisms for suggestions and complaints increasing accountability. Coordination with relevant stakeholders enhances timely delivery of responses and strengthens referral mechanisms. Continuous monitoring and evaluation already amalgamated into the project assesses the impact of protection activities and minimizes any harmful and unintended negative consequences.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

The project has already incorporated various mechanisms to engage communities including providing feedback on project interventions. Regular community meetings, focus group discussions with various groups, and surveys will be conducted to provide platforms for exchange of ideas. These interactions will allow community members to express their opinions, share insights, and raise concerns regarding the project activities. The project team will analyze the received feedback to identify trends, patterns, gaps, lessons and suggestions for improvement. RI will closely work with the communities to design appropriate actions to address the concerns raised. Some of the suggestions will also be used to improve future programs by incorporating them in their design and as appropriate some of the feedback will inform advocacy initiatives as well as future donor engagements. The feedback will also lay a foundation for further community engagements identifying areas of collaboration and how the community can contribute in finding solutions to identified gaps. Constant monitoring will be maintained to track progress of various actions and inform adaptability to emerging community issues and needs fostering accountability and increased sense of ownership. For instance, feedback relating to low participation of adolescent girls in PSS activities may prompt discussions between the project team and community on how to support and enhance their participation. Guided by the community some of the changes may include rescheduling the activities to certain times of the day convenient for both parents and the adolescent girls.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

RI has established inclusive and accessible complaint and feedback mechanisms compliant with the inter agency approaches to accountability to affected people. The project teams will endeavor and ensure that feedback is collected from project participants through regular community engagement forums e.g. focus group discussions taking into account needs of boys, girls, women and men including people living with disabilities. The diverse mechanisms of providing feedback seek to ease accessibility, observe privacy and reduce harm to the project participants specifically for sensitive issues like GBV as well as SEA. The complaint and feedback channels will take into consideration appropriate language and literacy levels of project participants.RI actively participates in various Inter-Agency coordination forums, this will continue and further strengthened to ensure that the project team is updated on relevant procedures and guidelines, reporting as well as enhance collaboration and coordination with other agencies. Supporting and utilizing existing referral mechanisms including jointly taking part in establishing new ones will ensure project participants timely access life-saving services in cognizance that RI may not be able to address all community needs. Staff training on SEA prevention and reporting utilizing appropriate channels will significantly contribute to strengthening adherence to sector-wide standards. Participation in inter agency capacity building initiatives will enhance a common approach to issues, strengthen adherence to standards and increase accountability to affected people. RI will also utilize the expertise available within the interagency teams to provide relevant trainings and participate in joint assessments and monitoring visits geared towards learning new skills and approaches including designing harmonized response approaches. The project teams will utilize the interagency resources including awareness raising materials on PSEA, common messages and engage the PSEAH network in conducting SEA investigations and follow ups in line with their established guidelines and procedures. A responsive complaints response mechanism, regular audits, and integration of community feedback into decision-making processes underscore our dedication to continuous learning, improvement, and transparency in addressing the needs and concerns of affected people.
