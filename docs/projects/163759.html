1 - Needs

The situation in Libya is such that on a daily basis thousands of migrants boys and girls and refugees from Middle East and Sub-Saharan Africa leave their countries with the hope of crossing the Mediterranean to reach Europe. The route is controlled by criminal networks and leave those that travel this route at risk of various human rights abuses. On their way to and during their stay in Libya, migrants, refugees and asylum-seekers, including children, are reportedly at risk of being subjected to widespread and systematic human rights violations and abuses at the hands of smugglers, traffickers, armed groups, militias, criminal gangs and state officials acting with impunity. Violations and abuses reportedly include unlawful deprivation of liberty and arbitrary detention, torture and other forms of ill-treatment, including rape and other forms of sexual violence, abduction for ransom and other forms of extortion, forced labour and unlawful killing. Some other through entry at border points end up staying on smuggling camps and once out or escaped from there are similarly to the other category placed in detention facilities alongside with adults, leaving them exposed to further risks, without no or limited opportunities for protection nor release unless a durable solution is provided. For the most part, refugee children are living in poor living conditions in cramped urban spaces. Moreover, the breakdown of community family protective mechanisms makes them particularly vulnerable to abuse, violence, exploitation neglect. More investment is required on building Community-based networks’ self-reliance to respond to certain issues in the absence of national systems in country due to the situation of Libya. The aim is to guarantee that boys and girls youth will live in friendly environments that promote protection through the establishment of sustainable systems within and owned by the Community. The system will work towards providing holistic services for girl boy survivors while building the capacity of refugees, host communities local authorities to protect children at risk. A network of trained youth recreational facilitators will lead recreation activities establish clubs to promote safe psychosocially supportive play for different groups of children. UNHCR continues, together with the protection partners to play a key role in coordinating strengthening best interest processes, as well as ensuring family-based alternative care arrangements are made for UAMs ongoing case management of CP incidents. With the refugee and migrants populations absorbed in the sector system in Libya, further advocacy efforts and strategic directions to ensure these children remain prioritized on the humanitarian platforms. In 2020, UNHCR will continue intensifying its efforts towards better identification and reporting of child abuses with a higher percentage of at least 20% children with special needs identified being assisted and at least 20% of the UASC are facilitated with Best Interest Determination (BIDs).

2 - Activities

1. A holistic protection monitoring system will be enhanced whereby vulnerability of refugees will be monitored and checked, to expand and strengthen their protection space;2. A comprehensive needs-based service provision will be strengthened and scaled up, ensuring access to a variety of related services (Psychosocial activities, educational activities, awareness-raising activities, Case Management for children in need and survivor of SGBV, Financial Assistance) for the identified most vulnerable POCs at Community Development Centers and Social Center, which will be functioning as in 2018 as a social hub for the different communities, and where to carry out all the social activities (from one-off events to structured non-formal educational classes for minors), following a model which proved to be a safe space for social cohesion and integration;3. Support to UNHCR in the logistic management of the response will be granted and improved, thus creating a reliable and conducive mechanism for fast emergency provision of needed items. Refugees and asylum seekers experience several challenges to access basic services provided by the Libyan Government and, in many cases, lack a proper and sustainable safety net to support them. In time, they have been affected by additional in-country displacements, which have once again negatively impacted their resilience and coping capacities. While meeting these needs, it remains essential to bear in mind that in any emergency, factors such as belonging to a caring family or community and maintaining well-known traditions and culture are equally crucial to sustaining the community. The intervention will thus focus on service provision as well as resilience improvement, through an integrated approach aimed at identifying durable solutions while still supporting them in recovering and coping with the everyday struggle.

5 - Mitigation measures

Agencies will continue engagement throughout 2020, as a member of the Steering Committee, to find solutions while also directly implementing pilots of alternatives to cash. The CMWG is addressing the currency depreciation and is seeking alternatives. Agencies will continue its engagement with the Working Group, supporting the identification of shared solutions. Identification of alternatives, upon a thorough evaluation of the options in coordination with UNHCR, including payment of a vendor through bank account, distribution of vouchers, etc.Maintain strong country and regional analysis of trends, so as to adapt programmes and security procedures ahead of new risks. Regularly and frequently review risks and risk management. Maintain good security networks. Collecting information and advocate trough clusters system for access to closed areas.The opposition to refugees and migrants at large is increasing by the day, not only among communities and political representatives alike, consistently decreasing local acceptance. To minimise the consequence of a decreased local acceptance agencies will seek to ensure regular meetings with relevant authorities and ministries officials with the support of Liaison Officers. UNHCR to provide agencies with continuous support and assistance specifically vis a vis pressing demands and enquiries from the local police and investigative authorities.Suspension of activities and relocation to alternative sites.

6.1 - Other accountability mechanisms

The project was developed on the basis of age, gender and diversity participatory assessment in 2019, carried out with all relevant age, gender and diversity groups with UNHCR and partners. The project involves ongoing community engagement at every stage, and the views of each child are an integral part of every intervention. Beneficiaries will be able to benefit from the CFM, and from agency and UNHCR global and local hotlines and investigative and corrective mechanisms. M&E structure will engage affected populations in assessing and changing the project.
