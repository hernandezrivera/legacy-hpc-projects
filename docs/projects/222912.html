Project Summary

"Enlighten the Underprivileged Candle" represents AMAN's robust commitment to providing essential educational support to Rohingya Refugees/FDMN, focusing primarily on the academic development of children, adolescents, and youth. The project endeavors to elevate the quality of formal education in collaboration with the education sector and UNICEF, integrating the Myanmar curriculum.With a primary focus on educational rehabilitation, the project outlines extensive plans for Camps 8E, and 15 including critical enhancements in WASH facilities to incorporate a comprehensive disaster risk reduction strategy. Moreover, it aims to ensure proper health and hygiene maintenance by providing well-segregated facilities. Teacher training and student assessments, aligned with professional development, will be fundamental in enhancing the educational landscape. Additionally, the distribution of educational materials will be a significant component, encompassing the Myanmar Curriculum for diverse age groups, coupled with school feeding programs to augment the learning environment.
Capacity-building initiatives targeting staff and community stakeholders, sensitization programs, and community engagement via learning circles are pivotal to enriching the educational framework. The project will be strictly governed by stringent safety measures, adherence to child rights, and safeguarding policies. Furthermore, it aims to offer critical mental health support to ensure a holistic learning environment.
Emphasizing inclusivity, gender equity, and protection, the project has a strategic focus on marginalized groups, particularly individuals with disabilities and LGBTQI individuals. Collaborations with stakeholders, camp leaders, and SMC groups form a critical aspect of the project, integrating various cross-cutting issues and ensuring an inclusive educational approach.
The proposed locations for the project encompass Camps 8E (Block B), and 15 (Block D), with a target of 380 learners. Specifically, the project aims to enroll 3% of disabled learners and LGBTQI individuals. With a budget of US$134,760 earmarked for 2025, AMAN is well-prepared and equipped with essential policies. The organization's active participation in the ISCG and PSEA networks further fortifies its commitment to delivering this crucial educational program.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The education system for Rohingya refugees and the host community in Bangladesh faces significant barriers that limit access to quality education for children and adolescents. According to the 2024 Inter-Sector Needs Analysis (ISNA), 59% of children aged 3-18 are enrolled in education facilities, but 41% are not. Gender disparities are prominent, with enrollment rates for boys (62%) surpassing those for girls (57%), and only 8% of girls aged 15-18 attend school, compared to 75% of girls aged 5-14. Cultural norms and security risks contribute to these disparities, particularly for girls, who are often pulled out of school after puberty, and boys, who are forced to work and drop out. Educational infrastructure remains inadequate, with overcrowding in Learning Centers (LCs) and limited weather-resilient facilities. Only 14% of children attend Community-Based Learning Facilities (CBLFs), and 4% are in Multi-Purpose Centers (MPCs). The shortage of trained teachers and lack of professional development also affect education quality, leading to high dropout rates. Moreover, alternative learning pathways, especially for adolescent girls, are scarce, limiting opportunities for continued education beyond primary school. The challenges faced by LGBTI+ individuals in the community, including discrimination and exclusion from education, further exacerbate inequalities. Addressing these issues requires comprehensive reforms that focus on increasing enrollment, improving infrastructure, and providing gender-sensitive and inclusive educational spaces. Teacher training and professional development are also crucial to improving the quality of education. Expanding secondary education and vocational training for older children, particularly girls, will help reduce dropout rates and enhance future opportunities. Recommendations include increasing community outreach to promote education, especially for girls, and advocating for the construction of durable, weather-resistant learning spaces. Security measures must be implemented to ensure children’s safety, particularly for girls and older children. Creating safe spaces for LGBTI+ children and supporting the formal recognition of learning outcomes through certification or accreditation will also improve access and opportunities. With these efforts, both Rohingya refugee children and host community children can benefit from a more inclusive, supportive education system, helping to secure a better future for all.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

AMAN incorporates climate change adaptation and mitigation into education through various activities aimed at building resilience and reducing environmental impacts. For adaptation, AMAN integrates climate change topics into curricula, conducts workshops on climate resilience, and promotes sustainable practices like water management and eco-friendly agriculture. Schools are equipped with energy-efficient infrastructure and renewable energy sources, and gardening programs are introduced to teach sustainable food systems.For mitigation, AMAN focuses on reducing carbon footprints through waste management, energy conservation, tree planting campaigns, and promoting sustainable materials and low-carbon transportation options.Additionally, AMAN’s Disaster Risk Management (DRM) strategy includes disaster risk reduction (DRR) activities like risk assessments, early warning systems, and disaster preparedness training. Schools receive disaster response kits and conduct evacuation drills.These combined efforts empower communities to adapt to climate change, promote sustainability, and prepare future generations to engage in climate action.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

The Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) plan for "Enlighten the Underprivileged Candle" aims to safeguard students and staff through proactive risk assessment, tailored training, and resource readiness. The plan identifies key hazards, ensuring high-risk groups receive targeted support. Staff, students, and community members undergo training on evacuation and first aid, with emergency supplies like first aid kits and educational materials strategically stocked. Clear evacuation routes and safe shelters are designated, backed by a reliable communication system with parents and leaders. Emphasizing protection, the plan integrates child safeguarding, gender-sensitive support, and mental health aid. After emergencies, rapid assessments and temporary learning spaces help stabilize learning continuity. Regular monitoring and updates ensure the plan remains responsive and effective, securing a resilient educational environment.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

The "Enlighten the Underprivileged Candle" project prioritizes youth engagement as a cornerstone of its educational initiatives for the Rohingya Refugee/FDMN community. With a focus on empowering young people, the project will engage 200 youth as active participants in decision-making, skill-building, and leadership roles. These youth will contribute to project design and participate in advisory groups, ensuring their voices are heard in shaping the educational programming. To prepare youth for leadership and community roles, the project will offer training workshops in leadership, vocational skills, and life skills, enabling them to serve as peer mentors and community facilitators. These young leaders will be crucial in promoting the project, raising awareness, and guiding their peers, especially in marginalized groups like disabled and LGBTQI individuals. A strong emphasis will be placed on safeguarding policies, providing mental health support and creating safe spaces to ensure the well-being of all youth participants. Collaborative efforts with community leaders and stakeholders will foster an inclusive environment, further empowering youth and strengthening their role in building community resilience. These initiatives will help develop youth as key agents of change, supporting the project's overall focus on inclusivity, gender equity, and protection.
