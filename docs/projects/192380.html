Project Summary

The project will support local and community partners promote resilience, social cohesion and create a stronger protective environment for children, caregivers and communities, contributing to better protect vulnerable population (women, children/youth, girls and boys, caregivers) from abuse, violence, exploitation and neglect in Beirut and Mount Lebanon.
This project is designed to deliver appropriate, accessible and quality services and strengthen community-based psychosocial support (PSS) systems to children, young people and adults to build their resilience and boost their potential, through a holistic and integrated approach to child protection and Psycho-Social Support. The program has two phases; in the first phase, the first cycle of PSS sessions will be provided by SIF’s technical team and observed by the identified Facilitators, the sessions will take place in dedicated safe spaces within the Partner’s Centres. In parallel, the partners’ staff conducting the capacity building training and took part of the facilitation with SIF` staff. The second cycle will be facilitated by the trained facilitator and supported by SIF CP-PSS qualified team.
SIF will utilize a child-rights approach and strictly adhere to our Child Safeguarding and PSEA Policies, as well as use participatory, community-based and gender-based approaches in all project phases. SIF will focus on optimizing local resources, strengthening existing capacities of non-specialized frontline staff of LNGOs, Volunteers and peers on Psychological First Aid (PFA). Low-intensity brief psychological interventions, child protection and referral mechanism training will provide quality non-specialized community-based protection and PSS to refugee/migrants and vulnerable Lebanese. SIF has already developed PSS Resource material including curricula, training materials and tools adapted to the remote modalities, which will be used by coordinators and frontline staff, volunteers and Peers and shared with other Protection actors. SIF will provide child protection and psychological support as well as ensuring a more robust referral mechanism of affected population at the community level in the current emergency crisis.
The target beneficiaries are children, youth and caregivers who will benefit from child protection PSS sessions. Recreational activities will include all members of the communities, regardless of nationality, in the highly vulnerable BML areas of Shatila, Nabaa, and Burj camp. Boys and girls (6-11 years old), youth (12-17 years old) and caregivers (above 18 years old) will be targeted. 70% of the total direct beneficiaries of the project are children, with equal numbers of girls and boys. The project will identify and equip community safe centers, register the beneficiaries to implement psychosocial support, sports for development sessions, and parenting program, in addition to Basic Literacy and Numeracy, and English courses. A target of 400 beneficiaries from the different age groups will be included in the project and divided between the three centers depending on the area of intervention within 2 cycles. Each cycle will last for a 3 months’ duration. Beneficiaries with special needs and acute vulnerabilities will be referred to specialized agencies through the preexisting mechanisms. Additionally, community members will be trained and mobilized as community focal points within a peer to peer component on Child Protection standards, detection of medium to high risk cases, referral pathways, and community based child protection. A special focus is given on reinforcing their role as frontlines to provide reliable information and psychosocial support sessions within their own community members.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

PSS sessions for children and caregivers will be implemented for the first cycle by SIF’s Monitor with the observation of identified Facilitators and for the second cycle by Partners’ facilitators with the monitoring of SIF’s Monitor. Recreational activities for children (8 sessions) encourage play, group work and it will promote values such as tolerance, non-discrimination, supporting young people in conflict prevention and non-violent communication skills, Specific sport events could be organized too. Caregiver Support program consists of 8 sessions based on needs expressed collected during FGD with beneficiaries: stress management, decision-making, effective communication skills, four sessions on Positive Discipline, and the last session will be a closure session.PSS program for children consists of 8 sessions based on beneficiaries needs: Child rights, safet , emotions anger management , self-confidence, and communication skills. SIF services cover community-based PSS too respond to needs identified on the field requiring specific support through SIF referral to Case Management. SIF will continue using RIMS (Referral information management system) platform to refer cases. SIF will support in the referral of vulnerable children from different nationalities to other NGOs for cash assistance.Two Community initiative will be created by the youth/caregivers of project beneficiaries of to foster social cohesion within the community (cultural event, upcycling, clean up).

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

SIF will strictly adhere to our Child Safeguarding and PSEA Policies in all project phases. SIF has a Child Safeguarding Policy in place, which establishes safeguarding focal points at Country Office levels. The focal points participate in TOTs organized by the HQ safeguarding team and they conduct sensitization activities in the Missions, to staff, volunteers, partners and suppliers. The Safeguarding team in HQ is also responsible for the implementation of the Policy, by following up on the plans and reports prepared at Mission level and by conducting regular capacity building. The SIF Code of Conduct, Child Safeguarding Policy and PSEA Policy are including in all staff, Volunteers, Partners and services providers` inductions, and the SIF. SIF Lebanon has a PSEA Policy and is committed to ensuring our approach is consistent with our national and international disclosure obligations, and complies with all applicable laws, statutes, regulations and codes of conduct.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

SIF pays particular attention to accountability; thus, beneficiaries will be involved in the project at different stages and encouraged to actively participate in activities design and implementation. SIF MEAL team will conduct routine monitoring of project activities as well as Focus Group Discussions and Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) at the end of the implementation. From the beginning of the program, beneficiaries will be informed about the availability of a SIF hotline, open to facilitate the collection of complaints and comments complain boxes will be distributed in all distribution points and partner’s locations. The principle of “Do no harm” is applied in every phase of the project and SIF always tries to assist all parts in eventual conflict without stigmatizing its beneficiaries. The project will be implemented in accordance to the guidance provided under the COVID-19 theme by the World Health Organization, the Government, and the Food Security Cluster.
